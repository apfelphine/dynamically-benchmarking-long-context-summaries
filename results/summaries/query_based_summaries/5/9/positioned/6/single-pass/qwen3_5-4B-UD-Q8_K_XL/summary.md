# **Concise Summary:**

Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City to a Jewish family. After attending a summer program at Columbia University and working as a volunteer at Rockefeller University, he worked at the IBM New York Scientific Center as a high school graduate before enrolling at Harvard University in 1970. He graduated magna cum laude in 1974 and pursued a doctorate in physics at MIT, though he left the program to focus on programming at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. There, he became a prominent hacker and began developing software tools like Emacs, GCC, and GNU make. Frustrated by the restriction of software access and the shift toward proprietary software, Stallman founded the Free Software Foundation in 1985 and launched the GNU project to create a free operating system. He advocated for "free software" as a moral imperative, promoting the concept of copyleft through the GNU General Public License (GPL) to ensure users retain the freedom to share, study, and modify software.

Stallman is an outspoken political campaigner for the free software movement, advocating against software patents, digital rights management, and proprietary software. He has been involved in various international initiatives, including persuading the Indian state of Kerala to switch its schools to free software and advising the president of Ecuador. Stallman has faced controversy regarding his comments on the Jeffrey Epstein scandal and his past writings on child pornography, leading to his resignation from MIT and the FSF in 2019 before his return to the FSF board in 2021. He continues to champion free software, maintain a computer running free software to resist surveillance, and receive numerous honors, including the MacArthur Fellowship in 1990.
