Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City, and maintained an early interest in computers, reading manuals for the IBM 7094 as a pre-teen. He studied at Harvard University, where he felt he found a home, and later became a programmer at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, where he was known by his initials, RMS.

Stallman became a vocal critic of restricted computer access, campaigning against password control systems at MIT. This dissatisfaction led to his staunch advocacy for free software, culminating in the creation of the GNU project in 1983. He published the GNU Manifesto in 1985, outlining his motivation for a free operating system compatible with Unix. Stallman popularized the concept of copyleft through the GNU Emacs General Public License and released the first program-independent GNU General Public License (GPL) in 1989. He remains an outspoken political campaigner for the free software movement, opposing proprietary software and digital rights management.
