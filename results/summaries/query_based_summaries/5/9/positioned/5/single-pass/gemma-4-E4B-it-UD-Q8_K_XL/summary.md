Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City, and held a strong interest in computers from a young age. He began his career writing a numerical analysis program in Fortran in 1970 and later became a regular in the hacker community at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, where he earned a physics bachelor's degree from Harvard in 1974.

Stallman became a staunch critic of restricted computer access, which led to the fragmentation of hacker culture in the late 1970s and early 1980s as manufacturers began using restrictive licenses for software. This shift convinced him of the need for software users to have the freedom to modify and share the software they use. In 1984, he quit his job at MIT to work full-time on the GNU project, which was announced in 1983. In 1985, he published the GNU Manifesto, outlining his motivation for creating a free operating system called GNU, compatible with Unix. He subsequently founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to provide legal infrastructure for the free software movement and popularized the concept of copyleft, which was first implemented in the GNU Emacs General Public License and later in the GNU General Public License (GPL) in 1989.
