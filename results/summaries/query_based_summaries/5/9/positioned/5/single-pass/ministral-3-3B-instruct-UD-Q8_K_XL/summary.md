Richard Stallman, born March 16, 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement. A physicist by training, he became disillusioned with proprietary software and its restrictive licensing practices, which he saw as limiting users' freedom to study, modify, and share software. In 1983, Stallman founded the GNU project, aiming to create a free operating system compatible with Unix. He introduced the concept of copyleft, a legal mechanism to ensure that free software remains free, and developed the GNU General Public License (GPL), which became a cornerstone of the free software movement.

Stallman established the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 1985 to support free software development and advocate for software freedom. He is known for his advocacy against software patents, digital rights management (DRM), and corporate control over software. Stallman’s philosophy emphasizes the importance of user freedom and the ethical responsibility of software developers to ensure that their work is accessible and usable by all. His contributions have significantly shaped the modern landscape of open-source and free software, influencing how software is developed, distributed, and perceived globally.

Stallman’s activism has led to numerous controversies, including debates over his views on child pornography and his personal relationships, which have occasionally overshadowed his significant contributions to technology and ethics. Despite these challenges, his impact on promoting transparency, collaboration, and ethical software development remains profound.
