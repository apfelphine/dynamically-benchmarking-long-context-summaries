Richard Stallman is a computer scientist and software freedom advocate born in 1953 in New York City. He earned a bachelor's degree in physics from Harvard University and later worked at MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, where he developed software like Emacs and contributed to the field of artificial intelligence. In 1983, he announced the GNU project, a free software initiative aimed at creating a free operating system compatible with Unix. He founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to support the movement and introduced the concept of copyleft to protect users' freedoms to modify and redistribute software. Stallman's work led to the creation of essential tools such as GNU Emacs, GCC, and GNU make. He is a strong proponent of free software and has advocated against software patents, digital rights management (DRM), and proprietary software, promoting instead a "reduced copyright" model. He has been a vocal critic of government and corporate surveillance and has supported open-source and free software principles, including the use of free operating systems in public institutions. Stallman has faced controversy over his past writings on child pornography and has been involved in public debates, including resigning from the FSF and MIT in 2019 due to comments on the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, later returning to the FSF board in 2021. He lives in Boston, speaks multiple languages, and is an atheist of Jewish ancestry. Stallman has received numerous honors, including the MacArthur Fellowship and the Electronic Frontier Foundation's Pioneer Award, and has written extensively on software freedom and philosophy. He is recognized for his foundational role in the free software movement and his ongoing efforts to promote user freedom and ethical technology.
