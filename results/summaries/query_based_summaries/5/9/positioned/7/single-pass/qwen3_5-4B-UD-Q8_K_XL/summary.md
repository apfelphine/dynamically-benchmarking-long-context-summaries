# Richard Stallman

**Early Life and Education**
Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City to a family of Jewish heritage. He developed an early interest in computers, reading manuals for the IBM 7094 as a pre-teen. He attended a Columbia University Saturday program from 1967 to 1969 and served as a volunteer laboratory assistant at Rockefeller University. His first professional computer experience occurred in 1970 as a high school summer worker at the IBM New York Scientific Center, where he wrote numerical analysis programs in Fortran and a text editor in APL. As a first-year student at Harvard University in 1970, he excelled in Math 55 and later enrolled as a graduate student at MIT, where he pursued a doctorate in physics for one year before focusing on programming at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory.

**Programming and MIT**
Working at the MIT AI Laboratory starting in 1975, Stallman collaborated with Gerry Sussman to publish a 1977 paper on an AI truth maintenance system called dependency-directed backtracking, a technique still considered the most general form of intelligent backtracking. He contributed to software projects including TECO, Emacs, and the Lisp machine operating system (CONS and CADR). He became an ardent critic of restricted computer access in the lab, famously sending decrypted messages to users when a password control system was installed in 1977.

**The GNU Project and Free Software Movement**
In 1980, after being refused access to the source code of a new Xerox laser printer, Stallman founded the GNU project in February 1984. He described the system as "GNU's Not Unix" and created a nonprofit corporation, the Free Software Foundation (FSF), in 1985. Stallman popularized the concept of copyleft and released the GNU General Public License (GPL) in 1989. Key contributions to the GNU system include GNU Emacs, the GCC compiler, the GNU Debugger, and GNU make. In 1991, Linus Torvalds created the Linux kernel using GNU tools, forming the GNU/Linux operating system. Stallman remains a visiting scientist at MIT and continues to lead the GNU project.

**Activism and Philosophy**
Stallman is an outspoken political campaigner for the free software movement, advocating that users should have the freedom to share, study, and modify software. He rejects the term "open source," arguing that "free software" is a political movement emphasizing freedom rather than just a development model. He also rejects the term "intellectual property," distinguishing between copyright, patents, and trademarks. His activism extends to protesting digital rights management (DRM), software patents, and proprietary software, leading to the creation of the Virtual Richard M. Stallman (vrms) tool.

**Controversies and Recent Years**
Stallman faced significant controversy in 2019 regarding comments made about Jeffrey Epstein and Marvin Minsky, which led to his resignation from both MIT and the FSF in September 2019. He later returned to the FSF board in March 2021 after an open letter requesting his removal was published. In September 2023, Stallman revealed he had been diagnosed with follicular lymphoma but stated his prognosis was good and he was in remission. He is an atheist of Jewish ancestry, speaks English, French, Spanish, and some Indonesian, and uses free software exclusively, including a ThinkPad X200 running Trisquel GNU/Linux and Libreboot.
