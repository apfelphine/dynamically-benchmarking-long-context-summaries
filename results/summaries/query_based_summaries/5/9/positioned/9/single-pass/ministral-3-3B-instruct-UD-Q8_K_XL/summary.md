Richard Stallman, born March 16, 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement. A computer scientist, he is best known for founding the GNU Project in 1983, aiming to create a free and open-source alternative to proprietary operating systems like Unix. Stallman’s philosophy centers on the principle of software freedom, advocating for users' rights to freely share, study, modify, and distribute software. He developed foundational tools such as GNU Emacs, the GNU Compiler Collection (GCC), and the GNU General Public License (GPL), which became cornerstones of the open-source community.

Stallman’s activism has been instrumental in promoting ethical software practices, challenging restrictive licensing models, and fostering collaboration among developers. His advocacy led to the establishment of the Free Software Foundation (FSF), a nonprofit organization dedicated to supporting free software. Despite controversies, including debates over terminology (e.g., "free software" vs. "open source") and ethical concerns, Stallman’s legacy remains central to the global movement for digital freedom and innovation.
