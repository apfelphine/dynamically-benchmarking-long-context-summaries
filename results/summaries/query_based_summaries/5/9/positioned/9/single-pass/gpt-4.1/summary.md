## Richard Stallman: Summary

Richard Stallman, born in 1953 in New York City, is a prominent figure in computer science and a leading advocate for software freedom. After excelling in mathematics and physics at Harvard University, he joined MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory in 1971, quickly becoming an influential hacker within the community. Stallman contributed to several important software projects at MIT, notably TECO, Emacs, and the Lisp machine operating system. His experiences at MIT, including the loss of open access to source code and his frustrations with proprietary software, spurred his commitment to software freedoms.

Stallman founded the GNU Project in 1983 to create a free Unix-compatible operating system and established the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 1985 to promote and support free software development. He introduced the concept of copyleft, which protects users' rights to modify and redistribute software, and authored the widely used GNU General Public License (GPL). Stallman developed key software tools for GNU, such as GNU Emacs, GCC, and GNU Debugger. He continues to be a vocal activist for digital freedom, influencing the culture of hacker communities, software licensing, and terminology related to free software. Stallman's advocacy and uncompromising stance have made him a controversial but highly influential figure, recognized with numerous awards and honors for his contributions to computing and free software.
