Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City. He developed a strong interest in computers early in life, first using the IBM 7094 manuals at a summer camp, and later working as a programmer at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Stallman became increasingly critical of the industry trend of proprietary software, which limited copying and redistribution via restrictive licenses. He argued that blocking user freedom was a "crime against humanity." This conviction led him to announce the GNU project in September 1983 to create a free operating system compatible with Unix. In 1985, he published the GNU Manifesto and founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to legally support the free software movement. Stallman popularized the concept of copyleft, which was first implemented in the GNU Emacs General Public License, and he is responsible for contributing vital tools like GNU Emacs and GNU Make to the project.
