Richard Stallman, born March 16, 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement. He is best known for founding the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** in 1985 and developing the **GNU Project**, which aimed to create a free and open-source operating system compatible with Unix. Stallman’s philosophy centers on the **four freedoms** of software: the freedom to run the program for any purpose, to study and modify it, to redistribute copies, and to distribute modified versions. He introduced the concept of **copyleft**, a legal mechanism to ensure that software remains free, and co-authored the **GNU General Public License (GPL)**, which became a cornerstone of open-source software.

Stallman’s activism extends beyond software, advocating for **user freedom, privacy, and ethical technology use**. He has been vocal in opposing proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and surveillance, often speaking out against corporate control over technology. His work has significantly influenced the development of open-source movements worldwide, shaping modern computing culture by emphasizing collaboration, transparency, and the democratization of technology. Stallman’s contributions have made him a global advocate for **digital rights and a free and open internet**.
