Richard Stallman is a pioneering figure in the free software movement, born in 1953 in New York City. He is best known for founding the **GNU Project** in 1983, aimed at creating a free and open-source operating system compatible with Unix. Stallman’s philosophy centers on the principle of **software freedom**, advocating for users' rights to freely share, study, modify, and distribute software.

Stallman established the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** in 1985 to support and promote free software, introducing the concept of **copyleft**—a legal mechanism to protect the rights of software users. He developed foundational tools like **GNU Emacs**, the **GNU Compiler Collection (GCC)**, and **GNU Make**, which became essential components of the GNU system. Stallman’s advocacy for open-source principles has significantly influenced modern computing, challenging proprietary software practices and fostering a culture of collaborative innovation.

Beyond software, Stallman has been a vocal activist for **digital rights, privacy, and ethical technology use**. He has campaigned against **copyright restrictions, digital rights management (DRM), and surveillance**, often advocating for civil disobedience to protect users' freedoms. His work has inspired movements like **open-source software, digital privacy advocacy, and ethical technology design**, leaving a lasting impact on both the technical and social spheres.
