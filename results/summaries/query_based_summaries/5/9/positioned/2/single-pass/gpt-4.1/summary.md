## Richard Stallman: Key Facts and Achievements

Richard Stallman, born in New York City in 1953, demonstrated an early interest in computers and excelled academically, attending Harvard University and later working at MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. Stallman became deeply involved in the hacker culture, contributing to projects such as Emacs and the Lisp machine operating system. In the early 1980s, reacting to shifting trends toward proprietary software and restricted access to source code, Stallman became an advocate for software freedom, emphasizing the ethical and social necessity of user rights to share, study, and modify software. In 1983, he launched the GNU (GNU's Not Unix) project to develop a completely free operating system compatible with Unix, later founding the Free Software Foundation (FSF) and introducing concepts like copyleft and the GNU General Public License (GPL).

Stallman made major technical contributions as the main author of GNU Emacs, GCC (GNU Compiler Collection), GNU Debugger, and other core GNU tools. He is known for promoting strict definitions and terminology, emphasizing the importance of "free software" over "open source" and advocating for the acknowledgment of the GNU project's role in the widely used GNU/Linux operating system. Stallman has been an outspoken activist, writing essays, speaking globally, and campaigning on issues including software patents, DRM, copyrights, surveillance, and user privacy. He has received numerous awards and honorary doctorates for his work and is recognized as a principal founder of the free software movement. Despite controversies, Stallman remains a pivotal figure in computing, dedicated to user empowerment and software freedom.
