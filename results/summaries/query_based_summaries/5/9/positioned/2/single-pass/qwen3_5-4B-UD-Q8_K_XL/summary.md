# Richard Stallman

Richard Stallman, born in 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement, best known for founding the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation (FSF). He began his career as a programmer at MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory in the 1970s, where he developed essential tools like Emacs and GCC while advocating for unrestricted access to software source code. His philosophy, summarized by the motto "free software," asserts that software users should have the freedom to run, study, modify, and distribute programs, arguing that these freedoms are a moral necessity for societal progress rather than merely a pragmatic technical advantage. In 1983, Stallman quit his academic position to work full-time on the GNU Project, which aimed to create a complete, free operating system compatible with Unix.

Stallman's activism has extended beyond software development to include campaigns against proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and restrictive patents. He famously promoted the term "free software" over "open source" to emphasize the political and ethical dimensions of user freedom. Despite facing significant personal and professional challenges, including public controversy regarding his views on the Jeffrey Epstein case and subsequent resignation from MIT and the FSF in 2019, Stallman returned to lead the FSF in 2021. He remains a vocal advocate for privacy, user sovereignty, and the preservation of intellectual freedom in the digital age, influencing fields ranging from computer science to economics and education.
