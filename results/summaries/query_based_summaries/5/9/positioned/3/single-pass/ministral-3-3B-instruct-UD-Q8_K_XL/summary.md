Richard Stallman, born in 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement. A computer scientist and activist, he is best known for founding the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** in 1985 and the **GNU project**, which aimed to create a free and open-source operating system compatible with Unix. Stallman championed the concept of **copyleft**, a legal mechanism to protect the rights of users to modify and redistribute software freely, and introduced the **GNU General Public License (GPL)**, a foundational license for open-source software.

Stallman’s philosophy centers on **software freedom**, emphasizing that users should have the right to access, modify, and share software without restrictions. He has been a vocal advocate against proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and corporate control over technology. His work has significantly influenced the development of modern computing, including the rise of open-source software and the broader movement toward digital freedom.

Beyond his technical contributions, Stallman has been a prominent activist, speaking out on issues like **copyright reduction, surveillance resistance, and the ethical implications of technology**. His advocacy has led to the creation of numerous open-source initiatives and influenced public policy discussions around digital rights. Despite controversies, including debates over his views on child pornography and his personal struggles, Stallman remains a defining figure in the history of computing and free culture.
