## Richard Stallman: Concise Summary

Richard Stallman is a pioneering computer programmer and advocate for software freedom, born in 1953 in New York City. Early in his life, Stallman showed a strong aptitude for mathematics, science, and programming; he attended Harvard University, and soon became a prominent figure in the hacker community at MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. Stallman contributed to several influential software projects including TECO, Emacs, and the Lisp machine operating systems. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, as proprietary software began to dominate, Stallman became a strong critic of restrictions on software sharing and user freedoms.

Stallman launched the GNU Project in 1983 with the goal of creating a completely free Unix-like operating system, and founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 1985 to support these efforts. He popularized the concept of "copyleft," creating the GNU General Public License (GPL) to ensure users' rights to modify and share software. He has been an active campaigner for software freedom, digital rights, and user privacy, publishing numerous essays and giving frequent lectures. Stallman is known for his strong views on terminology—promoting the use of "free software" and "GNU/Linux"—and his uncompromising commitment to ethical software development. Despite several controversies and resignations, Stallman continues to be recognized for his substantial impact on computing, receiving numerous awards and honorary doctorates worldwide for his pioneering work.
