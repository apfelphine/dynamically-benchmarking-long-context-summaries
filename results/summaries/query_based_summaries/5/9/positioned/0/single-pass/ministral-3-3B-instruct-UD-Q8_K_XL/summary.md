Richard Stallman, often referred to as RMS, was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City, and is best known as the founder of the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** and the **GNU Project**. His early fascination with computers led him to attend Harvard University, where he excelled in mathematics and physics. In 1971, he joined the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, where he became a prominent figure in the hacker community.

Stallman’s defining contributions include his advocacy for **software freedom**, which he articulated through the **GNU General Public License (GPL)**, a copyleft license designed to ensure that software remains free and open-source. He envisioned the GNU project as a free operating system compatible with Unix, aiming to provide users with the freedom to modify, distribute, and use software without restrictions. His work laid the foundation for modern open-source movements, emphasizing ethical and democratic access to technology.

Stallman’s philosophy was deeply rooted in his belief that software should be free from proprietary control, promoting transparency, collaboration, and innovation. His early battles against restrictive software licenses and his resistance to corporate control of technology set him apart. Though he resigned from MIT and the FSF in 2019 amid controversies, his influence persists in the global open-source community, where his ideas continue to shape debates on intellectual property, digital rights, and the ethical use of technology. His legacy is marked by his relentless pursuit of **freedom in computing** and his enduring commitment to democratizing technology.
