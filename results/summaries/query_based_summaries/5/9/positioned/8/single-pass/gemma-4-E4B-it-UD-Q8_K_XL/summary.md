Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City. He developed an early interest in computers, first working on numerical analysis and a text editor in APL during his high school years and later at the IBM New York Scientific Center.

At MIT's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, Stallman gained notoriety as a hacker and was involved in software projects like TECO and Emacs. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, he became increasingly critical of proprietary software and restrictive licensing, leading him to proclaim that restricting software use was "a crime against humanity." This conviction fueled the creation of the GNU project in 1983. He co-founded the Free Software Foundation to legally support the free software movement, popularizing the concept of copyleft and releasing the GNU General Public License (GPL) in 1989. Stallman's activism has consistently focused on user freedom, which he views as a moral value, and he advocates for "reduced copyright."
