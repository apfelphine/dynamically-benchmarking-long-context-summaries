## Richard Stallman: Summary

Richard Stallman, born in 1953 in New York City, is a prominent computer programmer and activist who initiated the GNU Project in September 1983 with the goal of creating a Unix-compatible free operating system. He published the influential GNU Manifesto in 1985, founding the Free Software Foundation (FSF) shortly thereafter to support free software development and advocacy. Stallman contributed key software tools including GNU Emacs, GCC, GNU Debugger, and GNU make, and pioneered the concept of copyleft, first implemented in the GNU Emacs General Public License and later in the widely used GNU General Public License (GPL). He has been central to the free software movement, insisting on the term "free software" to emphasize user freedom, and has advocated for using the term "GNU/Linux" rather than just "Linux" to recognize GNU’s contributions.

Throughout his career, Stallman has written extensively and campaigned for user rights, digital privacy, and opposition to digital rights management (DRM) and proprietary software. He initiated the use of clear terminologies in the free software discourse and has remained a steadfast political activist for software freedom, inspiring projects like vrms and public campaigns such as Defective by Design. Stallman’s tenure has involved numerous honors and some controversies, including his resignation from MIT and the FSF in 2019, but he has continued to influence the free software community and returned to the FSF board in 2021. He remains actively involved despite health challenges and has received multiple awards recognizing his technical and social contributions.
