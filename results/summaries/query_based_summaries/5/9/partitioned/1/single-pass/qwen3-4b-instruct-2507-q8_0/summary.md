Richard Stallman is a computer scientist and software activist who founded the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to promote software freedom. He developed key free software tools like GNU Emacs, GCC, and GNU Make, and popularized the concept of copyleft to protect users' rights to modify and redistribute software. Stallman advocates for a free software movement emphasizing user freedom, opposing proprietary software, and promoting open-source principles. He has been a vocal critic of software patents, digital rights management, and corporate control over technology. He has also contributed to discussions on software ethics, including opposing restrictive practices and advocating for reduced copyright. Stallman has faced controversy over past writings on child pornography and pedophilia, leading to his resignation from MIT and the FSF in 2019, followed by a return to the FSF board in 2021. He lives in Boston, identifies as an atheist of Jewish ancestry, and is known for his strong commitment to free software, open knowledge, and user empowerment.
