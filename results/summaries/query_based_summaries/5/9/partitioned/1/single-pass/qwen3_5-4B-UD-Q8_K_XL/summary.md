Richard Stallman is a computer scientist and activist best known as the founder of the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation (FSF). He established the concept of "free software," arguing that users should have the freedom to study, modify, and redistribute software, a philosophy he codified in the GNU General Public License (GPL) and popularized through the copyleft mechanism. Stallman's technical contributions include developing essential tools such as GNU Emacs, GCC, and GNU Make, though he noted the absence of a kernel, which was later provided by the Linux kernel. He is a staunch advocate for the terms "free software" and "GNU/Linux," distinguishing them from "open source" and "proprietary software," and has consistently fought against restrictive licensing, digital rights management, and intellectual property laws that he views as threats to user freedom.

Stallman's activism extends beyond software to include political campaigning, public education, and direct confrontation with corporations and governments. He has advocated for copyright reform, supported the creation of the Virtual Richard M. Stallman (vrms) to identify non-free software on Debian systems, and engaged in various international efforts to promote free software, including influencing Indian state schools to discard proprietary software. His career is marked by significant personal controversies, particularly regarding his comments about Jeffrey Epstein and Marvin Minsky in 2019, which led to calls for his resignation from both MIT and the FSF; he subsequently resigned in 2019 but returned to the FSF board in 2021. Despite ongoing criticism, Stallman remains a central figure in the free software movement, maintaining his commitment to user freedom and publishing extensively on the subject.
