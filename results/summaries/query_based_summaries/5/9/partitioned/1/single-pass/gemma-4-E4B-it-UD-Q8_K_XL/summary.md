Richard Stallman announced the GNU operating system plan in September 1983 and published the GNU Manifesto in 1985, outlining his motivation for creating a free operating system compatible with Unix. He subsequently founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF), serving as its nonsalaried president. Stallman popularized copyleft, a legal mechanism to protect free software modification and redistribution rights, which was first implemented in the GNU Emacs General Public License, followed by the GNU General Public License (GPL) in 1989.

Stallman contributed essential GNU tools, including GNU Emacs, the GCC compiler, GNU Debugger, and GNU make. He has been an outspoken campaigner for the free software movement since the early 1990s, advocating for free software and criticizing proprietary software and digital rights management (DRM). He strongly advocates for the use of the term GNU/Linux to denote the operating system created by combining the GNU system with the Linux kernel, arguing that omitting "GNU" diminishes the free software philosophy.
