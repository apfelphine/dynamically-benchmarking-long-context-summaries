Richard Stallman, born in 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement. He founded the **GNU Project** in 1983, aiming to create a free operating system compatible with Unix. Stallman developed foundational tools like **GNU Emacs**, the **GNU Compiler Collection (GCC)**, and **GNU Make**, which became essential components of modern computing. His advocacy for **copyleft licensing**, exemplified by the **GNU General Public License (GPL)**, ensures that software remains free and open-source, preventing proprietary control over essential tools.

Stallman’s philosophy centers on **software freedom**, emphasizing users' rights to modify, distribute, and study software. He has been a vocal critic of **digital rights management (DRM)**, advocating for reduced copyright restrictions and ethical treatment of users. His work has inspired movements promoting transparency, innovation, and ethical technology use. Stallman remains a central figure in the free software community, despite controversies over his past statements on underage sex and child pornography, which he later clarified and addressed. His contributions have shaped the ethical and technical landscape of computing.
