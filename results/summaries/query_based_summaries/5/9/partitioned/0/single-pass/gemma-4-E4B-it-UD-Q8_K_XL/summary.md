Richard Stallman announced the plan for the GNU operating system in September 1983, which he created as a free and Unix-compatible system. He subsequently published the GNU Manifesto in 1985, detailing his motivation for creating a free operating system. He founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to support the free software movement, becoming its nonsalaried president.

Stallman popularized the concept of copyleft, first implemented in the GNU Emacs General Public License, and later in the program-independent GNU General Public License (GPL). He was responsible for developing numerous necessary tools for the GNU project, including GNU Emacs, GCC, GNU Debugger, and GNU make. He advocates for free software as a political movement, distinguishing it from the "open source" development model. Stallman also campaigned against proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and government/corporate surveillance, refusing to use cell phones due to tracking concerns.
