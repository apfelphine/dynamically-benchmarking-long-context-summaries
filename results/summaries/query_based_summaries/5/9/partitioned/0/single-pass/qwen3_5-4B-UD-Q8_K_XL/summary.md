Richard Stallman is a computer scientist, mathematician, and the founder of the Free Software Movement. Born in 1953, he studied at Harvard and MIT, where he became a prominent hacker known by his initials, RMS. He left MIT in 1984 to dedicate himself full-time to the GNU Project, a free software initiative designed to create a complete, compatible operating system. Stallman established the Free Software Foundation (FSF) and championed the concept of "free software," arguing that users should have the freedom to run, study, modify, and distribute software. He developed the GNU General Public License (GPL) and tools like Emacs and GCC to support this vision, often emphasizing the moral imperative of freedom over pragmatic arguments.

Stallman has been a persistent activist against proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and software patents, frequently boycotting companies like Microsoft, Adobe, and AMD. He advocates for a "reduced copyright" model and has faced significant personal and professional challenges, including controversies regarding his comments on the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, which led to his resignation from MIT and the FSF in 2019 before his eventual return. Despite these setbacks, he remains a central figure in the open-source community, known for his uncompromising stance on software freedom and his lifelong commitment to the GNU/Linux operating system.
