# Summary: Richard Stallman

Richard Stallman, born in 1953, is a pivotal figure in the development of free software and the GNU project. He announced the GNU operating system plan in 1983, aiming for a Unix-compatible free OS, and published the influential GNU Manifesto in 1985. Stallman founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to support free software development and introduced the concept of copyleft, notably implemented in the GNU General Public License (GPL). He personally contributed key tools such as GNU Emacs, GCC, and GDB. While the GNU kernel project (GNU Hurd) remains incomplete, the Linux kernel—developed by Linus Torvalds—combined with GNU tools forms the widely used GNU/Linux operating system, a naming distinction Stallman strongly advocates.

Beyond software development, Stallman is an active political campaigner for software freedom and digital rights, promoting open access, reduced copyright terms, and opposing digital rights management. He insists on precise terminology to emphasize freedom, rejecting terms like “intellectual property” and “open source” in favor of “free software” and “GNU/Linux.” Over the years, Stallman’s uncompromising stance and controversial statements, notably his 2019 comments about the Jeffrey Epstein scandal and related topics, have sparked significant backlash leading to his resignation from MIT and the FSF. Despite this, he returned to the FSF board in 2021 amid divided community opinions. Stallman lives in Boston, identifies as an atheist, and is recognized with numerous awards including the ACM Software System Award and membership in the US National Academy of Engineering.
