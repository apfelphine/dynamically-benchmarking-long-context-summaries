Richard Stallman, born March 16, 1953, is a pioneering figure in the free software movement, best known for founding the **GNU Project** in 1983. His mission is to create a free operating system, **GNU**, compatible with Unix, and to promote the principles of **software freedom**—allowing users to freely use, study, share, and modify software. Stallman established the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)**, a nonprofit organization dedicated to supporting free software, and popularized the concept of **copyleft**, a legal mechanism to ensure that free software remains free. He developed foundational tools like **GNU Emacs**, the **GNU Compiler Collection (GCC)**, and **GNU Make**, which became essential components of modern computing.

Stallman’s advocacy extends beyond technical contributions. He has been a vocal critic of **copyright restrictions**, **digital rights management (DRM)**, and **proprietary software**, arguing that these practices limit users' freedom. His stance on **free software** has sparked significant debate, with some accusing him of promoting extremist views, particularly regarding child pornography and pedophilia, though he later acknowledged the harm of such acts. Despite controversies, Stallman remains a central figure in the free software movement, advocating for ethical and democratic access to technology.

His work has influenced the broader tech community, inspiring the **open-source movement** and challenging the dominance of proprietary software. Stallman’s philosophy emphasizes **user autonomy**, **collaboration**, and **scientific integrity**, making him a defining voice in the fight for open and accessible technology.
