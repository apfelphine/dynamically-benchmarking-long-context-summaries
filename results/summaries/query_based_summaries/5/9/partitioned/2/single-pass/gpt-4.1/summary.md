## Summary: Richard Stallman

Richard Stallman is a prominent figure in the world of software freedom and digital rights. Born in 1953 in New York City, Stallman showed early interest and talent in mathematics and programming, leading him to study at Harvard University and later work at MIT’s Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. Throughout his career, Stallman became known for his contributions to several foundational software projects, including EMACS and GCC, and is credited with pioneering concepts such as copyleft and the General Public License (GPL). In 1983, he initiated the GNU project to develop a free, Unix-compatible operating system, which catalyzed the broader free software movement and later resulted in the formation of the Free Software Foundation (FSF).

Stallman is equally notable for his activism, which includes advocating for reduced copyright terms, resisting digital rights management (DRM), fighting against user data surveillance, and emphasizing the crucial role of terminology in software (preferring "free software" and "GNU/Linux" over "open source" and "Linux"). His uncompromising stance has led to both significant influence and controversy, including criticism over comments made regarding sensitive topics and periods of resignation and return to leadership roles. Stallman has received numerous awards and honorary titles in recognition of his technical and social contributions but remains a divisive figure due to his outspoken views and personal conduct. He continues to reside in Boston, writes essays and music, and, despite health challenges in recent years, remains active in promoting software freedom.
