Richard Stallman is a software freedom activist and the founder of the Free Software Movement. He initiated the GNU project in 1983 to create a free, open-source operating system compatible with Unix, emphasizing user freedom to study, modify, and redistribute software. Stallman developed key GNU tools like Emacs, GCC, and GNU Make, and championed the copyleft licensing model, first implemented in the GNU General Public License (GPL). He is a vocal critic of proprietary software, digital rights management (DRM), and software patents, advocating for a "reduced copyright" and opposing practices that restrict sharing. He promotes open-source terminology, such as "free software" and "free software," over "open source," to emphasize freedom over development models. Stallman has supported initiatives like the International Music Score Library Project and opposed the use of DRM in e-books, arguing they limit access and usability. He is known for his activism, including protests against software patents and a public stance on the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, which led to his resignation from MIT and the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 2019. He later returned to the FSF board in 2021. Stallman lives in Boston, speaks multiple languages, and identifies as an atheist of Jewish ancestry. He has received numerous awards, including the MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship and the Grace Murray Hopper Award, and is recognized for his contributions to computing and software freedom.
