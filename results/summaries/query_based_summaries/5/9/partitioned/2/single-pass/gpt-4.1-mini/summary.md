# Summary: Richard Stallman

Richard Stallman, born in 1953 to a Jewish family in New York City, is a seminal figure in the free software movement. Interested in computers from a young age, he excelled in mathematics and physics, studied at Harvard University (earning a bachelor's in physics in 1974), and worked at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. There, he was an active hacker and programmer, contributing to projects like TECO and Emacs, while opposing restrictions such as password protections that limited software freedom. Frustrated by the rise of proprietary software and licensing restrictions in the late 1970s and early 1980s, Stallman proposed a vision of software freedom that emphasized the users' right to study, modify, share, and redistribute software.

In 1983, Stallman announced the GNU Project, aiming to create a free Unix-compatible operating system, and in 1985, he published the GNU Manifesto outlining this vision. He founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) as a non-profit to support this movement. Stallman contributed critical GNU tools including the GNU Emacs editor, GCC compiler, GNU Debugger, and GNU Make build system; however, the kernel component lacked maturity until the Linux kernel—developed independently—combined with GNU tools to form a widely used operating system often called GNU/Linux, a terminology Stallman advocates for to emphasize GNU’s role and philosophy. He popularized the concept of copyleft through the GNU General Public License to protect software freedoms.

Stallman's activism extended beyond software to broader digital rights, opposing restrictive copyright terms and DRM, advocating for reduced copyright durations, and encouraging civil disobedience against laws harmful to user freedoms. He insists on precise use of terminology, promoting "free software" over "open source," and "GNU/Linux" for the OS incorporating GNU components and the Linux kernel. He has voiced strong concerns about surveillance, refusing to use conventional mobile phones and employing privacy-respecting tools.

In 2019, Stallman became embroiled in controversy after comments related to the Jeffrey Epstein scandal and past writings on sensitive topics, leading to his resignation from MIT and the FSF. He returned to the FSF board in 2021 amid mixed reactions and criticism from several organizations. Stallman lives in Boston, speaks multiple languages, identifies as an atheist of Jewish ancestry, and continues to lead and influence free software advocacy. He has received numerous honors, including the MacArthur Fellowship and membership in the US National Academy of Engineering.
