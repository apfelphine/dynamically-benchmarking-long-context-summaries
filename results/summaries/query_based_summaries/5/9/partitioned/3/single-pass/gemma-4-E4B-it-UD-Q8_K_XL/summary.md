Richard Stallman was born on March 16, 1953, in New York City, and is a vocal political campaigner for the free software movement. In 1983, he announced the plan for the GNU operating system, aiming to create a free operating system compatible with Unix. He subsequently founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to support free software programmers and establish a legal framework for the movement. Stallman is a proponent of "reduced copyright," suggesting a 10-year copyright limit, and advocates that no form of non-commercial sharing of copies should be considered a copyright violation.

Stallman strongly emphasizes correct terminology, urging the use of "free software" and "GNU/Linux" while opposing the terms "intellectual property" and "piracy." He views "free software as a political movement," differentiating it from the "open source" development model. Stallman has been a consistent advocate for user privacy and surveillance resistance, refusing to own mobile phones due to the lack of entirely free software options, and he has spoken out against proprietary software vendors and digital rights management (DRM).
