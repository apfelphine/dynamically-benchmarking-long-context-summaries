Richard Stallman is a computer scientist and software freedom activist who founded the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation (FSF) to promote free and open-source software. He developed key tools like GNU Emacs, GCC, and GNU Make, and championed the GNU General Public License (GPL) to ensure software freedom. Stallman has been a vocal critic of proprietary software, digital rights management, and software patents, advocating for user freedom and ethical computing practices. He has faced controversy over past writings on child pornography and pedophilia, which he later retracted, and has been involved in public debates over terminology, such as insisting on "free software" over "open source." Despite criticism and a 2019 resignation from the FSF and MIT due to misunderstandings, he remains central to the GNU project. Stallman is also known for his activism, including promoting free software in schools and opposing software patents, and for using free software in his personal computing, emphasizing freedom over convenience.
