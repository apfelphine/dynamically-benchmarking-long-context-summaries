## Alfred North Whitehead Summary

Whitehead fundamentally rejected the notion of separate and unchanging matter as the basis of reality, advocating instead for a process ontology where reality consists of interrelated, dynamic "becoming." He considered all basic elements of reality to be experiential, meaning everything is constituted by its experience, which he termed "prehension." He distinguished between causal efficacy (sensing environmental influence) and presentational immediacy (pure sense perception). In higher organisms, these modes merge into "symbolic reference," linking appearance with causation. Whitehead also posited that all things possess some measure of creativity, meaning there is no wholly inert matter.

His philosophy incorporates significant theological elements, viewing God not through traditional notions of a ruling king, but as a dynamic entity. God has a primordial nature—the unlimited possibility of the universe—and a consequent nature, which is God's reception of the world's activity, meaning God is changed by the universe. In education, Whitehead argued for a transdisciplinary model that emphasizes few important, interconnected concepts loaded with values, cautioning against "inert ideas," and stressing the vital role of imagination in illuminating facts.
