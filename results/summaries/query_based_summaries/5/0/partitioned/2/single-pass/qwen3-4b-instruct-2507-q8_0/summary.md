Alfred North Whitehead was a British mathematician and philosopher who made foundational contributions to mathematical logic through his co-authorship of *Principia Mathematica* with Bertrand Russell. He later developed a comprehensive metaphysical system known as process philosophy, which rejects the notion of static, unchanging matter in favor of a dynamic, interrelated world of events. Central to his philosophy is the idea that reality is composed of processes of "becoming" rather than static "being," with all entities experiencing and relating to one another. He introduced the concept of "prehension," a form of perception that applies to both conscious and unconscious entities, allowing them to incorporate aspects of the perceived into themselves. Whitehead emphasized that perception occurs in two modes: causal efficacy (physical prehension) and presentational immediacy (conceptual prehension), forming the basis of entities' constitution through their relations.

Whitehead viewed life as purposeful, driven by a desire to improve and adapt, and argued that complex organisms evolved not for survival alone but to actively modify their environments in pursuit of greater satisfaction. He posited that all entities possess some measure of freedom or creativity, leading to a panexperientialist view where even inanimate processes are experiential. His conception of God diverges from traditional theism, presenting God not as a distant, unmoving force but as a dynamic, relational being who "saves" the world by embracing and transforming individual experiences. God is both eternal and immanent, with the world and God fulfilling each other through a mutual process of becoming. This view, described as "the brief Galilean vision of humility," emphasizes love, tenderness, and responsiveness to the world's events, and is central to his process theology.

In education, Whitehead advocated for a transdisciplinary, values-laden approach, rejecting the teaching of "inert ideas" and promoting the integration of knowledge with real-life applications. He believed education should foster imagination and allow students to make meaningful connections across disciplines. His influence in theology, science, and education has been significant, particularly through American process theologians like Charles Hartshorne, John B. Cobb, and David Ray Griffin. His work has inspired developments in ecology, sustainability, and organizational theory, and his metaphysical insights continue to influence contemporary discussions in physics, biology, and philosophy. Despite initial resistance due to the difficulty and density of his writing, Whitehead's ideas have gained renewed appreciation for their relevance to modern scientific and philosophical challenges.
