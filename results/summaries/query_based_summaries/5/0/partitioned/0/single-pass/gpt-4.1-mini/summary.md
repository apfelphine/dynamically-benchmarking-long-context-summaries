## Summary on Alfred North Whitehead

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was an English mathematician, logician, and philosopher whose career transitioned from mathematics to profound philosophical work in metaphysics and education. Educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, he collaborated with Bertrand Russell on *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913), a foundational but complex work in mathematical logic. Later, Whitehead developed process philosophy, emphasizing a reality constituted by interrelated events and experiential processes rather than static matter, challenging Cartesian dualism and the materialist worldview. His metaphysics introduced the concept of "prehension" to describe a broad mode of perception applicable to all entities, living or not.

Whitehead's philosophical influence was most notable in progressive theology, especially process theology, where God is viewed as relational and affected by temporal events. Despite the difficulty of his writings and their initially limited acceptance, his ideas permeated areas including ecology, education, business administration, and physics, inspiring diverse interdisciplinary applications. His educational philosophy criticized rote learning and exam-driven curricula, advocating instead for imaginative, value-laden, and transdisciplinary education that fosters genuine understanding and application. Late in life, Whitehead served as a professor of philosophy at Harvard University, producing seminal works like *Process and Reality* (1929), which though challenging, remain influential among niche scholars in philosophy and theology.
