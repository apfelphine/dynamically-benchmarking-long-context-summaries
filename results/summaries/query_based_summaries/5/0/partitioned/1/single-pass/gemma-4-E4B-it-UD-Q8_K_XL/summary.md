**Alfred North Whitehead** advocated for a transdisciplinary approach to education, emphasizing the teaching of a few important concepts that students could organically link to diverse areas of knowledge, valuing wisdom and general principles over rote learning. He strongly criticized standardized examinations, arguing that they lead to "educational waste," and stressed the importance of imagination, stating that "knowledge does not keep any better than fish"—all knowledge must have imaginative application to students' lives to avoid being useless trivia.

In metaphysics, Whitehead rejected the idea of fundamentally independent, unchanging "bits of matter," favoring an event-based or "process" ontology where events are primary and fundamentally interrelated. He described reality as composed of dynamic "becoming" rather than static "being." He introduced the concept of "prehension" to describe perception as an incorporation of aspects of the perceived thing into itself, differentiating between causal efficacy and presentational immediacy. Whitehead's conception of God is not that of an imposing ruler but rather a tender, humble presence that receives and cherishes the world's activity, thus being changed by the universe's experiences.
