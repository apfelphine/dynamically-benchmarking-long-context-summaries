## Alfred North Whitehead: Summary

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was an influential British mathematician, philosopher, and educator. Initially renowned for his work in mathematics and logic, particularly as co-author (with Bertrand Russell) of *Principia Mathematica*, Whitehead later became a pioneering philosopher, especially in metaphysics and process philosophy. His conceptions rejected static notions of reality, favoring "process"—the idea that reality consists of interrelated events and dynamic "becoming" rather than unchanging matter. Whitehead developed unique theories on perception, proposing "prehension" as a mode of perceiving that applies to all entities, living or non-living, and introduced "symbolic reference" to explain how sense perception and causal relations combine in experience.

Whitehead wrote extensively on education, advocating imaginative, value-laden instruction instead of rote memorization. He argued for curricula shaped by the abilities and context of students, emphasizing creative thinking and the interconnectedness of knowledge. Later in life, Whitehead's philosophical work became foundational for process philosophy and theology, with enduring influence in areas such as American progressive theology, metaphysics, and educational theory. Despite the difficulty and density of his prose—which limited his wider influence—Whitehead’s ideas have shaped debates in philosophy, theology, science, and education, especially through followers at institutions like Harvard and Claremont.
