Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was a British philosopher and mathematician whose work significantly influenced process philosophy and metaphysics. His most notable contributions include *Process and Reality* (1929), which introduced a process-based ontology rejecting the traditional notion of static matter in favor of dynamic, interconnected events. Whitehead’s philosophy emphasizes the experiential nature of reality, where all entities are constituted by their relations and experiences, rejecting Cartesian dualism and materialism. His ideas also shaped process theology, particularly through his collaborator Charles Hartshorne, who developed process philosophy into a theological framework emphasizing God’s relational nature and the dynamic interaction between divine and finite existence.

Whitehead’s influence extended beyond philosophy into science, education, and ecology. His holistic metaphysics provided an alternative to mechanistic worldviews, particularly in environmental ethics, where his emphasis on interconnectedness resonated with ecological movements. In education, he advocated for imaginative, transdisciplinary learning over rote memorization, stressing the importance of values and holistic understanding. Though his work remains complex and often misunderstood, it has inspired diverse fields, from theology to artificial intelligence, and continues to be studied for its innovative approach to metaphysics and the nature of reality.
