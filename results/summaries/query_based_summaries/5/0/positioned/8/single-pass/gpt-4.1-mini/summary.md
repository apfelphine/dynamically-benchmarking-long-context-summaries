**Alfred North Whitehead**

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was a British mathematician, logician, and philosopher noted for his significant contributions across mathematics, logic, philosophy, and education. Educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, he excelled in mathematics before becoming a fellow and writing major mathematical works including *A Treatise on Universal Algebra* (1898) and, in collaboration with Bertrand Russell, the seminal *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913), which aimed to establish a logical foundation for all mathematics. Although initially focused on mathematics and physics, Whitehead later shifted toward philosophy, producing influential works such as *Science and the Modern World* (1925) and *Process and Reality* (1929). His philosophical system, often called process philosophy, posits an event-based ontology emphasizing interrelatedness, change, experience, and creativity as fundamental aspects of reality, contrasting with traditional materialistic views.

Whitehead's educational philosophy emphasized teaching interconnected conceptual understanding over inert facts, advocating for curriculum tailored to student needs and minimizing rigid exams. His metaphysical and religious ideas, including a novel conception of God as both primordial potential and consequent experience, have shaped process theology and inspired diverse fields including ecology, physics, and education. Despite the difficulty of his writings, Whitehead’s influence persists particularly in theology and some educational and ecological theories. The Whitehead Research Project continues critical editions of his works, highlighting his enduring intellectual legacy.
