Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was a British mathematician and philosopher who moved to the United States in 1924 to join the faculty at Harvard University, where he spent the remainder of his life. His most significant contributions include co-authoring *Principia Mathematica* with Bertrand Russell (1910–1913), a foundational work in mathematical logic, and publishing *Process and Reality* (1929), a seminal text in metaphysics that established "process philosophy." Whitehead argued against the Cartesian notion of static matter, proposing instead that reality is composed of dynamic, interrelated events or "occasions of experience" that are fundamentally creative and experiential.

Beyond his theoretical works, Whitehead was a prominent educational reformer who advocated against teaching "inert ideas," urging instead for a transdisciplinary curriculum focused on imagination and the application of knowledge to real life. His philosophy, which emphasized the interdependence of all entities and the active role of creativity in the universe, deeply influenced fields ranging from theology and ecology to computer science and business administration. While his own prose remains notoriously difficult, his ideas have found widespread application in modern discussions of sustainability, systems theory, and the relational nature of existence.
