Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was an English mathematician, logician, and philosopher who made significant contributions to mathematics, logic, and metaphysics. Born in Ramsgate, Kent, he studied at Sherborne School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he earned his B.A. in 1884 and became a Fellow of Trinity. He co-authored *Principia Mathematica* with Bertrand Russell, a foundational work in mathematical logic that aimed to formalize all of mathematics using symbolic logic. His work on universal algebra and his contributions to the foundations of mathematics are highly regarded.

After teaching at Cambridge, Whitehead moved to London and later to Harvard University in 1924, where he became a professor of philosophy. At Harvard, he produced his most influential philosophical work, *Process and Reality* (1929), which introduced a process-based metaphysics in which reality is fundamentally composed of dynamic, interrelated events rather than static matter. This "process philosophy" emphasized that all entities are in a state of becoming, experience, and relation, rejecting Cartesian dualism and the notion of irreducible matter. He argued that change and interdependence are primary, and that entities are constituted by their experiences and relations.

Whitehead's philosophy also addressed perception, proposing the concept of "prehension" as a way of understanding how entities incorporate aspects of the perceived world. He developed a theory of value, suggesting that life evolves toward the goal of increasing satisfaction and that all entities possess a degree of creativity and freedom. His views on God were distinct, portraying God not as a distant, unchanging ruler, but as a "fellow sufferer" who is deeply involved with and transformed by the world, embodying both a primordial potential and a consequent reality.

Whitehead's influence extends beyond philosophy into education, science, and theology. In education, he advocated for a curriculum centered on imagination, values, and transdisciplinary learning, rejecting the teaching of "inert ideas." His ideas on ecology, sustainability, and environmental ethics have inspired modern ecological thought, particularly in China, where his philosophy is integrated into efforts toward an "ecological civilization." His work has also influenced fields such as biology, physics, and organizational theory, with thinkers like Ilya Prigogine and David Bohm drawing on his metaphysical insights.

Though Whitehead's work is often considered difficult and obscure, it has had a lasting impact on 20th-century thought, particularly in process theology, where it has inspired a relational, dynamic view of God and the universe. His legacy endures through the Center for Process Studies and a growing interest in his ideas across diverse disciplines.
