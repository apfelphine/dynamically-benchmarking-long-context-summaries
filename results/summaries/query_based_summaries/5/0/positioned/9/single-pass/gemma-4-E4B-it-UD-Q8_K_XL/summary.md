**Alfred North Whitehead** was an English mathematician and philosopher, born in 1861. He began his career at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied mathematics, earned his B.A. in 1884, and became a fellow of the college. He taught mathematics and physics at Cambridge until 1910, during which time he collaborated with Bertrand Russell on *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913).

In 1924, Whitehead moved to the United States to teach philosophy at Harvard University, where he produced significant philosophical contributions, including *Science and the Modern World* (1925) and *Process and Reality* (1929). His philosophy, often termed "process philosophy," fundamentally rejected "scientific materialism," arguing instead for an event-based ontology where all entities are fundamentally interrelated processes. He developed the concept of "prehension" to describe perception, applicable to all entities, and viewed creativity as the absolute principle of existence.
