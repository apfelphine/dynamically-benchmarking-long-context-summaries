**Alfred North Whitehead**

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was an English mathematician, logician, and philosopher renowned for his profound contributions to mathematics and metaphysics. Educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, he worked extensively in mathematics, collaborating with Bertrand Russell on the landmark *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913), a foundational work in mathematical logic. Later turning to philosophy, Whitehead developed a process-oriented metaphysical system emphasizing reality as a series of interrelated and experiential events rather than static matter, known as "process philosophy." His significant philosophical works include *Science and the Modern World* (1925) and *Process and Reality* (1929). He held academic posts in the UK before joining Harvard University in 1924, where he influenced various fields including theology, education, and ecology.

Whitehead's philosophy challenged classical notions of materialism by positing that change, relations, and creativity are fundamental to existence. He introduced concepts such as "prehension" to describe perception beyond conscious beings and advanced a unique "dipolar" conception of God necessary for his metaphysics. His educational philosophy emphasized the avoidance of "inert ideas," advocating for imagination and transdisciplinary learning. Although his works are recognized as difficult, his process thought has influenced theology (notably process theology), ecology, business administration, and education theory, with growing interest especially in the United States and China.
