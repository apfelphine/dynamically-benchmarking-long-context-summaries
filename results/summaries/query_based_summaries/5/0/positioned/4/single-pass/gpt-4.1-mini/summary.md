## Summary: Alfred North Whitehead

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was an English mathematician, logician, and philosopher. Educated at Sherborne and Trinity College, Cambridge, he excelled in mathematics, co-authoring the influential *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913) with Bertrand Russell, which aimed to ground all mathematics in symbolic logic. He also authored *A Treatise on Universal Algebra* and *An Introduction to Mathematics*, contributing significantly to mathematical logic and the foundations of mathematics. Later in life, Whitehead shifted focus to philosophy, producing seminal works such as *Science and the Modern World* (1925) and *Process and Reality* (1929), where he developed process philosophy—a metaphysical view emphasizing events and relations over static material substances.

Whitehead held academic posts at Cambridge and Imperial College London before moving to Harvard University in 1924 to teach philosophy until his retirement in 1937. His process philosophy posits reality as a web of interrelated, experiential events ("occasions of experience") rather than enduring material objects, and it includes a unique conception of God as both primordial potentiality and consequent actuality, deeply involved with the world's processes. His work has influenced diverse fields—from education and ecology to theology and physics—and though complex and challenging, it remains impactful in specialized philosophical and theological circles, notably in process theology and ecological thought.
