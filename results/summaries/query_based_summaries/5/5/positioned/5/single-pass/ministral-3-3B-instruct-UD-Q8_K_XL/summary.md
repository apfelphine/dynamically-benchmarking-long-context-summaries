Philip Rubin is a prominent figure in the fields of speech technology, computational modeling, and cognitive science. Born in 1949, he earned his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut, focusing on speech production, perception, and the biological bases of language. Rubin’s research spans speech synthesis, articulatory modeling, and theoretical approaches to understanding embodied cognition, particularly in speech and language. His work includes developing foundational systems like ASY (an articulatory synthesizer) and SWS (a sinewave speech synthesis system), which were pivotal in advancing computational models of speech production and perception.

Rubin has made significant contributions to understanding how speech is generated and perceived through dynamic systems and action theory perspectives. His research emphasizes the importance of spatiotemporal coordination in speech, focusing on how physiological and acoustic signals interact. He also explored the integration of auditory and visual information in speech processing, contributing to innovations in audiovisual speech analysis and synthesis. Rubin’s theoretical contributions include biomechanical constraints on inverse mapping, which help in recovering vocal tract shapes from acoustic signals, and his work on the gestural-computational model of speech production.

Beyond academia, Rubin has played key roles in science policy and advocacy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and later as a senior advisor in the Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) under the Obama administration. Rubin has been involved in shaping federal policies related to human subjects research, neuroscience, and cognitive science. His advocacy has included efforts to modernize human subjects regulations, promote neuroscience research, and support interdisciplinary collaboration in science and technology.

Rubin has received numerous honors, including fellowships from the Acoustical Society of America, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the Association for Psychological Science. He has also been recognized for his leadership in behavioral and cognitive sciences, including his service as the President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). His work bridges theoretical insights with practical applications, making him a notable figure in the intersection of cognitive science, speech technology, and policy.
