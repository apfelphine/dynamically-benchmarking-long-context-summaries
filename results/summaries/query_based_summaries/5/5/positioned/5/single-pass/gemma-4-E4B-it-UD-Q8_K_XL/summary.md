Philip Rubin obtained his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971, followed by a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975, supervised by Michael Turvey, Ignatius Mattingly, Philip Lieberman, and Alvin Liberman.

His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, focusing heavily on the biological basis of speech and language. Rubin is notably recognized for his work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, and sinewave synthesis. At Haskins Laboratories, he designed prominent software systems like ASY and SWS, and developed the HADES signal processing system and SPIEL programming language. In addition to his scientific work, he has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy advocacy.
