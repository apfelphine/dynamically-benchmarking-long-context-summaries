### Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a distinguished psychologist and researcher best known for his pioneering work in speech technology, particularly articulatory synthesis and sinewave synthesis. He earned his BA from Brandeis University in 1971 and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975, where he studied under Michael Turvey. At Haskins Laboratories, Rubin was instrumental in developing ASY, a computational model for articulatory synthesis that introduced the concept of speech gestures, and SWS, the first system for automatic, large-scale sinewave synthesis. His theoretical contributions include a dynamical systems perspective on speech, emphasizing spatiotemporal coordination and embodied constraints, as well as innovations in audiovisual speech processing.

Beyond his research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) from 2000 to 2003 and later as a senior advisor in the NSF's Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences Directorate. In February 2012, he joined the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences, where he led the White House's neuroscience initiative. Rubin is also a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and has been recognized with numerous fellowships and awards for his contributions to psychology, speech science, and public service.
