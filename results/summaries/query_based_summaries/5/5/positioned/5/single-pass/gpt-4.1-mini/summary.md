## Summary of Information on Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an experimental psychologist and researcher known for his interdisciplinary work combining computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches, particularly related to speech production and perception. He is renowned for his foundational contributions to articulatory synthesis (modeling speech physiology and acoustics), speech perception, sinewave synthesis, and signal processing. Rubin developed influential software systems during his tenure at Haskins Laboratories, including ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES (signal processing), significantly advancing understanding of speech gestures and multimodal speech communication. His theoretical work emphasizes dynamical systems perspectives, embodiment, audiovisual integration, and the perceptual organization of speech signals.

In addition to his research, Rubin has held prominent leadership roles in science administration and policy. From 2000 to 2003, he served as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF), where he helped launch key programs in cognitive neuroscience and endangered language documentation. Later, as an Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) from 2012 to 2015, Rubin led the national neuroscience initiative and contributed to diverse science policy areas including open data, biomedical innovation, forensic science, and human subjects research ethics. He has also been actively involved in ethical oversight of research, advocacy for behavioral and brain sciences, and continues engagement with scientific and educational organizations. Rubin’s achievements have been recognized by numerous fellowships and awards, and he remains influential in both scientific research and science policy communities.
