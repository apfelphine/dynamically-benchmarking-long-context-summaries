## Summary of Information on Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a psychologist and linguist noted for his multidisciplinary research combining computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, most notably in the biological bases of speech and language. He earned his BA in 1971 from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology in 1975 from the University of Connecticut. Rubin is renowned for his foundational contributions to speech technology, particularly articulatory synthesis, speech perception, sinewave synthesis, signal processing, and modeling of temporal events. He developed key computational systems such as ASY, a program for articulatory synthesis, and SWS, for sinewave synthesis, which have been influential in studying speech production and perception.

Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy, including directing the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) from 2000 to 2003 and serving as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). His policy work has spanned neuroscience, ethics in research, behavioral science, and human subjects protections. He has been active in science advocacy, ethical oversight of emerging technologies, and advancing interdisciplinary speech and cognitive science research. Rubin also engages in community and institutional leadership, currently serving as President (and Past President in 2024) of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and Chair of the Board of Directors of Haskins Laboratories. His honors include fellowships in multiple scientific societies and distinguished service awards recognizing his leadership bridging research and policy.
