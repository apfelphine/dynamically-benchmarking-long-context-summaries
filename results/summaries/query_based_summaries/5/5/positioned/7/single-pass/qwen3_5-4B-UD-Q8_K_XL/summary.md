Philip Rubin is a prominent figure in the fields of experimental psychology, speech technology, and science policy. He earned his PhD in experimental psychology in 1975 from the University of Connecticut under Michael Turvey and is best known for his pioneering work on articulatory synthesis and speech perception. During his tenure at Haskins Laboratories, Rubin developed significant computational models, including ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and CASY (configurable articulatory synthesizer). These systems advanced the understanding of speech production by modeling the vocal tract as a set of coordinated physical gestures and events, influencing fields ranging from linguistics to artificial intelligence.

Beyond his research, Rubin has held high-level leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and later as the Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), where he led the White House's neuroscience initiative. His career also includes extensive involvement in science advocacy, such as chairing the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences and serving on various advisory committees regarding human subjects research and emerging technologies. Rubin is a Fellow of numerous prestigious organizations, including the American Association for the Advancement of Science and the American Psychological Association.
