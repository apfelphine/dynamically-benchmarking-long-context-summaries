## Summary of Information on Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a psychologist and linguist whose research integrates computational, engineering, physiological, and psychological methods to study embodied cognition, especially focusing on the biological bases of speech and language. Notably, Rubin pioneered articulatory synthesis—including the development of ASY and CASY vocal tract models—and sinewave speech synthesis (SWS), both foundational in exploring speech production and perception. His theoretical work emphasizes dynamical systems approaches, perceptual organization, event-based gestures, and embodiment in speech, as well as multimodal integration involving audiovisual speech.

Beyond research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation and as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, where he led initiatives in neuroscience, mental health, forensic science, and science ethics. Rubin is an advocate for ethical considerations in human subjects research and emerging technologies. He has maintained involvement with multiple professional organizations, including serving as President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences and Chair of the Board of Directors at Haskins Laboratories. Rubin’s contributions have been recognized through numerous fellowships and awards across psychological, engineering, and scientific communities.
