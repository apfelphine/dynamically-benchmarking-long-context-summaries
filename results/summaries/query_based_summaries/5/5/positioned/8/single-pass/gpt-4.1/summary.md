## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American scientist recognized for his multidisciplinary research in speech science, articulatory synthesis, and speech perception. He developed key computational models such as ASY and SWS for speech analysis at Haskins Laboratories, contributing foundational work on the physiological and acoustic modeling of speech production. Rubin’s efforts advanced understanding of embodied cognition, perceptual organization, and theoretical models of speech as dynamic, gestural events, including influential collaborations that integrated articulatory phonology and task dynamics into speech modeling. He co-designed CASY, a configurable 3D articulatory synthesizer, developed tools for audiovisual speech research, and was a core leader of Haskins Laboratories’ NIH-funded P-01 program on speech and reading.

Beyond research, Rubin held significant roles in science administration and policy. He served as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation, Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, and chaired several boards and committees focused on science policy, human subjects research, and advocacy. Rubin led national initiatives in neuroscience, public access to research, education reform, and ethics in emerging technologies. He was president of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS), contributed to science education and museum projects, and served in advisory roles for various scientific organizations. Rubin is a fellow of several major scientific societies and has received numerous honors for both his research and service.
