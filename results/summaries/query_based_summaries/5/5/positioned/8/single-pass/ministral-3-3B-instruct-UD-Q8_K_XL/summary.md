Philip Rubin is an influential figure in the fields of speech technology, computational modeling, and cognitive science. Born in 1949 in Newark, New Jersey, he earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut. His research focuses on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, and the physiological and psychological bases of speech and language production.

Rubin is best known for his pioneering work on articulatory synthesis, including the development of ASY (Articulatory Synthesizer) and SWS (Sinewave Synthesizer) at Haskins Laboratories. ASY introduced speech gestures as a method for controlling articulatory events, while SWS explored the spatiotemporal aspects of speech signals. His contributions extended to theoretical approaches in speech production, emphasizing the embodied nature of speech and the importance of spatiotemporal coordination in perception and production. Rubin also developed the HADES signal processing system and the SPIEL programming language.

Throughout his career, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration, policy, and advocacy. He served as Director of the National Science Foundation’s Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences (2000–2003), where he helped launch key programs in cognitive neuroscience, human origins, and other fields. He later worked at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), leading initiatives in neuroscience, mental health, and language and communication research. Rubin has also been involved in human subjects research policy, serving on advisory committees and contributing to reports on ethical considerations in research.

Beyond academia, Rubin has been active in science policy, advocacy, and public engagement. He has received numerous honors, including fellowships from the Acoustical Society of America, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the Association for Psychological Science. His work has been recognized for its impact on understanding speech and language, advancing computational models, and influencing science policy. Rubin continues to be a prominent figure in the intersection of cognitive science, technology, and public policy.
