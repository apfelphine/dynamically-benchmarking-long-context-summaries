## Philip Rubin: Key Contributions and Career

Philip Rubin is an American scientist whose interdisciplinary research has focused on the biological bases of speech and language, spanning computational modeling, engineering, linguistics, psychology, and physiology. He is renowned for foundational work in speech technology, including articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, speech perception, and signal processing. Rubin designed influential computational models at Haskins Laboratories, notably the ASY articulatory synthesis program, the SWS sinewave speech synthesis system, and the HADES signal processing system. He contributed substantially to theoretical perspectives such as dynamical systems approaches to speech, spatiotemporal models of perceptual organization, and embodied cognition, helping advance the understanding of speech gestures and vocal tract modeling.

Beyond research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served at the National Science Foundation (NSF) as Director of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and as a senior advisor, initiated and led major federal programs, and chaired related boards at the National Academies. In the Obama Administration, Rubin was Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), leading the White House neuroscience initiative, supporting federal research priorities, and contributing to policies on open science, human subjects research, and behavioral science. He also served as President and Chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and has received numerous honors recognizing his research, service, and advocacy, including fellowships from major scientific societies. Rubin’s broader impact includes work on science ethics, public policy, STEM education, and multimedia collaborations.
