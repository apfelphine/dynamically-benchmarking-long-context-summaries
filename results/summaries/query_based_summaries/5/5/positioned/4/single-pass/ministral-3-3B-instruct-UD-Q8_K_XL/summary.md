Philip Rubin is a distinguished scholar and researcher in the fields of embodied cognition, speech production, and speech technology. Born in 1949, he earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut. Rubin’s foundational contributions include the development of **ASY** and **SWS**, two influential computational models for articulatory and sinewave speech synthesis, respectively. These systems were pivotal in advancing understanding of speech production and perception, particularly in the study of spatiotemporal coordination in speech signals.

His theoretical work emphasizes **gestures, events, and embodiment** in speech production, integrating physiological, psychological, and computational perspectives. Rubin’s approach to speech synthesis, such as the **configurable articulatory synthesizer (CASY)**, and his exploration of **perceptual organization** through sinewave synthesis have been instrumental in shaping modern speech technology. He also contributed significantly to **audiovisual speech research**, collaborating on innovations in analysis, synthesis, and simulation of multimodal speech.

Rubin held key leadership roles in science administration, policy, and advocacy, including serving as Director of the National Science Foundation’s Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences (2000–2003) and as a senior advisor in the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (2012–2015). His work in **human subjects research ethics** and **technology policy** reflects his commitment to responsible scientific practice. Rubin’s honors include election to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the Psychonomic Society, among others. His legacy lies in bridging theoretical advancements in speech science with practical applications in technology and policy.
