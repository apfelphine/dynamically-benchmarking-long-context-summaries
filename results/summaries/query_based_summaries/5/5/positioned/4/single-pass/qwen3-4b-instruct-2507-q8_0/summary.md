Philip Rubin is a prominent researcher in computational and cognitive science, known for his work on articulatory synthesis, speech production, and perception. He received his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut. 

Rubin's research spans multiple disciplines, combining computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, particularly the biological bases of speech and language. He is best known for his foundational contributions to articulatory synthesis, including the development of the ASY (Articulatory Synthesis) and SWS (Sinewave Synthesis) programs, which were used to model and simulate speech production. His work on speech perception and perceptual organization has also been influential.

In addition to his research, Rubin has held leadership roles in science administration and policy, including serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and as a senior advisor at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). He has also been involved in science policy and advocacy, including serving as Chair of the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences and as President and Chair of the Board of Directors of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS).

Rubin is recognized for his theoretical contributions, such as the dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, and has authored numerous publications on topics including speech perception, perceptual organization, and embodied cognition. He is a Fellow of several prestigious scientific organizations, including the Acoustical Society of America, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the American Psychological Association.
