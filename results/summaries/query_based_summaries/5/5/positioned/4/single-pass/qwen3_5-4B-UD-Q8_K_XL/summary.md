**Philip Rubin** is an experimental psychologist and researcher best known for his foundational work in speech synthesis, speech perception, and the biological bases of language. His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, and physiological approaches to study embodied cognition, particularly focusing on articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, and signal processing. Rubin developed significant systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES, which have been instrumental in advancing the understanding of how speech is produced and perceived. He also created the SPIEL programming language, a precursor to MATLAB.

Beyond his academic contributions, Rubin has held prominent leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and later as the Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), where he led the White House's neuroscience initiative. He has also been involved in various advisory capacities, including roles at the National Academies, the Discovery Museum, and the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). Rubin's work has influenced fields ranging from artificial intelligence and robotics to the ethical oversight of emerging technologies and intelligence methods.
