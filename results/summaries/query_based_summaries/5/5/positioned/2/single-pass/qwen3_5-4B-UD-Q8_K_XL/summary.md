# Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a prominent figure in the fields of psychology, linguistics, and speech technology, best known for his work on articulatory synthesis and the biological bases of speech production. He earned his BA from Brandeis University in 1971 and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975, where he studied under mentors including Michael Turvey and Alvin Liberman. During his early career at Haskins Laboratories, Rubin developed foundational computational models such as ASY (articulatory synthesis) and SWS (sinewave synthesis), which advanced the understanding of speech gestures and the spatiotemporal coordination of speech signals. He also co-created the HADES signal processing system and the SPIEL programming language, a precursor to MATLAB.

Throughout his career, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) from 2000 to 2003 and later as a senior advisor at the NSF and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). At OSTP, he led the White House's neuroscience initiative and served as Principal Assistant Director for Science. He has been involved in numerous advisory boards and committees, including the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences and the FABBS Board of Directors. Rubin is also recognized for his advocacy regarding ethical issues in research, particularly concerning human subjects protection and the oversight of technologies used by intelligence and law enforcement agencies.
