Philip Rubin earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971, followed by a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, particularly the biological basis of speech and language.

Rubin is best known for his foundational work in speech technology, including contributions to the ARPANET Network Voice Protocol and developing digitized speech extensions. At Haskins Laboratories, he was responsible for designing significant computational models, such as ASY (Haskins articulatory synthesis program) and SWS (Haskins sinewave synthesis program). His theoretical work introduced a dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, focusing on the spatiotemporal coordination of global aspects of the system. In addition to research, he has held leadership roles in science administration and policy, serving as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation from 2000 to 2003, and later serving at the Office of Science and Technology Policy.
