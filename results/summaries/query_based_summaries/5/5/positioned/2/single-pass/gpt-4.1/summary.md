## Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American scientist whose work has significantly impacted the fields of speech science, cognitive neuroscience, and science policy. Educated in psychology and linguistics at Brandeis University (BA, 1971) and experimental psychology at the University of Connecticut (PhD, 1975), Rubin has combined computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, especially speech and language. He is renowned for foundational contributions to articulatory synthesis (such as the ASY and CASY systems for modeling speech production), sinewave synthesis, and the development of experimental and theoretical models for perception, speech gestures, and complex temporal events. Rubin has held leadership roles at Haskins Laboratories, played a key role in the National Institutes of Health/NICHD program, helped found AVISA (Auditory-Visual Speech Association), and contributed to advances in audio-visual speech analysis and synthesis.

Beyond research, Rubin has been influential in science administration and policy, including serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF), Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), and chairing several national committees related to neuroscience, research ethics, and behavioral science. He has advocated for ethical oversight in emerging technologies, contributed to modernization of human subjects protection, and testified before Congress on behavioral science and security. Rubin also served as President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS), participated in numerous advisory and editorial boards, and has been recognized with multiple fellowships and awards for his contributions to science and policy. His interdisciplinary approach and leadership have advanced both theoretical research and broader societal understanding of cognition, communication, and the ethical dimensions of technology.
