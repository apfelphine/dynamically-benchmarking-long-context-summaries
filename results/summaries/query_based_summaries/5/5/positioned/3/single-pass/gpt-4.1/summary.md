## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American scientist and leader whose research spans computational modeling of speech production, speech perception, signal processing, and theoretical approaches to complex temporal events. He played a key role at Haskins Laboratories, designing notable systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES (signal processing), and contributed to the gestural-computational model of speech. He is recognized for pioneering work describing articulatory gestures, spatiotemporal coordination in speech perception and production, and multimodal speech integration. Beyond research, Rubin helped launch programs at the National Science Foundation, led science policy initiatives at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, and advocated for ethical guidelines in emerging technologies and research involving human subjects.

Rubin has held multiple leadership positions, including President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and chairing boards related to cognitive science, public policy, and education. His honors include fellowships in major scientific societies, notable awards for research service, and induction into prestigious organizations. Rubin is also known for his contributions to technical advisory groups, public science outreach, and interdisciplinary collaborations linking research and policy.
