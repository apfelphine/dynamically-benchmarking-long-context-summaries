Philip Rubin received his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971 and earned his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, focusing particularly on the biological bases of speech and language.

Rubin is widely known for his work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, and signal processing. At Haskins Laboratories, he designed significant software systems, including ASY (the Haskins articulatory synthesis program) and SWS (the Haskins sinewave synthesis program). Theoretically, he introduced a dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, emphasizing spatiotemporal coordination of global system aspects rather than momentary signal cues. Furthermore, he held leadership roles in science administration, serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation from 2000 to 2003, and later as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the Office of Science and Technology Policy.
