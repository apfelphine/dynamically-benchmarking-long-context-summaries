## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American cognitive scientist whose education includes a BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University (1971) and a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut (1975). His interdisciplinary research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological methods, focusing on embodied cognition, especially the biological bases of speech and language. Rubin is renowned for his contributions to speech technology, including foundational work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, signal processing, perceptual organization, and modeling complex temporal events. At Haskins Laboratories, he designed key speech synthesis systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), the HADES signal processing system, and the SPIEL programming language. He played a principal role in developing computational models and software used to study speech gestures and the dynamics of speech production.

Rubin’s theoretical and collaborative work advanced dynamical systems approaches to speech, audiovisual speech, and perceptual organization, notably through gestural-computational modeling and task dynamics. He held leadership roles in scientific administration and policy, serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF), Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), and president of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). He has been involved in science advocacy, ethics, and public policy related to research and emerging technologies, and has received multiple honors, including fellowships from leading scientific societies and prestigious service awards. Rubin was born in Newark, New Jersey, in 1949, and continues to contribute to research, policy, and scientific leadership.
