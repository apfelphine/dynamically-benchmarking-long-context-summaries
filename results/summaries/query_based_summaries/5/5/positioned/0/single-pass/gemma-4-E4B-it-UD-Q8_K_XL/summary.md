## Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971, followed by a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, focusing particularly on the biological bases of speech and language. He is recognized for his work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, sinewave synthesis, and modeling complex temporal events.

Rubin was heavily involved in early speech technology, designing prominent computational models like ASY and SWS at Haskins Laboratories in the 1970s. His theoretical contributions include developing a dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, which emphasizes the spatiotemporal coordination of global system aspects. Beyond research, he has held numerous leadership roles in science administration and policy, serving as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation from 2000 to 2003, and later serving in high-level positions at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.
