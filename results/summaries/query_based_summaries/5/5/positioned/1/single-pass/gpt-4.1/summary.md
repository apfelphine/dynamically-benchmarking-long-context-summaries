## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a researcher whose interdisciplinary work spans computational modeling, engineering, linguistics, physiology, and psychology, with a particular focus on the biological bases of speech and language. He is renowned for foundational contributions to articulatory and sinewave synthesis, speech perception, signal processing, and perceptual organization, notably designing systems like ASY, SWS, HADES, and SPIEL at Haskins Laboratories, as well as developing theoretical frameworks based on dynamical systems and gestural models. His leadership roles include core direction of key NIH/NICHD-funded research, founding AVISA, contributing to speech and multimodal coordination models, and advocating for ethical and science policy standards related to human subjects and emerging technologies.

Rubin has held significant positions at the National Science Foundation, as Director of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences, and later as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, where he led initiatives in neuroscience, open science, behavioral policy, and regulation reform. He continues to influence science policy as president of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and in various academic, advisory, and advocacy appointments. Rubin’s numerous honors include fellowships and awards from the Acoustical Society of America, AAAS, APA, and recognition for research, service, and leadership in neuroscience, psychology, and science administration.
