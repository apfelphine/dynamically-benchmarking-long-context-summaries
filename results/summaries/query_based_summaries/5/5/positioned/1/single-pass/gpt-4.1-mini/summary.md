**Philip Rubin** is a psychologist and linguist known for his interdisciplinary research on speech and language, combining computational, engineering, physiological, psychological, and linguistic approaches. He earned his BA from Brandeis University in 1971 and PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. Rubin has contributed significantly to articulatory synthesis, speech perception, sinewave synthesis, signal processing, and the theoretical modeling of temporal speech events.

Notably, Rubin developed key computational tools such as ASY (articulatory synthesizer) and SWS (sinewave synthesis program) in the 1970s at Haskins Laboratories, enhancing understanding of speech production and perception. He introduced a gestural model of speech production and collaborated on the CASY 3D vocal tract model. Rubin’s approach emphasizes embodied cognition, dynamical systems theory, and multimodal speech perception, including audiovisual speech.

In leadership roles, Rubin served as Director of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation and later as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, where he led the neuroscience initiative and contributed to science policy on mental health, forensic science, behavioral science, and open data.

He has also been involved in science advocacy and ethics, addressing human subjects protection and critiquing unproven technologies in intelligence and security. Rubin currently holds positions such as President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences and Chair of the Board of Directors at Haskins Laboratories. He has received numerous honors, including fellowships in the Acoustical Society of America, AAAS, APS, and the IEEE.
