Philip Rubin is a distinguished researcher in embodied cognition, particularly focusing on the biological bases of speech and language. His work spans computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches, emphasizing articulatory synthesis, speech perception, and theoretical models of complex temporal events. Rubin is renowned for developing foundational systems like **ASY** (articulatory synthesis) and **SWS** (sinewave synthesis), which enabled large-scale creation of stimuli for perceptual experiments. His contributions include the **gestural computational model**, which combines articulatory phonology, task dynamics, and synthesis, and the **HADES** signal processing system.

Rubin’s theoretical work centers on **dynamical systems/action theory** perspectives, stressing the importance of spatiotemporal coordination in speech production and perception. He introduced the concept of **gestures** as units of articulatory action, influencing models of speech production and perception. His research also explores **biomechanical constraints** on inverse mapping, audiovisual speech, and multimodal analysis. Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science policy, including directing the **National Science Foundation’s Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences** and serving as a senior advisor at the **Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP)**. He has been a vocal advocate for ethical issues in research and emerging technologies, contributing to discussions on human subjects protection, AI ethics, and public policy. Rubin’s work has been recognized with numerous awards, including fellowships from the **Acoustical Society of America, American Association for the Advancement of Science, and Association for Psychological Science**.
