## Philip Rubin: Summary

Philip Rubin is an American experimental psychologist and scientist known for his pioneering work in speech synthesis, speech perception, articulatory synthesis, and computational modeling of speech production. He received his PhD from the University of Connecticut in 1975, and has led innovative research on the biological, psychological, and computational bases of speech and language. At Haskins Laboratories, Rubin designed foundational systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES (signal processing), which enabled advanced studies of speech gestures, event-based articulatory coordination, and the spatiotemporal organization of speech signals. He contributed theoretical approaches that emphasize dynamical systems, perceptual organization, embodiment, and multimodality, collaborating with prominent researchers on models that integrate computational, linguistic, physiological, and psychological perspectives.

In addition to his scientific research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration, policy, and advocacy. He served as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF), chaired the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences, and was Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences in the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy under President Obama, where he led neuroscience initiatives and contributed to policy on ethics, research infrastructure, and public access. Rubin has received numerous honors for his achievements, including election as Fellow to several prominent scientific societies, and continues to be active in advisory, administrative, and advocacy roles promoting behavioral and brain sciences and the ethical implications of emerging technologies.
