Philip Rubin earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971, followed by a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, particularly focusing on the biological bases of speech and language.

Rubin is highly known for his work on articulatory synthesis (computational modeling of speech production), speech perception, and sinewave synthesis. He designed prominent programs like ASY and SWS at Haskins Laboratories. His theoretical contributions include introducing a dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, emphasizing perceptual organization through underlying spectral coherence, and expanding speech modeling to incorporate event-based approaches, leading to systems like TADA. Furthermore, Rubin has held significant roles in science administration, policy, and advocacy, serving as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation from 2000 to 2003, and later serving in leadership positions within the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.
