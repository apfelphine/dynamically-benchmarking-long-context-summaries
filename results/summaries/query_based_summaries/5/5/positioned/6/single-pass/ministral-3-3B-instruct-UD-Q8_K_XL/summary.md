Philip Rubin is a distinguished scholar and researcher in embodied cognition, particularly focusing on the biological and computational aspects of speech and language. Born in 1949, he earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut. Rubin’s foundational contributions include the development of articulatory synthesis systems, such as **ASY** and **CASY**, which model the physiology and acoustics of speech production. His work on **sinewave synthesis** and **signal processing** has significantly advanced speech perception and perception research.

Rubin’s theoretical contributions emphasize the importance of **spatiotemporal coordination** in speech and perception, moving beyond traditional feature-based approaches. He has explored the role of **gestures, events, and embodiment** in speech production, integrating insights from dynamical systems theory and action theory. His research also spans **audiovisual speech**, multimodality, and the ethical implications of emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence and human subjects research.

Throughout his career, Rubin has held key leadership roles in science administration and policy, including serving as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and advising the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). He has been instrumental in shaping policies related to neuroscience, human subjects research, and the ethical use of technology. Rubin has received numerous honors, including fellowships from the Acoustical Society of America, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the Association for Psychological Science, among others.

His work bridges computational, physiological, linguistic, and psychological perspectives, making him a pivotal figure in advancing our understanding of speech, language, and human cognition.
