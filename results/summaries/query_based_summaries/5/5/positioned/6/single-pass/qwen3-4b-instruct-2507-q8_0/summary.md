Philip Rubin is a prominent figure in computational and cognitive science, known for his pioneering work in speech synthesis, speech perception, and embodied cognition. He earned a Bachelor of Arts in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut under the supervision of Michael Turvey and others.

Rubin's research spans computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches, focusing on the biological bases of speech and language. He is best known for developing the ASY and SWS systems—articulatory and sinewave synthesis programs—which enabled the modeling of speech production and perception. His work on speech gestures and task dynamics led to the creation of the CASY and TADA systems, which integrate articulatory phonology and task-based coordination in speech.

A key theoretical contribution is his dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, emphasizing spatiotemporal coordination over traditional cues. He also developed models of perceptual organization and explored audiovisual speech, demonstrating how multimodal information contributes to speech understanding.

Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science policy and administration, including serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). He has been instrumental in advancing research on human subjects and in promoting ethical standards in science.

He is a Fellow of the Acoustical Society of America, the American Psychological Association, and the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and has received numerous honors, including the Distinguished Service Award from COSSA and the IEEE Senior Member designation. Rubin is currently President and Chair of the Board of Directors of Haskins Laboratories and a member of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). His work continues to influence the fields of cognitive science, speech technology, and human-computer interaction.
