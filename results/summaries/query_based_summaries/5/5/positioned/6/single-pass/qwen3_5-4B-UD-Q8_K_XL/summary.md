# Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a prominent researcher in speech technology, cognitive science, and science policy, best known for his foundational work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, and the biological bases of language. A graduate of Brandeis University and the University of Connecticut, he began his career at Haskins Laboratories in the 1970s, where he led the development of computational models such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES. His theoretical contributions include the application of dynamical systems theory to speech production and the concept of speech gestures, which model articulatory events as coordinated physiological processes rather than isolated acoustic cues. Rubin also pioneered signal processing systems and is credited as a predecessor of MATLAB through his design of the SPIEL programming language.

Beyond his research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and advocacy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and later as a senior advisor at the NSF and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), where he led the White House's neuroscience initiative and oversaw broadening participation in science. He has been a long-time chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and has contributed extensively to ethical oversight in human subjects research, notably through his work with the National Research Council and the AAUP. Rubin is also recognized for his involvement in the arts and technology community, having founded the Yale Macintosh Users Group and serving as a judge for the DARPA Robotics Challenge.
