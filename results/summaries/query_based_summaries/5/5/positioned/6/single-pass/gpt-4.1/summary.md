## Philip Rubin: Summary

Philip Rubin is an American scientist whose multidisciplinary research integrates computational, linguistic, engineering, physiological, and psychological approaches to understanding embodied cognition, particularly in speech and language. He is noted for his pioneering work in articulatory synthesis, speech perception, sinewave synthesis, signal processing, perceptual organization, and theoretical modeling of complex temporal events. Rubin developed influential software systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesizer), SWS (sinewave synthesizer), and HADES (signal processing system), contributing to advances in the modeling and analysis of speech production and perception. He has published extensively on the dynamical systems perspective in speech, biomechanical constraints, event-based gestural modeling, and multimodal (audiovisual) speech research.

In addition to his scientific achievements, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the NSF and as Assistant Director for Science at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, where he led neuroscience initiatives and helped launch major research programs. Rubin has been actively involved with ethical issues relating to research and emerging technologies, serving on national committees focused on human research protections and advising on bioethics and technology oversight. He is known for his advocacy in science policy, contributions to public science education, and leadership in organizations such as the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and Haskins Laboratories. Rubin’s work has earned fellowships and numerous awards across professional and scientific societies.
