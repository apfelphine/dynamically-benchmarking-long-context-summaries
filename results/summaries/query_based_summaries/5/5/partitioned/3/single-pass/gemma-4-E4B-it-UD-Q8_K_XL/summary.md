Philip Rubin's expertise lies at the intersection of computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study embodied cognition, especially concerning the biological foundations of speech and language. He is notably known for his work on articulatory synthesis (computational modeling of speech production), speech perception, and sinewave synthesis.

Rubin has been involved in significant leadership roles in science administration and policy. He served as the Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation from 2000 to 2003, and later held positions as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). His research and advocacy have frequently addressed ethical issues in research, particularly concerning human subjects, and he was a co-founder of AVISA, the Auditory-Visual Speech Association.
