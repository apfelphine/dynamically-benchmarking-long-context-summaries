## Philip Rubin: Key Contributions and Activities

**Education and Career:**  
Philip Rubin holds a BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University (1971) and a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut (1975). His work combines computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches, focusing on speech production, speech perception, articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, and embodied cognition. Rubin is known for developing influential speech technology systems at Haskins Laboratories, including ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES (signal processing), and for theoretical advances in speech gestural modeling and dynamical systems. He has also held significant administrative roles, notably as Director of NSF's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and was instrumental in launching major scientific programs.

**Science Policy, Leadership, and Advocacy:**  
Rubin has served in prominent positions including Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, OSTP Principal Assistant Director for Science, and Co-Chair of multiple interagency committees and working groups. His leadership spans science advocacy, ethical oversight of research (especially human subjects protection), neuroscience, behavioral science, and modernization of scientific infrastructure and policy. He is the current President and Board Chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and Chair of the Board of Directors at Haskins Laboratories. Rubin remains active in national and state-level advisory and governance roles, contributing widely to research policy, ethics, education, and innovation.

**Popular Culture and Honors:**  
Rubin’s pioneering work in speech synthesis and sinewave speech has left its mark in popular culture and audio art. He has received numerous honors and fellowships from organizations such as the Acoustical Society of America, APA, AAAS, and IEEE, recognizing his scientific achievements and service. Aspects of his career—including scientific service, educational initiatives, policy work, and ethical advocacy—are frequently highlighted in interviews and professional profiles.
