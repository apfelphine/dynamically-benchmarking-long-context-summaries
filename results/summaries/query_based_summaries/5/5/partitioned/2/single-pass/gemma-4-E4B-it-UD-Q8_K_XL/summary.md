Philip Rubin has maintained significant involvement in science policy, advocacy, and academic leadership across various roles. His career includes serving as the NSF ex officio representative to the National Human Research Protection Advisory Committee (NHRPAC) and co-chairing the inter-agency National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) Committee on Science (COS) Human Subjects Research Subcommittee (HSRS) under the President's Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). He served as the Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at OSTP in 2012 and later retired from OSTP and the NSF in 2015.

His research focuses on embodied cognition, particularly the biological foundations of speech and language, employing computational, engineering, linguistic, and physiological approaches. Key contributions include the design of articulatory synthesis programs (like ASY) and the sinewave synthesis system (SWS), which was used to study the time-varying characteristics of speech signals. He has also made theoretical contributions by introducing a dynamical systems/action theory perspective on speech, emphasizing spatiotemporal coordination of global system aspects.
