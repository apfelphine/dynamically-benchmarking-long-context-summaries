Philip Rubin is a prominent researcher and science policy leader known for his work on speech production, perception, and embodied cognition. He received his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut. His research combines computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to study the biological foundations of speech and language, with key contributions in articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, and perceptual organization. He developed influential software systems such as ASY, SWS, and HADES, and pioneered the use of gesture-based models in speech production. Rubin has held leadership roles in science administration, including Director of the NSF's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and Senior Advisor in the Directorate for Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences. He is currently President and Chair of the Board of Directors of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). Additionally, he has been recognized with numerous honors, including the iGIANT Pioneer Award, and has contributed to science policy, ethics, and advocacy, particularly in human subjects research and emerging technologies. His work has had a lasting impact on both theoretical models and practical applications in speech technology.
