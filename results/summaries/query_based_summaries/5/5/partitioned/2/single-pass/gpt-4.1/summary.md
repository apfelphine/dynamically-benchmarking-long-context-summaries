## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a cognitive scientist and leader known for pioneering research in speech production, speech technology, articulatory synthesis, and perceptual organization, notably developing the ASY articulatory synthesizer and SWS sinewave synthesis system at Haskins Laboratories. His work integrates computational, physiological, and engineering approaches to embodied cognition and spatiotemporal coordination in speech and language. Rubin was instrumental in theoretical advances such as event-based modeling of speech gestures, dynamical systems perspectives on speech, and the study of audiovisual speech and multimodality. He also contributed to foundational technologies including the ARPANET Network Voice Protocol and was technical advisor at ZeniMax Media.

Beyond research, Rubin has held major science policy roles, serving as Director of NSF’s Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences, Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, and President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). His leadership has shaped programs in cognitive neuroscience, ethics in human subjects research, and neuroscience policy. Rubin has also made significant contributions in education, public science advocacy, and organizational governance, such as at Haskins Laboratories and the University of Connecticut Board of Trustees. He has been recognized with numerous honors, including fellowships in scientific societies and awards for research management and science policy.
