Philip Rubin is a prominent researcher in speech science and cognitive science, best known for his pioneering work in articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, and the computational modeling of speech production. Holding a BA from Brandeis University and a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut, Rubin developed foundational systems at Haskins Laboratories, including the ASY and SWS synthesizers, which replaced traditional formant-based models with event-based and gestural approaches to describe the physiology and acoustics of speech. His theoretical contributions emphasize spatiotemporal coordination, dynamical systems, and the role of embodiment in perception, influencing modern speech understanding technologies.

Beyond his scientific research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science administration and advocacy. He served as Director of the NSF's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences (2000–2003) and as a senior advisor in the NSF's SBE Directorate. In the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), he led the neuroscience initiative and co-chaired the NSTC Committee on Science, focusing on priorities such as the BRAIN Initiative, mental health, and human subjects research ethics. Rubin has also been a long-time member of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS), serving as its President, and has contributed to science policy through various advisory councils, including the National Academy of Public Administration's NASA Advisory Council and the National Research Council.
