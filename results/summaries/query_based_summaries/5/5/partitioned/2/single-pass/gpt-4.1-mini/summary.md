## Summary of Information on Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is a prominent researcher and science policy leader specializing in speech science, cognitive science, and behavioral sciences, with a career blending computational, linguistic, physiological, and psychological approaches to embodied cognition, particularly in speech and language. Educated at Brandeis University (BA, 1971) and the University of Connecticut (PhD, 1975), Rubin is recognized for foundational work in articulatory synthesis, speech perception, sinewave synthesis, signal processing, and modeling of temporal events. His significant contributions include designing landmark computational models such as the articulatory synthesis programs ASY and CASY, sinewave synthesis system SWS, and development of gestural computational systems integrating articulatory phonology and task dynamics. Rubin’s work extends to audiovisual speech and the multimodal coordination of speech, face, and gesture.

Beyond research, Rubin has held influential leadership roles in science administration and policy. He chaired the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences (2006–2011), directed the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (2000–2003), and served from 2012 to 2015 as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, where he led the neuroscience initiative and co-chaired multiple interagency science committees. Rubin has actively engaged in ethical issues in human subjects research and emerging technologies, contributing to national advisory committees and providing congressional testimony on programs like TSA’s SPOT. He is President and Chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS), co-founder of the informal IS Group exploring interdisciplinary scientific questions, and has advised various scientific boards and organizations focused on science policy, behavioral sciences, and technology ethics.
