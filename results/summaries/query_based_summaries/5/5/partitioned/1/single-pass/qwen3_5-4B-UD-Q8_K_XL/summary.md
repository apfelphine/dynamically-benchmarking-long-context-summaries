**Philip Rubin** is a prominent figure in science policy, research administration, and the fields of speech processing and embodied cognition. He is best known for his pioneering work on articulatory and sinewave synthesis at Haskins Laboratories, where he developed computational models that revolutionized the understanding of speech production and perception. His theoretical contributions emphasize dynamical systems and gesture-based approaches to how the vocal tract functions. Beyond his research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in federal science agencies, including serving as the Director of the NSF's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and as a senior advisor in the NSF's SBE Directorate. He also served as the Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), where he led the White House's neuroscience initiative and advocated for open science and broad participation in research. Rubin is currently the President and Chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and continues to serve on various advisory boards, including those for NASA, the UConn Board of Trustees, and the Franklin Institute.

In addition to his professional achievements, Rubin has maintained an active presence in popular culture and the arts. He is a former guitarist in the New Jersey garage band The Institution and a photographer specializing in urban wall art. His work on sinewave synthesis has been recognized for its influence on sound design, with references drawn to his techniques in media such as the Star Wars franchise (R2-D2's voice) and various artistic projects. Rubin's career spans over four decades, marked by a unique blend of technical innovation in cognitive science, high-level advocacy for human subjects protection and scientific integrity, and engagement with the broader cultural landscape through media appearances and public service.
