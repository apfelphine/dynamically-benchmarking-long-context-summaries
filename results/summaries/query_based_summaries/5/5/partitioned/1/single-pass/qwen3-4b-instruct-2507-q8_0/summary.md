Philip Rubin is a prominent figure in science policy, advocacy, and research, with a distinguished career in behavioral and cognitive sciences. He has held key leadership roles, including Chair of the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences and President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). A leading researcher in speech production and perception, Rubin developed influential computational models such as ASY and SWS for articulatory synthesis and sinewave synthesis, and pioneered the task-dynamic model of speech production. His work has significantly advanced the understanding of embodied cognition, perceptual organization, and the spatiotemporal coordination of speech.

Rubin has been a strong advocate for ethical research, serving on human subjects committees and contributing to policy development, including work on the Common Rule modernization. He has also been involved in science administration, serving as Director of the National Science Foundation’s Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and as a senior advisor in the Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences Directorate. His leadership has extended to promoting gender-inclusive innovation through the iGIANT initiative and advancing science policy through the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.

He is recognized for his contributions with numerous fellowships, including Fellowships from the American Psychological Association, the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the American Psychological Society. His work has been acknowledged in popular culture, including references to his sine-wave synthesis in music and illustrations in art publications. Rubin is a professor at the University of Connecticut and has authored numerous publications on speech technology, cognitive science, and science policy.
