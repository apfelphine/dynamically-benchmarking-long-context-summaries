## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American cognitive scientist whose research integrates computational, linguistic, psychological, and physiological approaches toward understanding speech and language, particularly the biological bases of speech production and perception. He is renowned for foundational work in speech technology, including the development of articulatory synthesis and sinewave synthesis systems, and for significant theoretical contributions to embodied cognition, perceptual organization, multimodal speech processing, and action theory perspectives in speech science. Rubin's leadership roles include serving as Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation (NSF), and various positions in science policy and advocacy, including chairing federal committees on human subjects research and serving as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences in the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.

Rubin has held prominent roles in organizations advancing behavioral and brain science, such as President and Chair of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS) and leadership positions at Haskins Laboratories as Vice-Chair and Chair of the Board of Directors. He has a record of public service, including participation on the University of Connecticut Board of Trustees and advisory groups for NASA and the Franklin Institute. Rubin’s honors include fellowships from numerous scientific societies, the APA Meritorious Research Service Commendation, and lifetime achievement awards. His contributions have influenced both research and popular culture, with references to his sinewave speech synthesis work and involvement in science-policy discourse. He is also an accomplished photographer, musician, and science communicator, authoring and co-authoring many influential scientific papers and reports.
