Philip Rubin is a prominent scientist and policy leader known for his work in computational linguistics, speech technology, and science administration. He earned his BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University in 1971 and his PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut in 1975. Rubin is best recognized for his foundational contributions to speech synthesis, including the development of the Haskins articulatory synthesis program (ASY) and the sinewave synthesis system (SWS). He pioneered the concept of "speech gestures" to model the physiology of speech production and co-founded the Auditory-Visual Speech Association (AVISA). His research spans computational modeling, signal processing, and the biological bases of speech, with a focus on embodied cognition and dynamical systems theory.

In addition to his academic research, Rubin has held significant leadership roles in science policy and advocacy. He served as the Director of the National Science Foundation's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences and later as a senior advisor and Principal Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), where he led initiatives in neuroscience and the BRAIN Initiative. His career also includes administrative roles at Haskins Laboratories, where he led the NIH-funded "Nature and Acquisition of the Speech Code and Reading" project, and leadership positions in professional organizations such as the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS). Rubin is also a musician, guitarist, and photographer, and has contributed to the development of signal processing tools like the SPIEL programming language.
