Philip Rubin is a prominent scientist and science policy leader whose research spans embodied cognition, speech production, and speech perception. He developed foundational computational models such as ASY and SWS for articulatory synthesis and sinewave speech, contributing significantly to the understanding of speech as a dynamic, spatiotemporal process. He is known for his work on gesture-based models of speech production, perceptual organization, and the integration of audiovisual information. 

Rubin has held key leadership roles in science policy, including serving as Chair of the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences, Director of the Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences at the National Science Foundation, and Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. He has been instrumental in advancing ethical standards in human subjects research and has advocated for the responsible use of emerging technologies, including in intelligence and security applications.

He is also active in education and innovation, serving on numerous advisory boards, including the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS), where he became President. Rubin has contributed to public discourse on science and technology through publications, public lectures, and media appearances. He is a Fellow of the Acoustical Society of America, the American Psychological Association, and the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and has received multiple honors, including the Distinguished Service Award from COSSA and the iGIANT Pioneer Award. A photographer and artist, Rubin's work explores urban wall art and its cultural significance. He is married to Joette Katz, a retired justice, and has two children.
