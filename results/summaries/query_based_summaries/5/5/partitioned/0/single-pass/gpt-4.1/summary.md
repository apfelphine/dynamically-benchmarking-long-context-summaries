## Summary: Philip Rubin

Philip Rubin is an American scientist whose interdisciplinary research has contributed significantly to the understanding of speech production, perception, and embodied cognition. He is celebrated for pioneering work in articulatory synthesis, sinewave synthesis, and computational modeling of speech, designing influential systems such as ASY (articulatory synthesis), SWS (sinewave synthesis), and HADES (signal processing), with applications spanning linguistics, psychology, engineering, and neuroscience. Rubin has co-developed innovative frameworks and software for studying vocal tract dynamics, articulatory gestures, and audiovisual aspects of speech, and has published extensively on these topics.

In addition to his scientific achievements, Rubin has held prominent leadership positions related to science policy, advocacy, and ethical oversight, including Chair of the National Academies Board on Behavioral, Cognitive, and Sensory Sciences, Director at the National Science Foundation, Assistant Director at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, and President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences. He has served on various advisory boards and committees focused on human research protections, ethics of emerging technologies, and promotion of public trust in science, earning multiple honors and fellowships from major scientific societies. Beyond academia, Rubin has been engaged in activities linking science with education, public engagement, and popular culture.
