Philip Rubin holds a BA in psychology and linguistics from Brandeis University (1971) and a PhD in experimental psychology from the University of Connecticut (1975). His research integrates computational, engineering, linguistic, physiological, and psychological methods to study embodied cognition, especially the biological basis of speech and language. He is recognized for his work on articulatory synthesis, speech perception, signal processing, and theoretical modeling of complex temporal events.

Rubin's career spans significant contributions to speech technology, including designing ASY (Haskins articulatory synthesis program) and SWS (Haskins sinewave synthesis program). Beyond his laboratory work, he has held multiple leadership roles in science administration and policy, serving as the NSF ex officio representative to key human research protection committees. He has also held senior positions, including as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the OSTP during the Obama administration, and is currently the President of the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences (FABBS).
