John von Neumann was a highly influential polymath whose contributions spanned mathematics, physics, computer science, and economics. In mathematics, he was instrumental in founding abstract Hilbert spaces, axiomatically defining them, and developing spectral theory of operators. He founded the field of game theory, proving the minimax theorem, and made major contributions to linear programming, including inventing the theory of duality.

In physics, von Neumann was a key figure in establishing the rigorous mathematical framework for quantum mechanics through the Dirac–von Neumann axioms. He also made foundational contributions to ergodic theory, measure theory, and fluid dynamics, developing the Taylor–von Neumann–Sedov blast wave solution. Furthermore, he was a pioneer in computer science, designing the IAS machine and inventing the merge sort algorithm. In economics, his work on an expanding economy was recognized as a seminal contribution to mathematical economics.
