# John von Neumann

John von Neumann (1903–1957) was a Hungarian-American polymath who made groundbreaking contributions to mathematics, physics, computer science, economics, and defense. In mathematics, he axiomatized set theory, proving the consistency of first-order arithmetic, and developed the theory of von Neumann algebras, continuous geometry, and ergodic theory. In physics, he established the rigorous mathematical framework for quantum mechanics, including the Dirac–von Neumann axioms, the concept of density matrices, and quantum logic, arguing that wave function collapse requires conscious observation. He also worked on fluid dynamics, notably contributing to the theory of blast waves and detonation models during World War II.

Von Neumann's influence extended to game theory, where he founded the field by proving the minimax theorem and co-authoring *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*. In economics, he developed the model of an expanding economy and invented the theory of duality in linear programming. As a pioneer in computer science, he designed the stored-program architecture (though not solely credited with it), invented the merge sort algorithm, and proposed the von Neumann universal constructor. His work on the Manhattan Project involved designing explosive lenses for the atomic bomb, and post-war he served as a key advisor on intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBM) and nuclear weapons, playing a pivotal role in the development of the hydrogen bomb and the ICBM program.
