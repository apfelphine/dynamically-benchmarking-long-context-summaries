## John von Neumann

John von Neumann, born in Budapest in 1903, was a child prodigy tutored in multiple languages, showing early mathematical talent such as mastering calculus by age eight. His early education included studying advanced calculus and publishing major mathematical papers by age nineteen. He later received a degree as a chemical engineer from ETH Zurich in 1926 while concurrently pursuing a Ph.D. in mathematics at the University of Budapest.

Von Neumann made foundational contributions across numerous fields. In set theory, he provided techniques to exclude sets that belong to themselves and was central to the axiomatization of set theory. His work on the Banach–Tarski paradox demonstrated how a disk could be rearranged into two disks. In functional analysis, he was the first to axiomatically define an abstract Hilbert space and made seminal contributions to spectral theory of operators. Furthermore, he founded the study of von Neumann algebras and made major advancements in ergodic theory and measure theory. His early career also involved work in game theory, where he proved his minimax theorem in 1928, and he was a founding figure in computer science, designing the IAS machine.

In the realm of physics, von Neumann established the rigorous mathematical framework for quantum mechanics in his 1932 work, *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics*. He introduced the density matrix and the von Neumann measurement scheme, and he was the first to propose a quantum logic, demonstrating that quantum mechanics requires a propositional calculus different from classical logic. In his later years, he was heavily involved in defense work, contributing to the design of the explosive lenses for the Fat Man weapon during the Manhattan Project, and later served as a commissioner of the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC).
