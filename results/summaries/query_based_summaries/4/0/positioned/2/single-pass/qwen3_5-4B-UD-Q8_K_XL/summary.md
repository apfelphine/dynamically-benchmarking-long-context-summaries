# John von Neumann

John von Neumann (1903–1957) was a Hungarian-American mathematician, physicist, and polymath who made foundational contributions to set theory, functional analysis, quantum mechanics, and computer science. In set theory, he provided the first rigorous axiomatization, introducing the axiom of foundation to resolve paradoxes. In quantum mechanics, his 1932 work *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics* established the rigorous Hilbert space formalism and introduced the concept of the density matrix. He also pioneered the study of operator algebras and lattice theory, including the development of continuous geometry.

Von Neumann was a founding figure in computer science, designing the stored-program architecture that underpins modern computing (though often attributed to Eckert and Mauchly, the architecture was based on his "First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC"). He invented the merge sort algorithm and contributed to cellular automata and the theory of self-reproducing automata. In economics, he co-founded game theory with Oskar Morgenstern and developed the mathematical theory of linear programming and duality. His work on fluid dynamics included the Taylor–von Neumann blast wave solution, and he played a pivotal role in the Manhattan Project, designing the implosion mechanism for the atomic bomb. Following the war, he served as a top advisor on nuclear weapons and ICBMs, holding positions at the Atomic Energy Commission and the Pentagon.
