## John von Neumann

John von Neumann was a prolific polymath whose intellectual contributions spanned numerous fields, including mathematics, physics, economics, and computer science. In mathematics, he founded the field of game theory, proving the minimax theorem in 1928, and co-authored the 1944 *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior* with Oskar Morgenstern. He was a foundational figure in computing, designing the IAS machine and contributing to the development of the single-memory, stored-program architecture. His work also includes inventing the merge sort algorithm and introducing stochastic computing in 1953.

In physics, von Neumann established the rigorous mathematical framework for quantum mechanics in his 1932 work, *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics*, which introduced concepts like the density matrix and quantum logic. His work was also instrumental in fluid dynamics, including the development of the Taylor–von Neumann–Sedov blast wave solution. Furthermore, he made major contributions to mathematical statistics, deriving the Durbin–Watson statistic. His expertise led to significant involvement in defense work, notably as a key authority in the Manhattan Project, where he was instrumental in the concept and design of explosive lenses for the Fat Man weapon.
