Noam Chomsky is a distinguished American linguist, philosopher, and political activist who pioneered the field of generative grammar and is widely credited with launching the cognitive revolution. His linguistic work, beginning in the 1950s at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, posits that humans possess an innate "language acquisition device" and universal grammar, enabling children to master complex language structures despite limited exposure. This theoretical framework, rooted in rationalism and biolinguistics, challenged dominant behaviorist views of language learning.

Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a prominent political dissident and critic of U.S. foreign policy, imperialism, and corporate media. He argues that mainstream media operates under a "propaganda model" that censors dissent to serve elite interests, a theory detailed in his book *Manufacturing Consent*. His activism focuses on issues such as the Vietnam War, the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and global inequality, often drawing sharp criticism from conservative commentators and institutions. Despite facing significant controversy and censorship, Chomsky remains a highly influential intellectual figure whose work spans linguistics, philosophy of mind, and political theory.
