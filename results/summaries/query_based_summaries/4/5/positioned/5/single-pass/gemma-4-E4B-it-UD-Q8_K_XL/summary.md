## Noam Chomsky Summary

Noam Chomsky's intellectual contributions span two major, influential fields: linguistics and sociopolitical analysis. In linguistics, he is credited with inaugurating the "cognitive revolution," moving the field away from behaviorism. His foundational work, such as *Syntactic Structures* and *Aspects of the Theory of Syntax*, developed the theory of generative grammar and transformational grammar. Chomsky's theories are rooted in biolinguistics, asserting that all humans share an underlying linguistic structure—Universal Grammar—which he posits is innate, a view he supports by citing the "poverty of the stimulus."

Outside of linguistics, Chomsky is a prominent political dissident whose work critiques American imperialism, U.S. foreign policy, and the role of mass media. His political philosophy often identifies him as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist. He developed the propaganda model with Edward S. Herman, which dissects how mainstream media reinforces state ideologies through subtle corporate filtering. Chomsky is known for his consistent, critical assessments of global power structures, such as his analysis of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and his critique of American intervention in various geopolitical crises.
