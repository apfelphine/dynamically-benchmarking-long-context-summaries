Noam Chomsky is a prominent linguist, philosopher, and political activist. He is best known for founding generative grammar and the theory of universal grammar, which posits that humans are born with an innate capacity for language. His linguistic work, including *Syntactic Structures* and *Aspects of the Theory of Syntax*, revolutionized the field by challenging behaviorist views and introducing a cognitive, internalist approach to language acquisition. Chomsky's work laid the foundation for the cognitive revolution in linguistics and significantly influenced cognitive science, computer science, and philosophy.

Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a leading critic of U.S. foreign policy, advocating for democratic control of the economy and opposing imperialism, militarism, and the suppression of dissent. He argues that mainstream media serves as a tool of state propaganda, promoting a narrative that masks the true nature of political power and economic inequality. His political writings, such as *Manufacturing Consent* and *The Fateful Triangle*, analyze how governments manipulate public perception and maintain control. Chomsky has been a vocal critic of U.S. involvement in conflicts, including Vietnam, the Middle East, and the Iraq War, and has consistently called for peace, transparency, and social justice.

He identifies as an anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist, emphasizing individual freedom, community, and democratic self-governance. Chomsky has been widely recognized as one of the most influential intellectuals of the 20th and 21st centuries, receiving numerous awards and honors, including the US Peace Prize and the Nobel Prize in Literature. Despite facing criticism and controversy—particularly for his views on Israel and Holocaust denial—his work continues to shape public discourse on politics, media, and human rights.
