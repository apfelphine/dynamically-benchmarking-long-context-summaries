## Summary of Information on Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky (born December 7, 1928) is a renowned American linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, historian, social critic, and political activist. Raised in Philadelphia by Ashkenazi Jewish immigrant parents, he was influenced early on by leftist politics and anarchism. He began his academic career studying philosophy, logic, and languages at the University of Pennsylvania and later completed his doctoral dissertation on transformational grammar at Harvard. Chomsky revolutionized linguistics by founding generative grammar and advocating for the innate biological basis of language acquisition, notably proposing the concept of universal grammar. His linguistic theories challenged behaviorism, established formal models such as the Chomsky hierarchy, and significantly influenced cognitive science, computer science, and psychology.

Parallel to his linguistic work, Chomsky became a prominent political dissident and critic of U.S. foreign policy, media propaganda, and capitalism. Identifying as an anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist, he advocates for liberty, social justice, and democratic control of economic resources. Chomsky is widely known for his critique of mainstream media's role in shaping public opinion, especially with Herman Herman in their propaganda model presented in *Manufacturing Consent*. His political writings have sparked considerable controversy, including debates on his defense of free speech even for Holocaust deniers. Despite this, he has received numerous honors, including multiple honorary doctorates and awards for his contributions to linguistics, cognitive science, and activism. After retiring from MIT in 2002, he remained active in political discourse and academia until a severe stroke in 2023, after which he moved to Brazil to recuperate.
