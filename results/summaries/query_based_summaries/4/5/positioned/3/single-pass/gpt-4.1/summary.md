## Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky was born in 1928 to Ashkenazi Jewish immigrants in Philadelphia. Raised in a politically active, intellectually vibrant household, he excelled academically, developed early interests in anarchism and linguistics, and completed his undergraduate and graduate studies at the University of Pennsylvania, followed by research at Harvard. Chomsky became a pivotal figure at MIT from the mid-1950s, founding its linguistics program and publishing groundbreaking work, including "Syntactic Structures," which revolutionized the study of language. He introduced the theory of generative grammar, argued for universal grammar—a biologically innate structure underpinning all human languages—and developed models such as transformational grammar, government and binding theory, and the minimalist program. His ideas inaugurated the "cognitive revolution" in linguistics and moved the field toward cognitive science, sparking both acclaim and prolonged debate within linguistics and philosophy.

Beyond academia, Chomsky became a prominent political dissident, anarcho-syndicalist, and libertarian socialist, critiquing American foreign policy, capitalism, mass media, and corporate power. He is recognized for his analyses exposing propaganda and manipulation in democratic societies, notably co-authoring the "propaganda model" in "Manufacturing Consent." Chomsky's activism and writings have influenced political discourse, anti-war movements, and grassroots organizations worldwide. He has received numerous awards and honorary degrees for his scholarly and activist contributions. Despite controversy and criticism regarding some of his political positions and alliances, Chomsky remains one of the most cited living authors, widely acknowledged as "the father of modern linguistics" and a leading public intellectual, with significant impact across linguistics, cognitive science, philosophy, computer science, and global activism.
