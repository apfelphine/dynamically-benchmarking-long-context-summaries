Noam Chomsky is a prominent intellectual known for his work in linguistics and as a political dissident. In linguistics, he is credited with ushering in the "cognitive revolution," shifting the field away from structural linguistics. He founded the tradition of generative grammar, which models language using explicit, testable rule systems, initially developing transformational grammar. His linguistic theory is based on biolinguistics, arguing that humans share an underlying linguistic structure due to an innate capacity, which he describes as "universal grammar."

Chomsky is also a leading political theorist, often identifying as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist. His political work heavily critiques U.S. imperialism and American foreign policy, asserting that the U.S. seeks to establish economically and politically controlled "open societies." He developed the propaganda model with Edward S. Herman, which dissects how mainstream media reinforces state ideologies through five filters. His activism has led to numerous controversies, including criticism regarding the Bosnian War and his defense of freedom of speech for Holocaust denier Robert Faurisson.
