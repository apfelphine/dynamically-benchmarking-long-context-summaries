## Noam Chomsky – Concise Summary

Noam Chomsky, born in 1928 in Philadelphia to Jewish immigrant parents, is a renowned linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, political activist, and public intellectual. He studied philosophy, linguistics, and logic, earning a PhD from the University of Pennsylvania in 1955 after foundational work on transformational grammar. Chomsky began teaching at MIT in 1955, where he played a key role in establishing the modern field of linguistics, introducing the theory of generative grammar. His work revolutionized language study by arguing for an innate, biological basis for human linguistic ability—what he termed "universal grammar"—and founded the research tradition of generative grammar, including transformational grammar and the minimalist program. He has also made significant contributions to computational linguistics and automata theory, developing the influential Chomsky hierarchy. Chomsky ushered in the "cognitive revolution" and is often cited as the father of modern linguistics and a founder of cognitive science.

Chomsky is equally prominent as a political dissident and writer, advocating anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist ideals throughout his life. He is a sharp critic of U.S. foreign policy, state-corporate power, and the mainstream media, developing the "propaganda model" with Edward S. Herman in "Manufacturing Consent" to analyze how media shapes public perception. He has written extensively about the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and other global issues, maintaining a steadfast commitment to activism, freedom of speech, and intellectual critique. Chomsky is widely recognized for his academic achievements, receiving numerous awards and honorary doctorates worldwide, and his ideas have profoundly influenced linguistics, philosophy, computer science, psychology, and political theory. Even after retiring from MIT in 2002, he continued research, public lectures, and activism. While Chomsky remains a polarizing figure due to his uncompromising analytical stance, his dual legacy as a leading scholar and influential social critic is enduring.
