## Summary of Information on Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky, born in 1928 to Ashkenazi Jewish immigrant parents in Philadelphia, is a renowned linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, historian, social critic, and political activist. Raised in a politically engaged Jewish household, he developed early interests in anarchism and anti-Bolshevik views. After studying philosophy, logic, and languages at the University of Pennsylvania, he shifted his focus to theoretical linguistics under the influence of Zellig Harris. He earned his Ph.D. from Harvard with a dissertation on transformational grammar, which laid the foundation for generative grammar and biolinguistics. His linguistic theory emphasizes an innate, biologically based universal grammar common to all humans, opposing behaviorist views of language acquisition. Chomsky developed transformational grammar and later frameworks like the minimalist program, significantly influencing linguistics, philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and computational linguistics.

Parallel to his academic career, Chomsky became a prominent political dissident and critic of U.S. foreign policy, capitalism, state propaganda, and media manipulation. Identifying as an anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist, he advocates for liberty, community, and democratic control of social and economic institutions. His political activism includes anti-war protests against Vietnam, critiques of Israel and U.S. Middle East policies, defense of free speech even in controversial contexts, and extensive writings on propaganda and imperialism. Despite his academic accolades—including membership in prestigious societies and many honorary degrees—Chomsky remains a polarizing figure due to his outspoken criticism of international power structures. He retired from MIT in 2002 but continued public intellectual work until a severe stroke in 2023, after which he moved to Brazil.
