**Noam Chomsky** was born on December 7, 1928, in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. His early life was influenced by his Ashkenazi Jewish immigrant parents, and he was exposed to leftist and far-left politics through his family and intellectual circles. Academically, Chomsky began at the University of Pennsylvania, later being heavily influenced by linguist Zellig Harris, which led him to pursue theoretical linguistics.

Chomsky is recognized for launching the tradition of **generative grammar** and for his work in **linguistics**, developing concepts such as transformational grammar. Simultaneously, he established himself as a prominent **political dissident**. His political writings heavily critique American imperialism, the role of mass media in propaganda (co-authored with Edward S. Herman in *Manufacturing Consent*), and U.S. foreign policy. Chomsky identifies as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist. His career involved extensive anti-war activism, particularly regarding the Vietnam War, which led to his arrest on multiple occasions. He continued his political critique into later years, addressing issues like the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the nature of capitalism.
