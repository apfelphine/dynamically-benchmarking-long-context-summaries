# Summary: Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky, born in 1928 in Philadelphia to Ashkenazi Jewish immigrant parents, emerged as a pivotal figure in linguistics, philosophy, and political activism. Raised with strong leftist and anarchist influences, he shifted his academic focus to theoretical linguistics under Zellig Harris, developing transformational grammar which revolutionized the study of language. At MIT, Chomsky gained prominence through his critique of behaviorism and foundational works like *Syntactic Structures* (1957). His linguistic theories, particularly universal grammar and generative grammar, emphasize innate cognitive structures enabling language acquisition, challenging prevailing empiricist views.

Concurrently, Chomsky became a vocal critic of U.S. foreign policy, especially the Vietnam War, and broader systems of media propaganda, exemplified by works such as *Manufacturing Consent* (1988). His political stance aligns with anarcho-syndicalism and libertarian socialism, focusing on exposing elite manipulations and advocating democratic participation and social justice. Despite controversies, including defending free speech rights for Holocaust deniers and criticism of Israel, Chomsky remains a highly influential intellectual. He retired from MIT in 2002 but continues active research and political commentary, maintaining significant sway in both academic and activist circles.
