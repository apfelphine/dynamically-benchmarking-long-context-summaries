## Summary on Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky, born in 1928 in Philadelphia to Ashkenazi Jewish immigrant parents, is a pioneering linguist, philosopher, and political dissident. Influenced early on by his politically active family and leftist thinkers, he developed an interest in anarchism and rejected Bolshevik ideology. Educated at the University of Pennsylvania and Harvard, Chomsky revolutionized linguistics with his theory of universal grammar and transformational-generative grammar, emphasizing innate cognitive structures underlying human language acquisition. His critiques of behaviorism and contributions to generative grammar fundamentally reshaped the field, making him a foundational figure in modern linguistics and cognitive science. Politically, Chomsky is known for his anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist views, his outspoken criticism of U.S. foreign policy and imperialism, and his analyses of mass media propaganda, notably coauthoring *Manufacturing Consent*. He has been a prominent anti-war activist, especially against the Vietnam War, and continues to influence political thought and activism globally.

Throughout his career, Chomsky has maintained a strong commitment to freedom of speech and critical inquiry. His extensive writings blend linguistic theory, philosophy of mind, and a demand for political transparency and social justice. Despite controversies—such as defending free speech rights for Holocaust deniers and criticisms of his political stances—he remains one of the most cited and influential intellectuals worldwide. Retiring from MIT in 2002, Chomsky continued active research and activism until a massive stroke in 2023 curtailed his public engagements. His legacy spans linguistics, cognitive science, philosophy, and political activism, marking him as a seminal intellectual figure of the 20th and 21st centuries.
