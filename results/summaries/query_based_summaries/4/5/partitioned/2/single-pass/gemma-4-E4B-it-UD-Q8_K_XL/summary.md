## Noam Chomsky Summary

Noam Chomsky is a figure renowned for his contributions across linguistics, political activism, and social critique. In linguistics, he is credited with launching the "cognitive revolution," establishing the field as a formal science. His work, including *Syntactic Structures* and *Aspects of the Theory of Syntax*, centers on the concept of **universal grammar**, which posits that syntactic knowledge is partially inborn, explaining language acquisition despite the "poverty of the stimulus." He is known for founding the research tradition of **generative grammar**, which uses explicit rule systems to model subconscious grammatical knowledge.

Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a prominent political dissident, generally identifying as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist. His sociopolitical analysis critiques U.S. imperialism and the influence of elite corporatocracies on political realities, famously outlining the **propaganda model** with Edward S. Herman to explain how mainstream media reinforces state policies. He has been a consistent critic of U.S. foreign policy, the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and capitalism. Chomsky is recognized as a defining Western intellectual figure with a dual legacy in both academia and political dissent.
