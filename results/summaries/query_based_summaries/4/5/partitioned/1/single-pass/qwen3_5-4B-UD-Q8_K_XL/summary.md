Noam Chomsky is a renowned linguist, philosopher, and political activist known for founding the cognitive revolution in linguistics and proposing the theory of universal grammar, which posits that humans possess an innate biological capacity for language acquisition. His work on generative grammar revolutionized the scientific study of language by arguing that language is a unique evolutionary development distinct from animal communication, contrasting with behaviorist views that emphasize learning. Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a prominent political dissident who identifies as an anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist. He is a fierce critic of American imperialism, corporate media, and the global elite, famously articulating the "propaganda model" which suggests that mainstream media reinforces state and corporate interests while marginalizing dissenting viewpoints.

Throughout his career, Chomsky has engaged in extensive anti-war activism, particularly against U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War, the Iraq War, and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. He is well-known for defending the rights of refugees and criticizing authoritarian regimes, including the defense of Robert Faurisson's right to advocate Holocaust denial despite the resulting controversy in France. While he has faced significant criticism and censorship from conservative groups and the mainstream press, often labeling him an enemy of the United States, Chomsky maintains that his role is to expose manipulations of truth by powerful institutions. He retired from MIT in 2002 but continued his academic and activist work until a stroke in 2023 limited his mobility, after which he resided primarily in Brazil.
