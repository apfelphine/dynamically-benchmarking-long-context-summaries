Noam Chomsky is a prominent linguist, cognitive scientist, and political dissident known for his contributions to generative grammar and the cognitive revolution in linguistics. His linguistic theories, including the Chomsky hierarchy and universal grammar, laid the foundation for modern linguistics and influenced fields such as computer science and cognitive science. A staunch critic of U.S. foreign policy, he argues that the U.S. promotes a system of global dominance through military and economic means, and he has been a vocal advocate for democratic reform and transparency. Chomsky is also known for his work on media propaganda, particularly through the "propaganda model" co-developed with Edward S. Herman, which critiques the role of corporate media in shaping public perception. He has faced significant criticism and controversy, including accusations of antisemitism and Holocaust denial, though he maintains that freedom of speech must extend to all viewpoints, even those considered extreme. Chomsky's political activism, including support for movements in Palestine, anti-war efforts, and critiques of U.S. imperialism, has made him a central figure in global anti-imperialist discourse. Despite being marginalized by mainstream media, his work is widely cited and influential across disciplines, and he is regarded as one of the most important intellectuals of the 20th and 21st centuries.
