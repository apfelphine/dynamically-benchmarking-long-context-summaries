# Summary of Information on Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky (born December 7, 1928) is a prominent linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, historian, social critic, and political activist. Raised in a Jewish immigrant family with leftist and socialist influences, he became a leading figure in linguistics by developing transformational grammar and the theory of universal grammar, emphasizing innate cognitive structures underlying language acquisition. He is often called the "father of modern linguistics" and has significantly influenced cognitive science, computer science (notably automata theory and formal languages), and philosophy of language.

Chomsky's political activism is marked by outspoken criticism of U.S. foreign policy, media propaganda, capitalism, Israeli policies toward Palestinians, and various social injustices. He has advocated for anarcho-syndicalism and libertarian socialism, emphasizing direct worker control and democratic participation. His work with Edward S. Herman produced the propaganda model in *Manufacturing Consent*, exposing structural media biases. Despite controversy—such as his defense of free speech rights even for Holocaust deniers and criticism of mainstream media—Chomsky remains a highly cited intellectual and a respected figure in both academia and political dissent. He retired from MIT in 2002 and divides his time between the United States and Brazil. In 2023, after suffering a stroke, he moved permanently to Brazil.
