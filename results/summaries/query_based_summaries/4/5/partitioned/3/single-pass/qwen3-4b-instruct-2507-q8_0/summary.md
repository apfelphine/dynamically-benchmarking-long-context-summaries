Noam Chomsky is a prominent linguist, political activist, and intellectual whose work spans linguistics, philosophy, and political criticism. He is widely recognized as a key figure in the development of generative grammar, which posits that humans possess an innate, biologically based linguistic capacity (universal grammar) that enables them to acquire and produce language. His linguistic theories, rooted in nativism and rationalism, contrast with behaviorist views and emphasize the cognitive structures common to all humans. Chomsky's work has profoundly influenced cognitive science, computer science, and philosophy, earning him a status as one of the most cited scholars in the humanities.

Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a leading political dissident, known for his critical analysis of U.S. foreign policy, media manipulation, and global power structures. He argues that mainstream media operates under a "propaganda model" that suppresses dissenting viewpoints and serves elite interests, promoting a distorted reality. His political activism centers on exposing state and corporate misconduct, advocating for democratic reform, and promoting critical thinking as a tool for social change. He has been a vocal critic of U.S. military interventions, the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and economic inequality, often emphasizing the need for grassroots movements and public education to counteract systemic misinformation.

Chomsky's personal life includes a long-standing marriage to Carol Doris Schatz (later Valeria Wasserman), and he has lived in both the U.S. and Brazil. He retired from MIT in 2002 but remains active in public discourse, teaching and giving lectures. In 2023, he suffered a stroke and moved to Brazil full-time, where he continues to follow current events and engage in public intellectual work. Despite facing criticism and controversy—such as his defense of Holocaust denial advocate Robert Faurisson and accusations of being a "traitor to the Jewish people"—Chomsky maintains that his commitment to free speech and truth-telling is central to his intellectual and ethical stance. His influence extends widely, with his ideas shaping movements and debates in politics, education, and media.
