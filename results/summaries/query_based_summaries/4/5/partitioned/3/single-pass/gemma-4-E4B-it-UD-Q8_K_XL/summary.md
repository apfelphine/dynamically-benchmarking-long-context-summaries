**Noam Chomsky** is known for his dual legacy as a leader in linguistics and a prominent figure in political dissent. Linguistically, he is credited with inaugurating the "cognitive revolution," establishing linguistics as a formal, natural science. His work, particularly *Syntactic Structures*, revolutionized the field and is central to cognitive science, computer science, and philosophy. His linguistic theory is based on biolinguistics and the concept of Universal Grammar, which posits that humans share an innate linguistic structure, rejecting radical behaviorism.

In politics, Chomsky is a prominent dissident, typically identifying as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist. His sociopolitical analysis heavily critiques American imperialism, U.S. foreign policy, and the role of mass media in reinforcing state ideology, as detailed in his work with Edward S. Herman. He has been a vocal critic of capitalism and U.S. involvement in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, viewing it as a settler colony situation. Despite his academic success, his political activism has led to his being viewed as "on the outer margin of acceptability" by mainstream media.
