Noam Chomsky is a prominent linguist, philosopher, political activist, and critic whose work spans linguistics, cognitive science, and political theory. Born in 1928 in Philadelphia, he developed an early interest in linguistics under the influence of Zellig Harris, leading to his groundbreaking contributions to generative grammar, particularly transformational grammar, which revolutionized the study of language. His theory posits that language is biologically innate, with universal grammar providing a shared cognitive structure across all human languages, challenging behaviorist views that language is purely learned.

Chomsky’s linguistic theories, including his critique of B. F. Skinner’s behaviorism and his work on universal grammar, have profoundly influenced linguistics, cognitive science, and computer science. His ideas on syntax, semantics, and the nature of language competence remain foundational in these fields. His 1957 book *Syntactic Structures* is often credited with launching the modern field of linguistics.

Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a vocal political dissident, known for his critiques of U.S. foreign policy, corporate power, and media manipulation. His writings, such as *Manufacturing Consent* (1988), analyze how mainstream media shapes public perception through a propaganda model that suppresses dissenting views. He has been a prominent critic of U.S. imperialism, advocating for democratic control of economic and political systems. His views align with libertarian socialist and anarcho-syndicalist ideals, emphasizing worker autonomy, social justice, and opposition to authoritarian structures.

Chomsky’s activism has made him a target of criticism, particularly for his sharp critiques of U.S. policies, including the Vietnam War, Israeli occupation of Palestine, and global economic inequality. His political writings have been widely influential among anti-war and anti-capitalist movements, earning him recognition as a leading voice in critical social and political thought. Despite his academic achievements and intellectual impact, his political views have also drawn controversy, positioning him as a polarizing figure in both academic and public discourse.
