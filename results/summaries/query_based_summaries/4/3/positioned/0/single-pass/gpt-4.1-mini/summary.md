**Garry Kasparov** was born in Baku, Soviet Union, with a Jewish father and Armenian mother. Adopting his mother's surname at age 12, he began focused chess training at seven and rapidly rose through Soviet and international ranks, becoming the youngest-ever World No. 1 in 1984 and winning the World Chess Championship in 1985 at 22, the youngest at that time. His intense rivalry with Anatoly Karpov defined the era, encompassing five world championship matches. Kasparov broke from FIDE in 1993 to form the Professional Chess Association, leading to a 13-year split in world champion titles. He lost the classical title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000 but remained a dominant player until retiring from regular competition in 2005.

Post-retirement, Kasparov remained active in chess through exhibitions, coaching (notably Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura), online communities, and chess education. His legacy includes significant contributions to opening theory, a record-long tenure as world No. 1 player, and a dynamic, attacking playing style. Beyond chess, Kasparov has been deeply involved in Russian and international politics as a pro-democracy activist and vocal critic of Vladimir Putin, leading opposition movements and living in exile since 2013. He has authored extensive chess literature and political writings, maintaining a prominent role as both a chess legend and a political figure.
