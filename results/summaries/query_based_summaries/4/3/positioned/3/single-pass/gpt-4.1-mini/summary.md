## Summary of Information on Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR, has Jewish and Armenian heritage. He adopted his mother’s surname at age 12. Kasparov began serious chess study early, trained under Vladimir Makogonov and later Alexander Shakarov, won the Soviet Junior Championship twice as a teenager, and quickly rose through ranks to become the youngest-ever world No. 1 in 1984. He became world chess champion in 1985 after a controversial final match against Anatoly Karpov and successfully defended his title in subsequent matches until he lost it to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000. Kasparov played a dominant role in international chess for over two decades, holding the No. 1 rating record for 255 months and earning numerous tournament victories and accolades.

Kasparov’s playing style is noted for its dynamic, tactical depth, extensive opening preparation (including innovative use of computer analysis), and aggressive play, influenced by chess greats such as Alekhine and Tal. He made significant contributions to opening theory and has been involved in high-profile matches against chess computers, notably Deep Blue. Post-retirement from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov has engaged in coaching (notably Magnus Carlsen), exhibition matches, and has been active politically, especially as an opposition figure in Russia. He has written extensively on chess history and theory, including his multi-volume series *My Great Predecessors* and books linking chess with decision-making. Kasparov also launched an online chess platform and has participated in public discourse on politics and artificial intelligence.
