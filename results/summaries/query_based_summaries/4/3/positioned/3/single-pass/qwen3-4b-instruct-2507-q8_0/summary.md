Garry Kasparov was born in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR (now Azerbaijan), Soviet Union, to a Jewish family. His father, Kim Moiseyevich Weinstein, died of leukemia when Kasparov was seven years old, and he adopted his mother's surname, Kasparov, at age 12. He began studying chess at age seven after encountering a problem set up by his parents and proposed a solution. He trained under Mikhail Botvinnik's school and won the Soviet Junior Championship at age 13. Kasparov rose rapidly in the chess world, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 in 1984 at age 22, and went on to win the world championship in 1985 against Anatoly Karpov. He held the title until 2000, losing it to Vladimir Kramnik in a match that ended in a controversial draw. 

Kasparov became a central figure in chess, known for his dynamic playing style, deep opening preparation, and aggressive tactics. He made significant contributions to opening theory, including co-authoring several books and developing the "Kasparov Gambit" in the Sicilian Defense. He also developed the "Kasparov versus the World" game in 1999, where he played against over 50,000 people globally, which he described as "the greatest game in the history of chess." 

After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov remained active in chess, coaching Magnus Carlsen and participating in various exhibitions and rapid games. He also launched Kasparov Chess, a subscription-based online community. Beyond chess, he became a prominent political activist, criticizing U.S. foreign policy, the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and authoritarian regimes. He founded the United Civil Front in Russia to promote democratic reforms and was a vocal opponent of Vladimir Putin, eventually fleeing Russia in 2013 due to political persecution. 

Kasparov authored numerous books, including the "My Great Predecessors" series, which examines the careers of past world champions, and "Winter Is Coming," a political book advocating resistance to authoritarianism. He has been recognized as one of the most influential chess players and intellectuals of the 20th century, with a legacy that extends into politics, education, and global activism.
