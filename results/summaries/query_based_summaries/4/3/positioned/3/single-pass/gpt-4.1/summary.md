## Garry Kasparov: Key Facts & Contributions

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in 1963 in Baku, USSR, is widely regarded as one of the greatest chess players in history. He began studying chess in early childhood, trained at Mikhail Botvinnik's chess school, and quickly rose through ranks, becoming the youngest-ever USSR Championship qualifier at age 15. Kasparov became world champion in 1985 at the age of 22 after defeating Anatoly Karpov and held the title until 2000. Known for his dynamic playing style, deep opening preparation, and aggressive tactics, Kasparov dominated the world rankings from 1985 to 2005, holding the No. 1 rating longer than any other player. He won or tied for first in most tournaments he entered between 1981 and 1991 and set records for consecutive professional tournament victories and highest Elo rating, reaching 2851 in 1999. Kasparov played a pivotal role in championing computer chess, famously losing a match against IBM's Deep Blue in 1997.

Kasparov retired from professional chess in 2005, later engaging in exhibitions, coaching rising stars like Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura, and contributing significantly to chess literature, including his renowned *My Great Predecessors* series. He has written several books on chess strategy and decision-making. Beyond chess, Kasparov has been active in Russian politics, founding the United Civil Front and participating in opposition movements against Vladimir Putin. He has advocated for democracy and human rights, enduring harassment and ultimately going into exile. Kasparov has Croatian citizenship, lives in New York City, and is known for outspoken views on international affairs. He has received numerous awards, including eleven Chess Oscars, and continues to influence chess and global politics through his writing, activism, and speaking engagements.
