**Garry Kasparov** was born Garik Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan, to a Jewish father and Armenian mother. After his father's death at age seven, he adopted his mother's surname, Kasparov, and later fled anti-Armenian pogroms in Baku in 1990. A prodigy, he began chess training at age seven and achieved the rank of Grandmaster at 19, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 in 1984. He won the world championship title in 1985 at age 22, a record he held for over a decade. Kasparov is renowned for his dynamic playing style, influenced by Alekhine, Tal, and Fischer, and for his extensive opening theory contributions. His rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, known as the "two Ks," was one of the greatest in chess history, resulting in five world championship matches comprising 144 games.

Following a major break with FIDE in 1993 due to disagreements over the organization of world championships, Kasparov became the leader of the Professional Chess Association (PCA). After losing the title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000, he retired from competitive chess in 2005 to focus on writing (including the *My Great Predecessors* series) and political activism. He became a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin's government, opposing the 2014 annexation of Crimea and the invasion of Ukraine. Kasparov held various political positions, including a failed candidacy for the Russian presidency in 2008 and a run for FIDE president in 2013. Post-retirement, he has remained active in chess through coaching, exhibitions, and the launch of the Kasparov Chess online community.
