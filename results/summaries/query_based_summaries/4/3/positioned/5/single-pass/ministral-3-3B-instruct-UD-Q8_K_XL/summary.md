Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein, is a legendary chess player renowned for his extraordinary talent and dominance in the game. Born in Baku, Azerbaijan, in 1963, he adopted his mother’s surname, Kasparov, at age 12. His early exposure to chess began with his parents' challenge, and he quickly developed a deep passion for the game under the guidance of his coaches, including Vladimir Makogonov and Alexander Shakarov.

Kasparov rose through the ranks rapidly, becoming a Grandmaster at age 17 and the youngest-ever world champion at 22 in 1985, defeating Anatoly Karpov in a historic match. His rivalry with Karpov, often called the "Two Ks," defined an era in chess history. Kasparov was celebrated for his dynamic, tactical brilliance, innovative opening ideas, and unmatched strategic depth. His contributions to chess theory, including the development of new openings and variations, cemented his legacy as one of the greatest players of all time.

Beyond chess, Kasparov became a prominent political figure, particularly in Russia. Initially a member of the Communist Party, he later became a vocal critic of authoritarian regimes, advocating for democracy and human rights. His political activism often placed him in conflict with Russian authorities, leading to arrests, harassment, and eventual exile from Russia in 2013. Kasparov has been a vocal opponent of Vladimir Putin’s regime, frequently speaking out against human rights abuses and political repression.

Post-retirement from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov has remained active in chess education, publishing books, giving lectures, and engaging in public debates. His writings, such as *My Great Predecessors* and *Winter Is Coming*, blend chess analysis with broader reflections on history, politics, and society. Kasparov’s intellectual rigor, advocacy for democratic values, and enduring influence in both chess and politics underscore his status as a multifaceted intellectual and a key figure in modern discourse.
