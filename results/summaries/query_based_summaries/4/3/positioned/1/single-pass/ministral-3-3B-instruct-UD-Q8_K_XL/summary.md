Garry Kasparov, born **Garik Kimovich Weinstein**, is one of the greatest chess players in history, widely regarded as the most dominant in the game’s modern era. Born in **Baku, Azerbaijan (Soviet Union)**, he adopted his mother’s surname, **Kasparov**, at age 12. His early chess career was marked by rapid ascension, culminating in his title as **World Chess Champion** in 1985 at age 22, the youngest ever at the time. Kasparov’s rivalry with Anatoly Karpov defined the late 20th century, with five championship matches producing a record 21 wins, 19 losses, and 104 draws in 144 games.

Kasparov’s playing style was characterized by **dynamic aggression, deep strategic insight, and exceptional opening preparation**, often compared to the attacking brilliance of Alekhine. His contributions to chess theory included pioneering work on openings like the **Scotch Game** and the **Kasparov Gambit** in the Sicilian Defense. He held the record for the longest time as the **No. 1 ranked player in the world** (1984–2005) and was awarded the **Chess Oscar** eleven times for his annual excellence in the game.

Beyond chess, Kasparov became a prominent political figure, particularly in Russia. Initially a supporter of perestroika, he later became a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin’s regime, advocating for democratic reforms. His activism led to his exile from Russia in 2013, where he continues to engage in political commentary and advocacy. Kasparov’s intellectual breadth extends to economics, philosophy, and technology, though his primary focus remains chess and political dissent. His legacy endures as both a chess legend and a symbol of resistance against authoritarianism.
