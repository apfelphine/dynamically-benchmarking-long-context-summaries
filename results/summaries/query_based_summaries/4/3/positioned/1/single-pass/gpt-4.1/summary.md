## Garry Kasparov: Summary

Garry Kasparov was born in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR, to Armenian and Jewish parents. Following his father's death when he was seven, Kasparov adopted his mother's surname and fled Baku during anti-Armenian violence in 1990. His early chess development was guided at the Young Pioneer Palace and Botvinnik's school, leading to Soviet Junior Championship victories in 1976 and 1977. By age 13, he was a chess prodigy, continuing his rapid ascent by winning major tournaments and becoming the youngest-ever participant in the USSR Chess Championship at 15.

Kasparov's competitive career is marked by his fierce rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, culminating in five World Chess Championship matches (1984–1990). He became the youngest world champion at 22 by defeating Karpov in 1985 and retained the title through several close rematches. In 1993, Kasparov split from FIDE with Nigel Short, becoming PCA world champion, and held the title until his defeat by Kramnik in 2000. Kasparov dominated tournament chess, holding the world No. 1 rating from 1984 to 2005 and setting records for consecutive tournament victories and highest Elo rating (2851, held until 2013). Renowned for dynamic, aggressive play and deep opening preparation, Kasparov significantly expanded chess opening theory and pioneered using computers in analysis. He notably faced IBM's Deep Blue in two high-profile matches, losing the second in 1997, and interacted extensively with computer chess programs.

After retiring from regular competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov remained active through exhibitions, coaching top players, authoring critically acclaimed chess literature (including the "My Great Predecessors" and "Modern Chess" series), and contributing to chess education and politics. He played in international team events, launched online chess platforms, and ran for FIDE presidency, though unsuccessfully. Kasparov's legacy includes being widely recognized as one of the greatest chess players in history, serving as an influential figure in chess, and maintaining a prominent public profile in political activism and writing.
