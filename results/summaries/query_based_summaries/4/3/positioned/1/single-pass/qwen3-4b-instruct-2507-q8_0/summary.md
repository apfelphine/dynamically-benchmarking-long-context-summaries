Garry Kasparov was born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR (now Azerbaijan), Soviet Union. His father, Kim Moiseyevich Weinstein, was Jewish, and his mother, Klara Shagenovna Kasparov, was Armenian. After his father's death from leukemia at age seven, Kasparov adopted his mother's surname. He began studying chess at age seven, attending the Young Pioneer Palace in Baku and later training under Mikhail Botvinnik. Kasparov won the Soviet Junior Championship in 1976 and 1977, and by age 19, had become the youngest Candidate since Bobby Fischer.

Kasparov rose rapidly through the chess world, winning the 1982 Moscow Interzonal and defeating Alexander Beliavsky in his first Candidates match. He became world No. 1 in 1984 and defeated Anatoly Karpov in the 1985 World Chess Championship, becoming the youngest-ever world champion at age 22. He won five world championship matches against Karpov, and in 1990, he defeated Vladimir Kramnik in a match that ended with a 12½–11½ score.

After losing the title in 2000 to Kramnik, Kasparov remained the top-rated player in the world and continued to compete in major tournaments. He retired from competitive chess in 2005, citing a lack of personal goals and frustration over the failure to reunite the world championship. He remained active in chess through coaching, including working with Magnus Carlsen, and participated in numerous exhibitions and blitz events.

Kasparov became a prominent political figure, criticizing Russian President Vladimir Putin and advocating for democratic reforms. He founded the United Civil Front and was instrumental in setting up the anti-Putin movement "Other Russia." He was arrested and beaten in 2012 during a protest related to the Pussy Riot case and later fled Russia in 2013 due to fears of persecution. He has been vocal about issues such as the annexation of Crimea, the invasion of Ukraine, and the treatment of minorities.

In addition to chess, Kasparov authored numerous books, including the "My Great Predecessors" series, and wrote extensively on decision-making, philosophy, and politics. He launched Kasparov Chess, a subscription-based online community, and contributed to media and public discourse through interviews and public appearances. He has been recognized for his contributions to chess, politics, and intellectual thought, and remains a significant figure in both chess and global political activism.
