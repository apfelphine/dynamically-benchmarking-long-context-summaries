# Summary: Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku to a Jewish father and Armenian mother, is a former world chess champion and political activist. Beginning serious chess study at age seven, he quickly rose through the ranks, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 in 1984 and youngest world champion in 1985 by defeating Anatoly Karpov after a highly contested and controversial match. Kasparov held the world title through multiple tough matches with Karpov and later defended his title under the Professional Chess Association (PCA) after breaking with FIDE in 1993. He lost the classical world championship to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000 and officially retired from regular competition in 2005, though he continued playing in exhibition events and coaching top players like Magnus Carlsen.

Kasparov’s playing style was dynamic and aggressive, with major contributions to opening theory, notably popularizing and innovating in lines such as the Scotch Game and Sicilian Defense variations. His peak Elo rating of 2851 stood as a record for over a decade. Beyond chess, Kasparov has been a vocal political figure, opposing Vladimir Putin's regime and advocating for democracy and human rights in Russia. He ran for FIDE presidency in 2013 but lost amid controversies. Kasparov has written extensively on chess, politics, and historical revisionism, and has lived in New York since 2013. He holds Croatian citizenship and has been active in international human rights and political discourse.
