# Summary: Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Soviet Union, is a chess grandmaster of Jewish and Armenian descent who adopted his mother's surname at twelve. Starting serious chess study young, he trained under Vladimir Makogonov and later Alexander Shakarov, winning the Soviet Junior Championship twice by age 14. He rapidly rose through the ranks, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 player in 1984 and then the youngest world chess champion in 1985 by defeating Anatoly Karpov. Kasparov's five world championship matches against Karpov are legendary, culminating in his long reign at the top of the chess world until his 2000 loss to Vladimir Kramnik.

Kasparov had repeated conflicts with FIDE, leading to his breakaway formation of the Professional Chess Association (PCA) and dual world champions for 13 years. After retiring from professional chess in 2005, he remained active in the chess community by playing exhibition matches, coaching top players like Magnus Carlsen, and promoting chess education. Kasparov also engaged with chess computers, playing notable matches against Deep Blue and other programs. In 2013, he ran unsuccessfully for FIDE presidency.

Politically, Kasparov shifted to opposition activism after retirement, founding movements like the United Civil Front to promote democracy in Russia and denouncing Vladimir Putin’s authoritarianism. His political activism led to harassment and exile in 2013. Kasparov has been a vocal critic of Putin’s regime, the annexation of Crimea, Russian repression of dissent, and Russian aggression in Ukraine, advocating for Western sanctions and opposition to authoritarianism. He holds Croatian citizenship since 2014, lives in New York, and remains influential as a writer and commentator on chess, politics, and history. Kasparov has published extensively on chess, including the acclaimed *My Great Predecessors* series and political works like *Winter Is Coming*, which calls for mobilizing democracy against Putin’s Russia.
