## Garry Kasparov – Concise Summary

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Soviet Union, is recognized as one of the greatest chess players in history. Rising through the ranks from a prodigious youth who trained at Mikhail Botvinnik’s chess school and won the Soviet Junior Championship, Kasparov became the youngest-ever World Chess Champion in 1985 at age 22 after defeating Anatoly Karpov. He dominated world chess for two decades, holding the No. 1 ranking from 1984 to 2005 and winning the Chess Oscar eleven times. His dynamic, tactically aggressive style and profound opening preparation set new standards in the sport, and he innovated with computer-assisted analysis, notably facing IBM’s Deep Blue in a famous pair of matches.

Kasparov’s career extended beyond the board: he broke with FIDE in 1993 over governance disputes, organized alternative championships, and contributed heavily to chess theory and education. He has written numerous influential books, including the acclaimed "My Great Predecessors" series. Post-retirement, Kasparov has remained active in public chess through exhibitions, coaching (notably Magnus Carlsen), and launching online platforms. He has also become a prominent political activist, especially advocating for democracy and human rights in Russia and internationally, and is a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin’s regime. Kasparov holds Croatian citizenship and resides in New York City.
