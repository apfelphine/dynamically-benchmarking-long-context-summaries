Garry Kasparov was born in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR (now Azerbaijan), Soviet Union, to a Jewish father and an Armenian mother. After his father's death at age seven, he adopted his mother's surname, Kasparov. At age 12, he began training at Mikhail Botvinnik's chess school and quickly rose through the ranks, winning the Soviet Junior Championship at age 13. He became a Grandmaster at age 15 and went on to win numerous tournaments and titles, including becoming the youngest-ever world chess champion at age 22 in 1985.

Kasparov's career was marked by intense rivalries with Anatoly Karpov, with whom he played five world championship matches, winning 21 games, losing 19, and drawing 104. His rivalry with Karpov, which spanned over a decade, became one of the most significant in chess history. In 1985, he defeated Karpov in a match that included a famous game where he won with a "Sicilian Defence" move, considered one of the greatest games in chess history.

In addition to his chess achievements, Kasparov was deeply involved in politics and activism. After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, he turned to political activism, co-founding the United Civil Front to promote democracy in Russia. He was instrumental in organizing protests against Vladimir Putin and was arrested and beaten during one such demonstration. In 2008, he ran as a presidential candidate in Russia, though he withdrew due to difficulties in securing a meeting hall for supporters.

Kasparov has been a vocal critic of authoritarianism and Russian policies, particularly regarding the annexation of Crimea and the war in Ukraine. He has also advocated for democratic reforms and human rights, and has been a prominent figure in international human rights movements. He has supported various causes, including opposition to the use of nuclear weapons and the promotion of peace in the Middle East.

Beyond chess, Kasparov has written extensively, publishing a series of books on chess, including the "My Great Predecessors" series, which covers the lives and careers of previous world champions. He has also authored works on decision-making and innovation, such as "How Life Imitates Chess," and has been involved in promoting chess education globally.

In 2005, he retired from competitive chess, citing a lack of personal goals in the game and a desire to focus on politics and social issues. Since then, he has remained active in public life, including coaching young players like Magnus Carlsen and participating in international peace efforts. He has also launched Kasparov Chess, a subscription-based online platform for chess education and community engagement.

Kasparov is widely regarded as one of the greatest chess players of all time, and his influence extends beyond the board into politics, education, and social activism. His legacy includes not only his numerous titles and victories but also his ongoing commitment to promoting democracy, peace, and human rights.
