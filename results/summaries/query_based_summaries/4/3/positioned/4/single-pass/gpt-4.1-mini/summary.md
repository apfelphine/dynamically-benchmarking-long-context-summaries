**Summary of Information Regarding Garry Kasparov**

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Soviet Union, has a mixed Armenian and Jewish heritage but identifies culturally as Russian. He began focusing on chess seriously at a young age and rapidly ascended through competitive ranks, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 player by 1984. He won the World Chess Championship in 1985 at age 22, becoming the youngest champion, and famously rivaled Anatoly Karpov in a series of five world championship matches. Kasparov was dominant in the chess world for over two decades, holding the No. 1 ranking from 1984 to 2005 and achieving a peak Elo rating of 2851. After disputes with FIDE, he led a breakaway organization, the Professional Chess Association, resulting in split world championship titles. He eventually lost the classical title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000 and retired from regular competition in 2005 but continued to play in exhibition events, train players like Magnus Carlsen, and contribute to chess education.

Kasparov’s playing style is characterized by aggressive tactics, deep preparation, and innovative opening theory contributions, including reviving older openings and creating new variations (e.g., Kasparov Gambit). He has also embraced computer chess, competing against and training with various chess engines, most notably playing two historic matches against IBM’s Deep Blue. Post-retirement, Kasparov founded Kasparov Chess, an online chess platform, and remained active in promoting chess worldwide.

Politically, Kasparov’s early connection was with the Soviet Communist Party, which he left in 1990, thereafter opposing Vladimir Putin’s administration. He founded and led several pro-democracy movements in Russia and ran unsuccessfully for president in 2008 amid restrictive conditions. Kasparov faced harassment and censorship under Putin’s regime and eventually went into exile in 2013, living in New York City since. He remains an outspoken critic of Putin’s government and Russia’s actions, including the annexation of Crimea and the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, advocating for sanctions and political change in Russia. Kasparov has also criticized authoritarianism globally and spoken on international issues including Armenia’s Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, the Yugoslav Wars, and human rights abuses. He has written extensively, including his multi-volume *My Great Predecessors*, memoirs, and political commentary such as the 2015 book *Winter Is Coming*, warning against Putin’s threat to global democracy.

Kasparov’s personal life includes three marriages and children from each; his wife manages his worldwide business affairs. He holds Croatian citizenship and divides his time among the U.S. and Europe.
