Garry Kasparov, born **Garik Kimovich Weinstein**, was a Soviet and Russian chess prodigy who rose to become one of the greatest chess players in history. Adopting his mother’s surname, Kasparov began serious chess training at age 10 under Vladimir Makogonov and later under Mikhail Botvinnik. By age 12, he was already a rising star, winning the Soviet Junior Championship in 1976 and 1977. His breakthrough came in 1978, when he won the Sokolsky Memorial in Minsk, earning a provisional rating of 2545 and securing his place in the world’s elite.

Kasparov’s chess career was defined by his dominance, with a record of **11 Chess Oscars** (best chess player of the year) and a peak rating of **2851** (the highest in history until Magnus Carlsen surpassed it in 2013). He won the **World Chess Championship five times**, first in 1985 at age 22, making him the youngest world champion in history. His rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, often called the "two Ks," was one of the most intense in chess history, spanning five championship matches (1984–1990). Kasparov’s playing style was dynamic, tactical, and deeply strategic, with a focus on opening preparation and positional mastery.

Beyond chess, Kasparov became a vocal critic of Soviet and Russian politics, particularly under Vladimir Putin. He co-founded the **Grandmasters Association (GMA)** in 1986 to advocate for better treatment of professional chess players, though his break with FIDE in 1993 led to a split in the world championship. After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov shifted his focus to politics, founding the **United Civil Front** and later **The Other Russia**, a coalition opposing Putin’s regime. He was arrested multiple times for political activism and fled Russia in 2013 due to fear of persecution. Kasparov remains a prominent figure in chess education, publishing books like *My Great Predecessors* and *Winter Is Coming*, and continues to advocate for democratic values and human rights.
