Garry Kimovich Weinstein, born in Baku to Jewish and Armenian parents, adopted the surname Kasparov at age twelve after his father's death from leukaemia. A prodigy who began training at Mikhail Botvinnik's school, he achieved rapid success, winning Soviet junior championships at 13 and becoming a Grandmaster at 19. Kasparov broke records by becoming the youngest-ever World No. 1 in 1984 and the youngest world champion in 1985, defeating Anatoly Karpov in a match that was controversially terminated without a result. He retained the title in four subsequent matches, defeating Karpov 12½–11½ in 1985, 1987, 1988, and 1990, and famously defeated the computer Deep Blue in the first match in 1996 before losing the rematch in 1997.

Following his retirement in 2005, Kasparov remained the world's top-rated player and engaged in a prolonged split with FIDE over governance issues, eventually forming the Professional Chess Association (PCA) and winning the PCA title in 1993. He later lost the classical world championship to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000 but maintained his status as the highest-rated player. Beyond chess, Kasparov became a prominent political figure, opposing Vladimir Putin and advocating for democracy in Russia; he fled the country in 2013 and continued to campaign for Ukrainian admission to NATO and recognition of the Armenian genocide. He has also contributed to computer science, served as a trainer for Magnus Carlsen, and launched digital initiatives including an NFT collection and an online chess community.
