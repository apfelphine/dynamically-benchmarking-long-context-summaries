## Summary of Information about Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, originally born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Soviet Union, became the youngest-ever World Chess Champion at age 22 by defeating Anatoly Karpov in 1985. He rose rapidly through the chess ranks in his youth, winning multiple Soviet and international championships, and maintained the world No. 1 ranking from 1984 until his retirement in 2005. Kasparov was notable for his dynamic, aggressive playing style, deep opening preparation—often enhanced by computer analysis—and his historic rivalry with Karpov, who he faced in five intense world championship matches. Kasparov was also a pioneer in integrating computer technology into chess preparation. Post-retirement, he has remained active in chess through exhibition matches, coaching (notably Magnus Carlsen briefly), and founding the online platform Kasparov Chess.

Kasparov transitioned to political activism following his retirement, becoming a prominent critic of Vladimir Putin's regime in Russia. He founded the United Civil Front and played a leadership role in opposing authoritarianism, often facing harassment and threats, leading to his exile in 2013. Kasparov has been outspoken on international issues, endorsing Ukrainian sovereignty, condemning Russian aggression, and advocating for democratic reforms globally. He acquired Croatian citizenship and resides in New York City. Kasparov has authored numerous chess books, including the acclaimed five-volume *My Great Predecessors* series, as well as political commentary such as *Winter Is Coming*. He notably played historic matches against chess computers, including Deep Blue, symbolizing the human-machine rivalry in chess. Kasparov also ran unsuccessfully for FIDE presidency in 2014 and remains influential in chess and political spheres worldwide.
