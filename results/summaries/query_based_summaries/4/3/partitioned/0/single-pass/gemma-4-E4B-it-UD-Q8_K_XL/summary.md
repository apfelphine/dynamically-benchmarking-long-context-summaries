# Garry Kasparov

**Early Life and Career Ascent**
Garry Kasparov was born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR. He adopted the surname Kasparov at age twelve. Kasparov began serious chess study at age ten under coach Vladimir Makogonov and won the Soviet Junior Championship in Tbilisi in 1976 and 1977. He quickly rose through the ranks, winning the World Junior Chess Championship in Dortmund in 1980 and becoming a Grandmaster that same year. He achieved the No. 1 world ranking in January 1984, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1.

**World Championship and FIDE Break**
Kasparov secured the world crown in the 1985 rematch against Anatoly Karpov with a score of 13–11. He defended his title against Karpov in 1986 and retained it against Karpov again in 1987. After establishing the Grandmasters Association (GMA) in 1986, tensions grew with FIDE. In 1993, Kasparov and challenger Nigel Short played outside FIDE's jurisdiction, resulting in their ejection from FIDE. He subsequently won his PCA title in 1993, leading to a split in the FIDE World Championship lineage.

**Later Career and Politics**
Kasparov lost the world title to Viswanathan Anand in 1995. He ultimately lost the title to Vladimir Kramnik in a 2000 match in London. After retiring from regular competitive chess in March 2005, Kasparov became politically outspoken, notably opposing Vladimir Putin's regime from exile. His political stances include advocating for Armenian independence in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict and criticizing U.S. foreign policies.
