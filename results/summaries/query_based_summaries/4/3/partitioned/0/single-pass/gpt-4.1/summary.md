## Garry Kasparov: Key Information

Garry Kasparov is a renowned chess grandmaster, former world champion, prolific chess author, and political activist. He rose to prominence for his dynamic, aggressive chess style, extensive opening preparation, and tactical brilliance. Kasparov became world chess champion in 1985 and remained the world’s top-rated player from 1984 to 2005, holding the record for the longest cumulative period as world number one and for reaching the highest Elo rating (2851) until surpassed by Magnus Carlsen. He won numerous tournaments and Olympiad team events, and his rivalry with Anatoly Karpov is regarded as one of chess history’s greatest.

Kasparov made significant contributions to chess theory, especially in opening preparation, systematizing analysis with computers. He played historic matches against IBM’s Deep Blue, becoming the first world champion to be defeated by a computer under tournament conditions in 1997. After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov continued to participate in exhibitions, coach leading players, and write influential books (including the "My Great Predecessors" and "Modern Chess" series). He has also been actively involved in promoting chess education and founded Kasparov Chess, an online chess community. Outside chess, Kasparov has been a vocal political critic of Vladimir Putin, participating in pro-democracy movements and advocating for human rights, and he holds Croatian citizenship due to his past support for Croatian independence.
