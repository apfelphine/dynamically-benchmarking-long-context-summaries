Garry Kasparov is a world-renowned chess grandmaster and former world champion, born in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR (now Azerbaijan), with a Jewish and Armenian heritage. He adopted the surname Kasparov after his mother's name and was named after U.S. President Harry S. Truman, whom his father admired. He began studying chess at age seven and quickly rose through the ranks, winning the Soviet Junior Championship at age 13 and becoming the youngest world No. 1 in 1984 at age 22. He held the world title from 1985 to 2000, defeating Anatoly Karpov in a pivotal match in 1985 and later facing him in multiple title matches. His rivalry with Karpov is considered one of the greatest in chess history, with their five matches comprising 144 games.

Kasparov was instrumental in advancing chess theory, co-authoring books and introducing new openings, such as the Kasparov Gambit. He was also a pioneer in integrating computer technology into chess preparation, using chess databases and computer analysis. He played against numerous chess computers, including Deep Blue, and lost to Deep Blue in a 1997 match under tournament conditions, which marked the first time a reigning world champion was defeated by a computer. He later won against Deep Junior in a 2003 match, but lost a critical game due to a blunder.

After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov remained active in chess through exhibitions, teaching, and promoting chess in education. He coached Hikaru Nakamura and launched Kasparov Chess, a subscription-based online community. He also played in various blitz and rapid tournaments, maintaining a high level of performance and public engagement.

Beyond chess, Kasparov is a prominent political figure and activist. He has been a vocal critic of Russian President Vladimir Putin, advocating for democratic reforms and opposing authoritarianism. He left Russia in 2013 due to fears of persecution and has since been a leading voice in the anti-Putin movement, supporting democratic change and calling for Western action against Russian aggression. He has spoken out against the annexation of Crimea, the war in Ukraine, and Putin's human rights violations, comparing his regime to a fascist dictatorship.

Kasparov has also contributed to public discourse on artificial intelligence, noting that while AI poses challenges, individuals still hold the monopoly on evil. He has been recognized for his contributions to science, education, and human rights, receiving numerous awards and honors. His legacy includes not only his achievements in chess but also his influence as a thinker and activist, advocating for freedom, democracy, and the ethical use of technology.
