Garry Kasparov is a world-renowned chess grandmaster, former world champion, and influential figure in chess and political discourse. He began his chess career at a young age, winning major tournaments and becoming the youngest world No. 1 player in history. His rivalry with Anatoly Karpov defined much of his early career, culminating in a series of five world championship matches, in which he won the title in 1985 at age 22. After losing the title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000, he remained the top-rated player in the world until his retirement in 2005.

Post-retirement, Kasparov remained active in chess through exhibitions, training, and public engagement. He trained American grandmaster Hikaru Nakamura and participated in numerous simultaneous exhibitions and blitz tournaments. He also played in high-profile matches against IBM's Deep Blue, including a 1996 match where he won and a 1997 match where Deep Blue won, sparking debates over AI and human intelligence. He also led the "Kasparov versus the World" game in 1999, widely regarded as one of the greatest chess games in history.

Kasparov is a prominent political activist, particularly critical of Russian President Vladimir Putin. He has been vocal in opposition to Russian aggression in Ukraine, advocating for sanctions and regime change. In 2013, he was designated a "foreign agent" by Russia and later placed on a list of "terrorists and extremists." He has also criticized U.S. foreign policy, particularly regarding the Middle East and NATO, and expressed concerns about authoritarianism in various countries.

In addition to chess, Kasparov has authored several influential books, including the "My Great Predecessors" series, which explores the lives and careers of past world champions, and "Winter Is Coming," a political commentary on Putin and global threats. He has also written about decision-making in business and society, emphasizing parallels between chess and real-world strategy.

Kasparov is known for his strong views on historical revisionism, supporting the "New Chronology" theory despite later reservations. He has contributed to public discourse on artificial intelligence, technology, and governance, and has served as a consultant for media and technology projects, including Netflix's "The Queen's Gambit" and the book "Hyperautomation."

A three-time husband, he has two children with his current wife, Daria. He has lived in New York City since 2013 and is known for his intellectual rigor, activism, and advocacy for democratic values.
