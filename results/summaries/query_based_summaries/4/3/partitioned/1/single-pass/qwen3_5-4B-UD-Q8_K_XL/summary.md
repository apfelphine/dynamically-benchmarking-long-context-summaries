Garry Kasparov is a legendary chess player and former world champion who held the top rating from 1984 to 2005, a period defined by his dominance and rivalry with Anatoly Karpov. He achieved the historic feat of winning the World Chess Championship at age 22 in 1985, breaking Mikhail Tal's record as the youngest champion. His career was marked by an aggressive, dynamic playing style heavily influenced by Alexander Alekhine, characterized by deep calculation, extensive opening preparation, and tactical brilliance. Kasparov also made significant contributions to chess theory, including reviving the Scotch Game and developing the Kasparov Gambit in the Sicilian Defense.

Following his retirement from competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov transitioned into politics, becoming a prominent opposition figure in Russia. He founded the United Civil Front to promote democracy, ran for president in 2008, and faced persecution from the Kremlin, including arrests, fines, and his designation as a "foreign agent." He eventually fled Russia in 2013 to avoid persecution, later obtaining Croatian citizenship. Beyond politics, he has remained active in chess through training (notably for Magnus Carlsen), coaching Hikaru Nakamura, and establishing online platforms like Kasparov Chess and a subscription-based community.
