## Garry Kasparov: Concise Summary

Garry Kasparov is a Russian chess grandmaster who became the youngest-ever world champion in 1985 after defeating Anatoly Karpov. His chess career was marked by extraordinary achievements, including a record streak as the world’s No. 1-ranked player from 1984 to 2005, groundbreaking contributions to chess opening theory, and a dynamic, aggressive playing style lauded by peers such as Magnus Carlsen and Vladimir Kramnik. Post-retirement, Kasparov regularly played in high-profile exhibition matches, mentored players like Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura, and launched educational initiatives including the subscription-based Kasparov Chess platform.

Aside from chess, Kasparov is known for his political activism, particularly as a vocal opponent of Vladimir Putin. After retiring from competitive chess, he founded the United Civil Front, participated in Russian opposition movements, and eventually lived in exile due to fear of persecution. He continues to advocate for democracy in Russia and Ukraine, supports international human rights, and takes strong stances on world affairs. Kasparov’s prolific writing includes chess literature, political commentary, and books such as "Winter Is Coming". His legacy extends from the chessboard to global political arenas, establishing him as both a chess icon and a prominent advocate for human rights and democratic values.
