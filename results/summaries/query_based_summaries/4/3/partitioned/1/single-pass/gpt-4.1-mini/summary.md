## Summary: Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in 1963 in Baku, USSR, is a former World Chess Champion renowned for his dynamic and aggressive style, deep opening preparation, and historic rivalry with Anatoly Karpov. Rising rapidly through the ranks, he became the youngest-ever World No. 1 in 1984 and won the World Chess Championship in 1985 at age 22. Kasparov defended his title in subsequent matches until losing to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000. After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, he remained active in exhibition matches and chess promotion, including training Magnus Carlsen briefly and launching the subscription-based Kasparov Chess online community.

Kasparov gained international prominence as a political activist and outspoken critic of Vladimir Putin. After initially engaging in Russian politics and opposition movements, he fled Russia in 2013 due to political persecution and has since been active in exile, advocating for democracy in Russia and opposing Russian aggression in Ukraine. He holds Croatian citizenship and resides in New York. Kasparov has authored numerous chess books—including his acclaimed *My Great Predecessors* series—and political commentaries such as *Winter Is Coming*, in which he warns against Putin’s authoritarianism. He has also been involved in controversies regarding his chess matches against computers, notably Deep Blue, and has engaged with issues of historical revisionism post-retirement.
