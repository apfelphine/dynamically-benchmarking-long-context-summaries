Garry Kasparov is a former world chess champion renowned for his dynamic playing style, deep strategic understanding, and extensive opening preparation. He held the world's top rating from 1984 to 2005, achieving a record Elo of 2851 in 1999, and is credited with revolutionizing opening theory in the 1970s and 1980s. His career was defined by a fierce rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, resulting in five world championship matches, and by his historic defeat of IBM's Deep Blue in 1997, marking the first time a reigning champion lost to a computer under tournament conditions. Kasparov also explored the intersection of chess and computers through ventures like *Kasparov Chessmate* and matches against programs such as X3D Fritz and Deep Junior, often expressing regret over specific blunders despite generally feeling he outplayed the machines.

Beyond chess, Kasparov has been a prominent political figure in Russia, evolving from a Soviet dissident into a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin's administration. After retiring from competitive chess in 2005, he co-founded the "United Civil Front" and later the "Other Russia" coalition, organizing mass protests and participating in the 2008 presidential race. He was designated a "foreign agent" in 2022 and placed on a list of "terrorists and extremists" in 2024. In 2013, he fled Russia due to fear of persecution, continuing his opposition from exile. Kasparov remains active in international affairs, supporting Ukraine's sovereignty, advocating for Armenian independence, and engaging in debates on global politics, while also maintaining a successful career as a chess commentator, author, and educator through platforms like Kasparov Chess.
