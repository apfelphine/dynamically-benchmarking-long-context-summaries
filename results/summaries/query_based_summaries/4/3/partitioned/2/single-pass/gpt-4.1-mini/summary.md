**Summary of Information on Garry Kasparov**

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR, in 1963, is widely regarded as one of the greatest chess players in history. He began serious chess study at age seven and quickly rose through the ranks, becoming the youngest Candidate since Bobby Fischer at 19 and the youngest-ever world number one at 20. Kasparov won the World Chess Championship in 1985, defeating Anatoly Karpov, and held the title through a series of closely contested matches until 2000 when he lost to Vladimir Kramnik. He also led a significant split from FIDE in 1993, forming the Professional Chess Association and creating a schism in the world chess championship titles for over a decade. After retiring from regular competitive play in 2005, Kasparov continued to participate in exhibition matches, simultaneous displays, and rapid/blitz tournaments while coaching prominent players like Magnus Carlsen.

Kasparov played historic matches against chess computers, notably IBM's Deep Blue in 1996 and 1997, with the 1997 loss marking the first time a reigning world champion was defeated by a computer under tournament conditions. He continued to engage in man-vs-machine matches against various chess engines into the 2000s. Beyond chess, Kasparov has contributed extensively to chess literature, including the acclaimed multi-volume series *My Great Predecessors*, and has been involved in online chess initiatives.

Politically, Kasparov has been an outspoken critic of Vladimir Putin's regime, founding the United Civil Front and actively opposing Russian authoritarianism. After fleeing Russia in 2013 due to fears of persecution, he has remained a vocal opponent of Putin from exile, promoting sanctions and supporting Ukraine against Russian aggression. He has engaged in international political discourse, supporting democratic movements globally and advocating for stronger NATO ties for Ukraine. Kasparov holds Croatian citizenship and divides his time primarily between New York and Europe. His political writings include the 2015 book *Winter Is Coming*, which condemns Putin's authoritarianism and urges democratic countries to unite against threats to the free world.
