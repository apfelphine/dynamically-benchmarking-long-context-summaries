## Summary of Information Regarding Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR, is a former world chess champion renowned for his aggressive and dynamic playing style, deep opening preparation, and contributions to opening theory. He became the youngest-ever world No. 1 in 1984 and held that ranking until 2005. Kasparov won the World Chess Championship in 1985 by defeating Anatoly Karpov and defended his title through several close matches until losing it to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000. After retiring from professional chess in 2005, Kasparov remained active in chess through coaching (notably for Magnus Carlsen), exhibition matches, and online platforms such as Kasparov Chess. He also engaged in matches against computer programs, including the famous Deep Blue contests in 1996 and 1997, with his 1997 loss marking the first defeat of a reigning world champion by a computer under tournament conditions.

In politics, Kasparov transitioned from a Communist Party member in the 1980s to an outspoken opponent of Vladimir Putin and his regime, founding the United Civil Front and playing a key role in opposition coalitions like The Other Russia. He has faced harassment, arrest, and physical assault in Russia, eventually fleeing into exile in 2013. From abroad, Kasparov has actively campaigned against Putin's rule, called for democratic reforms, and criticized Russian military actions such as the annexation of Crimea and the invasion of Ukraine. He has been designated a "foreign agent" and labeled a "terrorist and extremist" by Russian authorities. Kasparov also sought to reform chess governance, running unsuccessfully for FIDE presidency in 2014 before being suspended over ethics violations related to the campaign.

Kasparov has authored numerous chess books, including his acclaimed multi-volume "My Great Predecessors" series and "Winter Is Coming," a political critique of Putin’s authoritarianism. His influence extends beyond chess to political activism and the promotion of intellectual and democratic freedoms.
