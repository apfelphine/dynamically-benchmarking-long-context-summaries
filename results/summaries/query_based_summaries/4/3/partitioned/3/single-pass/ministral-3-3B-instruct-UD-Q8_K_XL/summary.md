Garry Kasparov, born Garik Kimovich Weinstein, is a legendary chess player and one of the most influential figures in modern chess history. Born in Baku, Azerbaijan, in 1963, he adopted his mother’s surname Kasparov at age 12. Kasparov began his chess career at a young age, training under Vladimir Makogonov and later Mikhail Botvinnik, who famously declared, "The future of chess lies in the hands of this young man." His early success included winning the Soviet Junior Championship at age 13 and becoming a Grandmaster at 15.

Kasparov rose to prominence as a world-class chess player, becoming the youngest world champion in 1985 at age 22, defeating Anatoly Karpov in a historic match. His rivalry with Karpov, often referred to as the "Two Ks," defined an era in chess. Kasparov defended his title in multiple matches, showcasing his dynamic and tactical prowess, which earned him the nickname "The Magician of Chess." His aggressive play, deep strategic understanding, and innovative opening ideas made him a formidable opponent.

Beyond chess, Kasparov became a vocal critic of Russian politics, particularly under Vladimir Putin. He publicly opposed Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, calling for sanctions and condemning Putin’s regime. In 2013, he was designated a "foreign agent" by the Russian government and later fled the country due to fear of persecution. Kasparov’s political activism has made him a prominent figure in the global opposition to authoritarianism and human rights abuses.

Kasparov’s post-retirement career includes continued chess engagement, such as coaching top players and participating in exhibitions. He has also written extensively on chess theory, contributing to books like *My Great Predecessors* and *Winter Is Coming: Why Vladimir Putin and the Enemies of the Free World Must Be Stopped*. Beyond chess and politics, he has been involved in education, promoting chess as a tool for cognitive development and social skills. Kasparov remains a respected figure in both chess and global advocacy for democratic values.
