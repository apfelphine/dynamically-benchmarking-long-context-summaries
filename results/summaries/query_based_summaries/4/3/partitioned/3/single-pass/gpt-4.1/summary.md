## Garry Kasparov: Concise Summary

Garry Kasparov is a highly influential chess grandmaster and former world champion, renowned for his dynamic playing style, extensive opening preparation, and aggressive play. Rising up the ranks from a young age, Kasparov became the youngest-ever world chess champion in 1985 at age 22 after defeating Anatoly Karpov. He maintained dominance in the chess world for decades, holding the No. 1 ranking for an unprecedented length of time and achieving a record-high FIDE rating of 2851 in 1999. Kasparov won or tied for first place in every tournament he entered from 1981 to 1991 and claimed the Chess Oscar eleven times. His rivalry with Karpov is regarded as one of the greatest in chess history. Kasparov contributed significantly to chess theory, rejuvenating openings like the Scotch Game and pioneering computer-assisted preparation.

Kasparov was instrumental in integrating technology into chess, using computer databases for analysis and facing chess computers such as Deep Thought and Deep Blue. The latter defeated him in 1997, marking a milestone in artificial intelligence. Post-retirement, Kasparov continued to play in exhibitions, mentor top players like Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura, launch chess education initiatives, and advocate for chess globally. He authored numerous works on chess history, strategy, and annotated classic games. Kasparov also played a prominent role in chess governance, unsuccessfully running for FIDE presidency, and remains a vocal commentator on political issues, particularly regarding Russia, after entering politics and opposing the Putin regime from exile.
