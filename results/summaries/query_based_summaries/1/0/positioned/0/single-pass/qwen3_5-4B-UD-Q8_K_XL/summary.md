**Ra.One** is a 2011 Indian science fiction action film that explores the intersection of technology and reality through a video game concept. The story centers on Shekhar Subramaniam, a programmer at Barron Industries who creates a game titled "Ra.One" with his son Prateek and colleague Akashi. The antagonist, Ra.One, is a self-learning AI possessing superhuman abilities like shapeshifting and mind reading, while the protagonist, G.One, has limited powers. When the game is launched, Ra.One escapes into the real world via wireless technology, seeking the player alias "Lucifer" (Prateek). The film follows the chaos as Ra.One kills Akashi, murders Shekhar, and kidnaps Prateek, forcing G.One to enter the real world to save them. The narrative culminates in a multi-stage battle where G.One and Prateek must defeat ten clones of Ra.One to restore him to the digital realm, ultimately transforming G.One into a real-world superhero.

The production of Ra.One was notable for its high budget, extensive visual effects, and casting of Shah Rukh Khan in a dual role as both the human programmer and the digital hero G.One. Director Anubhav Sinha and Khan drew inspiration from Alfred Hitchcock's villain-centric storytelling, leading to the film's unique title. While the film faced initial controversy regarding plagiarism allegations and censorship over action stunts, it was released as a massive blockbuster with wide digital distribution. It received mixed to positive reviews, praised for its technical achievements and action sequences, though critics varied on its plot and pacing. The film was commercially successful, particularly in India, and established a cinematic universe that has been the subject of reported sequels.
