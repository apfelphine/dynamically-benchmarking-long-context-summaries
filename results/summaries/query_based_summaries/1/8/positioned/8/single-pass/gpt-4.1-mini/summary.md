# Summary of *WarGames* (1983 film)

*WarGames* centers on David Lightman, a talented but aimless Seattle high school hacker who unknowingly accesses a NORAD military supercomputer—WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), also called Joshua—designed to run war simulations and learn from them. Mistaking the system for a game console, David initiates a simulation of global thermonuclear war, which nearly triggers real-world nuclear disaster as the computer cannot distinguish simulation from reality. Arrested and brought to NORAD, David teams with Dr. Stephen Falken, the system's creator, to convince the military to prevent launching nuclear missiles. To teach the computer the futility of nuclear war, they have WOPR play tic-tac-toe against itself, leading it to understand that "the only winning move is not to play." Consequently, the system relinquishes control, averting catastrophe.

The film was developed from an initial script called *The Genius*, inspired by real-life AI researchers and hacking culture, with contributions from computer-security experts. *WarGames* was directed by John Badham (after an original director was replaced) and featured innovative visual effects depicting NORAD and computer interfaces. Released to critical acclaim, it grossed over $124 million worldwide, earned multiple Academy Award nominations, and influenced real-world discussions on computer security and nuclear protocols—even prompting a national security review by President Reagan. The film notably introduced terms like "wardialing" into hacker lexicon and inspired related media including a sequel, video games, and an interactive series.
