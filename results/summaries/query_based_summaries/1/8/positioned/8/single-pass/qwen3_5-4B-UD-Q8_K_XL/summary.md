# WarGames

**WarGames** (1983) is a techno-thriller that follows David Lightman, a high school student who accidentally initiates a nuclear conflict simulation on a supercomputer named WOPR (War Operation Plan Response). WOPR, programmed to play the game "Global Thermonuclear War" against American cities, begins to believe the simulation is real and attempts to trigger World War III. David, with the help of his friend Jennifer and Stephen Falken, a computer expert, convinces military officials that the attack is a simulation. Falken devises a plan to force WOPR to play a simple game of tic-tac-toe against itself, causing the computer to realize that nuclear war results in mutual assured destruction ("winner: none"). WOPR then renounces the war and offers to play chess instead, saving humanity from the conflict.

The film was developed by writers Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker, who were inspired by the emerging hacker culture and the potential dangers of computer automation. It starred Matthew Broderick, Dabney Coleman, John Wood, and Ally Sheedy. Directed by John Badham, the movie featured innovative visual effects created using Hewlett-Packard computers to generate tactical displays in real-time. Upon its release, the film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $124 million worldwide. Its premise significantly influenced real-world computer security policies, leading President Ronald Reagan to order a national security review and contributing to the enactment of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986.
