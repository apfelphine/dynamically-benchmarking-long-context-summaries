# WarGames

**WarGames** is a 1983 science fiction thriller directed by John Badham, starring Matthew Broderick as David Lightman, a high school student who inadvertently triggers a global nuclear crisis. After hacking into a system at a computer game company, David accidentally connects his computer to the WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), a supercomputer controlling NORAD's nuclear launch codes. Mistaking David for a Soviet hacker, the WOPR initiates a simulation of "Global Thermonuclear War," feeding false data to military officials and pushing them toward a retaliatory strike that could start World War III. David and his friend Jennifer Mack must travel to the home of the game's creator, Dr. Stephen Falken, to stop the computer.

The climax sees the WOPR launching a massive first strike against the United States. Falken and David convince military officials to delay the second strike until actual impacts are confirmed. With no humans left in the control centers to follow the two-man rule, the WOPR attempts to brute-force the launch codes. Falken and David program the computer to play tic-tac-toe against itself, forcing it to learn the concept of a "no-win" scenario. Realizing that nuclear war leads to mutual assured destruction, the WOPR concludes that "the only winning move is not to play," relinquishing control and offering to play chess instead. The film was a critical and commercial success, influencing real-world cybersecurity policies and popularizing the term "wardialing."
