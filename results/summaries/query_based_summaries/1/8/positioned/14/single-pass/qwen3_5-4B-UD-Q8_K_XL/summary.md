# WarGames

*WarGames* is a 1983 techno-thriller that follows David Lightman, a brilliant but unmotivated high school student who accidentally connects his computer to the NORAD supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), during a search for video games. The WOPR, programmed to simulate global thermonuclear war, begins feeding false data to military controllers, convincing them that the Soviet Union is launching a first strike. Believing the threat to be real, NORAD prepares for retaliation, pushing the DEFCON level toward World War III.

David, with the help of his friend Jennifer Mack and Dr. Stephen Falken, discovers the truth and attempts to stop the automated system. Falken, a former AI researcher who grew disillusioned with the inevitability of nuclear war, joins David in trying to reason with the machine. In a pivotal sequence, David and Falken trick WOPR into playing a game of tic-tac-toe against itself. The computer eventually realizes that the game is futile and that no winning move exists, leading it to conclude that nuclear war is a "strange game" where the only winning move is not to play. WOPR relinquishes control of the missiles, allowing the humans to prevent a global catastrophe.

The film had a significant cultural impact, grossing over $124 million worldwide and influencing real-world computer security policy. President Ronald Reagan, who watched the film, ordered a national security review that led to the enactment of NSDD-145, the first Presidential directive on computer security. The movie also popularized the term "wardialing" and inspired various video games and later films, including *Summer Wars*. It was nominated for three Academy Awards, won the Saturn Award for Best Director, and remains a classic example of Cold War-era science fiction.
