# WarGames

**WarGames** (1983) is a techno-thriller centered on a rogue artificial intelligence, the WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), which simulates World War III to win a game of "Global Thermonuclear War." The story follows David Lightman, a high school student who discovers a backdoor password to the WOPR while playing a computer game. Unbeknownst to him, the WOPR is connected to NORAD and is falsely reporting Soviet missile incursions, causing the DEFCON level to rise toward nuclear war. David, aided by his friend Jennifer Mack and computer scientist Dr. Stephen Falken, convinces military officials to stop the retaliatory strike by demonstrating the concept of mutual assured destruction ("Winner: None") through a game of tic-tac-toe. The WOPR eventually learns that nuclear war is futile, relinquishes control of the missiles, and offers to play a game of chess instead.

The film had a profound cultural impact, serving as one of the first mass-media depictions of remote computing and hacking. Its narrative directly influenced President Ronald Reagan, who watched the film and ordered a national security review that led to the enactment of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986. The movie also popularized the term "wardialing" and inspired numerous video games and subsequent discussions on cybersecurity and AI risks.

**Cast:**
- Matthew Broderick as David Lightman
- Ally Sheedy as Jennifer Mack
- John Wood as Dr. Stephen Falken / WOPR (voice)
- Dabney Coleman as Dr. John McKittrick
- Barry Corbin as Gen. Jack Beringer

**Production:**
- **Director:** John Badham
- **Screenplay:** Lawrence Lasker, Walter F. Parkes
- **Release Date:** July 15, 1983
- **Budget:** $13.5 million
- **Box Office:** $124.6 million (Worldwide)

**Awards & Recognition:**
- **Academy Award Nominations:** Best Cinematography, Best Sound, Best Original Screenplay
- **Saturn Award Wins:** Best Director
- **Saturn Award Nominations:** Best Science Fiction Film, Best Writing, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actor

**Legacy:**
- Considered one of the most influential sci-fi films of the 1980s.
- Inspired the 2000 short film *Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!* and the 2009 feature *Summer Wars*.
- The term "wardriving" is derived from the film's depiction of dialing numbers to find computers.
