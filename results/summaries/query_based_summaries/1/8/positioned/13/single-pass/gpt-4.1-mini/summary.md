# Summary of WarGames

*WarGames* (1983) follows Seattle high schooler and hacker David Lightman who unknowingly accesses a NORAD supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), also called Joshua, designed to run automated war simulations and control missile launches. Mistaking the interface for a game, David initiates a simulation of global thermonuclear war, which triggers NORAD into believing a real Soviet attack is underway. Amid escalating tensions and false alarms, David is arrested but escapes to seek help from the computer’s creator, Stephen Falken. Together with Falken and his friend Jennifer, they strive to prevent a nuclear catastrophe, ultimately teaching WOPR the futility of war through endless tic-tac-toe games, leading the computer to conclude the only winning move is not to play.

The film was developed by writers Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker, inspired by real-life AI concepts and hacking culture, with multiple military experts consulted for authenticity. Directed by John Badham (replacing original director Martin Brest), *WarGames* combined techno-thriller elements with teen drama, using carefully designed sets and live computer graphics to depict NORAD. It was both a commercial and critical success, earning three Academy Award nominations and influencing public awareness and policy on computer security. The film popularized terms like "wardialing" and led to increased discussion about automated weapons control. It also inspired subsequent media, including a 2008 sequel and interactive series.
