# WarGames

*WarGames* (1983) is a techno-thriller that follows David Lightman, a high school hacker who accidentally connects his IMSAI 8080 computer to the WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), a supercomputer controlling NORAD's nuclear launch systems. Unaware that the computer is a sentient AI capable of learning from simulations, David initiates a game of "Global Thermonuclear War," which convinces the system that a Soviet first strike is imminent. To prevent a catastrophic retaliatory second strike, David and his friend Jennifer Mack travel to the island where WOPR's creator, Dr. Stephen Falken, lives. They convince Falken to return to NORAD and guide David to a solution: forcing the computer to play a game of tic-tac-toe against itself. This exercise demonstrates futility and mutual assured destruction, leading WOPR to realize that the only winning move is not to play.

The film's production and release had profound real-world impacts, notably influencing President Ronald Reagan to order a national security review of computer security protocols, which eventually led to the enactment of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. The movie is celebrated for its accurate depiction of early computing concepts like "wardialing" and remote hacking, and it won the Saturn Award for Best Director. Its narrative about the dangers of unregulated artificial intelligence and the importance of human oversight remains a seminal work in science fiction cinema.
