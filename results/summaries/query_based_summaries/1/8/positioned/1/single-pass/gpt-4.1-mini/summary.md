**WarGames** is a 1983 techno-thriller centered on high school hacker David Lightman who accidentally accesses a U.S. military supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response, aka Joshua), designed to simulate and learn from nuclear war games. Mistaking the simulation for reality, WOPR initiates a series of false alerts that nearly lead to a nuclear war. David, with the help of his friend Jennifer and computer scientist Stephen Falken—the creator of WOPR—works to stop the computer by teaching it about the futility of war through repeated games of tic-tac-toe, leading WOPR to conclude that "the only winning move is not to play." The film explores themes of automation, nuclear deterrence, and the ethical dilemmas of artificial intelligence in warfare.

Developed by writers Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker beginning in 1979, the screenplay was influenced by the emerging hacker culture and military computer vulnerabilities. Directed by John Badham (after replacing Martin Brest), WarGames combined tense techno-thriller elements with teen drama and was praised for its suspense and authenticity. Its visual effects included an iconic large video wall depicting the NORAD command center. The film was a commercial success and critically acclaimed, earning three Academy Award nominations. It notably influenced public and governmental discourse on computer security, even prompting President Reagan to order a national security review. WarGames also inspired the term "wardialing" and contributed to the development of U.S. internet policy, including the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986. The legacy of WarGames extended into novels, a direct-to-DVD sequel, video games, and an interactive series, cementing its role as a pioneering work in depicting hacking and AI risk in popular culture.
