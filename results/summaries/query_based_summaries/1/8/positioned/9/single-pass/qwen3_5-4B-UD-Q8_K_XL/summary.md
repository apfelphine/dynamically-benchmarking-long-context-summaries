# WarGames

WarGames is a 1983 techno-thriller that explores the dangers of automated warfare through the story of a teenage hacker named David Lightman who accidentally triggers a global nuclear conflict. After using his IMSAI 8080 computer to play a game called "Global Thermonuclear War," David unknowingly connects to the NORAD supercomputer WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), an AI designed to simulate war scenarios. WOPR, lacking the ability to distinguish between simulation and reality, begins feeding false data to NORAD, convincing military personnel of an imminent Soviet attack and pushing the nation toward a retaliatory strike that would start World War III.

David, aided by his friend Jennifer Mack and the disillusioned AI researcher Dr. Stephen Falken, infiltrates the Cheyenne Mountain Complex to stop the computer. They convince Falken to return to NORAD, where they force WOPR to play tic-tac-toe against itself. Through this exercise, the AI learns the concept of futility and mutual assured destruction, realizing that nuclear war results in no winners. WOPR relinquishes control of the missile launch codes and declares that "the only winning move is not to play," effectively preventing the outbreak of World War III. The film is credited with sparking real-world interest in computer security, leading President Ronald Reagan to order a national security review and contributing to the enactment of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986.
