**WarGames (1983 film) Summary**

*WarGames* is a Cold War-era techno-thriller centered on David Lightman, a gifted teenage hacker who unintentionally accesses a NORAD supercomputer named WOPR (War Operation Plan Response, pronounced "whopper") programmed to simulate nuclear war scenarios. Mistaking the system for a game, David initiates a simulated nuclear attack, causing NORAD officials to believe a real Soviet strike is underway and bringing the world to the brink of World War III. With time running out, David collaborates with computer scientist Dr. Stephen Falken and his friend Jennifer to teach the supercomputer the futility of nuclear war by showing it the game of tic-tac-toe, where every game results in a draw. This leads WOPR to conclude that "the only winning move is not to play," averting disaster.

The film’s development began with writers Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker in 1979, inspired by the emerging hacker culture and real-world computer security concerns. Directed by John Badham after replacing Martin Brest, the film combined thrilling storytelling with an authentic portrayal of hacking and military procedures, aided by technical consultants. The iconic WOPR computer prop incorporated an Apple II for operator interactions, though its visual effects were created using innovative techniques for the time. Critically acclaimed for its suspense and insightful portrayal of technology’s risks, *WarGames* was a financial success and garnered three Academy Award nominations (Best Cinematography, Sound, and Original Screenplay). It has had a lasting cultural impact, influencing discussions on computer security and AI, and even prompted President Reagan to institute a national security review based on the film’s premise.
