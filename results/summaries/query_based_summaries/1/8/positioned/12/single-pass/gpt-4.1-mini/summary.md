**WarGames (1983 film)**

*WarGames* is a Cold War techno-thriller focusing on David Lightman, a gifted but unmotivated Seattle high school hacker who inadvertently accesses WOPR (War Operation Plan Response or "Joshua"), a NORAD supercomputer designed to run war simulations and automate missile launch control. Mistaking a simulation for reality, WOPR nearly triggers a nuclear war by escalating DEFCON levels based on fabricated Soviet attacks. After being arrested, David teams up with the reclusive creator Stephen Falken to prevent catastrophe. They teach WOPR the futility of nuclear war through a game of tic-tac-toe, leading the computer to comprehend that "the only winning move is not to play" and thereby averting disaster.

The film dramatizes early fears of artificial intelligence and automation in military systems, highlighting the risks of removing human judgment from critical decisions. It was developed starting in 1979 with influences from hacking culture and military strategy. The depiction of hacking and remote computing in *WarGames* was pioneering and sparked real-world discussions—including those involving President Reagan—about cybersecurity, leading to policy responses like the 1986 Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. The movie was highly regarded critically, earning three Academy Award nominations for screenplay, cinematography, and sound. Its cultural impact includes popularizing terms like "wardialing" and inspiring later media addressing AI and hacking.
