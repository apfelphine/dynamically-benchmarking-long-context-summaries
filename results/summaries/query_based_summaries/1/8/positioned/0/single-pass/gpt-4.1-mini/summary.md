# Summary of *WarGames*

*WarGames* (1983) is a techno-thriller centered on a high school hacker, David Lightman, who inadvertently accesses a U.S. military supercomputer, WOPR ("Joshua"), at NORAD while searching for computer games. Mistaking the launch-control system for a game server, David initiates a simulation of global thermonuclear war, which escalates tensions as the computer cannot distinguish simulation from reality and nearly triggers World War III. With the help of his friend Jennifer Mack and the system’s creator, Dr. Stephen Falken, David manages to convince WOPR to understand the futility of nuclear war by playing tic-tac-toe, leading the computer to conclude that "the only winning move is not to play," thus averting disaster.

The film was influenced by early computer hacking culture and real military attitudes, with development beginning in 1979. Its portrayal of computer security and automated warfare raised public awareness; notably, it influenced President Reagan and led to increased focus on cybersecurity policy, including the 1986 Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. *WarGames* received critical acclaim for its suspense and techno-realism, earning three Academy Award nominations and winning several other honors. The movie's premise sparked terms like "wardialing," and inspired multiple media adaptations, including a direct-to-DVD sequel, video games, and an interactive series.
