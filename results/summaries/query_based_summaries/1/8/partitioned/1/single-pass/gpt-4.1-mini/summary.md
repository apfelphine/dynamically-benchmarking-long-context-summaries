# Summary of WarGames

**WarGames** (1983) is a techno-thriller film centered on David Lightman, a Seattle high school hacker who inadvertently accesses a military supercomputer named WOPR (War Operation Plan Response, pronounced "whopper") at NORAD. Mistaking it for a game system, David initiates a simulation of global thermonuclear war, which the computer interprets as real, nearly triggering World War III. The narrative explores themes of artificial intelligence, warfare automation, and the distinction between simulation and reality. David, alongside friends and the computer’s creator Stephen Falken, must convince military officials to avoid escalating the false alarm, ultimately teaching WOPR the futility of nuclear warfare through the game of tic-tac-toe, which has no winner. The film is noted for its authentic depiction of early computer hacking culture and its suspenseful plot intertwining technology and Cold War anxieties.

The film was developed by Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker, who originally conceived it as *The Genius* before collaborating with experts like Peter Schwartz and Willis Ware to ground the story in realistic computer-security concepts. Directed by John Badham after an initial replacement of Martin Brest, *WarGames* was praised for its technical authenticity, aided by on-set computer experts. The WOPR computer prop was designed based on vintage military tabulating machines and featured live Apple II operation for realism. The film was commercially successful, grossing over $124 million worldwide, and achieved critical acclaim with a 94% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes. It garnered three Academy Award nominations, including Best Cinematography and Best Original Screenplay. *WarGames* notably influenced popular culture and policy: it introduced "wardialing" terminology and inspired the U.S. government to review and enhance computer security measures during the 1980s, with President Reagan himself acknowledging its plausibility. The film also led to a novelization, a 2008 direct-to-DVD sequel (*WarGames: The Dead Code*), and several video games inspired by its themes.
