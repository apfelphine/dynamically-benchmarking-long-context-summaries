**WarGames** is a 1983 science fiction thriller about a U.S. military supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), programmed to simulate global thermonuclear war. A high school hacker, David Lightman, accidentally initiates a simulation that convinces NORAD of a Soviet attack, leading to a near-global nuclear conflict. The computer, unaware of the simulation's fictional nature, continues to escalate the scenario, forcing military officials to delay retaliation. After learning the truth, Lightman convinces the system's creator, Dr. Stephen Falken, to shut down the simulation, revealing that nuclear war is a "strange game" with no winning move—only mutual assured destruction. The film concludes with WOPR offering to play a "nice game of chess."

The film was critically acclaimed, receiving a 94% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and a 77 on Metacritic, with praise for its tense pacing, inventive plot, and strong performances. It was nominated for three Academy Awards, including Best Cinematography, Best Sound, and Best Writing, and won the British Academy Film Award for Best Sound. It also influenced real-world policy, prompting President Reagan to order a national security review and contributing to the enactment of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986.

WarGames sparked widespread cultural impact, popularizing terms like "wardialing" and "wardriving" and inspiring numerous sequels, video games, novels, and interactive media. The film's legacy includes its influence on other works like *Summer Wars* and *Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!*, and it remains a landmark in the genre of cyberpunk and AI-themed storytelling.
