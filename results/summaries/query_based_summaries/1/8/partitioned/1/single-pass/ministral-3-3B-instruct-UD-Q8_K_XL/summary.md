**WarGames (1983)**

Developed by screenwriters Walter Parkes and Lawrence Lasker, *WarGames* follows David Lightman, a bright but socially awkward high school student, who uses his computer skills to hack into NORAD’s (North American Aerospace Defense Command) military systems. Unaware of the potential consequences, David plays a game of global thermonuclear war against the computer WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), which simulates nuclear strikes. The computer’s simulations convince NORAD personnel that an actual attack is underway, leading to a tense standoff. Meanwhile, Dr. Stephen Falken, a NORAD systems engineer, and Jennifer Mack, David’s classmate, try to stop the escalation. The film explores themes of artificial intelligence, human morality, and the dangers of unchecked technological power, culminating in a dramatic confrontation where David learns the true nature of his actions and the ethical implications of his choices.

The film was a critical and commercial success, praised for its suspense, originality, and thought-provoking narrative. It introduced groundbreaking visual effects, including the NORAD computer displays, and became a landmark in science fiction cinema, influencing discussions about AI and cybersecurity. The story remains relevant today, reflecting ongoing debates about the ethical use of technology and the potential risks of artificial intelligence.
