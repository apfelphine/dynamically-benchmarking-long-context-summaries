## WarGames Summary

WarGames (1983) is a techno-thriller film centered on David Lightman, a Seattle high school student and hacker who accidentally accesses NORAD’s supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response, aka Joshua), while searching for computer games. Unaware that he is interacting with a real military system, David initiates a game of Global Thermonuclear War, triggering simulations that NORAD mistakes for a genuine Soviet nuclear attack. As WOPR continues war simulations, the authorities escalate toward nuclear retaliation, but David, joined by his friend Jennifer and AI expert Dr. Stephen Falken, intervenes. They demonstrate to the computer the futility of war by forcing it to play tic-tac-toe and run every nuclear war scenario to a draw, teaching WOPR that "the only winning move is not to play." The crisis is averted and control of US missile defenses is restored.

The film explores themes of hacking, artificial intelligence, and the dangers of automated military decision-making. Critically acclaimed for its suspenseful and inventive storytelling, WarGames received three Academy Award nominations and has been credited with influencing public awareness of computer security and hacking culture. Its impact led to the first U.S. federal internet policy, including the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, and introduced terminology such as "wardialing." The cultural legacy includes multiple video games, a 2008 direct-to-DVD sequel, a novelization, and an interactive series. WarGames continues to be cited as a landmark in cyber-themed entertainment and policy influence.
