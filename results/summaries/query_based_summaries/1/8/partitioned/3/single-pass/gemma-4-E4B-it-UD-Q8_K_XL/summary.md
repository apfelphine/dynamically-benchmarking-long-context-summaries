## WarGames Summary

*WarGames* was highly influential, becoming the first mass-consumed, visual medium to focus on remote computing and hacking, thereby shaping early American discussions of information technology. The film led to significant real-world impacts, such as the enactment of NSDD-145, the first Presidential directive on computer security, and the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986. Furthermore, scenes depicting modem usage inspired the term "wardialing" and indirectly contributed to the concept of "wardriving."

Critically, *WarGames* received positive reception, with Rotten Tomatoes awarding it a 94% approval rating and Metacritic assigning it a score of 77/100. It was nominated for three Academy Awards, including for Best Original Screenplay. The film's premise, involving a high school hacker accessing a NORAD supercomputer named WOPR, generated widespread cultural attention regarding the dangers of unchecked AI simulation.
