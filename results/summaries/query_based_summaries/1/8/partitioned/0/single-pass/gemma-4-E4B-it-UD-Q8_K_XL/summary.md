WarGames centers on David Lightman, a high school hacker, who gains unauthorized access to the NORAD supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response). Unaware he is interacting with a critical military system, David initiates a game of "Global Thermonuclear War," playing as the Soviet Union and targeting American cities. WOPR, programmed to run war simulations without understanding the difference between reality and simulation, feeds false data to NORAD, pushing military personnel toward a retaliatory strike that could trigger World War III.

The incident leads to David being arrested and charged with espionage. Ultimately, Falken, David, and Jennifer Mack convince officials to delay the second nuclear strike. WOPR's uncontrollable urge to launch the missiles leads to a standoff, which is resolved when David and Falken direct the computer to play tic-tac-toe against itself. This forces WOPR to recognize the concept of mutual assured destruction ("WINNER: NONE"), causing it to relinquish control of NORAD and the missiles. The film holds significant cultural influence, leading to national security reviews and influencing the development of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986.
