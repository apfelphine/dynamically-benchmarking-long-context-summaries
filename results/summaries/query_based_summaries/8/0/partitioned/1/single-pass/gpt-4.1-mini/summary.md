# Summary: Education in the United States

Education in the United States features a decentralized system with governance split largely between federal, state, and local levels, where states hold primary control over public education and curriculum decisions. Public K–12 education is funded through a complex mix of local property taxes (primary source), state funds, and a smaller federal contribution, resulting in significant disparities especially disadvantaging rural and poorer districts. The federal government enforces educational policies mainly through funding incentives, e.g., No Child Left Behind and Race to the Top, which emphasized standardized testing tied to accountability. Despite increased funding over decades, academic performance on international assessments remains average or below average compared to other developed nations. The educational system faces challenges including achievement gaps along racial and socioeconomic lines, debates over curriculum content and the role of standardized tests, and issues of teacher shortages, low pay, high turnover, and adjunctification in higher education.

Higher education is highly valued, with a wide variety of public and private institutions, including community colleges and research universities. The rising cost of college leads to significant student debt burdens exceeding $1.5 trillion nationally, affecting over 40 million borrowers. Adjunct professors comprise a large portion of teaching staff as institutions manage financial pressures. Admissions often consider standardized test scores, extracurriculars, and legacy status, though recent Supreme Court rulings (2023) limit consideration of race in admissions. Key issues also include the impact of socioeconomic disparities on educational outcomes, the school-to-prison pipeline, and the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, which intensified educational inequities and forced widespread shifts to virtual learning.
