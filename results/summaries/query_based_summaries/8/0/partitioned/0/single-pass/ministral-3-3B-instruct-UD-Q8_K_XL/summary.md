Education in the United States spans a structured system from early childhood through higher education, with key milestones including compulsory schooling from kindergarten to grade 12, followed by optional higher education. Compulsory education begins at age five or six, with students attending public schools free of tuition, though many opt for private or homeschooling alternatives. The system is divided into three main stages: elementary (grades K–6), middle/junior high (grades 7–9), and high school (grades 10–12), with graduation requiring a diploma. High school enrollment is nearly universal, though some students take gap years or pursue vocational training.

Higher education is optional but widely valued, with over 4,495 colleges and universities offering diverse programs. Public institutions rely on state funding, while private schools charge higher tuition. The U.S. system is known for its flexibility, with students able to transfer between institutions and pursue specialized degrees. However, challenges persist, including high student loan debt, teacher shortages, and disparities in educational outcomes tied to socioeconomic status and race. The U.S. ranks relatively low in international comparisons of student performance, particularly in science and mathematics, though it remains a major destination for international students.
