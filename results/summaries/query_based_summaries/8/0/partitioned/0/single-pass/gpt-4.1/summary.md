## Education in the United States: Key Overview

**Structure and Stages:**  
Education in the United States is divided into early childhood education (preschool and kindergarten), K–12 (kindergarten through 12th grade), and higher education (college/university). Formal schooling typically starts around age 5 or 6 and is compulsory until age 16–18, depending on the state. K–12 education, provided mostly by public schools, encompasses elementary (grades K/1–5/6), middle (grades 6–8), and high school (grades 9–12), with some variability by state. Upon completion, students earn a high school diploma, which is required for higher education admission. Higher education includes associate, bachelor’s, and graduate degrees, offered by universities, colleges, and community colleges. Admission is competitive and based on transcripts, standardized test scores (e.g., SAT, ACT), and other factors. Education is primarily delivered in English, with select immersion programs and language accommodations for non-native speakers.

**Attainment, Funding, and Issues:**  
The adult population is highly educated: as of 2022, 89% had finished high school and 34% held a bachelor’s degree or higher. Schools are funded through a mix of federal, state, and local sources, with local property taxes making up a significant portion for K–12. Educational attainment correlates with higher earnings and lower unemployment, though college costs and student loan debt remain major concerns. Efforts to standardize curriculum and assess schools include federal legislation such as No Child Left Behind and Race to the Top, as well as widespread standardized testing. Persistent issues include racial, socioeconomic, and regional achievement gaps; debates over curriculum content; teacher shortages and pay disparities; and the high cost of college and student debt. The system also faces challenges related to inequality in school funding, curriculum and testing controversies, and the impacts of recent Supreme Court decisions on affirmative action in college admissions.
