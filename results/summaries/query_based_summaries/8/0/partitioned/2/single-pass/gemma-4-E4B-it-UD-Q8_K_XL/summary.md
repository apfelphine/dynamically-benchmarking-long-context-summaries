## Education in the United States

**Higher Education and Student Debt:**
In higher education, the median salary for those with a bachelor's degree ($47,345) significantly exceeds that of high school graduates ($27,967). However, student loan debt reached over \$1.5 trillion in 2018, affecting millions of Americans. American universities exhibit diverse structures, ranging from large research institutions to small liberal arts colleges. Post-secondary education is largely deregulated, offering a wide variety of programs, and admission to universities can require multiple factors, including test scores (ACT or SAT), transcripts, and essays.

**K-12 Education and System Issues:**
U.S. education is governed by federal, state, and local policies, and education is mandatory until age 16 (or 18 in some states). K-12 education is divided into elementary and secondary stages. Curriculum standards and testing are highly decentralized; for instance, standardized test scores are often criticized because student performance is influenced by factors outside of school control, such as innate intelligence and socioeconomic status. Significant inequalities exist, as racial achievement gaps persist, with African-American and Hispanic students more likely to face lower grades, lower test scores, and higher dropout rates compared to Asian and Caucasian students. Furthermore, low teacher retention and high salaries for educated peers contribute to teacher shortages, while the system faces debates over funding, credential inflation, and the impact of standardized testing.
