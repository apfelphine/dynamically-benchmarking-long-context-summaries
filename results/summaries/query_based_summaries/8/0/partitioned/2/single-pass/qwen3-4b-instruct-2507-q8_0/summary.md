**Education in the United States** involves a complex system with significant challenges and trends. 

**Higher Education:** 
- The average salary for a college graduate is $47,345, compared to $27,967 for a high school graduate, with engineering degrees yielding significantly higher salaries. 
- Student loan debt peaked at $1.5 trillion in 2018, with over 40 million borrowers, and has created financial barriers for many. 
- The return on investment (ROI) for top 1,000 colleges exceeds 4% over a high school degree. 
- A key issue is the rise of **adjunct professors**, who cost $1,800 per 3-credit class (vs. $8,000 for tenured professors), leading to concerns about teaching quality and grade inflation. 
- **Credential inflation** is criticized, with more college graduates than job openings, and studies showing that increased years of schooling without cognitive gains do not improve economic outcomes.

**Governance and Funding:** 
- Education is decentralized, with states having primary control, and federal oversight through the Department of Education. 
- Public K–12 funding is high—over $11,000 per student annually—though disparities exist, with rural schools facing underfunding due to property tax reliance. 
- School funding is heavily influenced by local property taxes, leading to inequities. 
- Federal funding (e.g., Title 1, IDEA, Child Nutrition) supports specific programs, but funding is subject to cuts or freezes if civil rights violations or fraud are detected. 
- Judicial intervention has occurred in state courts to ensure equitable funding, with cases like *Abbott v. Burke* leading to significant spending increases, though outcomes show little impact on student performance.

**Issues:** 
- **Affirmative action** was overturned in *Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard* (2023), ending race-based admissions. Critics argue it previously helped underrepresented groups, though it led to overrepresentation of recent immigrants. 
- **Corporal punishment** remains legal in 17 U.S. states, with over 167,000 paddlings reported in 2011–2012, primarily in the South. 
- **School safety** includes 93% of schools controlling access and 88% having crisis plans, but concerns remain over student safety and use of facial recognition technology. 
- **Cheating** is widespread, with up to 90% of high school students admitting to it, often due to perceived academic pressure and lack of clear definitions. 
- **Literacy** is taught via a three-cue system criticized for lacking scientific basis, with poor attention to spelling and reading. 
- **Curriculum** is highly decentralized, with no national standard. Standardized testing is used to evaluate performance, but critics argue it fails to account for innate ability, socioeconomic status, or out-of-school factors. 

**Inequality and Systemic Challenges:** 
- The **racial achievement gap** persists, with Black and Hispanic students scoring lower on standardized tests and having lower college completion rates. 
- **Economic disparities** are significant, with wealthier students having better access to test prep and resources, leading to a widening gap. 
- The **school-to-prison pipeline** disproportionately affects disadvantaged youth, linking harsh disciplinary policies to incarceration. 
- **Standardized testing** is criticized for being biased and for reinforcing systemic inequities. 
- **Teacher shortages** are growing, with high turnover rates and low salaries (average $61,600 in 2020–21) contributing to burnout and low job satisfaction. 
- **Teacher pay** is a major issue, with a "pay penalty" where teachers earn less than similarly educated peers in other fields. 

**Reading and Writing Habits:** 
- Libraries are a vital part of education, with Americans borrowing more books than Europeans. 
- Parental involvement is often lacking, and reading failure is linked to delinquency and crime. 

**Summary:** 
The U.S. education system is marked by high costs, inequitable access, and systemic challenges in funding, teacher quality, and curriculum. Despite high public investment, outcomes remain uneven, with significant gaps in achievement, access, and opportunity across race, income, and geography. Policy reforms, such as the end of affirmative action and increased reliance on standardized testing, have intensified debates over equity and effectiveness.
