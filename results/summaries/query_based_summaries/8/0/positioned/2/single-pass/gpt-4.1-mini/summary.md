## Education in the United States

Education in the United States has evolved since the 19th century, beginning with free public elementary schools largely developed in New England, influenced by reformers like Horace Mann. Following the Civil War, high schools expanded, though the South lagged, especially for Black students. Compulsory schooling laws emerged between 1852 and 1918 across states, with federal support via Morrill Land-Grant Acts for agricultural and engineering colleges. The late 19th century saw recommendations for 12 years of education (8 elementary, 4 high school). The 20th century experienced increased high school graduation rates and the spread of private and land grant colleges. Landmark rulings such as Brown v. Board of Education (1954) mandated desegregation. Federal acts, including the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965) and Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (1975), expanded funding and special education. The 21st century brought standardized testing mandates via No Child Left Behind and shifts toward college readiness, along with challenges like funding cuts during the Great Recession, and the impact of COVID-19 on remote learning and institutional finances.

As of 2022, 89% of adults completed high school and 34% obtained a bachelor’s degree or higher. Formal education typically starts with optional preschool (ages 3–5), followed by compulsory K–12 education divided into elementary, middle (junior high), and high school grades 1–12, usually completed by age 18. Post-secondary education involves colleges and universities granting associate, bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, with over 7,000 institutions available. Educational governance is decentralized—states control most public schooling, funded by a mix of local, state, and federal resources. Although U.S. education is widely accessible, persistent inequalities exist across socioeconomic and racial lines, affecting outcomes and access. Teacher shortages, funding disparities, and debates over standardized testing and curriculum content are ongoing issues. The U.S. also features a large private school sector, diverse pathways including vocational education, and complex admissions criteria for higher education. The Supreme Court's 2023 ruling restricted the use of race in college admissions, impacting affirmative action policies. The COVID-19 pandemic shifted delivery methods but highlighted and exacerbated educational inequities.
