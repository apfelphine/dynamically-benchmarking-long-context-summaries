Education in the United States is a decentralized system governed by federal, state, and local authorities, with states holding primary control. Public education is compulsory from kindergarten through 12th grade, and is free at the primary and secondary levels. The system includes a wide range of schools, from public and private to charter and international institutions. 

Key features include a K–12 structure, with students progressing through grades from kindergarten to 12th grade. The curriculum varies by state and district, with no national curriculum, and standardized testing is mandated under laws like No Child Left Behind. 

Higher education is largely self-regulated, with over 4,500 institutions offering undergraduate and graduate degrees. Tuition varies widely, with public universities generally being more affordable than private ones. The cost of higher education has risen significantly, contributing to a growing student loan debt. 

Major challenges include socioeconomic disparities, racial achievement gaps, teacher shortages, and inadequate funding. The system has faced criticism for standardized testing, teacher pay, and unequal access. Despite these issues, the U.S. ranks among the highest in higher education enrollment and research output. 

The system is also marked by significant diversity in educational approaches, including bilingual education, religious instruction, and specialized programs. Recent reforms, such as the Every Student Succeeds Act, aim to improve equity and accountability. The system continues to evolve in response to social, economic, and technological changes.
