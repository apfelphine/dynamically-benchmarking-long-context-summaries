Education in the United States is structured with **compulsory education** from kindergarten through 12th grade, though the exact age varies by state (typically ages 5–18). Public education is free, though many schools charge fees for supplies, uniforms, and extracurricular activities. The system is highly decentralized, with local, state, and federal governance, though federal policies like the **No Child Left Behind Act** and **Every Student Succeeds Act** have influenced accountability measures.

The U.S. education system emphasizes **standardized testing** and **academic rigor**, with a focus on core subjects like math, science, and English. High school graduates must pass standardized exams to earn a diploma, enabling them to pursue higher education. Higher education is optional but highly valued, with over **70% of adults** holding at least a high school diploma and **34%** having a bachelor’s degree or higher. Public universities and colleges are widely accessible, though tuition costs have risen significantly, leading to debates over affordability and student loan debt. The U.S. ranks **10th globally** in college completion rates but faces challenges like **achievement gaps** between racial, socioeconomic, and geographic groups, as well as debates over **curriculum content, teacher quality, and school funding**.
