Education in the United States is governed by a complex structure involving federal, state, and local policies, with states exercising the most control. Public education is generally free, though many schools require "voluntary" fees for expenses not covered by government funding.

The educational stages generally include pre-kindergarten, elementary school (K–6), middle/junior high school (grades 7–9), and high school/senior high school (grades 10–12). Education is mandatory until age 16 in most states, though this varies. Higher education is largely deregulated, with universities offering diverse programs, ranging from large research-oriented institutions to smaller liberal arts colleges. The system faces issues including significant socioeconomic disparities, the impact of standardized testing on student outcomes, and challenges related to teacher shortages and compensation.
