## Education in the United States

Education in the United States evolved significantly from the 19th century, starting with free public elementary schools emphasizing the "three Rs" and expanding into widespread high school education by the early 20th century. Compulsory education laws were progressively implemented nationwide between 1642 and 1919 in most states, leading to universal elementary education by 1930. The system grew to include diverse private parochial schools and large public universities funded via state and federal programs.

Modern U.S. education spans preschool, K–12 (kindergarten through 12th grade), and higher education. Preschool is non-compulsory but widely available through various programs. K–12 education is compulsory up to at least age 16; it includes elementary school (grades K–5 or 6), middle school (grades 6–8 or 7–8), and high school (grades 9–12). Approximately 85% of students attend public schools, funded by federal, state, and local sources, with significant variation and debates over funding equity. The federal government also administers specialized programs for disadvantaged and disabled students.

Higher education is diverse, including research universities, liberal arts colleges, community colleges, vocational schools, and for-profit institutions, with thousands of options and no single federal admission system. College admission considers multiple criteria including standardized tests, grades, essays, and recommendations. Although the U.S. has a high percentage of adults with college degrees, challenges remain with inequality, including racial and economic disparities in educational attainment and access. Issues such as teacher shortages, low pay, and burnout affect education quality. Student loan debt exceeds $1.5 trillion, impacting millions.

Curriculum and standardized testing vary widely by state and district, with federal initiatives like No Child Left Behind and Every Student Succeeds Act aiming to improve accountability and outcomes. However, these tests have faced criticism for cultural bias and pressure on teachers and students. Educational inequality persists, with noted achievement gaps across race and income, influencing economic outcomes and social mobility.

The COVID-19 pandemic shifted much education to online formats, exposing inequities in technology access and impacting learning outcomes. School safety, corporal punishment (legal in some states), and recent Supreme Court decisions on affirmative action have been significant contemporary concerns. Overall, education governance is decentralized with significant local and state control, supplemented by federal regulation and funding.
