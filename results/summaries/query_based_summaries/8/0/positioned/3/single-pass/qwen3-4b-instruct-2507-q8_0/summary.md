Education in the United States is a decentralized system governed by federal, state, and local authorities. It is compulsory from age 5 to 18, with public schools providing free education and private schools offering tuition-based alternatives. The system is divided into early childhood (pre-kindergarten to kindergarten), primary (grades K–5), and secondary (grades 6–12) education, with a K–12 structure that includes a transition to junior and senior high school. Students may pursue vocational training or higher education after high school, with public universities and community colleges offering diverse programs. Higher education is largely self-regulated, with institutions setting their own admission standards, though federal oversight exists through the Department of Education. The U.S. education system emphasizes standardized testing, with significant investment in teacher training and curriculum development. Despite challenges such as teacher shortages, socioeconomic disparities, and inequality in access to quality education, the U.S. ranks highly in terms of student outcomes, with strong performance in reading and a growing emphasis on STEM fields. The system faces ongoing debates over curriculum, teacher pay, and equitable access, particularly for marginalized communities. The rise of online learning and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic have further reshaped educational practices, highlighting the need for equitable access to technology and digital resources.
