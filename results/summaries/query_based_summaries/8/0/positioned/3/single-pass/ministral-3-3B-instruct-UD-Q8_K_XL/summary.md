Education in the United States is structured into **K–12 (Kindergarten to Grade 12) and higher education**, with compulsory education mandated until age 16 (or 18 in some states). The system is governed by federal, state, and local education policies, with public education being free and primarily funded by local and state taxes, supplemented by federal grants. Key features include:

- **K–12 Education**: Public schools cover grades K–12, with most students attending from ages 5–18. The curriculum varies by district, with standardized testing (e.g., NAPLAN) assessing literacy and numeracy. Private and homeschooling options exist, with homeschooling being a significant alternative.
- **Higher Education**: Over 4,495 colleges and universities offer degrees, including public institutions (funded by state governments) and private schools (often more expensive). Admission criteria include standardized tests (SAT/ACT), high school transcripts, and extracurricular activities. Public universities are more affordable for in-state residents, while private institutions charge higher tuition. Higher education is highly valued, with 34% of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, though disparities exist based on socioeconomic background.

**Funding and Challenges**:
- Public K–12 education receives substantial funding, with spending per student varying widely by state. Federal funding covers specific programs (e.g., Title I for low-income students), but local taxes remain the primary source.
- Higher education faces rising costs, with student loan debt reaching over $1.5 trillion. Issues include teacher shortages, credential inflation (excessive degrees for jobs), and disparities in educational outcomes tied to race, income, and geography.

**Curriculum and Assessment**:
- Curricula emphasize core subjects (math, science, reading) but vary in depth and focus. Standardized testing is a contentious issue, with debates over its fairness and impact on student performance.
- The U.S. ranks below average in science and mathematics literacy compared to other developed nations, though reading literacy is near universal. The No Child Left Behind Act and subsequent reforms aimed to improve equity and accountability, though implementation remains uneven.

**Social and Political Context**:
- Education policy is shaped by federal initiatives like the Every Student Succeeds Act, which replaced No Child Left Behind, and state-level reforms. Affirmative action in college admissions was overturned by the Supreme Court in 2023, affecting diversity in higher education.
- Issues like school violence, teacher shortages, and the school-to-prison pipeline persist, reflecting broader societal challenges. The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted education, leading to disparities in access to digital learning and mental health support.
