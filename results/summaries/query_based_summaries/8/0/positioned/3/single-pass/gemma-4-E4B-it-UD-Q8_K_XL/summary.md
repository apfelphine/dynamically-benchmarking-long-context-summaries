**Education in the United States**

In the U.S., education policy is shared between federal, state, and local governments, with states exercising most control over public education. The system is structured into stages: K-12 education, which is compulsory until age 16 (or 18 in some states), is divided into elementary school and secondary school. Post-secondary education is largely optional and includes community/junior colleges (offering associate or vocational degrees) and four-year universities (issuing bachelor's, master's, or doctorate degrees). While private schools are widespread, many public schools rely on local, state, and federal funding, though disparities exist between wealthy and rural districts.

Educational attainment is highly valued in American society, and higher education is often a key determinant of class and status, although there are significant socioeconomic gaps in graduation rates and college readiness benchmarks. The system is subject to ongoing debate regarding standardized testing, the influence of socioeconomic background on student outcomes, and issues such as teacher shortages, low pay, and the growing national student loan debt.
