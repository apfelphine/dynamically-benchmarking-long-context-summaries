# Education in the United States

Education in the United States has evolved over centuries, beginning with early free public elementary schooling in New England and progressive reforms led by figures like Horace Mann in the 19th century. Compulsory education laws were established statewide between 1852 and 1917. The system expanded to include high schools post-Civil War and saw the rise of private religious schools alongside public institutions. The 20th century featured landmark court decisions (e.g., Brown v. Board of Education) to desegregate schools and federal legislation to support education for all, including the disadvantaged and disabled. The No Child Left Behind Act (2001) and Every Student Succeeds Act (2015) aimed to increase accountability and standardized testing nationwide, although such testing remains controversial.

The U.S. education system is decentralized, with governance shared by federal, state, and local governments, and significant variation in curricula and funding. Public education is generally free and compulsory until ages 14 to 18 depending on the state, with about 85% of students attending public schools. Educational stages comprise preschool, elementary (K-6), middle (6-8), and high school (9-12), followed by optional higher education at colleges and universities. Postsecondary education is diverse, ranging from community colleges offering vocational training to elite research universities. Despite high spending per student, studies indicate persistent achievement gaps across socioeconomic and racial groups, with challenges including teacher shortages, standardized testing biases, and educational inequality. Student loan debt has surged, and COVID-19 introduced significant disruptions, particularly widening educational disparities.

Overall, while literacy rates are high and many Americans attain college degrees, critiques highlight uneven access, quality disparities, systemic inequities, and debates over admissions policies, curriculum content, and school safety. The educational landscape remains complex, balancing federal initiatives with strong local control and diverse institutional types.
