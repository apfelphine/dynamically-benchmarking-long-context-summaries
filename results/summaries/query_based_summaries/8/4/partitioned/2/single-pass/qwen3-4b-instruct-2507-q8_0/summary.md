**Education in Australia**

Australia's education system is structured into government and non-government schools, with government schools (64% of students) receiving state funding and non-government schools (Catholic and private) receiving federal funding. The Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) oversees a national curriculum in primary and secondary education, emphasizing literacy, numeracy, science, and social studies. 

Schooling is compulsory from age 6 to 17, with a national year 10–12 retention rate of 79.9%. Preschool education (ages 3–5) is encouraged through state-funded programs aligned with the Early Years Learning Framework. 

Schools vary by state in structure: primary education typically spans Years 1–6 (7 years), with some states like South Australia offering 8 years. Secondary education covers Years 7–12, with many schools offering junior and senior high divisions. Combined schools (K–12) and central schools in rural areas serve students across primary and secondary levels. 

Non-government schools include Catholic schools (19.7% of students) and private schools (16.3%), which charge tuition. Religious education is offered in government schools through optional special religious education classes. 

At the tertiary level, public universities dominate, with student fees subsidized via the HECS loan scheme. Australia ranks 16th in reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science in PISA, though it ranks second globally in the Education Index (0.929). 

Funding is managed by states and territories, with the federal government supporting non-government schools and public universities. Challenges include school funding disparities, Indigenous student achievement gaps, and efforts to improve equity through initiatives like the Better and Fairer Schools Agreement (BFSA). 

The system emphasizes equity, access, and quality, with ongoing reforms to curriculum, teacher training, and school governance.
