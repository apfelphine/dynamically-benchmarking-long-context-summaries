Education in Australia is structured around a mix of government and non-government schools, with the majority of students attending government schools (64% in 2023). The system is governed by state and territory education departments, with the federal government providing funding for non-government schools and subsidizing tertiary education through student loans. Compulsory education spans from age 5 to 17, with a national curriculum implemented since 2010, covering subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies. Primary education typically lasts seven years (pre-Year 1 to Year 6), focusing on foundational skills, while secondary education extends from Year 7 to Year 12, offering specialized tracks in STEM, humanities, and vocational fields.

The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) unifies qualifications across schools, vocational education, and tertiary education, ensuring consistency and recognition. Standardized assessments like NAPLAN (National Assessment Program—Literacy and Numeracy) measure student proficiency annually. While international rankings have shown mixed results, Australia’s education system emphasizes equity, access, and lifelong learning, with a strong emphasis on early childhood education and continuous support for students. The system also includes specialized schools for students with disabilities, Indigenous education initiatives, and programs to address school violence and improve teacher retention.
