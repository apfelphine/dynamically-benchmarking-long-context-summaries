## Education in Australia

Education in Australia is primarily governed at the state and territory level, with the federal government providing substantial funding especially for non-government schools and tertiary education. Compulsory education was progressively introduced between 1871 and 1916 across states. In 2023, Australia had over 4.1 million students enrolled across 9,629 primary, secondary, and special schools, with 64% attending government schools, 19.7% in Catholic schools, and 16.3% in independent private schools. The Australian Curriculum, managed by the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority, sets consistent national standards from pre-Year 1 to Year 12, focusing on a broad range of subjects including English, mathematics, sciences, social studies, arts, and languages.

Preschool education is widely encouraged but not mandatory, typically serving children aged 3 to 5 and often incorporating the Early Years Learning Framework. Government schools offer free education, with some voluntary fees, and are primarily zoned based on local districts unless special dispensation applies. Non-government schools, including Catholic systemic and independent private schools, receive government funding supplemented by tuition fees that can vary widely. At the tertiary level, Australia hosts 42 universities (mostly public), with strong international student enrolment making it a top global education provider. Challenges persist such as educational decline noted in international assessments, funding debates, and disparities in Indigenous education outcomes, including national efforts focused on closing educational gaps for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students.
