## Education in Australia

**Structure and Governance:**
The regulation, operation, and funding of education in Australia are the responsibility of the states and territories, though the Australian Government provides support, such as subsidizing tertiary education through a national student loan scheme (HECS). The Australian Qualifications Framework, implemented in 1995, provides a national system spanning higher education, vocational education and training (VET), and school-based education. At the primary and secondary levels, government schools educate approximately 64% of students, while non-government schools make up the remainder.

**Key Educational Features and Standards:**
A national Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented since 2010 for primary and secondary schools. Compulsory attendance requirements vary by state or territory. Tertiary education is dominated by public universities. Nationally, Australia has seen strong international enrollment, ranking as a major provider of international education. Performance metrics show mixed results; while the Education Index listed Australia second-highest in the world in 2018, 2018 PISA results indicated specific declines in reading, maths, and science scores.

**Curriculum and Early Years:**
The Australian system includes various types of schools, including government, non-government (such as Catholic and private schools), and specialized schools. Preschool provision is a relatively recent innovation, and federal and state legislation requires preschool services to follow the nationally approved Early Years Learning Framework.
