Education in Australia features a national system of qualifications underpinned by the Australian Qualifications Framework, covering school, vocational, and tertiary education. Compulsory education begins at age 5–6 and continues until age 15–17, with government schools educating approximately 64% of students and non-government schools (including Catholic and private schools) making up the remainder. The Australian Curriculum, developed since 2010, sets national standards for primary and secondary education, while the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) administers curriculum and assessment. 

Primary and secondary education are structured into key stages, with primary schools typically covering pre-Kindergarten to Year 6 and secondary schools from Year 7 to Year 12. School years generally run from late January/early February to mid-December for primary and secondary schools, and from late February to mid-November for tertiary institutions. A national year 10 to year 12 retention rate of 79.9% reflects student engagement. 

Preschool education, introduced in the 1980s, is not mandatory but encouraged by the federal government, with state legislation requiring early childhood programs to align with the Early Years Learning Framework. Government schools are funded by state and territory governments, while non-government schools receive federal funding and require co-payments from parents. 

Tertiary education is primarily delivered through public universities and registered training organizations (TAFE), with the Group of Eight (Go8) comprising eight prestigious universities. International students make up a significant portion of the tertiary sector, with Australia ranking as a top destination. The Education Index (2018) ranked Australia second-highest globally, though PISA results showed relatively low performance in maths and science. 

Funding for schools is managed at the state and territory level, with federal support through student loans (HECS) and grants. The Gonski Reports (2010 and 2018) recommended equitable funding based on student needs, with funding agreements implemented in most states from 2019. Indigenous students face persistent educational disadvantages, with targeted initiatives to improve outcomes in early childhood, literacy, and school attendance. Bilingual education programs, particularly for Indigenous and CALD communities, are supported by federal and state governments. 

The education system emphasizes equity, with policies promoting inclusive access, quality teaching, and student well-being. Challenges remain in addressing disparities in rural and remote areas, school funding, and student outcomes, with ongoing reforms to curriculum, assessment, and teacher education.
