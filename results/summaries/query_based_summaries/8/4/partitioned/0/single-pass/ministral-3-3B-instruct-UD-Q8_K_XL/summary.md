Education in Australia is structured around a national framework with significant state and territory autonomy in regulation and funding. Compulsory education began in the late 19th century, with varying timelines across states, ranging from 1871 (Western Australia) to 1916 (Tasmania). Today, primary and secondary education is predominantly delivered by government schools, which educate approximately 64% of students, while non-government schools (Catholic and independent) cater to the remaining 36%. Government schools operate free of tuition, though some charge voluntary fees for additional expenses. The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF), implemented in 1995, unifies qualifications across higher education, vocational education and training (VET), and school-based education.

The national curriculum, developed since 2010, is progressively implemented, covering subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies. Tertiary education is heavily subsidized, with public universities being the primary providers. Student fees are managed through the Higher Education Contribution Scheme (HECS), where payments are deferred until income surpasses a certain threshold. The system emphasizes a national approach to qualifications, ensuring consistency and transparency in education outcomes.

Education in Australia is characterized by a mix of public and private institutions, with a strong emphasis on equity and access. The country ranks highly in international education metrics, though challenges remain in addressing disparities in educational outcomes, particularly among Indigenous students. The system continues to evolve to meet global standards and ensure high-quality education for all students.
