# Education in Australia

Education in Australia is primarily administered and funded at the state and territory level, with the federal government providing significant funding support particularly for non-government schools and tertiary education. Approximately two-thirds (64%) of Australian students attend government (public) schools, which are operated by state agencies and offer free education, though additional voluntary fees for materials and activities may be requested. Non-government schools, including Catholic and independent schools, educate the remaining students and receive government funding supplemented by tuition fees paid by parents.

Australia’s education system is supported by the Australian Qualifications Framework, unifying qualifications across school, vocational, and tertiary sectors, with a national Australian Curriculum implemented progressively since 2010. Compulsory schooling age ranges from approximately 5-6 to 15-17 years, depending on jurisdiction, with a 79.9% apparent retention rate through years 10 to 12 in 2024. Specialized school types include selective government schools, special schools, and specialist schools focusing on areas like science or the arts. The country hosts 42 universities (mostly public), enrolling over 1.1 million students in 2021, and is a leading international education provider, with international students comprising around 26.7% of university enrollments as of 2019. Australia’s educational outcomes are highly ranked globally, with continuous efforts to address disparities, especially for Indigenous students, and ongoing reforms in school funding and bilingual education support.
