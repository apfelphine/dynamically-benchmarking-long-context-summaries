## Education in Australia

**Organisational Structure and Funding:**
Education in Australia is administered by the states and territories, as the Commonwealth government lacks specific constitutional power in this area. Overall, government schools educate approximately two-thirds of all school students, while non-government schools account for the remainder. Government schools, often called state or public schools, offer free education, although many may charge contribution fees for materials and services. Non-government schools, which operate under state or territory government authority but are not run by government departments, receive funding from the Commonwealth government.

**Curriculum and Assessment:**
A national Australian Curriculum has been progressively developed and implemented since 2010 to set expectations for what students across the nation should be taught. Students participate in national standardized assessments, such as NAPLAN, which annually measures literacy and numeracy achievement. The Australian Qualifications Framework unifies national qualifications across school-based education, vocational education and training (VET), and tertiary education.

**Key Data Points:**
In 2023, there were 4,086,998 students enrolled in primary, secondary, and special schools. In terms of educational attainment, the Education Index ranked Australia second-highest globally in 2018 (based on 2017 data). For tertiary education, there are 42 universities, comprising 37 public and 3 private ones.
