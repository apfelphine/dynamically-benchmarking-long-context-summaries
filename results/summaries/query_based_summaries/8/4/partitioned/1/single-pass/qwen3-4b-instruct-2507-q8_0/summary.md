**Education in Australia**

Australia's education system is structured into government and non-government schools. Government schools, which serve approximately two-thirds of students, are free and managed by state or territory departments, with some offering voluntary fees for supplies and activities. Non-government schools, including Catholic and private institutions, operate independently and receive federal funding. 

Schools are categorized into open or comprehensive, selective, special, and specialist schools, with selective schools admitting students based on academic merit. Special schools cater to students with learning difficulties or disabilities, while specialist schools offer focused programs in arts, sports, or STEM. 

The Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) oversees national curriculum standards, and all schools follow the same framework. Students in primary and secondary education are assessed through the National Assessment Program – Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN). 

Compulsory education spans from age 5 to 17, with students required to attend school until the minimum leaving age. The education system is supported by a national framework, with funding distributed through state and federal governments. 

In tertiary education, public universities dominate, and students access degrees through a national system. Australia ranks highly in international education, attracting over 800,000 international students. Despite strong performance in some areas, Australia faces challenges such as educational inequality, particularly for Indigenous students, and school funding disparities.
