## Education in Australia

Education in Australia consists of government and non-government schools, with government schools educating about 64% of students and non-government schools (including Catholic and independent private schools) making up the remainder. The system is regulated by state and territory governments, while the federal government provides significant funding, especially for non-government schools, and supports tertiary education through student loan schemes like HECS. Education is compulsory from around ages five or six until at least 15 to 17, depending on jurisdiction, with a national Australian Curriculum progressively implemented since 2010. Early childhood education includes preschool or kindergarten programs, offered primarily by state and territory governments or community groups, with about 85.7% attendance in the year before formal schooling.

Primary and secondary education cover from pre-Year 1 to Year 12 (or equivalent), with obligatory school uniforms and regulated funding largely provided by state governments for public schools. Non-government schools often charge tuition fees. The school year typically runs from late January or early February to December, with student enrolments exceeding 4 million in recent years and teaching staff numbering over 300,000 full-time equivalents. Specialised schools include selective, special education, specialist arts or sports schools, and international schools offering curricula such as the International Baccalaureate. Australia also supports bilingual and Indigenous language education in some regions. The tertiary sector comprises 42 universities—mostly public—with significant international student populations, positioning Australia as a leading provider of international education globally. The education sector faces ongoing funding challenges, especially regarding implementation of the Gonski funding reforms and addressing educational disparities affecting Indigenous Australians.
