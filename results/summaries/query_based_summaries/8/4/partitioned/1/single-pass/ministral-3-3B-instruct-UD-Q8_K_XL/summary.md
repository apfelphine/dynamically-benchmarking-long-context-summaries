Education in Australia is structured primarily into government and non-government schools, with government schools (public schools) educating approximately two-thirds of students. These schools operate under state government agencies, offer free education, and include various types such as open or comprehensive schools, selective, special, and independent public schools. Contributions for school-related costs (e.g., uniforms, materials) are often voluntary but common, with an estimated average cost of around $316 per year per child in 2010.

Non-government schools, which include Catholic and private schools, receive significant federal funding and cater to diverse educational needs. Catholic schools, the second-largest provider of school-based education in Australia, educate about one in five students, employing nearly 60,000 teachers. Private schools vary widely in governance and curriculum, with fees ranging from under $100 to $3,200 monthly depending on the student’s year level and socio-economic background. Some private schools are religiously affiliated, while others follow international curricula or pedagogical philosophies.

Education in Australia is compulsory from the age of five to six until the minimum leaving age, which varies by state. The Australian Curriculum, Assessment, and Reporting Authority (ACARA) oversees the national curriculum, ensuring consistency across states and territories. The system includes primary education (pre-Year 1 to Year 6), secondary education (Year 7 to Year 12), and specialized programs for gifted students, arts, and sports. The National Assessment Program – Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN) assesses students' literacy and numeracy skills annually in Years 3, 5, 7, and 9.

Funding for education is primarily managed by state and territory governments, with the federal government contributing to non-government schools and public universities. The Gonski Report and subsequent reforms aimed to improve school funding equity, though implementation has faced challenges. Indigenous students face significant educational disparities, with lower participation in early childhood education, reading, writing, and numeracy proficiency, and lower Year 12 attainment rates compared to non-Indigenous students. Bilingual education programs, particularly for Indigenous students, have been introduced to support language maintenance and revitalization.

School funding remains a contentious issue, with debates over equity and adequacy of resources. School violence and safety are ongoing concerns, though efforts are being made to address these through various initiatives. Tertiary education, including universities and vocational education and training (VET), is widely accessible, with international students contributing significantly to Australia’s education sector. The country is recognized for its high-quality education system, ranking highly in global indices like the Education Index and PISA.
