Education in Australia is structured into **government schools** (64% of students) and **non-government schools** (36%), including Catholic (19.7%) and independent (16.3%) schools. Compulsory education spans ages 6–15, with a national curriculum administered by the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA). The system emphasizes free education, though many schools charge voluntary fees for supplies and activities.

**Funding** is primarily state-based, with the federal government providing partial support for non-government schools. The **Gonski Report (2012)** and subsequent agreements aimed to improve equity in school funding, though implementation varied by state. Indigenous students face significant educational disparities, with gaps in literacy, numeracy, and school attendance persisting despite efforts like the **Closing the Gap** targets.

**Bilingual education** has historical roots, particularly in Indigenous communities, with programs like "both-ways" learning (e.g., Yirrkala School) aimed at language revitalization. Religious education in government schools is secular, though special religious instruction is offered in some non-government schools.

**Tertiary education** is heavily subsidized, with public universities and vocational education (TAFE) being the primary providers. International students form a significant portion of tertiary education, contributing to Australia’s economy. The system follows the **Australian Qualifications Framework**, ensuring consistency across schools, vocational training, and higher education.

**Performance** shows mixed results, with Australian students ranking **16th in reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science** in the 2018 PISA study, though literacy rates remain high. Challenges include **underfunding, teacher shortages, and disparities in rural and urban areas**. The COVID-19 pandemic further strained resources, highlighting the need for digital infrastructure and teacher training to support remote learning.
