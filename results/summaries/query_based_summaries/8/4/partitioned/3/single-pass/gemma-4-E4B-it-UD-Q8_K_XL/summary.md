## Education in Australia

**School Education Structure and Funding:**
In Australia, education regulation, operation, and funding are the responsibility of the states and territories, as the federal government lacks specific constitutional power over education. For primary and secondary education, the Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented since 2010. Government schools educate about two-thirds of students, while non-government schools account for the rest, with Catholic and independent schools being major non-government providers. While government schools offer education without compulsory tuition fees, many ask for "voluntary" fees. Federal funding helps support non-government schools and public universities, and successive agreements, such as the Better and Fairer Schools Agreement (BFSA), dictate funding levels. School education is compulsory across various state or territory ages, generally from age five to fifteen or seventeen, depending on the jurisdiction.

**Specific Educational Issues:**
Key areas of focus include Indigenous primary and secondary education, where Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children face significant educational disadvantages. Bilingual education exists for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and immigrant (CALD) groups. Regarding religious instruction, the country is constitutionally secular, but practices vary significantly across states, with some allowing religious instruction in government schools. Furthermore, the system incorporates specialist schools and selective schools, which operate based on specific criteria.
