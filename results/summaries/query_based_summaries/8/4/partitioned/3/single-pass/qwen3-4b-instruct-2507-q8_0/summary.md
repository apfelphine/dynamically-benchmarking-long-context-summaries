**Education in Australia**

Australia's education system is structured into government and non-government schools, with government schools educating approximately 64% of students and non-government schools (including Catholic and private) making up the remainder. The system is governed at the state and territory level, with federal oversight through the Department of Education, Skills and Employment. A national curriculum, developed and implemented since 2010, ensures consistent standards across all schools, while each state maintains its own curriculum framework.

Primary and secondary education is compulsory from age 5 to 17, with students progressing through a structured sequence of grades. The Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) oversees curriculum delivery, while the national assessment program NAPLAN measures literacy and numeracy skills annually. Schools are categorized into types including comprehensive, selective, specialist, and international schools, with a focus on equity and quality.

Funding for government schools is primarily provided by state and territory governments, while non-government schools receive funding from both the federal and state governments. A key reform, the Gonski Report (2012), recommended equitable funding based on student needs, leading to a national school funding agreement that increased federal funding to public schools to 25% from 20% in 2024–2025. This agreement ensures schools receive funding based on enrolment, student characteristics, and indexation.

Indigenous education remains a critical area of focus, with ongoing efforts to close the gap in outcomes such as literacy, numeracy, and school attendance. Bilingual education programs, particularly in remote communities, have shown effectiveness in improving student outcomes and retention. Religious education is permitted in government schools through the National School Chaplaincy Program, which emphasizes pastoral care over religious instruction.

Tertiary education is predominantly public and highly subsidized through a student loan program (HECS-HELP), with over 1,185,000 students enrolled in universities and vocational institutions. Australia ranks among the world's top providers of international education, with a high ratio of international students. The education system is evaluated through international benchmarks such as PISA, where Australian students placed 16th in reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science in 2018, though performance has seen a decline in recent years.

The system faces challenges in funding equity, teacher retention, and student outcomes, particularly for disadvantaged and Indigenous students. Ongoing reforms aim to improve access, equity, and quality, with a focus on modernizing curricula, enhancing teacher support, and ensuring inclusive education for all.
