**Education in Australia**

Australia has a decentralized education system, with education policy and funding primarily managed by state and territory governments. The federal government provides funding and oversight, particularly for non-government schools and tertiary education. Education is compulsory from age 5 to 17, with a national curriculum implemented since 2010.

Schools are broadly categorized into government and non-government institutions. Government schools (64% of students) are publicly funded and free, while non-government schools (36%) include Catholic and private schools, which charge tuition fees. All schools follow a national curriculum administered by the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA), with a focus on core subjects such as English, mathematics, science, and social studies.

Primary education spans from Kindergarten to Year 6, and secondary education covers Years 7 to 12. The education system includes a range of school types, including selective, specialist, and international schools. Kindergarten is now compulsory, with a focus on early childhood development and social skills.

The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) governs qualifications from early childhood through tertiary education. Students in secondary education take standardized assessments such as NAPLAN, which measures literacy and numeracy. Tertiary education includes public universities, private institutions, and vocational training providers.

Australia faces challenges in funding, with ongoing debates over equitable school funding and classroom resources. Indigenous students face significant educational disparities, with efforts underway to close the gap in achievement and attendance. Bilingual education programs support Indigenous language preservation, and religious education is permitted in government schools under specific conditions.

The country's education system is internationally recognized, with Australia ranked among the top in the world for education outcomes in the UN's Human Development Index. However, performance in international assessments like PISA has declined, prompting reforms to improve student outcomes. The government has committed to increasing investment in education, with a $53 billion education budget in 2024–25.
