## Education in Australia

Education in Australia is primarily the responsibility of the states and territories, with the federal government providing funding support, particularly to non-government schools and public universities. Compulsory schooling ranges typically from ages five or six to fifteen to seventeen, depending on the region. The Australian Qualifications Framework, established in 1995, unifies qualifications across school, vocational education and training (VET), and tertiary education sectors. A national Australian Curriculum has been implemented progressively since 2010, covering core subjects in primary and secondary education.

Approximately 64% of students attend government schools, with the remainder in Catholic or independent schools. Government schools are free but may request voluntary contributions; non-government schools charge fees. School years typically run from late January or early February to December. Early childhood education (preschool) is not mandatory but widely attended, aiming to prepare children from about age three to five before primary schooling.

Secondary education generally includes Years 7 to 12, moving from general to specialized subjects, with options for selective, specialist, or combined schools. The Australian Curriculum Assessment and Reporting Authority administers a national curriculum framework and coordinates national assessments, including NAPLAN, to measure literacy and numeracy. The school system accommodates co-educational and single-sex schools, day and boarding schools, and offers diverse specialist programs. 

Australia has a strong international education presence, ranking as the third-largest provider globally in 2012, with international students forming a significant portion of tertiary enrolments. In 2023, over 4 million students were enrolled in primary and secondary education, supported by about 311,000 full-time teaching staff. Tertiary education includes 42 universities (mostly public), vocational training providers, and registered training organisations, with over 1.18 million university students in 2021. Australian universities rank highly worldwide, with several institutions featured in global rankings. 

Key challenges include Indigenous educational disparities, with government targets aimed at closing gaps in attendance and achievement. Bilingual and Indigenous language programs exist, particularly in the Northern Territory, to support language maintenance and educational outcomes. Australian education funding has undergone reforms following the Gonski reports, aiming for more equitable and efficient allocation of resources. Despite these efforts, the system faces ongoing issues of educational outcomes and equity.
