## Education in Australia

Education in Australia is regulated and funded primarily by state and territory governments, with significant federal involvement in non-government schools and tertiary education. Schooling is compulsory for children generally between the ages of six and sixteen/seventeen, varying by jurisdiction. Government schools educate about two-thirds of students, while Catholic and independent schools account for the remainder, all following the Australian Curriculum framework. Preschool education, though not mandatory, is widely attended the year before formal schooling, with government funding support emphasizing quality early learning. Primary education spans from the "pre-Year 1" (such as Kindergarten or Prep) to Year 6, focusing on foundational literacy, numeracy, and social skills, while secondary education typically continues from Year 7 to Year 12, with increasing specialization and opportunities in STEM and humanities.

Australia’s tertiary sector, comprising universities and technical and further education (TAFE) institutions, is largely public, with significant private and international student enrollment. Student fees are subsidized via the HECS income-contingent loan program. Australia is a major destination for international students, and education forms a substantial national export. Scholar and school performance is monitored through national assessments like NAPLAN and international studies like PISA, with recent years showing declining outcomes and achievement gaps, particularly for Indigenous students. School types include government, Catholic, and private schools, alongside specialist, selective, and boarding options. Recent policy initiatives address funding equity, Indigenous education, bilingual programs, school violence, and continual refinement of curriculum and resourcing models to improve transparency and quality across the system.
