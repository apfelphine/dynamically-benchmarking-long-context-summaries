Education in Australia is structured into **primary (Kindergarten to Year 6)**, **secondary (Years 7–12)**, and **tertiary (university and vocational education)** education, with a national curriculum implemented since 2010. Compulsory education begins at age 6, extending to Year 12, with a focus on literacy, numeracy, and social skills. The system is divided into **government schools** (64% of students), **Catholic schools** (19.7%), and **independent schools** (16.3%), each regulated by state and territory education departments.

Key features include:
- **National Curriculum**: Standardized across states, covering subjects like English, math, science, and social studies, with assessment through standardized tests like NAPLAN.
- **School Funding**: Primarily state-funded, with the federal government supporting non-government schools and subsidizing tertiary education via student loans.
- **Performance**: Australian students ranked 16th in reading, 29th in math, and 17th in science in the 2022 PISA study, though proficiency rates below 60% in core subjects remain a concern.
- **Special Education**: Special schools cater to students with disabilities, while selective schools offer advanced programs for gifted students.
- **International Students**: Australia attracts significant international students, with a focus on tertiary education as a major export sector.

The system emphasizes equity, though disparities persist, particularly in **Indigenous education**, where attendance and academic outcomes lag behind non-Indigenous peers. Funding reforms, such as the Gonski Review, aim to address these gaps, though implementation has been slow.
