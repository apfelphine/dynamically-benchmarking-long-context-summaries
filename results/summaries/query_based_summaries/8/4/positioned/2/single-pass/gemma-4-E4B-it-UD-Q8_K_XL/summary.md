**Education in Australia**

In Australia, the responsibility for regulating, operating, and funding education rests with the individual states and territories, as the federal government lacks specific constitutional power over education laws. The Commonwealth Government, through departments like the Department of Education, Skills and Employment, assists by funding non-government schools and public universities, and by subsidizing tertiary education via a student loan program (HECS). A unified national system of qualifications, the Australian Qualifications Framework, has been in place since 1995, encompassing school-based education, vocational education and training (VET), and higher education.

For primary and secondary education, a national Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented since 2010. Compulsory attendance requirements vary by state or territory, typically ranging from ages five to six through fifteen to seventeen. Education delivery is split between government schools (which educate approximately 64% of students) and non-government schools (including Catholic and private institutions). Tertiary education includes 37 public universities and 3 private universities, with a large proportion of these being public. Australia has also implemented national standardized assessments, such as NAPLAN, to measure literacy and numeracy achievement.
