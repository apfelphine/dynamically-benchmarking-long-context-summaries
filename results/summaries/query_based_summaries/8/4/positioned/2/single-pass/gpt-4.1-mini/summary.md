# Summary of Education in Australia

Education in Australia is primarily regulated and funded by state and territory governments, with the federal government providing support to non-government schools and public universities. Compulsory education laws were introduced progressively across states from 1871 to 1916. Government schools educate about 64% of students, while Catholic and independent schools account for the remainder. Early childhood education (preschool/kindergarten) is widely attended but not mandatory nationwide. The national Australian Curriculum, developed since 2010, guides learning from primary through senior secondary levels, with school years starting typically in late January/early February and ending by December.

Primary education generally covers pre-Year 1 through Year 6, while secondary education spans Years 7 to 12, with compulsory attendance varying by state between ages 5-6 up to 15-17. There are specialized schools including selective, specialist, and special-needs schools. Non-government schools include Catholic systemic schools and private institutions with varied religious affiliations. School uniforms are common across sectors. Australia employs national assessments such as NAPLAN to measure literacy and numeracy, with mixed results in international comparisons like PISA.

Tertiary education comprises public and private universities, colleges, and vocational training (TAFE), with over 1.18 million students in 2021. Australia is a top destination for international students, who make up a significant portion of university enrolments. Student loans under the HECS scheme assist with higher education costs. Rankings indicate Australia has some highly ranked universities globally, although educational outcomes have shown a decline in recent decades according to PISA, despite a high Education Index globally. Issues include funding debates, Indigenous education disparities, bilingualism challenges especially for Indigenous languages, debates over religious education in public schools, and concerns about school violence and funding reforms.
