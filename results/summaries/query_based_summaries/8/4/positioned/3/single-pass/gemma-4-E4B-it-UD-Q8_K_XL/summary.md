The provided texts focus exclusively on the education systems of Malaysia, Australia, the United States, and Finland.

**Education in Australia:**
The regulation, operation, and funding of education are managed by the states and territories, although the federal government provides support and subsidizes tertiary education through a student loan program (HECS). Australia utilizes the Australian Qualifications Framework, which encompasses school-based, vocational (VET), and tertiary education. A national Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented since 2010 for primary and secondary schools. Compulsory attendance varies by state and territory, generally ranging from ages five to sixteen or seventeen. Government schools educate about two-thirds of students, while non-government schools (Catholic and private) educate the remainder.

**Education in Finland:**
The Finnish education system is highly decentralized, with most schools being funded and administered by municipalities. The compulsory education system spans from age seven to eighteen and includes a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1–9). The system emphasizes learning through play, holistic well-being, and individualized development, with no high-stakes tests in basic education. Upper secondary education (starting at age 15 or 16) is compulsory and students can choose between academic tracks or vocational tracks. Both primary and upper secondary education are free for domestic students, though non-EEA students must pay tuition fees.

**Education in Malaysia:**
Education in Malaysia has evolved from early Islamic schools to a modern system overseen by the federal government's Ministry of Education. The Razak Report of 1956 established the framework for a national school system featuring Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil-medium schools. Public primary schools are divided into Malay-medium National Schools (SK) and non-Malay-medium National-type Schools (SJK). Secondary education is generally provided through National Secondary Schools (SMK) with Malay as the main medium, although the Dual Language Program (DLP) exists to enhance English proficiency.

**Education in the United States:**
The U.S. education system is decentralized, with states exercising the majority of control over public education, though the federal government influences policy through various acts. Education is compulsory until age 16 (or 18 in some states). The system is divided into elementary school (Kindergarten through 6th grade) and secondary education (middle/junior high and high school). Public education is typically free, while private schools operate independently. The system faces challenges related to socioeconomic disparities, standardized testing controversies, and teacher shortages.
