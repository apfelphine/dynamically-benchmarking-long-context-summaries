## Education in Australia: Key Features

Education in Australia is regulated primarily by state and territory governments, with the federal government playing a funding and policy role, especially in non-government schools and universities. Schooling is compulsory from ages 5 or 6 to 15–17, depending on jurisdiction, and is organized into preschool, primary (Years K–6 or K–7), and secondary (Years 7–12) levels. Government schools educate about 64% of students; the remainder attend Catholic or independent (private) schools, both of which receive substantial government funding in addition to tuition fees. A national Australian Curriculum, covering subjects like English, maths, science, social studies, arts, technology, health, and foreign languages, has been progressively implemented since 2010.

Preschool is not mandatory but widely attended, and the government aims to provide universal access, especially emphasizing play-based learning. Secondary education offers increased specialization and culminates in state-issued Senior Secondary Certificates that provide pathways to university, vocational training, or employment. Selective schools admit students based on exams or criteria, while special schools serve those with additional needs. Most schools are co-educational, and about 2.5% offer boarding facilities. Performance on international assessments such as PISA is above the OECD average, though recent trends show some decline.

## Tertiary Education and International Engagement

Australia’s tertiary sector includes 42 universities (mostly public), vocational education and training (VET) providers (including TAFEs), and a unified national qualification system—the Australian Qualifications Framework. University fees are subsidized via income-contingent loans (HECS). Australian universities are globally recognized, with several ranking highly internationally. Australia is a top destination for international students, who comprise a significant portion of enrolments and contribute notably to the economy. School-level education for international students is regulated separately.

Recent policy efforts have focused on equitable funding across schools through agreements like the Better and Fairer Schools Agreement, which boosts public school funding. Other ongoing issues include achievement gaps for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students, school violence, religious education policy, and debates over bilingual and multicultural education. Australia’s approach continues to evolve toward greater consistency, inclusion, and international competitiveness.
