**Education in Australia**

Australia's education system is decentralized, with responsibility shared between federal, state, and territory governments. The Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) oversees national curriculum standards, which include English, mathematics, science, social studies, arts, technology, health education, and foreign languages from pre-primary through secondary education. 

The education system is divided into three stages: pre-primary (nursery and kindergarten), primary (Grades 1–6), and secondary (Grades 7–12). Kindergarten is optional but widely available, with state-funded programs offering free early childhood education. 

Public education is free, though many schools charge voluntary fees for supplies, uniforms, and extracurricular activities. Government schools are operated by state or territory authorities and receive public funding, while non-government schools—such as Catholic and private institutions—receive partial government funding and charge tuition. 

Schools are categorized into government, Catholic, and private sectors. Government schools are further divided into open or comprehensive, selective, and specialist schools. Selective schools use entrance exams to admit students based on academic merit, while specialist schools cater to students with disabilities or specific learning needs. 

The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) provides a national system of qualifications, with senior secondary education leading to a certificate of education (e.g., WACE, HSC, SACE). Students may also pursue the International Baccalaureate (IB) or other international programs. 

Education is compulsory from age 6 to 17, with a national school year typically running from late January to mid-December. 

Key issues include teacher shortages, teacher pay, and school funding. Teacher turnover is high, and salaries are below those of other professions. Funding is primarily state and local, with federal support for specific programs. 

The country faces challenges in Indigenous education, with significant disparities in achievement and attendance. Bilingual education is supported, particularly for Indigenous students, with programs in the Northern Territory promoting language revitalization. 

Schools are monitored through national assessments like NAPLAN, which measure literacy and numeracy. Despite strong performance in international assessments, Australia has faced criticism over student outcomes, teacher quality, and equity. 

The government has invested in education reform, including the Gonski reports, which recommended increased funding and equitable resource distribution. 

Australia ranks highly in global education indices, with a strong emphasis on student well-being, holistic development, and inclusive education.
