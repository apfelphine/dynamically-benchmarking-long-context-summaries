Education in Australia is structured with compulsory schooling starting at age five, covering primary (Years 1–6) and secondary education (Years 7–12). Primary education focuses on foundational literacy, numeracy, and social skills, while secondary education includes specialized subjects and prepares students for further education or employment. The system is divided into government schools (64% of students), Catholic schools (19.7%), and independent schools (16.3%), each regulated by state and territory education authorities. The Australian Curriculum, Assessment, and Reporting Authority oversees standards, and standardized tests like NAPLAN assess student proficiency in reading, writing, language, and numeracy.

Key features include a national curriculum, small class sizes, and free education for government schools, though many ask for voluntary fees. The country has a strong emphasis on early childhood education, with preschools offering quality early learning. Tertiary education is largely publicly funded, with universities and vocational institutions offering diverse programs. International students contribute significantly to Australia’s education sector, and the country is recognized for its high education standards, though challenges include funding disparities, teacher shortages, and educational inequality.
