**Education in Australia**

- **Compulsory education** is mandatory from age 6 to 17, with government schools educating approximately 64% of students and non-government schools (Catholic and private) making up the remainder. 
- The **Australian Curriculum**, developed nationally and implemented since 2010, provides a unified framework for primary and secondary education, covering core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies.
- **Preschool education** is optional and typically begins at age 3–4, with government-funded programs available in many regions. 
- **Primary and secondary education** follows a structured system: primary (Grades 1–6) and secondary (Grades 7–12), with a focus on developing foundational literacy, numeracy, and social skills. 
- The **school year** runs from late January/February to mid-December, with three holiday breaks. 
- **School types** include government, Catholic, and private schools, with private schools often offering international curricula (e.g., IB, Cambridge). 
- **Funding** is primarily managed by state and territory governments, with the federal government providing support for non-government schools and tertiary education through student loan programs. 
- **Higher education** includes public universities, polytechnics, and private institutions, with a strong emphasis on research and practical skills. The **Bologna Process** has standardized degree structures across institutions. 
- **Student performance** in international assessments (PISA) has improved over time, with Australian students ranking 16th in reading, 29th in math, and 17th in science. 
- **Key challenges** include regional disparities, teacher shortages, and the need for equitable access to quality education. 
- **Tertiary education** is heavily subsidized, with public universities and polytechnics offering accessible pathways to higher education. 
- **International student enrollment** is a major component of Australia’s education sector, with over 50,000 students studying abroad annually. 
- **Educational policy** emphasizes equity, inclusion, and the development of 21st-century skills such as critical thinking and problem-solving.
