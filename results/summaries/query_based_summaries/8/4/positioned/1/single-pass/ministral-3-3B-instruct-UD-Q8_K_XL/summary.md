Education in Australia is structured with compulsory schooling from age six to 17, divided into primary (Years 1–6) and secondary education (Years 7–12). The system is regulated by state and territory governments, with the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA) setting national standards. Public schools educate approximately 64% of students, while non-government schools (Catholic and independent) cover the remaining 36%. Education is free in government schools, though some schools charge voluntary fees for materials and activities.

The curriculum spans subjects like English, mathematics, science, social studies, arts, technology, and health education. Secondary education includes core subjects and specialized tracks such as STEM, humanities, and vocational programs. Standardized testing, such as NAPLAN, assesses literacy and numeracy, while the National Senior Certificate (NSC) is awarded upon completion of Year 12. The system emphasizes equity, with initiatives like the Gonski Report aiming to address disparities in funding and educational outcomes. Higher education is largely free for public institutions, with universities and polytechnics offering diverse programs, including international collaborations.
