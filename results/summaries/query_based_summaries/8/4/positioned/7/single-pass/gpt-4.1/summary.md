## Education in Australia: Key Overview

Education in Australia is predominantly administered by state and territory governments, with compulsory attendance beginning between ages five to six and continuing until ages fifteen to seventeen, depending on jurisdiction. Schooling is divided into preschool (not mandatory, but increasingly encouraged), primary (pre-Year 1/Reception/Kindergarten/Prep to Year 6 or 7), and secondary education (Year 7 or 8 to Year 12), with a national curriculum progressively implemented since 2010. In 2023, there were over 4 million students enrolled in 9,629 schools, of which about 64% attended government schools, 19.7% Catholic schools, and 16.3% other independent schools. School education is usually provided without compulsory fees in government schools, while non-government schools charge tuition and fees. Most Australian students wear prescribed uniforms, and academic years run from January/February to December.

At the tertiary level, the majority of Australian universities are public and student fees are subsidized through a government loan scheme. Australia maintains a unified national qualifications framework that covers school, vocational, and higher education. In international comparisons, Australia has high educational attainment and ranks second globally on the UN’s Education Index, though recent PISA scores show a decline in student performance in reading, maths, and science. Australia is a major destination for international students and has robust frameworks for vocational education and training (VET) as well as university education. Ongoing issues in the sector include equitable school funding, Indigenous education gaps, school violence, and mixed results in testing and international rankings.
