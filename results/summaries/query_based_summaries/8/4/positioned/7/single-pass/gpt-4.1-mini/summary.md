## Education in Australia

Education regulation and delivery in Australia is primarily managed by the states and territories, with the federal government providing funding support to non-government schools and public universities, and subsidizing tertiary education through a national student loan scheme (HECS). The Australian Qualifications Framework, established in 1995, underpins a unified national system covering school-based, vocational, and higher education. Since 2010, the national Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented, encompassing core subjects from primary through lower secondary education.

Approximately 64% of Australian primary and secondary students attend government schools, while 36% are enrolled in non-government schools including Catholic and independent institutions. Schooling is compulsory from ages five or six to between fifteen and seventeen, varying by state. The school year typically runs from late January/early February to December. Early childhood education is encouraged but not mandatory, with government-funded preschools for children aged three to five offering up to 600 hours per year. Secondary education spans Years 7–12, with opportunities for specialization.

Tertiary education includes 42 universities (mostly public) and numerous registered vocational training providers. Australia is a leading international education provider, ranked third globally for international student numbers, with a high proportion of international students particularly in university sectors. Australian 15-year-olds ranked 16th in reading, 29th in math, and 17th in science in the 2018 PISA study. Indigenous education faces ongoing challenges, with government targets aiming to close educational disparities.

School types include government, Catholic, independent, selective, specialist, and international schools. Most Australian schools require uniforms. National assessments such as NAPLAN measure literacy and numeracy in Years 3, 5, 7, and 9. The federal government continues to fund education reforms including funding models like the Gonski reports to address equity and quality, though implementation has been uneven. Overall, Australia maintains a comprehensive, diverse education system with significant public and private sector involvement.
