## Education in Australia

**K-12 and Early Childhood Education:**
In Australia, the responsibility for the regulation, operation, and funding of education rests with the states and territories, as the Australian Government lacks a specific constitutional power in this area. Primary and secondary education is largely provided by government schools (approximately 64% of students in 2023), while approximately 36% attend non-government schools (Catholic or private). A national Australian Curriculum has been progressively implemented since 2010 for primary and secondary education, and the system is underpinned by the Australian Qualifications Framework (implemented in 1995), which unifies qualifications across schools, vocational training (VET), and tertiary education. Compulsory attendance requirements vary by state or territory, generally ranging from age five to six up to age fifteen to seventeen. Preschool provision has become a recent innovation, with the federal government encouraging early childhood education based on the nationally approved Early Years Learning Framework.

**Tertiary Education and National Assessment:**
Tertiary education is primarily provided by universities, which are mostly public. Student fees at universities are subsidized through a national student loan scheme, where payments are due upon reaching a certain income level (HECS). The Australian Qualifications Framework governs post-compulsory education. National assessments, such as the National Assessment Program – Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN), are administered annually to measure basic skills in reading, writing, and numeracy. While Australia ranks highly on the Education Index, 2018 OECD PISA results indicated that Australian students ranked 16th in reading, 29th in math, and 17th in science, suggesting a continued decline in educational standards.
