**Education in Australia**

Australia's education system features compulsory education introduced progressively from 1871 to 1916 across states, with a national curriculum implemented since 2010. Government schools educate approximately 64% of students, while non-government schools (including Catholic and private) account for the remainder. The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) provides a unified system for school, vocational, and tertiary education. 

Primary and secondary education follows a national curriculum with core subjects in English, maths, science, social studies, arts, technology, health, and foreign languages. A national Australian Curriculum is administered by the Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA). School years typically run from late January/early February to mid-December for primary/secondary and from late February to mid-November for TAFE and universities. 

In tertiary education, public universities dominate, with student fees subsidized via the HECS-HELP loan program. International education is a major export, with Australia ranking third globally in international student numbers. In 2018, PISA results showed Australian students ranked 16th in reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science, though performance has declined. The Education Index (UN Human Development Index) ranked Australia second-highest globally in 2018. 

Funding is primarily state-led, with federal support for non-government schools and tertiary education. The Gonski reports (2010, 2018) influenced school funding reforms, with a 2025 agreement increasing public school funding to 25% of total. Indigenous education faces persistent disparities, with lower attainment in literacy, numeracy, and Year 12 completion. Bilingual education programs, especially in the Northern Territory, aim to preserve Indigenous languages. Religious education is permitted in government schools under a pastoral care framework, with optional special religious instruction. 

School violence remains a concern, with 48% of principals reporting physical incidents in 2024. The system includes diverse school types, such as combined and central schools, and a range of preschool programs. Preschool is not compulsory but encouraged, with government-funded programs for children aged 3–5. The system is structured with a strong emphasis on equity, national standards, and ongoing reform.
