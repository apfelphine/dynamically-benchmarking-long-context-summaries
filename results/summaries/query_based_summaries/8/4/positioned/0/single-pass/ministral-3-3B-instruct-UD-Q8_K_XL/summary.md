Education in Australia is structured around a national system with key milestones in compulsory education introduced progressively from 1871 across different states. Primary and secondary education is primarily delivered by government schools, which educate approximately 64% of students, with the remaining 36% attending non-government schools. Tertiary education is heavily subsidized through student loan programs, and the system is governed by the **Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF)**, which unifies qualifications across higher education, vocational education and training (VET), and school-based education. The **Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA)** oversees the national curriculum, ensuring consistency across states and territories.

Education in Australia is compulsory from age 5 to 17, with a national curriculum implemented since 2010. The system emphasizes a balanced approach to learning, integrating both traditional and modern educational philosophies. While government schools are the primary providers, non-government schools (including Catholic and private schools) also play a significant role, with funding distributed based on student demographics and school type. The **Education Index** ranks Australia highly globally, though challenges such as disparities in educational outcomes, teacher quality, and infrastructure remain. The country also faces issues like school violence, funding disparities, and the impact of remote locations on educational access.
