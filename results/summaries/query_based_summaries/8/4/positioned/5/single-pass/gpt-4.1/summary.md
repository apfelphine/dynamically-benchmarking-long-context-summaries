## Education in Australia

Education in Australia is administered primarily by state and territory governments, with the federal government supporting funding and curriculum standardisation. School attendance is compulsory from around ages 5 or 6 to at least age 15 to 17, depending on the jurisdiction. The education system encompasses preschool, primary (Pre-Year 1/Kindergarten to Year 6 or 7), secondary (to Year 12), and tertiary levels. Government schools educate around 64% of students, while Catholic and other private/independent schools serve the rest, with a national curriculum guiding standards across sectors. Non-government schools often have religious affiliations and charge fees; all schools, regardless of type, follow the Australian Curriculum and generally require uniforms.

Australia has implemented the Australian Qualifications Framework for unified recognition of qualifications across schools, vocational education, and universities. Most students earn a Senior Secondary Certificate in their final years, with standardised testing (NAPLAN) assessing basic skills nationally. Tertiary education includes universities, TAFEs, and vocational institutions, with significant federal subsidisation through income-contingent loan schemes (HECS). Australia is a major destination for international students, who form a large proportion of tertiary enrolments. Key issues in Australian education include equitable school funding, performance gaps for Indigenous students, access for rural/remote areas, bilingual and religious instruction in schools, and declining scores in international assessments.
