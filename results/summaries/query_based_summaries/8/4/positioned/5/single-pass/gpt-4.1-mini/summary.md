# Education in Australia

Education in Australia comprises early childhood education, primary and secondary schooling, and tertiary education, with regulation and funding primarily managed at the state and territory levels. Compulsory education was introduced progressively from 1871 to 1916 across states. Approximately 64% of students attend government schools, with the remainder in non-government (Catholic and independent) schools. The Australian Qualifications Framework (since 1995) standardizes qualifications across school, vocational, and tertiary levels, and a national Australian Curriculum has been developed and implemented since 2010. The academic year generally runs from late January/early February to December for schools and TAFE, and late February to mid-November for universities.

Preschool attendance is widespread but not mandatory, targeting children aged 3 to 5, often run by state governments or private organisations following the Early Years Learning Framework. Primary education covers pre-Year 1 to Year 6 (or Year 7 in some states until recently), focusing on literacy, numeracy, and social skills. Secondary education spans Year 7 to 12, with junior high (Years 7–9) and senior high (Years 10–12), offering specialized pathways and selective or specialist schools for gifted, arts, or sports students. The school system supports bilingual and Aboriginal bilingual education programs.

Tertiary education includes 42 universities (majority public), vocational education, and registered training organisations (TAFE). Australian universities are internationally recognized, with a significant proportion of international students contributing to the sector. Students in tertiary education benefit from a national student loan scheme (HECS), where fees are subsidized and repayment is income-contingent.

Assessment is conducted via national standardized tests such as NAPLAN (literacy and numeracy) for Years 3, 5, 7, and 9, complementing international assessments like PISA. School funding is primarily provided by state governments for public schools and the federal government supports non-government schools.

Issues include disparities in Indigenous education outcomes, with government "closing the gap" targets focusing on participation, literacy and numeracy, Year 12 attainment, and attendance. The funding model continues to evolve, with major reforms like the Gonski Reports aiming to enhance equity and resource allocation. School violence remains a concern in some regions, with ongoing efforts to address it. Both co-educational and single-sex schooling options exist, and boarding schools represent a small percentage of schools. Uniforms are commonly required in most schools.
