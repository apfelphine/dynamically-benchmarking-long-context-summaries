Australia’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with compulsory schooling beginning at age 5 or 6 and extending to Year 12. Education is primarily delivered through **government schools** (64% of students), with smaller portions in **Catholic (19.7%)** and **independent (16.3%)** schools. The **Australian Curriculum** sets national standards, and students are assessed through standardized tests like **NAPLAN** (literacy and numeracy) and international assessments such as **PISA**.

**Primary education** (Years 1–6) focuses on foundational skills in literacy, numeracy, and social development, while **secondary education** (Years 7–12) offers specialized subjects, including STEM, humanities, and vocational tracks. The system emphasizes **inclusive education**, with provisions for students with disabilities and bilingual programs for Indigenous and immigrant communities. **Tertiary education** includes universities and vocational institutions, with public universities being the most common, though international students also study in Australia.

Funding for schools is primarily state-based, with federal support for non-government schools. Recent reforms, such as the **Gonski Review**, aim to improve equity and quality, though challenges persist in **rural education access, teacher shortages, and funding disparities**. The system is recognized for its **high standards and global competitiveness**, though some areas, like **STEM performance**, have seen declines in recent PISA rankings.
