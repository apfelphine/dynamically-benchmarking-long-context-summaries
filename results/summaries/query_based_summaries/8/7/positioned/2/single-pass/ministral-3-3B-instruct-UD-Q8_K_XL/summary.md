Finland’s education system is renowned for its high quality and equity, emphasizing free, universal education from kindergarten to upper secondary school. Compulsory education spans nine years (grades 1–9) in the **comprehensive school** (*peruskoulu*), followed by a bifurcated upper secondary education system starting at age 15 or 16. Students can choose between **academic upper secondary education** (*lukio*), preparing for university, or **vocational upper secondary education** (*ammattikoulu*), focusing on practical skills for the workforce. Both tracks are free, with students receiving free meals, books, and transportation.

Early childhood education is highly valued, with universal access to daycare and kindergarten from ages 8 months to 6 years. Finland’s approach prioritizes play-based learning, social development, and holistic well-being, fostering lifelong learning. The system emphasizes small class sizes, minimal homework, and a relaxed, student-centered environment. Grading is based on a 4–10 scale, with continuous assessment rather than high-stakes exams. Finland’s education is also notable for its focus on **phenomenon-based learning**, integrating traditional subject-based instruction with hands-on, real-world applications.

Tertiary education is divided into **universities** (*yliopisto*), offering research-focused degrees, and **universities of applied sciences** (*ammattikorkeakoulu*), focusing on practical, work-oriented training. Admission to universities is highly competitive, based on high school grades and entrance exams. Tuition is free for EU/EEA students, while international students pay fees. Finland’s education system is globally recognized for its success, particularly in literacy, numeracy, and critical thinking, contributing to its high ranking in international assessments.
