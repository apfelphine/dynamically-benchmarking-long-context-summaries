# Education in Finland

Education in Finland is characterized by a historically strong emphasis on literacy linked to Lutheranism, with schooling evolving from Swedish-language instruction to a comprehensive national system mandating grades 1 through 9, and bifurcating into academic (lukio) and vocational tracks thereafter. Early childhood education, including high-quality daycare and preschool until age seven, focuses on play-based learning, social skills, and individualized development. Compulsory education extends from ages 7 to 18, encompassing comprehensive school (peruskoulu) from grades 1 to 9, followed by either upper secondary academic or vocational education. Schools are primarily municipally funded, with few private schools that do not charge tuition or select students; classes are small, and multilingual education is standard. Teacher education is rigorous, requiring a master’s degree, reflecting the profession’s high status and selective admission.

Upper secondary education lasts three to four years and is free; students choose between vocational training and academic preparation for university. The matriculation examination is nationally graded and pivotal for university entrance. Finnish higher education is divided into research universities focusing on theoretical knowledge, and universities of applied sciences catering to practical skills and industry. Admissions are merit-based, transparent, and free of biases such as essays or extracurricular weighting. Tuition has been free for EU/EEA students, but since 2017, non-EEA students pay fees to support educational exports and quality. Adult education, including open universities and "Free Education" institutions, provides lifelong learning opportunities. Finland is engaged in ongoing educational reforms, such as the 2016 National Curriculum Framework introducing mandatory phenomenon-based learning to foster 21st-century skills. Finnish education expertise is also internationally promoted through export initiatives and Finnish-inspired schools abroad.
