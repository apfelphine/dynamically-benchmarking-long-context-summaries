# Summary of Education in Finland

Education in Finland has a deep historical foundation rooted in Lutheranism, emphasizing literacy as essential for religious and civic participation. The modern Finnish education system evolved from Swedish rule, with Finnish gradually replacing Swedish as the language of instruction in the 19th century. Compulsory comprehensive schooling spans nine years (grades 1–9), extended since 2021 to include upper secondary education (ages 7 to 18). After grade 9, students choose between academic upper secondary school (lukio) or vocational school, both pathways preparing for tertiary studies or employment. Early childhood education focuses on play-based learning and social skills development, supported by universal access to daycare and preschool, though not mandatory.

The Finnish education system is characterized by small class sizes, highly qualified teachers (all with master’s degrees), state-funded and municipally administered schools with minimal tuition fees, and a nationally guided but locally autonomous curriculum. Finnish students learn multiple languages from early grades. Upper secondary academic schools culminate in a matriculation examination used for university admissions, which are transparent, merit-based, and devoid of subjective criteria like essays or extracurriculars. Tertiary education includes traditional universities emphasizing research and universities of applied sciences focused on practical skills, with pathways between them. Finland imposed tuition fees for non-EEA students since 2017, while domestic students study tuition-free. Finnish education emphasizes equity, lifelong learning, and individual development, with growing international influence through education export programs and Finnish-style schools abroad.
