## Education in Finland: Key Features and Structure

Education in Finland is marked by a commitment to equity, high-quality teacher preparation, and a comprehensive, publicly funded system. The compulsory education system begins with early childhood education, emphasizing play-based learning and the holistic development of children, available universally and free or highly subsidized. Formal education starts at age seven with nine years of comprehensive (primary and lower secondary) school, followed by an expansion to compulsory upper secondary education (academic or vocational tracks) for ages 15–18 as of September 2021. Schools are primarily municipally run, with minimal private sector presence and prohibitions on tuition fees and selective admissions. The national curriculum encourages autonomy for teachers, small class sizes, and prioritizes learning for all students, with advanced pupils supporting their peers.

Upon completing compulsory school, students can choose academic upper secondary (lukio) or vocational tracks. Academic upper secondary includes preparation for the nationally graded matriculation exam, important for university admissions. Both primary and secondary teachers must hold master’s degrees, making teaching a competitive and respected profession. Higher education is provided by traditional research universities and universities of applied sciences, with merit-based admissions and transparent processes. Tuition is free for EU/EEA students, while fees apply for non-European students since 2017. Finnish education also promotes lifelong learning through robust adult education programs and supports internationalization via export initiatives and Finnish-style schools abroad. Finland’s curriculum reform emphasizes phenomenon-based learning and 21st-century skills, and the country consistently ranks highly in international education comparisons.
