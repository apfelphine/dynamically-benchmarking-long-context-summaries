Finland’s education system is renowned for its high quality and equity, emphasizing comprehensive and inclusive learning from early childhood through tertiary education. Compulsory education spans nine years of **basic comprehensive school** (peruskoulu), starting at age 7, followed by **upper secondary education** (lukio or ammattikoulu), which is now mandatory from age 15. The system prioritizes small class sizes, minimal homework, and a focus on holistic development, including arts, music, and physical education.

Key features include:
- **Free education** for all students, with minimal tuition fees for international students from outside the EU/EEA.
- **No standardized testing** for basic education, though upper secondary students take a nationally graded matriculation exam (Ylioppilastutkinto).
- **Teacher autonomy** and high standards, with teachers holding master’s degrees and receiving strong union support.
- **Universal access** to early childhood education, including free daycare and kindergarten for children aged 1–6, fostering foundational literacy and numeracy skills.
- **Vocational and academic pathways**, allowing students to pursue either vocational training (professional competence) or academic studies (preparation for university) after upper secondary school.

The system is funded primarily by municipalities, ensuring local control and community involvement. Recent reforms aim to integrate phenomenon-based learning with traditional subject-based instruction, aligning with global 21st-century skills like collaboration and critical thinking. Finland’s approach to education is celebrated for its emphasis on equity, student well-being, and lifelong learning.
