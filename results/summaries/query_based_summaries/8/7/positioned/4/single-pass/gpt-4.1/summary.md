## Education in Finland: Key Features

Education in Finland is renowned for its emphasis on equality, quality, and lifelong learning. Literacy developed early due to the Lutheran tradition, with schooling gradually becoming compulsory and universally accessible. Today, children benefit from free, high-quality early childhood education and universal daycare, and start formal schooling at the age of seven. The compulsory system consists of a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1–9), recently extended to include upper secondary education or vocational school until age 18. Municipalities administer nearly all schools, and even private schools must not charge tuition or select students selectively. Finnish education prioritizes individual development, cooperative skills, and social responsibility, with small class sizes and a strong emphasis on play-based and student-centered learning. Students receive free lunches, materials, healthcare, and sometimes transportation, while homework and high-stakes testing are minimized.

Upper secondary education (from age 15/16) offers academic (lukio) or vocational (ammattioppilaitos) tracks, both tuition-free. Academic students take a nationally graded matriculation exam for university entry. Teachers are required to hold a master's degree, making teaching a highly respected and competitive profession. Tertiary education is divided between research-focused universities and more practically oriented universities of applied sciences. University admission is merit-based and objective, relying on GPA and entrance exams. Tuition is free for EU/EEA students; non-EU/EEA students pay set annual fees. Finnish students commonly learn two foreign languages in addition to their mother tongue. Adult education is supported by municipal and independent institutes, and the Open University model provides flexible opportunities. Finland's educational system is marked by its strong social support, pedagogical autonomy, innovative curricula (such as phenomenon-based learning), and ongoing structural reforms aimed at maintaining excellence and responding to changing societal needs.
