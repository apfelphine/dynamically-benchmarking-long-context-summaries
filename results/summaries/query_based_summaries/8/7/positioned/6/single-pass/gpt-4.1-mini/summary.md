## Education in Finland

Education in Finland is deeply rooted in the country's Lutheran heritage, where literacy was historically essential for reading the Bible. The Finnish education system evolved from Swedish-language institutions into a mandatory Finnish-language system by the early 20th century. Compulsory education covers nine years of comprehensive schooling (grades 1–9) and, since 2021, includes upper secondary education (academic lukio or vocational ammattikoulu) up to age 18. Early childhood education (daycare and preschool/kindergarten) is highly valued but not mandatory, emphasizing play-based learning and social development until formal schooling begins at age seven.

Finnish education is characterized by small class sizes, strong teacher autonomy, and a broad curriculum including multiple languages and arts from early grades. Upper secondary education offers both academic and vocational paths, with matriculation exams used for university entry. Teachers must hold master’s degrees, reflecting the profession’s respected status. Tertiary education is divided into research universities and universities of applied sciences, with selection based on transparent, merit-based criteria. Since 2017, tuition fees apply only to non-EEA students. Finland also promotes adult education and lifelong learning through open universities and diverse “free education” institutions. Recent reforms include a 2016 National Curriculum Framework mandating phenomenon-based learning alongside traditional instruction. Finnish education is internationally recognized and also exported through official and inspired schools abroad.
