Finland's education system is characterized by a highly integrated, nine-year comprehensive school (*peruskoulu*) that is fully funded and administered by municipalities, with nearly no private schools operating under a selective or tuition-based model. The system emphasizes small class sizes, student autonomy, and a focus on play-based learning in early childhood (which includes universal, free daycare for children under five). Compulsory education covers grades 1–9 (ages 7–16) and has recently been extended to include upper secondary school (grades 10–12) or vocational training, making it mandatory until age 18. There are no high-stakes standardized tests; instead, students are assessed through continuous evaluation and report cards. Admissions to universities and universities of applied sciences are strictly merit-based, relying on high school grades and the national matriculation examination (*ylioppilastutkinto*), with a focus on research and practical application respectively. Teachers must hold a master's degree, and the curriculum is undergoing reform to include phenomenon-based learning to foster 21st-century skills. Tertiary education is free for students from the European Economic Area (EEA), while non-EEA students face tuition fees.
