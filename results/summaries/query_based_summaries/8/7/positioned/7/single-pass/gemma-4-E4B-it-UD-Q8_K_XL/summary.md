Education in Finland is characterized by a highly regarded and equitable system, with a strong emphasis on developing the individual child. Early childhood education, which lasts until age seven, emphasizes respect for individuality, social and interactive skill development, and learning through play, rather than formal instruction in reading and mathematics. The goal of this early period is to cultivate the ability to learn and develop into a responsible, active citizen.

The compulsory education system in Finland consists of a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1 to 9), which is free and administered by municipalities. Compulsory education was later expanded to include upper secondary school or vocational school from age seven to eighteen. Finnish schools do not feature "gifted" programs, and more advanced students are expected to support those who are slower to catch on. Teachers are highly respected professionals who hold a master's degree, yet they are granted significant autonomy over teaching methods, and classes are kept small, typically no more than twenty pupils.
