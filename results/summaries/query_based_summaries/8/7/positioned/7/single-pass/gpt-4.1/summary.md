## Education in Finland: Overview

Education in Finland is characterized by a strong emphasis on equity, high-quality early childhood education, and a comprehensive compulsory system. Early childhood education—free, universally accessible, and focused on play and social development—lays the foundation for lifelong learning. Compulsory education spans from age 7 to 18, covering nine years of comprehensive schooling and, since 2021, includes upper secondary academic or vocational education. The system is predominantly publicly funded and administered at the municipal level, with private schools being rare and closely regulated. Small classes, highly qualified teachers (all with master's degrees), and a culture that values reading and individualized learning are hallmarks of Finnish schooling. There are no high-stakes tests or gifted programs; instead, advanced students help peers, and assessment is mostly verbal or through a simple grading scale.

After compulsory schooling, students may pursue academic upper secondary school or vocational pathways, both tuition-free and leading to tertiary opportunities. Entry to universities and universities of applied sciences is transparent and merit-based, relying on final grades and entrance exams rather than extracurriculars or essays. The tertiary system distinguishes between research universities and practical universities of applied sciences. While tuition is free for EU/EEA students, non-European students pay moderate fees. Finland is committed to education export, offering both official Finnish curriculum abroad and “Finnish-style” schools emphasizing play-based and child-centered pedagogy. Continuous reforms—such as phenomenon-based learning and curriculum updates—ensure the system promotes creativity, collaboration, and critical thinking.
