## Education in Finland: Summary

Finland’s education system has deep historical roots tied to Lutheranism, emphasizing literacy as a religious and cultural foundation. The modern compulsory comprehensive education spans nine years (grades 1–9), recently extended to cover upper secondary education (grades 10–12 or vocational equivalent) for ages 7 to 18. Early childhood education, though not mandatory, is widely used and focuses on individual development through play and social skills, supported by universal access to free daycare and preschool programs. A unique feature is the cooperative approach between parents and society to prepare children for formal schooling at age seven. Finnish schools prioritize small class sizes, multilingual instruction (typically Finnish/Swedish and at least two additional languages), and non-competitive, supportive learning environments. Assessment is formative with minimal high-stakes testing, using a grading scale of 4 to 10, and failing grades require remedial examinations before possible repetition.

Upper secondary education is divided into academic (lukio) and vocational tracks, both compulsory since 2021, with flexible pathways between them and to higher education. Admission to universities is based transparently on matriculation exam performance and GPA, without subjective criteria. Finland’s tertiary education comprises traditional universities focused on research and applied sciences universities (polytechnics) offering practical qualifications. Teaching is highly respected, requiring a master’s degree with competitive entry to teacher training. Tuition was free for all students until 2017; now only non-EEA students pay fees. The Finnish National Curriculum Framework, adapted periodically, embraces phenomenon-based learning alongside traditional subjects, promoting 21st-century skills such as collaboration and critical thinking. Finnish education is internationally recognized for its focus on equality, quality teaching, and student well-being, with ongoing reforms adapting to societal and economic needs.
