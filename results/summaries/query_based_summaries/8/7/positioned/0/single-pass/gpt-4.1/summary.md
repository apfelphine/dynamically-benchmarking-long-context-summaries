## Education in Finland

Finland’s education system features a robust history of promoting literacy dating back to the Lutheran Reformation, culminating in compulsory and universal basic education by the early 20th century and near-universal literacy rates. The current structure consists of free, high-quality early childhood education, followed by a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (peruskoulu), where attendance is now mandated until the age of 18, including upper secondary or vocational pathways. The system is largely public, with private schools being rare, strictly regulated, and prohibited from charging tuition or selective admission. Instruction emphasizes small classes, teacher autonomy, and minimal homework; there are no “gifted” programs, and advanced students are expected to support peers. Students benefit from free meals, healthcare, and school materials, while reading for pleasure is actively promoted from birth.

Post-basic education splits into academic (lukio) and vocational (ammattioppilaitos) tracks, both of which are compulsory until age 18 as of 2021. Vocational graduates may access tertiary education, although university entrance exams remain challenging for them. Teachers, highly respected and required to hold master’s degrees, are rigorously selected. Tertiary education is divided into research-focused universities and more practically oriented universities of applied sciences, with admissions fully merit-based and largely free for European Union students. The system also includes adult education options and innovative approaches such as phenomenon-based learning mandated in the 2016 National Curriculum. Finland's commitment to equality extends to the structure, funding, and governance of schools, making Finnish education a model internationally recognized for its outcomes and innovative philosophy.
