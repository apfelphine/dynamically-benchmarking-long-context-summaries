Education in Finland is characterized by a free, comprehensive, and high-quality system that emphasizes equity, student well-being, and lifelong learning. The system is primarily municipal-funded, with few private schools allowed, and mandates a nine-year compulsory basic education (*peruskoulu*) for children aged 6–15. This stage includes primary school (*alakoulu*) and middle school (*yläkoulu*), where small class sizes, a focus on play-based learning in early childhood, and the use of both Finnish and Swedish as languages of instruction are standard. After age 15, education bifurcates into academic (*lukio*) and vocational tracks, both of which are free and accessible. Finnish students typically learn two foreign languages by high school, and the curriculum emphasizes soft skills, arts, music, and physical activity, with minimal homework and a strong culture of reading.

Tertiary education in Finland consists of two parallel sectors: traditional universities (*yliopisto*) focused on theoretical research and degrees (bachelor, master, doctorate), and universities of applied sciences (*ammattikorkeakoulu*) focused on practical skills and work-oriented training. Admissions to universities are strictly merit-based, transparent, and objective, relying on GPA and specific entrance examinations rather than application essays or extracurricular activities. Since the 2017 reform, non-EEA students face tuition fees ranging from €1,500 to €18,000 annually, while EEA students continue to study free. The system is underpinned by a highly respected teaching profession requiring a master's degree, and recent reforms include a shift toward phenomenon-based learning and the expansion of compulsory upper secondary education to age 18.
