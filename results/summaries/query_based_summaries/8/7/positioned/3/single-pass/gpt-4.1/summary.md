## Education in Finland

Education in Finland is characterized by a strong emphasis on equality, universal access, and high professional standards for teachers. The compulsory education system consists of a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1–9), now extended to include upper secondary school (academic or vocational tracks) until age 18. Early childhood education is universally available and focused on play-based learning and social development, with parents and society cooperating in children's upbringing before formal schooling begins at age seven. Instruction in comprehensive schools is relaxed and informal, with small class sizes and a curriculum that requires all students to learn at least two languages besides their mother tongue, alongside arts, music, and practical skills. Homework is minimal and all students receive free school health care, lunch, materials, and, if needed, housing.

Teachers in Finland must hold a master's degree, and entry into teacher education programs is highly competitive. Schools are primarily funded and managed by municipalities, with a small number of private (mostly faith-based or Waldorf) schools operating under strict regulations that prohibit tuition fees and selective admissions. Upper secondary education, compulsory since 2021, bifurcates into academic and vocational pathways, both of which are free and provide access to further study. The tertiary education sector includes research-oriented universities and universities of applied sciences, with admissions determined solely by merit (GPA and exam performance). Tuition is free for Finnish and EU/EEA students, with fees for students from outside the EU/EEA since 2017. Adult education is widely available through open universities and state-supported institutes. Internationally, Finland is recognized for its educational philosophy, and exports Finnish education expertise through initiatives such as the Education Finland programme and Finnish-style schools abroad. The 2016 National Curriculum Framework encourages phenomenon-based learning and the development of 21st-century skills.
