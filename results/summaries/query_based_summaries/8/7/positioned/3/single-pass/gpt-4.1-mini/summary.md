# Summary: Education in Finland

Education in Finland has a long history rooted in Lutheran emphasis on literacy, with formal schools established since the 17th century and literacy reaching near-universality by the late 19th century. The current compulsory education system consists of a comprehensive nine-year basic school (grades 1 to 9) followed by an upper secondary stage that is now mandatory until age 18, offering academic (lukio) or vocational tracks. Early childhood education is highly valued, emphasizing play-based learning until age seven, with nearly universal use of free, high-quality daycare and preschool programs designed to develop social, communication, and independence skills. Finnish schools feature small class sizes, no ability grouping or gifted programs, and an autonomous teaching profession requiring master's level qualifications.

Higher education in Finland is divided into traditional research universities and universities of applied sciences (polytechnics), both publicly funded and admissions are merit-based via transparent examinations and GPA evaluations. Bachelor’s degrees typically take three to four years, and master’s degrees are often pursued directly after, with doctoral degrees awarded mainly by universities. Finland implemented tuition fees for non-EEA students from 2017 onward. The country promotes bilingualism with mandatory study of two additional languages alongside the mother tongue. Recent educational reforms include phenomenon-based learning introduced through the 2016 national curriculum, promoting interdisciplinary and 21st-century skills. Finland also supports adult education through open university programs and free education institutes focused on lifelong learning and personal development. Internationally, Finnish education expertise is exported through formal Suomi-koulut schools and Finnish-inspired private schools worldwide, coordinated by the national Education Finland program.
