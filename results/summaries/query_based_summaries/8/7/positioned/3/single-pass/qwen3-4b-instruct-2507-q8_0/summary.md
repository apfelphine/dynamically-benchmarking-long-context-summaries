Education in Finland emphasizes early childhood development, equitable access, and holistic learning. From age 5 to 18, students attend a nine-year comprehensive school (peruskoulu), with no mandatory grades or high-stakes testing. The curriculum is student-centered, focusing on play-based learning, creativity, and social-emotional development. After completing compulsory education, students choose between academic (lukio) or vocational (ammattioppilaitos) tracks, both leading to national qualifications. Finnish education is publicly funded, with small class sizes (up to 20 students), minimal homework, and strong teacher autonomy. The system prioritizes student well-being, with free school meals, healthcare, and a strong emphasis on reading and language development. Higher education is divided into universities (research-focused) and universities of applied sciences (practical, work-oriented), with a transparent, merit-based admission process. The system is highly regarded globally, with a focus on equity, student agency, and lifelong learning.
