**Education in Finland**  
Finland’s education system emphasizes equity, student well-being, and holistic development. From age 7 to 18, students attend a nine-year comprehensive school (peruskoulu), which includes early childhood education (kindergarten) and primary school. The system is compulsory and free, with small class sizes (typically 20 students), and a strong focus on play-based, child-centered learning. Students learn two languages (usually Finnish or Swedish) in addition to their mother tongue, with a focus on reading, writing, and critical thinking.  

Upper secondary education, introduced in 2021 as compulsory, includes academic (lukio) and vocational (ammattioppilaitos) tracks. Academic students prepare for university, while vocational students gain practical skills. Both tracks lead to national matriculation exams (ylioppilastutkinto), which are central to university admissions. The academic curriculum offers advanced levels in subjects like mathematics, with performance assessed through a percentile-based national exam.  

Teachers are fully unionized and follow a state curriculum with significant autonomy in teaching methods. The system is highly equitable, with no standardized testing, and students receive free meals, books, and school trips.  

Higher education is divided into traditional universities (research-focused) and universities of applied sciences (practice-oriented). Admissions are merit-based, transparent, and objective, with no essays or bias. A bachelor’s degree typically takes 3–4 years, followed by a master’s, with doctoral degrees available only in universities.  

Adult education is accessible through municipal "citizens’ institutes" (kansalaisopisto), offering lifelong learning in humanities, arts, and practical skills. Finland also promotes international education through "Suomi-koulut" (Finnish schools abroad) and Finnish-style education models globally.  

The system is renowned for its outcomes: high literacy, low inequality, and strong student well-being. Finland’s education model is often cited as a global benchmark for equity, innovation, and student success.
