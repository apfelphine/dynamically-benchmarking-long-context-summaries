Finland’s education system is renowned for its comprehensive and equitable approach, emphasizing quality and holistic development. Compulsory education spans nine years (grades 1–9), known as *peruskoulu* ("basic school"), with an expanded system now including upper secondary education (grades 10–12) or vocational training (*ammattikoulu*). The system is decentralized, primarily funded and administered by municipalities, with minimal private sector involvement. Small class sizes, minimal homework, and a focus on play-based learning in early childhood education are hallmarks of Finnish pedagogy. Students learn two languages beyond their native tongue, and the curriculum prioritizes critical thinking, creativity, and practical skills over rote memorization. Grading ranges from 4 to 10, with no high-stakes testing, fostering a supportive and inclusive environment. Upper secondary education offers pathways to university preparation (*lukio*) or vocational training, with admissions based on academic performance rather than quotas or extracurricular activities. Finland’s emphasis on early childhood education, teacher autonomy, and student-centered learning has positioned it as a global model for educational excellence.
