## Education in Finland

Education in Finland is characterized by a highly equitable, publicly funded system covering all levels from early childhood to tertiary education. Compulsory education includes a nine-year comprehensive school covering ages 7–16, followed by mandatory upper secondary education (either academic or vocational) up to age 18. Schooling emphasizes small class sizes, minimal homework, and a relaxed, informal atmosphere. There are few private schools, most of which are faith-based or Waldorf institutions that do not charge tuition or select students by ability. Students receive free school meals, health care, and educational materials. Early childhood education, including universal daycare and preschool, strongly emphasizes individualized development and play-based learning, with parents and society jointly fostering children's social skills and readiness for formal learning.

Teachers in Finland must hold master's degrees, and the profession is highly respected, with competitive university admissions and autonomy in instructional methods. All students learn at least two foreign languages in addition to their mother tongue. Secondary education begins at 15 or 16, offering either vocational or academic tracks, both tuition-free and entitling students to continued benefits. Vocational graduates receive qualifications for employment, while academic students undertake the prestigious nationwide matriculation examination, which serves as a key university entrance metric. Tertiary education comprises traditional research-focused universities and universities of applied sciences, with admissions based objectively on academic merit and entrance exams. The system is further complemented by extensive adult education and state-supported lifelong learning opportunities, and a strong commitment to educational equity and continuous structural improvement through national reform initiatives.
