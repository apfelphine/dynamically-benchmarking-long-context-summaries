# Education in Finland

The Finnish education system is characterized by high accessibility, equity, and a strong emphasis on student well-being and autonomous learning. Compulsory basic education consists of a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1–9) and, since September 2021, upper secondary education (grades 10–12) is also mandatory, with a bifurcation into academic tracks for university preparation and vocational tracks for employment. The system is almost exclusively funded and administered by municipalities, with few private schools operating under strict regulations that prohibit tuition fees and selective admissions. Teachers hold master's degrees, are fully unionized, and enjoy significant autonomy in instructional methods and textbook selection, fostering a relaxed, informal school atmosphere with small class sizes.

Tertiary education is divided into two distinct sectors: traditional universities focused on theoretical research and the universities of applied sciences (AMK) focused on practical industry development. Admission to both sectors is transparent, merit-based, and objective, relying on high school grades and examinations without application essays or extracurricular weighting. Tuition is free for all students from the European Economic Area (EEA); however, since 2017, students from outside the EEA must pay annual fees ranging from €1,500 to €18,000. The system emphasizes universal access, free school lunches, and extensive early childhood education, with a recent shift toward mandatory phenomenon-based learning in the 2016 curriculum framework to enhance 21st-century skills.
