**Education in Finland**

Finland's educational system emphasizes comprehensive and equitable learning, starting with free, universal daycare and nursery-kindergarten for children aged eight months to five years. The compulsory basic comprehensive education spans nine years (grades 1–9), and new legislation has expanded this to cover ages 7 to 18, including upper secondary or vocational school. Finnish schools are largely funded and administered by municipalities, with tuition fees strictly prohibited even in private schools; these private institutions must adhere to the same educational and social benefits provided to municipal students. Teachers are highly respected professionals who must hold a master's degree and are granted significant autonomy in instructional methods.

The system features a non-rigid structure where vocational and academic tracks are integrated; graduates from vocational schools can qualify for universities of applied sciences, while academic graduates take the nationally graded matriculation examination for university entrance. Upper secondary education is compulsory from September 2021, and while there are no "gifted" programs, more advanced students are expected to support their peers. Additionally, Finland offers "Free Education" (*vapaa sivistystyö*) through independent institutes to support multifaceted personality development outside of professional or degree-oriented learning.
