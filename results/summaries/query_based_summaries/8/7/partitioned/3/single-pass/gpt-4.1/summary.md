## Education in Finland

Education in Finland is founded on a comprehensive, publicly funded system that emphasizes equality, accessibility, and lifelong learning. Compulsory education begins with a nine-year basic comprehensive school (peruskoulu), covering ages 7 to 16; this mandate has been expanded as of September 2021 to include upper secondary education (academic or vocational) up to age 18. Early childhood education—available universally and considered critical for long-term development—is focused on play-based learning and social skill development, preparing children for formal learning at age seven. The system, almost entirely run by municipalities, features small class sizes, highly qualified teachers (all requiring master’s degrees), and a curriculum that stresses both academic and practical subjects. Students benefit from free school meals, healthcare, materials, and transport; there are no private tuition fees, selective enrollment, or rigid gifted programs, with advanced students expected to assist others.

Post-compulsory options include academic upper secondary schools (lukio) and vocational schools (ammattikoulu), both of which are free and lead to qualifications for further study or employment. Vocational and academic tracks can intersect, and students may pursue both types of certification. The matriculation examination serves as a significant university entrance assessment. Tertiary education is divided into research-focused universities and practical universities of applied sciences, with admissions based primarily on objective academic measures and transparent testing rather than subjective criteria or extracurriculars. Tuition at public universities is free for European Union/EEA students (fees apply for non-EU/EEA students). Finland also provides adult education and open university opportunities for lifelong learning and upskilling. The Finnish educational philosophy centers on autonomy for teachers, important social entitlements for students, minimal homework or testing in early years, and fostering reading and language skills across the curriculum.
