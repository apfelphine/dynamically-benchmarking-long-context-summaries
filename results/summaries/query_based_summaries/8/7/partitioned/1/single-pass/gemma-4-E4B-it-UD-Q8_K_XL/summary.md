**Education in Finland**

Finland's compulsory education system encompasses a nine-year comprehensive school (grades 1 to 9), which was expanded by new legislation to cover ages 7 to 18, including upper secondary school (academic or vocational). The system emphasizes that there are no "gifted" programs, and advanced students are expected to assist those who are slower to progress. Schools are almost exclusively funded and administered by municipalities, and private schools are prohibited from charging tuition fees or admitting students selectively. Teachers are fully unionized, adhere to state curriculum guidelines while maintaining significant autonomy over teaching methods, and students benefit from social entitlements such as free daily lunch and school healthcare.

Early childhood education, which lasts until age 7, focuses on developing cooperation, communication, and individuality through play, rather than formal instruction in reading or mathematics. The educational philosophy is deeply rooted in the belief that early education is the first and most critical stage of lifelong learning. At the tertiary level, the system includes traditional universities and universities of applied sciences. Admissions are merit-based, transparent, and objective, and a bachelor's degree typically takes three to four years, often serving as an intermediate step toward a master's degree.
