Finland’s education system is characterized by its **comprehensive and compulsory** structure, emphasizing equality and student-centered learning. The system consists of **nine years of compulsory basic education** (grades 1–9) followed by **upper secondary education** (grades 10–12), which became compulsory in September 2021. Previously, upper secondary education was optional, but it now includes both academic (lukio) and vocational (ammattikoulu) tracks, with students choosing between university preparation or vocational training. The system is highly autonomous, with teachers having significant control over teaching methods and curriculum implementation.

Key features include:
- **Small class sizes** (typically fewer than 20 students), fostering personalized attention.
- **Free education** at all levels, funded by municipalities, with minimal private schools.
- **No standardized testing** for basic education, relying instead on continuous assessment and verbal evaluations.
- **Two languages** taught from an early age, with Finnish or Swedish as the primary language and English as a second language.
- **Emphasis on creativity, critical thinking, and practical skills**, with minimal homework and a relaxed school environment.
- **Grading system** ranging from 4 to 10, with detailed breakdowns for advanced students.
- **Compulsory free lunch** and health care for students.
- **No "gifted" programs**, with advanced students expected to help peers.

Upper secondary education offers specialized curricula, including advanced mathematics and languages, with students choosing between academic and vocational tracks. Admission to universities is based on high school grades and entrance exams, with no racial or socioeconomic quotas. Finland’s education system is globally recognized for its high quality, low dropout rates, and strong emphasis on lifelong learning and innovation.
