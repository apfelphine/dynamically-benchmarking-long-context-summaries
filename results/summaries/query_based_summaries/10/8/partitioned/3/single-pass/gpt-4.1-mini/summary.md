# Summary: Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria follows a 9-3-4 system comprising six years of primary education, three years of junior secondary, and three years of senior secondary education, culminating in the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) or the National Examination Council (NECO). Subjects at the primary level include core academics like Mathematics and English, religious studies, indigenous languages, and sometimes French and Computer Science. Secondary education offers a mix of compulsory and elective subjects, with technical colleges providing vocational training alongside academic pathways. Federal government colleges ("unity schools") are elite boarding schools with merit-based admission, while state schools often face challenges like underfunding and unqualified teachers; private schools tend to have smaller classes, better resources, and higher fees.

Tertiary education comprises universities (federal, state, private), polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with admissions typically requiring a minimum SSCE credit score and passing the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) exam. The Nigerian Universities Commission oversees accreditation, with recent expansions including the approval of numerous private universities. Vocational education is overseen by the National Board for Technical Education but suffers from teacher shortages and outdated curricula. Informal education and literacy programs exist but face funding and political commitment challenges. Advanced level programs such as IJMB and JUPEB offer direct university entry to qualified candidates. Overall, while Nigeria has made strides in expanding education access, regional disparities, funding constraints, and quality issues remain significant hurdles.
