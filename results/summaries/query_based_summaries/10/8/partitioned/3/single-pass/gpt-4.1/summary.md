## Education in Nigeria

**Structure and Governance:**  
Education in Nigeria comprises formal and informal systems, overseen mainly by the Ministry of Education, with additional input from agencies such as UBEC (Universal Basic Education Commission), National Board for Technical Education, and various local/state entities. Formal education follows the 9-3-4 system: nine years of Basic Education (combining 6 years of primary and 3 years of junior secondary), three years of senior secondary, and typically four years of tertiary education. Curriculum is delivered in English and major indigenous languages. Education is officially free and compulsory for the first nine years, but issues with funding, facilities, teacher qualifications, and regional disparities persist.

**Key Features and Challenges:**  
Primary education starts around age five; secondary education is divided into junior and senior cycles, with a broad curriculum that includes core and elective subjects. Vocational and informal education is also significant, with apprenticeships and literacy programs targeting children and adults, though hampered by political and funding challenges. Tertiary education includes universities (federal, state, and private), polytechnics, and colleges, with entry via national exams (e.g., JAMB, IJMB, JUPEB, SSCE). Despite the establishment of new universities and the rise of private involvement, access and equity remain concerns. Nigeria’s literacy rate is improving but varies regionally and by gender; recent government initiatives aim to boost traditional and digital literacy, but issues like insecurity, poverty, and cultural barriers (especially for girls) continue to impact enrollment and attainment.
