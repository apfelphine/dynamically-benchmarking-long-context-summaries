## Education in Nigeria

**Tertiary Education and University Landscape:**
Nigeria's tertiary education sector is characterized by a mix of public and private institutions. As of August 2017, there were 153 registered universities, comprising 43 federal, 47 state, and 68 private universities. In February 2021, the government approved the establishment of 20 new private universities. Entry into most Nigerian universities typically requires a minimum of SSCE/GCE Ordinary Level Credits with a minimum JAMB entrance examination score of 180 out of 400. Advanced entry programs include the Interim Joint Matriculation Board (IJMB) and the Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board (JUPEB), which offer direct entry into 200 Level.

**Overall System and Vocational Training:**
The education system in Nigeria includes various tiers of schooling. In vocational education, the National Board for Technical Education oversees the sharing of regionally specific knowledge. Current challenges in vocational training include teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and poor labor market statistics. Furthermore, informal education and literacy programs are significant, though their implementation is often stalled by funding issues and political challenges. While female education is increasing and seen as vital for national development, challenges such as teen pregnancy, poverty, and insecurity impact gender equality in the system.
