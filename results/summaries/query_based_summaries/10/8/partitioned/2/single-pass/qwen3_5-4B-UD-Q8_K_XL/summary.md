Education in Nigeria is a multi-tiered system comprising primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, characterized by a mix of federal, state, and private institutions. Primary education is free and compulsory under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) policy, covering six years from age 5 to 11, followed by three years of junior secondary school and three years of senior secondary school. The system is administered by various examination bodies, primarily the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and the National Examination Council (NECO), which conduct the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) to determine university admission. Tertiary education includes 153 registered institutions: 40 federal, 47 state, and 68 private universities, alongside polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education. The government has recently approved 20 new private universities to expand capacity, though challenges persist regarding infrastructure, teacher shortages, and regional disparities, particularly in rural areas and the North.

Vocational education is overseen by the National Board for Technical Education, with a strong emphasis on apprenticeships and informal training to address unemployment. However, formal vocational programs face issues such as outdated curricula, poor statistics on labor market needs, and a shortage of qualified instructors. Informal education and literacy programs have also seen growth, supported by NGOs and government initiatives like the Premier Reading Club, aiming to improve adult and child literacy rates which remain low in certain regions. Admission to higher education requires passing the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME) or alternative programs like the Interim Joint Matriculation Board (IJMB) and the Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board (JUPEB), which facilitate direct entry into the second year of university.
