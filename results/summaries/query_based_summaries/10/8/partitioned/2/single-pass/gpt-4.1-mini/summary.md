# Summary: Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria follows a structured system beginning with six years of primary education, followed by three years of junior secondary and three years of senior secondary education, culminating in tertiary education typically lasting four years. Primary education emphasizes core subjects including Mathematics, English, Religious Knowledge, Agricultural Science, and indigenous languages, with continuous assessment and automatic promotion. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) scheme makes primary and junior secondary schooling free, compulsory, and monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission.

Secondary education spans junior and senior levels, covering a broad curriculum with core and elective subjects; students undertake the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) or the National Examination Council (NECO). Federal government colleges ("unity schools") and military-run schools offer model education with merit-based admission and relatively low fees, while state-owned secondary schools face challenges such as underfunding and teacher shortages, leading to reliance on unqualified teachers. Private secondary schools are costlier but often provide better resources and qualified staff. For higher education, Nigeria has numerous federal, state, and private universities and polytechnics, with additional admission pathways like the Interim Joint Matriculation Board programme allowing direct university entry. Efforts to improve literacy and female education are ongoing, though hindered by funding constraints, socio-cultural barriers, and regional disparities, especially in northern Nigeria.
