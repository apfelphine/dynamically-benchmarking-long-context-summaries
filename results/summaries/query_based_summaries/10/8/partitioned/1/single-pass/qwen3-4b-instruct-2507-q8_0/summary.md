**Education in Nigeria** follows a 9-3-4 system: 9 years of primary and junior secondary education (ages 6–14), followed by 3 years of senior secondary school (ages 15–18), and 4 years of tertiary education. The Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE), administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC), is taken at the end of senior secondary school. Students may also sit for the General Certificate of Education (GCE) in October/November as a supplement.  

Tertiary education includes universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with a total of 153 universities registered by the National Universities Commission (NUC). The government has majority control, with public and private institutions coexisting.  

The system includes formal and informal education, with vocational training administered by the National Board for Technical Education. Students may pursue a National Technical Certificate or Advanced National Technical Certificate. Apprenticeships are common, and informal education addresses literacy and skills development.  

Private schools, including religious and independent institutions, offer alternative curricula and are often more expensive.  

Education in Nigeria faces challenges such as teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, insecurity, regional disparities, and low literacy rates (62.02% of adults literate as of 2018). Efforts to improve access, quality, and equity are ongoing, including through government initiatives, international partnerships, and digital literacy programs.  

The system emphasizes the role of education in national development, with policies aimed at achieving universal basic education and reducing gender disparities, though challenges remain in rural and conflict-affected areas.
