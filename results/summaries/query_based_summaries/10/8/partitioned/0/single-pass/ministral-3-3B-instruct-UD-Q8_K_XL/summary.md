Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary education, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) framework, established in 1999. Primary education lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate, covering core subjects like Mathematics, English, and indigenous languages. Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 system, with students spending three years in Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three in Senior Secondary School (SSS), leading to the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE). The system is divided into federal, state, and private schools, with federal schools offering lower tuition fees and state schools often facing underfunding.

Tertiary education in Nigeria includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education, with the Federal University Commission (FUC) and National Universities Commission (NUC) overseeing the sector. Admission to universities typically requires the SSCE or equivalent, with minimum cut-off marks set by JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board). The country has seen significant growth in private universities, with 99 approved institutions as of 2021. Vocational education is also a key component, managed by the National Board for Technical Education, aiming to address high unemployment rates among graduates. Challenges persist, including teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and limited access to quality education in rural areas.
