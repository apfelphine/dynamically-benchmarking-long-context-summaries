**Education in Nigeria**  
- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 5, lasting six years. Curriculum includes Mathematics, English, Religious Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and one of the three main indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). Students take a Common Entrance Examination for admission to secondary schools.  
- **Secondary Education**: Follows a 6-3-3-4 system (6 years primary, 3 years junior secondary, 3 years senior secondary). Students take the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by WAEC. Core subjects include English, Mathematics, and Science; students choose electives in areas like Arts, Business, or Science.  
- **Federal and State Schools**: Federal schools are elite, merit-based, and funded by the government. State schools are less prestigious, with teacher shortages and lower funding. Private schools are expensive, with fees rising rapidly, and offer smaller class sizes and better facilities.  
- **Private and Religious Schools**: Private schools are costly and increasingly popular, with some religious schools offering bilingual or international curricula.  
- **Tertiary Education**: Includes public, private, polytechnics, and colleges of education. The government has approved 99 universities, including 20 new private universities in 2021. Entry requires JAMB scores (minimum 180 out of 400) or NCE/ND qualifications.  
- **Vocational Education**: Administered by the National Board for Technical Education; offers National Technical Certificates. Apprenticeships are common and valued for instilling work ethics.  
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, insecurity, and regional disparities affect rural education. Literacy remains low (62.02% in 2018), with efforts underway to improve access and digital literacy.  
- **Research and Evidence**: A 2024 systematic review on AI in open and distance learning found mixed effects on student performance and noted methodological gaps. A multilayered framework for predicting academic performance in ODL contexts was proposed.
