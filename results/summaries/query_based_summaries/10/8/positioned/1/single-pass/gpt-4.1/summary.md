## Education in Nigeria: Key Features

Education in Nigeria is overseen by various government agencies, with the system divided into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education starts around age five, lasts six years, and teaches core subjects including Mathematics, English, religious studies, science, and one of the main indigenous languages (Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo), with private schools offering additional subjects. After passing the Common Entrance Exam, students transition to secondary school, which comprises three years of junior (JSS) and three years of senior secondary school (SSS), culminating in the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by the WAEC and NECO. Technical colleges offer alternate vocational tracks, and international as well as advanced (A-level) programs such as IJMB and JUPEB provide direct entry into the second year of university.

Tertiary education includes universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with the government controlling the majority. There are federal, state, and private universities, the latter proliferating in recent years. University programs vary from four to six years depending on the discipline, with medicine and law taking longer. Entry requires passing the SSCE/GCE and a national entrance exam (JAMB). Vocational education, managed by the National Board for Technical Education, has expanded in response to unemployment, offering certificates and apprenticeships. Informal and adult literacy programs exist but face challenges due to decentralized funding and political instability. Efforts to improve literacy continue, though regional gaps persist and female education faces cultural and security barriers, with initiatives such as UNICEF's Girls’ Education Project supporting increased enrollment and completion rates for girls. The Nigerian government, in partnership with UNESCO, is addressing both general and digital literacy through targeted policies and programs.
