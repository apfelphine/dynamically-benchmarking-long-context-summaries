Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary education, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) program. Primary education lasts six years, followed by three years of junior secondary school (JSS) and three years of senior secondary school (SSS), culminating in the Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE). The curriculum includes core subjects like English, Mathematics, Social Studies, and religious education, alongside electives such as Computer Science, Fine Arts, and vocational studies.

Secondary education is divided into federal-sponsored, state-sponsored, and private/religious schools. Federal schools, often referred to as "unity schools," are funded and managed by the federal government, offering a more elite education due to their merit-based admissions. State schools, while also government-funded, face challenges like understaffing and inadequate resources. Private and religious schools cater to a more affluent population, offering specialized curricula and better facilities.

Promotional examinations, such as the SSCE, are critical for progression. The SSCE is administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and determines eligibility for university admission. Tertiary education includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education, with admission based on SSCE results. The Nigerian education system has faced challenges, including teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and high dropout rates, particularly in rural areas. Despite these issues, efforts are being made to improve access and quality through initiatives like the Free Universal Basic Education (FUBE) and vocational training programs.
