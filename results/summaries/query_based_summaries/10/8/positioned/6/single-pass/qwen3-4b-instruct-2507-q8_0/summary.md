Education in Nigeria is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education begins at age 5 and lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. Students take core subjects including Mathematics, English, Christian Religious Knowledge/Islamic Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and one of the three main indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, or Igbo). Admission to secondary schools is based on the Common Entrance Examination (CECE). 

Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 system, with six years of primary school, three years of Junior Secondary School (JSS), and three years of Senior Secondary School (SSS). Students study core subjects like Mathematics, English, Social Studies, and Science, with electives in areas such as Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Commerce. The Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSCE) is administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and is a key gateway to tertiary education.

Tertiary education in Nigeria includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. The government controls most universities, with a majority being public. The system has faced challenges such as high competition for university admission, teacher shortages, and poor infrastructure. The National Examination Council (NECO) and WAEC administer examinations. 

Private schools are expensive, with fees increasing rapidly, and often have smaller class sizes and better facilities. Vocational education is administered by the National Board for Technical Education and includes National Technical Certificates and Advanced National Technical Certificates. 

Female education faces challenges including gender-based stereotyping, teen pregnancy, and poverty. Efforts to improve female education include government initiatives and NGO programs. The literacy rate is low, at 62.02% as of 2018, with significant regional disparities. The government has prioritized literacy and digital literacy, aiming for 95% digital literacy by 2030.
