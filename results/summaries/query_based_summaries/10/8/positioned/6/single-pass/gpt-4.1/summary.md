## Education in Nigeria

Nigeria’s education system comprises primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, overseen by federal and state governments, as well as private entities. Primary education begins at age 5, lasts six years, and culminates in a first school-leaving certificate and Common Entrance Examination. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, initiated in 1999, mandates nine years of free and compulsory schooling, combining six years of primary with three years of junior secondary education. Subjects taught at the primary level include Mathematics, English, Christian or Islamic Religious Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and a major indigenous language.

Secondary education follows the "6-3-3-4" format: three years each of junior and senior secondary school, with a curriculum split between core subjects and electives. Federal government colleges, known as unity schools, are considered elite and have stricter admissions via national merit exams, while state and private secondary schools vary widely in quality, facilities, and teacher qualifications. Students take promotional exams, notably the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) and the General Certificate of Education (GCE), managed by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and National Examination Council (NECO).

Tertiary education comprises universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education, with governance shared among federal, state, and private institutions. Admission is typically based on SSCE/GCE results and Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) scores. Universities are categorized into generational tiers, with newer private universities accelerating expansion. Vocational education is shaped by the National Board for Technical Education and includes technical certificates, apprenticeships, and informal training. The education system faces persistent challenges of literacy (adult literacy rate at 62% in 2018), regional disparities, teacher shortages, variable education quality, and barriers to female education, with ongoing government and international initiatives—such as the Girls' Education Project—aimed at improving enrollment, literacy, digital skills, and inclusion for marginalized groups.
