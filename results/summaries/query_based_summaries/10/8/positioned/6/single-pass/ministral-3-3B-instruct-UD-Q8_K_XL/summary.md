Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary education, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) framework. Primary education lasts six years, covering foundational subjects like Mathematics, English, and indigenous languages, culminating in the Common Entrance Examination for secondary school admission. Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 system, with three years in Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three in Senior Secondary School (SSS), culminating in the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE). Admission to Federal Government Secondary Schools is merit-based, while State and private schools require entrance exams and fees, respectively.

The tertiary education sector is highly diversified, with public universities (e.g., University of Ibadan, University of Lagos) and private institutions offering degrees in various fields. Vocational education, managed by the National Board for Technical Education, is also prominent, though it faces challenges like teacher shortages and outdated curricula. The system prioritizes free and compulsory education for all children, with efforts to improve literacy and vocational training, though disparities persist, particularly in rural and economically disadvantaged areas.
