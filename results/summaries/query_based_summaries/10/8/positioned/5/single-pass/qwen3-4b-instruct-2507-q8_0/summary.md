**Education in Nigeria**

- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 5, lasting six years. Students study subjects including Mathematics, English, Christian Religious Knowledge/Islamic Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and one of three indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). A Common Entrance Examination determines admission to secondary schools. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) policy, introduced in 1999, mandates free, compulsory, and nine-year schooling (6 years primary + 3 years junior secondary).
- **Secondary Education**: Follows a 6-3-3-4 system (6 years primary, 3 years junior secondary, 3 years senior secondary, 4 years tertiary). Students take the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) at the end of senior secondary. The curriculum includes core subjects (English, Mathematics, Economics, Civic Education) and electives. Federal Government Colleges are elite institutions with merit-based admissions via the National Common Entrance Examination (NCE).
- **Tertiary Education**: Includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. The government controls most institutions. Admission to universities is based on SSCE results and the Joint University Matriculation Board (JAMB) entrance exam. Public universities are tuition-free, while private institutions charge higher fees. The system faces challenges like low teacher qualifications, inadequate infrastructure, and gender disparities.
- **Private and Religious Schools**: Private schools are expensive, with fees increasing rapidly (up to 30 million Naira). They offer smaller class sizes and better facilities. Religious schools (Catholic, Methodist, Baptist) are historically less expensive but can be costly. The government has introduced policies to promote equity and access, including scholarships and free textbooks.
- **Vocational Education**: Administered by the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE). Students pursue National Technical Certificates (NTC) or Advanced NTCs. Challenges include outdated curricula, poor teacher training, and limited industry alignment. Apprenticeships are instrumental in skill development.
- **Female Education**: Women’s participation in education has increased, but gender-based barriers persist. Teen pregnancy, early marriage, religious beliefs, and poverty hinder female enrollment. The UNICEF Girls' Education Project supports girls in rural areas through cash transfers and community-based programs.
- **Literacy and Challenges**: Literacy rates are low (62.02% in 2018), with significant regional disparities. Efforts to improve literacy include adult literacy programs and digital literacy initiatives. The government has set a target of 95% digital literacy by 2030.
- **Education Reform**: The UBE system aims to improve access and quality. Challenges remain in teacher training, curriculum relevance, and infrastructure, especially in rural and conflict-affected areas. The government continues to invest in education to address inequality and promote inclusive development.
