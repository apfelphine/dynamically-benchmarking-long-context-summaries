Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary education, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) program. Primary education lasts six years, covering core subjects like Mathematics, English, and indigenous languages, with a Common Entrance Examination determining admission to secondary schools. Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 system, with three years in Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three years in Senior Secondary School (SSS), culminating in the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE). Federal, state, and private schools offer varying levels of resources, with federal schools often viewed as elite due to merit-based admissions.

The tertiary education sector includes public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education, with admission determined by the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) exams. Vocational education is also prominent, though challenges like teacher shortages and outdated curricula persist. The education system faces issues such as regional disparities, high out-of-school rates, and inadequate infrastructure, particularly in rural areas. Efforts to improve literacy and digital education are ongoing, with partnerships and initiatives aimed at enhancing access and quality.
