**Education in Nigeria**

Education in Nigeria is structured primarily into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education begins at about age five and lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. Subjects taught at this level include Mathematics, English, religious knowledge, agricultural science, home economics, and major indigenous languages. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, established in 1999, integrates six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education, making nine years of continuous, free, and compulsory schooling under monitoring by the Universal Basic Education Commission. 

Secondary education spans six years divided between junior and senior secondary schools, with curricula encompassing core subjects like English, Mathematics, Economics, and Civic Education, alongside electives in sciences, arts, and vocational areas. Federal government colleges ("unity schools") and state-owned schools differ in management and resources, with federally funded schools often perceived as elite. Private and religious secondary schools typically feature smaller class sizes and better resources but are more expensive. Nigeria's tertiary education system includes public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges, with admissions mainly governed by standardized exams. The sector faces challenges such as teacher shortages, underfunding, rural educational infrastructure deficits, and disparities in literacy rates, particularly along gender and regional lines. Various government and NGO initiatives target improving female education, literacy, and vocational training. Despite these efforts, literacy rates remain low compared to global averages, and insurgent activities in certain regions have destabilized education access.
