Education in Nigeria is a highly centralized system overseen by the Federal Ministry of Education and the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC), which enforces a policy of free, compulsory, and universal basic education for nine years (six years of primary school and three years of junior secondary school). The curriculum is structured under a "6-3-3-4" system, leading to the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by bodies such as the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and the National Examination Council (NECO). Secondary education is divided into Federal, State, and Private sectors; Federal schools are merit-based and elite, while State schools often face funding constraints and understaffing issues, and Private schools offer higher fees but superior facilities and teacher qualifications. Tertiary education includes 153 universities and polytechnics, with a mix of public institutions owned by the government and privately owned colleges, though admission remains competitive and often requires direct entry pathways like the IJMB or JUPEB for students bypassing the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME).

Challenges facing the Nigerian education system include significant disparities in literacy rates, particularly in the North, where insecurity, poverty, and cultural factors hinder enrollment. Vocational education is administered by the National Board for Technical Education but suffers from outdated curricula and a shortage of qualified instructors. While initiatives like the Girls' Education Project have sought to address gender gaps, barriers such as teenage pregnancy, militancy (e.g., Boko Haram), and poor infrastructure persist. Furthermore, the system faces criticism for rote learning methods, low teacher remuneration in public sectors, and a heavy reliance on private tutoring due to the perceived inadequacies of public education quality. Despite these hurdles, the government continues to implement reforms aimed at expanding access, improving digital literacy targets, and integrating modern pedagogical approaches.
