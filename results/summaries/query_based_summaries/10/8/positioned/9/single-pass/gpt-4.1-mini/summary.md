# Summary: Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria begins with six years of primary schooling starting around age five, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) scheme, established in 1999 and monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission, mandates free, compulsory, and continuous nine years of formal education, combining six years of primary and three years of junior secondary schooling. Secondary education, under a 6-3-3-4 or 9-3-4 system, comprises three years each of junior and senior secondary school, covering core and elective subjects. Federal-sponsored secondary schools (“unity schools”) and state schools differ in funding, reputation, and teacher qualifications, with federal schools generally better resourced. Private secondary schools tend to have smaller class sizes and better facilities but are more expensive.

Tertiary education consists of universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with over 150 universities (federal, state, private). Admission usually requires Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) results and a Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) exam score. Vocational education is supervised by national boards but faces challenges such as outdated curricula and teacher shortages. Informal education and literacy programs exist but are hampered by decentralization and underfunding. Female education has improved via government and NGO initiatives but still faces barriers from cultural, economic, and security factors. Literacy rates remain low nationally, with regional disparities influenced by poverty and insecurity. International schools operate English-medium curricula and advanced-level programs like A-levels through IJMB and JUPEB for university entry. Overall, Nigeria’s education system struggles with funding, quality, equity, and infrastructure challenges despite ongoing reforms and expansion efforts.
