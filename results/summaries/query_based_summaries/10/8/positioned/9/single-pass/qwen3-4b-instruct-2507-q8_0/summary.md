Education in Nigeria is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education begins at age 5 and lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. The curriculum includes Mathematics, English language, Christian Religious Knowledge/Islamic Knowledge, Agricultural science, Home economics, and one of the three main indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). Students must pass a Common Entrance Examination to gain admission to secondary schools. 

Secondary education spans six years (3 years of Junior Secondary School and 3 years of Senior Secondary School) under the 6-3-3-4 system. The curriculum includes core subjects like English, Mathematics, and Social Studies, along with elective subjects such as Biology, Chemistry, Physics, or Business. The Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE), administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC), is the key assessment. Students may also take the National Examination Council (NECO) exams.

Tertiary education includes public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. The government has majority control, with a total of 153 universities registered. Admission to universities is competitive, based on performance in the SSCE/GCE and the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) entrance exam. The typical duration of undergraduate programs varies by discipline, with engineering and medicine taking longer. 

Vocational education is administered by the National Board for Technical Education and includes National Technical Certificates. Informal education and literacy programs are also significant, especially in rural areas. Female education faces challenges such as teen pregnancy, early marriage, and cultural barriers, though initiatives like the Girls' Education Project aim to improve access. Literacy rates remain low, particularly in the North-East and North-West, with efforts underway to improve both traditional and digital literacy.
