Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary levels, emphasizing both formal and informal education. **Primary education** begins at age 5, lasting six years, with subjects including Mathematics, English, and indigenous languages. It is compulsory, free, and monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC), which ensures access, equity, and quality. The **Universal Primary Education (UPE)** program, introduced in 1976, was later revised to the **Universal Basic Education (UBE)** in 1999, making education free, compulsory, and a right for all children aged 6–14.

**Secondary education** follows, structured into Junior Secondary School (JSS) and Senior Secondary School (SSS), each lasting three years. Students must pass the Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE) to progress. Federal, state, and private schools offer varying levels of quality, with federal schools often viewed as elite due to merit-based admissions. Teacher qualifications vary, with many state schools facing shortages and low salaries. Vocational education is also available, with options like technical colleges and apprenticeships.

**Tertiary education** includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges, with public institutions being the most common. Admission is competitive, often requiring the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME) scores. Private universities are also expanding, though they face regulatory challenges. Vocational training and informal education dominate, addressing labor market needs and improving employability.

**Challenges** include underfunding, teacher shortages, and regional disparities, particularly in rural areas. Efforts to improve literacy and digital education are ongoing, with initiatives like the **Centre of Excellence for Literacy and Literacy Education (CELLE)** and partnerships with UNESCO. Despite these efforts, systemic issues persist, including poverty, insecurity, and cultural barriers, which hinder equitable access to education.
