## Education in Nigeria

Primary education in Nigeria starts around age five and lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. The curriculum includes Mathematics, English, religious knowledge, agricultural science, home economics, and one of the three major indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo), with private schools offering additional subjects like Computer Science, French, and Fine Arts. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, initiated in 1999, integrates six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education, making nine years of free, compulsory schooling overseen by the Universal Basic Education Commission.

Secondary education follows a "6-3-3-4" system with three years each of junior and senior secondary school. Junior secondary covers general subjects, while senior secondary includes core subjects plus electives. Federal government colleges (unity schools), state-sponsored schools, and private/religious institutions make up the secondary system, with varying standards and teacher qualifications. Examinations such as the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) and General Certificate of Education (GCE) are administered by bodies like WAEC and NECO. Higher education consists of public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges, with federal and state governments playing major roles in governance. Vocational education is administered by the National Board for Technical Education, but challenges persist in curriculum quality, funding, and enrollment. Gender disparities and regional inequities in literacy and school attendance remain significant issues, with ongoing government and NGO initiatives targeting female education and literacy improvement.
