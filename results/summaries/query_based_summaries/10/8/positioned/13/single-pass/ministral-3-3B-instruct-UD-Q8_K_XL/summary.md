Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary education, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education at the foundational levels.

**Primary Education** begins around age 5 and lasts six years, culminating in the Common Entrance Examination for admission to secondary schools. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, introduced in 1999, ensures that education is free, compulsory, and a right for all children, covering nine years of schooling (six years of primary and three years of junior secondary). The Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) oversees this program, ensuring its implementation and quality.

**Secondary Education** follows primary education, divided into Junior Secondary School (JSS) and Senior Secondary School (SSS). The 6-3-3-4 system, introduced in 1982, organizes education into six years of primary, three years of junior secondary, three years of senior secondary, and four years of tertiary education. Students must pass the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) to proceed to tertiary education. Federal, state, and private schools offer varying levels of education, with federal schools being the most prestigious due to their merit-based admissions.

**Tertiary Education** includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. Admission to universities is highly competitive, with students typically taking the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME). The Federal Government and state governments own and manage many universities, while private institutions also operate. Vocational education and training (TVET) are emphasized to address labor market needs, with programs ranging from National Technical Certificates to Advanced National Technical Certificates.

**Challenges** include underfunding, teacher shortages, and disparities in educational quality between urban and rural areas. Despite these challenges, Nigeria has made strides in expanding access to education, though challenges in quality and equity persist. The government continues to prioritize education reform, aiming to improve infrastructure, teacher training, and overall educational standards.
