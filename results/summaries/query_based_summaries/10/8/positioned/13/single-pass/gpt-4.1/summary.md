## Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria comprises primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, overseen predominantly by government agencies such as the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) and the National Board for Technical Education. Primary education begins around age five, lasts six years, and is followed by a compulsory, government-backed Universal Basic Education (UBE) program that encompasses three years of junior secondary school, thus guaranteeing nine years of basic schooling. Subjects taught include Mathematics, English, local languages, and other core disciplines. Promotion from primary to secondary school involves the Common Entrance Examination, and the six-year secondary stage is divided into junior and senior segments, with students taking further promotional and qualifying examinations such as the BECE and the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by WAEC and NECO.

Secondary education exists in federal, state, private, and religious schools, with significant disparities in infrastructure, teacher qualifications, and educational resources. Federal "unity schools" are considered elite due to merit-based admissions and government funding, while many state schools face challenges with underqualified staff. Private schools generally offer superior facilities and attract highly trained teachers but command much higher fees. Technical colleges and vocational education are available, although challenges persist with outdated curricula, inadequate resources, and low teacher supply. Informal education and literacy programs, often supported by NGOs, supplement formal systems, but adult and youth literacy rates remain relatively low—roughly 62% of adults were literate in 2018, with ongoing government efforts to improve functional and digital literacy.

Tertiary education is delivered through a mix of public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges. Nigeria has a significant number of universities and higher education institutions, with entry typically contingent on SSCE/GCE credits and performance in the JAMB exam. Undergraduate programs last from four to six years depending on the field; federal, state, and private universities, as well as advanced-level programs like IJMB and JUPEB, are available for university-bound students. Although women's participation in education is rising, challenges such as early marriage, religious influences, insecurity, and poverty continue to hinder gender parity, especially in northern regions. Recent policy trends focus on increasing literacy, digital skills, and inclusive education for vulnerable groups.
