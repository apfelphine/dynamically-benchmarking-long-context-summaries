## Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education generally begins around age 5 and lasts for six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, established in 1999, aims to make the first nine years of schooling free and compulsory.

Secondary education follows primary school, with the "6-3-3-4" system allocating six years to secondary school: three years of Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three years of Senior Secondary School (SSS). The SSS curriculum includes four core subjects complemented by elective subjects from a list of 17 options. Students must pass the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) to proceed to tertiary education, which can be pursued in public, state, or private institutions.

Challenges within the Nigerian education system include regional disparities, poor infrastructure in rural areas, low teacher remuneration leading to unqualified staff in state schools, and low adult literacy rates, despite ongoing government initiatives to improve education access.
