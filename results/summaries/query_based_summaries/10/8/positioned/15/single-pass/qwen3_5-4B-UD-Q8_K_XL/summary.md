Education in Nigeria is structured around a 6-3-3-4 system, though reforms are transitioning toward a 9-3-4 model to consolidate the first nine years of schooling into a continuous, free, and compulsory Universal Basic Education (UBE) framework monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC). Primary education, lasting six years, covers foundational subjects including indigenous languages and religious knowledge, while secondary education is divided into three years of Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three years of Senior Secondary School (SSS). Admission to secondary schools requires passing the Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE), and progression to tertiary institutions is determined by the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE), administered by bodies such as the West African Examination Council (WAEC) and the National Examination Council (NECO), where grades range from A1 to F9.

The tertiary sector comprises universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with the government holding majority control through federal and state institutions, alongside a growing number of private universities. Admission to universities typically requires a minimum Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) score of 180 and specific subject credits, with entry occurring between ages 17 and 18. Vocational education is administered by the National Board for Technical Education and overseen by the National Business and Technical Examinations Board (NABTEB), offering certificates and apprenticeships. Challenges within the system include regional disparities, particularly low literacy rates in the North, insufficient funding, teacher shortages, and security issues in rural areas, alongside significant barriers to female education such as poverty, teenage pregnancy, and cultural factors.
