Education in Nigeria:

- **Primary Education**: Begins around age 5, lasting six years. Students study subjects including Mathematics, English, Christian Religious Knowledge/Islamic Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and one of the three indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). Private schools may offer Computer Science, French, and Fine Arts. Students take a Common Entrance Examination to qualify for secondary schools.
- **Secondary Education**: Follows a 6-3-3-4 system (6 years primary, 3 years junior secondary, 3 years senior secondary). Core subjects include English, Mathematics, Social Studies, and Science. Elective subjects vary by track (e.g., Biology, Chemistry, Physics for science; Literature, History, Geography for humanities). Students take the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by the West African Examination Council (WAEC) or the National Examination Council (NECO).
- **Tertiary Education**: Includes public and private universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. Admission requires SSCE/GCE credits and a high score on the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) exam. Programs vary in duration (e.g., 4 years for Social Sciences, 5 years for Engineering, Medicine). Private universities are increasingly common, with new ones approved by the government.
- **Vocational Education**: Administered by the National Board for Technical Education, with programs leading to National Technical Certificates. Apprenticeships are also available.
- **Female Education**: Women's participation is increasing, but gender-based stereotyping, teen pregnancy, poverty, and poor school facilities remain barriers. UNICEF and other organizations have implemented programs to support girls' education.
- **Literacy**: As of 2018, the adult literacy rate was 62.02%. Efforts to improve literacy are ongoing, with a government target of 95% digital literacy by 2030.
- **Challenges**: Inadequate infrastructure, teacher shortages, low teacher salaries, and political instability hinder the system. Private tutoring is widespread and costly, with households spending up to 61% of their educational expenditure on it. Youth unemployment remains high (36.1% in 2019).
