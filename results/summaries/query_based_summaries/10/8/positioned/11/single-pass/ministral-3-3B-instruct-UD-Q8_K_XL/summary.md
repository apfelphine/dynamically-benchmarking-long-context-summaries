Education in Nigeria spans a structured system from primary to tertiary levels, with a strong emphasis on compulsory and free education. Primary education begins at age 5 and lasts six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. The **Universal Basic Education (UBE)** program, introduced in 1999, ensures free, compulsory, and accessible education for all children aged 6–14, covering primary and junior secondary school. The system is divided into **Federal**, **State**, and **Private** schools, with Federal and State schools offering free education, though students often incur costs for books, uniforms, and miscellaneous expenses.

Secondary education follows, with students spending three years in Junior Secondary School (JSS) and three years in Senior Secondary School (SSS), leading to the **Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE)**. Students can also pursue vocational training at technical colleges. Admission to universities is highly competitive, with most students applying through the **Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB)**. The system faces challenges such as teacher shortages, inadequate infrastructure, and regional disparities in education quality.

Vocational education and informal learning play significant roles, with apprenticeships and non-formal education programs aimed at improving employability. Despite progress, Nigeria’s education system continues to grapple with issues like underfunding, unequal access, and the need for systemic reforms to enhance quality and equity.
