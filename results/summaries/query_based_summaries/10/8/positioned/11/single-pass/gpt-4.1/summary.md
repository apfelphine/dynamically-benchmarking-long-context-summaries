## Education in Nigeria

Education in Nigeria is structured into three main levels: primary, secondary, and tertiary education. Primary school begins at age 5, lasts six years, and covers core subjects including Mathematics, English, religious studies, agricultural science, and indigenous languages. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) program, established to offer nine years of uninterrupted free and compulsory education (six years of primary plus three years of junior secondary), is monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) and also encompasses adult literacy and special groups. Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 system: three years of junior and three years of senior secondary school, with a curriculum spanning arts, sciences, vocational subjects, and technology. After junior secondary school, students may proceed to technical colleges. Promotional and exit exams such as the BECE, WAEC SSCE, and NECO SSCE are required, with performance influencing access to higher education.

The tertiary sector comprises universities (federal, state, and private), polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with over 150 universities as of 2017. University admission is based on SSCE/GCE results and the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) exam, while alternative entry routes include the IJMB and JUPEB advanced level programs. Vocational and informal education are overseen by bodies such as the National Board for Technical Education and NABTEB. Despite ongoing efforts to improve literacy—with recent initiatives aiming for broad functional and digital literacy—challenges persist, including regional disparities, underfunding, infrastructure deficits, teacher shortages, gender inequality, and high out-of-school rates, especially in northern regions. International schools and advanced-level programs (IJMB, JUPEB) are available, and female education is supported by targeted interventions, though obstacles such as poverty, insecurity, and cultural factors remain prevalent.
