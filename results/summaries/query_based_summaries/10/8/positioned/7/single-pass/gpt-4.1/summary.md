## Education in Nigeria: A Concise Summary

Education in Nigeria begins with six years of primary schooling starting at age five, with a curriculum including core subjects and indigenous languages. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) policy, launched in 1999, made nine years of schooling (six primary, three junior secondary) free and compulsory, aiming to improve access and literacy. Secondary education follows a 6-3-3-4 (now often termed 9-3-4 or 6-3-3-4) structure, including three years each of junior and senior secondary school. Students undertake various subjects and examinations such as the SSCE, administered by WAEC and NECO, which are prerequisites for tertiary education. Vocational and technical training options are available, along with informal education and literacy programs for adults and special groups. There is significant involvement of private, religious, and international schools; private school fees are high and rising.

Tertiary education consists of universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education, with both public and private ownership. Admissions require certain exam results and cut-off scores, with alternatives like IJMB and JUPEB offering direct university entry. Despite growing enrollment, literacy rates remain a challenge, particularly in northern regions and rural areas. Female education has improved through numerous initiatives, though barriers persist. Nigeria’s education system faces issues such as underfunded state schools, teacher shortages, regional disparities, and quality concerns, but ongoing government and international efforts target improvements in both traditional and digital literacy, vocational training, access, and governance.
