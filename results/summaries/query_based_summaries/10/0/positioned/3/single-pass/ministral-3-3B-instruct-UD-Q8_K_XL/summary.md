Sweden’s education system is structured around a **nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola)**, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**, and higher education. Compulsory education begins at age six, covering core subjects like Swedish, mathematics, English, and science, with a focus on foundational skills and social development. Students receive grades starting from year six, using a system of A–E, with F being failing. The curriculum is standardized, though schools may offer additional language courses or extracurricular activities.

Upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) is elective but highly attended, with a **99% enrollment rate**. It is divided into **18 national programs**, including preparatory and vocational tracks, each lasting three years. Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, and mathematics) to qualify for upper secondary education. The system emphasizes practical skills and career readiness, with vocational programs offering hands-on training. Admissions are based on elementary school grades, though some programs require additional entrance exams.

Higher education in Sweden follows the **Bologna Process framework**, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Public universities are free for EU/EEA citizens, while international students pay tuition fees. The system includes **universities** (offering theoretical degrees) and **higher vocational education** (focused on practical skills). Grading varies by institution but generally follows a structured system, with many universities adopting the ECTS system for international comparability. The education system is known for its emphasis on **equality, research, and innovation**, with strong support for lifelong learning and vocational training.
