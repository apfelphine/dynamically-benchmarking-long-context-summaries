## Education in Sweden

The Swedish education system is structured across multiple stages, including preschool, primary education, upper secondary education, and higher education. Preschool education is available for all children from age one, with all children eligible for at least three hours of free preschool education daily starting at age three. The compulsory school, or *grundskola*, is a nine-year system spanning ages six to fifteen or sixteen.

The compulsory curriculum covers 16 required subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, and science. Students receive grades starting from year six, using a system of A, B, C, D, and E (with F being a failing grade). Upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*) typically lasts three years and is divided into national programmes, which can be either preparatory or vocational. Higher education is organized into three cycles compatible with the Bologna Process, encompassing Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral levels, with universities and research entitled university colleges offering these degrees.

## Key Features

*   **Compulsory Education:** Mandatory from ages six to fifteen or sixteen within the comprehensive school (*grundskola*).
*   **Primary/Middle Education:** Nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*) covering ages 6–15.
*   **Upper Secondary:** Three-year *gymnasieskola* divided into national preparatory or vocational programmes.
*   **Higher Education:** Follows the Bologna Process (Bachelor's, Master's, Doctoral) offered by Universities and University Colleges.
*   **Independent Schools:** Privately run independent schools (*friskolor*) receive public funding but cannot discriminate or charge additional fees, though the system faces ongoing debate regarding standards and the profit-making model.
