Sweden’s education system is structured around a **9-year compulsory compulsory school (grundskola)**, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)** and higher education. Key features include:

- **Compulsory School (Grundskola, ages 6–15):**
  - **Curriculum:** 16 core subjects (Swedish, English, math, science, etc.), with optional foreign language courses. Grading starts in **Grade 6**, using letters (A–E) and numerical scores (10–20 points per subject), with a total score used for upper secondary school applications.
  - **National Tests:** Administered in Grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess student progress.
  - **Focus:** Broad academic foundation, emphasizing critical thinking and practical skills.

- **Upper Secondary School (Gymnasieskola, ages 16–19):**
  - **Structure:** 3-year programs divided into **preparatory** (general studies) and **vocational** tracks. Students choose from 18 national programs, each with specialized courses.
  - **Diploma Project:** Mandatory for all programs, requiring students to plan, execute, and reflect on a project.
  - **Eligibility:** Students must pass 8 core subjects (Swedish, English, math) and 4 additional subjects for vocational tracks or 12 for preparatory tracks.
  - **Admission:** Based on elementary school grades, with some programs requiring entrance exams.

- **Higher Education:**
  - **Degrees:** Follows the **Bologna Process** with three cycles: bachelor’s (180 credits), master’s (120 credits), and doctoral (240 credits). Public universities offer free education, while private institutions charge fees.
  - **Language:** Swedish and English are primary languages for instruction, with proficiency tests required for admission.
  - **Student Aid:** Government-funded loans and grants support students, though fees apply for non-EU/EEA/Swiss citizens.

- **Key Features:**
  - **Free Education:** Public schools and universities are tuition-free for Swedish/EU students.
  - **Equality:** Schools aim to provide equal opportunities, though challenges remain in addressing disparities among students.
  - **Reform:** Recent reforms emphasize **inclusive learning**, **digital integration**, and **vocational training** to prepare students for the workforce.

Sweden’s system balances academic rigor with practical skills, aiming to foster **critical thinking, social inclusion, and career readiness**.
