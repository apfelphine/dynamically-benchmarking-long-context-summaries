# Summary of Education in Sweden

Sweden’s education system is structured into compulsory primary education (grundskola) spanning nine years from ages 6–7 to 15–16, followed by elective upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) typically lasting three years. The comprehensive school provides 16 compulsory subjects across lower, middle, and upper stages, with grades assigned from year 6 onward. Upper secondary school offers 18 national programs divided into preparatory and vocational tracks, all providing eligibility for university education. Students apply to programs based on their grades, with some requiring entrance exams. National standardized tests in grades 3, 6, and 9 help ensure equitable assessment. Preschool education is widely available and partially subsidized, primarily aimed at supporting working parents and early childhood development.

Higher education follows the Bologna Process with degrees at bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels awarded by universities and university colleges. Admission typically requires completion of upper secondary school and language proficiency in Swedish and English. Sweden offers publicly funded independent schools (friskolor), established since a 1992 reform, which receive public funding but cannot charge fees or discriminate in admissions. This model has stirred mixed public opinion. Grading systems vary across institutions but commonly include A–F scales, with the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) serving as an additional university admission route. Student financial aid is provided by the National Board of Student Aid and higher education is free of charge for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. Vocational post-secondary education exists independently of university degrees and emphasizes workplace experience. Sweden’s education exhibits strong government oversight while encouraging diversity through alternatives such as independent schools and specialized upper secondary programs.
