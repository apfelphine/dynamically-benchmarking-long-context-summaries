Sweden’s education system is structured around a nine-year compulsory **grundskola** (comprehensive school) followed by three years of **gymnasieskola** (upper secondary education). Compulsory education spans ages 6–16, with free, standardized instruction in core subjects like Swedish, mathematics, English, and science. Students receive grades from year 6, with the grading system (A–F) determining eligibility for upper secondary school.

Upper secondary education is elective but highly enrolled, divided into **preparatory** and **vocational** tracks. Students take standardized national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9, though these are not used for grading. The system emphasizes academic rigor, with a focus on critical thinking and practical skills. Higher education is free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens, with degrees structured under the **Bologna Process** (bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels). Public universities and university colleges offer diverse programs, while independent schools (friskolor) operate under public funding but with greater autonomy.

Sweden’s education system is noted for its emphasis on equality, innovation, and practical preparation for both academic and vocational careers. Recent reforms aim to improve equity and adapt to digital learning, though debates persist over the role of independent schools and the quality of public education.
