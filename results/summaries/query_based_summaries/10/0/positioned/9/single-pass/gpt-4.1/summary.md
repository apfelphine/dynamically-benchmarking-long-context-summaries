## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is structured into distinct levels including preschool (Förskola; ages 1–6), preschool class (Förskoleklass; age 6–7), compulsory primary school (Grundskola; grades 1–9, ages 7–16), upper secondary school (Gymnasieskola; three years, ages 16–19), and higher education at university colleges and universities. The compulsory Grundskola curriculum covers 16 subjects, with the grading system shifting from written assessments to a letter grade scale (A–F) starting from grade 6. All students are required to pass mathematics, Swedish, and English to proceed to upper secondary education. Upper secondary school is elective and divided into 18 national programmes—categorized as either preparatory or vocational. Admission depends on grades from compulsory school, and students who do not qualify may enter introductory programs, commonly for immigrants or those needing to fulfill core subject requirements.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process, with degrees organized into three cycles: bachelor's (kandidatexamen, three years), master's (magisterexamen, one year; masterexamen, two years), and doctoral (PhD or licentiate, two to four years). Entry to higher education typically requires completion of upper secondary school and language proficiency in Swedish and English. Universities and university colleges provide general academic degrees and a wide range of professional qualifications; grading systems vary but are compatible with the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS). Swedish students receive government aid for studies, and tuition is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. Additionally, there is an independent school (friskola) sector, offering publicly funded alternatives to municipal schools. Sweden participates in international assessments such as PISA and PIRLS, and the education system continues to adapt in response to results and evolving national policies.
