**Education in Sweden**  

Sweden’s education system is based on a nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola), mandatory from ages 6 to 16. It is divided into three stages: primary (ages 6–13), lower secondary (ages 13–16), and upper secondary (ages 16–19).  

Primary education includes 16 compulsory subjects, with students taking a national test in grades 3, 6, and 9. From grade 6, students receive an Individual Development Plan (IUP), and grades are assigned from A to E, with F indicating failure. The total score for university applications is calculated from subject grades (E=10, D=12.5, C=15, B=17.5, A=20).  

Upper secondary education, known as gymnasieskola, offers 18 national programs—15 preparatory and 3 vocational—focused on general studies or practical skills. Students must pass 8–12 subjects (vocational) or 12 (preparatory) to qualify. A mandatory diploma project is required.  

Higher education is offered through universities and university colleges, with degrees aligned to the Bologna Process. Students must meet general university admissions criteria (generell studiekompetanse). Private schools, such as those with religious or pedagogical alternatives, are approved and grant-aided but cannot select students based on ability.  

Special education is provided in ordinary schools, with support from educational psychologists and social welfare workers. The system emphasizes inclusion, universal design, and individualized instruction.  

Sweden ranks highly in international assessments, with strong performance in reading and mathematics, though gaps in achievement persist. The education system is highly centralized, with the National Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensuring quality.  

Private and international schools are limited, with around 10% of higher education students attending private institutions. The system has faced criticism over grading practices and equity, particularly in access to quality education.
