## Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system consists of a comprehensive nine-year compulsory school (grundskola) covering ages 6–16, divided into lower, middle, and upper stages. Students study 16 compulsory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, and social studies, with non-compulsory foreign languages introduced from grade 6. Grading starts in grade 6 using a scale from A to F, and national standardized tests are conducted in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment. After compulsory education, nearly all students (99% enrollment) enter upper secondary school (gymnasieskola), which lasts three years and offers 18 national programmes split between vocational and preparatory tracks. Students must meet subject-specific eligibility criteria to enter these programmes, and all students complete a diploma project.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process with bachelor's, master's, and doctoral levels, offering general academic degrees alongside professional qualifications. Admissions require completion of upper secondary education or equivalent, and applicants must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English. Universities and university colleges provide a variety of programs, with various grading scales aligned with the European Credit Transfer System. Sweden also has higher vocational education focusing on practical skills and workplace experience, distinct from the university system. Since the 1990s, private independent schools (friskolor) have been publicly funded but face ongoing debates about standards and profit-making; they currently enroll a significant proportion of students, with some concerns over grade inflation. Sweden has shown fluctuating results in international assessments like PISA and PIRLS, with immigrant influx identified as a contributing factor to recent declines.
