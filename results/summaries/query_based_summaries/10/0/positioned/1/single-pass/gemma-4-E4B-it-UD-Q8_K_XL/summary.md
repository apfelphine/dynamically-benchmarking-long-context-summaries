## Education in Sweden

**Structure and Levels:**
The Swedish educational system is divided into several levels. Preschool education is offered to children aged 1–6, while the compulsory school system is a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*), mandatory for children aged 6–16. This system is further divided into a lower stage (*Lågstadiet*, ages 7–10), a middle stage (*Mellanstadiet*, ages 11–13), and an upper stage (*Högstadiet*, ages 14–16). Above compulsory education, there is upper secondary education (*Gymnasieskola*), which usually lasts three years and is elective, though it has a 99% enrollment rate. Tertiary education is offered through universities (*Universitet*) and university colleges (*Högskola*).

**Key Features:**
In the primary school (*Grundskola*), no official grades are given, although teachers may provide commentary. Students begin receiving grades in lower secondary school (*Ungdomsskole*), and these grades affect their acceptance into upper secondary school. Upper secondary education is based on national programmes, which are divided into preparatory and vocational types. The system is generally based on public schools, although independent schools (*friskolor*) exist and are publicly funded, provided they meet certain criteria.

**Higher Education:**
Higher education follows the Bologna Process and is broadly divided into three cycles: bachelor's, master's, and doctoral levels. Universities focus on theoretical subjects, while university colleges offer a wider range of choices, including engineering and vocational training. Admission to most higher education schools requires a general university admissions certificate (*generell studiekompetanse*).
