Sweden’s education system is structured into **nine years of compulsory compulsory school (grundskola)**, followed by **three years of upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**. Compulsory education covers ages 6–16, with a focus on 16 core subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, and social studies. Grading begins in **year 6**, using a letter scale (A–E) with numeric equivalents (E=10, D=12.5, etc.), and a total score is calculated for university applications.

Upper secondary education is **elective but highly enrolled**, divided into **18 national programs** (preparatory or vocational). Students take standardized exams (e.g., **Gymnasieexamen**) to qualify for university. The system emphasizes **free education** for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, though fees apply for international students. Higher education follows the **Bologna Process**, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, with universities and university colleges as primary providers. Private schools (friskolor) are subsidized but not controlled by the state, offering alternative curricula. The system has faced criticism over **grading fairness, standardized testing, and digital learning challenges**, though it remains a model for **equitable access to education**.
