# Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system is structured into a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola), which covers primary and lower secondary education. Students begin at age 6 and attend school until age 16, with a strong emphasis on equality, inclusion, and student well-being. The curriculum includes core subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, science, social studies, and physical education, with students also having the option to study a foreign language. 

Preschool education is available to all children from age 1, with free access for children of working parents. From age 3, children receive at least three hours of daily preschool education, which is free or subsidized based on family income. 

In upper secondary education (gymnasieskola), students follow one of 18 national programs, including preparatory and vocational tracks. The system emphasizes individual choice, with students selecting their own subjects and electives. A key component of the program is the diploma project, a project-based learning experience that allows students to plan, execute, and reflect on a larger project. 

The grading system uses letter grades (A–E) with F indicating failure. Students receive an Individual Development Plan (IUP) before grade 6, which guides their academic progress. The total score for university admission is calculated by summing subject grades, with a maximum possible score of 340. 

Higher education in Sweden is organized under the Bologna Process and includes bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees. Public universities and university colleges offer a wide range of academic and professional programs. The system is highly accessible, with free higher education for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. 

Sweden has a strong focus on equity, inclusion, and student well-being. Independent schools (friskolor), funded by municipalities, offer alternative pedagogies and are open to students of all backgrounds. However, concerns have been raised about potential disparities in educational standards and the impact of profit-driven models on the quality of education. 

The country consistently ranks highly in international assessments, with strong performance in reading, mathematics, and science, and is recognized for its high-quality, inclusive, and student-centered education system.
