Sweden’s education system is structured around a **nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola)**, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**, and higher education. Compulsory education begins at age six and covers core subjects like Swedish, mathematics, English, and science, with a focus on foundational skills and social development. Students receive grades starting from year six, using a system of A–E, with F being a failure.

The **comprehensive school** includes three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (grades 4–6), and upper (grades 7–9). Students can choose a non-compulsory foreign language course in grade six. National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess student progress, though these are not used for grading until year six. The curriculum emphasizes critical thinking, creativity, and practical skills, with a strong emphasis on equality and inclusivity.

After compulsory education, students enter **upper secondary school (gymnasieskola)**, which is elective but highly enrolled (99%). The system offers **18 national programs**, divided into preparatory (e.g., social sciences, business) and vocational tracks (e.g., technology, healthcare). Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, mathematics) to qualify for admission. The school year is divided into two semesters, with mandatory attendance until age 18, though students can pursue further education, vocational training, or employment.

Higher education in Sweden is structured into **three cycles** (bachelor’s, master’s, doctoral), aligned with the **Bologna Process**. Public universities and university colleges offer free education for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens, with tuition fees capped at €9,535 annually for non-EU students. The system emphasizes research, innovation, and practical skills, with a strong focus on accessibility and equity.
