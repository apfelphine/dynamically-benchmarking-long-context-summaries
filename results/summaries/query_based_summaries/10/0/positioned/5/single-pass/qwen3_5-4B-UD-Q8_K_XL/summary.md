Sweden's education system is characterized by a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*) where attendance is mandatory between ages 6 and 16. This is supported by a free preschool system available from age one, with full-day free education starting at age three. Higher education is integrated into the Bologna Process, offering three cycles: bachelor's (1–2 years), master's (1–2 years), and doctoral levels. The system is highly centralized but has seen significant debate regarding recent reforms, particularly concerning independent schools (*friskolor*), which received public funding in 1992 and have sparked concerns over grading inflation and equity. International assessments, such as PISA, have shown Sweden's scores declining from their peak in the 2000s, prompting political discussions on curriculum changes.
