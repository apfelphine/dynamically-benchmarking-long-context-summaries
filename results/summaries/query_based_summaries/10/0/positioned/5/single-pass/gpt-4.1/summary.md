## Education in Sweden

Sweden's educational system is structured as a progression from preschool (förskola, ages 1–6), to a mandatory nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola, ages 6/7–15/16), followed by elective upper secondary education (gymnasieskola, ages 16–19) and tertiary education at universities and university colleges. Preschools prioritize pedagogical activities and equal opportunity, with free access for three hours daily from age three; family income determines additional fees. Grundskola consists of three stages—lower, middle, and upper—where students study 16 compulsory subjects and can add a foreign language from grade 6. Grades from A (highest) to F (fail) are introduced in year 6, and national tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9. Students need passing grades in mathematics, Swedish, and English, plus several additional subjects, to qualify for upper secondary education. Upper secondary is course-based with both vocational and preparatory national programmes; approximately one-third of students pursue vocational tracks. All programmes require a diploma project for graduation.

Higher education is organized in cycles following the Bologna Process: undergraduate degrees (Bachelor's), graduate degrees (Master's), and doctoral degrees (PhD). Admission requires language proficiency in Swedish and English, and applicants may take the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) for university entry. Student financial aid is available via the National Board of Student Aid (CSN). Sweden also offers higher vocational education, which is distinct from the university system and often includes workplace experience. Independent "friskolor" (free schools) receive public funding and enroll about 15% of primary and 30% of upper secondary pupils; friskolor have faced scrutiny over profit-making and grade inflation. Education standards are monitored by national assessments, and Sweden participates in international student comparisons such as PISA and PIRLS. Recent reforms responded to concerns about grading integrity and educational equity, particularly as rising immigration has influenced assessment outcomes.
