# Education in Sweden: Summary

Education in Sweden is structured into distinct stages, starting with preschool (ages 1–6), followed by a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola) covering ages approximately 7 to 16. The comprehensive school includes three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (4–6), and upper (7–9), with students taking 16 compulsory subjects and the option to study a non-compulsory foreign language from grade 6 onward. Grading begins in grade 6, using a system from A (highest) to F (fail), and is used in applying for upper secondary school (gymnasieskola). National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment standards.

Upper secondary education (ages 16–19) is elective with a 99% enrollment rate, divided into preparatory and vocational national programmes, all qualifying for university admission. Students complete a diploma project and must meet eligibility criteria based on grades, particularly in core subjects. Admission is competitive and may include entrance exams in some programmes. Higher education follows the Bologna system, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees at universities and university colleges, with various professional degrees also available. Admission typically requires completion of gymnasieskola or equivalent qualifications, with language proficiency in Swedish and English required for all applicants.

Since the 1990s, independent (free) schools have been funded publicly on a per-student basis, allowing alternative pedagogies and profiles, including religious and international schools. About 10% of pupils attend such schools, although there has been debate about profitability and quality standards. Sweden’s education system has shown fluctuating performance in international assessments like PISA and PIRLS, with immigrant influx and testing issues cited as contributing factors. Overall, the Swedish educational framework emphasizes accessibility, comprehensive schooling, broad curricular offerings, and ongoing reforms in grading and assessment.
