# Summary: Education in Sweden

Sweden’s education system is structured around comprehensive compulsory schooling called *grundskola*, lasting nine years typically from ages 6–7 to 15–16, followed by elective three-year upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*). Preschool education (*förskola*) is available from ages 1–6 and preschool class (*förskoleklass*) is for 6–7-year-olds, preparing them for compulsory school. The curriculum at compulsory levels includes 16 subjects with a strong emphasis on Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, and social studies. Students start receiving official grades from year 6, with a grading scale from A to F, and national tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment. Upper secondary education offers 18 national programmes divided into preparatory (academic) and vocational tracks, both qualifying students for higher education. Admission is competitive and based on grades from compulsory school.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process, organized into three cycles: bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (1–2 years), and doctoral studies (up to 5 years). Degrees include professional titles as well. Admission requires completion of upper secondary school or equivalent qualifications and proficiency in Swedish and English. The Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) is also used for university admission. Education is mostly public and free for Swedish and EU/EEA citizens, with student aid available for living costs. Sweden has a significant independent (free) school sector publicly funded but privately run, offering alternative pedagogies under government regulation. Sweden ranks well internationally in early childhood education and strives to maintain equity and quality despite challenges, including recent concerns about grading standards and immigrant student integration.
