Sweden’s education system is structured around a **nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola)**, followed by **three years of upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)** and higher education options. Key features include:

- **Comprehensive School (Grundskola, ages 6–16):**
  - **Compulsory subjects** include Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and core sciences (physics, chemistry, biology). Students also study religion, geography, and home economics.
  - **Grading system**: Uses letters (A–E) with numerical values (A=20, E=10), with a maximum score of 340 based on 16–17 subjects.
  - **National tests** in grades 3, 6, and 9 assess student progress, though results are not used for grading.
  - **Curriculum**: Emphasizes foundational skills, with a focus on critical thinking and practical application.

- **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola, ages 16–19):**
  - **Three-year programs** divided into **preparatory** (general studies) and **vocational** tracks, with 18 national programs available.
  - **Core subjects**: English, Swedish, mathematics, religion, and physical education, supplemented by specialized electives.
  - **Diploma Project**: A mandatory project for all students, graded as either **E (pass)** or **F (fail)**.
  - **Eligibility**: Students must pass 8 core subjects (or 12 for preparatory programs) to qualify, including Swedish, English, and mathematics.

- **Higher Education:**
  - **Bachelor’s (180 credits)**, **Master’s (120 credits)**, and **Doctoral (240 credits)** degrees follow the **Bologna Process** framework.
  - **Tuition**: Free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss students; international students pay fees (€15,000–€30,000/year).
  - **Admission**: Requires completion of upper secondary education, with some programs requiring additional entrance exams.

- **Key Features:**
  - **Free education** for all students in public schools and universities.
  - **Decentralized governance**: Local municipalities fund schools, with oversight by the Ministry of Education.
  - **Focus on equity and accessibility**: Efforts to reduce disparities in education, including support for students with special needs and those from disadvantaged backgrounds.
  - **International recognition**: Sweden’s education system is known for its emphasis on **critical thinking, practical skills, and inclusive policies**, though challenges remain in addressing inequality and improving digital literacy.
