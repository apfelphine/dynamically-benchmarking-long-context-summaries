# Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is structured across several levels, from preschool to postgraduate studies.

**Key Educational Stages:**
*   **Preschool Education:** Offered to all children from age one, with free attendance for at least three hours daily starting from age three. This is intended to support parental employment and promote fundamental values through activities like play.
*   **Compulsory School (Grundskola):** This is a nine-year comprehensive school, mandatory for ages six to sixteen. The curriculum requires 16 compulsory subjects across three stages (lower, middle, and upper). Core subjects include Swedish, mathematics, English, and sciences. Students receive developmental assessments (IUP) before grade six, and formal grading begins in year six, using letters A, B, C, D, and E (with F as failing).
*   **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola):** This elective, three-year system offers 18 national programs, categorized as preparatory or vocational. All students must complete a diploma project. Eligibility depends on passing required subjects, including mathematics, Swedish, and English.
*   **Higher Education:** Tertiary education is provided by public universities and university colleges. Swedish degrees align with the Bologna Process, organized into three cycles: Bachelor's (1st cycle), Master's (2nd cycle), and Doctoral (3rd cycle).

**System Features and Governance:**
*   **Independent Schools (Friskolor):** Private schools receive public funding based on enrollment, but they cannot discriminate or charge extra fees. They are allowed to offer alternative pedagogies, though debates persist regarding their profit-making models and potential impact on educational standards.
*   **Assessment:** National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equivalent assessment across the Grundskola.
*   **Student Support:** Students receive financial aid through the National Board of Student Aid (CSN).
*   **Quality Concerns:** While performance in international tests showed variability, concerns have been raised regarding declining standards in some areas and the integrity of national testing due to issues like unauthorized test dissemination.
