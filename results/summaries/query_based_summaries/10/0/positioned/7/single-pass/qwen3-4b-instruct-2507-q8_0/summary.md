Education in Sweden is structured into a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola) followed by a three-year upper secondary school (gymnasieskola). The curriculum in primary and secondary education includes core subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and social sciences, with additional language courses and electives. Students in upper secondary education take a diploma project and are assessed through a total score based on subject grades. The grading system uses A–E, with E being the minimum passing grade. 

The education system emphasizes active learning, national assessments, and student development through individual development plans (IUP). Preschool education is available to all children from age 1, with free access for those from low-income families. A significant feature of the system is the presence of independent schools (friskolor), which are publicly funded and offer diverse educational approaches. 

Higher education in Sweden is organized under the Bologna Process and includes bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees. The system is characterized by a strong emphasis on research and quality assurance, with institutions required to meet European standards. The country has a high level of educational equity and access, with a focus on inclusive practices and teacher development. The system has faced challenges such as student absenteeism and teacher shortages, but ongoing reforms aim to improve quality and outcomes.
