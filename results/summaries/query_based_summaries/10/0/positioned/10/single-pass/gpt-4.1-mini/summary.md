## Education in Sweden

Sweden’s education system is structured around a nine-year comprehensive compulsory school (grundskola) serving children aged 6–16, divided into lower, middle, and upper stages. Pupils study 16 compulsory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, social studies, religion, and physical education. From grade 6 onward, students receive formal grades (A–F, with F failing) and national standardized tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment. Preschool education is available and partially subsidized for children aged 1–6 to prepare them for school. 

Upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) typically lasts three years and offers 18 national programmes divided into preparatory tracks for university and vocational tracks leading to careers or further education. Admission depends on grades from compulsory school, with some programmes requiring specific subjects. A diploma project is required to pass upper secondary education. Sweden’s higher education aligns with the Bologna Process, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, with universities and university colleges under the Ministry of Education. Entry often requires passing the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) or relevant grades. Higher vocational education exists outside the traditional university system. The country also has independent (free) schools funded by public money but run privately, including some with alternative pedagogies; as of 2008, about 10% of pupils attend such schools. Swedish universities use various grading systems, and students must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English. Student financial aid, including loans and grants, is available primarily for Swedish and EU/EEA citizens, and higher education is generally free for these groups. Sweden has focused on anti-discrimination policies and quality assurance in education.
