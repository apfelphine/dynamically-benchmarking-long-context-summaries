Sweden’s education system is structured into three main phases: **preschool, compulsory school (grundskola), and upper secondary school (gymnasieskola)**, followed by higher education.

### **Preschool (Förskola)**
- Free for children aged 1–6, with at least three hours of daily education starting from age 3.
- Focuses on play-based learning, social development, and foundational skills in language, math, and creativity.

### **Compulsory School (Grundskola, Ages 6–15)**
- **Nine years of mandatory education** (ages 6–15), divided into three stages:
  - **Lower stage (Lågstadiet, Grades 1–3):** Basic literacy, numeracy, and social skills.
  - **Middle stage (Mellanstadiet, Grades 4–6):** Expanded curriculum in core subjects (Swedish, math, English, science, history, etc.).
  - **Upper stage (Högstadiet, Grades 7–9):** Advanced core subjects, with an optional foreign language course (e.g., Spanish, French, German).
- **Grading System:** Uses letters (A–E) and a numeric scale (E=10, D=12.5, C=15, B=17.5, A=20), with a total score calculated from 16–17 subjects.
- **National Tests:** Administered in Grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess student progress and ensure equity.
- **Free and compulsory** for all students, with no tuition fees.

### **Upper Secondary School (Gymnasieskola, Ages 16–19)**
- **Three-year elective programs** (99% enrollment rate), divided into **preparatory (general) and vocational tracks**.
- **Preparatory Programs (e.g., Social Sciences, Business, Natural Sciences):** Prepare students for university admission.
- **Vocational Programs (e.g., Engineering, Hospitality, Technology):** Focus on practical skills and industry readiness.
- **Diploma Project:** A mandatory research-based project for all students.
- **Eligibility:** Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, math) and a minimum number of credits (8 for vocational, 12 for preparatory programs).
- **Admission:** Based on elementary school grades, with some programs requiring entrance exams.

### **Higher Education**
- **Bachelor’s (Kandidatexamen, 180 credits):** 3 years (public universities) or 2–3 years (university colleges).
- **Master’s (Magisterexamen, 120 credits):** 1–2 years (public universities) or 1 year (university colleges).
- **Doctoral (PhD, Doktorsexamen, 240 credits):** 4 years (public universities).
- **Free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens** (since 2011), with low fees for others.
- **Grading:** Uses a mix of letter grades (A–F) and numeric scales (e.g., 1–6), with ECTS compatibility for international recognition.

### **Key Features**
- **Equality:** Free education, no tuition fees for compulsory and upper secondary levels.
- **Flexibility:** Students can choose between academic and vocational paths.
- **Quality Assurance:** Regular national tests and inspections to maintain standards.
- **Inclusivity:** Special education for students with disabilities, including integrated classrooms.

Sweden’s education system emphasizes **equality, practical skills, and academic rigor**, with a strong emphasis on lifelong learning and social mobility.
