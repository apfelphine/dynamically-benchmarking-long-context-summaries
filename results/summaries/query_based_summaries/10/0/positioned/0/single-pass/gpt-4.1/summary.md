## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is organized as a comprehensive system beginning with optional preschool (Förskola) for children aged 1–6, followed by a mandatory nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola) for ages 6–16. Grundskola covers three stages: lower, middle, and upper, with all students studying 16 compulsory subjects and receiving grades from year 6. Upon completion, students proceed to upper secondary education (gymnasieskola), usually lasting three years and comprising 18 national programs that are categorized as either vocational or preparatory, with all programs providing eligibility for university entrance. Admission to upper secondary programs is merit-based, and students who fail to qualify can attend introductory programs.

Higher education in Sweden is structured according to the Bologna Process, with bachelor's (three years), master's (one or two years), and doctoral (two to four years) cycles offered by universities and university colleges. Entry to these programs is based on completion of the previous cycle and, often, proficiency in Swedish and English. University grades use various systems but are convertible to ECTS. Sweden also provides publicly funded independent schools (friskolor), which are tuition-free and follow national curriculum guidelines, and maintains strict anti-discrimination standards and student support via financial aid. Sweden’s education system encourages inclusiveness and equity, though recent concerns over standardization, grading, and performance in international assessments have led to ongoing reforms.
