# Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system comprises preschool (ages 1–6), a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola) for ages 6–16, followed by voluntary upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) lasting usually three years, and higher education offered by universities and university colleges. Preschool education is widely accessible and partially free, aiming to prepare children for comprehensive school with pedagogical activities emphasizing equality. The compulsory school curriculum includes 16 subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, and English, with formal grading starting from year 6. National tests are administered at grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure standardized assessment. Students select from vocational or preparatory programs at the upper secondary level, with 99% enrollment; admission is based on comprehensive school results and possible entrance exams for specific programs. Completion of secondary school allows entry to higher education institutions offering bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees aligned with the Bologna Process.

Higher education degrees in Sweden follow a three-cycle system (bachelor, master, doctoral), and education is publicly funded with student aid available. Independent schools ("friskolor") receive public funding but must not charge tuition or discriminate; they have diversified Sweden's educational landscape but have also sparked debates regarding profit-making and educational standards. Sweden's educational outcomes as measured by PISA and PIRLS have shown fluctuations, with challenges linked in part to immigration and testing irregularities. Guidelines address increasing concerns of violence in schools, and various special education options exist to accommodate indigenous Sámi students and students with disabilities.
