Education in Sweden is characterized by a centralized, nine-year comprehensive school system (*grundskola*) that is free and compulsory for all children from age six to sixteen. The curriculum is highly standardized, requiring students to study sixteen core subjects including Swedish, mathematics, English, and science across three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (grades 4–6), and upper (grades 7–9). Students are assessed using a grading scale from A to F, with grades introduced formally at year 6. Admission to the elective upper secondary level (*gymnasieskola*), which typically lasts three years, is highly competitive; eligibility depends on passing specific core subjects in compulsory school, though an introductory program is available for those who do not qualify. This system emphasizes equality, with municipalities funding schools based on student numbers, and includes provisions for special needs education and adult education (*komvux*).

Higher education in Sweden is structured according to the Bologna Process, offering three cycles: bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees. While public universities and university colleges generally charge tuition fees for non-EU/EEA/Swiss students, the system is accessible with a focus on regional recruitment. Admission requires demonstrating proficiency in Swedish and English, often via the SweSAT aptitude test. The system also features a robust sector of independent schools (*friskolor*), which receive public funding per student and offer diverse pedagogical approaches, alongside a separate higher vocational education system that provides professional degrees but remains non-Bologna compatible. Recent challenges include declining PISA scores attributed to immigration and ongoing debates regarding the quality and standards within the independent school sector.
