Sweden’s education system is structured around a **9-year compulsory compulsory school (grundskola)**, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)** and higher education. Key features include:

### **Compulsory School (Grundskola)**
- **Duration:** Ages 6–15 (9 years), divided into three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (grades 4–6), and upper (grades 7–9).
- **Curriculum:** Mandatory subjects include Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and core sciences (physics, chemistry, biology). Students also choose a foreign language (Spanish, French, or German) in grade 6.
- **Grading:** Uses grades A–E (A = highest), with F as failing. Students start receiving grades in grade 6, with national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess progress.
- **Focus:** Emphasizes foundational skills, critical thinking, and social development, with a strong emphasis on equality and inclusive education.

### **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola)**
- **Duration:** Ages 16–19 (3 years), elective but highly enrolled (99% participation rate).
- **Programmes:** Divided into **preparatory** (e.g., social sciences, business, arts) and **vocational** (e.g., engineering, healthcare, hospitality) tracks. Students take core courses in English, Swedish, mathematics, and natural sciences, plus specialized electives.
- **Diploma Project:** All students complete a project to demonstrate their skills.
- **Eligibility:** Students must pass 8 core subjects (including Swedish, English, and mathematics) for vocational tracks or 12 for preparatory tracks. Those who fail can attend an **introductory programme** to gain eligibility.

### **Higher Education**
- **Universities & University Colleges:** Offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, aligned with the **Bologna Process** (3-cycle system: bachelor’s, master’s, doctoral).
- **Grades:** Universities use varied grading systems (e.g., A–F, VG/G/U), with ECTS for international comparability.
- **Admission:** Requires completion of upper secondary education or equivalent qualifications. International students must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English.
- **Student Aid:** Swedish students receive financial support (loans/grants) from the **National Board of Student Aid (CSN)**.

### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Publicly funded, with minimal fees for higher education (free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens).
- **Inclusivity:** Strong focus on equity, with provisions for students with disabilities and special needs.
- **Reform-Driven:** Recent reforms (e.g., 2011) introduced standardized grading, national tests, and a more structured curriculum.
- **International Recognition:** Sweden consistently ranks highly in global education assessments (e.g., PISA), though challenges include immigrant integration and teacher quality.

Sweden’s system balances **equality, practical skills, and academic rigor**, with a strong emphasis on lifelong learning and social mobility.
