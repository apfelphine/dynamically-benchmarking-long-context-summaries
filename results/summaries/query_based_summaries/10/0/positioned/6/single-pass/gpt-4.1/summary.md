## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is structured as a comprehensive system that begins with preschool (Förskola) for children aged 1–6, followed by a preschool class (Förskoleklass) at age 6–7, and nine years of compulsory schooling (Grundskola) for children aged 7–16. The compulsory curriculum includes 16 subjects: Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, handicrafts, music, visual arts, technology, physics, chemistry, biology, history, social studies, religion, geography, and home economics. In grade 6, students may elect to study a third language. Grading starts at year 6 and uses a letter scale from A to F, with grades converted to a numerical score for admission to upper secondary school (Gymnasieskola).

Upper secondary education (Gymnasieskola), attended by nearly all 16–19-year-olds, offers both vocational and preparatory national programs, each typically lasting three years. Entry into a program is competitive, based on compulsory school grades. All programs include core courses, orientation-specific courses, and an obligatory diploma project. Students who do not meet eligibility requirements may enter introductory programs for further preparation. About one-third of students pursue vocational programs.

Higher education is offered through public universities and university colleges, with degrees following the European Bologna Process framework: bachelor's level (kandidatexamen, 3 years), master's level (magisterexamen, 1 year and masterexamen, 2 years), and doctoral level (PhD, 4 years; licentiatexamen, 2 years). Admission requires proficiency in Swedish and English. Grading varies between institutions but is criteria-referenced. Tuition is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, and students are eligible for loans and grants. Independent (friskolor) schools, introduced in 1992, receive public funding but may be privately run; as of 2023, they enroll a significant share of students and are subject to ongoing review regarding their impact on educational standards and equality. Sweden has performed near or above the OECD average in international assessments but has experienced fluctuation in results, partly attributed to changes in demographics and educational policy.
