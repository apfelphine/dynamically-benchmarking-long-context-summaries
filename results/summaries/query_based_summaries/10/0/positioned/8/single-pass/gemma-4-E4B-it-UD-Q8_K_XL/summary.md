**Education in Sweden**

The Swedish education system is structured across several stages, beginning with preschool education (Förskoleklass) for children aged 6–7, and compulsory schooling, which is a nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola) mandatory between the ages of 6–7 and 15–16. This compulsory phase is divided into three stages: Lower stage (grades 1–3), Middle stage (grades 4–6), and Upper stage (grades 7–9). Students in compulsory school take 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, and others, and are assessed via national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equivalent assessment.

Upper secondary education, known as gymnasieskola, is elective and typically lasts three years, offering 18 national programs divided into preparatory and vocational streams. Students must complete a diploma project in all programs. In higher education, the system is aligned with the Bologna Process, featuring Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral levels. Swedish students receive aid from the National Board of Student Aid (CSN), and while higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, the system includes a system of independent schools (friskolor) that receive public funding.
