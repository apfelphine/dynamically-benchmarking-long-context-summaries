Sweden’s education system is structured around a **9-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola)**, followed by **3-year upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**. Key features include:

- **Comprehensive School (Grundskola, ages 6–16):** Mandatory, free, and includes core subjects like Swedish, mathematics, English, and physical education. Students receive grades (A–E) starting from grade 6, with standardized national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9. The curriculum emphasizes foundational skills, with an optional foreign language course in grade 6.

- **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola, ages 16–19):** Elective but highly enrolled (99%), divided into **preparatory** (e.g., social sciences, business) and **vocational** programs (e.g., technology, healthcare). Students take 2500+ credits of courses, including core subjects like English, Swedish, and mathematics. A **diploma project** is required for graduation, though it does not factor into merit calculations for further education.

- **Higher Education:** Adheres to the **Bologna Process**, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Public universities are tuition-free for EU/EEA/Swiss students, while international students pay modest fees. Admission is based on grades, standardized tests (e.g., SweSAT), and language proficiency (Swedish/English).

- **Independent Schools (Friskolor):** Privately run but publicly funded, offering alternatives to public schools. They face scrutiny over educational standards and profit motives, with debates on their impact on equity and quality.

Sweden’s education system is known for its **equitable access, emphasis on critical thinking, and strong focus on early childhood education**, though challenges include high student absenteeism among foreign-origin students and debates over the effectiveness of independent schools.
