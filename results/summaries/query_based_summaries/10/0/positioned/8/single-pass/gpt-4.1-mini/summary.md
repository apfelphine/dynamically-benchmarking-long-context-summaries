**Education in Sweden**

Sweden’s education system is built around a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola) starting at age 6–7 and ending at 15–16, followed by an elective three-year upper secondary school (gymnasieskola) with preparatory and vocational national programmes. Preschool is available from age one, with free access from age three for at least three hours daily. The compulsory comprehensive school covers 16 subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, languages, sciences, and social studies, with students receiving grades from year 6 onward. National standardized tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment. Upper secondary education offers 18 national programmes, varying from social sciences to vocational training, with strict eligibility criteria for progression based on subject passes. Introductory programmes support students not meeting these criteria.

Higher education follows the Bologna Process with bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral cycles, alongside professional degrees and separate higher vocational education. Admission requires completion of gymnasieskola, and programs are offered by universities and university colleges; private higher education is limited. Swedish students receive financial support through government aid (CSN), and language proficiency in Swedish and English is required for admission. Since the 1990s, independent “free schools” (friskolor) have been publicly funded alternatives to municipal schools but have sparked debates over profit motives and educational equity. Sweden’s international assessments (PISA and PIRLS) showed a decline from 2006 to 2012 with a later partial recovery; immigrant student diversity is a cited factor affecting results. Efforts continue to improve equality, quality, and the handling of school violence and discrimination.
