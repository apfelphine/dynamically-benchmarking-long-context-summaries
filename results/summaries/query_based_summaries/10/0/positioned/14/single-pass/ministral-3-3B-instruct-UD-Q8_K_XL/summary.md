Sweden’s education system is structured around a nine-year compulsory **grundskola** (foundation school) followed by three years of **gymnasieskola** (upper secondary school), making it a 12-year compulsory education framework. The system is free for all students, with the primary goal of providing a balanced education that fosters both academic and practical skills.

### **Compulsory Education (Grundskola)**
- **Duration:** Ages 6–16 (grades 1–9)
- **Structure:** Divided into three stages:
  - **Lower stage (Lågstadiet):** Grades 1–3 (ages 6–9)
  - **Middle stage (Mellanstadiet):** Grades 4–6 (ages 10–13)
  - **Upper stage (Högstadiet):** Grades 7–9 (ages 14–16)
- **Core Subjects:** Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, handicrafts, music, visual arts, technology, and social studies (including history, geography, and religion).
- **Grading System:** Students receive grades from **A (best)** to **E (passing)**, with **F (fail)** reserved for those who do not meet basic requirements. Grades are calculated numerically (A=20, B=17.5, C=15, D=12.5, E=10) to determine eligibility for upper secondary education.
- **National Tests:** Administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess student progress and ensure equitable assessment standards.

### **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola)**
- **Duration:** Ages 16–19 (grades 10–12)
- **Structure:** Three-year program divided into 18 national programmes, categorized into **preparatory** and **vocational** tracks. Students must pass 8 or 12 core subjects (depending on the track) to qualify.
- **Programmes:** Examples include Social Science, Business Management, Natural Science, Technology, Arts, and Vocational tracks like Electricity, Vehicle Maintenance, and Hospitality.
- **Diploma Project:** A mandatory project where students plan, execute, and reflect on a larger-scale task related to their studies.
- **Admission:** Based on elementary school grades, with some programmes requiring additional entrance exams.

### **Higher Education**
- **Types:** Universities and university colleges offer degrees aligned with the **Bologna Process** (Bachelor’s, Master’s, and Doctoral levels).
- **Degrees:**
  - **Bachelor’s (Kandidatexamen):** 180 credits (3 years).
  - **Master’s (Magisterexamen/Masterexamen):** 60–120 credits (1–2 years).
  - **Doctoral (PhD, Doktorsexamen):** 240 credits (4 years).
- **Language Requirements:** Applicants must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English.
- **Student Aid:** Financial support (loans and grants) is available for students studying in Sweden or abroad.

### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** All compulsory and upper secondary education is free.
- **Inclusive Approach:** Schools emphasize equality, with provisions for students with special needs.
- **Grading & Assessment:** Standardized grading and national tests ensure consistency.
- **Vocational Focus:** Vocational tracks prepare students for careers in fields like technology, healthcare, and business.

Sweden’s education system balances academic rigor with practical skills, aiming to prepare students for both higher education and the workforce.
