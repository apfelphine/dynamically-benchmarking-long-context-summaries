## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden consists of a well-defined, centrally organized system. Preschool (förskola) is available from age 1 and is largely subsidized, while compulsory education starts with a preschool class (förskoleklass) at age 6 and continues through grundskola (primary/lower secondary school) for nine years (ages 7–16). The curriculum covers 16 compulsory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, science, English, and social studies, with grades (A–F) awarded from year 6 onwards. After grundskola, 99% of students continue to gymnasieskola (upper secondary school), which lasts three years and offers both vocational and preparatory programmes covering a wide range of academic and practical fields. Admission to gymnasieskola is based on grundskola grades; students failing core subjects attend introductory programmes.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process, offering degrees at the bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels at universities (universitet) and university colleges (högskola). Admission requires completion of gymnasieskola or equivalent, and language proficiency in Swedish and English. Grading systems at universities vary but are compatible with the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS). Sweden supports student financial aid via CSN and tuition is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. Additionally, there is a system of publicly funded independent schools (friskolor) that operate alongside municipal schools, as well as adult education (komvux) and special education options. Sweden ranks close to the OECD average on international educational assessments and has active policies addressing educational equality and anti-discrimination within schools and universities.
