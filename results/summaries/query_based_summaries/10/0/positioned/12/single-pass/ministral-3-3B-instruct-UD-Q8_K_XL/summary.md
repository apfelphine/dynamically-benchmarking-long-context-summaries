Sweden’s education system is structured around a **9-year compulsory compulsory school (grundskola)**, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)** and higher education. Key features include:

### **Compulsory School (Grundskola)**
- **Duration:** Ages 6–16 (9 years), divided into three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (grades 4–6), and upper (grades 7–9).
- **Curriculum:** Core subjects include Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and foreign languages (Spanish, French, German). Students also take elective courses, with over 85% studying a third language.
- **Grading:** Students receive grades from **A (best) to E (passing)**, with **F (fail)** reserved for extreme cases. The total score is calculated by summing grades (A=20, B=17.5, C=15, D=12.5, E=10), with a maximum of **340 points** (16 subjects) or **320 points** (15 subjects if no foreign language).
- **National Tests:** Administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to assess student progress and ensure equity.

### **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola)**
- **Duration:** 3 years (ages 16–19), with a **99% enrollment rate**.
- **Programmes:** Divided into **preparatory** (e.g., social sciences, natural sciences) and **vocational** tracks (e.g., engineering, healthcare). Students take **2,500+ credits** of courses, including core subjects (Swedish, English, mathematics, religion, and civics).
- **Eligibility:** Students must pass **8 core subjects** (including Swedish, English, and mathematics) for vocational tracks or **12 subjects** (including geography, social studies, and religion) for preparatory tracks.
- **Diploma Project:** All students complete a project to demonstrate their skills, graded as **E (pass)** or **F (fail)**.
- **Admission:** Based on grades from compulsory school; some programmes require entrance exams.

### **Higher Education**
- **Structure:** Follows the **Bologna Process**, with degrees divided into three cycles:
  - **Bachelor’s (180 credits, 3 years)**
  - **Master’s (120 credits, 1–2 years)**
  - **Doctoral (240 credits, 4 years)**
- **Admission:** Requires completion of gymnasieskola or equivalent. International students must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English.
- **Tuition:** Free for Swedish/EU/EEA/Swiss citizens; international students pay fees (~€10,000–€35,000/year).
- **Vocational Education:** Separate system offering practical training (e.g., IT, hospitality) with apprenticeships.

### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Compulsory school and gymnasieskola are free; higher education is subsidized.
- **Equality:** Schools are non-discriminatory, though some independent (friskola) schools operate under public funding.
- **Reform Focus:** Recent reforms emphasize **active learning, digital integration, and equity**, though challenges remain in addressing student absenteeism and disparities in rural areas.

Sweden’s system balances **equality, practical skills, and academic rigor**, with a strong emphasis on lifelong learning and social mobility.
