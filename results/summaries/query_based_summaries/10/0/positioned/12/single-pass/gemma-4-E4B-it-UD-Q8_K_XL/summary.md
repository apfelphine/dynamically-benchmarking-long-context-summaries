Education in Sweden is structured around a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*), which is mandatory for ages six to sixteen. This system covers three stages: lower stage (*Lågstadiet*, ages 7–10), middle stage (*Mellanstadiet*, ages 11–13), and upper stage (*Högstadiet*, ages 13–16). All students take 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, and others like history and physical education.

The upper secondary education (*Gymnasieskola*) is elective, lasting three years, and is divided into national programs that are either preparatory or vocational. Students must pass eight (for vocational) or twelve (for preparatory) subjects to be eligible for upper secondary education. In higher education, degrees align with the Bologna Process, offering Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral levels. Furthermore, the independent school system (*friskolor*) exists, which is publicly funded and not allowed to discriminate or charge additional fees, though its profit-making model has faced political debate.
