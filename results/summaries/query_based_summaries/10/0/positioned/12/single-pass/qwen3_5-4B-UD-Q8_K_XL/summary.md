# Education in Sweden

The Swedish education system is characterized by a centralized, secular, and free public school model, organized into a nine-year compulsory comprehensive school (*grundskola*) attended by all children aged six to sixteen. This stage includes preschool classes for working parents from age one, offering free education from age three. The curriculum covers sixteen subjects across three stages—lower, middle, and upper—emphasizing equality and inclusivity, though challenges such as the impact of immigration on PISA scores and a decline in reading proficiency have prompted political debate and reform efforts since the mid-2000s.

Post-compulsory education consists of an elective three-year upper secondary school (*gymnasieskola*) and a higher education system aligned with the Bologna Process. The university system offers degrees at the bachelor's, master's, and doctoral levels, with most public institutions providing free tuition for EU/EEA and Swiss citizens, while non-EU students face significant fees. The system also includes independent schools (*friskolor*) that receive public funding per pupil and offer alternative pedagogies, as well as a distinct higher vocational education sector that operates independently of the university framework. Admission to higher education requires demonstrating proficiency in Swedish and English, often assessed through the *Högskoleprovet* (SweSAT).
