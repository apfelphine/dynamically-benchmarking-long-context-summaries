**Education in Sweden** is structured into a comprehensive, nine-year compulsory primary school (grundskola) from ages 6 to 16, followed by a three-year upper secondary school (gymnasieskola) from ages 16 to 19. The primary school curriculum includes 16 compulsory subjects, with a focus on foundational skills in Swedish, mathematics, English, and social sciences. Students receive grades from year 6, with a total score calculated for university admissions. National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9, though their use has been controversial due to unauthorized dissemination. 

Upper secondary education offers 18 national programs, including preparatory and vocational tracks, with students required to complete a diploma project. Admission is based on grades from compulsory school, with some programs requiring entrance exams. Higher education is divided into bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels, with degrees aligned to the Bologna Process. Public universities and university colleges offer general academic and professional degrees, while independent schools (friskolor) provide alternative pedagogies and are funded publicly. 

The system emphasizes equity, with free preschool for children aged 1–6, and a strong focus on inclusion and anti-discrimination policies. Despite high educational standards and strong performance in international assessments, recent concerns have emerged over grading inconsistencies and the rising influence of private, profit-driven schools.
