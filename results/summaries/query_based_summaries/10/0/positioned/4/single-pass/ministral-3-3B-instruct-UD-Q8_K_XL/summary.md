Sweden’s education system is structured around **compulsory education** from ages 6 to 16, followed by **upper secondary education** (gymnasieskola) for three years, and optional **higher education** (universities and university colleges). Key features include:

- **Comprehensive School (Grundskola):** Nine years of mandatory education (ages 6–16), covering core subjects like Swedish, mathematics, English, and science. Grading starts in year 6 (A–E, with A being the highest).
- **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola):** Three-year programs divided into **preparatory** (e.g., social sciences, business) and **vocational** tracks (e.g., technology, healthcare). Enrollment is near-universal (99%).
- **Higher Education:** Free for Swedish/EU/EEA/Swiss citizens, with degrees aligned to the **Bologna Process** (bachelor’s, master’s, doctoral levels). Admission is competitive, often requiring standardized tests like SweSAT.
- **Independent Schools (Friskolor):** Publicly funded private schools, offering diverse curricula (e.g., Montessori, international) and accounting for ~10% of students.
- **Vocational Training:** Integrated into upper secondary education, emphasizing practical skills and apprenticeships.
- **Language Requirements:** Swedish proficiency is mandatory for higher education; English is widely taught in schools.

The system emphasizes **equity**, **quality**, and **practical skills**, with ongoing reforms to address challenges like teacher shortages and digital literacy.
