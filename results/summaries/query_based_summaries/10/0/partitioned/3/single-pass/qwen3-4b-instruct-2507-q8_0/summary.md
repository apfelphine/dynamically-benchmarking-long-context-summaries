**Education in Sweden**  
Sweden's education system is structured into nine years of compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola) from ages 6 to 16, followed by three years of upper secondary school (gymnasieskola).  

**Primary Education (Grundskola):**  
Students take 16 compulsory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, physical education, English, and arts. A non-graded student choice subject (elevens val) is offered. Grading begins in grade 6, with a system of A–E (passing) and F (fail). A total score, calculated from subject grades (E=10, A=20), determines eligibility for upper secondary school. Core subjects (English, Swedish, math) are mandatory; failure in any disqualifies a student from secondary school. National tests in grades 3, 6, and 9 assess core subjects, with results informing grading.  

**Preschool:**  
All children from age 1 are eligible for free, three-hour daily preschool. Fees apply for longer or younger children, based on income. Activities focus on play, singing, and drawing to promote equality and foundational skills.  

**Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola):**  
A three-year elective program with 18 national programs—15 preparatory (leading to university) and 3 vocational. Students must pass 8–12 subjects (depending on program), with core subjects required. A diploma project is mandatory. Admission is competitive, based on grades and school performance.  

**Higher Education:**  
Degrees follow the Bologna Process: bachelor’s (180 ECTS), master’s (120 ECTS), and doctoral (240 ECTS). The system includes university (Universitet) and university college (Högskola) institutions.  

**Grading & Language:**  
Grading is criteria-based, with five levels (A–E). The grading system is converted to ECTS for international comparisons. Students must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English for higher education.  

**Anti-Discrimination & Equity:**  
Universities have anti-discrimination guidelines and work with the equality ombudsman to ensure fair admission, tuition, and exams.  

**Student Aid:**  
The National Board of Student Aid (CSN) provides financial support, including loans and grants, for students in Sweden or abroad. Higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens.  

**Private Schools (Friskolor):**  
Independent schools (friskolor) are publicly funded and offer an alternative to public schools. They are not allowed to discriminate or charge fees, though they may accept private donations. Concerns exist over grading practices and educational standards, especially among elite sports or religious schools.  

**International Assessments:**  
Sweden ranks highly in PISA (reading, math, science), with scores stable since 2006. A 2021 report found flaws in national testing, including widespread unauthorized test leaks.  

**Special Education:**  
Students with disabilities are supported through inclusive education, with specialized schools and services. The system emphasizes integration and accessibility.  

**Policy & Reforms:**  
The system has evolved through reforms, including the introduction of national tests and anti-violence guidelines. Efforts to improve equity and inclusion continue.  

**Summary:**  
Sweden’s education system emphasizes equity, inclusion, and quality. It features a strong public, comprehensive structure with clear pathways to higher education, supported by robust student aid and anti-discrimination policies.
