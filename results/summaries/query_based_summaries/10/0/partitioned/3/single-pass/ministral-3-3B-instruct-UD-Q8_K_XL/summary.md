Sweden’s education system is structured around **comprehensive schooling (grundskola)**, which is mandatory from ages 6–16, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**, which lasts three years and is elective but highly enrolled. The **comprehensive school** spans nine years, with students receiving grades starting from **Grade 6** (ages 11–12), using a system of A–E (passing grades) and F (fail). Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, mathematics) to qualify for upper secondary education.

**Upper secondary education** includes **18 national programmes**, divided into **preparatory** (e.g., Social Science, Business Management, Natural Science) and **vocational** tracks (e.g., Technology, Health Care). Students apply based on their **Grade 9 scores**, with some programs requiring entrance exams. The system emphasizes **individualized learning** and **practical skills**, though it has faced criticism over **inequality in access** and **teacher shortages**, particularly among students with foreign backgrounds.

**Higher education** follows the **Bologna Process**, offering degrees at **bachelor’s (180 credits)**, **master’s (120 credits)**, and **doctoral levels (240 credits)**. Admission to universities requires a **Grade 9 score** (or equivalent) and, for some programs, a **psychometric test**. Tuition is **free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens**, while international students pay fees. The system is **highly centralized**, with universities and university colleges (högskolor) offering diverse programs, including **professional degrees** in fields like engineering, medicine, and business.

**Education funding** is provided by the **National Board of Student Aid (CSN)**, offering loans and grants for students. **Anti-discrimination policies** ensure fair admissions and examinations, though challenges remain in **equity and quality**, especially in rural areas and among immigrant students. The system prioritizes **equality, research, and practical skills**, with ongoing reforms to improve **digital learning, teacher training, and vocational education**.
