## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is organized into several stages: preschool, compulsory comprehensive school (grundskola), upper secondary school (gymnasieskola), and higher education. Preschool is available to children from age one, with free daily preschool education offered from age three. Compulsory schooling lasts nine years, beginning at age six or seven and continuing until age fifteen or sixteen, and covers 16 core subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, and more. From grade 6, students receive letter grades (A–F, with A as highest and F as failing), which determine eligibility for upper secondary education. National tests are administered at various grade levels to ensure standardized assessment.

Upper secondary education, while elective, has a near-universal enrollment rate and is divided into 18 national programmes, categorized as either preparatory (e.g., Social Science, Natural Science, International Baccalaureate) or vocational (e.g., Electricity and Energy, Building and Construction). All programmes require completion of a diploma project and 2500 points worth of courses, including core subjects. Admittance is competitive and based on compulsory school grades; students not qualifying may enter introductory programmes to gain eligibility.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process and is divided into three cycles: basic level (bachelor's), advanced level (master's), and doctoral level (PhD or Licentiate). Entry requirements progress with each level, and Swedish universities and university colleges primarily deliver general academic and professional degrees. Grading systems vary among institutions and can be translated into the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) for international comparison. Proficiency in both Swedish and English is required for admission, and student financial aid is provided via the National Board of Student Aid (CSN).
