## Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system begins with preschool available to all children aged one and above, with free access to at least three hours daily from age three. The comprehensive compulsory schooling (grundskola) spans nine years from ages six to sixteen, covering 16 mandatory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, social studies, and arts. Students receive grades starting from year 6, and their overall merit is based on grades from core and elective subjects, which determine eligibility for upper secondary education (gymnasieskola). Approximately 15.6% of students exiting compulsory school in 2018 did not qualify directly for upper secondary education and can enter introductory programs designed to help them meet national program requirements. Upper secondary education lasts three years and offers 18 national programs categorized into preparatory (academic) and vocational tracks, all of which provide eligibility for higher education.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process, divided into three cycles: basic (bachelor’s), advanced (master’s), and doctoral levels. Admission to the bachelor's level requires completion of upper secondary education or equivalent. Degrees include Higher Education Diploma (2 years), Bachelor (3 years), Master’s (1–2 years), Licentiate (2 years), and Doctorate (PhD, 4 years). Programs may be at universities or university colleges, with some institutions offering professional degrees. Sweden uses a variety of grading systems aligned with the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS). The system also includes higher vocational education, which is separate, typically lasting 1–2 years with a mix of theory and workplace experience but is not Bologna-compatible and doesn’t award traditional academic degrees. Student aid is available mainly to Swedish and eligible EU/EEA citizens, with higher education being free for these groups since 2011. All universities are mandated to combat discrimination and ensure fair admissions and assessments.
