Education in Sweden is characterized by a highly centralized, secular, and comprehensive system designed to ensure equality of opportunity and high-quality education for all citizens. The foundation of the system is the nine-year compulsory *grundskola* (primary school), which covers ages 6–16 and mandates attendance. Students follow a unified curriculum with 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, science, and arts, and progress through three stages: lower (grades 1–3), middle (grades 4–6), and upper (grades 7–9). Grading begins in year 6 using a scale from A (best) to F (fail), with specific passing grades (B and D) and a maximum total score of 340 points, which serves as the primary metric for university admission. While grades are mandatory for upper secondary eligibility, students must also pass core subjects of English, Swedish, and mathematics; failure in these subjects necessitates participation in an introductory program to gain qualification.

Beyond compulsory education, the Swedish system offers extensive post-compulsory options. Upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*) is elective, lasting three years, and divided into 18 national programs categorized as either preparatory (academic) or vocational, catering to diverse career and university pathways. Higher education is integrated with the Bologna Process, offering three cycles: bachelor's (180 credits, 3 years), master's (120 credits, 1–2 years), and doctoral degrees (240 credits, 4 years). Access to higher education is competitive, requiring admission based on high school grades, SweSAT (Scholastic Aptitude Test) scores, and proficiency in Swedish or English. The system is heavily funded by the state, providing free tuition for citizens and EU/EEA/Swiss residents, supported by the National Board of Student Aid (CSN) for loans and grants. Additionally, the system includes independent schools (*friskolor*) that receive public funding per student but operate autonomously, and a robust vocational education sector that is distinct from the academic university system.
