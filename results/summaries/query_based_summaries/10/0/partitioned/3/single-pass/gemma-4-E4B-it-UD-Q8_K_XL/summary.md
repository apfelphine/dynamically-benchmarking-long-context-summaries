## Education in Sweden

**Primary and Compulsory Education:**
The Swedish educational system features a nine-year compulsory school, called *grundskola*, which runs from ages six to sixteen. All students take 16 compulsory subjects across three stages: lower, middle, and upper stages. Core subjects include Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and others. Grading in compulsory school begins in sixth grade using letter grades (A–E) with F indicating failure. National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equivalent assessment across all students.

**Upper Secondary and Higher Education:**
Upper secondary education, called *gymnasieskola*, is typically a three-year elective system with a 99% enrollment rate, divided into preparatory and vocational national programmes. All students must complete a diploma project. Students who do not qualify for national programmes can attend an introductory programme. Swedish degrees adhere to the Bologna Process, structured into three cycles: Bachelor's level (*kandidatexamen*), Master's level (*masterexamen*), and Doctoral level (*doktorsexamen*). Higher education is offered by public universities and university colleges, with options for general academic and higher vocational education.

**Other Aspects:**
Preschool education is available to children from age one, and from age three, all children are eligible for at least three hours of free daily care. Students receive financial aid from the National Board of Student Aid (CSN). All universities are required to have guidelines on handling discrimination.
