## Education in Sweden

Sweden’s education system is structured around a compulsory nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola) for children aged 6–16, covering 16 compulsory subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, religion, and social studies. Students begin receiving grades from year 6, with a grading scale from A to F, and national tests are conducted in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure standardized assessment. Preschool education is widely available from age one, with free access from age three for at least three hours a day, aiming to promote equal values and prepare children for school. Upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) is elective, typically lasting three years, with students choosing among 18 national preparatory or vocational programs that qualify them for higher education; eligibility requires passing certain core subjects.

Higher education in Sweden comprises universities and university colleges offering Bologna-compliant degrees at basic (bachelor’s), advanced (master’s), and doctoral levels. Admission requires proficiency in Swedish and English, and in some cases performance on the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT). Grading systems vary among institutions but are criteria-referenced and aligned with ECTS standards. Students may receive financial support through the National Board of Student Aid. Additionally, Sweden offers post-secondary vocational education distinct from traditional higher education, emphasizing workplace experience without awarding academic degrees. Since the 1990s, Sweden has seen an expansion of publicly funded independent (free) schools known as friskolor, which receive municipal funds based on enrollment and have diversified educational approaches, including international and special-needs profiles, though their profit-making model and impact on educational equality remain subjects of political debate.
