## Education in Sweden

**Primary and Compulsory Education:**
The Swedish compulsory school, called *grundskola*, is a nine-year comprehensive school spanning from ages 6–7 to 15–16. Students study 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and social studies. From age 6, students begin receiving grades, using the scale A–F, with B and D serving as filling grades. National tests are administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equivalent assessment across all students. Students who fail core subjects lack qualification for secondary school, though they can attend an introductory program.

**Upper Secondary and Higher Education:**
Upper secondary education, or *gymnasieskola*, typically lasts three years and is divided into 18 national programmes, categorized as preparatory or vocational. All national programmes provide a basic qualification for university attendance. At the higher education level, students can pursue advanced or doctoral degrees. To gain admission, applicants must demonstrate proficiency in Swedish and English, and the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) is used as an admission tool. Financial aid is provided through the National Board of Student Aid (CSN), and higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens.

**Independent Schools:**
A significant segment of the system involves independent schools (*friskolor*), which receive public funding from municipalities based on enrollment. These schools are not permitted to discriminate or charge extra fees, though they can accept private donations and may offer alternative pedagogies. Public opinion regarding the profit-making model of these schools remains divided.
