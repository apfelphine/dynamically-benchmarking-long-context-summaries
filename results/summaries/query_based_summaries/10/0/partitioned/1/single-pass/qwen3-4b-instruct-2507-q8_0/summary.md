**Education in Sweden**  

**Preschool**: Available to children from age 1, with free access for 3 hours daily. Children aged 3+ receive at least 3 hours of free preschool education. Fees apply for over 3 hours or under 3 years, based on family income. The focus is on play, singing, and drawing to promote equality and foundational values.  

**Comprehensive School (Grundskola)**: A nine-year mandatory system from ages 6–16. All students take 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, physical education, English, handicrafts, music, visual arts, technology, physics, chemistry, biology, history, social studies, religion, and geography. From grade 6, students may choose a foreign language, with over 85% of grade 9 students studying a third language. A student’s total score for university applications is calculated using letter grades (E=10, D=12.5, C=15, B=17.5, A=20), with a maximum of 340 points.  

**National Tests**: Administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equitable assessment. Results are used for grading but not for early-year evaluations. Tests are criticized for widespread cheating due to leaked content, leading to the use of substitute tests, which are deemed inconsistent.  

**Violence Guidelines**: In 2014, the National Agency for Education published guidelines for handling armed violence in schools. Physical violence reports increased by 129% (2012–2017), with threats rising 46%.  

**Other Primary Education**: Includes Sámi schools, special needs schools, and komvux (adult education). Homeschooling is permitted for children with severe illness.  

**Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola)**: Elective, with a 99% enrollment rate. Divided into 18 national programs—12 preparatory and 6 vocational. Preparatory programs qualify students for university. Vocational programs lead to professional qualifications. Students must pass 8–12 subjects (including math, Swedish, English) to qualify.  

**Diploma Project**: All students complete a project involving planning, execution, and reflection. Graded as E (pass) or F (fail), it is not included in university merit scores.  

**Admission**: Students apply to specific programs, with entry based on elementary school grades. Some programs (e.g., arts) require entrance exams.  

**Introductory Programmes**: For students who fail to meet national program requirements, helping them gain eligibility.  

**Higher Education**: Follows the Bologna Process. Degrees include bachelor’s (3–4 years), master’s (1–2 years), and PhD (3–5 years). Public universities and university colleges offer general and professional degrees.  

**Grading**: Five systems used (e.g., A–F, VG/G/U, pass/fail). Grades are converted to ECTS for European comparison.  

**Language Requirements**: Applicants must prove proficiency in Swedish and English via standardized tests (e.g., SweSAT, TOEFL).  

**Student Aid**: Free higher education for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. Financial aid available via CSN.  

**Independent Schools (Friskolor)**: Privately run, publicly funded schools offering an alternative to public education. Funded by municipalities based on student numbers. Offer diverse pedagogies (e.g., Montessori, international). Controversial due to claims of inflated grades and rising inequality.  

**PISA and PIRLS**: Sweden’s PISA scores declined between 2006–2012, with a recovery in 2015. PIRLS scores dropped significantly but recovered. Concerns about testing integrity and immigrant student impacts have been raised.  

**Education Policy**: Emphasis on equality, play-based learning, and teacher autonomy. The system is highly structured yet adaptable, with ongoing reforms to address equity and quality.
