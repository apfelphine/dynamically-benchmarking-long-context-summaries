# Summary: Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system includes compulsory comprehensive schooling (grundskola) of nine years for children aged about 6–16, followed by an elective upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) lasting three years. The upper secondary level offers 18 national programmes divided into preparatory (academic) and vocational tracks, each requiring students to complete at least 2500 points worth of courses. Students must pass core subjects such as Swedish, English, and mathematics to proceed. Introductory programmes exist to prepare students who do not initially meet eligibility. A hallmark of upper secondary education is the diploma project, a substantial graded assignment mandatory for all students. Admission to programmes is competitive and often based on grades and sometimes entrance exams.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna Process framework, offering bachelor’s (basic level), master’s (advanced level), and doctoral degrees. Admission requires proficiency in Swedish and English, and the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) is another path to university study. Universities use varying grading systems aligned with European standards. Education at universities is generally free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens. Sweden also has a significant number of independent (free) schools funded by municipalities but operated privately, though concerns have arisen over profit motives and grading standards. Vocational education post-secondary is distinct from university education and focuses on short programmes with workplace experience. Sweden participates actively in anti-discrimination efforts within education, ensuring equal opportunities across institutions.
