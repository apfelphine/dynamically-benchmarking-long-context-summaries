# Education in Sweden

Sweden's education system starts with preschool available from age one, focusing on foundational values through play and pedagogical activities. Compulsory education spans nine years in comprehensive schools (grundskola) from about age six to fifteen or sixteen, covering 16 compulsory subjects including Swedish, mathematics, English, sciences, and social studies. Students receive grades starting from year 6, with national tests administered in grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure consistent assessment. Upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) is elective, typically lasting three years, divided into preparatory and vocational national programmes, and requires passing core subjects for eligibility. All students complete a diploma project as part of their final assessment. Admission to upper secondary programmes is competitive, based principally on grades from compulsory school.

Higher education in Sweden follows the Bologna system with three cycles: bachelor's (first cycle, 2–3 years), master's (advanced level, 1–2 years), and doctoral degrees (third cycle, 4+ years). Admission to higher education requires completion of upper secondary education or equivalent qualifications. Universities employ varying grading scales, with Swedish and English language proficiency mandatory for admission. The Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT) is another pathway for university entry. Independent (free) schools, publicly funded since reforms in the 1990s, coexist with municipal schools and encompass alternative pedagogies and specialized profiles but have faced debate over funding and performance. Overall, Sweden emphasizes equitable access, quality assurance, and a broad curriculum from early childhood through advanced tertiary education.
