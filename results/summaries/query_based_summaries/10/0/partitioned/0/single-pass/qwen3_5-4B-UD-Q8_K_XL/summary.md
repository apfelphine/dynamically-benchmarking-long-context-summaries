Based strictly on the provided text, the Swedish education system is characterized by a free, comprehensive approach ranging from preschool through higher education. The foundation is the nine-year compulsory *grundskola* (ages 6–16), where students attend either a public school or an independent school (*friskola*). While public schools are funded by municipalities, independent schools receive similar public funding per pupil and are allowed to offer alternative pedagogies or international profiles, though they cannot charge extra fees or discriminate. The system emphasizes inclusivity and equality, with preschool available to all children from age one, and upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*) being largely elective with a 99% enrollment rate.

Higher education in Sweden is structured around the Bologna Process, offering three cycles: bachelor's (180 credits), master's (120 credits), and doctoral (240 credits) degrees. Admission generally requires a Swedish degree at the basic level or an equivalent foreign qualification, and all applicants must demonstrate proficiency in both Swedish and English. While higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, international students may face fees. The system includes a distinct higher vocational education track that is not Bologna-compatible, focusing on workplace experience and professional degrees. Despite high enrollment, challenges exist regarding the influx of immigrants affecting international test scores, and the quality of independent schools has faced scrutiny regarding grading standards.
