**Education in Sweden**

Sweden's educational system includes primary education through compulsory schooling, which is a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*) mandatory for ages six to fifteen and sixteen. This compulsory schooling covers sixteen subjects across three stages: lower, middle, and upper stages. Students receive an Individual Development Plan (*IUP*) before Grade 6, and grades begin from that year, using A, B, C, D, and E as passing grades. National tests are administered in Grades 3, 6, and 9 to ensure equivalent assessment across all students.

Upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*) is elective and typically lasts three years, divided into preparatory and vocational national programmes. To enter upper secondary education, students must pass a minimum number of subjects, including Mathematics, Swedish, and English. In higher education, Swedish degrees are integrated into the Bologna Process, offering cycles for Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral levels. Financial aid is provided by the National Board of Student Aid (*CSN*), and while higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, independent schools (*friskolor*) are also funded by local municipalities.
