## Education in Sweden

Education in Sweden is highly structured and largely publicly funded, beginning with preschool offered to children aged one and above, with fees varying by age and family income. Compulsory comprehensive schooling (grundskola) lasts nine years, typically for students aged six or seven through fifteen or sixteen. The curriculum includes 16 compulsory subjects with additional options for foreign languages, and national tests administered at key stages to ensure equitable assessment. Grades are provided from year 6 onward using a scale from A (highest) to F (fail), and students' combined grades determine eligibility and merit for upper secondary school (gymnasieskola).

Upper secondary education (gymnasieskola) is elective, with a 99% enrollment rate, and is divided into 18 national programmes classified as either preparatory or vocational. All students complete at least 2500 points of coursework, including universal core subjects, and must undertake a diploma project. Eligibility for entry depends on passing required subjects, with introductory programmes available for those who do not qualify. After upper secondary, students may pursue higher education at universities or university colleges, which offer degrees at bachelor, master, and doctoral levels aligned with the European Bologna Process. Entrance to higher education may require the Swedish Scholastic Aptitude Test (SweSAT), and students receive financial aid through the National Board of Student Aid (CSN). Independent schools ("friskolor"), publicly funded yet privately operated, offer alternative pedagogies and have contributed to debate over educational quality and equality.
