Sweden’s education system is structured around **comprehensive school (grundskola)**, a nine-year compulsory program from ages six to fifteen, followed by **upper secondary education (gymnasieskola)**, a three-year elective program. The **comprehensive school** includes mandatory subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, English, physical education, and science, with students receiving grades from year six onward. The grading system ranges from **A (highest)** to **F (fail)**, with a numerical conversion used for university applications.

Upper secondary education is divided into **preparatory** and **vocational** tracks, with over 18 national programs offering specialized paths like business, natural sciences, technology, and arts. Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, and mathematics) to qualify for admission. The system emphasizes practical skills and career readiness, with a high enrollment rate (99%) and a focus on vocational training alongside academic studies.

Sweden’s higher education system follows the **Bologna Process**, offering degrees at **bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels**. Public universities provide free education for EU/EEA students, while vocational education focuses on applied skills and industry partnerships. The system is known for its emphasis on **quality, equity, and lifelong learning**, with strong international recognition and a focus on integrating technology and innovation into education.
