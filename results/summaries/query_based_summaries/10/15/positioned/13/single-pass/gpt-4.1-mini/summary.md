## Education in Norway

Education in Norway has a long history, with formal schooling tracing back to cathedral schools established shortly after 1153. Compulsory education began to take shape with mandated reading training in 1736 and the introduction of the folkeskole (people’s school) in 1827, which evolved into the current grunnskole (foundation school) system. The Independent Schools Act of 2003 allowed private schools, which remain few and must comply with government recognition and non-discriminatory admission rules.

Today, the Norwegian education system is divided into three main parts: compulsory elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). The barneskole and ungdomsskole constitute the grunnskole and are mandatory. At lower secondary level, students receive formal grades that influence upper secondary admission. Upper secondary education offers both general and vocational tracks under a merged system reform, including apprenticeships. Higher education includes universities, university colleges, and a small private sector, with admissions often based on upper secondary grades. Norway emphasizes inclusive education, offering special education within mainstream schools based on individual needs. Grading systems vary by educational level, with transitions to the Bologna system at tertiary institutions. The academic year runs from mid-August to June, with breaks for holidays and national observances.
