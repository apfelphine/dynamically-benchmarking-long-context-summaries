## Education in Norway

Education in Norway features a comprehensive, publicly funded system divided into three main levels: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary schooling (grunnskole) are compulsory for all children between 6 and 16 years old, focusing on inclusive teaching that introduces core subjects such as mathematics, English, science, religion, geography, history, and social studies. Grades and entrance exams do not occur until lower secondary school, where performance and preferences begin to influence upper secondary school placements. While private schooling exists under the Independent Schools Act, it remains relatively uncommon and must meet strict government criteria. School years run from mid-August to June, with several breaks throughout the year.

Upper secondary school offers both general and vocational studies, with reforms designed to facilitate switching between tracks and incorporate mandatory IT usage. Completion of upper secondary grants eligibility for higher education, which encompasses universities, university colleges, and private institutions. Most higher education is state-run, tuition-free, and admission is typically based on upper secondary performance. Norway maintains a robust system of quality assurance in higher education. Special education is integrated into local schools, and the grading systems vary but are largely criteria-referenced. Norway also prioritizes inclusive learning environments for students with diverse needs and enacts policies for universal design within universities and colleges.
