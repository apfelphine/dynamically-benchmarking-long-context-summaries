Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory. Primary school begins with play-based learning and progresses to core subjects like mathematics, English, science, and religion. Students are graded from 1 to 6, with 2 being the lowest passing grade; grades are assigned by teachers and may include a final exam for some subjects. 

Lower secondary school introduces formal grading, with students' grades and geographic location determining eligibility for upper secondary school. Students can choose one elective (valgfag) and one language, with options including German, French, and Spanish. Upper secondary education is primarily public, with 93% of students attending public schools. It offers general or vocational study paths, with vocational programs following a "2+2 model" of two years of school training and two years of apprenticeship. 

Educators include preschool teachers (førskolelærer), adjunct teachers (adjunkt), and lecturers (lektor), with qualifications ranging from bachelor's to master's degrees. Higher education in Norway is accessible through universities and university colleges, which offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Admission requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), and students are ranked by grades for competitive programs. 

Special education is integrated into local schools, with support from educational psychologists and social welfare workers. The grading system uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 4 or above considered passing. Examinations at the end of upper secondary school include teacher-assigned grades and optional exams (trekkfag). The academic year runs from August to June, with breaks including summer, winter, and holiday periods. Norway emphasizes inclusive, student-centered education with a strong focus on equity and quality.
