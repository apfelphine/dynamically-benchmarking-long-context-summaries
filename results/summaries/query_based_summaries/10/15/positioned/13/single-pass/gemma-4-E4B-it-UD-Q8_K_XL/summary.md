Education in Norway is structured into three parts: elementary school (ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (ages 16–19). The elementary and lower secondary levels are mandatory for all children aged 6–16, collectively known as *grunnskole*.

In elementary school, students focus on educational games, social structures, basic arithmetic, English, science, religion (covering all religions), aesthetics, and music, with no official grades given at this stage. Lower secondary school introduces formal grading, and students can choose one elective and one language. Upper secondary school (*videregående skole*) is primarily public; the 1994 reform merged general, mercantile, and vocational branches into a single system, allowing students to choose between a general studies (*studieforberedelse*) or vocational studies (*yrkesfag*) path.

Norway has a system of public schools, though private schooling is available under the Independent Schools Act of 2003, requiring government approval and heavy grant-aids, while private schools cannot select students based on subjective criteria. Higher education is mostly state-run and overseen by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT).
