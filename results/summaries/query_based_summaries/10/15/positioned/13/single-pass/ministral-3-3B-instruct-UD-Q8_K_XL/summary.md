Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **primary (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**, with the first two being compulsory. Primary school focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic English, while lower secondary introduces grading and elective subjects like foreign languages. Upper secondary education is divided into general and vocational tracks, with students choosing between academic and career-focused studies. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with special education integrated into mainstream schools, and is funded publicly, ensuring free access to primary and lower secondary education.

Grading in Norway uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. Students must pass core subjects to qualify for upper secondary school, and graduation requires passing all subjects. Higher education is accessible beyond upper secondary school, with institutions like universities and university colleges offering degrees aligned with the Bologna Process. Norway’s education system is known for its emphasis on equality, with minimal tuition fees for higher education and a focus on practical, student-centered learning. Recent reforms have aimed to modernize curricula and improve digital integration, reflecting ongoing efforts to enhance educational quality and accessibility.
