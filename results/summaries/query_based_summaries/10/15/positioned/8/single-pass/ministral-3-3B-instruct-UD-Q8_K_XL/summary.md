Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **primary (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Primary and lower secondary education are compulsory, with the latter requiring students to pass core subjects (Swedish, English, and mathematics) to proceed. Upper secondary education is elective but highly competitive, offering both general and vocational tracks, with students often required to switch schools between these stages.

Norway’s education system emphasizes **free, high-quality public schooling**, with minimal tuition fees for higher education (except for private institutions). The curriculum is standardized, focusing on core subjects like Swedish, English, mathematics, science, and social studies, supplemented by electives such as foreign languages and arts. Grading is based on a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. The system prioritizes **inclusive education**, ensuring all students receive support if needed, though challenges like teacher shortages and rural disparities persist.

Higher education is accessible to those with a general university admissions certificate, obtained through upper secondary studies or alternative pathways. Universities and university colleges (higher education institutions) are state-funded, with a strong emphasis on research and practical training. Private schools exist but are less common, accounting for only about 10% of higher education students. The system is known for its **equality and accessibility**, though debates continue over funding disparities and the impact of globalization on traditional education values.
