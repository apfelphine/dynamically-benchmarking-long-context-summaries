## Education in Norway

Education in Norway consists of three main stages: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory, forming the nine-year "grunnskole," while upper secondary education is non-compulsory but attended by most. Public schools dominate at all stages, and private schools are permitted but highly regulated, focusing on recognized international or alternative curricula. The curriculum in primary school covers social skills, basic subjects like mathematics and English, religion (including multiple faiths), aesthetics, and music. Grading begins in lower secondary school and influences upper secondary school admission, and students take electives and languages such as German, French, or Spanish.

Upper secondary education offers general studies (academic) and vocational studies, with the "2+2 model" prevalent in vocational tracks—two years at school and two years of apprenticeship. Reforms have aimed to allow flexibility between studies and strengthen IT integration. Higher education, provided mainly by public universities and university colleges, requires a general university admissions certificate, typically earned by completing general studies in upper secondary school. Admission to popular programs is grade-competitive. The majority of higher education institutions are state-run, while private schools remain limited. All education is characterized by strong government oversight, equity, and inclusion standards.

Special education is mostly integrated into local schools, where students with learning differences are entitled to tailored support. The grading systems use both numbers and, at higher levels, letters, following the Bologna process. The academic year runs from August to June with two semesters, and features multiple breaks and national holidays. Norway also maintains a robust quality assurance system for higher education (NOKUT) and applies universal design principles to foster inclusion in learning environments.
