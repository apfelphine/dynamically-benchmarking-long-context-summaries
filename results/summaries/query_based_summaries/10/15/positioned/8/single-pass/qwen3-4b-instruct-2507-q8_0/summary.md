Education in Norway is based on a nine-year compulsory system divided into three stages: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). The system emphasizes inclusive, student-centered learning, with no formal grades until upper secondary. Students are assessed through teacher evaluations and examinations, with a focus on holistic development. The curriculum includes core subjects such as Swedish, mathematics, science, and religion, with optional electives and language courses. The Norwegian school year consists of two semesters, with breaks including summer, winter, and national holidays. 

Higher education is accessible through public universities and university colleges, which offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Admission requires a general university entrance certificate (generell studiekompetanse), typically achieved through upper secondary education. The system values quality and equity, with strong oversight by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT). Special education is integrated into mainstream schools, with support provided by educational psychologists and counselors. The grading system uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest, and students receive both teacher-assigned and exam-based assessments. The education system promotes equality, accessibility, and a strong emphasis on student well-being and social inclusion.
