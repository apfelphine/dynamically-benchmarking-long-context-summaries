## Education in Norway

Education in Norway has evolved from ancient organized schooling for priests in the 12th century to a universal, state-supported system. Mandatory schooling began with the folkeskole (primary school) in 1827, which gradually expanded before being replaced by the grunnskole ('foundation school'), comprising elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12) and lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16) levels. These are compulsory for all children aged 6–16; students then progress to upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19), which offers both academic and vocational paths. Vocational programs follow a 2+2 model of school training plus apprenticeship. Private schools exist under strict regulation but are rare compared to public schools.

Higher education chiefly consists of state-run universities and university colleges offering bachelor's, master's, and PhD degrees. Admission requires a general university admissions certificate, primarily earned through academic tracks in upper secondary school, though alternative paths exist for older applicants. Norway also promotes special education within mainstream schools and ensures inclusivity in higher education environments. Grading varies across levels but is standardized for diplomas. The academic year runs from August to June with several breaks, and students are exempt from school on national holidays. The Kunnskapsløftet reform emphasizes IT integration and curriculum improvements.
