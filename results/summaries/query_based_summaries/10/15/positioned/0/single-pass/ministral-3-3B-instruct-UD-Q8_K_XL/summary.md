Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Compulsory education begins at age 6, covering the first two stages, with the latter being divided into **grunnskole** (foundation school). The curriculum emphasizes core subjects like mathematics, English, science, religion, and social studies, with no formal grades until the upper secondary level.

Norway’s education system is highly centralized, with the government overseeing curriculum development, teacher training, and funding. Public schools are free, and private schooling is available but heavily regulated, offering curricula aligned with international standards or alternative pedagogical approaches. The system has seen reforms to improve inclusivity and digital integration, though challenges remain in addressing regional disparities and ensuring equitable access to quality education.

Higher education in Norway is accessible beyond upper secondary school, with public institutions offering degrees from bachelor’s to PhD levels. Admission to universities is based on high school performance, and many students pursue further education abroad due to limited spots. Vocational education is also prominent, with programs like the "2+2 model" combining school training with apprenticeships. The country’s education system is noted for its emphasis on equality, with efforts to reduce class size and improve teacher quality, though issues like teacher shortages and regional inequities persist.
