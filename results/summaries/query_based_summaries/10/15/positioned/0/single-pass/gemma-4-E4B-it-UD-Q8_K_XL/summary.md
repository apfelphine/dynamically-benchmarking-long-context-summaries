## Education in Norway

Norway's education system has evolved significantly since organized schooling began in 2000 B.C. The current structure is divided into three parts: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary school are mandatory for children aged 6–16, forming the "foundation school" (grunnskole).

The elementary school curriculum introduces basic skills, with no official grades given at this level, though teachers provide comments and analysis. Lower secondary school is where students begin being graded, and these grades influence acceptance into upper secondary school. Upper secondary education is primarily public, though private schooling is available under the Independent Schools Act of 2003. The system is reformed, and since the introduction of Kunnskapsløftet in 2006, students can follow either a general studies (studieforberedelse) or a vocational studies (yrkesfag) path, often utilizing a "2+2 model" for apprenticeships. Higher education is managed by state-run universities and university colleges, with quality assured by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT).
