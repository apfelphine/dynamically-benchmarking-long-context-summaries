Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **primary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**, with the first two being compulsory. Primary school focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic subjects like science, history, and social studies, without formal grading until the start of lower secondary school. Lower secondary school introduces grading and allows students to choose elective courses, including languages and practical subjects.

Upper secondary school is divided into general and vocational tracks, with students preparing for further education or careers. The system emphasizes a balanced curriculum, integrating technology and practical skills. Grading is based on a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. Students must pass core subjects to qualify for university admission, which is based on standardized exams and academic performance. The education system is publicly funded, with most students attending public schools, though private schools are available under specific conditions. Higher education in Norway includes universities and university colleges, offering degrees from bachelor’s to doctoral levels, with tuition fees generally low for public institutions. The system is known for its emphasis on equality and accessibility, with a strong focus on inclusive education and continuous teacher training.
