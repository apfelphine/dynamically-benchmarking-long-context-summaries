# Education in Norway

Norway's education system is characterized by a unified, state-funded structure divided into elementary school (*barneskole*, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (*ungdomsskole*, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (*videregående skole*, ages 16–19). The first two stages are compulsory for all children, with mandatory education historically starting at age 7 but shifting to age 6 in 1997. Students typically change schools at the transition to lower secondary and upper secondary levels, as schools often specialize in specific age groups. The *barneskole* focuses on play, social structures, and basic literacy, while *ungdomsskole* introduces grading and allows students to choose electives and languages, which influence their future high school placement.

Upper secondary education offers two main paths: general studies (*studieforberedelse*) for university preparation and vocational studies (*yrkesfag*), which often follow a "2+2 model" combining school training with an apprenticeship in an enterprise. Since 2007, 93% of upper secondary students attend public schools, with private options available but limited in number. The system emphasizes inclusivity and equity, with a focus on universal design and special education integrated into ordinary schools. Higher education is provided by universities and university colleges, with the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensuring standards. The academic year runs from August to June, featuring long summer and Christmas breaks, and the system consistently performs well internationally in student assessments.
