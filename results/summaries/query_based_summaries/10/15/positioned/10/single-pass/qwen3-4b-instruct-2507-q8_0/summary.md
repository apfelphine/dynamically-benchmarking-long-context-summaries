**Education in Norway**  
Norway's education system is structured into three main levels: primary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Primary and lower secondary education are compulsory.  

Primary schools focus on play-based learning, social development, and foundational skills in reading, writing, and mathematics. Students are assessed informally with teachers providing comments and feedback.  

Lower secondary schools introduce formal grading, with grades influencing upper secondary school admissions. Students choose one elective and one language (e.g., German, French, Spanish).  

Upper secondary education is primarily public, with 93% of students attending public schools. It offers general, vocational, or combined pathways. Vocational programs follow a "2+2 model" (two years of school, two years of apprenticeship). Students earn a diploma (russ) upon graduation.  

Higher education includes universities (theoretical subjects) and university colleges (vocational and professional programs). Admission requires a general university qualification (generell studiekompetanse), often achieved through upper secondary studies or a specific exam.  

Teachers are categorized by level: preschool teachers (førskolelærer), adjuncts (adjunkt), and lecturers (lektor). The grading system uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest passing grade.  

Education is highly equitable, with strong emphasis on inclusion and special education. The system is supported by national policies, quality assurance (NOKUT), and a strong focus on student well-being and active learning.  

The academic year runs from mid-August to June, with breaks in October, December, and February. Students celebrate with parties after final exams.  

Norway consistently ranks among the top in international assessments, particularly in reading and mathematics, with strong performance in science and student well-being.
