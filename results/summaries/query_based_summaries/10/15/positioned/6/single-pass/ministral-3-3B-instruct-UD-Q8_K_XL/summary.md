Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. The first two stages are compulsory, with the latter being non-compulsory but highly accessible, accounting for 93% of students in 2007.

### **Structure and Compulsory Education**
- **Elementary School (Barneskole, Grades 1–7, Ages 6–12):**
  - Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic English.
  - No official grades are given, though teachers provide informal feedback.
  - Emphasizes social development, basic religious education, and exposure to arts and music.

- **Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole, Grades 8–10, Ages 13–16):**
  - Introduces grading, with students choosing one elective (e.g., German, French, or additional English) and one language.
  - Grades influence school placement for upper secondary education.
  - Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, mathematics, English) to proceed.

### **Upper Secondary School (Videregående Skole, Grades VG1–VG3, Ages 16–19)**
- **Compulsory Pathways:**
  - **General Studies (Studieforberedelse):** Prepares students for university, covering core subjects and electives.
  - **Vocational Studies (Yrkesfag):** Focuses on practical skills, often including apprenticeships (2+2 model: two years of school training followed by two years of work experience).

- **Curriculum and Grading:**
  - Students take standardized exams (e.g., grade 10 exams) and may sit for random exams (trekkfag) for final diplomas.
  - Grading ranges from 1 (lowest) to 6 (highest), with 4+ being passing.
  - The system encourages flexibility, allowing students to switch between general and vocational tracks.

### **Higher Education**
- **Admission:** Requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), achieved through upper secondary studies or alternative pathways (e.g., work experience).
- **Institutions:** Public universities and university colleges dominate, offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Private schools exist but are less common.
- **Quality Assurance:** The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) oversees higher education standards.

### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Both primary and secondary education are free, with minimal fees for upper secondary school.
- **Equality and Inclusivity:** Norway emphasizes inclusive education, with special education services available for students with disabilities.
- **Emphasis on Practical Skills:** Vocational tracks are well-developed, integrating apprenticeships to prepare students for the workforce.

Norway’s education system is known for its balance between academic rigor and practical preparation, ensuring high standards while fostering inclusivity and adaptability.
