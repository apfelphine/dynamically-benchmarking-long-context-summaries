The education system in **Norway** is structured around a **compulsory, free, and publicly funded** framework, emphasizing equality and access. Education is divided into three main stages:

### **Primary Education (Barneskole, Grades 1–7, Ages 6–12)**
- **Compulsory** for all children aged 6–12.
- Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, math, and basic English, supplemented by subjects like science, history, geography, and arts.
- No formal grading until the end of primary school, though teachers provide informal feedback.
- Students attend five days a week, typically from 7:30 AM to 3:30 PM.

### **Lower Secondary Education (Ungdomsskole, Grades 8–10, Ages 13–16)**
- **Compulsory** for ages 13–16.
- Introduces grading, with students choosing one elective (e.g., German, French, or Spanish) and one language course (e.g., Norwegian or English).
- Students’ grades and regional location determine their acceptance into upper secondary schools.
- Emphasizes academic rigor, with a focus on critical thinking and problem-solving.

### **Upper Secondary Education (Videregående Skole, Grades VG1–VG3, Ages 16–19)**
- **Not compulsory**, but nearly all students attend.
- Divided into **general studies** (preparing for university) and **vocational studies** (preparing for careers).
- **Reform 94** merged these tracks, ensuring students gain a broad education regardless of their chosen path.
- Students can choose between **2+2 model** (two years of school training followed by two years of apprenticeship) or **fully school-based programs**.
- **Graduation** is required to obtain a diploma, with exams determining final grades.
- **Compulsory military service** applies for men aged 19–26, though exemptions exist.

### **Higher Education**
- **Free for Norwegian citizens** (EU/EEA/Swiss students also qualify).
- **General university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse)** is required for entry.
- **Universities** (e.g., University of Oslo, NTNU) and **university colleges** (e.g., Agder University College) offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD programs.
- **Private schools** exist but are rare compared to public institutions.
- **Quality assurance** is overseen by the **Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT)**.

### **Key Features**
- **Free and compulsory** education from ages 6–16.
- **Strong emphasis on equality** and inclusive policies.
- **High academic standards** with rigorous exams.
- **Vocational training** integrated into upper secondary education.
- **Free higher education** for citizens, fostering access to tertiary education.

Norway’s education system is widely regarded for its **equity, quality, and focus on lifelong learning**, reflecting its commitment to social mobility and academic excellence.
