# Summary of Education in Norway

Education in Norway has a long history dating back to 2000 B.C., with organized education evolving significantly through the centuries. The current school system comprises three levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education (grunnskole) is compulsory. In primary school, teaching focuses initially on play and foundational skills without formal grading, while from lower secondary onward, students receive grades which influence upper secondary admission. Upper secondary schooling offers both general studies and vocational paths, with reforms enabling credit transfer between tracks and promoting IT integration.

Educator qualifications vary by teaching level: preschool teachers hold bachelor’s degrees, adjunct teachers (5th–10th grades) require a bachelor’s plus pedagogy training, and lecturers (8th grade and up) hold master’s degrees. Higher education in Norway includes universities and university colleges offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, with admission primarily based on upper secondary grades or age/work experience pathways. Quality assurance is managed by NOKUT. Private schools exist but are few and must follow approved curricula. Special education is mostly delivered in mainstream schools, with support services ensuring inclusive learning. The academic year spans mid-August to June with two semesters and breaks for holidays such as Christmas, Easter, and Constitution Day. Norwegian grading varies by education level, transitioning to the Bologna system at university; lower-level education uses a 1–6 scale, with 2 as the lowest passing grade.
