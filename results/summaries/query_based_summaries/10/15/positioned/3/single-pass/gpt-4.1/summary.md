## Education in Norway

Education in Norway is overseen primarily by the state and is highly regulated, with compulsory schooling for children aged 6 to 16 through the grunnskole system comprising elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12) and lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16). Upper secondary education (videregående skole, ages 16–19) is not mandatory but widely attended, with a graduation rate of 73% as of 2017; 93% of students were enrolled in public schools in 2007. Schools are largely public, though private schools exist and must follow strict criteria for government approval. The curriculum includes core academic subjects and offers vocational and general study paths in secondary school, allowing students flexibility to switch between tracks. The education system emphasizes inclusivity, universal design, and support for special education needs within local schools.

Higher education in Norway includes universities, university colleges, and specialized private schools; the vast majority are state-run and quality is regulated by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT). Admission typically requires a general university admissions certificate, obtainable through upper secondary education or combined schooling and work experience for adult learners. Norwegian higher education awards bachelor, master, and PhD degrees, and many counties facilitate access to IT resources for students. The academic year runs from mid-August to June with substantial holiday breaks throughout. Educator titles and qualifications vary: preschool teachers require a bachelor’s degree, adjuncts a bachelor’s plus pedagogy, and lecturers a master’s with pedagogy. Norway maintains a multi-level grading system and employs both teacher-determined grades and randomly assigned external exams for upper secondary students. Special education services are integrated, and universities are required to ensure an inclusive and flexible learning environment.
