Education in Norway follows a structured, compulsory system spanning primary, lower secondary, and upper secondary education, culminating in higher education.

**Primary School (Barneskole, Grades 1–7, Ages 6–12):**
- Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and core subjects like science, history, and social studies.
- No formal grading until the end of primary school, though teachers provide informal assessments.
- Emphasizes social development and basic English skills.

**Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole, Grades 8–10, Ages 13–16):**
- Introduces grading and prepares students for upper secondary education.
- Students choose one elective subject (e.g., German, French, or additional English) and one language.
- Core subjects include mathematics, English, and Swedish, complemented by electives like technology or arts.
- Grades are used to determine eligibility for upper secondary school.

**Upper Secondary School (Videregående Skole, Grades VG1–VG3, Ages 16–19):**
- Mandatory for all students, with high enrollment rates.
- Divided into general studies (preparatory for university) and vocational studies (preparatory for careers).
- Students can choose between general studies (e.g., humanities, natural sciences) or vocational tracks (e.g., engineering, healthcare).
- Vocational programs often include apprenticeships.
- Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, mathematics) and electives to graduate.

**Higher Education:**
- Accessible beyond upper secondary school, typically requiring a general university admissions certificate.
- Universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.
- Public institutions are state-funded, with tuition fees minimal for domestic students.
- Private schools exist but are less common, focusing on specialized fields like business or arts.

**Key Features:**
- Compulsory education from ages 6–16, with free public schooling.
- Grading system ranges from 1 (lowest) to 6 (highest), with no plus/minus distinctions.
- Emphasis on inclusivity, with special education integrated into mainstream schools.
- Strong focus on practical and theoretical learning, with a blend of academic and vocational paths.
