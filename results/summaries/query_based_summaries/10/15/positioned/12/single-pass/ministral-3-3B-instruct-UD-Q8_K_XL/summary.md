Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **primary (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. The **barneskole** and **ungdomsskole** levels are compulsory, with the former focusing on foundational skills like reading, writing, and basic mathematics, while the latter introduces electives and prepares students for further education.

Upper secondary education is divided into **general studies** (preparing for university) and **vocational studies** (leading to apprenticeships). Students must pass core subjects (Swedish, English, and mathematics) to qualify for upper secondary school. The curriculum emphasizes critical thinking, practical skills, and a balanced approach to both academic and vocational paths. Higher education in Norway is free for EU/EEA/Swiss students, with public universities offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees, and private institutions catering to specialized fields. The system is highly centralized, with the government overseeing curriculum, teacher qualifications, and educational standards.
