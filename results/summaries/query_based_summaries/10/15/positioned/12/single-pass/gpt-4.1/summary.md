## Education in Norway

Education in Norway is divided into three levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education form grunnskole ('foundation school'), which is compulsory and free for all children ages 6–16. The curriculum covers foundational subjects such as Norwegian, mathematics, English, science, religion (including all major religions), history, social studies, and arts, with formal grading starting in lower secondary school. Upper secondary education is not compulsory but publicly funded, offering both general and vocational studies. The "2+2" vocational model combines school-based instruction with apprenticeships. Graduation rates for upper secondary students reach 73%. 

Higher education in Norway includes universities, university colleges, and some private schools, mostly state-run and tuition-free. Access typically requires a general university admissions certificate but can also be obtained through work experience and exam completion for adults. Norway’s higher education institutions are responsible for their own curricula and quality assurance, overseen by NOKUT. Teacher qualifications vary: preschool teachers require a bachelor’s degree, adjunct teachers hold university or college degrees plus pedagogy, and lecturers possess a master’s degree and pedagogy. Grading uses a six-point scale, transitioning to letter grades (A–F) at the university level. 

Private schools must be based on internationally recognized curricula or alternative pedagogies, are grant-aided, and constitute a small minority of student enrollment. Special education is provided within ordinary schools according to individual needs, supported by an educational/psychological service network. The academic year runs August–June, with substantial holiday breaks. Norwegian education emphasizes universal access, inclusivity, and flexibility in learning environments.
