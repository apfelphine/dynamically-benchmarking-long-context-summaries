# Education in Norway

The Norwegian school system is structured into three main parts: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory. Primary school begins with play-based learning and progresses to core subjects like mathematics, English, science, and religion. Students are graded from year 6 onward, with grades influencing access to upper secondary school. Lower secondary school includes elective courses and language options, with students often changing schools. Upper secondary education is primarily public, with a reform in 1994 merging general, mercantile, and vocational paths into a unified system. Students can choose between general studies (studieforberedelse) or vocational studies (yrkesfag), with vocational programs often following a "2+2 model" of two years of school training and two years of apprenticeship. 

Educators include preschool teachers (førskolelærer), adjunct teachers (adjunkt), and lecturers (lektor) with varying qualifications. Higher education is broadly divided into universities (theoretical subjects), university colleges (vocational and professional degrees), and private institutions. Admission requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), and students are ranked by grades. The system emphasizes inclusivity and quality, with special education provided in local schools. Grading uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. Examinations at the end of secondary education are crucial for university admission. The academic year runs from mid-August to June, with breaks in December, February, and summer. Norway's education system is known for its high international performance, especially in reading, and a strong focus on equity and inclusion.
