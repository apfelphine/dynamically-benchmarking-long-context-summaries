## Education in Norway

The Norwegian school system is structured into three parts: elementary school (*barneskole*, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (*ungdomsskole*, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (*videregående skole*, ages 16–19). The elementary and lower secondary school levels are compulsory for all children aged 6–16, collectively known as *grunnskole* ('foundation school').

In elementary school, students focus on educational games, social structures, the alphabet, basic math, English, science, religion (covering various religions), aesthetics, and music. No official grades are assigned at this level, though teachers may provide comments or unofficial grades based on tests. Lower secondary school introduces formal grading, which, along with geographic location, determines upper secondary school acceptance. Students can choose one elective and one language from various options. Upper secondary school is primarily public, though private schools are permitted under the Independent Schools Act of 2003 if they offer an international or alternative pedagogical approach.

Higher education, which occurs beyond upper secondary school, is largely state-run. Quality is assured by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT). Higher education is divided into Universities, which concentrate on theoretical subjects, and University Colleges (*høgskole*), which offer a range of educational choices including professional vocations. There is no formal distinction between vocational and non-vocational higher education.
