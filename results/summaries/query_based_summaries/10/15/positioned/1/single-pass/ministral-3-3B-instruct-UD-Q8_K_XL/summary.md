**Education in Norway**

Norway’s education system is highly structured, with **compulsory education** from ages 6 to 16, divided into three main stages: **primary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Primary and lower secondary education are mandatory, while upper secondary education is optional but highly regarded.

**Primary School (Ages 6–12)** focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and languages, with a curriculum that includes social studies, history, geography, and arts. Grading is informal, with teachers providing comments rather than formal marks.

**Lower Secondary School (Ages 13–16)** introduces grading and allows students to choose elective subjects, including foreign languages (German, French, Spanish) and career-oriented electives. Students must pass core subjects (Norwegian, English, mathematics) to progress.

**Upper Secondary School (Ages 16–19)** is divided into **general studies** (preparing for university) and **vocational studies** (training for specific careers). Students can choose between **2+2 model** (two years of school training followed by two years of apprenticeship) or **general studies** (theoretical focus). Graduation requires passing all subjects, with exams determining eligibility for university admission.

**Higher Education** is free for all students, with admission based on upper secondary school grades. Universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees, with many programs taught in Norwegian. Private schools are rare but exist, offering specialized curricula.

**Special Education** ensures inclusive learning for students with disabilities, supported by specialized services and resources. The system emphasizes **universal design** to accommodate diverse needs.

**Grading** uses a scale of 1–6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. Exams are teacher-assigned for most subjects, with a minority of students randomly selected for standardized exams.

**Academic Year** runs from mid-August to mid-June, with breaks including summer, Christmas, and mid-term holidays. Norway’s education system is known for its **high standards, emphasis on equality, and strong focus on both academic and vocational paths**.
