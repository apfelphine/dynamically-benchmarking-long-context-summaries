# Education in Norway

Education in Norway is organized into three main tiers: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory and collectively known as grunnskole ('foundation school'). The system has evolved from historical roots dating back to 2000 B.C., with compulsory education expanded from seven to nine years by 1969. Since 2003, private schools exist but remain limited; they must follow approved curricula and are heavily state-aided without subjective student selection.

At the elementary level, teaching focuses on foundational literacy, numeracy, social skills, and introductions to subjects like science, religion, and languages, without formal grades. Lower secondary introduces graded assessments and elective subjects, including modern languages and practical electives, with students' grades affecting upper secondary school admission. Upper secondary education, largely public, offers general and vocational studies, with the latter typically following a "2+2" model (two years school-based followed by apprenticeship). Reforms since 1994 and 2006 have unified previous branch systems and enhanced curriculum coherence, with increased integration of IT as mandatory.

Higher education encompasses universities, university colleges, and private institutions, requiring a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse). Admission is competitive and may include re-sitting upper secondary exams to improve grades. Universities emphasize theoretical disciplines and professional studies, while colleges offer a broad range including vocational education. Quality assurance is managed by NOKUT, ensuring standards consistent with European frameworks. Norway's academic calendar begins mid-August, with two semesters and multiple breaks, including national holidays and summer vacation.

Special education is integrated within mainstream schools, supported by specialist services and legislation promoting inclusive learning environments. Norwegian educators’ qualifications vary by teaching level, from preschool teachers with bachelor's degrees to lecturers with master's degrees leading upper secondary education. Grading in lower education uses a 1 to 6 scale; at university level, multiple grading systems coexist, including the Bologna-compatible letter system. Final upper secondary diplomas feature teacher-assigned grades and may include exams randomly assigned (trekkfag).

Overall, Norway's education system emphasizes accessibility, quality assurance, integrated special needs support, and a balance between general and vocational pathways, with strong state oversight and alignment with European educational standards.
