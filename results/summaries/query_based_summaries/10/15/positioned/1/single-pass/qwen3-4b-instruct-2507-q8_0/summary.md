**Education in Norway**  
Norway's education system is structured into three main levels: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Compulsory education spans from age 6 to 16.  

**Primary and lower secondary education** are mandatory and free, with a focus on foundational subjects including Swedish, mathematics, science, and social studies. Students begin with play-based learning in kindergarten, progressing to structured curricula in primary school. In lower secondary, students take graded assessments and choose electives in languages, arts, or sciences.  

**Upper secondary education** offers a choice between general and vocational tracks. The general path includes a broad curriculum, while vocational programs combine theory with practical training, often including apprenticeships. Students must pass national exams to graduate.  

**Private schools** are limited and must follow national curricula or offer religious or pedagogical alternatives. They are heavily grant-aided and cannot discriminate in admissions.  

**Higher education** is broadly divided into universities and university colleges. Admission requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), typically earned through upper secondary education. Norway is part of the European Higher Education Area, with a quality assurance system aligned with European standards.  

**Special education** is provided for students with disabilities, with a focus on inclusion. Special educators and support services ensure students receive tailored instruction.  

**Grading and assessments** use a scale from 1 to 6, with grades reflecting teacher assessments and final exams. Students receive a diploma upon completion.  

**School year and breaks** run from August to June, with summer, Christmas, and winter breaks. Students in upper secondary may take exams early if they meet exemption criteria.  

The system emphasizes equity, teacher development, and student well-being, with a strong focus on active learning and student engagement.
