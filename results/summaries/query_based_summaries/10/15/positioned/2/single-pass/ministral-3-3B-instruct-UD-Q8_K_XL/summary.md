The education system in Norway is structured into three main phases: **elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory, collectively referred to as *grunnskole* ("foundation school").

In **elementary school**, students learn core subjects like reading, writing, mathematics, science, and social studies, with no formal grading until the start of lower secondary school. The curriculum emphasizes foundational skills and social development, with a focus on inclusivity and equal opportunities.

**Lower secondary school** introduces grading and allows students to choose elective subjects, including languages (German, French, Spanish) and career-oriented electives. Students’ grades and regional performance determine their acceptance into upper secondary schools. The system emphasizes critical thinking and practical skills, though it has faced criticism for perceived favoritism toward certain students.

**Upper secondary school** (Videregående skole) is primarily public, with a high enrollment rate (93% in 2007). It offers both **general studies** (preparing students for higher education) and **vocational studies**, with the latter following a "2+2 model" (two years of school training and two years of apprenticeship). The curriculum integrates technology and prepares students for careers or further education. Graduates are called *russ* and often celebrate with parties before final exams.

The system is highly centralized, with the Ministry of Education overseeing curriculum, teacher training, and assessments. Higher education is accessible beyond upper secondary school, with public universities and university colleges offering degrees from bachelor’s to PhD levels. Grading is based on a scale of 1–6, with 6 being the highest passing grade. The education system is noted for its emphasis on equality, inclusivity, and practical skills, though challenges include teacher shortages and debates over curriculum changes.
