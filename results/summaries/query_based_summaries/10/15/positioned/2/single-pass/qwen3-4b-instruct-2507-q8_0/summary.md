**Education in Norway**

Norway's education system is structured into three main stages: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory and commonly referred to as *grunnskole*. 

In primary school, students focus on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and social studies. No formal grades are given, though teachers provide comments and informal assessments. At the lower secondary level, students begin receiving grades, which determine their eligibility for upper secondary school. Students can choose one elective and one language (e.g., German, French, or Spanish), with options varying by school.

Upper secondary education is primarily public, with 93% of students attending public schools. It is divided into general and vocational tracks. The general path provides a broad curriculum, while the vocational path emphasizes practical skills and includes a "2+2 model" with two years of classroom training and two years of apprenticeship. Students must pass core subjects to graduate and receive a diploma (*russ*). 

Teachers in Norway are categorized by level: preschool teachers (førskolelærer), adjunct teachers (adjunkt), and lecturers (lektor). Higher education is accessible through universities and university colleges, offering degrees from bachelor’s to doctorate. The grading system varies, with multiple scales used, including a 1–6 scale and a Bologna-compatible system. 

The system emphasizes inclusivity, with special education provided for students with learning difficulties, supported by educational psychologists and social workers. The curriculum is nationally standardized, with a focus on active learning and student-centered pedagogy. The Norwegian model is recognized for its equitable access, strong teacher training, and integration of students with special needs into mainstream classrooms.
