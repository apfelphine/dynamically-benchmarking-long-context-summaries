## Education in Norway: Concise Summary

Education in Norway is characterized by a highly structured and comprehensive system. Mandatory education begins at age 6 and lasts ten years, encompassing both elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12, grades 1–7) and lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16, grades 8–10), collectively referred to as grunnskole ("foundation school"). Students proceed to upper secondary education (videregående skole, ages 16–19, grades VG1–VG3), which offers both general and vocational tracks under the "2+2 model" for vocational studies—a combination of school-based learning and apprenticeships. While private schools exist, they represent a small share and must meet strict curricular and operational standards set by the government. The academic year runs from mid-August to June, and students benefit from several school breaks and national holidays.

Higher education in Norway is predominantly publicly funded and includes universities, university colleges (høgskole), and specialized private institutions, most overseen by the state or requiring government accreditation. Admission typically requires a "general university admissions certificate," based on upper secondary achievement, with additional pathways for mature applicants. The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensures quality standards. Education reform has focused on integrating IT, allowing easier movement between academic and vocational tracks. Special education is integrated in local schools, while grading systems use a scale of 1–6 in lower levels and letter grades at university. Teacher qualifications differ by school level, requiring bachelor's or master's degrees in education or specific subjects.
