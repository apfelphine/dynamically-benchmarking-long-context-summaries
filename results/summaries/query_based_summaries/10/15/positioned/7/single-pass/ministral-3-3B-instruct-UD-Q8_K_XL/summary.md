Education in Norway is structured into three main phases: **primary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Primary education is compulsory and free, focusing on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic sciences, with no formal grading until lower secondary school. Lower secondary school introduces grading, where students choose elective courses, including languages and practical electives. Upper secondary education is divided into general studies (preparing for university) and vocational studies, with the latter often involving apprenticeships.

Compulsory education begins at age 6, with the school year split into two semesters. Grading in Norway uses a scale from 1 (lowest) to 6 (highest), with 4 or above considered passing. Higher education is accessible to those with a general university admissions certificate, achieved through either upper secondary school studies or a national exam. Public universities and university colleges dominate higher education, with tuition-free education for citizens, though international students face higher fees. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with provisions for special education and a focus on lifelong learning.
