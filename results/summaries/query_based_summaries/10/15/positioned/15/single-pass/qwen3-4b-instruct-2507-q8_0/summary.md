**Education in Norway**  
Norway's education system is structured into three main stages: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). The first two are compulsory. Elementary school emphasizes play-based learning, social skills, and foundational subjects like reading, math, and English. Lower secondary school introduces formal grading, with students' performance determining eligibility for upper secondary school. Electives in languages (e.g., German, French, Spanish) or career studies (arbeidslivsfag) are offered.  

Upper secondary education is primarily public, with 93% of students attending public schools. It is divided into general (studieforberedelse) and vocational (yrkesfag) paths. Vocational studies follow a "2+2 model": two years of classroom training and an apprenticeship of two years. General studies emphasize theoretical knowledge and prepare students for higher education. Students are called "russ" upon graduation and celebrate with festivities.  

Educators include preschool teachers (bachelor’s degree), adjuncts (bachelor’s in a subject plus pedagogy), and lecturers (master’s degree with pedagogy training). Higher education, lasting three years or more, is accessible through a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse). Institutions include universities (focused on theory) and university colleges (offering vocational and professional degrees). Private schools are limited and must offer religious or pedagogical alternatives.  

Special education is integrated into local schools, with support from specialized services. Grading uses a 1–6 scale (6 being best), with final diplomas reflecting teacher-assessed grades and optional exam grades (trekkfag). The academic year runs from August to June, with breaks in December, February, and summer. National holidays include Constitution Day (May 17), Easter, and Ascension Day, with students free from school on these days.  

The system emphasizes inclusivity, equity, and quality, with strong performance in international assessments like PISA. Norway's education is known for its high standards, comprehensive support, and focus on student well-being.
