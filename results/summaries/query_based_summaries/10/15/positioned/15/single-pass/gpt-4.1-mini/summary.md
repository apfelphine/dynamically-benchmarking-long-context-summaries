# Education in Norway

Education in Norway has a long history dating back to 2000 B.C., with organized schooling evolving from cathedral schools established post-1153 and Latin schools mandated in market towns after the Reformation. Compulsory reading training began in 1736, and the folkeskole primary school system became mandatory for seven years in 1889, later extended to nine years in 1969. The folkeskole was replaced by the grunnskole ("foundation school") in the 1970s and 1980s. Private schooling is permitted under the Independent Schools Act of 2003 but remains limited and regulated, requiring government approval and adherence to recognized curricula.

The contemporary Norwegian education system is divided into three main stages: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary schooling are compulsory, collectively referred to as grunnskole. Primary education emphasizes foundational skills and social development, with no formal grades given, while lower secondary introduces graded assessments and elective languages or practical subjects. Upper secondary education offers general and vocational tracks, incorporating theoretical and practical learning, including apprenticeships. Norway has embraced reforms to increase inclusivity and integration of IT in education. Higher education encompasses universities and university colleges, with admission largely based on upper secondary school performance and regulated by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT). Special education has shifted from separate institutions to mainly inclusive settings within local schools, with support services provided to meet individual needs. The academic year spans two semesters from August to December and January to June, with various breaks and holidays observed. Grading systems vary by education level and institution, transitioning to align with the Bologna Process at the university level.
