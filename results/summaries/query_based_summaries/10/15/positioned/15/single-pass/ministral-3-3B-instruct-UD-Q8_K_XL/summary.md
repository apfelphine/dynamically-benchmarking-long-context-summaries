Education in Norway is structured into three main phases: **primary (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Compulsory education spans ages 6–16, with primary and lower secondary school being mandatory.

**Primary school** (grades 1–7) focuses on foundational skills like reading, writing, mathematics, and basic English, with no formal grading until grade 8. Students begin receiving grades in lower secondary school (grades 8–10), where they choose electives (e.g., languages, arts) alongside core subjects. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with special education integrated into mainstream schools.

**Upper secondary school** (grades VG1–VG3) is elective but highly competitive, offering both general and vocational tracks. Students apply to schools based on grades, and the curriculum includes mandatory IT integration. Graduates receive a diploma, qualifying them for higher education or direct entry into the workforce. The system is funded publicly, with private schools limited and heavily subsidized, adhering to national standards. Norway’s education system is known for its emphasis on equality, critical thinking, and practical skills, with a strong focus on lifelong learning and social inclusion.
