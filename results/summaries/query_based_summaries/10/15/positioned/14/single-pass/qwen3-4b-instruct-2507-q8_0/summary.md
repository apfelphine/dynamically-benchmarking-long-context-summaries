**Education in Norway**  
Norway's education system is structured into three main stages: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Compulsory education spans ages 6–16, with a focus on inclusivity and equity.  

Primary school emphasizes play-based learning and foundational skills in social structures, reading, math, and language. From age 13, students are graded, and their grades influence access to upper secondary school. Elective subjects, including foreign languages and career studies, are offered in lower secondary school.  

Upper secondary education is primarily public, with 93% of students attending public schools. It offers general or vocational tracks, with vocational programs following a "2+2 model" (two years of school training and two years of apprenticeship). The system emphasizes practical skills and flexibility, with a focus on IT integration.  

Higher education is accessible through universities and university colleges, with state-run institutions dominating. Admission requires a general university certificate (generell studiekompetanse), and students are ranked based on grades. Higher education includes theoretical studies (arts, sciences) and professional programs (law, medicine).  

The system values inclusive and flexible learning environments, with special education integrated into local schools. Grading uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 2–6 being passing. Examinations are teacher-assessed, with some students selected for random exams (trekkfag).  

Norway's education is known for its high performance in international assessments, with students outperforming peers in OECD evaluations. The system prioritizes equity, teacher development, and student well-being, with strong public funding and a commitment to universal access.
