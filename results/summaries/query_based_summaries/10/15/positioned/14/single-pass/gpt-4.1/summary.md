## Education in Norway: Concise Summary

Education in Norway is divided into three main levels: elementary school (ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (ages 16–19), with the first two being compulsory and collectively referred to as grunnskole. The system is predominantly public, with private schools existing only under strict government approval and heavy regulation. Norway's education system emphasizes inclusivity, universal design, and equal access; since the 1970s, special education largely occurs within ordinary schools. Norwegian law mandates education from age 6 to 16, after which students enter upper secondary school, offering both general studies and vocational tracks, with reforms allowing more course flexibility and easier transitions between paths. Instruction focuses on foundational subjects, and grades are only assigned from lower secondary school onward. Norway also supports camp school experiences and offers special educational accommodations.

Higher education in Norway is primarily provided by state-run universities and university colleges, with studies ranging from bachelor’s to PhD degrees. Admission typically requires completion of general studies at the upper secondary level, or alternative qualification paths for adult students. Universities have autonomy over curricula and also offer professional programs like law and medicine. The grading system has shifted from numerical to letter-based evaluations in accordance with the Bologna Process. The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) oversees academic standards. Education is characterized by a high degree of accessibility, low private sector participation, and strong state investment in infrastructure, quality assurance, and universal educational rights.
