**Education in Norway** dates back to the establishment of cathedral schools in 1153, evolving through the Reformation into mandatory primary education by 1889. The modern system, known as *grunnskole* (foundation school), was introduced in the 1970s, merging elementary (*barneskole*, ages 6–12) and lower secondary (*ungdomsskole*, ages 13–16) into a single compulsory stage. This is followed by optional upper secondary school (*videregående skole*, ages 16–19), which offers both general studies and vocational tracks under the "2+2 model" (two years of school followed by two years of apprenticeship). While public schools dominate the system, the Independent Schools Act of 2003 legalized private schools, which must adhere to internationally recognized curricula and cannot select students based on subjective criteria.

Higher education in Norway is state-run and divided into universities (theoretical subjects) and university colleges (professional and technical degrees). Admission generally requires a general university admissions certificate, though specific programs like medicine or engineering demand higher grades or special electives. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with a focus on universal design and special education integrated into ordinary schools. Grading typically uses a scale from 1 to 6, and the curriculum incorporates international standards through the Bologna Process, ensuring compatibility with European higher education frameworks.
