# Education in Norway

Organized education in Norway dates back to 2000 B.C., with significant developments such as the establishment of cathedral schools after 1153 and compulsory reading training from 1736. The folkeskole, a primary school system, became mandatory for seven years in 1889 and was later replaced by the grunnskole ('foundation school') covering ages 6 to 16. The modern Norwegian education system consists of three main parts: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), both compulsory and collectively called grunnskole, and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19), which is elective. Private schools exist but are few and must adhere to recognized curricula and governmental approval.

Elementary education focuses on foundational skills and social learning without official grades, while lower secondary introduces grading and electives like foreign languages. Upper secondary education offers general and vocational paths, with reforms aimed at integrating theory and practical training. Higher education includes universities, university colleges, and private institutions, with degrees following bachelor, master, and doctoral cycles. Admission to higher education is competitive and often requires a university admissions certificate. Grades at various levels differ, with a transition from numeric to letter grades at universities. Norway emphasizes inclusive education, including special education mostly integrated within normal schools. The academic year is divided into two semesters with various holidays, and a strong emphasis is placed on quality assurance and accessibility throughout the system.
