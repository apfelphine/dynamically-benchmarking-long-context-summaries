Education in Norway is structured into three main phases: **primary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Compulsory education spans ages 6–16, with free, state-funded public schools being the norm, though private schools are available under specific conditions.

### **Key Features:**
- **Primary School (Grades 1–7):** Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, math, and social studies. No formal grading until Grade 6, with assessments conducted through teacher evaluations and home tests.
- **Lower Secondary School (Grades 8–10):** Introduces grading, with students choosing electives (e.g., languages, arts). Students’ grades and regional location determine their acceptance into upper secondary schools.
- **Upper Secondary School (Grades VG1–VG3):** Mandatory for ages 16–19, offering three-year programs with a mix of general and vocational studies. Students can pursue general studies (preparing for university) or vocational tracks (e.g., engineering, healthcare). The system emphasizes practical work experience, often through apprenticeships.

### **Higher Education:**
- **Compulsory Admission:** Students must complete upper secondary school to access higher education. Admission is based on grades from upper secondary school, with some programs requiring additional exams.
- **Public Universities & University Colleges:** Mostly free, with tuition fees capped for domestic students. Programs include bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees across disciplines, with some vocational and professional degrees.
- **Grading & Assessment:** Universities use a mix of traditional exams and continuous assessment, with grades ranging from 1 (lowest) to 6 (highest). The Bologna Process influences degree structures, aligning with European standards.

### **Special Education & Accessibility:**
- **Special Needs:** Schools provide inclusive education, with specialized support for students with disabilities. The Educational/Psychological Service offers tailored assistance.
- **Private Schools:** Limited but allowed under the Independent Schools Act, focusing on religious, pedagogical, or international curricula. They receive significant government funding but cannot discriminate in admissions.

### **Educational Reforms & Challenges:**
- **Reform 94 (1994):** Unified upper secondary education, reducing fragmentation between academic, vocational, and mercantile tracks.
- **Kunnskapsløftet (2006):** Emphasized IT integration and standardized curriculum, though debates persist over grading fairness and equity.
- **Challenges:** High student-to-teacher ratios, particularly in rural areas, and debates over the effectiveness of standardized testing. The system prioritizes accessibility and practical skills, though quality varies across regions.

Norway’s education system is known for its **equality, free access to basic education, and emphasis on practical and theoretical learning**, though it faces ongoing challenges in maintaining high standards and addressing regional disparities.
