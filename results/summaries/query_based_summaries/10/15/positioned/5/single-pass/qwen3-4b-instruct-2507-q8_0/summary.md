**Education in Norway**  
Norway’s education system is structured into three main stages: primary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Compulsory education spans ages 6–16, with students transitioning between levels.  

Primary education emphasizes foundational skills in reading, writing, math, and social studies, with a focus on play-based learning in early years. Students begin receiving grades in grade 6, and the curriculum includes a mix of core and elective subjects.  

Lower secondary school introduces formal grading, with students choosing one elective and one language (e.g., German, French, or Spanish). Grades are based on a scale from A to F, with a total score calculated from subject grades for university admissions.  

Upper secondary education is divided into general and vocational tracks, with students completing a diploma project. The reform of 1994 merged vocational and general studies, allowing flexibility in pathways.  

Higher education is primarily state-funded, with public universities and university colleges offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Admission is based on grades from upper secondary school, and the system is aligned with the Bologna Process.  

Private schools, known as friskolor, are funded by municipalities and offer alternatives to public education, though concerns about quality and inequality exist.  

Special education is integrated into mainstream schools, with support from educational psychologists and social welfare workers.  

The system emphasizes equity, with policies to reduce class sizes and improve access, especially in rural areas. Norway ranks highly in international assessments, though challenges remain in teacher quality and student engagement.
