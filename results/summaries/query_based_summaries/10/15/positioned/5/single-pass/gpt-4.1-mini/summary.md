# Education in Norway

Education in Norway has a long history, with organized schooling dating back to around 2000 B.C. After various historical developments, the modern Norwegian education system comprises three main compulsory levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary schooling constitute the grunnskole (‘foundation school’), which is mandatory for all children aged 6–16.

Primary education emphasizes foundational skills through interactive learning without official grades in early years, while grading begins in lower secondary. Students choose electives from the 8th grade onward. Upper secondary education, primarily public, offers general studies and vocational tracks, including apprenticeships, with a graduation rate around 73%. The education reform «Kunnskapsløftet» introduced in 2006 updated curricula and increased IT integration. “Camp school” (leirskole) is an optional extended stay program during primary and secondary schooling.

Educator qualifications vary with training and degree level: preschool teachers hold bachelor's degrees, adjunct teachers bachelor's plus pedagogy, and lecturers master’s degrees plus pedagogy, mostly teaching from grades 8 and above. Higher education requires a general university admissions certificate, obtained through upper secondary schooling or other pathways. Universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, with the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) overseeing quality. Admission competitiveness often leads students to retake upper secondary exams to improve grades.

Norway’s independent schools are limited due to regulation; private schools must follow recognized curricula and receive government grants. The grading system includes numerical and letter grades, with a transition to the Bologna system at tertiary levels. Special education is inclusive and mostly integrated into local schools, supported by educational psychologists and special educators.

The academic year runs from mid-August to June with two semesters and several breaks, including summer, Christmas, and autumn and winter holidays. National holidays like Constitution Day (May 17) and others are observed as days off from school.
