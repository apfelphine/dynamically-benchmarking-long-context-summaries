## Education in Norway

Education in Norway is highly organized and predominantly public, with a history spanning back to 2000 B.C. Norway’s modern system is divided into three main stages: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). The grunnskole (foundation school), covering elementary and lower secondary education, is compulsory for all children aged 6–16, and emphasizes inclusive learning. Primary education introduces children to basic skills in language, mathematics, social structures, and multiple religions, with no official grades at this stage. Lower secondary begins formal grading and allows elective choices such as languages and practical subjects. Upper secondary school offers both general and vocational paths through a unified structure since the 1994 reform ("Reform 94"), supporting flexible movement between study lines. Graduation rates from upper secondary reached 73% in 2017.

Higher education in Norway, chiefly state-run, consists of universities (focusing on theoretical subjects), university colleges (including vocational degrees), and a small number of private institutions. Admission typically requires a general university admissions certificate from upper secondary school, or equivalent qualifications and experience. The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education oversees quality standards. Norway practices a Bologna-compatible grading system, with the academic year running from August to June and generous vacation periods. Special education is integrated mostly within ordinary schools, and all higher education institutions are required to provide an inclusive learning environment by law. Private schools exist, but make up a small fraction and must be government-approved, often following internationally recognized curricula or alternative pedagogical/religious methods.
