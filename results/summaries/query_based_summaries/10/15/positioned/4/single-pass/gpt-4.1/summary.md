## Education in Norway

Norway's education system is divided into three main levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary schooling, known together as grunnskole or 'foundation school', is compulsory for all children aged 6–16. Primary school focuses on foundational learning through play and basic subjects including mathematics, English, science, social studies, and religion, with no official grades given at this stage. Grading begins in lower secondary school, which also introduces electives and language choices. Transition to upper secondary is determined by students' grades and location, and at this level, students choose between general studies (studieforberedelse) for university preparation or vocational studies (yrkesfag), with flexibility to switch tracks. The 2006 reform emphasized IT in education and standardization across vocational and general pathways.

Higher education institutions in Norway include universities and university colleges, mostly state-run, offering bachelor, master, and PhD degrees. Admission typically requires a general university admissions certificate, obtainable through upper secondary studies or the "23/5 rule" (age, experience, and specific exams). Private schools exist but are less prevalent and are government supported and regulated. Norway practices inclusive education policies; special education usually takes place within mainstream schools, supported by specialist services. Educator roles and required qualifications are clearly defined by school level, and IT is integrated into instruction. The academic year runs mid-August to June, with two semesters, and students enjoy extended holidays throughout the year. Grading systems follow numerical scales, and at the conclusion of upper secondary, diplomas are issued based on teacher-assigned grades, with some students selected for supplementary exams.
