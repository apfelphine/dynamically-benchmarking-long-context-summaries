Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: **primary school (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. The first two levels are compulsory, with the latter being optional but widely attended.

**Primary school** focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic English. No formal grades are given, but teachers provide feedback and occasional informal evaluations. Students begin learning core subjects like mathematics, English, science, and social studies from the second grade onward.

**Lower secondary school** introduces grading, with students choosing elective courses such as German, French, or additional English. The curriculum emphasizes core subjects like Turkish literature, mathematics, and science, with a focus on preparing students for further education or vocational paths. The school year is divided into two semesters, starting in mid-August and ending in June, with significant breaks, including summer, Christmas, and fall/winter holidays.

**Upper secondary school** (Videregående Skole) is divided into general and vocational tracks. Students can pursue a variety of subjects, including arts, sciences, and technical studies, with the option of apprenticeships for vocational programs. The system aims to provide a balanced education, ensuring students are prepared for either higher education or the workforce. The school year follows the same two-semester structure as primary and lower secondary education.

Norway’s education system is known for its emphasis on equality, inclusivity, and student-centered learning. The curriculum is designed to foster critical thinking, creativity, and practical skills, with a strong focus on integrating technology and modern pedagogical approaches. The system also places significant importance on social and emotional development, aiming to prepare students for both academic and real-world challenges.
