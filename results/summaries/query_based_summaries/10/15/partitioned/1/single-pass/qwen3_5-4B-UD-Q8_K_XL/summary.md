**Education in Norway** is a centralized, state-funded system divided into compulsory elementary and lower secondary school (grunnskole) and optional upper secondary school (videregående skole). Elementary school (ages 6–12) and lower secondary school (ages 13–16) are mandatory, featuring a curriculum that emphasizes play and socialization in early grades before introducing formal grading. Students in lower secondary school can choose elective subjects, including languages and vocational studies, and often change schools upon entering upper secondary education. Upper secondary school (ages 16–19) is primarily public, with 93% of students attending state schools; it offers both general studies and vocational tracks, including a "2+2 model" involving apprenticeships. Graduation from upper secondary school is required for university admission, and students who wish to pursue competitive fields like medicine or engineering often retake exams to improve their grades.

Higher education in Norway is divided into universities, which focus on theoretical subjects, and university colleges (høgskole), which offer vocational and professional degrees. Admission generally requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), though a specific law allows individuals over 23 with five years of schooling and work experience to gain entry. The system is highly decentralized, with counties responsible for public schools, and the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) oversees institutional quality. Grading at the university level traditionally ranged from 1.0 to 6.0, though the Bologna process has introduced European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) credits. Special education is legally mandated to be inclusive, with a strong emphasis on universal design and support within ordinary schools.
