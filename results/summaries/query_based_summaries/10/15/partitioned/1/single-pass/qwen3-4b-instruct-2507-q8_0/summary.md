**Education in Norway**

**Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole, Grades 8–10, Ages 13–16):**  
Students may take the grade 10 exam in a subject early with an exemption from the elementary/middle school curriculum. In 2009, Norwegian 15-year-olds outperformed other Scandinavian countries in PISA, though top 10% lagged three years behind Shanghai students. Criticism from sociologist Sanna Sarromaa highlights delays in teaching and parental responsibility for foundational knowledge.

**Upper Secondary School (Videregående Skole, Grades VG1–VG3, Ages 16–19):**  
Primarily public (93% attendance in 2007), with private schools limited to religious, pedagogical, or alternative curricula. The 1994 "Reform 94" merged general, mercantile, and vocational paths into one system, emphasizing general studies and flexibility in transitioning between tracks. The "2+2 model" (two years of school training, two years of apprenticeship) is common in vocational studies. Students graduate with a "russ" diploma, celebrated with festivities before final exams. Graduation rate is 73%.

**Educators:**  
- **Preschool teacher (førskolelærer):** Bachelor’s degree required.  
- **Adjunct teacher (adjunkt):** Bachelor’s in a subject; teaches minor subjects; requires pedagogy course.  
- **Lecturer (lektor):** Master’s degree; academic approach; teaches from grade 8 onward.

**Higher Education:**  
- Access requires general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), achievable via general studies or age/qualifications (23+ years).  
- Broadly divided into:  
  - **Universities:** Focus on theoretical subjects (arts, humanities, sciences); offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees.  
  - **University Colleges (høgskole):** Offer university-level degrees, engineering, and vocational programs; require accreditation from NOKUT.  
  - **Private schools:** Specialize in business, arts; 10% of higher education students.  
- No formal distinction between vocational and non-vocational higher education.  
- **Timeline:** Key institutions include University of Oslo (1811), Norwegian School of Economics (1936), University of Bergen (1946), and University of Stavanger (2005).  
- **Grading:** Uses a 1–6 scale (6 = best); Bologna system introduced, with dual grading (numbers and letters) for transitional students.  
- **Examinations:** Final diploma includes teacher-assigned grades and optional exam grades ("trekkfag").  
- **Academic Year:** Two semesters (August–December, January–June); long breaks in summer (June–August), winter (February), and Christmas (December–January).  
- **Special Education:** Students with learning needs are educated in local schools; special education services include psychologists, social workers, and teachers. Institutions promote inclusive, flexible environments.  

**Additional Notes:**  
- Education is compulsory from age 6 to 16.  
- School year begins mid-August; national holidays include Constitution Day (May 17), Easter (April–May), and New Year’s Day.  
- Emphasis on equality, inclusivity, and student well-being.
