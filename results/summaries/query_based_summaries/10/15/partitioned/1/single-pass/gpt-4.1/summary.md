## Education in Norway – Summary

Education in Norway is compulsory for children ages 6–16, comprised of primary school (barneskole, grades 1–7), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, grades 8–10), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, grades VG1–VG3). Elementary and lower secondary levels form the grunnskole (foundation school), with a curriculum that introduces basic academics, languages, religion, social studies, and the arts. Formal grading begins in lower secondary, where students’ grades and location affect admission to upper secondary schools. Upper secondary education, which is primarily public, offers general studies (studieforberedelse) and vocational studies (yrkesfag), following reforms that facilitate movement between academic and vocational paths. Graduation rates as of 2017 stood at 73%.

Educators must meet specific training requirements, ranging from bachelor's degrees for preschool and adjunct teachers to master's degrees for lecturers in high schools. Higher education comprises universities, university colleges, and private schools, mostly state-run, accessible with a general university admissions certificate, or via a combination of work and study experience. Universities and university colleges offer bachelor, master, and PhD programs, and are regulated for quality by NOKUT. Norway promotes inclusive education, with special education provisions mainstreamed in ordinary schools. National holidays, seasonal breaks, and a two-semester calendar mark the academic schedule, while grading systems have transitioned from numeric to international letter grades in higher education. Private schooling exists but is limited and heavily regulated.
