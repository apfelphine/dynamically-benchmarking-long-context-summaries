# Education in Norway

The Norwegian education system is divided into three main levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory and collectively called grunnskole ('foundation school'). In primary school, children initially engage in play-based learning and basic skills without official grading. From lower secondary school onward, students receive grades, which influence admission to upper secondary schools. Offering both general and vocational paths, upper secondary education integrates theory and apprenticeships, and has undergone reforms to increase flexibility, incorporate IT, and unify educational branches. Graduation rates at this level were about 73% as of 2017.

Higher education in Norway requires a general university admissions certificate, typically acquired through upper secondary education; special provisions exist for mature students. Institutions include universities, university colleges, and some private schools, with public institutions dominant. Universities focus on theoretical disciplines and offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees, while university colleges provide a broad range of academic and vocational programs subject to accreditation. The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) oversees quality. The grading system spans numeric and letter scales, reflecting Norwegian and Bologna Process standards. Education holidays include a two-semester academic year starting in August, with several breaks and national holidays. Special education emphasizes inclusion, with legislation supporting education of students with individual needs largely within local schools.
