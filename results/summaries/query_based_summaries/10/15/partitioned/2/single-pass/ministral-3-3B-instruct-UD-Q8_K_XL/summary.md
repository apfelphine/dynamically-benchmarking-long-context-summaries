Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: **elementary school (barneskole, grades 1–7, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, grades 8–10, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary school (videregående skole, grades VG1–VG3, ages 16–19)**. The first two levels are compulsory, with the latter being elective but highly influential in determining future academic and vocational paths.

### **Educators in Schools**
- **Preschool teachers (førskolelærer/barnehagelærer):** Required a bachelor’s degree to teach in kindergartens or the first four grades of primary school.
- **Adjunct teachers (adjunkt):** Work primarily in lower secondary school (grades 5–10) and require a bachelor’s degree in their subject area, along with a one-year pedagogy course.
- **Lecturers (lektor):** Focus on upper secondary school (grades 8–12) and need a master’s degree in their field, including a pedagogy course.

### **Higher Education**
- **Compulsory Admission:** Students must achieve a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse) to enter higher education, typically through upper secondary school studies or an alternative pathway for those over 23 with relevant work and exam experience.
- **Institutions:** Norway’s higher education system includes **universities** (offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees in arts, humanities, natural sciences, and professional fields like medicine and law) and **university colleges** (providing bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees in engineering, teacher training, and vocational studies). Private schools are rare but exist, offering specialized programs.
- **Quality Assurance:** The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensures the quality of higher education programs.
- **Tuition:** Higher education is largely free, though some programs may have minor fees.

### **Grading System**
- **Lower Education:** Grades range from 1 (lowest) to 6 (highest), with 4 or above considered passing.
- **Higher Education:** Uses a mix of numerical (1.0–6.0) and letter grades (A–F) depending on the institution’s system.

### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Grunnskole (elementary and lower secondary school) is mandatory for all children aged 6–16.
- **Vocational Pathways:** Upper secondary school offers both general and vocational tracks, with vocational studies often including apprenticeships.
- **Inclusive Learning:** Special education is integrated into mainstream schools, with support from specialized services.
- **International Recognition:** Norwegian qualifications are widely recognized globally, with universities often ranked among the best in Europe.

The system emphasizes accessibility, quality, and practical preparation for both academic and vocational careers.
