## Education in Norway – Key Overview

Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19), with elementary and lower secondary being compulsory. The curriculum in primary school emphasizes foundational skills, social learning, and introduction to core academic subjects. Lower secondary school introduces formal grading and elective subjects, including foreign languages and practical studies, which influence students’ future educational pathways. Historically, Norway established cathedral schools for priests, with compulsory reading training from 1736, followed by the introduction of folkeskole and later grunnskole as the basis for general education.

Secondary education is predominantly public, with private schools permitted if they follow recognized alternative or international curricula and are government approved. The 1994 Reform unified the previously separate general, mercantile, and vocational upper secondary tracks, fostering flexibility for students switching paths and integrating theory into vocational curricula. Vocational studies follow the "2+2 model": two years of school-based learning and two years of apprenticeship. Special education is delivered inclusively within ordinary schools based on individual need, supported by government psychological and educational services.

Higher education, overseen mostly by state institutions, includes universities, university colleges, and private institutions, all subject to quality assurance by NOKUT. Access requires a general university admissions certificate, and universities offer bachelor, master, and PhD programs. Norway uses multiple grading systems linked to both local and international standards. The academic year starts in August and ends in June, with scheduled vacations and national holidays. Norway's education system prioritizes equity, universal access, and inclusivity, with most students attending public institutions and private schools remaining a small minority.
