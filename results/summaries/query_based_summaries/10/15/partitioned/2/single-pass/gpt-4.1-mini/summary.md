# Education in Norway

Norway's education system is divided into three main stages: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, grades 8–10, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Compulsory education covers elementary and lower secondary levels, commonly called grunnskole. Primary education focuses on foundational skills and broad subjects including religion and languages, without official grades but with teacher assessments. In lower secondary, grading begins, and students choose electives and languages, influencing their admission to upper secondary schools. Upper secondary education includes general and vocational tracks; reforms have integrated study paths to allow credit transfer between fields. The majority attend public schools, with private schools being rare and regulated under the Independent Schools Act of 2003.

Higher education has a rich history dating back to the 18th century, with institutions like the University of Oslo (founded 1811) and others established through the centuries, including universities and university colleges. Norwegian higher education institutions enjoy autonomy, with quality assurance overseen by NOKUT. Grading systems have transitioned from numeric scales to the Bologna system, sometimes mixing both. Special education has evolved from separate institutions to inclusive education in local schools, supported by educational psychologists and specialists. The academic calendar includes two semesters (August–December and January–June) with multiple breaks and national holidays. Examinations in upper secondary include a unique system where some students are randomly selected to sit exams ("trekkfag").
