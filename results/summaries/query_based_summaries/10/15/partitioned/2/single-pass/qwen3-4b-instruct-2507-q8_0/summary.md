**Education in Norway**  

**Educators in Norwegian schools:**  
- **Preschool teacher (førskolelærer or barnehagelærer):** Trained with a bachelor’s degree, works in kindergartens and early grades.  
- **Adjunct teacher (adjunkt):** Requires a bachelor’s degree in a subject and a one-year pedagogy course; teaches grades 5–10, often in minor subjects.  
- **Lecturer (lektor):** Holds a master’s degree and a pedagogy course; teaches in upper secondary and high schools with an academic approach.  

**Higher education:**  
- Requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse), attainable via general studies or through age and qualification criteria (e.g., 23+ years with specific qualifications).  
- Divided into universities (offering arts, humanities, natural sciences, professional degrees like law, medicine, psychology) and university colleges (offering vocational and professional programs).  
- Quality assured by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT).  
- No formal distinction between vocational and non-vocational higher education.  

**Timeline of Norwegian higher education:**  
- 1750: Norwegian Military Academy established as a "Free Mathematical School."  
- 1811: University of Oslo founded.  
- 1910: Norwegian University of Life Sciences established.  
- 1936: Norwegian Institute of Technology founded.  
- 1943: BI Norwegian Business School established.  
- 1961: Oslo School of Architecture and Design established.  
- 1972: University of Tromsø established.  
- 2005: University of Stavanger formed from Stavanger University College.  
- 2007: University of Agder formed.  
- 2011: University of Nordland established.  
- 2014: University College of South-Eastern Norway formed.  
- 2018: University of South-Eastern Norway granted university status.  

**Special education:**  
- Policy since the 1970s ensures all children are educated in local schools.  
- Special education is tailored to individual student needs, with support from Educational/Psychological Service, including psychologists, social workers, and teachers.  
- Inclusive learning environments are required in all institutions.  

**Grading system:**  
- Multiple systems exist:  
  - Lower levels use a 1–6 scale (6 is highest, 2 is lowest passing grade).  
  - University level uses a Bologna system with both numeric and letter grades.  
  - Final diplomas feature teacher-assigned grades and optional exam grades (trekkfag).  

**Academic schedule:**  
- Two semesters: August to December and January to June.  
- Long breaks: Summer (8 weeks), Christmas (after December), and fall/winter breaks.  
- National holidays include Easter, May Day, Constitution Day, Ascension Day, and Pentecost.  

**Examinations:**  
- Upper secondary students obtain a diploma after passing all subjects.  
- A minority of students are randomly selected to take an exam (trekkfag) in a subject.  

**Education today:**  
- Compulsory education from age 6 to 16.  
- Primary (barneskole, ages 6–12): Focuses on play-based learning, social skills, language, and basic math.  
- Lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16): Students are graded; grades influence upper secondary school admissions. Electives include languages (German, French, Spanish) or career studies (arbeidslivsfag).  
- Upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19): Students earn a "russ" diploma; graduates celebrate with festivities.  

**Private education:**  
- Private schools are limited; only religious (Christian), Steiner, Montessori, or pedagogically alternative schools are legally recognized.  
- Must follow an internationally recognized curriculum or pedagogical approach and are heavily grant-aided.  

**Inclusive and accessible education:**  
- Strong emphasis on inclusivity, universal design, and student-centered learning.  
- Special education is integrated into mainstream schools, with support services provided by trained professionals.  

**Key features:**  
- High-quality, equitable, and inclusive education system.  
- Emphasis on student well-being, individualized learning, and social development.  
- Strong public funding and state oversight ensure accessibility and quality.
