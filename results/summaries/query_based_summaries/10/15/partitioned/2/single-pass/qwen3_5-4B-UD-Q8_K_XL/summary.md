# Education in Norway

The Norwegian education system is structured into three main levels: elementary school (*barneskole*, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (*ungdomsskole*, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (*videregående skole*, ages 16–19). The first two stages are compulsory and collectively known as *grunnskole* (foundation school). Primary education focuses on social development, basic skills, and introduces subjects like mathematics, English, science, and religion, with no official grades assigned; teachers instead provide qualitative comments on tests. Lower secondary education begins formal grading, and from eighth grade, students may choose electives and languages to determine their eligibility for upper secondary school.

Higher education in Norway is divided into universities and university colleges (*høgskoler*). Universities typically focus on theoretical subjects in arts, humanities, and natural sciences, offering bachelor's, master's, and PhD degrees, while university colleges provide a wider range of options including vocational training and professional degrees like nursing and teaching. Access to higher education generally requires a general university admissions certificate (*generell studiekompetanse*), which can be obtained through upper secondary studies or by meeting specific age and experience criteria. The system is largely state-funded and regulated by the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT). Vocational education in upper secondary school often follows a "2+2" model, combining two years of academic training with two years of apprenticeship in an industry or public institution.
