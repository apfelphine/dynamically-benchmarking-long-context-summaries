Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: elementary school (*barneskole*, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (*ungdomsskole*, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (*videregående skole*, ages 16–19). The first two levels are compulsory and collectively referred to as the *grunnskole*. Elementary education focuses on play-based learning and basic skills without official grading, while lower secondary education introduces grading, allowing students to choose electives and languages to influence their upper secondary school placement. Upper secondary education is non-compulsory but highly popular, offering either general studies (*studieforberedelse*) to prepare for university or vocational studies (*yrkesfag*), often following a "2+2" model involving school training followed by apprenticeships.

The higher education system in Norway includes universities, university colleges, and private schools. It is broadly divided between institutions focusing on theoretical subjects and those offering professional or vocational degrees. Admission generally requires a general university admissions certificate (*generell studiekompetanse*), obtained through upper secondary studies or specific exams for adults. The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensures quality across the sector. Historically, the system evolved from early military and mining academies to the Humboldt Model at the University of Oslo in 1811, leading to the establishment of numerous specialized institutions over the centuries, including the University of Tromsø in 1972 and the University of Stavanger in 2005. Private schools, though rare in secondary education, are more common in higher education, specializing in subjects like business and fine arts.
