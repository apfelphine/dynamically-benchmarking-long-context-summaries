Norway’s education system is structured into three main stages: **primary (barneskole, grades 1–7, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, grades 8–10, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, grades VG1–VG3, ages 16–19)**. Education is compulsory for all children from ages 6 to 16, with the first two stages (primary and lower secondary) being mandatory.

### **Primary School (Grades 1–7, Ages 6–12)**
- Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, mathematics, and basic English.
- No formal grading is given, but teachers provide informal feedback.
- Students begin learning social structures, basic arithmetic, and foundational English skills.

### **Lower Secondary School (Grades 8–10, Ages 13–16)**
- Students are introduced to grading and elective courses (e.g., German, French, or additional English).
- The curriculum includes core subjects like mathematics, science, history, and geography, supplemented by electives.
- Students may choose one elective and one language, though practical electives were previously an option.
- Grades determine eligibility for upper secondary school, with students often required to change schools due to limited capacity.

### **Upper Secondary School (Grades VG1–VG3, Ages 16–19)**
- Divided into **general studies** (for university preparation) and **vocational studies** (for career-focused training).
- The **Reform 94** (1994) merged vocational and general studies into a unified system, allowing flexibility in switching paths.
- The **Kunnskapsløftet** (2006) introduced a structured approach, emphasizing general studies for university eligibility.
- Vocational studies often follow the **2+2 model**, combining school training with apprenticeships.
- Students can choose between general studies (for higher education) or vocational studies (for direct employment).

### **Grading System**
- Prior to the Bologna Process, grades ranged from 1.0 to 6.0, with 4.0 or above being passing.
- The new system integrates both grading scales, allowing students to have transcripts with grades from both systems.
- Lower levels use a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest and 2 the lowest passing grade.

### **Examinations**
- Upper secondary students must pass all subjects to graduate, with some students selected randomly for exams (trekkfag).
- The academic year is divided into two semesters, with breaks including summer, Christmas, and fall/winter holidays.

### **Higher Education**
- Admission to higher education requires a general university admissions certificate (generell studiekompetanse).
- Universities and university colleges offer degrees in arts, humanities, natural sciences, and professional fields.
- Private schools are less common but exist, often focusing on specialized subjects like business or fine arts.
- The Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) ensures quality standards in higher education.

### **Special Education**
- Special education is integrated into mainstream schools, with the Educational/Psychological Service providing support for students with disabilities.
- Institutions must create inclusive learning environments, adhering to universal design principles.

### **Key Features**
- **Free education** at all levels, with minimal fees for upper secondary and higher education.
- **Compulsory attendance** from ages 6 to 16, with a focus on foundational skills and critical thinking.
- **Flexible pathways** in upper secondary education, allowing students to transition between general and vocational studies.
- **Strong emphasis on inclusivity**, with special education integrated into mainstream schools.
