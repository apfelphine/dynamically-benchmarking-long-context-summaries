## Education in Norway – Concise Summary

Education in Norway is structured into three main levels: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education are compulsory for children aged 6–16. The curriculum covers languages (including English), mathematics, sciences, religion (covering multiple faiths), social studies, and the arts, with formal grades introduced in lower secondary school. Upper secondary school offers both general studies (preparing for higher education) and vocational studies, with flexible pathways allowing credit transfer between tracks. As of 2017, 73% of students graduate from upper secondary school. Private schools exist but are few; most are religious or based on alternative pedagogies and receive heavy government grant aid.

Higher education in Norway includes universities, university colleges, and private schools. Admission typically requires completion of upper secondary school and a general university admissions certificate, though alternative routes exist for adults with relevant work and academic experience. Most institutions are state-run, and the majority of students attend public universities or university colleges, which offer bachelor, master, and doctoral degrees. University colleges must obtain accreditation for advanced programs, and quality assurance is overseen by NOKUT, an independent government agency. Grading systems have shifted from unique numeric systems to those compatible with the Bologna Process. Special education is provided within mainstream schools, and pupils unable to benefit from typical instruction have a legal right to special education. The Educational/Psychological Service supports students and families, and institutions must ensure inclusive, universally designed learning environments.
