## Education in Norway

**Higher Education:**
The timeline of Norwegian higher education shows its development beginning with the establishment of the Norwegian Military Academy in 1750. Key milestones include the founding of the University of Oslo in 1811, the Norwegian University of Life Sciences in 1859, and the establishment of the Norwegian Institute of Technology in 1910. Universities and university colleges have undergone several reorganizations and status changes, such as Stavanger University College becoming the University of Stavanger in 2005, and Bodø University College becoming the University of Nordland in 2011. Higher education is generally state-run, and the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) assures its quality.

**K-12 Education and System Structure:**
The Norwegian school system is divided into three parts: elementary school (grades 1–7, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (grades 8–10, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (grades VG1–VG3, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary school are compulsory for all children aged 6–16. In primary school, no official grades are given, though teachers may provide comments or unofficial grades. When students enter lower secondary school, they begin to be graded.

**Curriculum and Pathways:**
Upper secondary school is primarily based in public schools, with a high percentage of students attending public institutions. The 1994 high school reform merged general, mercantile, and vocational branches into a unified system. Under the 2006 reform, students can pursue either a general studies (*studieforberedelse*) or a vocational studies (*yrkesfag*) path. Vocational studies often follow a "2+2 model," which combines school training with an apprenticeship in an enterprise.

**Special Education:**
Norway has a large-scale institution for people with intellectual disabilities, founded in 1898. Since the 1970s, the government has legislated that all children should be educated in local schools, meaning special education largely takes place within ordinary schools, depending on the individual student's needs.
