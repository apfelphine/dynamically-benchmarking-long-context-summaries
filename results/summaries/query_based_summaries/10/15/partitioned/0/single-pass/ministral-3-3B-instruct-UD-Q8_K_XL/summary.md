Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: **primary (barneskole, ages 6–12)**, **lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**, and **upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19)**. Compulsory education spans elementary and lower secondary school, with the latter including a mandatory general studies or vocational track. Upper secondary education is elective but highly regarded, with 73% of students graduating in 2017.

**Primary school** (barneskole) focuses on foundational skills like reading, writing, basic math, and English, with no formal grading until the end of the program. Students transition to **lower secondary school** (ungdomsskole) at age 13, where they choose between general studies (studieforberedelse) or vocational training (yrkesfag). The curriculum emphasizes core subjects like Norwegian, English, math, and science, with electives including languages and arts. Vocational tracks often include apprenticeships, while general studies prepare students for higher education.

**Upper secondary school** (videregående skole) lasts three years and is divided into **VG1–VG3**, with students taking exams at the end of VG3. The system, reformed in 1994, aims to provide a balanced education, blending theory and practical skills. Students can also opt for specialized vocational training, including internships. The education system is highly accessible, with 93% of students attending public schools. Higher education is free and widely available, with universities and university colleges offering diverse programs, including professional degrees in fields like medicine and engineering. The country emphasizes quality and inclusivity, ensuring all students have access to education.
