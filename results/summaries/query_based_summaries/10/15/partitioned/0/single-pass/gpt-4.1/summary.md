## Education in Norway: Summary

Education in Norway is organized into three main levels: elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education, collectively called grunnskole, are compulsory for all children. At the upper secondary level, students choose between general studies (preparatory for higher education) and vocational tracks, often following a "2+2 model" that includes both school and apprenticeship. Since the 2006 reform Kunnskapsløftet, information technology is integrated, and switching between electives has become more regimented.

Norwegian schools are overwhelmingly public, though private options exist and must meet strict regulations. Educator qualifications are tiered: preschool teachers require a specific bachelor’s, adjuncts a bachelor's plus pedagogy, and lecturers typically hold a master's degree. Higher education is state-run, with entry mostly based on upper secondary grades. Universities offer bachelor, master, and PhD degrees, while university colleges provide a wider range of academic and vocational options. Norway emphasizes inclusive education, with special education services available in local schools, and maintains universal design principles for learning environments. The academic year runs August-June, featuring holidays such as summer, Christmas, fall, and winter breaks.
