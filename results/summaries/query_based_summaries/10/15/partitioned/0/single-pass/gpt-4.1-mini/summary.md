# Education in Norway

Education in Norway is structured into three main stages: elementary school (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary school (videregående skole, ages 16–19). Elementary and lower secondary education combined are called grunnskole and are compulsory for children aged 6 to 16. Upper secondary education, which can be general or vocational following the 1994 reform ("Reform 94"), is mostly public and not compulsory, with 93% attending public schools as of 2007 and a graduation rate of 73% in 2017. Vocational tracks often follow a "2+2 model" combining school-based training and apprenticeships. Education reforms emphasize general studies eligibility for higher education and IT integration.

Educators in Norway include preschool teachers (with a bachelor's degree, teaching kindergarten and early primary levels), adjunct teachers (typically bachelor’s degree plus pedagogy, teaching grades 5–10), and lecturers (master’s plus pedagogy, teaching upper secondary levels). Higher education, starting after upper secondary school, requires a general university admissions certificate. Institutions comprise universities (offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhDs, including professional studies), university colleges (offering a range of degrees), and some private schools. Admission to competitive programs is grade-based, and the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) oversees quality assurance. The grading system varies across levels but typically uses numeric and letter scales. The academic year runs from mid-August to June with multiple holidays and two semesters. Special education is inclusive and integrated mostly in ordinary schools, supported by educational psychologists and specialists. Norwegian higher education institutions grant degrees autonomously and have evolved through a series of historical developments since the 18th century.
