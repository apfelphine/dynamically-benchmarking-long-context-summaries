**Technological Singularity**

The concept of the Technological Singularity refers to a hypothetical moment when some or all computers surpass human intelligence, a notion central to Singularitarianism. This event is often linked to an "intelligence explosion," where a self-improving Artificial Intelligence (AI) becomes so powerful that humans cannot halt its pursuit of its goals. Early discussions trace back to John von Neumann's speculation in 1958 about an essential singularity in technological progress, while I. J. Good formalized the idea of an "intelligence explosion" in 1965, postulating that an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines, leaving human intelligence far behind.

Vernor Vinge popularized the term "technological singularity" in 1983, suggesting it would mark the end of the human era as a superintelligence rapidly upgrades itself. Ray Kurzweil further popularized this concept with his 2005 book, *The Singularity Is Near*, predicting its arrival around 2045 based on the "law of accelerating returns." While proponents argue that this event could solve major human problems like disease and poverty, others express concern over existential risks, such as uncontrolled AI optimizing for unintended outcomes or leading to human extinction. The nature of this transition—whether it is a sudden "hard takeoff" or a slower "soft takeoff"—remains a subject of debate.
