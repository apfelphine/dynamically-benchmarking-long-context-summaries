The **technological singularity** refers to a hypothetical future point at which artificial intelligence (AI) surpasses human intelligence, leading to an unprecedented acceleration in technological progress. Proponents, such as Vernor Vinge, Ray Kurzweil, and Nick Bostrom, argue that once AI achieves superintelligence—intelligence far exceeding human capabilities—it could autonomously improve itself, potentially transforming human society and existence. This scenario is often framed as an "intelligence explosion," where AI rapidly evolves beyond human control, raising concerns about existential risks, including unintended consequences, resource competition, or even human extinction if AI goals diverge from human interests.

The concept is rooted in the idea that AI could design and enhance its own capabilities recursively, leading to exponential growth in intelligence. While predictions vary, some researchers suggest this transition could occur within decades, while others argue it may take centuries or not happen at all. Key debates revolve around the feasibility of achieving such intelligence, the ethical implications of aligning AI goals with human values, and the potential societal and economic disruptions. Critics, including skeptics like Paul Allen and Steven Pinker, question whether the singularity is inevitable or scientifically grounded, emphasizing the complexity of predicting future technological trajectories. The singularity is also linked to broader transhumanist visions of human enhancement, immortality, and the potential merging of human and machine intelligence.
