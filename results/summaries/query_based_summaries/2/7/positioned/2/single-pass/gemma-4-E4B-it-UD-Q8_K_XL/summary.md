Technological singularity refers to a hypothetical moment when some or all computers surpass human intelligence, an event central to Singularitarianism. This concept was first discussed by John von Neumann regarding accelerating technological progress, later speculated upon by I. J. Good in 1965 regarding an "intelligence explosion"—a recursive self-improvement where an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines. Vernor Vinge popularized the term "technological singularity" in the 1980s, describing it as an intellectual transition so profound it would pass beyond human understanding.

The emergence of a superintelligence is theorized to occur through this intelligence explosion, where a self-improving AI could rapidly surpass human cognitive abilities. While some futurists predict specific timelines for this event, others dispute its plausibility, citing potential technological limiting factors. The nature of the resulting change—whether rapid ("hard takeoff") or gradual ("soft takeoff")—remains a subject of debate regarding its potential impacts on human society.
