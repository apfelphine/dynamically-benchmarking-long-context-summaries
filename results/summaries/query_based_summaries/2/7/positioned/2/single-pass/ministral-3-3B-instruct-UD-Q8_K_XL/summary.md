The **technological singularity** refers to a hypothetical future point at which artificial intelligence (AI) surpasses human intelligence, leading to an exponential acceleration in technological progress. Proponents like Vernor Vinge, Ray Kurzweil, and I. J. Good argue that such an event could result in an "intelligence explosion," where AI rapidly self-improves, creating a superintelligent entity that fundamentally alters human existence. This concept envisions a scenario where AI achieves levels of intelligence beyond human comprehension, potentially leading to a shift in human capabilities, societal structures, and even the definition of what it means to be human.

The singularity is often associated with the idea of a **hard takeoff**, where AI rapidly surpasses human intelligence, or a **soft takeoff**, where the transition occurs more gradually, allowing for human oversight and alignment. Key predictions include the emergence of artificial general intelligence (AGI), which could solve complex problems, automate tasks, and even surpass human creativity and problem-solving abilities. However, the plausibility of the singularity remains debated, with critics arguing that technological progress has historically followed more predictable patterns and that the potential risks, such as unintended consequences or alignment problems, necessitate careful consideration. The singularity is also linked to broader transhumanist visions of human enhancement, immortality, and the potential for AI to redefine human identity and societal norms.
