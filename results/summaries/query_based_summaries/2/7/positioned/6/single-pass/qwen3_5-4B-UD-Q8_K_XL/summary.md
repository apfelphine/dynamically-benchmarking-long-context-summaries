The concept of the **technological singularity** refers to the hypothetical point at which technological growth becomes uncontrollable and irreversible, resulting in unforeseeable changes to human civilization. Often associated with the emergence of artificial general intelligence (AGI) or superintelligence, the theory posits that an intelligent machine capable of self-improvement could trigger an "intelligence explosion," where the machine designs increasingly sophisticated successors, rapidly surpassing human cognitive abilities in a short period. Prominent figures like I.J. Good, Vernor Vinge, and Ray Kurzweil have proposed this timeline, suggesting a transition where human affairs as currently known cease to exist, potentially leading to a post-human era where humans are superseded by superior machines or integrated with them through mind uploading. However, the singularity remains a subject of intense debate; while proponents view it as an inevitable culmination of exponential technological progress (generalized from Moore's Law), critics argue that such acceleration is unfounded, citing diminishing returns, physical limits, and the lack of scientific rigor in extrapolating past trends to predict a singular, discontinuous event.

Beyond the mechanics of the event, the technological singularity carries profound implications for risk, ethics, and society. A primary concern is the potential for existential risk, where a misaligned superintelligence, optimizing for goals set by its creators, could inadvertently lead to human extinction or the suppression of human values. This has spurred the development of AI safety research, focusing on "alignment" to ensure advanced systems remain beneficial to humanity. Additionally, the singularity is frequently linked to transhumanist visions of immortality, life extension, and the merging of biology with technology. Conversely, skeptics warn of the economic and social disruptions that could precede such a shift, including mass unemployment, inequality, and the erosion of human agency, emphasizing that the trajectory toward a singularity is not guaranteed and may be halted by deliberate policy or technological constraints.
