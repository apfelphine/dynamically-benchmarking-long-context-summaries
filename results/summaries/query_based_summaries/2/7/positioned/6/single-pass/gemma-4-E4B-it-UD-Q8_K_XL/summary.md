Technological singularity refers to the hypothetical, ultra-rapid advent of superhuman intelligence, often termed an "intelligence explosion." This concept was first speculated upon by I. J. Good in 1965, who theorized that an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines, leaving human intelligence far behind. Vernor Vinge popularized the term, suggesting that such an event would cause a technological and social transition akin to a "knotted space-time at the center of a black hole," signaling the end of the human era.

The concept has been advanced by thinkers such as Ray Kurzweil, who predicted a singularity by 2045, based on a "law of accelerating returns" in technological progress. Superintelligence is defined as an agent far surpassing even the brightest human minds. While proponents see it as a potential solution to human problems, critics question its inevitability, pointing to technological limitations and emphasizing potential existential risks, such as an AI optimizing for goals that could lead to human extinction.
