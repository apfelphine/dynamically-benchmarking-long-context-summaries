The **technological singularity** refers to a hypothetical future point at which artificial intelligence (AI) surpasses human intelligence to the point of fundamentally altering human society. Proponents like Vernor Vinge, Ray Kurzweil, and I. J. Good argue that once an AI achieves superintelligence—intelligence far exceeding human capabilities—it could recursively self-improve, leading to an "intelligence explosion" that accelerates technological progress exponentially. This scenario could result in a rapid transformation of human civilization, potentially rendering human intelligence obsolete or merging it with AI.

Key debates center on the feasibility and timing of this event. Some predict it within decades, citing exponential growth in computing power and AI advancements, while skeptics argue that current technological trends do not support such rapid progress. Concerns include the potential for AI to become uncontrollable, leading to unintended consequences such as human extinction, or to disrupt societal structures by automating jobs, altering economic systems, and reshaping human identity. The singularity is also linked to broader transhumanist visions of technological enhancement, immortality, and the merging of human and machine intelligence. Philosophical and ethical questions arise regarding the nature of consciousness, the implications of AI alignment with human values, and the moral responsibilities of those developing such technologies.
