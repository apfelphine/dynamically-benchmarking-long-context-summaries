The concept of **technological singularity** refers to a hypothetical future point at which artificial intelligence (AI) surpasses human intelligence to the point of fundamentally altering human existence. Proponents, such as Vernor Vinge, I. J. Good, and Ray Kurzweil, argue that this transition would involve an "intelligence explosion"—where AI recursively improves itself exponentially, leading to superintelligence far beyond human capabilities. This could result in rapid technological advancements, societal transformations, and even the end of human dominance as we know it.

The singularity is often linked to the idea of **superintelligence**, an AI that surpasses human cognitive abilities, potentially enabling it to design and optimize its own systems autonomously. This could lead to radical changes in fields like medicine, economics, and governance, though the exact nature of these changes remains speculative. Concerns about unintended consequences, such as loss of human control or existential risks, have prompted debates on ethical alignment, safety, and governance of AI. While some predict the singularity will occur within decades, others argue it may never materialize due to technical, philosophical, or societal barriers. The debate centers on whether humanity can safely navigate this transition or if it will inevitably lead to unforeseen disruptions.
