## Technological Singularity

The technological singularity is a hypothesized event characterized by a rapid, potentially incomprehensible acceleration of technological progress, often tied to the advent of superhuman artificial intelligence. Early discussions trace back to John von Neumann's observations of accelerating technological change. Key figures like I. J. Good posited an "intelligence explosion" where a machine could recursively improve itself, leading to intelligence far surpassing human capabilities, making the first ultraintelligent machine the final invention humans need to make.

This concept was popularized by Vernor Vinge, who used the term "singularity" to describe an intellectual transition beyond human understanding following the creation of superintelligence. Ray Kurzweil predicts this singularity by 2045, based on the "law of accelerating returns," where exponential growth in technology and medical advances allows humanity to transcend biological limitations. While proponents argue its inevitability through trend extrapolation, critics dispute the exponential nature of the growth or warn of existential risks stemming from uncontrolled superintelligence.
