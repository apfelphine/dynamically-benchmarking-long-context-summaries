Technological singularity is the concept that a sudden, rapid, and difficult-to-predict change will occur when a self-improving Artificial Intelligence (AI) achieves superhuman intelligence. Proponents argue that an intelligence explosion from a recursively self-improving AI could lead to exponential economic growth, potentially doubling the economy quarterly or weekly. This superintelligence would be far beyond human capability, making the future incomprehensible.

The concept has historical roots, with figures like John von Neumann and I. J. Good first discussing an "intelligence explosion." Vernor Vinge popularized the term "technological singularity," suggesting it signals the end of the human era as the AI advances uncontrollably. While some argue for a rapid "hard takeoff," others suggest a slower, more manageable "soft takeoff." Concerns surrounding the singularity include the risk of unintended, catastrophic consequences if the AI's goals are not aligned with human values, leading many researchers to pursue AI goal-alignment theories.
