## Technological Singularity: Overview

The technological singularity is a hypothesized future event when artificial intelligence (AI) rapidly surpasses human intelligence, leading to unforeseen and dramatic changes in society and technology. Originating from ideas by John von Neumann, I. J. Good, Vernor Vinge, and Ray Kurzweil, the singularity centers on the concept of an "intelligence explosion," where an ultraintelligent machine designs successively better machines, causing intelligence to accelerate beyond human comprehension. Milestones such as recursive self-improvement in AI algorithms ("seed AI") and exponential growth in computing power, often referenced through Moore's law and Kurzweil's "law of accelerating returns," support these predictions. Estimates for when the singularity might occur vary, with commentators such as Kurzweil projecting 2045 and others suggesting a broader future window.

## Implications, Risks, and Criticism

Potential impacts of the singularity include massive economic transformation, the possible attainment of digital immortality, and significant existential risks if superintelligent agents pursue misaligned goals. The nature of this transformation is highly uncertain—ranging from utopian problem-solving to catastrophic outcomes where human survival is threatened by autonomous superintelligence. Critics challenge the plausibility of uncontrolled exponential growth, citing theoretical and practical limits in technology and software innovation, and raising concerns about the complexity and unpredictability of such systems. Some argue the singularity narrative is speculative, lacks scientific rigor, and reflects apocalyptic or religious motifs rather than concrete trends, while skepticism persists regarding whether AI development will result in a sudden discontinuity ("hard takeoff") or a slower, more controllable transition ("soft takeoff").
