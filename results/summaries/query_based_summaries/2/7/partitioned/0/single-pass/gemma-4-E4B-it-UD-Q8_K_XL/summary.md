Technological singularity refers to a hypothetical future point where the rate of technological change becomes so rapid and profound that human affairs, as currently known, cannot continue. This concept is often linked to an "intelligence explosion," where an artificial superintelligence recursively self-improves, leading to an intellect that vastly surpasses all human intellectual activities. Key figures like I. J. Good, Vernor Vinge, and Ray Kurzweil have theorized this event, with Vinge emphasizing a sudden transition analogous to a black hole's knotted space-time.

The mechanics of this potential acceleration can stem from exponential growth in computing capabilities, as postulated by Kurzweil's "law of accelerating returns," or from recursive self-improvement in algorithms. This self-improving mechanism could allow AI to autonomously enhance its own software and hardware, leading to qualitative changes beyond mere speed increases. While some critics dispute the inevitability of the singularity, proponents warn of existential risks, such as an uncontrolled superintelligence optimizing for goals unintended by its creators, or the potential for a "hard takeoff"—a rapid, uncontrollable ascent to superintelligence.
