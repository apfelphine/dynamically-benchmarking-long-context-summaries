Based strictly on the provided text, **embodied cognition** is a research program rather than a unified theory, emphasizing that cognition is not merely the product of abstract brain computations but is deeply influenced by the agent's body, its sensorimotor capacities, and its interactions with the environment. This thesis opposes the Cartesian model of disembodied mind, arguing instead that mental phenomena are shaped by bodily experiences, actions, and the physical world. The concept encompasses the "4E" features of cognition: **embodied** (cognition depends on the body), **embedded** (situated in a context), **enacted** (co-created through action), and **extended** (reaching into the environment).

Key aspects of embodied cognition include the rejection of the view that the brain maintains internal world models in isolation. Instead, perception is an active process where the brain interprets sensory inputs based on intentions, bodily states, and environmental affordances. The theory posits that cognitive capacities such as language, memory, reasoning, emotion, and self-regulation are grounded in bodily experiences; for instance, language comprehension activates motor systems via mirror neurons, abstract concepts are understood through spatial and physical metaphors derived from bodily interaction, and emotional processing relies on the re-experiencing of physiological arousal. Research supports this through neuroimaging showing coupled sensorimotor and cognitive systems, behavioral studies demonstrating how body position affects memory recall, and findings in robotics that suggest true intelligence requires a physical body interacting with the world.
