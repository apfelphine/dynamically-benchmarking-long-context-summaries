**Embodied Cognition**

Embodied cognition is a broad research program, rather than a unified theory, that posits that an agent's cognition is significantly shaped by the active role of its body, extending beyond mere abstract brain representations. This thesis opposes the disembodied Cartesian model by reintroducing bodily experiences into cognitive accounts. The enactive approach highlights that cognition relies on the sensorimotor capacities derived from having a body, which is itself situated within a broader biological, psychological, and cultural context.

The concept can be viewed narrowly as the deep dependence of cognitive features—such as perception, attention, and memory—upon the physical characteristics of an agent's body. This perspective suggests that cognition emerges from the interaction between the agent and its environment, where knowledge acquisition is a step toward guiding behavior, rather than just building accurate representations. This has led to the concept of 4E cognition (embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended), emphasizing that cognition is shaped by dynamic interactions between the brain, body, and both physical and social environments.
