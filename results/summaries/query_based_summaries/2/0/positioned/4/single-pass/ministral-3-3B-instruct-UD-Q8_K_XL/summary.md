Embodied cognition posits that cognition is fundamentally shaped by an agent's bodily experiences, interactions with the environment, and sensorimotor capacities. Unlike the Cartesian model, which isolates cognition from the body, embodied cognition emphasizes that mental processes are deeply intertwined with physical and sensory-motor activities. This perspective challenges traditional views by asserting that understanding cognition requires examining how an agent’s body interacts with its surroundings, influencing perception, memory, reasoning, and even abstract thought.

Key tenets include the idea that cognitive functions like perception, language, and memory are not merely abstract mental processes but are grounded in bodily experiences and sensorimotor engagement. For instance, language comprehension involves motor simulations, and spatial reasoning relies on embodied spatial experiences. Research in embodied cognition has expanded across disciplines, revealing that cognitive processes are influenced by the body’s physical constraints, affordances, and interactions with the environment. This approach has implications for fields like neuroscience, robotics, and education, offering new ways to conceptualize intelligence and human-machine interaction.
