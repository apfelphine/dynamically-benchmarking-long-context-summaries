Embodied cognition posits that an agent's cognition is not solely the result of abstract representations within the brain, but is profoundly shaped by the agent's body and its interactions with the environment. This thesis challenges the disembodied Cartesian model by reintegrating bodily experiences into accounts of cognition. Specifically, the enactive approach highlights two aspects of embodiment: cognition depends on the sensorimotor capacities derived from having a body, and these individual sensorimotor capacities are embedded within a broader biological, psychological, and cultural context.

A narrower view of embodiment specifies that many cognitive features are deeply dependent on the agent's physical body, meaning the beyond-the-brain body plays a significant causal role in cognitive processing. This perspective suggests that cognition emerges from the dynamic relationship between an agent and the affordances of its environment, rather than being an end in itself. This shift has led to the concept of "4E features of cognition" (embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended), indicating that cognition is shaped by dynamic interactions involving the brain, body, and both physical and social surroundings.
