Proponents of embodied cognition emphasize the critical and active role the body plays in shaping an agent's understanding and cognitive capacities. This thesis posits that cognition is not merely a product of abstract, disembodied mental representations, contrasting with the Cartesian model. The enactive approach defines "embodiment" as cognition depending on bodily experiences derived from sensorimotor capacities, which are themselves situated within a broader biological, psychological, and cultural context.

Narrower views specify embodied cognition as features deeply dependent on the agent's physical body, meaning the body plays a causal or constitutive role in processes like perception, attention, memory, and reasoning. This perspective suggests that perception is an active process influenced by the agent's intentions, emotions, and bodily states, rather than a passive reception of sensory input. Applications of this theory show its influence across various fields, including education (favoring embodied learning), robotics (leading to situated and embodied AI), and clinical settings, where bodily experience is integral to cognitive function.
