Embodied cognition is a broad research program that posits that an agent's cognition is not solely the result of abstract, disembodied brain representations, but is fundamentally shaped by the agent's body and its interactions with the environment. This thesis opposes the Cartesian view of mind-body separation by reintroducing the significance of bodily experiences into cognitive accounts. A narrower view of embodied cognition suggests that many cognitive features, such as perception, attention, memory, and reasoning, are deeply dependent on the physical characteristics of the agent's body, where the beyond-the-brain body plays a significant causal role in cognitive processing.

This perspective has wide-ranging applications across various disciplines. In education, embodied learning incorporates physical movement, gestures, and tangible manipulatives to improve conceptual understanding. In robotics and AI, it drives the development of situated robotic perspectives, creating more dynamic systems that perform better in complex, unpredictable environments by integrating perception and physical action. Furthermore, embodied cognition informs theories of emotion, suggesting that emotional re-experience involves a partial multimodal reenactment within the originally implicated sensory-motor systems, and it links language comprehension to the simulation of actions described by words.
