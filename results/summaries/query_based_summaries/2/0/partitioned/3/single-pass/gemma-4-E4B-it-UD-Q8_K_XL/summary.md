Embodied cognition posits that an organism's sensory and motor systems are dynamically integrated, challenging the notion that cognition is solely an internal, disembodied process. This theory highlights that an agent's cognition is strongly influenced by the characteristics of its physical body and its active interactions with the environment. Key concepts include sensorimotor contingencies (SMCs), which state that the quality of perception is determined by how sensory information changes when one acts in the world. Furthermore, the enactive approach suggests that cognition depends on the kinds of experience derived from having a body with various sensorimotor capacities, embedded within biological, psychological, and cultural contexts.

The implications of embodied cognition are wide-ranging across various fields. In learning, the action-based language theory suggests that motor control mechanisms are exploited for language acquisition, while gestures can aid in learning and reasoning. Emotion processing is also viewed through this lens, with theories like James–Lange suggesting that physiological arousal shapes emotional experience. In social cognition, bodily states and nonverbal synchrony influence how individuals interact and experience others' perspectives. Moreover, embodied artificial intelligence (AI) integrates sensorimotor skills, moving research away from purely algorithm-driven approaches toward systems that interact with the physical world via a body.
