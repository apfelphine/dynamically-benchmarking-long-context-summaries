The Chinese room thought experiment questions whether a computer merely simulates understanding or actually possesses a mind. In the setup, a person in a room follows a program, taking Chinese characters as input and outputting Chinese characters, behaving as if it understands the language perfectly. Searle argues that even if the computer passes the Turing test, the person in the room—who is only manipulating symbols syntactically without knowledge of their semantics (meaning)—would not understand Chinese. Therefore, he concludes that the computer also lacks genuine understanding and, consequently, does not have a mind in the human sense, invalidating the strong AI hypothesis.

Replies to the Chinese room include the "system reply," which posits that the entire collection of the man, the program, and the materials constitutes the understanding, and the "virtual mind reply," which suggests a simulated mind can exist within a computer. Other responses attempt to ground meaning by connecting the symbols to the real world, such as through a "robot reply" that allows for a causal connection between symbols and objects. Searle counters these by maintaining that no matter the complexity or external connections, the person inside the room remains only manipulating meaningless symbols, asserting that syntax is insufficient for semantics.
