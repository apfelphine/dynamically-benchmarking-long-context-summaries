The **Chinese Room** thought experiment, proposed by philosopher John Searle, challenges the idea of **strong AI** by arguing that a computer simulating human understanding through symbolic manipulation lacks genuine comprehension. In the scenario, a person inside a room follows a set of instructions (a program) to manipulate Chinese symbols, producing Chinese responses indistinguishable from a human. Searle contends that, despite the machine’s ability to pass the **Turing Test** (where a human judge cannot distinguish it from a human), it does not truly understand the language—it merely follows rules without semantic meaning. His core argument hinges on the distinction between **syntax** (the formal structure of symbols) and **semantics** (their meaning), asserting that understanding requires a biological mind capable of intentionality and causal powers, not mere computational processing.

Searle’s critique is rooted in his **biological naturalism**, which posits that consciousness and understanding depend on specific physical–chemical properties of human brains. He argues that even if a machine mimics human behavior, it lacks the necessary **causal powers** to generate subjective experience or intentionality, thereby denying it a mind in the same sense as humans. His thought experiment serves as a philosophical counterpoint to functionalist and computational theories of mind, emphasizing that intelligence cannot be reduced to symbolic manipulation alone.
