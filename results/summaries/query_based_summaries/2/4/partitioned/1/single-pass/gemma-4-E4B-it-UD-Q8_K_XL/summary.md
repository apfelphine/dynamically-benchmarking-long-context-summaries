The Chinese Room argument, primarily in the philosophy of mind, posits a thought experiment where a person inside a room, who speaks no Chinese, manipulates Chinese symbols according to a detailed rulebook (program). By following these syntactic rules, the person produces outputs that are indistinguishable from a native Chinese speaker, effectively passing the Turing test. Searle argues that despite the successful external behavior, the person inside does not possess actual understanding or "intentionality" regarding the symbols, leading to the conclusion that the computer running the program also lacks a genuine mind.

This argument challenges Strong AI, which posits that a correctly programmed computer would possess a mind identical to a human's. Critics offer various responses, including the "systems reply," which claims the entire collection of parts forms a mind, and the "virtual mind reply," suggesting the mind exists as a virtual entity. Other critiques address Searle's assumptions, such as questioning the reliance on personal experience of consciousness or suggesting that the room's symbols derive meaning from external context or a robot's physical interaction with the world.
