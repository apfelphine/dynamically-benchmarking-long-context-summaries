The **Chinese room** thought experiment, proposed by philosopher John Searle in 1980, challenges the idea of strong AI by arguing that a machine can simulate understanding without actually comprehending. In the experiment, a person inside a room manipulates symbols according to a program written in a foreign language (e.g., Chinese), producing responses indistinguishable from a native speaker. Searle contends that, despite the machine’s ability to pass a Turing test (where an interrogator cannot distinguish it from a human), it lacks genuine understanding—it merely follows rules without possessing intentionality or consciousness. This thought experiment critiques functionalism and computational theories of mind, suggesting that understanding requires biological or neural processes, not just symbolic manipulation. The debate surrounding the Chinese room revolves around whether a machine can truly "think" or merely simulate it, highlighting fundamental questions about the nature of intelligence, consciousness, and the limits of artificial systems.
