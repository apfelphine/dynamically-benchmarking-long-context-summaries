The Chinese room thought experiment, introduced by John Searle, challenges the functionalist and computational theory of mind, particularly as it relates to the Turing test. The scenario involves a person in a room who operates a program using Chinese characters as input and outputs, perfectly simulating understanding without actually comprehending Chinese. Searle argues that because the person only follows formal rules (syntax) without understanding the meaning of the symbols (semantics), the system cannot truly "think" or possess a "mind" in the human sense.

This argument leads Searle to conclude that the strong AI hypothesis—that a sufficiently programmed computer *is* a mind—is false. He posits "biological naturalism," asserting that genuine understanding and consciousness depend on the specific physical-chemical properties of actual human brains. Replies to the Chinese room include the "system reply," which claims the whole setup understands, and the "robot reply," which attempts to ground symbol meaning in physical interaction with the environment.
