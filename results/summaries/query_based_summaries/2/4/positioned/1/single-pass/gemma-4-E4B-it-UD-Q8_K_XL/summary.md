The Chinese room is a thought experiment proposed by John Searle to challenge the Strong AI hypothesis and the computational theory of mind. The scenario involves a person in a room who follows a program, using Chinese characters as input and producing Chinese characters as output, making the system appear to understand Chinese, even if the person in the room—who only understands English—does not actually grasp the meaning of the characters.

Searle argues that because the man in the room manipulates symbols according to formal rules (syntax) without understanding what the symbols represent (semantics), the computer running the program also lacks true understanding. Therefore, he concludes that syntax alone is insufficient to generate semantics, meaning a computer program, regardless of how well it simulates a mind, does not possess a mind in the same sense that human beings do. This directly challenges the notion that passing the Turing test is adequate proof of consciousness or intentionality.
