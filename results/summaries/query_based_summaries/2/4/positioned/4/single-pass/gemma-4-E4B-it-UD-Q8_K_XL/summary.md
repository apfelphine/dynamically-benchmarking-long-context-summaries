The "Chinese room" thought experiment, proposed by John Searle in 1980, questions whether a machine can truly understand language or merely simulate understanding. The scenario involves a person in an English-speaking room following a program step-by-step, taking Chinese characters as input and producing Chinese characters as output, such that the system appears to understand Chinese perfectly. Searle argues that because the person inside does not understand Chinese, the system does not understand it either, concluding that running a program that simulates a mind is not equivalent to actually having a mind.

Searle uses this to challenge the strong AI hypothesis, which asserts that a properly programmed computer would possess a mind identical to a human's. He distinguishes between **syntax** (symbol manipulation, which the room performs) and **semantics** (meaning or intentionality, which Searle claims the room lacks). Although the computer system has a design analogous to a "Turing complete" machine, Searle contends that symbol manipulation alone is insufficient to generate semantics, thus arguing that computers running programs cannot possess a mind in the human sense.
