The "Chinese room" thought experiment, proposed by John Searle in his 1980 paper *Minds, Brains, and Programs*, argues that a machine can perfectly simulate understanding a language, such as Chinese, by manipulating symbols according to a program, without actually understanding the meaning of those symbols. Searle conducts this thought experiment by imagining himself in a room with an English program and Chinese characters. By following the program's instructions, he produces correct Chinese output, making him appear to understand the conversation.

Searle's central conclusion is that because he, operating the program by hand, lacks genuine "understanding" or "intentionality," the computer executing the program cannot possess a mind in the same sense humans do. Therefore, he argues that the Turing test—which only assesses external behavior—is insufficient to prove that a machine is actually thinking or has a mind. This argument challenges the strong AI hypothesis, which asserts that a correctly programmed computer would possess a mind identical to a human's.
