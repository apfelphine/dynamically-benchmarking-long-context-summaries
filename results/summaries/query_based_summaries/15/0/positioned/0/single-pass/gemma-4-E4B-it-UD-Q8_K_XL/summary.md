The University of Melbourne was established by Act of Incorporation on January 22, 1853, following a proposal made in November 1852, and initially conferred degrees in arts, medicine, laws, and music. Its foundation was supported by Victoria's gold rush wealth, and classes commenced in 1855. Over time, the university expanded its academic offerings, establishing a law school in 1857, followed by faculties of Engineering (1861) and Medicine (1862). In 1881, women were admitted, with Julia 'Bella' Guerin graduating in 1883 as the first woman to graduate from an Australian University.

In the 2008 restructuring, Vice Chancellor Glyn Davis introduced the "Melbourne Model," which replaced traditional specialist undergraduate degrees with a two-degree undergraduate/graduate structure. Expansion continued through amalgamations in the 1980s and 1990s, and in 2001, the Melbourne School of Population Health was established. The university operates through various campuses, including Parkville (the primary campus), Burnley, Southbank, Werribee, and regional campuses like Creswick and Dookie. Governance is grounded in the University of Melbourne Act 2009, with the Council as the peak governing body.
