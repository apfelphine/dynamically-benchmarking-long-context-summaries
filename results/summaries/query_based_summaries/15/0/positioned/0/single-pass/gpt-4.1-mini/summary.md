The University of Melbourne, established in 1853, is Australia's second-oldest university, founded with funding from the Victorian government amid the gold rush prosperity. Located primarily at its historic Parkville campus north of Melbourne's CBD, the university has expanded to several metropolitan and regional campuses dedicated to specialized disciplines such as horticulture (Burnley), performing arts (Southbank), veterinary science (Werribee), and agriculture (Dookie and Creswick). The institution underwent significant growth and restructuring in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, notably introducing the "Melbourne Model" in 2008, which replaced specialist undergraduate degrees with broader generalist degrees followed by graduate specialization.

Governed by a 13-member council and an academic board, the university comprises nine faculties including Arts, Science, Law, Medicine, Engineering, and Business. It holds an endowment of approximately $1.335 billion and leads Australian research expenditure after CSIRO. The University of Melbourne is highly ranked globally and nationally across various subject areas, with strong graduate employment outcomes and employer satisfaction. Student life is enriched by over 200 clubs, multiple residential colleges, vibrant student unions, and comprehensive sports programs through Melbourne University Sport. Recent challenges include staff layoffs, casual workforce disputes, political student protests, and ongoing campus redevelopment projects, including a new engineering campus at Fishermans Bend planned to open by 2026.
