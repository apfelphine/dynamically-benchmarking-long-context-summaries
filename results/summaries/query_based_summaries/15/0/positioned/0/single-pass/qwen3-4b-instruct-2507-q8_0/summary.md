The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 by an Act of Incorporation, initially offering degrees in arts, medicine, law, and music. It began with 16 students and 3 professors, including the first chancellor, Redmond Barry. The university expanded through the 20th century, establishing faculties in engineering, agriculture, and dentistry, and became a leader in research. In the 1980s and 1990s, it underwent significant expansion through mergers, including the College of Advanced Education and the Victorian College of the Arts. In 2001, the Melbourne School of Population Health was established. In 2008, the "Melbourne Model" was introduced, replacing traditional degrees with a two-degree structure, which sparked controversy. The university has faced criticism over staff layoffs, student welfare, and financial practices, including a 2020 job cut and a 2023 protest over investments in Israeli companies. It has three campuses in metropolitan Melbourne—Parkville, Southbank, and Burnley—with additional campuses in regional Victoria. The university has a strong research profile, with a large endowment and high global rankings, and is known for its academic reputation, student outcomes, and diverse student body.
