The University of Melbourne is one of Australia's oldest and most prestigious research universities, established in 1853 following a proposal by Finance Minister Hugh Childers and enabled by the gold rush in Victoria. Its history includes significant expansions, such as the amalgamation of tertiary colleges in the late 20th century, which increased its student population to over 35,000. A major structural change occurred in 2008 under Vice-Chancellor Glyn Davis with the introduction of the "Melbourne Model," replacing specialist undergraduate degrees with a two-stage system of generalist degrees followed by specialized graduate courses, a move that faced criticism from staff and students. The university operates across multiple campuses in metropolitan Melbourne (Parkville, Southbank, Burnley, Werribee) and regional Victoria (Creswick, Dookie, Shepparton), offering a diverse range of faculties including Engineering, Law, Medicine, and the Arts. It maintains a substantial endowment of approximately $1.335 billion and is consistently ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally.

Recent years have seen further administrative restructuring and workforce challenges. In 2013–2015, Vice-Chancellor Davis implemented the "Business Improvement Program," which resulted in significant job cuts and a shift toward casualized employment, with reports indicating that a majority of the 11,000 employees were on casual contracts by 2023. This period also saw controversies regarding workplace culture and high executive salaries. In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the university faced financial pressures and staff redundancies, including the axing of 450 staff in 2020 despite planned capital works. Governance has been subject to scrutiny, including an investigation into privacy breaches by the use of surveillance technology to identify protesters during the 2024 Gaza conflict protests. As of 2025, Emma Johnston has been appointed the university's first female Vice-Chancellor, succeeding Duncan Maskell.
