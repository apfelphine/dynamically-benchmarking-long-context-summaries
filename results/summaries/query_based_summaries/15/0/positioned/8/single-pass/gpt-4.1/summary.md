## University of Melbourne – Summary

The University of Melbourne, established in 1853, is a leading Australian research university located primarily on its historic Parkville campus in Melbourne, Victoria. Founded with significant government support following the Victorian gold rush, it rapidly expanded to include key faculties such as law, engineering, and medicine. The university later embraced residential colleges, opened its doors to women in 1881, and diversified academically throughout the 20th century, absorbing various colleges and affording a broad spectrum of disciplines. The implementation of the "Melbourne Model" in 2008 restructured its curriculum, moving from specialised undergraduate degrees to generalist degrees followed by graduate professional or research programs.

Today, the University of Melbourne encompasses numerous metropolitan and regional campuses—such as Burnley, Southbank, Dookie, Creswick, Shepparton, Werribee, and the upcoming Fishermans Bend campus—each specialising in areas like horticulture, arts, music, veterinary science, forestry, and agriculture. Governance rests with a University Council and an Academic Board, underpinned by legislative frameworks. The university comprises nine faculties, houses vast library and museum collections, and sustains a $1.3 billion endowment. Renowned for its research output, it consistently leads national and global rankings, admitting high-achieving students, including significant international cohorts. Student life is vibrant, with over 200 clubs, residential colleges, sporting achievements, and media outlets such as Farrago. Notable alumni include multiple Nobel laureates, several Australian prime ministers, and industry leaders, with Emma Johnston set to become its first female Vice-Chancellor in 2025.
