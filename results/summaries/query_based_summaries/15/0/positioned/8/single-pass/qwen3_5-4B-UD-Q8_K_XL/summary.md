The Federal University of Rio de Janeiro was established on September 7, 1920, through Law Decree 14343, evolving from the "University of Rio de Janeiro." It serves as a direct descendant of Brazil's first higher education courses, tracing its roots back to institutions founded in the 18th and 19th centuries. The university underwent significant restructuring, including a name change to "University of Brazil" in 1937 under a government reform aimed at standardizing national higher education. It achieved financial, didactic, and academic autonomy in 1965, adopting its current name and the standard for federal university naming conventions.

UFRJ is an autarchy linked to the Ministry of Education and is organized into six academic centers, including the Center of Health Sciences, the Center of Technology, and the Center of Law and Economic Sciences. It operates multiple campuses in Rio de Janeiro, including the main College City on Ilha do Fundão and the Praia Vermelha campus, along with satellite locations in Macaé, Duque de Caxias, and other regions. The university is renowned for its extensive medical network, comprising nine hospitals such as the Clementino Fraga Filho University Hospital, and its robust research output, highlighted by the motto "In a university, one teaches because one researches," attributed to scientist Carlos Chagas Filho. It boasts a diverse student body and a history of producing notable alumni in various fields, including Oscar Niemeyer, Vital Brazil, and Carlos Chagas.
