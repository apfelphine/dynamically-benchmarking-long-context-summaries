The University of Melbourne is a leading public research university in Australia, established in 1853 following a proposal by Hugh Childers. Its foundations were laid with an endowment of £9,001 and a special grant of £20,000, with classes commencing in 1855. Over the years, the university has expanded significantly through the amalgamation of tertiary colleges, such as the Melbourne Teachers' College in 1988 and the Victorian College of the Arts in 1992, leading to a student population exceeding 35,000. A major restructuring in 2008, known as the "Melbourne Model," replaced traditional specialist degrees with a two-degree undergraduate/graduate structure, though this change was controversial among staff and students. The university's endowment is approximately $1.335 billion, and it has consistently ranked highly nationally and internationally, placing 1st in Australia in the 2024 Aggregate Ranking of Top Universities.

Campuses are distributed across metropolitan Melbourne and regional Victoria, including the primary Parkville campus, the creative arts-focused Southbank campus, and the horticultural Burnley campus. Additional campuses in Creswick (forestry), Dookie (agriculture), and Shepparton (rural health) support specialized research and teaching. The university operates a $2 billion capital works program, with a new engineering campus at Fishermans Bend planned for opening in 2026. Governance is overseen by a 13-member Council chaired by the Chancellor, with the Vice-Chancellor serving as the chief executive. Notable figures associated with the institution include Nobel laureates such as Elizabeth Blackburn, Sir James Mirrlees, and Sir John Eccles, as well as prominent alumni like Prime Minister Julia Gillard and actress Cate Blanchett.
