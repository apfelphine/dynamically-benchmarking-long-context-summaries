## University of Melbourne – Summary

The University of Melbourne, established in 1853, is Australia's second-oldest university. It originated from a government initiative to promote higher education, with classes beginning in 1855 and the foundation stone laid in 1854. The university rapidly expanded its academic offerings and infrastructure through the late 19th and early 20th centuries, becoming a pioneer in admitting women (first graduate in 1883) and adding faculties such as law, engineering, medicine, dentistry, and agriculture. Growth continued via the amalgamation of other tertiary colleges throughout the 1980s–2000s, with student numbers exceeding 35,000. In 2008, it adopted the "Melbourne Model," shifting from specialized undergraduate degrees to a North American-inspired structure of generalist undergraduate degrees followed by specialized graduate programs. This model, along with other significant structural reforms, led to ongoing debates around student experience and employment conditions.

The University of Melbourne comprises multiple metropolitan and regional campuses, with Parkville as the main site. Its academic organization includes nine faculties covering architecture, arts, business and economics, education, engineering, law, medicine, fine arts, and science. The university leads in research—claiming the second-largest research expenditure in Australia—and is affiliated with more than 100 research centers. It consistently ranks as Australia's top university and among the world's top 40 in global rankings. Student life features over 200 clubs, strong arts and sporting programs, and residential colleges. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, prime ministers, scientists, artists, and public intellectuals. The most recent era has been defined by large building projects, social activism, workforce restructuring, and the appointment of its first female vice-chancellor, Emma Johnston, in 2024.
