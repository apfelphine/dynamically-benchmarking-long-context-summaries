## University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 following a proposal by Hugh Childers, supported by government funding and initial buildings opened in 1855. It began with faculties in arts, medicine, law, and later expanded to engineering, education, dentistry, agriculture, and veterinary sciences, among others. The university underwent significant expansions throughout the 20th century, including amalgamations with teachers' colleges and the Victorian College of the Arts. In 2008, the "Melbourne Model" was introduced, restructuring undergraduate offerings from specialist degrees to generalist bachelor's degrees followed by graduate specializations, a move that drew both support and criticism. Recent decades have seen continued campus development, workforce restructuring, and challenges linked to casual staff employment, as well as responses to COVID-19 with shifts to online learning and staff layoffs.

The university operates multiple campuses primarily in metropolitan Melbourne, with the Parkville campus serving as the main site featuring historic Gothic revival architecture and modern facilities. Other campuses include Southbank for the arts, Burnley for horticulture, and regional sites at Creswick, Dookie, Shepparton, and Werribee, specializing in forestry, agriculture, rural health, and veterinary science respectively. A new engineering campus at Fishermans Bend is planned for 2026.

Governance is managed by a Council chaired by a Chancellor, with academic oversight by an Academic Board, and leadership by a Vice-Chancellor. The university comprises nine faculties spanning architecture, arts, business, education, engineering, fine arts, law, medicine, and science. It has a strong research profile, with significant funding, numerous research centers, and a global reputation as one of Australia’s leading research universities.

Student life is vibrant, supported by the University of Melbourne Student Union (UMSU) and the Graduate Student Association (GSA), with over 200 student clubs and extensive sports programs overseen by Melbourne University Sport. Nine affiliated residential colleges and six university-owned halls offer accommodation, alongside suite-style housing options. Alumni include Nobel laureates and prominent figures across politics, sciences, arts, and business, such as multiple Australian prime ministers, scientists, and cultural icons. The university is consistently ranked as Australia's top or among the top national universities by various global ranking agencies.
