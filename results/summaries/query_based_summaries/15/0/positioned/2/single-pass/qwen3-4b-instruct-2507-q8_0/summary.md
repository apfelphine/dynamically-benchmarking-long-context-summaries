The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 by a proposal from Hugh Childers, with the first chancellor being Redmond Barry. The university expanded significantly in the 20th century, establishing faculties in law, medicine, and engineering, and later forming a new "Melbourne Model" curriculum in 2008, which replaced traditional degrees with a two-degree structure. It has faced controversies over student welfare, including mental health issues and a 2020 job cut of 450 staff, and has been involved in protests over investments in Israeli companies. The university operates multiple campuses, including the main Parkville campus, and has a strong research profile, with a large endowment and a focus on innovation. It is known for producing notable alumni in politics, science, and business, including Julia Gillard, Peter Singer, and David Warren. The university has a diverse student body and a strong commitment to student life, with residential colleges, student unions, and a range of student activities.
