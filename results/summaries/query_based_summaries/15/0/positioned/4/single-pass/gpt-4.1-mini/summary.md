## University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 following a budget proposal by Hugh Childers, with classes commencing in 1855. It began with degrees in arts, medicine, laws, and music, expanding over time to include fields like engineering and dentistry. Notably, it admitted its first female graduate in 1883. The university grew significantly through the 20th century, absorbing several tertiary colleges and expanding its campuses, including affiliations with the Victorian College of the Arts and the introduction of the Melbourne Model in 2008—a restructure replacing specialized undergraduate degrees with broad generalist ones followed by professional or research graduate degrees. The model sparked controversy amid staff and student criticism.

In recent years, the University of Melbourne has undergone administrative restructures, capital works, and faced workforce tensions including layoffs and protests related to casual staff pay and governance. It is planning a $2 billion engineering campus in Fishermans Bend to open in 2026. The university comprises multiple campuses: the main Parkville campus with historic Gothic revival buildings, Southbank (arts), Burnley (horticulture), Creswick (forest science), Dookie (agriculture), Shepparton (rural health), and Werribee (veterinary science). Governance is led by a 13-member council chaired by a chancellor, with academic activities overseen by the Academic Board.

Academically, it has nine faculties including Arts, Business and Economics, Engineering and IT, Law, Medicine, Fine Arts, Science, and a graduate school. Its library system holds over 3.5 million items, and it maintains several museums and galleries, such as the Grainger Museum and the Ian Potter Museum of Art. The university is highly research-intensive, with substantial federal research funding and more than 100 research centers.

Recent rankings place it as Australia’s top university and consistently within the top 20–40 globally across major ranking systems. Admissions are highly competitive, with a median undergraduate ATAR above 94, and international students make up 44% of the student body. The university supports a wide range of student activities through two official unions, Melbourne University Student Union (UMSU) and the Graduate Student Association (GSA), over 200 clubs, and Melbourne University Sport overseeing diverse athletic programs. It affiliates with nine independent residential colleges and several halls of residence. The university boasts numerous notable alumni, including multiple Australian prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and highly prominent figures in arts, science, politics, and business.
