## University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne was established by Act of Incorporation in 1853, with its foundation stone laid in 1854 and classes commencing in 1855. It began with faculties in arts, medicine, laws, and music, and expanded into engineering, dentistry, agriculture, and other disciplines by the early 20th century. The institution admitted women starting in the late 19th century and expanded through amalgamations with teachers' colleges and arts institutions during the 20th century. The university introduced the controversial "Melbourne Model" curriculum restructuring in 2008, shifting from specialized undergraduate degrees to broader generalist degrees followed by graduate specialization. It faced significant changes in governance, staff layoffs, and workforce casualization through the 2010s and 2020s, alongside infrastructure expansion including new campuses and buildings. Student activism and strikes have taken place as recently as 2024 regarding campus policies and investment transparency.

The university's main campus is located in Parkville, Melbourne, with additional campuses at Southbank (arts and music), Burnley (horticulture), Creswick (forest science), Dookie (agriculture), Shepparton (rural health), Werribee (veterinary science), and a planned engineering campus at Fishermans Bend slated to open in 2026. The Parkville campus features a mix of Gothic revival and modernist architecture, multiple libraries, museums, and theatres, including the Grainger Museum and the Ian Potter Museum of Art. Academically, the University of Melbourne comprises nine faculties covering fields from architecture, arts, business, education, engineering, fine arts, law, medicine, to science. It maintains a strong research profile with significant expenditures and numerous affiliated research centers. In global university rankings (2024–2026), it is consistently placed as Australia's top university, ranking within the top 40 internationally. Its student body includes a high proportion of international students, and admission is highly competitive, with median ATAR around 94. Student life comprises active student unions, numerous clubs and societies, residential colleges, and extensive sports programs overseen by Melbourne University Sport. The university has produced many Nobel laureates and prominent alumni in diverse fields such as politics, science, arts, and business.
