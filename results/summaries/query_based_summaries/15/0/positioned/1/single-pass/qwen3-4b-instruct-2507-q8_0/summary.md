The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 as a "civilising influence" during Victoria's gold rush, with its first degrees awarded in 1855. It expanded through amalgamations and curriculum reforms, notably adopting the "Melbourne Model" in 2008, which replaced traditional specialist degrees with a two-degree structure. The university has a large student population, with over 35,000 students, and is known for its research output, ranking among the top universities in Australia. It has faced controversies over staffing, student welfare, and political interference, including allegations of toxic workplace culture and underpayment of casual staff. The university operates three campuses in metropolitan Melbourne, including Parkville, Southbank, and Burnley, and is involved in significant capital works and expansions. It has a strong academic reputation, with high research funding and a diverse student body, and is ranked among the top universities globally. The university has a history of student activism and protests, particularly regarding investments and research ties, and has implemented reforms in response to these issues.
