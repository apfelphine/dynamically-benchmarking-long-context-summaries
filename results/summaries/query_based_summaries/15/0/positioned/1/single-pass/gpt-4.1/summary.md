## University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne, established in 1853 in Victoria, Australia, is a leading public research university recognized for its historical significance and ongoing influence. Originating with three professors and sixteen students, the institution grew rapidly, introducing several faculties such as law, engineering, medicine, and dentistry over time. It has nine faculties covering major areas of research and teaching, and distinctive features include the Melbourne Model, a two-degree undergraduate/graduate structure introduced in 2008, which replaced traditional specialist degrees with generalist undergraduate degrees followed by graduate specialization. The university has expanded its presence, now operating multiple campuses in metropolitan Melbourne (Parkville, Burnley, Southbank, Werribee) and regional Victoria (Creswick, Dookie, Shepparton), including a major engineering-focused Fishermans Bend campus under development.

Melbourne is widely regarded as Australia's top university, consistently ranking highly in global university rankings (e.g., #19 QS, #37 THE, #38 ARWU in 2025/26). It boasts a diverse student body, with international students comprising 44% and strong employer satisfaction and graduate outcomes. Research output is a key strength, with high expenditure, numerous research centers and institutes, and extensive publication rates. The university's libraries house over 3.5 million items and multiple specialist museums and galleries, while student life is vibrant with active unions, over 300 clubs and societies, and nine affiliated residential colleges. Leadership has transitioned through prominent vice-chancellors and chancellors; notably, in September 2024, Emma Johnston was appointed as the university's first female Vice-Chancellor. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, Prime Ministers, and influential figures in arts, sciences, and public life. Recent years have been marked by major capital works, student activism, and workforce reforms.
