## University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne was established by Act of Incorporation in 1853, funded initially by gold rush wealth and inaugurated with three professors and sixteen students. It expanded early faculties in law, engineering, and medicine, and opened residential colleges affiliated with major Christian denominations. The university grew throughout the 20th century, incorporating other tertiary colleges and expanding campuses, notably adopting the “Melbourne Model” in 2008, which restructured undergraduate education into broad bachelor's degrees followed by graduate specialization. This model, inspired by North American and Bologna process systems, was met with controversy among staff and students. Administrative restructures and large capital works programs have continued into the 2010s and 2020s, including a new engineering campus planned for Fishermans Bend. The university has faced challenges including staff layoffs, casual workforce controversies, and student protests.

The university’s primary campus is Parkville, featuring historic Gothic revival architecture alongside modernist buildings, with extensive facilities including multiple libraries and cultural venues. Other campuses include Southbank (home to arts and music faculties), Burnley (horticulture), Creswick (forestry), Dookie (agriculture), Shepparton (rural health), and Werribee (veterinary). The university operates several faculties including Arts, Business and Economics, Engineering, Law, Medicine, and Science, totalling nine faculties. It is highly research-active and ranked among the top Australian and global universities, with a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary and international research centers. The student body is diverse, with high admission standards and significant international enrolment. Student life is vibrant with extensive clubs, unions, sport teams, and residential colleges. The university boasts numerous notable alumni, including Nobel laureates and Australian political leaders, and maintains a broad cultural tradition emphasizing academic excellence and social engagement.
