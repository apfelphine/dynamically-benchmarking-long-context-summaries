The University of Melbourne is Australia's oldest university, established in 1853, and operates across multiple campuses including Parkville (its primary location), Southbank, Burnley, Werribee, and regional sites in Creswick, Dookie, and Shepparton. The institution is known for its distinctive "Melbourne Model" of undergraduate education, which utilizes nine general bachelor's degrees followed by specialized graduate degrees. Governance is overseen by a Council acting as the peak governing body, supported by an Academic Board and a Committee of Convocation representing graduates.

Academically, the university ranks highly internationally, consistently placing in the top 50 globally and first nationally in Australia across major ranking systems. It boasts a diverse range of faculties, including Architecture, Arts, Business, Education, Engineering, Fine Arts, Law, Medicine, and Science, and maintains significant research output. The campus features extensive facilities such as multiple libraries, museums (including the Grainger Museum and Ian Potter Museum of Art), and residential colleges. Student life is supported by the University of Melbourne Student Union (UMSU) and Graduate Student Association, which manage over 300 clubs and societies, while athletics include the world's second-oldest Australian rules football club. Recent developments include the planning of a new engineering campus in Fishermans Bend, scheduled to open in 2026.
