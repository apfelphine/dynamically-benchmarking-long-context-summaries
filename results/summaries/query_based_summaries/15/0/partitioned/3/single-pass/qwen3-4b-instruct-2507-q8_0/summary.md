The University of Melbourne is a leading Australian research university with a rich history dating back to 1853. It operates across multiple campuses, including the primary Parkville campus, and regional sites in Burnley, Southbank, Werribee, Creswick, Dookie, and Shepparton. The Parkville campus features historic Gothic Revival architecture and is central to academic and research activities, while Southbank hosts the Victorian College of the Arts and the Melbourne Conservatorium of Music. The university is known for its strong student life, with a vibrant community supported by residential colleges, student unions, and a wide range of clubs and societies.

Academically, the university offers a unique "Melbourne Model" undergraduate structure, replacing traditional specialist degrees with generalist degrees that allow students to specialize later. It has nine faculties, including Arts, Science, Engineering, Medicine, and Law, and is home to numerous research centers and institutes. The university is a major contributor to scientific research, with one of the largest research student cohorts in Australia and significant research expenditure. It is consistently ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally, with strong performance in research output, employability, and academic reputation.

Notable alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates such as Elizabeth Blackburn, Sir John Eccles, and Sir Frank Macfarlane Burnet. The university also has a strong international presence, with over 44% of its student body being international. It is recognized for its contributions to innovation, entrepreneurship, and community engagement, with initiatives like the Melbourne Entrepreneurial Centre and student-led projects in science and technology. The university also faces ongoing discussions around student welfare, mental health, and academic freedom.
