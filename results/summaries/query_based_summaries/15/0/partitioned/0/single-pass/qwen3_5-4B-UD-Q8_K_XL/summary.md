The University of Melbourne was established in 1853 following a proposal by Hugh Childers, funded by the Victorian gold rush, with its first classes commencing in 1855. Originally a small institution with Irish founders, it expanded significantly through the late 19th and 20th centuries, adopting the "Melbourne Model" in 2008 to replace specialized undergraduate degrees with generalist degrees. Governance and administration underwent major restructuring under Vice-Chancellor Glyn Davis, including the "Business Improvement Program" which reduced staff numbers, and the introduction of a new capital works program. The university operates from its primary Parkville campus and several other locations in metropolitan Melbourne, including Burnley, Southbank, Werribee, and regional sites in Creswick, Dookie, and Shepparton, with a new engineering campus planned for Fishermans Bend.

In recent years, the university has faced challenges regarding workforce conditions, including allegations of systemic underpayment of casual staff, leading to significant financial repayments and union protests. It also experienced disruptions due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which forced a shift to online teaching and resulted in substantial job cuts despite capital investment plans. Internationally, the university has grown increasingly reliant on overseas student revenue. Currently, the university is led by Vice-Chancellor Emma Johnston, succeeding Duncan Maskell, and maintains a high academic reputation, ranking among the top universities in Australia and globally, with a focus on research excellence and a diverse student body comprising over 44% international students.
