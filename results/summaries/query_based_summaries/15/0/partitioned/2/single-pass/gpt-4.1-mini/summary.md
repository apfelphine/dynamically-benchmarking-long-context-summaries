# University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne, established in 1853, is a leading Australian research university with multiple campuses including its primary Parkville campus and specialized campuses at Burnley (horticulture), Southbank (arts), Werribee (veterinary science), Creswick (forestry), Dookie (agriculture), Shepparton (rural health), and a future engineering campus planned at Fishermans Bend. The Parkville campus, founded in 1853, is located just north of Melbourne’s CBD and features a mix of Gothic revival architecture and modern facilities, including several libraries, theaters, and research institutes. The university has nine faculties covering a broad range of disciplines, and it follows the “Melbourne Model,” a structure offering generalized three-year undergraduate degrees followed by specialized graduate study.

Governance is through a Council led by a Chancellor, with an Academic Board overseeing academic standards. The university has a strong research profile, with significant government and private funding, and hosts over 100 research centers. It is nationally ranked first among Australian universities and internationally recognized. The university supports extensive student life, including historic sports clubs such as the Melbourne University Football Club and various clubs and residential colleges. Notable alumni include several Australian Prime Ministers, Nobel laureates like Elizabeth Blackburn, and prominent figures in politics, academia, arts, and sciences. Recent years have seen large-scale campus developments, administrative restructures, and active engagement with social and student movements.
