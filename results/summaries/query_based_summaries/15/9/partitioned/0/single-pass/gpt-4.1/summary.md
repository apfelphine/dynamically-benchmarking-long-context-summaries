## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) – Summary

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), established in 1910, is a public research university in Mexico City and the largest in Latin America, with over 324,000 students enrolled across numerous undergraduate, graduate, and high school programs. UNAM's main campus is Ciudad Universitaria, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it operates multiple satellite campuses and research centers throughout Mexico and abroad. The university is notable for its autonomy, diverse academic offerings organized into schools and colleges, and a leading role in Mexican education, research, and culture.

UNAM consistently ranks among the top universities in Mexico and Latin America, with internationally recognized research output, especially in fields like artificial intelligence and robotics. It houses several national research institutes and maintains advanced scientific infrastructure, including supercomputers. UNAM's cultural impact extends to its meaningful seal, motto (“For my people the spirit shall speak”), and sports traditions—most notably the Pumas football team and iconic imagotype emblem. The university is distinguished by political activism, advocacy for minority inclusion, and vibrant Day of the Dead celebrations. UNAM boasts an impressive alumni network, including three Nobel laureates (Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, Mario Molina), multiple Mexican presidents, and leaders in arts, sciences, business, and beyond.
