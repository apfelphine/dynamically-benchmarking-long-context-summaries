## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) Summary

The National Autonomous University of Mexico was founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra. Its creation aimed to establish a secular and national institution, distinct from its 19th-century predecessor, by merging and expanding Mexico City's decentralized colleges of higher education. The initial project unified various schools, including Fine Arts, Engineering, and Medicine. Early challenges were political, related to the Mexican Revolution, though the university achieved autonomy in the mid-1920s following student strikes. Key developments include the creation of the coat-of-arms in 1921 by José Vasconcelos and the planning to move to the consolidated campus of Ciudad Universitaria in the 1940s.

UNAM is structured into schools (which offer undergraduate and graduate studies) and National Schools (which focus only on undergraduate studies). The university is a major center for research, with notable institutes covering disciplines like Astronomy, Biotechnology, and Nuclear Sciences, and is recognized as one of the most international research universities in Latin America. Notable alumni include world heads of state such as Andrés Manuel López Obrador and numerous Nobel laureates, including Octavio Paz and Mario Molina. The university also maintains an Open University and Distance Education System (SUAyED).
