The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded in **1910** by Justo Sierra, a minister under Porfirio Díaz, aiming to create a secular, national university distinct from the colonial-era Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. The university was initially established as a merger of various colleges and schools, with Joaquin Eguía y Lis as its first rector. Early challenges included political instability during the Mexican Revolution, leading to student strikes and resistance to its establishment.

UNAM gained autonomy in the **1920s**, with José Vasconcelos becoming rector in 1920 and introducing the university’s emblem—a map of Latin America flanked by an eagle and a condor, symbolizing unity and cultural identity. The university expanded its academic offerings, including engineering, law, medicine, and the arts, and became a hub for research and innovation. In the **1930s**, under rector Manuel Gómez Morín, UNAM resisted socialist education initiatives and supported academic freedom, aligning with Catholic and liberal values.

Today, UNAM is Mexico’s largest and most prestigious university, with over **324,000 students** across its multiple campuses, including the iconic **Ciudad Universitaria** in Mexico City. It is renowned for its research, particularly in fields like artificial intelligence, robotics, and biomedical sciences, and is recognized globally for its contributions to education and culture. UNAM’s motto, *"Para mi pueblo la palabra habrá de hablar"* ("For my people the spirit shall speak"), reflects its commitment to fostering a culture of knowledge and social progress. The university’s legacy includes producing Nobel laureates, influential politicians, and groundbreaking scientists, cementing its status as a cornerstone of Mexican academia.
