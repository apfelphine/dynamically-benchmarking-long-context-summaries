The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) was founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra to create a secular and national institution, distinct from its predecessor, the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. Its initial goal was to unify and expand Mexico City's decentralized higher education colleges. UNAM gained autonomy through student strikes in the mid-1920s.

UNAM is structured into schools or colleges, offering both undergraduate and graduate studies across various fields. It is recognized as a research-oriented university with international competitiveness and has been awarded the UNESCO recognition for impactful research on Artificial Intelligence. Key cultural aspects include its motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak," imprinted on its seal, which features a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor. The university's main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, was completed in 1954 and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.
