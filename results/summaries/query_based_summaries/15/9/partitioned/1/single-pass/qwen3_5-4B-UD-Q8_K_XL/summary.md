# National Autonomous University of Mexico

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) is one of the world's largest universities, with over 324,000 students, organized into schools and colleges rather than departments. Founded in 1910 to be secular and national in scope, it has historically advocated for academic freedom and minority rights, notably women in technology, while also producing prominent right-wing politicians and billionaires. The university boasts a diverse array of campuses, including the historic Ciudad Universitaria in Mexico City and numerous satellite locations across the country, alongside extension programs in the United States, Canada, and Europe. UNAM is recognized as a research-intensive institution, having produced all three of Mexico's Nobel laureates (Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina) and maintaining significant infrastructure in fields such as artificial intelligence, robotics, and basic research.

Academically, UNAM offers undergraduate, graduate, and professional studies across numerous schools, including Engineering, Medicine, Law, and the Arts, alongside a specialized Open University and Distance Education System serving approximately 32,000 students. Its research output accounts for more than 50% of Mexico's scientific production, supported by institutes dedicated to astronomy, biotechnology, nuclear sciences, and renewable energy. Culturally, the university is defined by its iconic seal designed by José Vasconcelos and its motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak." It maintains a vibrant political tradition, with student organizations actively engaging in social movements, and is renowned for its successful professional football club, Club Universidad Nacional (Pumas), which competes in Liga MX.
