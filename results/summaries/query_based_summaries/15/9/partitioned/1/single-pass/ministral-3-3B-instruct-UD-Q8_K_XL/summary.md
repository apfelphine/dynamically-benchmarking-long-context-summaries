The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** is a prestigious public university founded in 1910, originally established to replace the colonial-era Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. It is Mexico’s largest and most influential institution, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs across 17 national schools and 11 national preparatory schools. UNAM is organized into schools such as the School of Engineering, School of Medicine, School of Law, and School of Social Sciences, among others, and is known for its strong emphasis on research, innovation, and cultural contributions.

The university operates multiple campuses, including its central **Ciudad Universitaria** in Mexico City, which was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. UNAM also has satellite campuses across Mexico and international centers in the U.S., Canada, and Europe. Its academic reputation is globally recognized, particularly in fields like engineering, medicine, and social sciences. UNAM has produced numerous Nobel laureates, including **Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina**, and is renowned for its commitment to social equity, particularly in promoting women’s participation in STEM fields.

UNAM is deeply involved in political activism, cultural traditions, and community engagement, reflecting its role as a cornerstone of Mexican society. Its motto, *"Para mi pueblo la palabra habrá de hablar"* ("For my people the spirit shall speak"), encapsulates its mission to foster education, research, and cultural development for the nation.
