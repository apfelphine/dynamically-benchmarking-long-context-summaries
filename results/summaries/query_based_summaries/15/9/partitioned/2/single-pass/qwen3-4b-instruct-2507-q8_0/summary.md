The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) is a prominent public university founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, aiming to create a secular, national institution to modernize higher education in Mexico. It unified several existing colleges and established a new campus, Ciudad Universitaria, in San Ángel, which was completed in 1954 and later designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. UNAM's leadership evolved through key figures such as José Vasconcelos, who designed its iconic seal featuring an eagle and condor over a map of Latin America and established the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak," emphasizing cultural and educational empowerment. The university gained academic autonomy in the 1920s through student-led strikes and has since become a major center for research and education.

UNAM operates a large academic structure with multiple schools and colleges, including the School of Engineering, Law, Medicine, and the National Preparatory School, and offers both undergraduate and graduate programs. It is recognized globally for its research, particularly in artificial intelligence, robotics, and environmental sciences, and is consistently ranked as Mexico's top university. The university is also known for its strong commitment to social equity, including initiatives to promote women in technology and support minority groups.

UNAM has a vibrant student life, with active participation in athletics, cultural traditions such as the annual Day of the Dead offerings, and political activism. Its sports teams include the Pumas (football) and Pumas (volleyball), both of which have achieved national and international success. The university's student body is politically active, with a history of protests and advocacy, including major strikes in 1912, 1966, and 1999–2000. UNAM has produced numerous notable alumni, including Nobel laureates Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina, as well as prominent politicians, scientists, artists, and humanists such as Carlos Slim, Miguel de la Madrid, and Octavio Paz.

The university's campuses extend beyond Ciudad Universitaria to include satellite campuses in various parts of Mexico and international centers in countries like China, Spain, the United States, and Canada, reflecting its global outreach. UNAM is a leading institution in Latin America and plays a vital role in shaping Mexico’s academic, cultural, and scientific landscape.
