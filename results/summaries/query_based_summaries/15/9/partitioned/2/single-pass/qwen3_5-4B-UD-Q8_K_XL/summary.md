Founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra as a secular, national institution to replace the 19th-century Royal and Pontifical University, the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) emerged during the Mexican Revolution to reorganize higher education and embody positivism. Following initial political challenges and student strikes regarding administrative autonomy, José Vasconcelos became rector in 1920 and secured full independence from the government in 1926. Vasconcelos also designed the university's iconic seal featuring a double-headed eagle and the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak," symbolizing a new era of Latin American unity and cultural enlightenment.

UNAM is currently the largest university in the world by enrollment, with over 324,000 students across its primary Ciudad Universitaria campus in Mexico City and numerous satellite campuses nationwide. The university is renowned for its significant research output, hosting institutions like the National Autonomous University of Mexico Supercomputer and attracting global scientists, while maintaining a strong tradition of political activism and a diverse student body that includes prominent Nobel laureates, presidents, and influential intellectuals.
