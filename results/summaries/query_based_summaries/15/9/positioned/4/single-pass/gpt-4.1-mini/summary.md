## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

Founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra, UNAM was created to unify and expand Mexico City's decentralized higher education institutions into a secular and national university, distinct from its 16th-century religious predecessor. Early challenges included political unrest associated with the Mexican Revolution and opposition to government control. The university gained autonomy in the 1920s after student strikes and faculty support, establishing the rector as its final authority independent of governmental oversight.

UNAM’s main campus, Ciudad Universitaria in Mexico City, was built in the 1950s and declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. It operates numerous satellite campuses throughout Mexico and abroad, offering comprehensive undergraduate and graduate programs via various schools or colleges. The university is Mexico’s largest educational institution, with over 324,000 students. UNAM is a major research university with internationally recognized institutes across fields such as astronomy, biotechnology, nuclear sciences, ecology, and physics. It has strong commitments to academic freedom, political activism, and minority inclusion, particularly empowering women in technology. Its sports teams, symbolized by a puma emblem designed in 1974, have national prominence.

Notable alumni include all three Mexican Nobel laureates, several presidents of Mexico and other Latin American countries, prominent politicians, diplomats, Nobel-winning scientists, leading academics, business magnates such as Carlos Slim, and influential cultural figures like Octavio Paz, Alfonso Cuarón, and Guillermo del Toro. The university’s motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak," reflects its humanistic and pan-American identity.
