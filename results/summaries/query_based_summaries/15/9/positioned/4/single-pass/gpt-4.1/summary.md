## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) – Concise Summary

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, is Mexico's largest and most prominent university. Originally intended as a secular and national educational institution, UNAM evolved out of Mexico City's decentralized colleges and established autonomy from government control in the 1920s. Its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, located in southern Mexico City, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its architecture and central role in Mexican higher education. UNAM also maintains satellite campuses across Mexico and international extension centers, and houses major museums and cultural facilities such as the Palacio de Minería.

UNAM is organized into numerous schools covering fields such as engineering, law, medicine, and sciences, supporting both undergraduate and graduate programs. The university is a major research institution in Latin America, responsible for over half of Mexico’s scientific output and hosting premier research institutes for diverse areas, including Artificial Intelligence and Robotics. UNAM has a strong tradition of political activism and inclusivity, supporting minority and women’s initiatives. It boasts a vibrant athletic program, notably through its Pumas football club, and rich cultural traditions.

UNAM counts over 324,000 students and is consistently ranked as the best university in Mexico and among the top in Latin America. Its alumni include all of Mexico’s Nobel laureates, several Mexican presidents, influential scientists, artists, and business leaders. The university's motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak," reflects its commitment to social responsibility and cultural leadership.
