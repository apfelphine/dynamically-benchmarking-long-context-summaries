The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was established on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra with the intent to create a secular, national university replacing the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. It consolidated various colleges in Mexico City into a unified institution emphasizing positivism and liberalism. Early political turmoil, including the Mexican Revolution, and internal student strikes marked its initial years. Achieving autonomy in the 1920s allowed the university rector full authority and academic freedom. The campus was centralized with the construction of Ciudad Universitaria in the 1950s, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The university has a history of political activism, including protests during the 1968 Olympics and student strikes over examination regulations. 

UNAM consists of a broad range of schools and national preparatory institutions spread across Mexico, offering undergraduate, graduate, and distance education programs. It is Mexico’s largest university and a major center of research and culture. Known for academic excellence, UNAM is ranked among Latin America's best universities and is internationally recognized for impactful research in fields such as artificial intelligence and robotics. Its alumni and faculty include Mexican Nobel laureates, prominent scientists, politicians, artists, and business leaders like Carlos Slim. The university fosters cultural traditions and political engagement among its community and emphasizes inclusion of minorities and women in technology fields.
