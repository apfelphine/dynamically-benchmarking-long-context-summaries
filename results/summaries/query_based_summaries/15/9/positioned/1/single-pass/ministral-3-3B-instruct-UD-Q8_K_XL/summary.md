The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** is a prestigious public university founded on **September 22, 1910**, by Justo Sierra, then Minister of Education under President Porfirio Díaz. Unlike its predecessor, the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico, UNAM was established as a secular, national institution aimed at reorganizing higher education in Mexico, embodying the principles of positivism and Mexican liberalism.

### **History & Structure**
UNAM emerged from the merger of various educational institutions in Mexico City, including the National Preparatory Schools, the National School of Fine Arts, and the National Schools of Business, Political Science, Jurisprudence, Engineering, Medicine, and Normal Education. Its early rector, Joaquín Eguía y Lis, faced political challenges due to the ongoing Mexican Revolution, leading to student strikes and eventual autonomy for the university in the 1920s.

The university underwent significant transformations, including the creation of its iconic campus, **Ciudad Universitaria (University City)**, designed by architects like Mario Pani and inaugurated in 1954. This campus, declared a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007**, serves as the academic and cultural heart of UNAM, housing over 324,000 students across its various schools and colleges.

### **Academic Excellence & Research**
UNAM is Mexico’s largest and most prestigious university, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral programs across **six main academic centers** (Health Sciences, Technology, Mathematical and Natural Sciences, Law and Economic Sciences, Philosophy and Humanities, and Literature and Arts). The university is renowned for its research contributions, particularly in fields like **artificial intelligence, robotics, and biomedical sciences**, and has been recognized by UNESCO for its impactful research.

The university’s **coat of arms**, featuring a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor, symbolizes its commitment to cultural and scientific advancement. Its motto, **"For my people the spirit shall speak"**, reflects its mission to foster a new era of education and cultural unity in Latin America.

### **Global Recognition & Impact**
UNAM consistently ranks among the top universities in Latin America and is recognized globally for its academic rigor and contributions to research. It has produced numerous **Nobel laureates**, including **Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina**, and has played a pivotal role in shaping Mexico’s intellectual, political, and scientific landscape.

Today, UNAM remains a cornerstone of higher education in Mexico, continuing its legacy of innovation, excellence, and social responsibility.
