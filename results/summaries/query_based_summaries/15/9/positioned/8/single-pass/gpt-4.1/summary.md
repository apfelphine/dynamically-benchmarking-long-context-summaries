## National Autonomous University of Mexico

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), founded in 1910, is one of Latin America's largest and most prestigious universities. Established by Justo Sierra to unify Mexico City's colleges and create a secular, nationally oriented institution, UNAM inherited elements of the former Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico (1551). UNAM gained autonomy in the 1920s, freeing it from government control over curricula and administration. Its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, was inaugurated in 1954 and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. UNAM oversees numerous satellite and extension campuses nationally and abroad, including specialized research centers and cultural institutions, and has strong international outreach.

UNAM offers a comprehensive array of schools and national colleges dedicated to disciplines like arts, sciences, engineering, medicine, law, business, and social sciences, along with specialized high schools and an Open University and Distance Education System. With over 324,000 students, it is recognized for its research, producing more than half of Mexico's scientific output and hosting over 30 institutes in areas ranging from astronomy to biotechnology. UNAM is consistently ranked among the best universities in Mexico and Latin America. The university has provided Mexico with all its Nobel laureates, world leaders, prominent intellectuals, scientists, and artists. Its cultural traditions, seal, and motto ("For my people the spirit shall speak") emphasize its humanistic and national mission. UNAM’s vibrant campus life includes notable athletics teams, student associations, and annual cultural events.
