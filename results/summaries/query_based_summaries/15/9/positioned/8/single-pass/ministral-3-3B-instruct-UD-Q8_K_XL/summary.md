The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded on **September 22, 1910**, by Justo Sierra, aiming to replace the colonial-era Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico with a secular, modern institution aligned with Mexican liberalism. Originally merging several decentralized colleges, UNAM became a national university with a focus on science, technology, and social sciences, emphasizing autonomy and academic freedom.

UNAM’s main campus, **Ciudad Universitaria (University City)**, in Coyoacán, Mexico City, was designed in the 1950s as a unified academic complex, replacing scattered buildings in the city center. The campus is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and features iconic structures like the **Estadio Olímpico Universitario** and the **Palacio de Minería**. Beyond Ciudad Universitaria, UNAM operates satellite campuses across Mexico, including in **Querétaro, Morelia, Mérida, and León**, as well as international centers in the U.S. and Canada.

Academically, UNAM is organized into **schools and colleges**, offering undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs. It is renowned for its **engineering, law, medicine, and social sciences**, with a strong emphasis on research. UNAM is consistently ranked among the top universities in Latin America and Mexico, known for its contributions to fields like **artificial intelligence, robotics, and environmental science**. The university has also been a leader in promoting gender equality and inclusion, particularly in technology and science.

UNAM’s culture is deeply tied to Mexican identity, with traditions like the **Day of the Dead offerings** and the university’s emblem, featuring a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor. Politically active, UNAM students and faculty have played significant roles in Mexican society, though tensions with government policies have occasionally sparked protests. Notable alumni include **three Nobel laureates (Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, Mario Molina)** and leaders in politics, science, and arts. UNAM remains a cornerstone of higher education in Mexico, blending academic excellence with cultural and social impact.
