The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra, then minister of education, to create a secular, national university distinct from the religiously tied Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. It initially unified several existing colleges, with Joaquín Eguía y Lis as its first rector. Despite early political resistance and student strikes, UNAM gained autonomy in the early 1920s, culminating in the removal of federal control over its curriculum and policies. The university's main campus, **Ciudad Universitaria**, was established in the 1950s in San Ángel, south of Mexico City, and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. UNAM has expanded to include satellite campuses across Mexico and abroad, and operates international centers for teaching and research.

Academically, UNAM is organized into schools and colleges, offering undergraduate and graduate programs. It includes national schools focused on practical training and a wide range of academic fields such as engineering, law, medicine, and the arts. The university is one of the world's largest, with over 324,000 students, including those in its affiliated institutions like the National Preparatory School and the College of Sciences and Humanities. UNAM is consistently ranked as Mexico’s top university and among the top 10 in Latin America.

UNAM is a leader in research, particularly in artificial intelligence and robotics, and is recognized by UNESCO for impactful research. It houses numerous research institutes, including the Institute of Astronomy, Biotechnology, and Ecology. The university has a strong tradition of political activism, with students and faculty engaging in protests and advocating for social and educational reform. Its culture is marked by traditions such as the Day of the Dead offerings and the use of the **puma** as a symbol of its athletic and academic spirit.

Notable alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates **Octavio Paz** (Literature), **Mario Molina** (Chemistry), and **Alfonso García Robles** (Peace), as well as prominent politicians, scientists, and artists such as **Carlos Slim** and **Adolfo Gilly**. The university’s seal, designed by José Vasconcelos, features a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor, symbolizing the unification of cultures, with the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak." UNAM continues to promote diversity and inclusion, notably through initiatives supporting women in technology and minority representation.
