The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** is one of Mexico’s largest and most prestigious universities, founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra. Originally established to replace the colonial-era Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico, UNAM was designed as a secular, national institution rooted in liberalism and positivism. It merged and expanded Mexico City’s decentralized higher education colleges, offering a broad range of academic programs in fields such as engineering, law, medicine, arts, and sciences.

UNAM’s early challenges included political instability due to the ongoing Mexican Revolution, but it gained autonomy in the 1920s, becoming a self-governing body. The university played a pivotal role in Mexico’s cultural and intellectual development, supporting initiatives like the Mexican muralism movement and fostering academic freedom. In 1943, UNAM began constructing its iconic **Ciudad Universitaria (University City)**, a sprawling campus in San Ángel, designed by architects like Mario Pani. This campus, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007, is a symbol of modern Mexican architecture and academic excellence.

UNAM is organized into schools and colleges, offering undergraduate and graduate programs, including national schools focused on practical experience. It is renowned for its research, particularly in fields like artificial intelligence, robotics, and biomedical sciences, and has produced many notable figures, including three Nobel laureates (Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina). The university has also been a hub for political activism, with students and faculty advocating for social and academic freedoms.

With over 324,000 students, UNAM is one of the largest universities in the world, serving as a model for higher education in Latin America. Its legacy spans education, culture, and innovation, making it a cornerstone of Mexico’s academic and intellectual landscape.
