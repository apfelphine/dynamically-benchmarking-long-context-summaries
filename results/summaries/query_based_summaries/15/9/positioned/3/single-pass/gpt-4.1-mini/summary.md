The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)**, founded on September 22, 1910, emerged as a secular, national institution aimed at unifying and modernizing higher education in Mexico, distinct from its colonial-era predecessor. Early challenges included political instability during the Mexican Revolution, student strikes, and efforts to gain autonomy, which was eventually secured in the 1920s, making the university independent of government control. The establishment of the main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, began in the 1940s and was completed in the 1950s, later recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Throughout its history, UNAM has served as a significant cultural, political, and educational force in Mexico, fostering minorities' inclusion and supporting political activism among students and faculty.

UNAM is one of the largest universities globally, organized into numerous schools and colleges offering undergraduate and graduate programs. It operates several satellite campuses across Mexico and extension schools internationally, emphasizing research with notable institutes in astronomy, biotechnology, physics, ecology, engineering, among others. The university has earned recognition for impactful research in fields such as artificial intelligence and robotics. Its cultural contributions include significant museums, the university seal symbolizing Latin American unity, and the puma emblem representing its pillars of education, research, and culture diffusion. UNAM has produced multiple Nobel laureates, prominent politicians including several Mexican presidents, distinguished academics, scientists, artists, and business leaders such as Carlos Slim. The university remains a politically active institution with a strong tradition of social engagement and minority advocacy.
