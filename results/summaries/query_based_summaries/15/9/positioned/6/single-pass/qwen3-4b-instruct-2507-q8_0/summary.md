The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** is a major public university founded on 22 September 1910 by Justo Sierra, aiming to create a secular, national, and modern institution distinct from the outdated, Catholic-oriented Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. It was established by merging and expanding Mexico City’s decentralized colleges, with its first rector being Joaquín Eguía y Lis. 

UNAM faced significant political and social challenges, including student strikes over examination methods, resistance to socialist education, and a major protest in 1968 (Mexico 68) that culminated in the Tlatelolco massacre. Despite these, the university gained autonomy in the 1920s and was formally granted full independence in 1921 through the creation of its coat-of-arms and the establishment of a rector-led authority. 

Its main campus, **Ciudad Universitaria**, was established in the 1950s in San Ángel, south of Mexico City, and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The university has expanded with satellite campuses across Mexico and internationally, including in Querétaro, Morelia, Mérida, and abroad through centers for teaching foreigners. 

UNAM is organized into schools and colleges offering both undergraduate and graduate programs, with a strong emphasis on research. It is consistently ranked as Mexico’s best university and one of Latin America’s top institutions. UNAM has produced numerous Nobel laureates, including Alfonso García Robles (Peace), Octavio Paz (Literature), and Mario Molina (Chemistry), and has a notable legacy in science, engineering, and public service. The university’s motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak," reflects its mission to promote cultural and educational empowerment. 

UNAM also has a vibrant student life, including political activism, cultural traditions like the Day of the Dead offerings, and a strong commitment to inclusivity, especially in technology and gender equity. It hosts numerous museums and research centers, including the Palacio de Minería, the National Biodiversity Pavilion, and the Museum of San Ildefonso. 

The university is a leader in research, particularly in artificial intelligence and robotics, and is recognized globally for its contributions to science and innovation. Its alumni include prominent political leaders, scientists, artists, and entrepreneurs, such as Carlos Slim, Andrés Manuel López Obrador, and numerous Nobel laureates.
