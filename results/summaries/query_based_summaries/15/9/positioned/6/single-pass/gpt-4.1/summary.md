## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) — Summary

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), founded on 22 September 1910, is Mexico's largest and most influential university, with historical roots dating back to the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico (1551). Conceived as a secular, national institution, UNAM unified multiple colleges and aimed to reorganize the country's higher education. Its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria in Mexico City—a UNESCO World Heritage Site—hosts numerous faculties, museums, and research facilities; additional campuses and centers operate throughout Mexico and internationally. UNAM comprises a vast network of schools, colleges, and national schools offering undergraduate and graduate programs, open and distance education systems, and preparatory high schools.

UNAM is recognized as the top university in Mexico and ranks highly in Latin America and globally, particularly for its research output. It hosts significant research institutes and centers spanning sciences, engineering, humanities, and the arts, and is noted for excellence in fields such as artificial intelligence and robotics. UNAM's diverse, international academic community includes over 324,000 students, with open access for international students and substantial outreach programs. Distinguished alumni include Mexico's Nobel laureates, multiple national presidents, artists, scientists, business leaders, and public intellectuals, underscoring UNAM's profound impact on Mexican society and global scholarship.
