# National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) was founded on September 22, 1910, by Minister of Education Justo Sierra to create a secular, national institution distinct from the Catholic-influenced Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. Initially unified as a single entity, UNAM faced significant political challenges during the Mexican Revolution and struggled to gain autonomy. It achieved independence in the mid-1920s, becoming the final authority on its own policies and curriculum. The university established its iconic coat of arms in 1921, featuring an eagle and condor surrounding a map of Latin America, with the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak."

UNAM is organized into schools and colleges rather than departments, offering both undergraduate and graduate studies across a vast network of campuses. Its primary campus, Ciudad Universitaria, located in Coyoacán, Mexico City, was designed in the 1950s to replace scattered downtown buildings and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. The university also operates numerous satellite campuses, extension schools in the United States and Canada, and international centers in China, Spain, the UK, France, and Costa Rica. Renowned for its research output, particularly in artificial intelligence and robotics, UNAM has produced many notable alumni, including Nobel laureates, heads of state, and prominent cultural figures, while maintaining a history of significant student activism and political engagement.
