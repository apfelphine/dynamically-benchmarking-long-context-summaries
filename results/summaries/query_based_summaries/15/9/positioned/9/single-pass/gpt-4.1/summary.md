## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) – Summary

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, is one of Latin America’s most prominent and largest universities, offering both undergraduate and graduate programs to over 324,000 students. UNAM consolidated Mexico City's higher education landscape, establishing itself as a secular institution distinct from its colonial predecessor, the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico (1551–1865). The university is organized into schools and national schools focused on diverse fields, including engineering, law, medicine, arts, and social sciences, as well as high schools through the National Preparatory School and the College of Sciences and Humanities. Its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria in Mexico City, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site, features significant modern Mexican architecture and hosts major museums, libraries, athletic stadiums, and cultural centers. UNAM also maintains satellite campuses throughout the country and operates extension schools abroad.

UNAM is renowned for its vibrant research initiatives, consistently ranking as Mexico’s leading university and among the top in Latin America. It has produced groundbreaking research in artificial intelligence, robotics, and across fundamental sciences, housing major research institutes such as the Institute of Astronomy, Biotechnology, Physics, and others. UNAM has a distinguished history of political activism, cultural traditions, and commitment to social inclusion. It counts all three Mexican Nobel laureates among its alumni—Alfonso García Robles (Peace), Octavio Paz (Literature), and Mario Molina (Chemistry)—and has educated numerous national presidents, artists, scientists, and business leaders. The university’s athletic teams, including the Pumas football club and college volleyball team, enjoy national recognition. UNAM’s seal, motto (“For my people the spirit shall speak”), and sports puma emblem reflect its profound influence on Mexican education, culture, and society.
