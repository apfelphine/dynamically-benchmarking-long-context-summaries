# Summary: National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), established on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra, transformed Mexico’s higher education by unifying various decentralized colleges into a secular, national institution distinct from its colonial predecessor. Early challenges included political instability during the Mexican Revolution, student strikes, and efforts to achieve autonomy from federal government oversight, which was eventually won in the 1920s, granting the university full control over its policies and curriculum. Key historical milestones include the creation of the university's emblem and motto by rector José Vasconcelos in 1921 and the development of its principal campus, Ciudad Universitaria, in the 1950s, which was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. UNAM has experienced significant student activism, notably during the 1968 protests, resulting in the tragic Tlatelolco massacre.

UNAM is among the largest universities globally, offering undergraduate and graduate programs across numerous schools and national schools, including specialized high schools. It operates a main campus (University City) in southern Mexico City, multiple satellite campuses nationally and internationally, and extension schools across the U.S., Canada, China, Europe, and Latin America. Renowned for its research output, UNAM leads Mexico in scientific production and innovation, with a strong interdisciplinary approach addressing national and global challenges. Its wide-ranging faculties cover sciences, humanities, engineering, law, medicine, and arts, among others. The university has a proud tradition of political engagement, advocacy for minorities, and cultural contributions, including organizing large Day of the Dead celebrations and supporting Latina participation in technology and hackathons. Notable UNAM alumni include three Mexican Nobel laureates, national leaders, prominent intellectuals, scientists, artists, and global figures across various fields.
