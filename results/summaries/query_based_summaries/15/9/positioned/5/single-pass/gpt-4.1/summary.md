## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) – Summary

Founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) was established with the aim of creating a secular and national university, distinct from its religious predecessor, the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico (1551). UNAM unified several decentralized colleges and played a pivotal role in reorganizing higher education in Mexico. Gaining autonomy in the 1920s, the university became a model of academic freedom, further exemplified by its independence from direct government control, the creation of its distinctive coat-of-arms and motto, and its active involvement in sociopolitical movements—including major student strikes and advocacy for minorities and women.

UNAM’s main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, completed in 1954 in southern Mexico City, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The university maintains several satellite campuses across Mexico and international extension schools and research centers in regions including the United States, Canada, Europe, and Asia. With over 324,000 students enrolled at various academic levels, UNAM is among the world’s largest universities and is consistently ranked as the top institution in Mexico and among the best in Latin America. UNAM has a comprehensive academic structure, covering fields from humanities to engineering, offering both undergraduate and graduate degrees, as well as robust open and distance education programs.

UNAM is distinguished for its research excellence, producing leading scientific output in Latin America and hosting prominent institutes in diverse disciplines. It is recognized for impactful work in artificial intelligence and robotics, and houses significant research infrastructure, including a supercomputer and premier research centers. The university culture is vibrant, marked by its iconic visual identity and strong student and alumni associations. UNAM maintains notable athletic teams and traditions, political activism, and programs for cultural enrichment. It boasts a distinguished community of alumni and faculty, including all Mexican Nobel laureates, multiple heads of state, scientists, artists, and business leaders who have made significant contributions in their fields.
