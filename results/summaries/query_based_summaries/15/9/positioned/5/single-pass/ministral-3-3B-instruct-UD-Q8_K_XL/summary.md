The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded in **1910** by Justo Sierra, aiming to create a modern, secular, and national university distinct from its 19th-century predecessor, the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. Established as a merger of various higher education institutions, UNAM was designed to reflect Mexican liberalism and positivist ideals, emphasizing education, research, and cultural development.

### **Key Features**
- **Founding & Early Challenges**: UNAM began with political and philosophical opposition, including student strikes and debates over its autonomy. The university faced instability due to the ongoing Mexican Revolution and government control over its policies.
- **Autonomy & Growth**: By the 1920s, UNAM gained autonomy, becoming a self-governing institution. The university expanded its academic offerings, including schools in engineering, medicine, law, and the arts, and established its iconic **Ciudad Universitaria** (University City) in San Ángel, Mexico City, in the 1950s.
- **Academic Structure**: UNAM organizes its education into schools (e.g., School of Engineering, School of Medicine) and national schools (focused on practical training). It also operates the **Escuela Nacional Preparatoria (ENP)** and the **Colegio de Ciencias y Humanidades (CCH)**, which include high schools.
- **Research & Innovation**: UNAM is renowned for its research in fields such as astronomy, biology, physics, and engineering. It hosts prestigious institutes like the **Instituto de Astronomía**, **Instituto de Biología**, and **Instituto de Física**, and has produced groundbreaking research in areas like robotics and artificial intelligence.
- **Global Recognition**: UNAM consistently ranks among the top universities in Latin America and is recognized for its academic excellence, research output, and contributions to society. It has been a leader in promoting inclusivity, particularly in technology and women’s education.

### **Campuses & Facilities**
- **Ciudad Universitaria**: The main campus, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, features iconic buildings like the **Estadio Olímpico Universitario** and the **Palacio de Bellas Artes**.
- **Satellite Campuses**: UNAM operates campuses in cities like Querétaro, Morelia, Mérida, and León, as well as extension schools in the U.S. and Canada.
- **Museums & Cultural Sites**: The university houses museums like the **Museo Nacional de Antropología**, the **Museo de Arte Moderno**, and the **Museo de San Ildefonso**, which celebrate Mexican culture and history.

### **Notable Achievements**
- **Nobel Laureates**: UNAM has produced three Nobel Prize winners, including **Alfonso García Robles**, **Octavio Paz**, and **Mario Molina**.
- **Political & Cultural Influence**: UNAM has been a hub for political activism, with students and faculty playing key roles in Mexican history, including during the **1968 Tlatelolco Massacre** and the **2019 protests**.
- **Entrepreneurship & Innovation**: The university has fostered innovation through programs like the **Centro de Investigación y de Estudios Avanzados (CINVESTAV)**, which has contributed to advancements in technology and medicine.

### **Current Status**
UNAM remains one of Mexico’s most prestigious universities, known for its rigorous academic standards, strong research output, and commitment to social and cultural development. It continues to expand its global presence while maintaining its roots in Mexican education and innovation.
