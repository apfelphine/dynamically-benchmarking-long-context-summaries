## National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

Founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra, UNAM was established to replace the older Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico with a secular, national institution embodying Mexican liberalism. Early challenges included political instability from the Mexican Revolution, government control over policies, and student protests, notably strikes over examination systems. Autonomy was finally granted in the 1920s, enabling the rector to have final authority. Notable developments include the establishment of Ciudad Universitaria (University City) as the consolidated main campus in the 1950s, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. UNAM has expanded with satellite campuses across Mexico and abroad, supports various museums (such as Palacio de Minería and Museum of San Ildefonso), and includes research centers and graduate institutes.

UNAM serves over 324,000 students through an organization of schools and national schools, offering comprehensive undergraduate and graduate programs. It maintains a robust distance education system (SUAyED) for about 32,000 students. Renowned for research, UNAM contributes over 50% of Mexico's scientific output, excelling in areas such as Artificial Intelligence and robotics. It houses prominent institutes including those of Astronomy, Biotechnology, Nuclear Sciences, and Ecology. The university fosters political activism, cultural traditions, and minority inclusion, especially promoting women in technology with initiatives like Latina Hackathons. UNAM's distinguished alumni include all three Mexican Nobel laureates and numerous heads of state and professionals across various fields.
