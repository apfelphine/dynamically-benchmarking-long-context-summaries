The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded in **1910** by Justo Sierra, then Minister of Education under President Porfirio Díaz, aiming to create a secular, national institution distinct from the colonial-era Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. Originally merging decentralized colleges, UNAM expanded its curriculum and faced political challenges during the Mexican Revolution, including student strikes and autonomy struggles. By the 1920s, it gained formal autonomy, establishing its coat-of-arms and motto, *"The Spirit shall speak for my people"*, reflecting its commitment to education and cultural unification.

UNAM operates on a **collegiate system**, with **17 national schools** offering undergraduate and graduate degrees across disciplines like engineering, medicine, law, and humanities. Its **Ciudad Universitaria (University City)** campus, designed by architects like Mario Pani, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, symbolizing its modernist architectural legacy. The university is renowned for its **research excellence**, particularly in fields like artificial intelligence, robotics, and biomedical sciences, and consistently ranks among the top universities in Latin America and globally.

UNAM’s academic culture emphasizes **critical thinking and social activism**, with a history of student-led movements, including the **1968 protests** against the Mexico City Olympics and the **1999–2000 strike** over tuition fees. The university has produced **nobel laureates** (e.g., Mario Molina, Alfonso García Robles) and influential figures in politics, science, and culture. Its **diverse student body** and commitment to **social inclusion**—such as initiatives supporting women in STEM—reflect its role as a public institution dedicated to fostering innovation and equity.
