The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) was founded on September 22, 1910, by Justo Sierra as a secular, national institution to replace the colonial Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. Initially unified from various decentralized colleges, the university faced significant political challenges during the Mexican Revolution, including student strikes and government interference. A pivotal moment occurred in the mid-1920s when student strikes successfully secured the university's autonomy from the Secretariat of Public Education. Under the leadership of José Vasconcelos, the university adopted its iconic seal featuring an eagle and condor, along with the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak." In 1952, UNAM relocated its scattered downtown buildings to the modern Ciudad Universitaria campus in San Ángel, designed by a team of prominent architects and declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.

UNAM is one of the world's largest universities, with over 324,000 students across more than 30 campuses in Mexico and international locations. Its academic structure comprises numerous schools offering undergraduate and graduate degrees, alongside national schools focused on practical experience. The university is renowned for its research output, having been recognized by UNESCO for impactful work in artificial intelligence and robotics, and it houses premier research institutions such as the National Astronomical Observatory. Culturally, UNAM is politically active, hosting diverse student movements and producing notable figures including Nobel laureates like Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina. The university maintains strong ties with industry and government, fostering entrepreneurship and diversity, particularly in technology fields.
