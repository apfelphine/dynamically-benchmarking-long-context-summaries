The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) was founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, who aimed to create a secular, national university to modernize higher education in Mexico. It merged and expanded from various existing colleges, including those in arts, law, engineering, and medicine, and established its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, in 1954. The university gained autonomy from the government in the 1920s, and student activism played a key role in shaping its development, including major strikes over examination methods and tuition fees. UNAM is one of the largest universities in the world, with over 324,000 students, and is consistently ranked as the top university in Mexico. It has a strong research focus, particularly in artificial intelligence and robotics, and is recognized globally for its contributions to science and technology. The university is known for its inclusive policies, such as empowering women in technology through initiatives like the "Latina Hackathons" and its commitment to diversity. UNAM's academic structure includes 12 faculties and schools, with a focus on undergraduate and graduate education. Its cultural identity is reflected in its seal, which features a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor, and its motto, "For my people the spirit shall speak." The university has produced numerous notable alumni, including Nobel laureates Octavio Paz and Mario Molina, and has a strong presence in politics, science, and the arts. UNAM maintains a vibrant student life, with traditions such as the Day of the Dead offerings and a strong tradition of political activism.
