## **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)**

**History and Governance**
Founded on September 22, 1910, by Minister of Education Justo Sierra, UNAM was established to create a secular, national institution distinct from the 19th-century Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico. The university aimed to reorganize higher education in Mexico, serve as a model of positivism, and encompass Mexican liberalism. Its first rector was Joaquin Eguía y Lis. Early challenges included political instability during the Mexican Revolution and internal student strikes in 1912 and 1920s regarding examination methods. Autonomy was eventually achieved in the early 1920s, ending the university's dependency on the Secretariat of Public Education and establishing the rector as the final authority. The university's coat-of-arms, featuring an eagle and condor surrounding a map of Latin America, was designed by rector José Vasconcelos in 1921.

**Campuses and Infrastructure**
UNAM's main campus, *Ciudad Universitaria* (University City), is located in the Coyoacán borough of Mexico City. Designed by architects including Mario Pani and Mauricio De Maria y Campos, it was built on an ancient lava bed between 1950 and 1954 to replace scattered downtown buildings. Declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007, the campus includes over 40 schools, institutes, the Estadio Olímpico Universitario, the Central Library, and the National Library of Mexico. Outside of Mexico City, UNAM operates numerous satellite campuses in the metropolitan area (Acatlán, Aragón, Cuautitlán, Iztacala, Zaragoza) and across the country in cities such as Querétaro, Morelia, Mérida, and León, primarily focusing on research and graduate studies. Additionally, it maintains extension schools in the United States, Canada, and international locations including Beijing, Madrid, and London.

**Academics and Research**
UNAM is organized into schools rather than departments and is responsible for the National Preparatory School (ENP) and the Science and Humanities College (CCH). It serves over 324,413 students, making it one of the world's largest universities. All schools offer both undergraduate and graduate degrees, with "national schools" offering only undergraduate degrees focused on practical experience. UNAM is a research-oriented institution recognized by UNESCO for impactful work in Artificial Intelligence and a leader in robotics (RoboCup). It houses premier research institutes such as the Institute of Astronomy, Biotechnology, and Nuclear Sciences, and operates a supercomputer named Miztli. The university has a strong commitment to diversity, having adopted United Nations platforms to support women in technology in 2016.

**Culture and Notable Figures**
UNAM is known for its political activism and cultural traditions, including annual Day of the Dead displays on the main square. The university's sports teams, the Pumas CU, compete in college football with a historic rivalry against the National Polytechnic Institute. Among its notable alumni are three Nobel laureates (Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina) and numerous heads of state, including Mexican Presidents Adolfo López Mateos, Luis Echeverría, Miguel Alemán Valdés, and Andrés Manuel López Obrador. Notable faculty include physicists Miguel Alcubierre and Rodolfo Neri Vela, and architect Teodoro González de León.
