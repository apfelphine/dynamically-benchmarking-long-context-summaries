The **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)** was founded in **1910** by Justo Sierra, a key figure in the Porfirio Díaz regime, aiming to create a secular, national institution that would modernize higher education in Mexico. Originally merging various pre-existing colleges, UNAM was designed to reflect positivist and liberal ideals, emphasizing knowledge dissemination and scientific advancement.

The university underwent significant political and social challenges, including student strikes, autonomy struggles, and cultural reforms. In **1952**, UNAM relocated to its iconic **Ciudad Universitaria (University City)**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, which serves as its central campus. Today, UNAM is Mexico’s largest university, offering over **324,000 students** across 17 national schools and 11 national preparatory schools, spanning fields like engineering, law, medicine, and the arts. It is renowned for its research, particularly in fields like artificial intelligence, robotics, and biomedical sciences, and is recognized globally for its contributions to education and innovation.

UNAM’s academic model includes a mix of traditional and modern approaches, with a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary studies. Its **coat of arms**, featuring a Mexican eagle and an Andean condor, symbolizes its commitment to cultural and scientific unity. The university has also been a leader in promoting gender equality and inclusion, particularly in technology and science. With a legacy of political activism, UNAM remains a cornerstone of Mexican higher education, blending tradition with forward-thinking initiatives.
