Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, is a prominent land-, space-, and sea-grant research university in Ithaca, New York. Established as New York’s first land-grant institution, Cornell has grown into a highly competitive and influential academic institution, renowned for its innovative research, diverse academic programs, and significant contributions to science, technology, engineering, and the arts.

The university operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs across 16 colleges, including seven graduate schools and four statutory colleges supported by New York State. Cornell is known for its rigorous admissions process, with a highly selective acceptance rate and a strong emphasis on academic excellence. The university is also recognized for its commitment to sustainability, innovation, and community engagement, with initiatives such as the Atkinson Center for Sustainability and the Cornell Tech campus on Roosevelt Island.

Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca is renowned for its picturesque and historic architecture, blending traditional and modern styles. The university has a significant international presence, with partnerships and programs in over 40 countries. Notable achievements include the establishment of the first electrified university in the U.S., pioneering advancements in computing, and hosting the world’s largest single-dish radio telescope at Arecibo Observatory. Cornell’s research output and faculty contributions have consistently placed it among the top universities globally, particularly in fields like engineering, medicine, and the humanities. The university’s commitment to accessibility and financial aid further solidifies its reputation as a leader in higher education.
