**Cornell University** was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, and was established as New York's land-grant institution. Initially, it was inaugurated on October 7, 1868, with 412 male students. The university has historically been technologically innovative, becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883, and it has integrated academic research into its infrastructure.

Cornell University maintains a decentralized structure, operating through 16 colleges—including four statutory and twelve privately endowed colleges—which grant significant autonomy in managing academic programs and admissions. The university is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity," and its endowment stands at $10.7 billion as of 2024. It is a land-, space-, and sea-grant university, and it offers extensive international programs, including the Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar.
