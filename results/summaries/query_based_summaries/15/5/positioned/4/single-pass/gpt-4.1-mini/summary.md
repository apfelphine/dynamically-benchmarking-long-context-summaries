**Cornell University** was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution, with its main campus in Ithaca, New York. Emphasizing innovation and inclusivity from its early years, Cornell was among the first U.S. universities to be electrified and to allow alumni-elected trustees. The university expanded into international programs in the 21st century, opening Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar in 2004 and establishing academic partnerships globally. Its 745-acre main campus combines diverse architectural styles and picturesque natural surroundings, including botanical gardens and gorges, while satellite campuses in New York City focus on medicine and technology, notably through the Cornell Tech campus on Roosevelt Island.

Academically, Cornell is a decentralized research university with 16 colleges, including four statutory (publicly funded) and 12 endowed colleges, offering a broad range of undergraduate and graduate programs. It practices highly selective admissions with an acceptance rate of 6.9% for the undergraduate class of 2026 and has strong commitments to diversity and financial aid. The university’s endowment was $10.7 billion as of 2024, supporting significant philanthropic contributions and research initiatives. Cornell is internationally ranked, with strengths in architecture, business, and sciences, housing extensive library collections and operating the first U.S. university press. Its research spans major advances in physics, computing, and engineering, and it maintains a vibrant student life with over 1,000 organizations, a large Greek system, and active arts and athletics programs. Notable alumni include multiple Nobel laureates across diverse fields.
