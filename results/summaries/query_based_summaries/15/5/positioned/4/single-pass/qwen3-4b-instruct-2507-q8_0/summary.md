Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution. The university's main campus is located in Ithaca, New York, and spans approximately 745 acres, featuring a mix of architectural styles including Collegiate Gothic and modernist designs. The campus includes notable buildings such as the Andrew Dickson White House, Bailey Hall, and the Computing and Communications Center, several of which are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Cornell expanded its international presence by establishing Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar in 2004, and later developed a new technology-focused campus, Cornell Tech, on Roosevelt Island in New York City. The university also maintains a strong commitment to sustainability, with initiatives such as a gas-fired combined heat and power facility, an on-campus hydroelectric plant, and a lake source cooling system. In 2023, Cornell became the first university in the nation to commit to Kyoto Protocol emission reductions.

Academically, Cornell is a large, primarily residential research university with a decentralized structure. It operates 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed colleges and four publicly supported statutory colleges. The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, with a strong emphasis on research and innovation. It is classified as an R1: Doctoral University – Very High Research Activity and has consistently ranked among the top universities in the world in research output and academic excellence.

Cornell's academic profile includes a strong research culture, with a focus on science, technology, engineering, and mathematics. The university has produced numerous Nobel laureates and has a robust research infrastructure, including the Cornell University Center for Advanced Computing and the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. It is also home to the Cornell University Press, one of the country's largest university presses.

Student life at Cornell is vibrant, with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including clubs, cultural groups, and performing arts ensembles. The university has a strong tradition of student activism, with notable events such as the 1967 fire in the Residential Club dormitory and the 1969 occupation of Willard Straight Hall. The student government, including the Student Assembly and Graduate and Professional Student Assembly, allocates approximately $3 million annually to support clubs and organizations.

Cornell's reputation is highly regarded, with consistent top rankings in global university rankings. In 2024, it was ranked 10th in the U.S. and 12th in the world by QS World University Rankings and 20th in the world by Times Higher Education World University Rankings. The university is also recognized for its contributions to research, community service, and sustainability, and has been named the most sustainable university in the world by QS World University Rankings in 2024.
