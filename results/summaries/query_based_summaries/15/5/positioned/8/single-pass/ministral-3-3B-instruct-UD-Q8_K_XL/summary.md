Cornell University was founded in 1865 in Ithaca, New York, as New York’s land-grant institution, initially established to promote agricultural and technological education. Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White laid the groundwork, with Cornell providing a farm and White serving as the first president. The university quickly became renowned for its innovative approach, integrating research and practical applications into its curriculum.

Cornell’s academic structure includes 16 colleges and seven professional schools, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs. It operates on a 4-1-4 academic calendar, emphasizing a residential experience with a focus on interdisciplinary learning. The university is classified as a "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity," with significant contributions to fields like engineering, medicine, and computer science. Cornell’s research expenditures are substantial, with federal funding playing a key role in supporting its extensive research initiatives.

Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca is a blend of historic and modern architecture, set against the backdrop of Cayuga Lake and the Finger Lakes region. The campus is known for its sustainability efforts, including energy initiatives, renewable resources, and a commitment to net carbon neutrality by 2035. Cornell also operates campuses in New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech) and Qatar (Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar), as well as various research facilities and study abroad programs.

The university is highly selective, with a competitive admissions process that attracts a diverse student body. Cornell offers need-based financial aid and is known for its strong emphasis on undergraduate education. Its academic reputation is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, particularly in fields like engineering, medicine, and business.

Cornell’s student life is vibrant, with over 1,000 student organizations, a large Greek life system, and a variety of cultural, athletic, and social activities. The university is also recognized for its commitment to sustainability, community service, and fostering a global perspective among its students and faculty. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, prominent leaders in business and academia, and influential figures in science and technology.
