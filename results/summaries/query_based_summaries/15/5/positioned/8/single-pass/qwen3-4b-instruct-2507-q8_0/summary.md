Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, is a private, land-grant university located in Ithaca, New York. It operates as a decentralized institution with 16 colleges and schools, including the College of Arts and Sciences, College of Engineering, and Weill Cornell Medicine in New York City. The university also maintains a medical campus in Qatar and a technology-focused campus in New York City (Cornell Tech). 

Cornell's main campus spans 745 acres and features a blend of architectural styles, including Collegiate Gothic and modernist designs. The university emphasizes sustainability, with initiatives like the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and a commitment to net carbon neutrality by 2035. It has a large endowment of $10.7 billion and is a leader in research, with a strong focus on science, technology, and engineering. 

Undergraduate admissions are highly competitive, with a need-blind policy for U.S. applicants. The university offers a wide range of academic programs, including joint degree opportunities and international study abroad programs. Cornell is ranked among the top universities globally, consistently appearing in the top 10 in the U.S. and top 20 worldwide in various rankings. 

Student life is vibrant, with over 1,000 student organizations, including clubs, sports, and cultural groups. The university is known for its strong traditions, such as Slope Day and Dragon Day, and its diverse student body. Cornell also provides comprehensive financial aid, with a history of supporting students from low-income families. Notable alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates, scientists, and prominent figures in business, politics, and the arts.
