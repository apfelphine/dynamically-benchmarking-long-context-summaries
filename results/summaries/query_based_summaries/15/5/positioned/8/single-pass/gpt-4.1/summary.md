## Cornell University Summary

Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White in Ithaca, New York, is a private Ivy League institution and New York’s land-grant university. It has grown to comprise 16 colleges: four publicly funded statutory colleges and twelve endowed colleges, offering extensive undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs. Cornell operates major campuses in Ithaca, New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech), and in Qatar (Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar), as well as multiple research facilities and study-abroad programs worldwide. The university is recognized for its technological innovation, diverse academic offerings, and international reach.

Cornell ranks among the world’s top universities in research and academics, with a $10.7 billion endowment and significant fundraising achievements. It is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" institution, with $1.18 billion in annual research expenditures. The university is noted for its competitive admissions (6.9% admit rate for the Class of 2026), commitment to diversity and financial aid, and extensive student life including over 1,000 organizations, robust Greek life, and leading athletic programs. Cornell’s faculty and alumni include 62 Nobel laureates, and the university is known for advances in science, engineering, architecture, international relations, and sustainability efforts, including a strong commitment to carbon neutrality by 2035.
