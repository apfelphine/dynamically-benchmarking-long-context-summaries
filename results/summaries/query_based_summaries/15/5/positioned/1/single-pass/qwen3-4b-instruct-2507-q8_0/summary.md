Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution. It began operations in 1868 with 412 male students and established a tradition of integrating academic research into campus infrastructure, such as becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883. The university's governance is decentralized, with 16 colleges and schools operating significant autonomy over academic programs, admissions, and financial matters. 

Cornell's main campus in Ithaca, New York, is characterized by an irregular layout and a mix of architectural styles, including Collegiate Gothic and modernist designs. The campus features notable buildings such as the Andrew Dickson White House and the Robarts Library, and is recognized for its natural surroundings, including views of Cayuga Lake and surrounding valleys. The university has implemented sustainability initiatives, including a gas-fired combined heat and power facility, an on-campus hydroelectric plant, and a lake source cooling system.

Cornell is a large and primarily residential research university with a strong emphasis on research, ranking among the top institutions globally. It is home to the prestigious Weill Cornell Medicine, a joint medical school with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital, and the Cornell Tech campus in New York City, a technology-focused graduate campus. The university has a strong academic reputation, with notable achievements in fields such as computer science, engineering, and business, and is ranked among the top universities in the world by QS, THE, and U.S. News rankings.

Cornell offers a wide range of academic programs and research opportunities, with over 170 undergraduate and graduate programs. It has a strong commitment to diversity and inclusion, with a significant proportion of its student body from underrepresented backgrounds. The university provides extensive financial aid, including need-based scholarships and grants, and has a robust student life with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including clubs, sports teams, and cultural groups. 

Cornell's academic structure includes a 4–1–4 academic calendar and a strong focus on interdisciplinary education. The university has a long-standing tradition of academic excellence and innovation, with notable alumni including Nobel laureates, business leaders, and prominent figures in science, technology, and the arts. The university is also recognized for its contributions to sustainability, with initiatives such as the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and the Earth Source Heating project.
