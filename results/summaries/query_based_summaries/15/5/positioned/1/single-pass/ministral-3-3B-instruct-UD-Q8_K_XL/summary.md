Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as New York’s land-grant institution. Located in Ithaca, New York, it became renowned for its innovative academic programs, including the first U.S. university to allow alumni-elected trustees on its board. Cornell expanded its research focus in the late 19th century, adopting a state-funded model and establishing research and extension programs jointly supported by New York State and the federal government. The university faced challenges during the late 20th century, including student activism and governance restructuring, but maintained its reputation for academic excellence.

Cornell’s Ithaca campus, spanning 745 acres, is a blend of historic and modern architecture, with notable buildings like Morrill Hall and the Computing and Communications Center. The university operates several satellite campuses, including Weill Cornell Medical College in New York City and Cornell Tech in Roosevelt Island. Cornell is a leader in research, with a strong emphasis on science, technology, engineering, and medicine, and consistently ranks among the top universities globally. It offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, with a notable focus on interdisciplinary studies and international partnerships. Cornell’s commitment to sustainability and innovation has earned it recognition in global rankings and initiatives.
