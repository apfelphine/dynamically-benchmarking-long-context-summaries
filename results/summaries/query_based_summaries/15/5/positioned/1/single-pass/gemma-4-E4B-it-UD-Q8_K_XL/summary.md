Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as New York's land-grant institution. It formally inaugurated on October 7, 1868, and developed as a technologically innovative institution, incorporating academic research into its infrastructure. The university became one of the first electrified universities in 1883 and allowed alumni-elected trustees on its board in 1872.

Today, Cornell is a large, primarily residential research university, boasting a significant global reputation. It offers numerous undergraduate and graduate programs and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity." Its main campus is located in Ithaca, New York, and features diverse architectural styles. Furthermore, Cornell expands its reach internationally through programs like Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and maintains various research facilities and cooperative extension services across the United States.
