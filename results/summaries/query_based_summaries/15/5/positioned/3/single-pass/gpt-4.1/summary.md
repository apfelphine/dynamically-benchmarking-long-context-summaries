## Cornell University: Key Facts and Features

Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White in Ithaca, New York, is a renowned Ivy League, land-grant institution known for its innovation in higher education, research, and outreach. Its main campus in Ithaca spans 745 acres, featuring a diverse architectural landscape and abundant natural surroundings, including two gorges and the Cornell Botanic Gardens. Cornell also operates significant campuses in New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech) and internationally (Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar).

Cornell comprises 16 colleges and schools, including four statutory colleges supported by New York State, and emphasizes a decentralized academic and administrative structure. The university is highly selective in admissions and committed to need-blind policies for U.S. students, with substantial financial aid options. Cornell offers a broad array of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs, and is recognized for its international collaborations, joint degree programs, and study abroad opportunities. With an endowment of $10.7 billion, Cornell ranks among the nation's top research universities and is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity," spending over $1.18 billion on research annually.

Cornell is celebrated for its academic excellence, routinely ranking among the top universities globally, with strong programs in architecture, business, international relations, engineering, and philosophy. The Cornell University Library is among the largest academic libraries in the U.S., and Cornell University Press is a leading publisher. Student life at Cornell is vibrant, featuring more than 1,000 student organizations, a robust Greek system, diverse housing options, and a rich tradition of athletics and outdoor education. Cornell boasts over 250,000 living alumni, including distinguished Nobel laureates, heads of state, and leaders in various fields. As of 2024, Cornell counted 62 Nobel laureates among its faculty and alumni.
