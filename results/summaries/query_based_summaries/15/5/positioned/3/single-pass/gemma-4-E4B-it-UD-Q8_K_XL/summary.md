Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as New York's land-grant institution. It was formally inaugurated on October 7, 1868, with 412 male students enrolling. The university has historically focused on being a technologically innovative institution, integrating academic research into its infrastructure.

Cornell's campus in Ithaca, New York, spans approximately 745 acres and features a blend of architectural styles, including ornate Collegiate Gothic and modernist structures. The university is a land-, space-, and sea-grant institution. It maintains a decentralized structure, operating through 16 colleges—including four statutory colleges (Agriculture and Life Sciences, Human Ecology, Industrial and Labor Relations, and Veterinary Medicine)—and seven schools offering graduate and professional programs. The university's endowment stands at $10.7 billion as of 2024, and it has established Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar.
