Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as New York’s land-grant institution. Located in Ithaca, New York, the university is renowned for its innovative academic programs, research contributions, and commitment to sustainability. Cornell operates on a decentralized structure with 16 colleges, including four publicly supported statutory colleges and 12 privately endowed ones, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs. The university is known for its strong emphasis on interdisciplinary research, entrepreneurial initiatives, and international collaborations.

Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca is expansive, featuring historic and modern buildings, extensive green spaces, and a blend of architectural styles. It is recognized for its commitment to sustainability, including initiatives like the Atkinson Center for Sustainability, which supports research and education on environmental challenges. The university also has notable off-campus facilities, such as Weill Cornell Medical College in New York City, Cornell Tech in Manhattan, and a medical campus in Qatar. Cornell’s academic reputation is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, particularly in fields like engineering, medicine, and business.

Cornell University has a long history of academic excellence, innovation, and community engagement, making it a prominent institution in higher education.
