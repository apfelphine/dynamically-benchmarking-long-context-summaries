**Cornell University** is a large, primarily residential research university located in Ithaca, New York. Founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, it was established as New York's land-grant institution and is known for its innovative academic programs and strong emphasis on research. The university operates a decentralized structure with 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed colleges and four publicly supported statutory colleges, each exercising significant autonomy in academic programming, admissions, and degree conferment. 

Cornell's main campus spans approximately 745 acres and features a mix of architectural styles, including Collegiate Gothic, Victorian, and modernist designs. The campus includes notable buildings such as Morrill Hall, a National Historic Landmark, and the Andrew Dickson White House. The university is committed to sustainability, with initiatives like a gas-fired combined heat and power facility, an on-campus hydroelectric plant, and a lake source cooling system. It also operates a six-solar-farm system and an enhanced geothermal system, aiming for net carbon neutrality by 2035.

Cornell is a leading research university, classified as an R1 institution with high research activity. It has a substantial endowment of $10.7 billion, the 14th-largest among U.S. universities, and has received major philanthropic support, including a $100 million gift from Sanford I. Weill and a $1 billion donation from Chuck Feeney for Cornell Tech. The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, including seven undergraduate colleges and seven graduate schools. 

The academic calendar follows a 4–1–4 structure, with fall, winter, and spring terms. Cornell is highly competitive in admissions, with a middle-50% SAT range of 700–760 in reading and writing and 750–800 in math. It practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and offers significant financial aid, with over 40% of full-time first-year students receiving financial need support. 

Student life at Cornell includes over 1,000 registered student organizations, such as clubs, sports teams, and cultural groups. The university has a robust residential system with North, West, and Collegetown campuses, and coeducational housing began in 1971. The Big Red Marching Band, the Cornell Glee Club, and the Outdoor Education and Outdoor Odyssey program are prominent student activities. 

Cornell is globally recognized for its academic excellence, ranking 10th in the U.S. and 12th in the world in QS World University Rankings and 20th in the world in Times Higher Education World University Rankings. It is known for its contributions to research, community service, and sustainability. The university is home to numerous faculty and alumni who have won Nobel Prizes, including 62 Nobel laureates. Notable alumni include heads of state, scientists, authors, and business leaders such as Thomas Pynchon, E. B. White, and Bill Nye. 

Cornell also maintains international programs, including Weill Cornell Medicine in Qatar and partnerships with institutions in India, China, and Singapore. The university has a strong commitment to diversity, with a student body that includes a significant number of underrepresented minorities. It is also recognized for its contributions to entrepreneurship, with a dedicated entrepreneurship arm and a successful track record of startup formation.
