Cornell University, founded in 1865, is a leading land-grant, sea-grant, and space-grant research university in Ithaca, New York. Established by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, it initially focused on technological and agricultural education but has since expanded into a comprehensive research institution. Cornell’s academic structure includes 16 colleges and schools, offering a wide range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs. The university is renowned for its strong research output, particularly in science, technology, engineering, and medicine, and consistently ranks among the top universities globally.

The Ithaca campus, spanning approximately 745 acres, is a blend of historic and modern architecture, with notable buildings like Morrill Hall and the Computing and Communications Center listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Cornell also operates campuses in New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech) and Qatar (Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar). The university emphasizes sustainability, with initiatives like the Atkinson Center for Sustainability and a commitment to achieving net carbon neutrality by 2035.

Cornell’s admissions are highly competitive, with a strong emphasis on academic merit. The university offers extensive financial aid, including need-based scholarships, and maintains a diverse student body. Cornell is known for its vibrant student life, with over 1,000 student organizations, a robust Greek life system, and a variety of athletic teams competing in NCAA Division I. Notable alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates, political leaders, scientists, and influential figures in various fields. Cornell’s reputation for innovation, research excellence, and community engagement continues to solidify its status as a premier educational institution.
