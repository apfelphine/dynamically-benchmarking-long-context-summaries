Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, and was established as New York's land-grant institution. It was formally inaugurated on October 7, 1868, with 412 male students enrolling. Cornell has developed as a technologically innovative institution, incorporating academic research into its infrastructure, and became one of the first electrified universities in 1883.

Cornell's main campus is located in Ithaca, New York, spanning approximately 745 acres and featuring a blend of architectural styles. The university maintains a decentralized structure with 16 colleges—including four statutory colleges (College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, College of Human Ecology, School of Industrial and Labor Relations, and College of Veterinary Medicine)—and seven private, endowed colleges. The university's endowment stands at $10.7 billion as of 2024. Cornell is known for its commitment to sustainability, having established the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and aiming for net carbon neutrality by 2035. Furthermore, it maintains an extensive international presence through its Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and numerous study abroad programs.
