Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution. It began with a campus in Ithaca, New York, and has since expanded to 745 acres, featuring diverse architectural styles and notable landmarks such as Morrill Hall, a National Historic Landmark. The university is known for its technological innovation, including becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883 and establishing the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future in 2007. It operates a decentralized structure with 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed and four statutory colleges, and maintains a strong emphasis on research, with a significant endowment of $10.7 billion as of 2024. 

Cornell has a robust international presence, with Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and partnerships in India, China, and Singapore. The university has a strong academic reputation, routinely ranking among the top institutions globally, with 2024 rankings placing it 10th in the U.S. and 12th worldwide. It is also recognized for its research output, with top rankings in fields such as astrophysics, chemistry, and engineering. 

Student life at Cornell includes over 1,000 registered student organizations, a vibrant campus culture, and a commitment to sustainability, including a gas-fired combined heat and power facility and a lake source cooling system. The university has a diverse student body and offers a wide range of study abroad and scholarship programs. It also maintains a strong tradition of student activism and has a notable history of student-led protests and social movements. In 2025, Cornell agreed to pay $30 million to the federal government and invest $30 million in agricultural research to address campus antisemitism and DEI practices.
