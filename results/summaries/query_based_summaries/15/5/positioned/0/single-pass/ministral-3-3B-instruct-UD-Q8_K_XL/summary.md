Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, emerged as a land-grant institution in New York State, initially offering education in agriculture, liberal arts, and sciences. Established on Ezra Cornell’s farm in Ithaca, the university quickly became a leader in technological innovation, becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883. Cornell’s early development was marked by a focus on research and outreach, with the university incorporating state-funded colleges in 1894 and administering joint research and extension programs with New York State and the U.S. federal government.

By the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Cornell expanded its academic offerings and became a hub for student activism, particularly during the 1960s, when it faced heightened protests related to civil rights, cultural issues, and opposition to the Vietnam War. Notable events included the occupation of Willard Straight Hall by anti-war protesters and the resignation of President James Alfred Perkins. Cornell’s academic reputation grew significantly, with its Ph.D. programs ranking among the top in the nation, including standout performances in fields like astrophysics, chemistry, and engineering.

Cornell’s governance is structured with a decentralized system, comprising 16 colleges with significant autonomy over admissions, scholarships, and academic programs. The university operates eCornell, offering online professional development and certificate programs, and participates in New York’s land-grant, sea-grant, and space-grant programs. Cornell’s financial endowment stands at $10.7 billion, making it one of the largest among U.S. universities, with major contributions from philanthropists like Jeffrey S. Lehman and Herbert Fisk Johnson III.

The university’s main campus in Ithaca, New York, is renowned for its historic architecture and scenic surroundings, including Fall Creek and Cascadilla Gorges. Cornell is committed to sustainability, with initiatives like a gas-fired combined heat and power facility, a lake source cooling system, and a focus on net carbon neutrality by 2035. The university also operates campuses in New York City, including Weill Cornell Medical College and Cornell Tech, and has international programs in Qatar and partnerships with institutions worldwide.

Cornell’s academic strengths span a wide array of disciplines, with notable rankings in fields like architecture, business, and international relations. The university’s research output is substantial, with its faculty contributing to advancements in nuclear physics, space exploration, and computing technology. Cornell’s student life is vibrant, featuring over 1,000 student organizations, a large fraternity and sorority system, and a variety of cultural and athletic activities. The university’s notable alumni include figures in politics, science, and business, with Cornell being the only institution to have four female Nobel laureates.
