Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as a land-grant institution to promote education and innovation. Located in Ithaca, New York, the university is renowned for its academic excellence, blending traditional liberal arts with cutting-edge research across disciplines. Cornell operates on a 4-1-4 academic calendar, offering a diverse range of undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs through its 16 colleges and schools, including the College of Arts and Sciences, Cornell Johnson Graduate School of Management, and Weill Cornell Medical College.

The university is known for its strong emphasis on research, sustainability, and international engagement. Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca features historic and modern architecture, with notable buildings like Morrill Hall and the Atkinson Center for Sustainability. It also has campuses in New York City, including Weill Cornell Medical College and Cornell Tech, and a medical college in Qatar. Cornell is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, celebrated for its contributions to fields like engineering, medicine, and the arts.

Cornell’s governance is structured with a board of trustees, including alumni-elected representatives, and a president who oversees the university’s strategic direction. The university’s commitment to accessibility is highlighted by its need-blind admission policy and substantial financial aid initiatives. Cornell’s vibrant student life includes over 1,000 student organizations, a strong Greek life tradition, and a focus on outdoor education and athletics. The university’s mission remains rooted in its founding principles: "I found it a university and it has grown into a great university."
