Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, serving as New York's land-grant institution. It was formally inaugurated on October 7, 1868, with 412 male students enrolled. Cornell developed as a technologically innovative institution, becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883. The university administers research and extension programs jointly funded by New York State and the U.S. federal government, and it was one of the first U.S. universities to allow alumni-elected trustees on its board in 1872.

Today, Cornell operates with a decentralized structure, comprising seven undergraduate colleges and seven schools for graduate and professional programs. Its main campus is in Ithaca, New York, featuring a diverse mix of architectural styles. Cornell maintains a substantial endowment of \$10.7 billion and is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity." The university is committed to sustainability, having established the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and committed to net carbon neutrality by 2035. Cornell also maintains an international presence, including Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and various partnerships across continents.
