Cornell University is a large, primarily residential research university located in Ithaca, New York, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White. It is a land-, sea-, and space-grant university with a decentralized structure, operating through 16 colleges, including seven undergraduate colleges and several graduate schools. The university's main campus spans approximately 745 acres and features a mix of architectural styles, historic landmarks, and natural surroundings including Cayuga Lake and the Cornell Botanic Gardens. Cornell is known for its strong emphasis on sustainability, with initiatives such as the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future, geothermal energy, and solar farms, and has committed to achieving net carbon neutrality by 2035.

Cornell operates additional campuses, including Weill Cornell Medicine in New York City, Weill Cornell Graduate School of Medical Sciences, and Cornell Tech on Roosevelt Island, which focuses on technology and engineering. The university has a significant endowment of $10.7 billion and is recognized for its research excellence, with a strong presence in fields such as engineering, computer science, and life sciences. It is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, including 10th in the U.S. and 12th worldwide in the QS World University Rankings, and is known for its contributions to research, community service, and sustainability.

Academically, Cornell offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, with a strong focus on interdisciplinary studies and innovation. The university employs a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is known for its rigorous admissions process, with a high acceptance rate for students who meet the middle-50% SAT and ACT score ranges. Financial aid is need-blind for U.S. applicants, and the university is committed to equitable access. Student life at Cornell is vibrant, with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including numerous cultural, political, and academic groups. The university has a strong tradition of student activism and is home to a large fraternity and sorority system.

Cornell is also notable for its prominent alumni and faculty, including 62 Nobel laureates, and has produced influential figures in science, business, politics, and the arts. The university's governance includes a 64-member Board of Trustees and a President who serves as the chief executive officer. It maintains a strong commitment to diversity, with a student body that includes a significant number of international students and underrepresented minorities. The university is recognized for its contributions to research, innovation, and community engagement, and continues to expand its global presence through international partnerships and research initiatives.
