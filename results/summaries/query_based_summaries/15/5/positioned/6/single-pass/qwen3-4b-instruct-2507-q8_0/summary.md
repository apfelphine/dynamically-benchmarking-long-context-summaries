Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution. It established its main campus in Ithaca, New York, on a 745-acre site, and has expanded significantly since its founding. The university is known for its strong emphasis on research, innovation, and academic excellence, with a diverse range of undergraduate and graduate programs across 16 colleges and schools. Cornell is a major research university, classified as "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity," and is recognized globally for its research output, with a $1.18 billion R&D budget in 2021. It has a large endowment of $10.7 billion, making it the 14th-largest university endowment in the U.S. The university operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is a land-, sea-, and space-grant institution. 

Cornell’s academic structure includes seven undergraduate colleges and seven graduate schools, with the College of Arts and Sciences being the largest by enrollment. The university is renowned for its strong academic programs, including its programs in engineering, computer science, and the humanities. Notable achievements include the establishment of Weill Cornell Medicine in New York City and Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar, which is the first U.S. medical school established outside the United States. The university also operates a technology-focused campus, Cornell Tech, in New York City, designed to foster entrepreneurship and innovation. 

Cornell is highly competitive in admissions, with a middle-50% SAT range of 700–760 in evidence-based reading and writing and 750–800 in math. It practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and offers significant financial aid, with a strong commitment to student access and equity. The university has a rich tradition of student life, with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including a large fraternity and sorority system, and a vibrant student media presence. 

Cornell’s academic reputation is outstanding, with consistent top rankings in global university rankings. In 2024, it ranked 10th in the U.S. and 12th in the world by QS World University Rankings and 20th in the world by Times Higher Education World University Rankings. The university is also recognized for its contributions to sustainability, with initiatives such as a net-zero carbon neutrality goal by 2035 and a climate solutions fund. 

Notable faculty and alumni include Nobel laureates, prominent leaders in science, business, and public service, and influential figures in various fields. The university has produced over 250,000 living alumni, including 34 Marshall Scholars and 31 Rhodes Scholars. Cornell is also known for its strong commitment to diversity, with a significant proportion of its student body identifying as underrepresented minorities. The university fosters a culture of innovation, academic rigor, and community engagement, and continues to expand its global reach through international partnerships and research initiatives.
