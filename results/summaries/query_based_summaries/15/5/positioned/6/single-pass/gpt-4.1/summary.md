## Cornell University Summary

Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, is a private Ivy League research university and New York's land-grant institution located in Ithaca, New York. It has developed a distinctive decentralized structure, comprising 16 colleges and schools, including both privately endowed and state-supported statutory colleges, each with significant autonomy. Cornell operates additional campuses in New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech), Qatar (Weill Cornell Medical College), and maintains various research facilities and international study programs. The university’s expansive Ithaca campus is noted for its blend of historic and modern architecture, natural surroundings, and sustainability initiatives.

Academically, Cornell is renowned for its rigorous and competitive admissions, diverse student body, and robust financial aid programs. The university offers comprehensive undergraduate, graduate, and professional curricula, and is consistently ranked among the top global universities. Cornell is a major research institution, classified as R1, with over $1 billion in annual R&D expenditures and notable contributions in science, technology, engineering, and philosophy. Student life at Cornell features over 1,000 organizations, active Greek life, vibrant traditions, and a strong athletics program. As of 2024, Cornell’s endowment stands at $10.7 billion, supporting its mission of research, outreach, and educational innovation. Notable alumni include heads of state, Nobel laureates, inventors, and leaders across various fields.
