Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, as New York's land-grant institution. Initially, Cornell developed as a technologically innovative institution, incorporating academic research into its infrastructure and outreach. The university became one of the first electrified universities in 1883 and allowed alumni-elected trustees on its board in 1872.

Cornell University has consistently ranked among the world's best academic institutions. It is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity." The university's main campus is located in Ithaca, New York, covering approximately 745 acres. It operates a decentralized structure featuring 16 colleges, of which four are statutory colleges (Agriculture and Life Sciences, Human Ecology, Industrial and Labor Relations, and Veterinary Medicine). Cornell's endowment stands at $10.7 billion as of 2024.
