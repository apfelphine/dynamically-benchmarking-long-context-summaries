**Cornell University** was founded on April 27, 1865, by entrepreneur Ezra Cornell and educator Andrew Dickson White in Ithaca, New York, as New York’s land-grant institution. Ezra Cornell donated his farm as the initial campus site and provided a $500,000 endowment. The university was formally opened in 1868 with 412 male students. It rapidly developed as an innovative institution, becoming one of the first electrified campuses in 1883 and incorporating state-funded colleges since 1894. Cornell’s academic governance includes a blend of private and public involvement, governed by a 64-member board of trustees with diverse appointed and elected members. The university operates a large main campus in Ithaca, additional campuses in New York City, and international programs including Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar.

Cornell is a prominent research university with strong programs in engineering, sciences, humanities, and business. It maintains an extensive library system and publishes numerous academic journals. Admissions are highly competitive, with a 6.9% undergraduate acceptance rate in 2022 and significant diversity among students. Financial aid initiatives have expanded to replace loans with scholarships for lower-income students. Cornell’s research strengths include contributions in nuclear physics, computing, and sustainability, supported by substantial federal funding and endowed gifts. Student life features a large fraternity and sorority system, over a thousand student organizations, extensive housing options, and a strong athletic presence in the Ivy League. The university emphasizes sustainability with multiple green energy projects and has implemented several initiatives for academic and community engagement worldwide.
