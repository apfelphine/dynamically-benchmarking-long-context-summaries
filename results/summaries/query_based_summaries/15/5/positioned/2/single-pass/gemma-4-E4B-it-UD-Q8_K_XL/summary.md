Cornell University was founded on April 27, 1865, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, following authorization by the New York State Legislature to serve as New York's land-grant institution. It was formally inaugurated on October 7, 1868. The university has historically been technologically innovative, becoming one of the first electrified universities in 1883, and it administers research and extension programs jointly funded by the New York State and the U.S. federal government.

Cornell's academic standing is high, with its Ph.D. programs being nationally ranked, and its College of Arts and Humanities ranked fifth in the nation in 1995. The university is organized into 16 colleges, including four statutory colleges (College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, College of Human Ecology, School of Industrial and Labor Relations, and College of Veterinary Medicine), and nine privately endowed, non-statutory colleges. The university's main campus is in Ithaca, New York, and it maintains a commitment to sustainability through numerous green initiatives.
