## Summary of Cornell University

Founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant university, Cornell University has developed into a prestigious institution known for its pioneering research and diverse academic programs. The main campus resides in Ithaca, New York, spanning approximately 745 acres, featuring a variety of architectural styles and historic buildings. Beyond Ithaca, Cornell operates notable campuses in New York City—including Weill Cornell Medical College and Cornell Tech—and an international medical college in Qatar. The university emphasizes sustainability, with initiatives such as solar farms and geothermal systems, aiming for carbon neutrality by 2035.

Cornell is governed by a 64-member Board of Trustees and comprises 16 autonomous colleges and schools offering extensive undergraduate and graduate programs. It boasts an endowment of $10.7 billion (2024) and a strong philanthropic tradition. Academically, it is regularly ranked among the world’s top universities, with excellence in areas including engineering, architecture, business, and sciences. Its library system is one of the largest in the U.S., and the university maintains a rich tradition of faculty and student publications. Cornell is a major research university with high R&D expenditures, notable contributions in physics, computing, and engineering, and a vibrant student life featuring over 1,000 organizations and active Greek life. Notable alumni include heads of state, Nobel laureates, prominent judges, authors, and innovators.
