Cornell University is a large, residential research university located in Ithaca, New York, and is classified as an R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity. It operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is a land-, sea-, and space-grant university. The university has a decentralized structure with 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed and four publicly supported statutory colleges, each exercising significant autonomy in academic programs, admissions, advising, and degree conferment.

Cornell's main campus in Ithaca spans approximately 745 acres and features a diverse architectural landscape, including Collegiate Gothic, Victorian, and modernist buildings. The campus includes notable landmarks such as the Andrew Dickson White House, Morrill Hall (a National Historic Landmark), and the Cornell Botanic Gardens. The university also has a strong commitment to sustainability, with initiatives like solar farms, geothermal systems, and the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future, which received an $80 million gift in 2007.

Cornell has a highly competitive admissions process, with a 2022 acceptance rate of 6.9% for the Class of 2026. The university is known for its diverse student body, with 34.2% of admitted students identifying as underrepresented minorities and a significant number of first-generation college students. It practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and offers robust financial aid, including need-based scholarships and loans.

The university has a strong international presence, with programs in Asia, the Middle East, and Qatar. It operates Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar, which offers a full four-year MD program and a two-year pre-medical program. Cornell also has a significant international outreach through programs like the Global Alliance in Management Education and partnerships with institutions in India, China, and Singapore.

Cornell's academic programs are diverse and include a wide range of undergraduate and graduate degrees. The university is particularly renowned in engineering, computer science, and the social sciences. Its research is highly regarded, with the National Science Foundation ranking it 14th in the U.S. for R&D expenditures in 2021. The university has a strong reputation in fields such as physics, chemistry, and computer science, and its faculty includes 62 Nobel laureates.

Cornell University offers a vibrant student life with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including a wide range of cultural, political, and athletic groups. The university has a strong tradition in student activism, with notable events such as the 1969 occupation of Willard Straight Hall. The student government, including the Student Assembly and the Graduate and Professional Student Assembly, allocates approximately $3.0 million annually to support student organizations.

The university's athletic teams, known as the Cornell Big Red, compete in NCAA Division I, primarily in the Ivy League and the Eastern College Athletic Conference (ECAC). Cornell has a strong tradition in men's wrestling, men's lacrosse, men's ice hockey, and rowing. The university also has a large outdoor education program, serving over 20,000 people annually with more than 130 courses.

Cornell has a strong emphasis on community and service, with programs like the Cornell Outdoor Education and Outdoor Odyssey, which provide outdoor courses and pre-orientation trips. The university also has a robust health and wellness program, including professional and peer counseling services and on-campus outpatient care through Cornell Health.

Cornell University has a strong alumni network with over 250,000 living alumni, including 34 Marshall Scholars and 31 Rhodes Scholars. The university is known for producing notable alumni in various fields, including politics, science, and the arts. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates Barbara McClintock and Toni Morrison, as well as inventors Willis Carrier and Robert Moog. The university also has a strong tradition of academic excellence, with faculty members including Nobel laureates such as Hans Bethe and Carl Sagan.
