# Summary of Cornell University

Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution, is a large residential research university with its main campus located in Ithaca, New York. The campus features diverse architectural styles and natural surroundings, including views of Cayuga Lake and the Cornell Botanic Gardens. Cornell operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is a land-, sea-, and space-grant university. Admission is highly competitive, with a 6.9% acceptance rate for the undergraduate Class of 2026, and the university practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants. Cornell emphasizes financial aid and international programs, including its Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and various global partnerships.

Cornell's research spans multiple disciplines, notably in science, engineering, and philosophy, with significant contributions in nuclear physics, cyberinfrastructure, and high-energy physics. The university maintains a large and historic academic library system, including operation of the arXiv e-print archive. Student life is vibrant with over 1,000 registered organizations, a substantial Greek life, performing arts, outdoor education, and competitive athletics, mainly in the Ivy League. The university supports professional and peer counseling services and has implemented safety measures regarding student health and mental well-being. Cornell boasts a distinguished alumni and faculty network, including 62 Nobel laureates, influential leaders, inventors, authors, and public officials.
