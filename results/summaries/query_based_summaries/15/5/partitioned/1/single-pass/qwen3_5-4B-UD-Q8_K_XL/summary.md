Cornell University is a large, primarily residential research university founded in 1865 as New York State's land-grant institution, characterized by a decentralized structure with 16 colleges operating significant autonomy. The campus system includes the main 745-acre site in Ithaca, New York, featuring a mix of historic Collegiate Gothic and modernist architecture, alongside specialized facilities such as Cornell Tech in New York City, the Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar, and numerous off-campus research stations in marine, agricultural, and ecological fields. Academically, Cornell is an R1 research university with a highly competitive admissions process and a diverse student body, offering extensive international programs, joint degree options, and a strong emphasis on sustainability and social mobility.

Governed by a 64-member Board of Trustees and led by a President appointed by the board, the university maintains a substantial endowment and a reputation for excellence in engineering, natural sciences, and the arts. Its financial support relies heavily on philanthropy, with major contributions from alumni fueling campus development and research initiatives. Cornell is a member of the Ivy League and the Russell Group, consistently ranking among the top universities in the United States and the world. Student life is vibrant, supported by over 1,000 registered organizations, a large Greek life system, and robust athletic programs, while administrative policies address issues ranging from campus safety to student representation and financial aid.
