# Cornell University

Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White as New York's land-grant institution, has developed into a prestigious research university known for its technological innovation and academic excellence. Its main campus is located in Ithaca, New York, spanning approximately 745 acres with diverse architectural styles, historic landmarks, and notable natural surroundings such as Cayuga Lake and the Cornell Botanic Gardens. The university has implemented significant sustainability initiatives, including solar power farms, geothermal systems, and a Center for a Sustainable Future funded by major alumni donations.

Cornell operates additional campuses and facilities, including the Weill Cornell Medical College in New York City affiliated with prominent hospitals, and the Cornell Tech campus on Roosevelt Island focused on technology and entrepreneurship. It opened Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar as the first U.S. medical school abroad. The university encompasses sixteen colleges—twelve endowed privately and four statutory—offering undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs with significant autonomy. Governed by a 64-member board of trustees, Cornell had an endowment of $10.7 billion as of 2024, supported by major gifts that have shaped its professional schools and research initiatives. The university maintains extensive research facilities, cooperative extension services across New York State, and diverse international academic programs.
