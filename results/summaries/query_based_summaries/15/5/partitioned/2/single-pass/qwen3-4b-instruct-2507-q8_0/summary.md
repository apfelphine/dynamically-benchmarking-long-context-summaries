**Cornell University** is a large, primarily residential research university in Ithaca, New York, established in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White. It operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is classified as an R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity. It is a land-, space-, and sea-grant university, and until 2014, also a sun-grant university.

Admission is highly competitive, with a 2022 acceptance rate of 6.9% for the Class of 2026. The university attracts a diverse student body, including a significant number of underrepresented minorities and international students. It practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and offers need-based financial aid, with a strong emphasis on reducing student debt—average debt upon graduation was $21,549 in 2010.

Cornell is ranked among the top universities globally, consistently appearing in the top 10 in the U.S. and top 12 worldwide in QS and THE rankings. It is particularly renowned for its programs in architecture, business, and international relations, with its Bachelor of Architecture program ranked best in the nation for much of the 21st century. The Johnson Graduate School of Management is ranked 9th nationally in Forbes and 11th globally in Bloomberg Businessweek.

The university's academic units and student groups publish numerous scholarly journals, including *The Philosophical Review* and *Physical Review*. The Cornell University Library, with over 10 million holdings, is the 13th-largest academic library in the U.S. Cornell University Press, established in 1869, is one of the country's largest university presses.

Cornell conducts extensive research in science, technology, engineering, and medicine, contributing significantly to patenting and startup formation. It has played a leading role in Mars exploration and operates the Cornell Electron Storage Ring. It also has a strong presence in international research, including the Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar and the Bridging the Rift Center in Israel–Jordan.

The university has a decentralized structure with 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed and four publicly supported statutory colleges. It is governed by a 64-member board of trustees, with a life trustee representing the descendants of Ezra Cornell. The university's endowment stands at $10.7 billion, making it the 14th-largest in the U.S.

Student life is vibrant, featuring over 1,000 registered student organizations, including a large fraternity and sorority system. The Big Red Marching Band and the Cornell International Affairs Society are notable groups. The university provides robust student support, including counseling, health services, and a strong focus on sustainability, with initiatives like the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and a commitment to net carbon neutrality by 2035.

Cornell's notable alumni include Nobel laureates, heads of state, and prominent leaders in science, business, and public service. The university has a strong tradition of academic excellence, with 62 Nobel laureates among its faculty and alumni. It is also known for its commitment to equity, diversity, and inclusion, with a history of affirmative action and support for underrepresented groups.
