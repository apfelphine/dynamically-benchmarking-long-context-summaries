**Cornell University** is a large, primarily residential research university that operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar. It is a land-, space-, and sea-grant university and is governed by a 64-member board of trustees, which is responsible for electing its President. Cornell's academic structure features seven undergraduate colleges and seven graduate/professional schools, with its four statutory colleges being Agriculture and Life Sciences, Human Ecology, Industrial and Labor Relations, and Veterinary Medicine.

Cornell maintains a highly competitive admission process; for the Class of 2026 in fall 2022, only 6.9% of the 71,164 applicants were accepted. The university is deeply involved in research across science, technology, and engineering, and has advanced areas like parallel computing. Financially, its endowment stands at $10.7 billion, and the institution emphasizes equal access through need-blind admissions for U.S. applicants and has expanded international programs, notably through Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar.
