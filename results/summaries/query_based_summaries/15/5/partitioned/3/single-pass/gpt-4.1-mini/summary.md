# Summary of Cornell University

Cornell University was founded in 1865 as New York's land-grant institution and has since developed into a large, primarily residential research university with significant academic prestige. Its main campus spans approximately 745 acres in Ithaca, New York, featuring diverse architectural styles and numerous facilities including academic buildings, laboratories, museums, and residential areas. The university operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is accredited by the Middle States Commission on Higher Education. Cornell practices highly selective undergraduate admissions, with a 6.9% acceptance rate for the class of 2026, and offers comprehensive financial aid programs emphasizing need-based support. The university sustains an extensive international program with study abroad opportunities on six continents, joint degrees with global institutions, and runs Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar.

Organized as a decentralized nonprofit, Cornell comprises 16 colleges with strong autonomy and is governed by a 64-member board of trustees. The university has a robust research profile, classified as “R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity,” with $1.18 billion in R&D expenditures in 2021. It excels in science, technology, and engineering research, contributing to nuclear physics, space exploration, automotive safety, and computing innovations, and holds a prominent place in patent applications and startup formation. Its library system ranks among the largest in the U.S. The New York City presence includes Cornell Tech on Roosevelt Island, a technology-focused graduate campus supported by major philanthropic gifts, enhancing entrepreneurship and innovation. Cornell’s endowment stands at $10.7 billion, bolstered by transformative donations supporting academic programs, sustainability initiatives, and campus expansions.
