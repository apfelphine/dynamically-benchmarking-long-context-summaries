Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, is a prominent land-, space-, and sea-grant research university located in Ithaca, New York. The university is renowned for its academic excellence, innovation, and commitment to sustainability. Cornell operates on a 4-1-4 academic calendar, offering a diverse range of undergraduate and graduate programs across its 16 colleges and schools.

### **Key Academic Highlights**
- **Research and Innovation:** Cornell is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity," with substantial contributions to fields like nuclear physics, space exploration, automotive safety, and computing. It is the fourth globally for producing graduates pursuing PhDs in engineering and natural sciences and fifth for graduates in any field.
- **Notable Programs:** Cornell’s College of Engineering, College of Arts and Sciences, and Weill Cornell Medical College are particularly renowned. The university also excels in international relations, sustainability, and entrepreneurship.
- **Campus:** Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca spans approximately 745 acres, featuring historic buildings, modern research facilities, and scenic natural surroundings. The university has also established significant off-campus research facilities and programs in New York City, Qatar, and other locations.

### **Student Life and Diversity**
- **Student Body:** Cornell attracts a highly diverse student population, with a significant number of international students and a strong commitment to inclusivity.
- **Student Organizations:** Over 1,000 registered student organizations cater to a wide array of interests, including sports, cultural groups, academic clubs, and professional societies.
- **Campus Traditions:** Cornell is known for its rich traditions, such as Slope Day and Dragon Day, which celebrate academic achievements and campus culture.

### **Campus Facilities and Sustainability**
- **Campus Facilities:** Cornell’s Ithaca campus includes residential colleges, academic buildings, athletic centers, auditoriums, museums, and dining facilities. The university is also home to the Cornell Botanic Gardens and various research centers.
- **Sustainability:** Cornell has made significant strides in sustainability, including the establishment of the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future, solar farms, and an enhanced geothermal system. The university aims to achieve net carbon neutrality by 2035.

### **Notable Contributions and Rankings**
- **Research and Rankings:** Cornell consistently ranks among the top universities globally, particularly in fields like engineering, medicine, and sustainability. It is recognized for its contributions to research, community service, social mobility, and sustainability.
- **Notable Alumni and Faculty:** Cornell has produced numerous Nobel laureates, Rhodes Scholars, and other distinguished figures, including prominent leaders in science, business, and public service.

### **Governance and Administration**
- **Board of Trustees:** Cornell’s governance is overseen by a 64-member board of trustees, which includes alumni-elected trustees, faculty-elected trustees, student-elected trustees, and non-academic staff-elected trustees.
- **Academic Structure:** The university is organized into 16 colleges and schools, each with significant autonomy in defining academic programs, admissions, and advising. Cornell also operates eCornell, which provides online professional development and certificate programs.

Cornell University remains a leading institution in higher education, known for its rigorous academic programs, innovative research, and dedication to fostering a vibrant and inclusive campus community.
