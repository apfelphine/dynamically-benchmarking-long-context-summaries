Cornell University is a large, residential research university with a strong emphasis on innovation and interdisciplinary study. Founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, it is located in Ithaca, New York, and expanded to include major campuses in New York City, including Weill Cornell Medicine in Qatar and Cornell Tech on Roosevelt Island. The university operates on a 4–1–4 academic calendar and is classified as an R1 institution with high research activity.

Research at Cornell spans science, technology, engineering, and computing, with notable contributions in high-energy physics, computing infrastructure, and space exploration. The university is home to the Cornell University Center for Advanced Computing and operates research facilities such as the Shoals Marine Laboratory and the Arecibo Observatory (formerly). It has a strong record in patenting and startup formation, with over 200 patents filed in the 2004–05 period.

Cornell is renowned for its academic programs, including the College of Arts and Sciences, College of Engineering, and the College of Architecture, Art, and Planning. The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate degrees, with a strong focus on international programs and study abroad opportunities. It has a diverse student body, with over 34% of admitted students identifying as underrepresented minorities.

The university is highly competitive in admissions, with a middle-50% SAT range of 700–760 in reading/writing and 750–800 in math. It practices need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and provides financial aid, with a significant portion of financial need met through scholarships. The university has a large endowment of $10.7 billion as of 2024, supported by major philanthropic gifts, including $1 billion from Chuck Feeney and $150 million from Herbert Fisk Johnson III.

Cornell is known for its strong academic reputation, consistently ranking among the top universities in the U.S. and globally. It is recognized in rankings such as QS and THE, with a 2024 ranking of 10th in the U.S. and 12th in the world. The university is also noted for its contributions to sustainability, with the Atkinson Center for a Sustainable Future and a commitment to net carbon neutrality by 2035.

Student life at Cornell includes a vibrant campus culture with over 1,000 student organizations, including clubs, cultural groups, and professional societies. The university offers a wide range of student services, including housing, health services, and student media such as The Cornell Daily Sun and The Cornell Review. Notable traditions include Slope Day and Dragon Day, and the university is home to the Cornell University Glee Club, one of the oldest student organizations.

Cornell has a strong faculty, with over 62 Nobel laureates and notable figures such as Hans Bethe, Carl Sagan, and Vladimir Nabokov. The university also has a strong alumni network, with over 250,000 living alumni, including three female Nobel laureates—Pearl S. Buck, Barbara McClintock, and Toni Morrison.

Cornell’s academic and research excellence is further supported by its partnerships with institutions such as the University of Toronto, the University of British Columbia, and the University of Melbourne, and its participation in global research initiatives. The university is a leader in education, research, and community engagement, with a commitment to academic freedom, diversity, and innovation.
