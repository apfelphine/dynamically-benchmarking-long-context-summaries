## Cornell University Summary

Cornell University, established in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White in Ithaca, New York, serves as New York State's land-grant institution. Noted for its technological innovation and research, Cornell encompasses a main campus in Ithaca and significant presences in New York City through Weill Cornell Medicine and Cornell Tech, as well as a global footprint with programs in Qatar, Israel-Jordan, and partnerships across Asia and Europe. The university administers a decentralized academic structure with 16 colleges—4 publicly supported statutory colleges and 12 privately endowed—which operate with significant autonomy. Cornell's endowment reached $10.7 billion in 2024, supporting distinguished programs that routinely rank among the world’s best, including architecture, engineering, and business.

With over 250,000 living alumni and 62 affiliated Nobel laureates, Cornell is internationally recognized for its research intensity (“R1” classification) with $1.18 billion in annual R&D expenditures, advanced contributions in physics, engineering, life sciences, and international relations, and a robust commitment to sustainability. The university fosters a vibrant student life with more than 1,000 organizations, extensive Greek life, diverse housing options, and top-tier athletics. Cornell provides need-blind admissions for U.S. applicants, rich financial aid programs, and global study opportunities, while its library system—holding over 10 million volumes—and its historic university press further support academic excellence and open dissemination of knowledge.
