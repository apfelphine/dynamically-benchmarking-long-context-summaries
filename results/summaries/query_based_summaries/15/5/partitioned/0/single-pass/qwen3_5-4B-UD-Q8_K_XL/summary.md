Cornell University, founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, is a leading land-grant institution located in Ithaca, New York. Established as New York's land-grant university, it operates on a decentralized structure with 16 autonomous colleges, including seven undergraduate colleges and seven graduate/professional schools. The university boasts a massive endowment of approximately $10.7 billion and a diverse student body with significant representation from underrepresented minorities. Its main campus in Ithaca spans over 745 acres, featuring a mix of architectural styles from Collegiate Gothic to modernist, and is recognized for its beauty and sustainability initiatives, such as achieving net carbon neutrality by 2035. Cornell is globally renowned for its research output, ranking highly in engineering, natural sciences, and social sciences, and maintains extensive partnerships with international institutions, including the Technion in Israel and the Qatar Foundation.

Beyond its traditional campuses, Cornell has expanded its global footprint with locations in New York City, including Cornell Tech on Roosevelt Island, and Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar, which is the first U.S. medical school established outside the United States. The university operates a cooperative extension service across New York State and offers numerous study abroad programs and joint degree opportunities. Financially supported by major philanthropic donations, Cornell has consistently ranked among the top academic institutions in the world and the United States, particularly excelling in architecture, business, and international relations. It remains a center for innovation, hosting significant research facilities and contributing substantially to advancements in fields ranging from high-energy physics to agricultural science.
