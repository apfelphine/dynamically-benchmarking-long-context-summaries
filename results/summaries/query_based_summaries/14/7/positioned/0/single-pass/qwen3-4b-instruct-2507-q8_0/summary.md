The University of Chicago is a prestigious research university with a long and influential history. Founded in 1890 through a collaboration between the American Baptist Education Society and a $600,000 donation from John D. Rockefeller, it began as a coeducational institution with a strong emphasis on liberal arts and research. The university was led by William Rainey Harper, who attracted top faculty and established the Divinity School as the first graduate professional school. Over time, it evolved through key leadership transitions, including Harry Pratt Judson, Robert Maynard Hutchins, and later figures like Lawrence Kimpton and Robert Zimmer, who oversaw major expansions and financial developments. The university is known for its academic excellence, housing over 100 Nobel laureates, and for pioneering the "Great Books" program and core curriculum in undergraduate education. It has maintained a strong reputation in economics, sociology, and the sciences, with a legacy including the Chicago School of economics and sociology. The campus, spanning 217 acres in Hyde Park and Woodlawn, features a historic and modern architectural mix, including the Main Quadrangles and the iconic Robie House, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The university operates on a quarter system and offers a wide range of academic programs across the College, four graduate divisions, and seven professional schools. It is a member of the Association of American Universities and has a strong research presence, with over $423.9 million spent on research in 2018. The university is also recognized for its significant contributions to science, including the development of the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction (Chicago Pile-1), and for its influential role in shaping modern economic and social thought. The university is currently led by President Paul Alivisatos, with a focus on financial stability, campus expansion, and academic innovation, including a unified 21-course core curriculum and increased investment in student life. It maintains a diverse student body and is known for its strong academic culture, student activism, and commitment to intellectual freedom.
