## University of Chicago

The University of Chicago traces its roots to the "Old University of Chicago," founded in 1857 by Baptist educators but closed in 1886 due to financial struggles exacerbated by the Great Chicago Fire and the Panic of 1873. The institution reopened in 1890 under the name University of Chicago, incorporating a $600,000 donation from Standard Oil co-founder John D. Rockefeller and land from Marshall Field. William Rainey Harper served as the first president, establishing the Divinity School as the university's first graduate professional school and recruiting senior faculty with large salaries. Under Harper's leadership, the university operated on a quarter system and focused on preparation for higher learning before advancing to more advanced courses.

Following Harper's death in 1906, the university faced financial austerity until Robert Maynard Hutchins became president in 1929. Hutchins reorganized the curriculum into a unified undergraduate college with a rigorous core, protected faculty salaries during the Great Depression, and recruited refugee scientists from Europe, including Enrico Fermi, who contributed to the Manhattan Project. In 1951, Lawrence Kimpton succeeded Hutchins as chancellor, implementing severe budget cuts that were balanced by 1954. The university continued to face financial deficits and enrollment challenges until the mid-1980s, when Paul Alivisatos became president in 2021.

The modern University of Chicago is a research university located in Hyde Park, featuring a 217-acre main campus with Gothic quadrangles and modern buildings designed by architects like Eero Saarinen and Ludwig Mies van der Rohe. It operates seven professional schools, including the Booth School of Business, the Pritzker School of Medicine, and the Harris School of Public Policy, alongside four graduate research divisions. The university is known for its high selectivity, with an acceptance rate dropping to 4.7% by 2023, and maintains significant global presence through satellite campuses in Hong Kong, London, Paris, and other international locations.
