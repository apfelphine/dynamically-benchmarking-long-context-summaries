**University of Chicago**

The University of Chicago, established in 1890 with significant financial support from John D. Rockefeller and local benefactors, is renowned as a prestigious private research university located in Hyde Park, Chicago. It operates on a quarter system with a comprehensive core curriculum emphasizing liberal arts, featuring a now-widespread model including the Socratic method and Great Books program. The university comprises an undergraduate college, four graduate divisions (biological sciences, humanities, physical sciences, social sciences), seven professional schools (including Booth School of Business, Law School, Pritzker School of Medicine), and the Graham School of Continuing Liberal and Professional Studies. Its campus is noted for its Gothic and modernist architecture, including landmarks like Rockefeller Chapel and the Joe and Rika Mansueto Library.

Academically, the University of Chicago is highly selective, with an acceptance rate dropping from 71% in 1996 to 4.7% in 2023 for undergraduates, and it was the first major research university to eliminate SAT/ACT requirements for admissions. It spends heavily on research, ranking 60th nationally with a $423.9 million R&D budget in 2018 and houses more than 140 research centers, including management of Argonne and Fermilab national laboratories. The university has been influential across numerous disciplines, giving rise to the Chicago schools of economics and sociology, and has been the site of major scientific breakthroughs (e.g., the first controlled nuclear chain reaction). Its faculty and alumni include 101 Nobel laureates and numerous notable figures in politics, science, business, and the arts. The school offers 19 NCAA Division III varsity sports known as the Maroons. Student life features over 400 student organizations, themed residential house systems, and annual traditions such as the University of Chicago Scavenger Hunt.
