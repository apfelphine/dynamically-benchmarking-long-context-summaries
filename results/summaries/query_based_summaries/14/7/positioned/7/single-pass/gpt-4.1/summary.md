## University of Chicago Summary

The University of Chicago, established in 1890 through an endowment from John D. Rockefeller and land from Marshall Field, is a private research university located in the Hyde Park and Woodlawn neighborhoods of Chicago, Illinois. Renowned for its rigorous academic programs, core curriculum, and influential faculty, the university operates on a quarter system and comprises the undergraduate college, four graduate divisions, seven professional schools, and the Graham School of Continuing Liberal and Professional Studies. The campus spans 217 acres and features notable Gothic and modern architecture, with additional centers in global locations such as Paris, London, Hong Kong, Beijing, and Delhi.

Consistently ranked among the top universities worldwide, the University of Chicago is known for its pioneering research, including the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction and the development of the Chicago school of economics. The university’s library system contains over 11 million volumes, supporting robust research across more than 140 centers and institutes. As of fall 2024, the student body includes 7,569 undergraduates and over 10,000 graduate students, with highly selective admissions and a need-blind policy for domestic applicants. Notable alumni and faculty have collectively earned 101 Nobel Prizes, and the university hosts more than 400 student organizations and active traditions.
