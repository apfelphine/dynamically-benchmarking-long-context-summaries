The University of Chicago is a prestigious research university known for its rigorous academics, influential faculty, and prominent contributions to science and public policy. It offers undergraduate and graduate programs across four collegiate divisions—Biological Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences, and Humanities—alongside seven professional schools, including the Booth School of Business, Law School, and Pritzker School of Medicine. The university is home to the John U. Nef Committee on Social Thought and has a strong tradition in interdisciplinary scholarship, with notable contributions to economics, sociology, and the Chicago School of economics. It operates over 140 research centers and is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" by the National Science Foundation. The university is also known for its research in nuclear physics (e.g., the Chicago Pile-1), economics, and the development of the Chicago School of economics. 

The university’s academic programs emphasize a core curriculum, with a strong focus on critical thinking and the Great Books. The undergraduate college offers 51 majors and 33 minors, and students participate in a liberal arts education. The university is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of 4.7% for the class of 2025. Its law and business schools consistently rank among the top three in the United States. The university is governed by a board of trustees and includes a president, provost, and 14 vice presidents. The current president is Paul Alivisatos, and the provost is Katherine Baicker. 

The university maintains a strong financial position, with an endowment valued at $10.1 billion in 2024, and a combined annual budget of $5.2 billion. Despite financial challenges, including deficits and debt, the university has continued to expand its campus and facilities. It is known for its student life, including over 400 student organizations, the University of Chicago Scavenger Hunt, and traditions like Kuviasungnerk/Kangeiko (Kuvia) and Summer Breeze. The university has a strong reputation for producing influential alumni, including Nobel laureates, CEOs, and public officials. 

The University of Chicago is also recognized globally, consistently ranking among the top universities in the world, with a strong presence in rankings such as US News, QS, and THE. It is known for its commitment to academic freedom, research excellence, and its role in shaping modern economic thought.
