## University of Chicago

The University of Chicago, established in 1890 with key donations from John D. Rockefeller and other Chicago philanthropists, is a prestigious research university located in Hyde Park, Chicago. It operates on a quarter system and offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs organized into four collegiate divisions and seven professional schools, including the Booth School of Business and the Law School, both highly ranked nationally and globally. The university is known for pioneering the liberal arts undergraduate core curriculum and the Socratic method, and for its significant contributions to various academic fields, including economics, sociology, physics, and nuclear research (notably the first man-made nuclear chain reaction). It manages major research facilities such as Argonne National Laboratory and Fermilab, and has established global centers in Paris, Beijing, New Delhi, and Hong Kong.

The main campus spans 217 acres in Chicago neighborhoods, featuring distinctive Collegiate Gothic architecture and modern research facilities like the Joe and Rika Mansueto Library. The university has a notable history of financial challenges and expansions, with recent leadership focusing on campus development and global academic presence. Its student body is diverse, with rigorous admissions and a core curriculum requirement. The university emphasizes interdisciplinary scholarship and operates over 140 research centers and institutes, including the Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures. It maintains a large private police force due to safety concerns in the surrounding neighborhoods and continues initiatives to improve student life, academics, and research excellence.
