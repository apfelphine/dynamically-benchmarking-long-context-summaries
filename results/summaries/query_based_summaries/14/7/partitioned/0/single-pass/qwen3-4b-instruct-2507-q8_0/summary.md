The University of Chicago, founded in 1890, is a prestigious research university known for its rigorous academic programs and influential faculty. It operates on a quarter system with a strong emphasis on the liberal arts, featuring a core curriculum that includes required courses in the humanities, social sciences, and foreign languages. The undergraduate college offers 51 majors and 33 minors across four divisions: Biological Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences, and Humanities. The university is home to the Chicago School of Economics and the Chicago School of Sociology, which have significantly influenced economic and social thought. 

It houses seven professional schools, including the Booth School of Business, the Law School, the Divinity School, the Pritzker School of Medicine, the Harris School of Public Policy, and the Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering. The university is a founding member of the Association of American Universities and is classified as an R1 institution with very high research activity. 

Key research centers include the Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures, the Argonne National Laboratory, and the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory. The university is also known for its contributions to science, including the development of the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction (Chicago Pile-1), the discovery of radiocarbon dating, and the Miller–Urey experiment on the origin of life. 

The university maintains a strong commitment to academic freedom and has a long history of student activism. It operates a large library system, including the Regenstein Library and the Joe and Rika Mansueto Library, and is home to the University of Chicago Press, one of the largest university presses in North America. The university's campus is located in Hyde Park, Chicago, and includes notable buildings such as the Robie House, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Henry Hinds Laboratory for Geophysical Sciences. 

The university has a strong international presence with campuses in Hong Kong, London, and Paris, and continues to expand its global reach through academic partnerships and research initiatives.
