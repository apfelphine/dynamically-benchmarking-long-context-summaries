The **University of Chicago** was founded in 1890 as a coeducational institution, initially using funds from John D. Rockefeller and land donated by Marshall Field. The university’s early years were marked by rapid growth, including the establishment of the Divinity School in 1891, the adoption of maroon as its official color, and the creation of the University Extension and the University of Chicago Press. The university’s financial struggles led to its closure in 1886, but it was revived in 1890 under the leadership of William Rainey Harper, who modernized its curriculum and expanded faculty.

In the early 20th century, the university became a leader in research and education, with notable contributions from figures like Robert Millikan, who conducted the oil-drop experiment to measure the electron’s charge. The university’s academic reputation grew significantly during the presidency of Robert Maynard Hutchins, who introduced the New Plan, a two-year general education curriculum, and emphasized academic rigor. The university’s expansion included the construction of iconic buildings like the Regenstein Library and the Keller Center, and it established partnerships with global institutions, such as the Marine Biological Laboratory in Woods Hole, Massachusetts.

The university’s academic divisions include the Biological Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences, and Humanities, each with its own graduate divisions. It is home to seven professional schools, including the Booth School of Business, the Law School, and the Pritzker School of Medicine, all of which are highly ranked nationally and internationally. The university operates over 140 research centers and institutes, including the Argonne National Laboratory and the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory, contributing significantly to scientific advancements.

The University of Chicago is renowned for its rigorous academic programs, particularly in economics, law, and business, and its commitment to interdisciplinary research. It has consistently ranked among the top universities globally, with its reputation bolstered by its contributions to fields like physics, chemistry, and social sciences. The university’s core curriculum, which emphasizes critical thinking and the study of great books, remains a hallmark of its educational philosophy.
