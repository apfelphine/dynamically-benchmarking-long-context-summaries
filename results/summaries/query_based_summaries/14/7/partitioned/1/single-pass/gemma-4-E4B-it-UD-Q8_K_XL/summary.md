The University of Chicago, established in 1890, initially struggled financially but was later supported by significant donations, including from John D. Rockefeller. Its academic structure includes four undergraduate divisions—Biological Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences, and Humanities—alongside seven professional schools and the Graham School of Continuing Liberal and Professional Studies. The university is known for introducing the Socratic method, the Great Books program, and the core curriculum.

The institution is a major research university, operating numerous research centers and affiliated with national laboratories such as the Argonne National Laboratory and co-managing the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory. Historically, the University of Chicago has been the site of significant scientific breakthroughs, including the Chicago Pile-1 (the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction), Robert Millikan's oil-drop experiment, the development of radiocarbon dating, and the discovery of REM sleep. The university is highly regarded for its prestigious law and business schools.
