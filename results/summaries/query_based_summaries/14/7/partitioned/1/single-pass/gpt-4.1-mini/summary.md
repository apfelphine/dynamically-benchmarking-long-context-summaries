# University of Chicago

The University of Chicago, founded in 1890 with support from figures like John D. Rockefeller, is a prestigious private research university located in Chicago's Hyde Park neighborhood. It operates on a quarter system and offers a rigorous undergraduate core curriculum emphasizing the liberal arts, shaped by the Socratic method and the Great Books tradition. Its academics are organized into four collegiate divisions and multiple graduate and professional schools, including seven professional schools such as the Booth School of Business, Law School, and Pritzker School of Medicine. The university is highly research active, managing over 140 research centers and institutes, and operates significant national laboratories like Argonne and Fermilab.

Governed by a board of trustees and led by President Paul Alivisatos as of 2021, the university has a sizeable endowment of approximately $10.1 billion (2024) and a large operational budget. Despite financial challenges, it maintains a highly selective admissions process with an acceptance rate of 4.7% for undergraduates (class of 2025) and a student body noted for diversity and academic excellence. The university participates in NCAA Division III athletics as the Maroons and hosts robust student life with over 400 organizations and traditions like the annual Scavenger Hunt. The University of Chicago has produced 101 Nobel laureates and is renowned globally for its contributions to economics, sociology, physics, chemistry, medicine, and free-market thought—the namesake of the Chicago school of economics.
