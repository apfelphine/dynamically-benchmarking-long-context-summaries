# King's College London

King's College London is a prestigious research university founded in 1829 as a rival to the secular University College London, established with royal patronage and the patronage of the Archbishop of Canterbury. The institution is a constituent college of the University of London and a founding member of the Russell Group. It is widely recognized for its medical and biomedical research, holding the title of the largest centre for healthcare education in Europe, and is home to major research institutions including the Francis Crick Institute. Academically, King's consistently ranks highly globally, often placed in the top 30-40 in international university rankings, and is particularly noted for its strengths in psychology, law, medicine, and business.

The university operates across five main campuses in central London, including the historic Strand Campus, the Waterloo Campus, the Guy's Campus, the St Thomas' Campus, and the Denmark Hill Campus, which houses the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience. Student life is supported by the King's College London Students' Union, which manages extensive facilities and over 300 societies, and features the iconic mascot, Reggie the Lion. King's maintains a strong tradition of religious affiliation through its Dean of King's College, an ordained minister of the Church of England, and offers unique programs such as the Associateship of King's College. The university is a significant hub for notable alumni, including Nobel laureates, heads of state, and distinguished figures in science, arts, and politics.
