King's College London is a leading public research university in London, founded in 1829 as King's College to provide an Anglican alternative to the secular University College, London. It operates on five campuses across central London, including the historic Strand Campus, the Guy's Campus, the Waterloo Campus, the St Thomas' Campus, and the Denmark Hill Campus. The university has undergone significant expansion and restructuring, merging with medical institutions like the United Medical and Dental Schools of Guy's and St Thomas' Hospitals in 1998 to form the GKT School of Medical Education, and later acquiring the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience (IoPPN). It is a founding member of the Russell Group and the University of London, and participates in the King's Health Partners academic health science centre.

Academically, King's is renowned for its medical faculties, which include the Florence Nightingale Faculty of Nursing, Midwifery and Palliative Care, the Faculty of Life Sciences and Medicine, and the Faculty of Dentistry, Oral and Craniofacial Sciences. It claims to be the largest centre for healthcare education in Europe and is home to the Drug Control Centre, the UK's only WADA-accredited anti-doping laboratory. The university boasts a strong research output, ranking 9th in the UK by the 2021 Research Excellence Framework, and maintains extensive partnerships with other institutions such as Imperial College London and University College London. Its student life is supported by the King's College London Students' Union, which manages a wide range of clubs, sports, and media outlets, including the award-winning newspaper Roar News.
