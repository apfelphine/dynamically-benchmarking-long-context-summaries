# Summary of King's College London

King's College London (KCL), founded in 1829 under royal patronage, is a prestigious research university located in central London, comprising five main campuses: Strand (original campus), Guy's (near London Bridge), Waterloo, St Thomas', and Denmark Hill. It maintains a strong historical association with healthcare education through its medical and dental schools, including the Florence Nightingale Faculty of Nursing, and is part of King's Health Partners academic health science centre. The university houses notable facilities such as the Maughan Library, the largest library at KCL, and multiple museums including the Gordon Museum of Pathology.

KCL encompasses nine faculties covering arts and humanities; business; dentistry, oral and craniofacial sciences; law; life sciences and medicine; natural, mathematical and engineering sciences; nursing, midwifery and palliative care; psychiatry, psychology and neuroscience; and social science and public policy. It awards the Associateship of King's College (AKC), a tradition dating to 1835. King’s holds a strong global reputation, ranked within the top 40 worldwide by major rankings (QS, THE, ARWU) and recognized for graduate employability with a gold rating from the Teaching Excellence Framework 2023. The student life is vibrant, supported by the King’s College London Students' Union, over 60 sports clubs, around 300 societies, student media outlets, a student-led think tank, and notable rivalries with University College London and the London School of Economics. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates such as Peter Higgs and Michael Levitt, and renowned figures across politics, science, arts, and religion.
