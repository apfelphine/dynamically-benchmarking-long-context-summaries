## King's College London: Summary

King's College London, founded in 1829, is a prestigious central London university comprising five campuses: Strand (original arts and sciences site), Guy’s (life sciences, medicine, and dental institute), Waterloo, St Thomas’, and Denmark Hill. It offers a wide array of undergraduate and postgraduate programs across diverse faculties, including arts & humanities, law, sciences, social sciences & public policy, business, dentistry, nursing, psychiatry, and medicine, and is renowned as Europe's largest center for healthcare education. King's is a founding member of the University of London and holds affiliations with major research consortia such as the Russell Group, King's Health Partners, the Francis Crick Institute, PLuS Alliance, and numerous international and industry partnerships.

Globally ranked in the top 36 universities by QS, THE, US News, and ARWU (2025–2026), King's excels in research—£256.9 million research income in 2023/24—and ranks highly in subject areas like Health Studies, Law, Business, and Psychology. King's is also notable for contributions to scientific discovery (including the structure of DNA), a vibrant student life (clubs, societies, Reggie the Lion mascot, and a large students’ union), and its rich cultural presence in London. The university offers extensive library resources—including the iconic Maughan Library—and specialized museums. Distinguished alumni include Nobel laureates, writers, politicians, and leaders in science, law, and the arts.
