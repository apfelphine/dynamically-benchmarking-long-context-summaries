King's College London was founded in 1829, in response to the secular nature of London University, to reaffirm the educational values of the established order. Initially, King's College was established as a rival institution to the secular London University. The college opened in October 1831, and its initial prospectus permitted nonconformists to attend, despite the requirement that governors and professors be members of the Church of England.

Over its history, King's has undergone significant changes, including its merger into the University of London in 1910, and subsequently regaining legal independence in 1980. Major developments include the opening of its own hospital in 1840, which later became King's College Hospital. In 2007, King's College London was granted independent degree-awarding powers. The college is based on five campuses in central London, including the Strand Campus and the Guy's Campus, and is a member institution of the federal University of London.
