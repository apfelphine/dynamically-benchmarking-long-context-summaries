### History and Foundation
King's College London was founded in 1829 by King George IV as a Tory response to the secular "London University" (later University College London), aiming to reaffirm Anglican educational values amidst the Industrial Revolution. Initially located on the Strand, it opened in 1831 with William Otter as the first principal. While the college permitted nonconformist students, it required staff to be members of the Church of England. Key developments included the establishment of a medical school in 1830, which later became a major international center for antiseptic surgery following Joseph Lister's appointment in 1877. King's gained independence from the University of London in 1980 and received direct government funding in 1993. It underwent significant mergers in the late 20th century, incorporating the Institute of Psychiatry (1997), Queen Elizabeth College, Chelsea College, and the United Medical and Dental Schools of Guy's and St Thomas' Hospitals (1998), forming the current multi-faculty structure.

### Academics and Campus
As of 2024, King's comprises nine academic faculties, including arts and humanities, law, life sciences and medicine, nursing, and psychiatry. It is headquartered on five campuses in central London: the original Strand Campus, the nearby Waterloo Campus, the St Thomas' Campus in Lambeth, the Guy's Campus near London Bridge, and the Denmark Hill Campus in Southwark. King's is recognized as the largest center for healthcare education in Europe and a major hub for biomedical research, hosting the Francis Crick Institute and the Drug Control Centre (the UK's only WADA-accredited anti-doping laboratory). The university is a founding member of the Russell Group and the UK Centre for Medical Research and Innovation (now the Francis Crick Institute).

### Notable People and Culture
King's has produced numerous Nobel laureates in physics, medicine, and chemistry, including Peter Higgs, Michael Levitt, and Roger Penrose. Prominent alumni include Archbishop Desmond Tutu, dramatist W.S. Gilbert, and author Virginia Woolf. The university maintains a fierce historical rivalry with University College London (UCL), centered on mascots (Reggie the Lion vs. Phineas) and rugby matches. Student life includes over 50 sports clubs and the "London Varsity" series. King's is also known for its music traditions, particularly the Choir of King's College London, which broadcasts on BBC Radio 3 and 4.
