# King's College London

King's College London was founded in 1829 in response to the secular nature of University College, London, establishing itself as a rival institution supported by King George IV to uphold Anglican educational values. Initially a college within the University of London, it gained direct degree-awarding powers in 2006. The university is organized into nine faculties, including arts and humanities, life sciences and medicine, dentistry, law, and social science. Historically significant for its contributions to science, King's played a crucial role in the discovery of the DNA double helix in 1953. It operates across five campuses in central London, with the Strand Campus housing the arts and sciences, and medical faculties located at Guy's, St Thomas', and the Waterloo Campuses.

King's College London is a major academic health science center through King's Health Partners and a founding member of the Francis Crick Institute. It holds the fourth-largest endowment of any UK university and is recognized as a "super brand" globally. The institution boasts a diverse student body and a strong tradition of alumni in politics, science, and the arts, including Nobel laureates Peter Higgs and Roger Penrose. The university maintains active rivalries with University College London and the London School of Economics, and it is a member of the Russell Group and the University of London.
