King's College London was founded in 1829, established as a rival to London University in response to the secular nature of that institution. It enjoyed royal patronage, and its initial prospectus permitted "nonconformists of all sorts to enter the college freely." The college opened in October 1831, initially organized into a senior department and a junior department.

Over time, King's College London evolved significantly, achieving degree-awarding authority from the University of London in 1839. The college opened its own hospital in 1840, and the appointment of Joseph Lister in 1877 greatly benefited its medical school. Key milestones in the 20th century include the crucial contributions of Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin to the discovery of the double helix structure of DNA in 1953. The institution regained its legal independence in 1980 and was granted independent degree-awarding powers in 2006. Today, it operates across five central London campuses, encompassing faculties such as Arts and Humanities, Life Sciences and Medicine, and Law, and is a member of the Russell Group.
