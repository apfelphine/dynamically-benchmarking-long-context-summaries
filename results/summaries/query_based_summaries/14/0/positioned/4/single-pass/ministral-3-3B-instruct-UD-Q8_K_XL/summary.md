King's College London (KCL) is a prestigious institution founded in 1829, originally established in response to the religious and social changes in England following the Napoleonic Wars. It was named in honor of King George IV and initially focused on Anglican education, though it quickly became inclusive of nonconformists. The college was created as a rival to London University, which had faced opposition for its secular nature.

KCL operates on five campuses in central London, with the main campus located on the Strand, adjacent to Somerset House. The campus includes historic buildings such as the King's Building, designed by Sir Robert Smirke, and the King's College London Chapel, which was redesigned by Sir George Gilbert Scott. The college also houses Bush House, Maughan Library, and several halls of residence.

KCL is organized into nine faculties, including Arts and Humanities, Dentistry, Life Sciences and Medicine, Natural, Mathematical, and Engineering Sciences, Nursing, and Social Science and Public Policy. The college is known for its strong research output, particularly in medicine, psychology, and law, and is a founding member of the Russell Group, an association of leading UK research universities.

The university is highly selective in admissions, with a focus on academic excellence. It has a long-standing reputation for fostering interdisciplinary research and innovation, and its graduates have made significant contributions to various fields, including science, law, and the arts. KCL is recognized globally for its academic rigor and commitment to advancing knowledge and education.
