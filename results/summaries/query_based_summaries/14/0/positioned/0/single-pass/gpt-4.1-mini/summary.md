**King's College London** was founded in 1829 under royal patronage, initially as an Anglican institution countering the secular University College London. It rapidly developed faculties in arts, medicine, and theology, and played a key role in London’s higher education expansion in the 19th century. The college opened its medical school hospital in 1840, introducing pioneering antiseptic techniques under Joseph Lister. Over time, King's extended its reach by incorporating several medical and dental schools, and merging with institutions such as Queen Elizabeth College, Chelsea College, and the Institute of Psychiatry. By 2006, it gained independent degree-awarding powers, further cementing its status as a leading university. Currently, King's operates multiple campuses in central London, including its historic Strand Campus, and faculties across arts, sciences, health, law, and business, featuring renowned research contributions such as the discovery of DNA’s double helix structure.

Governed by a royal charter, King's is organized into nine faculties offering a wide range of academic programs. It has a strong clinical and scientific research presence, notably in life sciences and medicine through partnerships like King's Health Partners and the Francis Crick Institute. The university maintains notable libraries, museums, and student life infrastructures including a vibrant students’ union, extensive societies, and competitive sports teams. With a prestigious alumni network including Nobel laureates, political leaders, and cultural figures, King's is internationally recognized for academic excellence, ranked within the global top universities, and embodies a tradition linking rigorous education with Anglican foundations and modern, inclusive scholarship.
