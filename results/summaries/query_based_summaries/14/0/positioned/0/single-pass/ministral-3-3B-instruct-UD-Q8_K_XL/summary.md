King's College London (KCL) was founded in 1829 as King's College in recognition of King George IV’s patronage, emerging from the theological controversies surrounding the establishment of London University. Initially, King's aimed to provide secular education, contrasting with the religiously affiliated universities of Oxford and Cambridge, which educated only wealthy Anglican students. The college was created as a rival to London University, reflecting broader social and religious changes in England.

King's College London was initially divided into a senior and junior department, with the junior department later becoming King's College School. The senior department offered courses in divinity, classical languages, mathematics, English literature, and history, as well as specialized medical, military, and theological studies. The college’s early growth was hindered by inefficiencies in its medical school and the divided loyalties of its faculty. In 1835, the government established an examining board to grant degrees, with King's becoming affiliated with the University of London in 1836, leading to the first University of London degrees being awarded to King's students in 1839.

King's College London expanded significantly in the 19th century, opening its own hospital in 1840 and establishing a military department in 1845. The college also pioneered evening classes in 1855. In 1882, the King's College London Act transformed the college into a non-profit institution, extending its educational scope to include women. The college merged with other institutions in the late 20th century, including Queen Elizabeth College and Chelsea College in 1985, and the Institute of Psychiatry in 1997, forming the GKT School of Medical Education.

In the 20th century, King's College London became a major center for scientific research, including the discovery of the double helix structure of DNA in 1953 by Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin. The college regained legal independence in 1980 and gained direct access to government funding in 1993. In 2006, King's was granted independent degree-awarding powers, leading to the first King's degrees being awarded in 2008. Today, King's College London is renowned for its academic excellence, research contributions, and diverse range of programs across arts, sciences, medicine, and law. The university operates five main campuses, including the historic Strand Campus, and is a founding member of the University of London and the Russell Group.
