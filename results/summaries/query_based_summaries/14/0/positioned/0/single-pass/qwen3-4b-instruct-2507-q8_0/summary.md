**King's College London** is a prestigious research university in central London, founded in 1829 as a response to the religious and social exclusivity of Oxford and Cambridge. Named in honor of King George IV, it began as a rival to the secular "London University" and was established with a royal charter, incorporating a strong Anglican tradition. Key historical events include a duel in 1829 between the Duke of Wellington and George Finch-Hatton over the university's religious policies, and the 1882 King's College London Act, which transformed it into a non-profit institution and expanded its educational access to women. 

The university operates across five campuses in central London, including the historic Strand Campus, Guy's Campus, Waterloo Campus, St Thomas' Campus, and Denmark Hill Campus. It is known for its strong emphasis on research, particularly in medicine, health sciences, and the social sciences. King's is a member of the Russell Group and has a long-standing tradition of academic excellence, with notable contributions to science, including the discovery of the DNA double helix structure by Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin. 

It has a unique academic structure with nine faculties and a distinct tradition of the Associateship of King's College (AKC), which offers students and staff opportunities to engage with religious, ethical, and philosophical issues. The university has a robust student life, including a student union, over 300 societies, and a vibrant culture of student-led initiatives such as King's Think Tank. 

King's has gained significant recognition in global rankings, including being ranked 9th in the UK for graduate employability and among the top universities in the world in several subject areas. It has also developed strong partnerships with institutions like the University of London, the Francis Crick Institute, and King's Health Partners. In 2006, King's was granted independent degree-awarding powers, allowing it to confer its own degrees. The university continues to innovate, with a rebranding initiative (dropped in 2015) and ongoing investments in research and student facilities.
