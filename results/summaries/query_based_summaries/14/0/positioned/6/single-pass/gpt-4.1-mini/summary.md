# Summary of King's College London

King's College London (KCL) was founded in 1829 as a response to the secular founding of University College London, with a mission to combine religious instruction within a comprehensive education. It was established under royal patronage and initially had a strong Anglican affiliation, despite some openness to nonconformist students. A notable event in its early history was the 1829 duel between the Duke of Wellington and the Earl of Winchilsea over religious and political principles connected to the college’s foundation. King's expanded throughout the 19th and 20th centuries, incorporating medical education linked to historic hospitals and contributing to major scientific achievements, notably key contributions to the discovery of DNA's double helix structure. It underwent several mergers with other institutions, evolving its academic portfolio and regaining legal independence in 1980. From 2006, King's gained independent degree-awarding powers, fully awarding its own degrees from 2008 onwards. Recent developments include opening a mathematics school in 2014 and acquiring the historic Bush House site in central London.

Today, King's College London operates five campuses in central London, including the original Strand Campus and sites in Waterloo, Guy’s, St Thomas’, and Denmark Hill, serving faculties across arts, science, health, law, business, and social sciences. It houses major medical facilities and is a founding partner of the Francis Crick Institute. Governed by a council and academic board, its president holds the title Vice-Chancellor and President. King's comprises nine faculties including arts and humanities; dentistry, oral and craniofacial sciences; life sciences and medicine; law; natural, mathematical and engineering sciences; nursing and midwifery; psychiatry, psychology and neuroscience; social science and public policy; and business. It maintains extensive library facilities, including the Maughan Library, and owns museums such as the Gordon Museum of Pathology. King's has a significant research footprint with income exceeding £250 million and ranks in the top 40 global universities in major rankings. The college supports a vibrant student life with a students’ union, numerous societies, media outlets, and sports clubs, hosting a historic rivalry with University College London. Notable alumni include 14 Nobel laureates such as Peter Higgs and Michael Levitt, as well as prominent figures in politics, arts, and sciences.
