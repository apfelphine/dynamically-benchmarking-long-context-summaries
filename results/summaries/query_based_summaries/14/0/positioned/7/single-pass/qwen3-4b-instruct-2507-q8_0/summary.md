**King's College London** is a prestigious research university in central London, founded in 1829 with a royal charter from King George IV. It began as a response to the secular nature of London University and established itself as a rival institution with a strong Anglican foundation. Over time, it evolved through significant mergers, including with Queen Elizabeth College, Chelsea College, and the Institute of Psychiatry, and gained independence from the University of London in 1980. In 2006, it was granted the power to award its own degrees, a milestone that was fully implemented in 2008.

The university spans five campuses in central London, including the historic Strand Campus, Guy's Campus, Waterloo Campus, St Thomas' Campus, and Denmark Hill Campus. The Strand Campus, home to the original King's Building and the Maughan Library, is central to the university’s academic and cultural life. King's is renowned for its strong programs in medicine, dentistry, law, and the arts, and is a leading center for biomedical research, housing the Francis Crick Institute and King's Health Partners.

King's College London is a member of the Russell Group and is recognized for its high research output, with strong performance in subjects such as health, social policy, and business. It is also known for its diverse student body, with a high proportion of international students and a gender balance favoring women. The university has a rich tradition of academic excellence, including Nobel laureates such as Peter Higgs, Sir Michael Houghton, and Michael Levitt, and notable alumni in politics, science, and the arts.

Student life is vibrant, with over 500 societies, a strong student union, and a range of cultural and sporting activities. The rivalry with University College London (UCL) is well-known, with traditions such as the annual Macadam Cup and the mascot Reggie the Lion. King's also hosts public-facing museums, including the Gordon Museum of Pathology and the Museum of Life Sciences, and contributes to public science through initiatives like Science Gallery London.

The university maintains a strong commitment to academic and social values, including its Associateship and Fellowship programs, and continues to play a vital role in the UK's higher education and research landscape.
