**King's College London** was founded in 1829 as a response to the secular nature of London University, aiming to uphold Anglican educational values. It has a long history of academic and institutional development, including a notable rivalry with University College London and a period of growth during the 20th century, including the establishment of the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience. The university operates across five campuses in central London, including the historic Strand Campus and the Guy's Campus, and offers a broad range of academic programs in arts, sciences, law, medicine, and social sciences. It is a member of the Russell Group and has a strong research profile, with a significant endowment and global reputation. King's is known for its research in medicine, particularly in psychiatry and neuroscience, and has contributed to major scientific advancements. The university emphasizes academic excellence, with a focus on research and innovation, and maintains a strong presence in international education and collaboration. It has a long-standing tradition of academic freedom and has been involved in public debates on issues such as divestment and social justice.
