**King's College London** was founded in 1829 under royal patronage as a response to the secular University College London. Its foundation reflected a Tory desire to uphold Anglican educational values. The college opened with a senior and junior division, the latter evolving into the current King's College School. Early degrees were awarded under the University of London, created in 1836. King's expanded through the 19th and 20th centuries, developing medical faculties (notably enhanced by Joseph Lister), military and theological departments, and incorporating women's education by the late 1800s. It suffered wartime damages and underwent major reconstructions and mergers, including joining Queen Elizabeth College, Chelsea College, and Institute of Psychiatry. Since 2006, King's awards its own degrees, having gained independent degree-awarding powers.

King's College London operates five London campuses with historic and modern facilities, including the original Strand Campus and specialized medical campuses at Guy's and Denmark Hill. The institution comprises nine faculties covering arts, sciences, law, health sciences, and social sciences, with prominent schools such as the Dickson Poon School of Law, the Florence Nightingale Faculty of Nursing, and the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience. It is a research-intensive university, a member of the Russell Group, and part of multiple academic and health partnerships including King's Health Partners and the Francis Crick Institute. With a 2024 income of over £1.27 billion, it holds the UK's fourth-largest endowment. King's is globally ranked within the top 40 universities and has a strong reputation in medicine, law, and social sciences. Its student life features a vibrant union, media outlets, numerous sports clubs, and a tradition-rich rivalry with University College London. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates (such as Peter Higgs), political leaders, religious figures, and cultural icons.
