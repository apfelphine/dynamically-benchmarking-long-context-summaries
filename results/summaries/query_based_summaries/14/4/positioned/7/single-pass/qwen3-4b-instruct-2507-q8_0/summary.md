Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, is located in Palo Alto, California, and modeled after Cornell University. It opened in 1891 on a former farm and is one of the largest campuses in the U.S., spanning 8,180 acres. The university is renowned for its strong ties to innovation, particularly in technology and entrepreneurship, with a history of fostering startups like Google, Hewlett-Packard, and Cisco. Stanford's research and academic excellence are reflected in its R1 classification, with significant contributions to science, including the discovery of DNA synthesis, laser technology, and the development of the internet's foundational protocols (TCP/IP). The university is home to the Stanford Research Park, a hub for Silicon Valley's tech industry, and the Hoover Institution, a conservative think tank. 

Academically, Stanford offers seven schools, including the Schools of Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, and Business, and is highly selective with an acceptance rate of 4%. It emphasizes a quarter system and offers a strong undergraduate program with high graduate coexistence. Stanford's student life is vibrant, with over 600 student organizations, including the Stanford Daily and KZSU radio station. Notable traditions include the "Big Game" rivalry with UC Berkeley, "Fountain Hopping," and the "Mausoleum Party." The university is known for its strong emphasis on innovation, with a culture that encourages students and faculty to launch their own companies. Stanford has produced numerous Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners, and billionaires, and is recognized globally for its research, entrepreneurship, and academic excellence.
