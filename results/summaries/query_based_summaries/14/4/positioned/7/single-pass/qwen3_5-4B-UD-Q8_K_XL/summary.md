# Stanford University

Founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, Stanford University opened in 1891 on their Palo Alto farm, modeled after Cornell University with a commitment to non-sectarian, inclusive education for men and women. The campus, spanning approximately 8,180 acres in the Santa Clara Valley, features diverse locations including the central campus, Jasper Ridge Biological Preserve, the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, and Hopkins Marine Station. The university is governed by a privately appointed board of trustees and organized into seven academic schools: Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, Sustainability, Law, Medicine, Education, and Business. It holds one of the four largest academic endowments in the United States, valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023, which covers a significant portion of university expenses.

Stanford is renowned for its high selectivity, boasting a 4% acceptance rate, and its role as the "model" for technology transfer and entrepreneurship. It is home to numerous research centers, including the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Martin Luther King Jr. Research and Education Institute, and is part of the Biohub medical research center. The university has produced over 22 Nobel laureates and is a primary incubator for Silicon Valley, having spawned companies such as Google, PayPal, and LinkedIn. Stanford maintains a strong athletic program with the Cardinal teams, traditionally rivaling the University of California, Berkeley in the "Big Game," and is consistently ranked among the top universities globally in reputation and research output.
