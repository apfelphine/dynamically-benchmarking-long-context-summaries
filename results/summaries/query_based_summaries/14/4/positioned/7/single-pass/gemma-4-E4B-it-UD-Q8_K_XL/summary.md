Stanford University was founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford to honor their only child, Leland Stanford Jr., and officially opened in 1891. Initially modeled after Cornell University, it quickly committed to being non-sectarian and inclusive of both men and women. Architecturally, the Stanfords aimed to blend English university styles with local California heritage, and the university established the Stanford Industrial Park in the 1950s, which helped spur the development of Silicon Valley.

Stanford is recognized as a highly prestigious institution, consistently ranking among the top universities globally. The university is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity" and is home to the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory. It has a strong culture of innovation and technology transfer; alumni have co-founded major companies like Google, Netflix, and PayPal, and the university is noted for its strong venture culture. Academically, it is structured into seven academic schools, and its endowment, valued at \$36.5 billion as of August 2023, is one of the four largest in the United States.
