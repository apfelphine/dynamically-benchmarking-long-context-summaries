## Stanford University Summary

Stanford University, established in 1885 in Palo Alto, California, was founded by Leland and Jane Stanford in memory of their son. It opened in 1891, modeled after Eastern universities like Cornell with a commitment to inclusivity for women and men, and focused on accessible, non-sectarian education. The campus, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, features distinctive architecture intended to evoke California's early Spanish heritage. Despite early financial and physical setbacks (notably the 1906 earthquake and federal litigation), the university expanded over time, adding professional schools and developing a unique residential system with extensive on-campus housing. A major force in the transformation of Silicon Valley, Stanford’s encouragement of entrepreneurship and high-tech innovation catalyzed the founding of companies such as Hewlett-Packard, Google, Sun Microsystems, Cisco, and Yahoo!.

Today, Stanford is recognized as one of the world's premier research universities, with a $36.5 billion endowment and an 8,180-acre campus (one of the largest in the U.S.). Academically, the university is organized into seven schools and offers highly selective admissions with robust need-based financial aid. Research at Stanford is extensive, with expenditures of $1.82 billion in 2021–22 and a tradition of discovery and innovation ranging from the biological synthesis of DNA to the design of the Internet's architecture. The institution boasts a distinguished faculty and alumni network—including heads of state, Nobel laureates, tech founders, and Supreme Court justices. Stanford leads in NCAA team and individual championships and fosters a vibrant student life through its residential system, student organizations, traditions, and entrepreneurial culture.
