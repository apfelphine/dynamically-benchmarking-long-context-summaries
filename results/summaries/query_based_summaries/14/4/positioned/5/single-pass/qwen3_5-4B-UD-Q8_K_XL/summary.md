# Stanford University

Founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, Stanford University opened in 1891 on their Palo Alto farm, modeled after Cornell University with a commitment to non-sectarian and inclusive education. The campus spans 8,180 acres in Silicon Valley, featuring the Main Quad, Memorial Church, and the Hoover Tower. Stanford is renowned for its "fathers of Silicon Valley" legacy, driven by Frederick Terman's encouragement of entrepreneurship and the presence of William Shockley and the Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory, which catalyzed the region's tech boom. The university operates seven academic schools, including Engineering, Business, Law, Medicine, and Education, and is governed by a private board of trustees. Stanford's endowment, valued at $36.5 billion, supports one of the largest academic endowments in the United States, while the university maintains significant commercial assets like the Stanford Research Park and Shopping Center.

Academically, Stanford is highly selective with an acceptance rate of approximately 4%, offering a quarter system and a curriculum that emphasizes graduate coexistence. It is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity" with research expenditures exceeding $1.8 billion annually. Stanford has been instrumental in the development of the Internet, co-creating the TCP/IP protocol with Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn, and is home to the Stanford Digital Library Project, which evolved into Google. The university boasts a strong entrepreneurial culture, having generated over $2.7 trillion in annual revenue from alumni-founded companies. Notable alumni include former U.S. President Herbert Hoover, Google founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin, Netflix founder Reed Hastings, and former UK Prime Minister Rishi Sunak. Stanford is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, often cited as the ultimate "dream college" by students and parents.
