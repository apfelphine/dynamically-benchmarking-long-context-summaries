Stanford University was founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their only child, Leland Stanford Jr. The university officially opened in 1891 on the former Palo Alto farm. Modeled after Cornell University, Stanford emphasized non-sectarian, inclusive education for both men and women. The campus, designed by landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted, features a blend of English university architecture and California heritage, with a central campus of 8,180 acres.

Stanford is a private, non-profit university administered by a board of trustees and governed by a president. The university has a large endowment valued at $36.5 billion as of 2023, one of the largest in the United States. It is organized into seven academic schools and has a strong focus on research, with research expenditures reaching $1.82 billion in 2021/22.

Stanford is highly ranked globally and nationally, consistently ranked among the top universities in the world. It is known for its excellence in science, engineering, and business, with its graduate programs in business, law, and medicine being particularly prestigious. The university is home to the Stanford Graduate School of Business, consistently ranked as one of the most selective and top-ranked business schools globally.

Stanford is renowned for its innovation and entrepreneurship, with a strong culture of technology transfer. The university has played a pivotal role in the creation of numerous high-impact companies, including Google, which began as a research project by Larry Page and Sergey Brin. Stanford's Office of Technology Licensing is responsible for commercializing university research and inventions, contributing to a legacy of innovation.

The university has a highly selective admissions process, with an acceptance rate of 4%, one of the lowest among U.S. universities. Students are required to live on campus for their first four years, with a strong emphasis on residential life and community. The student body is diverse, with a student retention rate of 99%.

Stanford is known for its academic traditions, including the "Big Game" rivalry with the University of California, Berkeley, and the tradition of "fountain hopping," where students tour the campus fountains. The university is also home to the Stanford Memorial Church, the Stanford Axe, and the Stanford Tree, which serves as the unofficial mascot.

Stanford's faculty and alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners, and leaders in science, technology, and public service. Notable alumni include Bill Gates, Sergey Brin, and Larry Page, as well as prominent figures in business, politics, and science. The university is also home to the Stanford Research Park, a major hub for high-tech companies and startups.

Stanford's academic programs emphasize interdisciplinary learning and research, with a strong focus on innovation and practical application. The university's curriculum encourages students to engage in research and innovation from an early stage, with programs such as the Stanford Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP) allowing students to work directly with faculty.

Stanford's reputation for excellence is further solidified by its global impact, with a strong presence in research, innovation, and leadership. It is a leader in fields such as artificial intelligence, computer science, and biotechnology, and is recognized as a top institution for graduate education and research.
