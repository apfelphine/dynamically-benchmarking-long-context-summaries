Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford, is a private, non-profit research university located in Palo Alto, California. Modeled after Cornell University, it emphasizes accessibility and inclusivity, with a strong focus on arts and sciences. The university's expansive 8,180-acre campus includes the central campus, the Jasper Ridge Biological Preserve, the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, and several off-campus facilities such as the Hopkins Marine Station and the Redwood City campus.

Stanford is governed by a board of trustees with a maximum of 38 members, who appoint a president and provost. The university's endowment, valued at $36.5 billion as of 2023, is one of the largest in the United States and is a major source of funding. The institution is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and is known for its leadership in technology and innovation.

Stanford's academic structure includes seven schools: Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, Sustainability, Law, Medicine, Education, and Business. The university follows a quarter system and offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs. Admission is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of 4%, and the university is need-blind for U.S. citizens and permanent residents.

Stanford is renowned for its contributions to science and technology, including the discovery of DNA synthesis by Arthur Kornberg, the development of the first transgenic organism by Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer, and the invention of the klystron tube and the internet's foundational protocols. The university has been a hub for entrepreneurship, with numerous successful companies founded by alumni, including Google, Hewlett-Packard, and Sun Microsystems.

The student life at Stanford is vibrant, with over 600 student organizations, including a strong presence in arts, politics, and community service. The university features a unique house system with 80 different housing options, including co-ops and fraternities and sororities. The official mascot is the Stanford Cardinal, and the university is known for traditions such as "Fountain Hopping," the "Big Game" rivalry with the University of California, Berkeley, and the "Mausoleum Party."

Stanford has a strong reputation in global rankings, consistently ranking among the top universities in the United States and worldwide. It is recognized for its excellence in business, law, and engineering, with the Stanford Graduate School of Business and Stanford Law School consistently ranked as the most selective and top-ranked in the world. The university has produced numerous Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners, and influential leaders in business, science, and public service.
