## Stanford University Summary

Stanford University was founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their only son, Leland Stanford Jr., officially opening in 1891 on the Stanfords' former Palo Alto farm. It was modeled after Eastern universities like Cornell and quickly established a commitment to being non-sectarian and inclusive of both men and women. The university's commitment to innovation was significantly bolstered in the 1950s by Frederick Terman, who encouraged engineering graduates to found companies, leading to the creation of the Stanford Industrial Park, which spurred the development of Silicon Valley.

Stanford is recognized as a highly prestigious institution, with its endowment being one of the four largest in the United States, valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023. The university boasts a strong culture of entrepreneurship, with companies founded by its alumni generating significant global revenue. Academically, it is known for its high selectivity, with an acceptance rate of 4% according to U.S. News & World Report. Notable historical commitments include the pioneering of the Internet's original design at the Stanford Research Institute, and the discovery of biological synthesis of DNA by Arthur Kornberg.
