Stanford University was founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford to honor their only son, Leland Stanford Jr., and officially opened in 1891 on the Stanfords' former Palo Alto farm. Initially modeled after Cornell University, it quickly adopted progressive ideals, becoming inclusive of both men and women and non-sectarian. Architecturally, the Stanfords aimed to blend English university styles with local California heritage.

The university has a deep history of technological innovation, with its research fueling the development of Silicon Valley. Stanford's commitment to entrepreneurship is evident, as companies founded by its alumni generate significant global revenue. In terms of academics, Stanford is known for its high selectivity (acceptance rate of 4% for the undergraduate class of 2026) and its comprehensive structure, organized into seven academic schools. Its endowment, valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023, is one of the largest in the United States.
