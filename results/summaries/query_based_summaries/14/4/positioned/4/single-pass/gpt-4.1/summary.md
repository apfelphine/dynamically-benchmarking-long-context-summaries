## Stanford University Summary

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford in memory of their son, is located on an extensive 8,180-acre campus in the heart of Silicon Valley, California. Stanford is renowned for its accessible, non-sectarian, and coeducational ethos, quickly rising in the 20th century from a regional school to one of the most prestigious and influential universities in the world. It is closely associated with the growth of Silicon Valley, notably through innovations in engineering and encouragement of entrepreneurship, with alumni and faculty founding numerous high-profile companies such as Google, Hewlett-Packard, NVIDIA, Cisco, and Yahoo!. Stanford’s academic structure spans seven schools—three (Humanities & Sciences, Engineering, Sustainability) offer both graduate and undergraduate programs, while the Schools of Law, Medicine, Education, and Business are focused on graduate studies. The university has one of the largest academic endowments in the U.S., valued at $36.5 billion as of 2023.

Stanford is consistently ranked among the top world universities and is a leader in research, innovation, and Nobel laureate affiliation. Its admission process is highly selective (about 4% acceptance rate), and it offers robust need-based financial aid, including a no-loan policy for undergraduates. The campus is marked by distinct architecture and iconic landmarks such as the Main Quad, Memorial Church, Hoover Tower, and the Rodin Sculpture Garden. Student life is vibrant, with 600+ student groups, a house-based residential system, and strong traditions, including Big Game and Full Moon on the Quad. Stanford athletics excel at the national level, with the university winning more NCAA team championships than any other, and achieving significant Olympic success. Notable alumni and faculty include Herbert Hoover, multiple Supreme Court justices, heads of state, and leaders in technology and business.
