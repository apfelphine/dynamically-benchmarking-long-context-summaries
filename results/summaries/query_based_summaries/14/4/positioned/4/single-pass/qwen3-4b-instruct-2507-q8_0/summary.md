Stanford University was founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their only child, Leland Stanford Jr. The university officially opened in 1891 on the Stanfords' former Palo Alto farm, modeled after Cornell University. It is often referred to as the "Cornell of the West" due to its early faculty being former Cornell affiliates. The university's campus is one of the largest in the United States, spanning 8,180 acres, and is located in the San Francisco Peninsula, approximately 37 miles southeast of San Francisco. The campus includes a seasonal lake, Lake Lagunita, and features notable landmarks such as the Main Quad, Memorial Church, Hoover Tower, the Rodin Sculpture Garden, and the Dish, a large radio telescope.

Stanford is a private, non-profit university administered as a corporate trust governed by a board of trustees. The current president is Jonathan Levin, who assumed office in August 2024. The university is organized into seven academic schools, including the Schools of Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, and Sustainability, as well as the Schools of Law, Medicine, Education, and Business. The academic structure emphasizes a balance between undergraduate and graduate programs, with a strong focus on research and innovation.

Stanford is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of 4%, one of the lowest among U.S. universities. It follows a quarter system with the academic year divided into four terms. The university has a large endowment valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023, one of the four largest academic endowments in the United States. Stanford's research expenditure in fiscal years 2021/22 was $1.82 billion, and it is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

Stanford is renowned for its contributions to science and technology, including the invention of the internet, the development of the first transgenic organism, and the discovery of DNA synthesis. The university has a strong culture of entrepreneurship, with a history of creating companies and licensing inventions. Notable companies founded by Stanford alumni include Google, Hewlett-Packard, Cisco, and Sun Microsystems. Stanford's Office of Technology Licensing is responsible for commercializing university research and intellectual property.

The student body is diverse, with a student body of 6,996 undergraduates and 10,253 graduate students in the 2019–2020 school year. Students are required to live on campus for the first six quarters of enrollment, and the university provides a wide range of housing options, including dormitories, co-ops, and fraternities and sororities. The university's student life includes a variety of traditions such as "Fountain Hopping," the "Big Game" football rivalry with the University of California, Berkeley, and the "Mausoleum Party" held annually at the Stanford Mausoleum.

Stanford has a strong reputation in global rankings, consistently ranked among the top universities in the world. It is considered one of the most prestigious research universities in the United States, and its graduate programs, especially in business and law, are consistently ranked among the best. The university is also known for its commitment to innovation, with a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary research and collaboration. Stanford is a leader in fields such as artificial intelligence, computer science, and biotechnology, and has produced numerous Nobel laureates and Turing Award winners. The university's research and academic achievements have made it a global leader in science and technology.
