# Stanford University

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, opened in 1891 on their former Palo Alto farm. Modeled after Cornell University, it quickly adopted progressive ideals of inclusivity and non-sectarianism. The campus, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and architect John Galen Howard, features classical English and Spanish colonial styles. Following the death of Leland Stanford in 1893, the university faced a federal lawsuit but continued under Jane Stanford's leadership. It suffered significant damage from the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, leading to the demolition of some original structures. In the early 20th century, Stanford established professional graduate schools, including its medical school (acquired in 1908) and business school (founded in 1925).

Frederick Terman encouraged engineering graduates to start companies, establishing Stanford Industrial Park and fostering the development of Silicon Valley. However, the university faced controversy in the 1950s and 1960s for intentionally restricting Jewish admissions, an issue it apologized for in 2022. It also faced criticism for the 1971 Stanford Prison Experiment and misuse of government funds in the 1980s. Stanford is administered as a corporate trust with a privately appointed board of trustees and has a massive endowment. It is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of 4%, and is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, particularly for business and engineering. The campus includes landmarks like Memorial Church, Hoover Tower, and the Dish, and hosts numerous research centers and affiliated institutions.
