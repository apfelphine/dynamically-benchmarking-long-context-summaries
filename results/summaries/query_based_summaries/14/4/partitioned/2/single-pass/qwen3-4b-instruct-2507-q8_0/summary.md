Stanford University is a private, non-profit research university founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, Leland Stanford Jr. Located on an 8,180-acre campus in the San Francisco Peninsula, it is one of the largest in the United States and is often referred to as the "Cornell of the West" due to its early structural and educational similarities. The university's founding grant emphasized a design inspired by Spanish adobe houses, with deep window seats, open fireplaces, and dark red tile roofs, and was designed by landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted, who also designed Cornell’s campus.

Stanford has a strong culture of innovation, historically fostering entrepreneurship through initiatives like the Stanford Industrial Park, which helped establish Silicon Valley. The university has a strong track record in technology transfer, with a high number of companies founded by alumni and faculty, generating over $2.7 trillion in annual revenue and creating 5.4 million jobs since the 1930s. Notable companies include Hewlett-Packard, Google, Cisco, and Netflix, all of which were co-founded by Stanford alumni or faculty.

The university offers a wide range of academic programs across seven schools: Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, Sustainability, Law, Medicine, Education, and Business. It is classified as an R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity. Stanford’s research expenditure in 2021/22 was $1.82 billion, and it operates over 18 independent research centers and institutes, including the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Hoover Institution.

Student life at Stanford is vibrant, with traditions such as the Big Game (annual football rivalry with UC Berkeley), the Stanford Axe (a trophy awarded to the winner of the Big Game), and "fountain hopping" at the campus fountains. The university has a strong student housing system, with 89% of undergraduates living on campus, and a robust student government, the Associated Students of Stanford University (ASSU), which oversees student organizations and activities.

Stanford is highly ranked globally, consistently appearing in the top 10 in various university rankings, including the Times Higher Education, QS World University Rankings, and U.S. News & World Report. It is known for its emphasis on academic excellence, interdisciplinary research, and innovation, with a strong commitment to student success and engagement. The university is also recognized for its contributions to scientific discovery, including the development of the klystron tube, the invention of the laser, and the foundational work in DNA synthesis and genetic engineering.
