# Stanford University

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son, is widely recognized as a model for technology transfer and entrepreneurship. Its Office of Technology Licensing commercializes university research, and companies founded by alumni have generated over $2.7 trillion in annual revenue and created millions of jobs since the 1930s. Notable institutions include Hewlett-Packard, Google, Netflix, and Cisco. The university boasts a massive endowment valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023, one of the largest in the U.S., which funds operations and supports a diverse student body. Academically, Stanford is classified as an "R1: Doctoral University – Very high research activity" institution, with significant research expenditures exceeding $1.8 billion annually. It is home to numerous research centers, including the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Hoover Institution, and is consistently ranked among the world's most prestigious universities, often cited as the "Cornell of the West" for its early commitment to non-sectarian, inclusive education.

Stanford's campus spans approximately 8,180 acres in the Santa Clara Valley, featuring a central area adjacent to Palo Alto and extensive off-campus lands including the Jasper Ridge Biological Preserve and the Stanford Research Park. Student life is characterized by a strong "venture culture" where students are encouraged to launch their own companies, often with funding support. The university offers a quarter system with a high degree of graduate student coexistence and mandates four-year housing for undergraduates in various dormitories and co-ops. Traditions include the annual "Big Game" rivalry with UC Berkeley, the "Fountain Hopping" custom, and the "Mausoleum Party." Historically, Stanford has been a leader in scientific discovery, with faculty and alumni contributing to fields ranging from nuclear physics to genetics, and has faced historical controversies regarding admissions policies and ethical research practices, such as the 1971 Stanford Prison Experiment.
