Stanford University is a private, non-profit research university located in the northwest part of the Santa Clara Valley (Silicon Valley), founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their only child. Modeled after Cornell University, it distinguishes itself with English-style architecture and a significant endowment valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023, one of the four largest in the United States. The endowment, which includes real estate and investments, covered approximately 22% of university expenses in the 2023 fiscal year. Stanford is renowned for its technology transfer and venture culture, having created companies that generate over $2.7 trillion in annual revenue and 5.4 million jobs since the 1930s. Notable alumni include co-founders of Google, Apple, and other major tech firms, as well as U.S. Presidents Herbert Hoover and Rishi Sunak.

Academically, Stanford is considered highly selective, with a 4% acceptance rate and a focus on arts and sciences with high graduate student coexistence. It operates on a quarter system and is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" institution. The university is home to numerous research centers, including the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, and has produced significant scientific discoveries such as the mechanisms of DNA synthesis, the first transgenic organism, and contributions to the development of the Internet. Stanford is consistently ranked among the top universities globally, often cited as the "Cornell of the West" for its early commitment to non-sectarian and inclusive education, and maintains a strong reputation in business, law, and the sciences.
