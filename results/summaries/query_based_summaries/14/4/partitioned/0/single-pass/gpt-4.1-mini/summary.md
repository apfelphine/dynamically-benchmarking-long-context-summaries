# Stanford University

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford, is a leading private research university located on a large campus in Silicon Valley. It boasts one of the largest endowments in the U.S., valued at $36.5 billion as of 2023, which supports about 22% of its expenses. Stanford is highly selective with a 4% acceptance rate and emphasizes academic excellence, diverse extracurriculars, and strong financial aid policies including no-loan assistance for most low- and middle-income families. The university operates on a quarter system with integrated undergraduate and graduate programs and hosts numerous advanced research centers—including SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Hoover Institution—under the leadership of prominent faculty.

Stanford has a strong culture of entrepreneurship and technology transfer, producing some of the world’s most successful companies such as Google, Hewlett-Packard, Cisco, and Netflix, founded by its alumni or faculty. Its extensive library system includes over 9.3 million volumes, highlighted by the Green Library, and the university manages prominent cultural institutions like the Cantor Center for Visual Arts. Student life is vibrant with over 600 organizations, themed residences, and unique traditions including the Big Game rivalry with UC Berkeley, the Stanford Axe trophy, Fountain Hopping, and various annual events. Stanford’s athletic program is highly decorated, holding the record for most NCAA team championships, and the student body is diverse, with approximately 29% Asian, 23% White, and 17% Hispanic students as of 2019–2020.
