## Stanford University: Overview and Key Highlights

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford in memory of their son, is situated on an 8,180-acre campus in Silicon Valley and has grown into one of the world's leading private research universities. The institution is renowned for its commitment to inclusive, non-sectarian, coeducational ideals and remains one of the largest landowners in the region, with an endowment valued at $36.5 billion as of August 2023. Stanford's innovative architectural style blends local heritage with English university influences, and its campus features notable landmarks such as Memorial Church, Main Quad, Hoover Tower, the Dish radio telescope, and the Cantor Center for Visual Arts.

Stanford's academic excellence is reflected in its highly selective admissions (4% acceptance rate), robust financial aid programs, and a distinguished faculty and alumni network comprising Nobel laureates, founders of major global companies like Google and Hewlett-Packard, and leaders in government, science, and technology. The university operates seven academic schools and oversees numerous research centers, including the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Hoover Institution. Stanford's intellectual property and technology transfer have fueled unprecedented levels of entrepreneurship, with alumni-founded companies collectively generating $2.7 trillion in annual revenue. The university also supports vibrant student life, traditions, and athletics, boasting numerous NCAA championships and Olympic athletes, and offering guaranteed on-campus housing for all undergraduate years. Its libraries house over 9.3 million volumes, and the institution consistently ranks at or near the top of global university rankings for innovation, research, and employability.
