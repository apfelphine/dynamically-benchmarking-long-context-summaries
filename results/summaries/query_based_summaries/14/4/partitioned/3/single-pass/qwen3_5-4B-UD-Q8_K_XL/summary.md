# Stanford University Summary

Stanford University, founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford as a tribute to their son Leland Jr., opened in 1891 on a 8,180-acre campus in the San Francisco Peninsula. Modeled after Cornell University, it was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and architecturally inspired by English and California heritage. The university is a private, non-profit entity governed by a board of trustees, with Richard Saller serving as interim president and Jonathan Levin set to become the 13th president in August 2024. Academically, Stanford is organized into seven schools, including Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, Sustainability, Law, Medicine, Education, and Business, and is classified as an "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" institution.

Stanford is renowned for its entrepreneurial culture and technology transfer, often considered the model for innovation in Silicon Valley. Alumni have founded major companies such as Google, Apple, Netflix, and LinkedIn, with combined annual revenues exceeding $2.7 trillion. The university boasts a massive endowment of $36.5 billion, one of the largest in the U.S., which covers approximately 22% of its expenses. Notable traditions include the "Big Game" rivalry with UC Berkeley, the annual "Full Moon on the Quad," and the "Mausoleum Party." The campus features landmarks such as Memorial Church, Hoover Tower, and the Dish, a 150-foot radio telescope. Stanford consistently ranks among the top universities globally and is a founding member of the Biohub research center alongside UC Berkeley and UCSF.
