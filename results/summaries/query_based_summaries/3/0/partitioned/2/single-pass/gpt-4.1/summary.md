## Deepfake: Overview and Impact

Deepfake technology uses advanced AI methods, particularly neural networks and generative adversarial networks, to synthesize highly realistic fake images, videos, and audio by manipulating one person's likeness with another's. Originating in academic research during the 1990s and later popularized by online communities like Reddit, its applications have rapidly expanded across industries and the public sphere. Deepfakes are now utilized for entertainment (e.g., virtual concerts and movies), art, political campaigning, fraud, blackmail, pornography, and even identity masking. Notably, they have enabled the creation of hyperrealistic avatars for virtual events and have been employed to recreate or resurrect deceased public figures.

The proliferation of deepfake media poses significant social, ethical, and legal challenges. Deepfakes threaten informational trust, facilitate disinformation, and can be weaponized for defamation, harassment, blackmail, and large-scale scams. The ease of creating such media raises critical concerns about democracy, security, and personal privacy, with women, marginalized groups, and public figures being frequent targets. Detection efforts include advanced AI models, media literacy campaigns, and technical standards, while legislative responses in multiple countries seek to regulate malicious deepfake creation and dissemination. Social media platforms and governments are enforcing content bans and labeling requirements, yet detection remains a "moving goalpost" as deepfake technology continues to evolve.
