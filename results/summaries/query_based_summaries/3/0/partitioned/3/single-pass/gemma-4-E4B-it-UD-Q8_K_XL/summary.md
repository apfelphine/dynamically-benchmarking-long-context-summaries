Deepfake technology, which involves transforming and manipulating faces and voices using AI, has numerous applications and poses significant societal concerns. Its uses range from satirical entertainment, such as in the *Sassy Justice* webseries, to creating digital clones of actors for commercial purposes like Disney's high-resolution face-swapping technology. Conversely, deepfakes are employed maliciously for fraud and scams, responsible for an estimated $12 billion in global losses through fake endorsements and investment schemes, and for creating damaging political propaganda.

The impact of deepfakes raises critical issues regarding the credibility of information, as they can lead to a situation where it becomes impossible to determine if content is genuine. They are a tool for disinformation, capable of damaging individual reputations through fabricated remarks or voice phishing, and they are a concern for democratic societies. Researchers are developing detection methods, such as analyzing software-induced artifacts or using deep learning, while legal and regulatory responses are evolving, with various international and national frameworks attempting to address the technology's potential misuse.
