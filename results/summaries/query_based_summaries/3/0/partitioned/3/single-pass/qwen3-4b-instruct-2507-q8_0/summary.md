Deepfakes are synthetic media created using artificial intelligence to manipulate faces, voices, or actions of real individuals, often to deceive or mislead. They have been used in various contexts, including political disinformation, entertainment, and fraud. Notable examples include a deepfake of Barack Obama by Jordan Peele in 2018, a deepfake of Donald Trump by Derpfakes in 2019, and a deepfake of President Volodymyr Zelenskyy during the 2022 Russian invasion, which was later debunked. Deepfakes have also been used in satire, such as in the 2020 web series *Sassy Justice*, and in political campaigns, including in India where deepfakes were used to translate speeches into regional languages. They are frequently employed in scams, such as fake investment schemes and audio-based social engineering, and have raised serious ethical and legal concerns, especially regarding non-consensual pornography, identity theft, and the spread of misinformation. Social media platforms have implemented measures to detect and flag manipulated content, and governments have introduced legislation to address deepfake-related harms, including laws in the United States, the United Kingdom, and China. The technology is also being explored in art and entertainment, such as in deepfake avatars for virtual concerts. Despite advances in detection methods, deepfakes remain a significant challenge due to their realism and ability to exploit public trust.
