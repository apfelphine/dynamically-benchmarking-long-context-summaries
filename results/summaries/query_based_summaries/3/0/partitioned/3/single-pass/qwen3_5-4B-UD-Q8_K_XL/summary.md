Deepfake technology involves using artificial intelligence to create realistic synthetic media, including audio and video, that mimics the likeness, voice, and actions of real individuals. This technology has been developed since the 1990s by academic researchers and has since been adopted by amateurs, industries, and governments for diverse applications ranging from entertainment and satire to educational exhibits and political campaigns. Notable examples include videos of public figures like Barack Obama, Donald Trump, and Volodymyr Zelenskyy, which have been used to expose the dangers of deepfakes, spread misinformation, or influence public opinion. The technology is also frequently employed for malicious purposes such as non-consensual pornography, fraud, scams, and the creation of "sockpuppets" (fake personas) for disinformation campaigns.

The rise of deepfakes has triggered significant global responses concerning ethics, regulation, and platform policy. Social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and Google have implemented measures to flag, remove, or watermark manipulated content, while legislation in the United States, United Kingdom, China, and the European Union aims to address issues such as non-consensual intimate imagery, election interference, and data privacy. Detection methods utilizing deep learning and forensic analysis have been developed to identify artifacts in synthetic media, though experts warn that the ability to distinguish between genuine and fake content is rapidly diminishing. Concerns persist regarding the potential for deepfakes to undermine trust in democratic institutions, facilitate identity theft, and create an "age of disinformation" where truth becomes unverifiable.
