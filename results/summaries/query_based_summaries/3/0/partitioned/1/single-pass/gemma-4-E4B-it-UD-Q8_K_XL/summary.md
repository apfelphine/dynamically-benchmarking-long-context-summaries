Deepfakes are a technology that uses artificial intelligence to generate synthetic media, capable of replacing faces or voices with those of others. Applications for deepfakes are diverse, ranging from entertainment—such as creating virtual concerts with deepfake avatars or using them in satire—to art. However, they also present significant risks in areas like fraud and scams, where likenesses of celebrities and politicians are used in fake endorsement or investment schemes, resulting in billions in global fraud losses. Furthermore, deepfakes can be used to generate blackmail material and to misrepresent politicians in videos.

Concerns surrounding deepfakes center on their potential to undermine the credibility of information, creating an era where determining truth from falsehood is difficult. Detection methods are actively researched, utilizing deep learning to identify subtle inconsistencies in video and audio. Responses to this technology include regulatory efforts worldwide, such as the EU AI Act, and technological countermeasures like digital signing of media. Societally, deepfakes are seen as a threat to individual reputation and democratic functions, prompting discussions on media literacy and legislative action to curb misuse.
