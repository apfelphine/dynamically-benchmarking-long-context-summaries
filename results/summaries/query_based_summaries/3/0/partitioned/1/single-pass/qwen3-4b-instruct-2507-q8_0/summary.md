Deepfakes are synthetic media created using artificial intelligence to manipulate faces, voices, or actions, often to deceive or mislead. They have been widely used in art, entertainment, and satire, such as in video installations, virtual concerts, and parody series like *Sassy Justice*. However, they pose significant ethical and societal risks, including misuse in blackmail, fraud, and political disinformation. Deepfakes can generate fake endorsements of celebrities, politicians, and public figures, leading to financial fraud and reputational damage. In politics, they have been used to misrepresent leaders, spread false narratives, and influence public opinion, with notable instances involving Barack Obama, Donald Trump, and others. Deepfakes have also been deployed in disinformation campaigns, including non-consensual intimate imagery and fabricated political content. 

Efforts to combat deepfakes include technical detection methods such as AI-based video analysis, watermarking, and forensic tools, as well as policy responses like the EU's AI Act, U.S. legislation such as the Deepfakes Accountability Act, and regulations in countries like China and the UK. Social media platforms have implemented policies to flag or remove deepfakes, though enforcement remains inconsistent. Experts and researchers emphasize the need for media literacy, public awareness, and stronger legal frameworks to address the growing threat of deepfakes in democratic and social contexts.
