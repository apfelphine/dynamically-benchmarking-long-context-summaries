Deepfake technology has evolved from early photo manipulation to modern AI-driven creation, with research covering both technical development and social implications. Technologically, deepfakes rely on autoencoders, often enhanced with Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to superimpose one person's features onto another. Advances have moved from simple facial reanimation to whole-body deepfakes, and researchers are continually tackling creation challenges like generalization, paired training, identity leakage, and temporal coherence.

The applications of deepfakes span entertainment (e.g., creating AI actors for virtual concerts), art, and politics, where they are used for satire or misinformation. Concerns are substantial, ranging from their use in creating non-consensual pornography to their deployment in fraud and political manipulation. Detection methods involve recognizing subtle inconsistencies in video and audio, though this is a "moving goal post" due to continuous technological improvement. Countermeasures being developed include media literacy education, digital watermarking, and regulatory responses such as the EU's AI Act.
