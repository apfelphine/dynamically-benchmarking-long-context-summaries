Deepfake technology involves the use of artificial intelligence, particularly generative adversarial networks (GANs), to create highly realistic but fabricated videos, audio, or images by manipulating or synthesizing digital content. Emerging from academic research in the 1990s, deepfakes have rapidly advanced, becoming accessible to both amateurs and commercial entities. Their applications span entertainment, fraud, blackmail, and political manipulation, often exploiting the technology’s ability to convincingly impersonate individuals, alter speech, or fabricate private or public statements.

The primary concerns surrounding deepfakes revolve around their potential to undermine trust in information, defame individuals, and manipulate public opinion. Detection remains a significant challenge, with academic research focusing on identifying subtle inconsistencies in deepfake videos and audio. Legal and regulatory frameworks are still evolving, with efforts to address issues like non-consensual deepfake pornography, political disinformation, and identity theft. Platforms like social media are implementing measures to combat the spread of deepfakes, though challenges persist in balancing transparency with the risks of misinformation. The technology’s rapid development underscores the need for ongoing innovation in detection methods, media literacy, and ethical guidelines to mitigate its misuse.
