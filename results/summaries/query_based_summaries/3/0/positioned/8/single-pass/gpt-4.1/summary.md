## Deepfake: Key Information

**Deepfake** refers to synthetic media created using deep learning techniques, primarily leveraging neural networks such as autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to superimpose, alter, or generate realistic audio, images, or videos. Developed in academic circles since the 1990s and popularized in online communities by 2017, deepfakes are now produced for diverse applications including entertainment, art, politics, education, identity masking, and commercial use. They are also increasingly employed in fraud, scams, blackmail, pornography, social media manipulation, and disinformation campaigns that impersonate celebrities and politicians.

The technology's rapid advancement poses significant social, ethical, and legal challenges. Deepfakes blur the distinction between truth and fabrication, threatening digital trust, privacy, election integrity, and reputational harm. Their convincing nature makes detection difficult, spawning research into AI-based identification methods and regulatory efforts globally, such as the EU AI Act and targeted U.S. laws. Countermeasures include legislative bans, platform policies, technical detection tools, and media literacy initiatives to mitigate risks. Scholars emphasize the need for critical thinking and public awareness to discern deepfakes and to address their evolving impact on society.
