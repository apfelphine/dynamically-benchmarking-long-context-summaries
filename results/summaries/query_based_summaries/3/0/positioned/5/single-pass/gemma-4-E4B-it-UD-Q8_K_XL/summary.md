Deepfake technology, which has evolved from 19th-century photo manipulation to modern digital video capabilities, was initially developed by academic researchers in the 1990s and later adopted by online amateurs and industry. Deepfakes are created using neural networks, often employing an autoencoder architecture, which allows for the superimposition of a target's details onto an underlying facial and body structure. Advanced techniques, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), constantly improve the realism of the output, making the content difficult to combat as it continually evolves.

The rise of deepfakes presents significant concerns across various domains. Socially, they are exploited for entertainment, political misinformation, and financial fraud, with scams leveraging celebrity and politician likenesses. Ethically and journalistically, deepfakes pose an "epistemic threat" by undermining public trust in media and making it difficult to distinguish truth from falsehood. Countermeasures being developed include deep-learning detection methods, as well as calls for media literacy and regulatory frameworks globally, although the technology's rapid advancement means that genuine and deepfake videos can become indistinguishable in a short time.
