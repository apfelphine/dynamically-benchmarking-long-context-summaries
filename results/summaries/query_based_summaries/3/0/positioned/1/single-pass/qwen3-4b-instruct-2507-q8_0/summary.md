Deepfakes are AI-generated videos or images that manipulate a person's likeness to create a false or misleading representation. Developed in the 1990s by academic researchers, deepfakes have evolved through advancements in computer vision and machine learning, becoming more realistic and accessible. The technology relies on neural networks, such as autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to synthesize facial expressions and movements from audio or video inputs. 

Deepfakes have widespread applications, including entertainment (e.g., celebrity performances, parody videos), acting (e.g., digital clones in films), and art (e.g., AI-generated performances). However, they are also used for malicious purposes such as fraud, blackmail, and disinformation, where they can be used to impersonate individuals, create fake endorsements, or spread misinformation. These uses have led to significant societal concerns, including the erosion of trust in media, the potential for democratic manipulation, and the impact on individuals' reputations and privacy.

Efforts to detect deepfakes include advanced algorithms that analyze subtle inconsistencies in facial movements, lighting, or audio, as well as AI-based detection tools developed by research institutions and companies. Detection remains challenging due to the rapid evolution of deepfake techniques, with new models continuously outpacing detection methods. Social science and humanities research explore the ethical, cultural, and psychological implications of deepfakes, including their role in disinformation, gender and racial biases, and their impact on public discourse.

Governments and organizations have responded with regulations, such as the EU’s AI Act, which includes provisions for high-risk AI systems, and national laws in countries like the U.S. and China that aim to regulate deepfakes and protect individuals from non-consensual content. Additionally, media literacy initiatives and public awareness campaigns are promoted to help individuals recognize and critically evaluate deepfakes. Despite ongoing technological and regulatory developments, deepfakes remain a growing threat, with experts warning of their potential to undermine democratic processes and public trust in digital information.
