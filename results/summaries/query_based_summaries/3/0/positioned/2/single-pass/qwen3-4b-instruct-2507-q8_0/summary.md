Deepfakes are artificial media created using AI that manipulate faces, voices, or actions to appear authentic. Developed since the 1990s through academic research, deepfakes have advanced rapidly, with current applications spanning entertainment, art, and malicious uses like fraud and blackmail. The technology relies on neural networks, particularly autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to generate realistic video and audio content. 

Deepfakes are widely used in entertainment and art, such as in music videos, satirical media, and digital performances, allowing creators to generate realistic content without physical actors. However, they also pose serious ethical and societal risks, including the spread of disinformation, political manipulation, and the creation of non-consensual intimate imagery. These risks have led to widespread concerns about trust in media, democratic processes, and individual privacy. 

Efforts to counter deepfakes include detection methods using machine learning, which analyze artifacts in videos, and policy responses such as legal regulations in the EU, UK, and U.S., which aim to restrict the use of deepfakes in political and commercial contexts. Additionally, media literacy and public awareness are promoted as cognitive defenses against misinformation. Despite these efforts, deepfakes remain a growing threat due to their increasing realism and ease of access, requiring ongoing vigilance and technological innovation to mitigate their impact.
