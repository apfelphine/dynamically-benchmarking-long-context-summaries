Deepfakes are synthetic media created using artificial intelligence to manipulate images, audio, or video, often to deceive or mislead. Originating from academic research in the 1990s and popularized by online communities, deepfakes have evolved into a widespread technology used across entertainment, politics, and malicious activities. They are created using neural networks like autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), which allow for realistic facial and bodily transformations. Applications include acting (e.g., digital clones in films), art, and satire, but also pose serious risks in fraud, blackmail, and political disinformation. Deepfakes have been used to fabricate false endorsements, manipulate public figures, and spread misinformation, often with harmful societal impacts. Concerns include the erosion of trust in media, the potential for misuse in elections, and the difficulty in detecting them. Detection methods rely on identifying subtle artifacts in video or audio, and organizations are developing tools and regulations to address the issue. Despite technological advances, deepfakes remain a significant challenge due to their ability to mimic reality convincingly, raising urgent questions about media literacy, privacy, and ethical use.
