Deepfake technology involves the use of artificial intelligence, particularly generative adversarial networks (GANs), to create highly realistic but fabricated videos, audio, or images. Emerging from academic research in the 1990s, deepfakes have rapidly advanced, becoming accessible to both amateurs and industry professionals. Their applications span entertainment, blackmail, political manipulation, and fraud, often exploiting vulnerabilities in human perception and trust.

The core technique relies on neural networks that encode facial and body features into a latent space, allowing the generation of convincing deepfakes. These systems are continually evolving, making detection increasingly challenging. Concerns about deepfakes include their potential to spread misinformation, damage reputations, and undermine democratic processes. Detection methods, such as analyzing subtle inconsistencies in facial expressions, lighting, and motion, are being developed, though they face the "moving goalpost" challenge as deepfake technology improves. Regulatory frameworks, including legislative efforts in the U.S., EU, and other regions, are being established to address the ethical and legal implications of deepfakes, though challenges remain in balancing innovation with safeguards.
