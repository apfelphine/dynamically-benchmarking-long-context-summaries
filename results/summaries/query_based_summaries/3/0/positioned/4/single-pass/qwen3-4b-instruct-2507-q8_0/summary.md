**Deepfake** refers to the use of artificial intelligence to create realistic but fabricated videos, audio, or images of people, often involving the manipulation of facial expressions, voices, or body movements. The technology relies on neural networks, particularly autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to generate content that appears authentic. 

Deepfakes have diverse applications, including entertainment (e.g., creating virtual avatars in films or music videos), art (e.g., re-creating historical figures or reimagining performances), and commercial uses (e.g., personalized training videos). However, they are also widely misused for malicious purposes such as fraud, blackmail, and political disinformation, including the creation of fake endorsements and deepfake videos of politicians or public figures. 

The rise of deepfakes has raised significant ethical and societal concerns, particularly regarding misinformation, identity theft, and the erosion of trust in media. To combat this, researchers have developed detection tools, including AI-based algorithms that identify subtle inconsistencies in video or audio, and initiatives like the Deepfake Detection Challenge. Media literacy and public awareness are emphasized as critical defenses against deception. 

Governments and organizations worldwide are responding with legislation and regulations, such as the EU’s AI Act and the U.S. state-level laws targeting non-consensual deepfakes, to address risks and ensure accountability. Despite these efforts, the technology continues to evolve rapidly, posing ongoing challenges for detection, regulation, and public trust.
