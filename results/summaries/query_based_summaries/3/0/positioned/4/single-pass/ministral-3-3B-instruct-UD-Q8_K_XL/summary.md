Deepfake technology involves the use of artificial intelligence, particularly neural networks, to create highly realistic synthetic media—such as videos, audio, or images—that appear to depict real people performing actions or saying words they did not actually utter. Originating from academic research in the 1990s, deepfakes have evolved rapidly, becoming accessible to both amateurs and professionals through various tools and applications. Their applications range from entertainment and artistic expression to malicious uses, including blackmail, fraud, political disinformation, and pornography.

A key aspect of deepfakes is their ability to manipulate facial expressions, voices, and body movements to create convincing impersonations. Techniques like autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) enable the seamless integration of synthetic content into real-world scenarios, making detection increasingly challenging. The technology has been exploited for both personal and societal harm, raising significant ethical, legal, and security concerns. Efforts to combat deepfakes include detection algorithms, media literacy initiatives, and regulatory frameworks, though the rapid advancement of the technology continues to pose ongoing challenges.
