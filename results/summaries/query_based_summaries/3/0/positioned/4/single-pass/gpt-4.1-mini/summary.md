# Summary of Deepfake

Deepfake technology, originating from advances in photo manipulation dating back to the 19th century and accelerating with digital video, uses AI, particularly neural networks such as autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to create highly realistic synthetic images and videos. Emerging prominently in 2017 through online communities (notably Reddit's "r/deepfakes"), deepfakes initially featured face swaps of celebrities in pornographic content but have since expanded into various domains including entertainment, education, political satire, art, and commercial applications like personalized avatars.

Deepfakes raise significant social, ethical, and legal concerns due to their potential misuse in misinformation, defamation, blackmail, identity theft, and political manipulation. Academic research spans computer vision techniques for both creating and detecting deepfakes and social sciences exploring their cultural, ethical, and societal implications. Detection methods analyze subtle artifacts in videos and audio, but as technology improves, distinguishing deepfakes from authentic media remains challenging, prompting calls for regulatory measures, media literacy, digital watermarking, and legislation to mitigate harm. Deepfakes have influenced popular culture and politics worldwide, with various governments and platforms responding through bans, content moderation, and legal frameworks to address associated risks.
