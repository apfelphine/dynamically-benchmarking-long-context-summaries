## Deepfake: Summary

Deepfakes are AI-generated synthetic media that convincingly mimic real people in video, audio, or images, relying on neural networks (autoencoders and GANs) to create realistic likenesses. They originated from academic research in the 1990s and gained widespread recognition in late 2017, when online communities began sharing celebrity deepfake videos, particularly in pornography. Commercially, deepfake apps and platforms now enable users and companies to generate personalized content, digital avatars, and realistic voice clones with minimal data input.

Deepfakes present major concerns for disinformation, privacy, and reputation; their applications range from entertainment, art, and education to blackmail, fraud, impersonation scams, and political manipulation. High-profile cases include political figures being misrepresented, non-consensual pornography targeting mainly women and minorities, and fraudulent schemes exploiting celebrity likenesses. Regulatory responses include AI detection tools, global and local legislation (like the EU AI Act, US state and federal laws, and China's Deep Synthesis Provisions), and social media platform policies banning non-consensual deepfake content—although enforcement varies. Media literacy and targeted countermeasures have been proposed to help the public navigate and identify deepfakes, but as the technology continues to evolve, distinguishing genuine media from manipulated content is becoming increasingly difficult, raising concerns about trust, democratic function, and personal security in the digital age.
