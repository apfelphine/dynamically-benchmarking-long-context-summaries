Deepfake technology, which evolved from 19th-century photo manipulation and rapidly advanced with digital video, has been developed by both academic researchers and online amateurs. Academic work focuses on creating and identifying deepfakes using computer vision, while humanities and social science research examines their social, ethical, and informational implications.

Technically, deepfakes often rely on autoencoders, frequently upgraded with Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to superimpose a target's details onto an original video's features. The technology has broad applications, ranging from entertainment (like Disney's use of high-resolution face swapping and digital clones of actors) to malicious uses, including fraud and scams, and political disinformation. Concerns center on deepfakes' potential to undermine public trust, damage reputations, and interfere with democratic processes. Countermeasures involve technological detection methods, media literacy education, and developing regulatory frameworks globally.
