**Deepfake** refers to the use of artificial intelligence to create realistic but fake videos, images, or audio recordings that mimic real people. Developed through academic research in computer vision and AI, deepfakes are now widely used in entertainment, acting, and art, where they can generate realistic performances, such as digital clones of actors. However, they also pose significant societal risks, including the creation of malicious content like blackmail, fraud, and political disinformation. Deepfakes can be used to fabricate false narratives, manipulate public opinion, and damage individuals' reputations through non-consensual intimate imagery or false statements. 

In response, various social, legal, and technological measures have been developed. Social media platforms have implemented policies to flag or remove deepfakes, while governments and organizations have introduced legislation to regulate their use. In the United States, laws such as the Malicious Deep Fake Prohibition Act and California’s AB 602 and AB 730 aim to protect individuals from harmful deepfake content. Additionally, research into deepfake detection has advanced, with tools and algorithms designed to identify manipulated media, though the technology remains a challenge due to the rapid evolution of deepfake techniques. Media literacy and public awareness are also seen as essential in helping individuals recognize and resist deepfakes, especially in political and social contexts.
