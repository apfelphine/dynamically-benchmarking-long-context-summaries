Deepfake technology originated from 19th-century photo manipulation and has rapidly advanced with digital video, evolving from academic research in the 1990s to widespread industry adoption. Academically, research focuses on both computer vision techniques for creating and detecting these fabrications, as well as the social, ethical, aesthetic, and informational implications.

Technically, deepfakes rely on neural networks, such as autoencoders and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), where a universal encoder compresses an image into a latent space, and a decoder reconstructs it with the target's features. Concerns surrounding deepfakes are extensive, encompassing misinformation, fraud, and reputational damage; they are also being used in entertainment for digital cloning of actors and in political discourse. Countermeasures include advanced detection methods (both audio and video-based), media literacy, and legislative efforts across various international jurisdictions.
