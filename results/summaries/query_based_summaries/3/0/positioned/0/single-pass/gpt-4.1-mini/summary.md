# Deepfake Summary

Deepfake technology originated in the 1990s with academic research in computer vision and has since rapidly developed through amateur online communities and commercial applications. It uses neural networks, particularly autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to create highly realistic synthetic videos and audio by superimposing one person's likeness onto another’s in existing footage. Early projects included facial reanimation and lip-syncing, evolving to encompass full-body deepfakes and applications beyond entertainment, such as medical imagery manipulation. Deepfakes became widely known in 2017 through Reddit communities, often for non-consensual celebrity pornography, but have since been adopted for acting (digital actor clones), satire, identity masking, politics, fraud, and entertainment.

Academic research on deepfakes includes technical methods to detect manipulated content using deep learning, spotlighting challenges like minimizing training data, maintaining temporal coherence, and avoiding identity leakage. Humanities and social sciences investigate ethical, social, and political implications, notably the threats to democracy, misinformation, and privacy, particularly concerning non-consensual pornography and political disinformation. Deepfakes create new challenges in verifying media authenticity, complicating trust in visual and audio evidence and raising concerns about regulation and media literacy. Several governments and platforms are implementing legal frameworks, bans on malicious content, detection challenges, and awareness campaigns to limit deepfake misuse while fostering detection technologies.
