# Summary of Accelerationism

Accelerationism is a complex and multifaceted philosophical and political movement concerned with speeding up the processes of capitalism, technology, and societal change, often embracing posthumanism and antihumanism. Historically, it emerged in three waves: from late 1960s French post-Marxists (Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, Baudrillard), through the 1990s Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU) led by Nick Land, to the 2010s left-accelerationism characterized by Promethean and rationalist ideals. The term was originally influenced by post-structuralist critiques of capitalism and has connections to thinkers like Nietzsche, Marx, and Lenin, emphasizing the destabilization of capitalism by intensifying its contradictions and technological momentum.

Accelerationism divides chiefly into right-wing and left-wing variants. Right-wing accelerationism, associated with Nick Land and neoreactionaries, advocates for unregulated techno-capitalist acceleration, sometimes endorsing the dissolution of humanity by hyperintelligent AI and technological transcendence, with a skepticism toward democratic structures. Left-wing accelerationism, promoted by thinkers like Nick Srnicek, Alex Williams, Ray Brassier, and Reza Negarestani, seeks to harness and democratize productive technologies to accelerate beyond capitalism toward a more rational post-capitalist future, emphasizing collective rational control and scientific progress. Both variants share a posthumanist stance, often rejecting stable human essences and embracing technological transformation, with "Prometheanism" being a central concept—viewing technology as a means to overcome natural limits. Accelerationism also employs the concept of "hyperstition," or self-fulfilling prophecies that enact transformations by bringing imagined futures into being.

Overall, accelerationism critiques traditional politics and humanism, instead advocating for embracing or leveraging capitalism's technological drives to precipitate radical societal transformation, though it faces criticism for potential elitism, human subjugation, and the neglect of human welfare.
