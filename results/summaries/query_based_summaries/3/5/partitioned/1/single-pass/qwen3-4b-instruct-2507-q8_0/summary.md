Accelerationism is a diverse and often contradictory philosophical movement that seeks to accelerate the forces of capitalism, technology, and societal change, often rejecting traditional humanist values in favor of technological or rationalist transformation. It is divided into key variants: **right-wing accelerationism**, championed by figures like **Richard Land**, which celebrates unregulated capitalism and technological self-improvement as inherently progressive, viewing capitalism as a self-reinforcing, deterritorializing force that leads to a "technocapital singularity" and human extinction; and **left-wing accelerationism**, associated with thinkers like **Nick Srnicek, Alex Williams, Ray Brassier, and Reza Negarestani**, which advocates for accelerating technological and rational development as a means to transcend capitalism, promote "inhumanism," and achieve a post-capitalist future through democratized technologies. Central concepts include **Prometheanism**, a belief in the power of technology to liberate humanity from constraints, and **hyperstition**, the idea that our imagined futures can retroactively shape the present. The movement is also linked to **posthumanism** and **anti-humanism**, with left-accelerationists using "inhumanism" to describe a revolutionary transformation of human identity through technology. Despite its intellectual diversity, accelerationism has been criticized for its potential to promote elitism, dehumanization, and even far-right extremist ideologies, including the use of the term to justify violent societal collapse by white supremacists. The movement's influence spans philosophy, political theory, and cultural critique, with key works such as *#Accelerate: The Accelerationist Reader* serving as foundational texts.
