Accelerationism is a philosophical and political movement characterized by the belief that contradictions within capitalism must be intensified to drive systemic change. The concept emerged from the work of figures like Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), who drew inspiration from French post-structuralists like Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari. Land’s right-wing accelerationism advocates for the unchecked acceleration of technological and economic progress, viewing capitalism as inherently destabilizing and leading to human extinction through technological singularity. He champions China’s economic policies and critiques traditional political structures, often aligning with neoreactionary and libertarian ideologies.

Left-wing accelerationism, espoused by thinkers such as Alex Williams, Ray Brassier, and Reza Negarestani, seeks to harness the same forces of technological and economic acceleration but for progressive ends. They argue that capitalism’s inherent contradictions can be exploited to accelerate beyond its constraints, promoting a post-capitalist future through the democratization of technology and the elimination of human limitations. This variant often draws on posthumanist and antihumanist philosophies, advocating for a future where human agency is transcended by technological and rational forces. Left-accelerationists emphasize the potential of hyperstition—the idea that future visions can retroactively shape present realities—and the role of fiction and speculative thought in imagining and realizing such transformations.

The movement is marked by a tension between its liberatory aspirations and the risks of technocratic elitism, as well as debates over whether accelerationism can genuinely liberate humanity or merely serve to subjugate it further. Its influence spans literature, art, and political theory, with notable works like *#Accelerate: The Accelerationist Reader* and *Fanged Noumena* serving as key texts. The term has also been co-opted by far-right extremist groups, who use it to justify violent disruption and societal collapse as a means to achieve racial purity and ethnostate-building.
