Accelerationism is a philosophical movement characterized by the belief that capitalism's inherent contradictions should be accelerated rather than resisted, a concept rooted in the work of Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU). It draws heavily on post-Marxist and continental philosophy, particularly the ideas of Deleuze and Guattari regarding desire-production and deterritorialization, alongside influences from science fiction and cyberpunk. The movement is divided into distinct variants: right-wing accelerationism, which views capitalism as an engine of exponential growth to be embraced for the sake of technological singularity and human extinction; and left-wing accelerationism, which seeks to democratize productive technologies to jump over capitalism and achieve a post-capitalist future. Key concepts within accelerationism include Prometheanism, which advocates for the unrestricted use of technology to transcend human limitations, and hyperstition, the idea that cultural beliefs can retroactively shape reality.

The philosophy has evolved through three historical waves, starting with French post-Marxists in the late 1960s, moving through Land's CCRU in the 1990s, and culminating in the Promethean left-accelerationism of the 2010s. While some scholars like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams have moved away from the term to avoid its association with nihilism and far-right extremism, accelerationism remains a significant framework for understanding the potential of technological forces. It is also increasingly associated with far-right extremism and terrorism, where it is used to justify violent destabilization of society to achieve ethnonationalist goals, distinct from the original technocapitalist interpretations.
