## Accelerationism Summary

Accelerationism encompasses diverse and sometimes contradictory ideas, with central tenets revolving around the belief that existing contradictions within systems, such as capitalism, must be amplified to counter them. Conceptually, it adheres to posthumanism and antihumanism, with some left-accelerationists utilizing the term "inhumanism." Accelerationists frequently employ neologisms drawn from continental philosophy, and the concept of "hyperstition"—where beliefs about the future can shape present realities—is a key element.

The movement features several distinct variants. **Right-wing accelerationism** (associated with Land) posits that unregulated capitalism's self-improvement and exponential growth are the optimal path, even leading toward the enhancement and replacement of humanity by technology. Conversely, **left-wing accelerationism** (represented by figures like Srnicek and Williams) seeks to accelerate past capitalism by democratizing productive technologies, viewing capitalism's advancement as a springboard toward a post-capitalist future. Other perspectives include **effective accelerationism (e/acc)**, which prioritizes unrestricted technological progress "at all costs" to solve global problems, and **Blacceleration**, which incorporates racial capitalism into accelerationist thought.
