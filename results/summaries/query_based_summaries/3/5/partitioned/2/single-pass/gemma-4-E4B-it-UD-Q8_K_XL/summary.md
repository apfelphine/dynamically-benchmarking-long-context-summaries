# Accelerationism Summary

Accelerationism is a concept characterized by various, often contradictory, ideas, fundamentally centered on the idea that contradictions within systems, particularly capitalism, must be countered by their own aggravation to drive change. Different variants exist, including right-wing accelerationism, associated with Denis Chistyakov's analysis of Land, who views capitalism's self-optimization as leading toward a "technocapital singularity." Right-wing accelerationism embraces technological progress and often incorporates anti-authoritarian movements, sometimes celebrating human extinction in the hyperintelligent AI singularity.

Left-wing accelerationism, espoused by figures like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seeks to "accelerate past capitalism by democratizing productive technologies." This strand draws from thinkers like Mark Fisher, employing concepts like "cultural accelerationism" to critique stagnant neoliberal culture. Concepts central to accelerationism include **Prometheanism**—the assertion that technology can surpass human limits—and **Hyperstition**, which posits that chosen beliefs about the future can retroactively shape present realities. While some critiques view accelerationism as inherently elitist or dangerously tied to the destructive aspects of capitalism, others propose it as a necessary path toward radical transformation.
