Accelerationism is a political and philosophical concept characterized by the belief that accelerating capitalism and technological development is the only viable path to a radical transformation of society, often involving the abandonment of humanist norms in favor of an "inhuman" future. The movement is divided into distinct variants, primarily right-wing and left-wing. Right-wing accelerationism, notably associated with Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), advocates for the complete dissolution of the human subject and traditional political structures through unregulated capitalist growth and technological singularity. This variant often embraces dystopian imagery, such as the "meltdown" of civilization, and views human extinction as a potential solution to access a higher, post-human reality. Conversely, left-wing accelerationism, championed by figures like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seeks to accelerate capitalism's productive forces to launch a post-capitalist future. Rather than rejecting capitalism entirely, this faction argues that existing technological infrastructure should be repurposed to achieve goals like automation, reduced working hours, and universal basic income, effectively using capitalism as a springboard for liberation.

The theoretical underpinnings of accelerationism draw heavily from continental philosophy, particularly the works of Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, who described capitalism as a process of constant deterritorialization. Accelerationists extend this logic, arguing that resisting or slowing down capitalist forces is futile; instead, one must ride the wave of acceleration to its extreme. Concepts such as "Prometheanism" and "hyperstition" are central, emphasizing the belief that technology can overcome natural and social limits and that self-fulfilling prophecies can shape reality. While left-accelerationists critique the nihilism and elitism of their right-wing counterparts, both share a commitment to the idea that the future is not a fixed destination but a trajectory to be actively accelerated through technological mastery, often at the expense of traditional humanistic values.
