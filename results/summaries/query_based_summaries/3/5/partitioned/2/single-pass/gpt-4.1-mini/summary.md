## Accelerationism

Accelerationism is a philosophical and political concept concerned with the idea that contradictions within capitalism and technology should be propelled or exacerbated to hasten radical social and technological transformation. It originated from post-Marxist French philosophers like Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, and Baudrillard, who conceptualized capitalism as a force of constant deterritorialization and reterritorialization of desire and social forms.

The concept has evolved into distinct variants. **Right-wing accelerationism**, notably espoused by Nick Land and figures linked to neoreactionary thought, views capitalism as an unstoppable engine driving exponential technological progress and societal transformation, even embracing the obsolescence or extinction of humanity through the rise of hyperintelligent AI and anti-humanist inhuman rationalism. Land’s accelerationism praises unregulated capitalism, technological singularity, and advocates a form of capitalist monarchism free from democratic constraints to achieve this acceleration.

**Left-wing accelerationism**, associated with thinkers like Nick Srnicek, Alex Williams, Ray Brassier, and Reza Negarestani, seeks to use technological and rational development to push beyond capitalism by democratizing productive forces and exploiting scientific advances to create post-capitalist futures. It draws on posthumanist thought and rejects capitalism's conservative control disguised as progress. Movements like xenofeminism align with left-accelerationism in promoting technology to overcome naturalist and essentialist constraints, aiming for liberation through inhumanism and collective rationality.

Accelerationism employs concepts like **Prometheanism** (faith in technology’s power to transcend natural limits), **posthumanism**, and **hyperstition** (self-fulfilling prophecies impacting reality). While proponents advocate embracing and accelerating technological progress, critics warn of risks such as human subjugation, elitism, and the liquidation of individual welfare. There is also a notable tension in accelerationism’s relationship with traditional humanism and its potential to justify reactionary or authoritarian political strategies.

In recent years, the term has been co-opted in far-right extremist movements to denote the violent acceleration of societal collapse aiming to establish white ethnostates, diverging significantly from academic accelerationist theory.
