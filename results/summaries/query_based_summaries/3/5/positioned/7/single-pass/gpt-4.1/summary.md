## Accelerationism: Key Concepts and Variants

**Accelerationism** refers to a body of philosophical, political, and cultural thought that centers on the idea of hastening the processes of technological, social, or economic change, often with the aim of transcending current systems or limitations. Originating from post-Marxist French thinkers in the late 1960s and 70s, the movement was later shaped by Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU) in the 1990s, and evolved into Promethean left-accelerationism in the 2010s. Accelerationist thought is characterized by its engagement with posthumanism, antihumanism, and Prometheanism—the belief that technology and reason can radically transform humanity and its conditions. Notable concepts include **hyperstition** (self-fulfilling prophecies that shape reality), and the tension between deterritorialization and reterritorialization within capitalism, as theorized by Deleuze and Guattari.

Accelerationism features significantly divergent variants: **right-accelerationism** (Land) celebrates unregulated capitalism and technological progress as self-optimizing forces, sometimes at the expense of humanity, even envisioning human extinction in the singularity. **Left-accelerationism** (Srnicek, Williams, Brassier, Negarestani) aims to leverage technological and social infrastructure to surpass capitalism, promoting collective rational transformation, automation, and universal empowerment. Additional offshoots include **effective accelerationism** (e/acc), which prioritizes unrestricted tech progress at all costs, and **xenofeminism**, which seeks liberation through technological gender abolition. Some extreme far-right groups have adopted the term to justify violent and destabilizing strategies to collapse current social systems. Critiques of accelerationism highlight its risks of technocratic elitism, its potential disregard for human welfare, and its susceptibility to misinterpretation and reactionary appropriation.
