Accelerationism is a philosophical framework divided into three historical waves, ranging from French post-Marxist thought in the late 1960s to the cybernetic ideas of Nick Land's CCRU in the 1990s and the modern Promethean left-accelerationism of the 2010s. The core tenet of accelerationism is the assessment that capitalism's contradictions must be countered by their own aggravation, often through the "acceleration" of technological progress and market forces. This ideology adheres to posthumanism and antihumanism, viewing the "human" as an arbitrary limit that should be transcended by technology. It encompasses concepts like Prometheanism, which asserts the power of technology to transform humanity, and hyperstition, the idea that fictional beliefs about the future can retroactively shape present realities.

Accelerationism is split into right-wing and left-wing variants. Right-wing accelerationism, associated with figures like Nick Land, advocates for unregulated capitalist growth and exponential technological self-improvement, often viewing human extinction as a necessary step toward accessing the "Real" or achieving a technocapital singularity. In contrast, left-wing accelerationism, supported by thinkers such as Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seeks to democratize productive technologies to accelerate past capitalism and toward a post-capitalist future. While some forms of accelerationism focus on radical technological mastery and inhuman rationality, others, like Xenofeminism, use technology to undermine biological essentialism. The movement also intersects with discussions on the technological singularity and intelligence explosions, where the development of superintelligent AI could fundamentally alter human existence, a topic that has also attracted attention from far-right extremist groups who utilize accelerationist rhetoric to justify societal collapse.
