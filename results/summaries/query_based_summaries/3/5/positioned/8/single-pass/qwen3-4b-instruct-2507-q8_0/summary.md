Accelerationism is a philosophical and political movement that advocates for the acceleration of technological and social change, particularly within capitalist systems. Rooted in the works of French post-Marxist thinkers like Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, it emerged in three waves: the 1960s–70s, the 1990s with Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), and the 2010s with left-accelerationist movements. The movement is characterized by a rejection of human-centered values and a belief in the self-organizing, deterritorializing nature of capitalism, which is seen as a force of progress and transformation.

Core concepts include posthumanism and antihumanism, with left-accelerationism promoting a "Promethean" vision of technology as a tool to transcend human limitations. Right-accelerationism, associated with Nick Land, celebrates unregulated capitalism as a self-accelerating, deterritorializing force, advocating for participation in its destructive and transformative processes. Left-accelerationism, represented by figures like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seeks to use capitalist infrastructure to achieve post-capitalist outcomes through democratization of technology, automation, and universal basic income, rejecting traditional left-wing localism and direct action.

Accelerationism is marked by its use of neologisms and its hyperstitional nature—where imagined futures shape present realities. It includes variants such as effective accelerationism, which prioritizes uncontrolled technological progress, and xenofeminism, which uses technology to abolish gender. The movement has also been co-opted by far-right groups to justify violent acceleration of societal collapse. Despite criticism for its potential to promote technological elitism and human subjugation, accelerationism remains a significant influence in contemporary debates about technology, capitalism, and the future of humanity.
