Accelerationism is a philosophical and political movement that advocates for the rapid acceleration of societal and technological progress, often prioritizing speed over traditional ethical or political considerations. Emerging from the works of French post-structuralists like Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari in the 1970s, accelerationism critiques the slow pace of societal change and argues that capitalism inherently drives exponential growth through technological and economic processes. Influenced by thinkers such as Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), accelerationism posits that capitalism’s inherent instability and self-amplifying tendencies should be harnessed to destabilize and ultimately dismantle existing systems.

The movement is divided into right-wing and left-wing variants. **Right-wing accelerationism**, exemplified by figures like Land, embraces unchecked technological and economic growth, viewing capitalism as inherently progressive and resistant to traditional political constraints. This perspective often aligns with libertarian or anti-statist ideologies, advocating for the uninhibited pursuit of technological and economic expansion. **Left-wing accelerationism**, represented by thinkers like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seeks to leverage capitalism’s technological advancements to accelerate societal change toward post-capitalist or socialist goals. This variant emphasizes the use of existing capitalist infrastructure to experiment with new economic models, such as automation, universal basic income, and reduced working hours, aiming to break free from capitalism’s constraints.

Accelerationism is characterized by its rejection of traditional political frameworks, favoring instead a focus on hyperstition—the idea that beliefs and expectations can shape reality through self-fulfilling prophecies. This perspective often intersects with posthumanist and inhumanist thought, advocating for a radical transformation of human and societal structures through technology. The movement has been associated with critiques of humanism, emphasizing the need to transcend biological and social limitations to achieve a more advanced, technologically driven future. However, accelerationism has also faced significant criticism, particularly from those who see it as a dangerous embrace of unchecked technological and economic forces, potentially leading to societal collapse or the erosion of democratic values.
