## Accelerationism: Concise Summary

Accelerationism is a philosophical and political concept that advocates for the speeding up (“acceleration”) of social, technological, and economic processes—most notably those of capitalism—to provoke transformative change, disruption, or even the collapse of existing systems. Originating in French post-Marxist thought in the late 1960s and 70s (notably Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, Baudrillard), the idea was later advanced in the 1990s by Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), and in the 2010s by left-accelerationists like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams. These thinkers question whether radical change comes through opposition or by intensifying prevailing trends, sometimes seeking to harness technological advancement to escape capitalist stagnation or to revolutionize society.

Accelerationism contains multiple rival strands. Right-accelerationists (Land, Moldbug) champion the self-perpetuating growth of capitalism and technology, even to the point of human obsolescence, viewing the elimination of human constraints as desirable. Left-accelerationists (Srnicek, Williams, Fisher, Negarestani, Brassier) seek to repurpose technological infrastructure for post-capitalist ends, advocating for automation, rational planning, and overcoming traditional limitations via collective mastery and Promethean innovation. Related concepts include hyperstition (the feedback effect of speculative ideas becoming real), posthumanism/inhumanism (transcending or deprioritizing human concerns), and Prometheanism (the drive to use technology to override natural limits). The term has also been misappropriated by far-right extremist groups to describe hastening societal collapse through violence, although original accelerationist theorists refute this association.
