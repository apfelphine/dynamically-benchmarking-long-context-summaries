Accelerationism is a philosophical and political movement that advocates for the rapid acceleration of societal, technological, and cultural change. Emerging from the 1960s and 1970s French post-Marxist thought, particularly the works of Gilles Deleuze, Félix Guattari, Jean-François Lyotard, and Jean Baudrillard, it gained prominence in the 1990s through figures like Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU). The movement is characterized by a rejection of traditional humanist and Marxist critiques of capitalism, instead embracing the idea that capitalism itself is inherently accelerating and destabilizing, offering a revolutionary path forward through its own forces of production.

Accelerationism is divided into right-wing and left-wing variants. **Right-wing accelerationism**, espoused by Land and others, views unregulated capitalism as the engine of technological and social progress, advocating for a "hard takeoff" where capitalism's inherent dynamism is fully harnessed to accelerate technological and societal transformation. This perspective often aligns with libertarian and anti-statist ideologies, emphasizing the benefits of unchecked technological and economic growth. **Left-wing accelerationism**, represented by thinkers like Nick Srnicek, Alex Williams, Ray Brassier, and Reza Negarestani, seeks to exploit existing capitalist infrastructure to accelerate beyond capitalism itself, advocating for a more controlled and socially beneficial acceleration of technology and societal change. This variant often draws on posthumanist and inhumanist philosophies, aiming to transcend human limitations through technological and rationalist means.

The movement is marked by concepts such as **posthumanism**, where humanity is seen as a transitional phase in a broader evolution toward a posthuman future, and **hyperstition**, the idea that beliefs and narratives can shape reality through feedback loops, influencing the trajectory of history. Left-accelerationists often emphasize **Prometheanism**, the belief in the power of technology to surpass human limitations and achieve a more rational, inhuman future. However, critics argue that accelerationism risks reinforcing existing power structures, promoting technocratic elitism, and overlooking the ethical and social implications of rapid technological change. The term has also been co-opted by far-right movements, which use it to justify violent resistance against societal progress, often framing it as a threat to traditional values and human nature.
