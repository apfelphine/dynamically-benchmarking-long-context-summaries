## Accelerationism

Accelerationism refers to a diverse set of theories positing that intensifying the dynamics and contradictions of capitalism and technological progress can spur radical change. Originating from French post-Marxist thinkers like Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, and Baudrillard in the late 1960s and 1970s, the concept was later developed in the 1990s by figures such as Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), and in the 2010s by left-accelerationists like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams. Influences include Nietzsche, Marx (notably the idea that free trade “hastens the social revolution”), and science fiction. Accelerationism can be divided into right-wing (Land, Moldbug), which embraces unregulated capitalism and technological singularity as a means to transcend human limitations, and left-wing variants (Srnicek, Williams, Fisher), which seek to repurpose technological advancement for emancipatory, post-capitalist ends.

Key concepts within accelerationism include **Prometheanism** (maximal mastery over society and nature through technology), **hyperstition** (beliefs about the future shaping current realities), and a commitment to posthumanism and antihumanism, emphasizing inhuman rationality and the transformation of humanity beyond its current form. While left-accelerationism advocates using existing capitalist infrastructure for post-capitalist goals, right-wing accelerationism celebrates capitalism’s self-reinforcing dynamics, even at the cost of human extinction. Accelerationism's controversial offshoots include effective accelerationism (e/acc), which prioritizes unrestrained technological progress above all else, and the adoption of the term by far-right extremists to justify violent social collapse. Critics argue that accelerationism risks technocratic elitism, loss of human agency, or perpetuation of colonialism, while proponents see it as a radical strategy to drive societal evolution through intensification, rather than resistance, of prevailing economic and technological forces.
