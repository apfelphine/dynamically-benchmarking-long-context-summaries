Accelerationism is a philosophical and political framework that advocates for the acceleration of existing technological and economic forces to destabilize or transform capitalism, rather than opposing technological progress. Emerging in three waves from the late 1960s to the 2010s, the movement draws heavily on post-structuralist thinkers like Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, who viewed capitalism as a deterritorializing force of desire and production. While early accelerationism focused on French post-Marxist theory, it evolved into two distinct variants: right-wing accelerationism, championed by Nick Land, which celebrates unregulated capitalist growth and human extinction as a path to becoming "inhuman," and left-wing accelerationism, supported by figures like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, which seeks to harness capitalist technological advancements to build a post-capitalist, democratic society. The movement also incorporates concepts like hyperstition, where beliefs about the future retroactively shape present realities, and Prometheanism, which asserts the power of technology to overcome human limitations.
