## Accelerationism Summary

Accelerationism is a concept with distinct historical waves, beginning with late 1960s and early 1970s French post-Marxists like Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, whose work influenced the movement. The term was later popularized in the 1990s by Nick Land and the CCRU, and subsequently evolved into the Promethean left-accelerationism of the 2010s. Central to its philosophy is the belief that the forces of capitalist production should be ridden and directed to destabilize capitalism itself by accelerating processes.

Key concepts within accelerationism include **Prometheanism**, which advocates using innovation and technology to surpass the limits of nature, viewing flesh-and-blood "humanity" as an arbitrary limit. **Hyperstition** describes the notion that chosen beliefs about the future can retroactively shape present realities. The movement includes variants such as **right-wing accelerationism**, which views unregulated capitalism as a runaway, self-improving process, and **left-wing accelerationism**, which aims to accelerate past capitalism by democratizing productive technologies. A prominent strain, **e/acc** (effective accelerationism), advocates for unrestricted technological progress "at all costs," believing that Artificial General Intelligence will resolve global problems, contrasting with longtermism's focus on AI existential risks.
