Accelerationism is a philosophical movement divided into three waves. The first wave, in the late 1960s and early 1970s, emerged from French post-Marxist thinkers like Gilles Deleuze, Félix Guattari, Jean-François Lyotard, and Jean Baudrillard, who viewed capitalism as a deterritorializing force. Their work, influenced by Nietzsche, emphasized desire as a productive flow rather than a lack, and proposed a revolutionary path through the acceleration of capitalist processes. The second wave, in the 1990s, was led by Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), who further developed accelerationist thought by embracing unorthodox, cyberpunk-inspired ideas. Land’s work, particularly on hyperstition—the idea that our beliefs about the future can shape present realities—became foundational. The third wave, in the 2010s, includes left-accelerationists like Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, who advocate for democratizing technology to transcend capitalism, and right-accelerationists like Land, who promote unregulated capitalism as a path to technological self-improvement. The movement is marked by diverse interpretations, including "prometheanism" (the belief in technology's power to transcend human limits) and "hyperstition." Critics argue that accelerationism risks promoting technological determinism, dehumanization, or reactionary ideologies, and some view it as a misrepresentation of capitalism or a dangerous form of technological optimism. The term has also been co-opted by far-right groups to justify violent extremism.
