Accelerationism is a concept with historical divisions, tracing its roots from late 60s and early 70s French post-Marxists like Deleuze and Guattari, and later evolving through the work of Nick Land in the 1990s and into the "Promethean left-accelerationism" of the 2010s. Fundamentally, accelerationist thought is built upon the assessment that contradictions within capitalism must be counteracted by their own aggravation.

The concept encompasses various strains, including right-wing accelerationism, which advocates participating in and accelerating capitalist growth to foster technological progress, and left-wing accelerationism, which seeks to accelerate past capitalism by democratizing productive technologies. Key concepts associated with the movement include posthumanism and antihumanism, and Prometheanism, which exalts technology to surpass human limits. Further concepts include "Hyperstition," the idea that chosen beliefs about the future can retroactively shape present realities.
