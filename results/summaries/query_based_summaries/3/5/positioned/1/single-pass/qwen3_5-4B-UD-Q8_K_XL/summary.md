Accelerationism is a philosophical movement characterized by the belief that the contradictions of capitalism should be intensified rather than resolved, aiming to destabilize the system through the acceleration of technological and economic forces. Originating in the late 20th century with thinkers like Gilles Deleuze, Félix Guattari, and Nick Land, the movement draws on post-structuralist theories, cyberpunk science fiction, and antihumanism. It is often divided into right-wing and left-wing variants: right-wing accelerationism, championed by Land, advocates for unrestricted capitalist growth and the eventual replacement of humanity by advanced technology, while left-wing accelerationism seeks to use existing capitalist infrastructure and technological advances to launch a post-capitalist society.

Central concepts within accelerationism include "hyperstition," the idea that self-fulfilling prophecies can shape reality, and "Prometheanism," the assertion that humanity should transcend biological limits through technology. The movement has faced significant criticism for its deterministic views, its association with far-right extremism and terrorism, and its potential to obscure human welfare in favor of systemic efficiency. Despite these controversies, accelerationism has influenced various political strategies, including the "Brussels effect," where European regulations like the GDPR drive global compliance, and continues to serve as a lens for analyzing the intersection of technology, capitalism, and the future of human existence.
