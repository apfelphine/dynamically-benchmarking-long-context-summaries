Accelerationism is a concept with a history divided into three waves, beginning with late 1960s/early 1970s French post-Marxists like Deleuze and Guattari. The term was first used in Roger Zelazny's 1967 novel, and it was popularized in the 2010s by Benjamin Noys to describe post-structuralists embracing unorthodox Marxist views of capitalist growth, with the goal of using these forces to destabilize capitalism.

Accelerationist thought is characterized by the belief that contradictions within capitalism must be countered by their own aggravation. Key concepts include adherence to posthumanism and antihumanism, where some variants, like Prometheanism, exalt technology and rationality to surpass human limits. Furthermore, some branches, such as the CCRU's accelerationism, incorporate ideas like hyperstition—the idea that future beliefs can retroactively shape present realities—and feature influences from science fiction, particularly cyberpunk. The ideology manifests in varied forms, including right-wing accelerationism, which supports exponential growth, and left-wing accelerationism, which seeks to accelerate past capitalism through democratizing productive technologies.
