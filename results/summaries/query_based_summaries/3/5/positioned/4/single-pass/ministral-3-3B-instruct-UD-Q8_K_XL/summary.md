**Accelerationism** is a philosophical and political movement advocating for the rapid acceleration of societal and technological progress, often through the exploitation of capitalist growth and technological advancements. Emerging from the 1960s–70s French post-Marxist thought (e.g., Gilles Deleuze, Félix Guattari), it gained traction in the 1990s with figures like Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU). The movement is divided into three waves: the first (post-1968), the second (1990s), and the third (2010s), with the latter emphasizing a "Promethean" left-accelerationism that embraces unchecked technological and economic growth as a means to destabilize capitalism.

Accelerationism posits that capitalism inherently accelerates technological and social change, and that resisting this process is futile or counterproductive. Key concepts include **hyperstition** (the idea that beliefs shape reality through self-fulfilling prophecies) and **posthumanism**, which rejects human-centric norms in favor of a future where technology and human agency merge. Left-accelerationists, such as Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, advocate for leveraging existing capitalist infrastructure to transition toward post-capitalist systems, while right-accelerationists (e.g., Nick Land) celebrate unregulated capitalism’s destructive and creative forces, often aligning with libertarian or anti-statist ideologies.

Critics argue that accelerationism risks exacerbating societal inequalities, fostering technocratic elitism, or overlooking ethical concerns in rapid technological advancement. The term has also been co-opted by far-right movements, which use it to justify violent disruption and societal collapse as a means to achieve racial or nationalist goals. Despite its controversies, accelerationism remains a significant influence in debates about technological progress, governance, and the future of humanity.
