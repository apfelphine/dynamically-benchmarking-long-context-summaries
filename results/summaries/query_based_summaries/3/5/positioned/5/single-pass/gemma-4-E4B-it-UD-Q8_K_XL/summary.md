Accelerationism is a concept with three historical waves, beginning with late 1960s and early 1970s French post-Marxists like Deleuze and Guattari. The term itself was first used in Roger Zelazny's 1967 novel *Lord of Light*. It was popularized by Benjamin Noys to describe post-structuralists who embraced unorthodox Marxist and counter-Marxist views of capitalist growth.

The movement fundamentally centers on the idea that contradictions within capitalism must be countered by their own aggravation, leading to an acceleration of the process. Influences include Nietzsche, Deleuze and Guattari's concept of desiring-production, and cybernetic culture research. Key concepts include **Prometheanism**, which advocates using technology to surpass nature's limits, and **Hyperstition**, which posits that chosen beliefs about the future can retroactively shape present realities. Accelerationism features distinct variants, including the right-wing form espoused by Land, which celebrates unchecked, self-improving capitalist growth, and left-accelerationism, which seeks to accelerate past capitalism by democratizing productive technologies.
