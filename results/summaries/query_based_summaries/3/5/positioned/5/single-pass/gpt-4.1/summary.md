## Accelerationism: Overview and Key Concepts

Accelerationism is a philosophical and political idea characterized by the belief that the intensification or "acceleration" of technological, social, or economic processes—most notably capitalism and technological advancement—can drive radical transformation in society. Tracing its origins to late 1960s and early 1970s French post-Marxists (Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, Baudrillard), the concept was clarified and popularized by Benjamin Noys in 2010. Accelerationist thought draws from various influences, including unorthodox Marxist readings, Nietzschean philosophy, and later, cybernetic and science fiction imagery. Three main waves are identified: post-structuralist theory in the 60s-70s, the cybernetic and antihumanist CCRU school led by Nick Land in the 90s, and the Promethean left-accelerationists of the 2010s.

### Variants and Impact

Accelerationism includes several, often contradictory, strands. Right-wing accelerationists, most notably Nick Land, advocate deepening the mechanisms of capitalism and technological progress—even if this leads to human obsolescence—while left-wing accelerationists (Srnicek, Williams, Brassier, Negarestani) seek to harness technological advances for post-capitalist, emancipatory goals, such as democratization of productive forces and increasing rational capacity. Associated concepts include posthumanism, Prometheanism (technological mastery over nature), and hyperstition (the idea that predictions or imagined futures accelerate themselves into reality). Accelerationism has influenced political theory (including "effective accelerationism") and culture, and, controversially, has been appropriated by far-right extremist networks as justification for violent destabilization to bring about societal collapse. Critics note tensions within accelerationism over elitism, technocratic tendencies, and the risks of discarding human agency and welfare.
