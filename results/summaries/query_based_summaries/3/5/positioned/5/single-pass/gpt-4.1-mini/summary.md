# Summary: Accelerationism

Accelerationism is a philosophical and political concept that emerged in three waves: the late 1960s-70s French post-Marxist thinkers (Deleuze, Guattari, Lyotard, Baudrillard), the 1990s work of Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), and the 2010s Promethean left-accelerationism. The term was popularized by Benjamin Noys in 2010 to describe certain post-structuralists who embraced the intensification of capitalist forces to destabilize capitalism itself. A key philosophical foundation is Deleuze and Guattari’s notion of desire as productive energy and capitalism as a radical deterritorializing force, constantly breaking down and reterritorializing social and economic structures.

Right-wing accelerationism, chiefly associated with Nick Land, advocates embracing and intensifying capitalism’s exponential technological growth to foster rapid progress and dissolution of traditional social orders, often linked with libertarian and neoreactionary ideas. Left-wing accelerationists, such as Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, seek to repurpose capitalist technologies democratically to surpass capitalism, emphasizing collective political agency and technological progress toward a post-capitalist society. Concepts like hyperstition—ideas that self-fulfill by shaping reality—and Prometheanism, the celebration of technological mastery to transcend human limits, are central. Accelerationism is intertwined with posthumanism and critiques traditional humanism, often debating the role of technology, reason, and human identity in driving historical change. The term has also been appropriated by far-right extremist groups to denote calls for societal collapse through violent means to enable ethno-nationalist futures, a departure from its philosophical origins.
