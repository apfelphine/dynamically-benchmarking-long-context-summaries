John L. Pollock is a philosopher known for his work in epistemology and defeasible reasoning. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota and a Ph.D. in philosophy from the University of California, Berkeley. He held faculty positions at several universities, including SUNY Buffalo, the University of Rochester, the University of Michigan, and the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. Pollock is widely recognized as the originator of defeasible reasoning, a framework that allows epistemic norms to be ceteris paribus conditions that can admit exceptions. His influential book *Knowledge and Justification* established him as a leading figure in epistemology. He also developed the OSCAR project, an artificial intelligence software prototype designed to implement defeasible reasoning, and contributed to the fields of nomic probability and the foundations of induction. Pollock's work has had significant impact on artificial intelligence and philosophy, particularly in the areas of rationality, decision-making, and cognitive architecture.
