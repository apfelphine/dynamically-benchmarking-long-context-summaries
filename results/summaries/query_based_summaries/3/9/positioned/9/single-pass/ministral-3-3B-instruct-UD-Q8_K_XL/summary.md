John L. Pollock (1940–2017) was a prominent philosopher known for his groundbreaking contributions to epistemology and defeasible reasoning. Born in Atchison, Kansas, he earned a triple-major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961 before pursuing a doctoral degree at the University of California, Berkeley, where his work on analyticity and implication laid the foundation for his later philosophical innovations.

Pollock’s most influential book, *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), introduced the concept of **defeasible reasoning**, challenging the rigid, monolithic frameworks of traditional epistemology. Unlike traditional theories that sought absolute certainty, Pollock argued that knowledge is often probabilistic and subject to exceptions—what he termed "ceteris paribus" conditions. His ideas became foundational in artificial intelligence, where non-monotonic reasoning (allowing for contradictions and exceptions) became essential. His work on defeasible reasoning, including concepts like *undercutting defeat* and *rebutting defeat*, reshaped how philosophers and AI researchers understood logical systems that accommodate uncertainty.

Beyond epistemology, Pollock explored **nomic probability** (*Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction*, 1990), examining how probabilities are derived from frequencies and statistical inference. He also contributed to cognitive science through his work on **OSCAR**, an artificial intelligence system designed to reason defeasibly about perception, causality, and decision-making. Though OSCAR was not a widely adopted commercial AI, it exemplified Pollock’s vision of an AI capable of nuanced, human-like reasoning.

Pollock’s legacy lies in his emphasis on **flexibility and contextuality** in knowledge and decision-making, offering a more pragmatic and adaptive approach to philosophy and AI. His ideas continue to influence fields such as cognitive science, legal reasoning, and ethical decision-making, particularly in systems that require handling uncertainty and exceptions.
