John Leslie Pollock, born John Leslie Pollock on January 28, 1940, earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. He received his doctoral dissertation in 1965 from the University of California, Berkeley, which included an appendix on defeasible reasoning that became a major contribution to his philosophical work. Pollock held faculty appointments at institutions including SUNY Buffalo, the University of Rochester, the University of Michigan, and the University of Arizona, where he was instrumental in founding the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock established himself in epistemology with his book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974). He became widely recognized as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" for his work on defeasible reasoning, a concept that significantly impacted the fields of Artificial Intelligence and law. He further developed his ideas in publications such as "Defeasible reasoning" in *Cognitive Science* (1987), and dedicated considerable later career time to the OSCAR software project, an AI prototype implementing his theories on defeasible reasoning, intentions, and cognitive architectural design. His investigations also included *Nomic Probability and The Foundations of Induction* (1990), which explored the link between defeasible reasoning and probability estimation.
