## John L. Pollock: Life, Career, and Philosophical Contributions

John L. Pollock (1940-2009) was an American philosopher renowned for foundational work in epistemology, logic, and artificial intelligence. Educated at the University of Minnesota and UC Berkeley, Pollock’s dissertation on analyticity and implication introduced the concept of defeasible reasoning, which became central to his philosophical legacy. Throughout his career—at SUNY Buffalo, University of Rochester, University of Michigan, and primarily at the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program—Pollock made substantial contributions to the study of knowledge and rationality.

Pollock is best known for the book *Knowledge and Justification*, which brought the concept of defeasible reasoning into mainstream philosophy, offering ceteris paribus epistemic norms that admit exceptions, a shift away from rigid analytic criteria. His work distinguished between "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat" and helped shape AI approaches to non-monotonic reasoning. Pollock also developed OSCAR, an AI software embodying his theories of defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture. His other major contributions include work on nomic probability and induction, substantial theoretical discussions on decision-making, and numerous influential publications spanning logic, probability, semantics, and the philosophical foundations of AI.
