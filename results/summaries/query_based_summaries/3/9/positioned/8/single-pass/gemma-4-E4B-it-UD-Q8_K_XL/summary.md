John Leslie Pollock was born on January 28, 1940, in Atchison, Kansas, and graduated from the University of Minnesota in 1961 with a triple major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy. His doctoral dissertation from the University of California, Berkeley, advised by Ernest Adams, contained an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which became a core contribution to his philosophical work.

Pollock is best known in epistemology for his book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), which moved beyond seeking analytic criteria for knowledge, instead governing his epistemic norms by defeasible reasoning—conditions that allow for exceptions. He gained renown as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and is credited with bringing these ideas into the philosophical mainstream. Later in his career, he developed the OSCAR software prototype, an artificial intelligence program that implemented his ideas on defeasible reasoning, intentions, and cognitive architecture.
