**John L. Pollock: Life and Contributions**

John Leslie Pollock (1940–2012) was a prominent American philosopher known chiefly for his work on defeasible reasoning and epistemology. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy, and completed his Ph.D. at UC Berkeley under Ernest Adams. Pollock held faculty positions at several universities, notably the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock’s influential book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974) addressed epistemological norms using defeasible reasoning—reasoning that admits exceptions—thus contributing significantly to mainstream philosophy. He was often dubbed "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning," particularly admired in artificial intelligence research for his clarity and early commitment to non-monotonic reasoning.

Later in his career, Pollock developed the OSCAR artificial intelligence program, an implementation of his defeasible reasoning concepts combined with models of intentions, problem-solving, and decision-making. OSCAR was ambitious in scope, aiming to emulate human-like rationality and cognition.

Pollock also contributed to foundational work in nomic probability and induction, elaborated through his book *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990). His broad range of publications includes numerous books and papers spanning logic, semantics, epistemology, AI, and the philosophy of action, reflecting a deep engagement with both theoretical and practical aspects of reasoning.

---

**Key Aspects of Pollock’s Philosophical Work**

- **Defeasible Reasoning:** Pollock’s pioneering distinction between "rebutting" and "undercutting" defeat has become a staple in reasoning and AI literature, influencing epistemology and computer science.

- **Epistemology:** He approached knowledge not as absolute but subject to norms that hold "ceteris paribus" (all else being equal), allowing for exceptions and thus addressing issues like the Gettier problem.

- **Artificial Intelligence:** Through OSCAR, Pollock sought to build a rational agent capable of defeasible reasoning about perception, causation, planning, and decision-making. His *How to Build a Person* and *Cognitive Carpentry* works outline his vision of AI combining philosophical rigor with computational implementation.

- **Probability and Induction:** Pollock advanced theories on how probabilities can be directly inferred from frequencies, extending classical Bayesian and statistical frameworks.

Pollock’s legacy is characterized by bridging rigorous philosophical analysis with the development of computational models to simulate human-like rationality.
