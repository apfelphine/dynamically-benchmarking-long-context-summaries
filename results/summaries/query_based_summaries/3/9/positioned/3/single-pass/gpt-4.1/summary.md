## Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was a noted philosopher and cognitive scientist whose career focused on epistemology and artificial intelligence. Born in Atchison, Kansas, he earned degrees in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota before completing his influential doctoral dissertation at UC Berkeley. Pollock held academic positions at several universities, most notably the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is recognized for his foundational work in epistemology, particularly through his book *Knowledge and Justification*, which introduced defeasible reasoning—epistemic norms that allow for exceptions. He further developed concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," which became central to the study of defeasible reasoning. His influence extended into artificial intelligence, where he created the OSCAR AI prototype to model defeasible reasoning and cognitive architectures. Pollock also contributed to the field of probability and induction with his work on nomic probability. Throughout his career, Pollock published extensively on logic, rationality, probability, semantics, and the intersection of philosophy and AI.
