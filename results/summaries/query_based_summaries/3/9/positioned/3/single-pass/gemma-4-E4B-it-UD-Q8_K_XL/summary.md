John Leslie Pollock, born on January 28, 1940, received a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. His doctoral dissertation from the University of California, Berkeley, included an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which became his primary philosophical contribution. He held faculty positions at several universities, including the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is widely known in philosophy for establishing his work in epistemology, particularly through his book *Knowledge and Justification*. He gained prominence as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" due to his focus on defeasible reasoning, which uses *ceteris paribus* conditions that allow for exceptions. He developed concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," and later dedicated time to the OSCAR software project, which implemented his ideas on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architectural design.
