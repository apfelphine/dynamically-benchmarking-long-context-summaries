John L. Pollock, born on January 28, 1940, earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. His doctoral dissertation, *Analyticity and Implication* from the University of California, Berkeley (1965), included an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which later became his primary contribution to philosophy.

Pollock held faculty positions at several universities, including the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. He became widely known as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning." His work on epistemology, particularly in *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), moved beyond identifying "analytic criteria" for knowledge, grounding his epistemic norms in defeasible reasoning. He also dedicated significant time to the OSCAR software project, an artificial intelligence prototype implementing his ideas on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architectural design. His later investigations included *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990).
