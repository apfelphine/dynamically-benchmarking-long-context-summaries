**Summary of Information Regarding John L. Pollock**

John L. Pollock (1940–from Atchison, Kansas) was a philosopher and cognitive scientist notable for pioneering work in defeasible reasoning and epistemology. He completed a triple major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy at the University of Minnesota and earned his doctorate at UC Berkeley under Ernest Adams, where he first explored defeasible reasoning—a type of reasoning allowing for exceptions—which became his primary philosophical contribution. Pollock held faculty positions at several universities, notably the University of Arizona, where he co-founded the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock’s influential book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974) challenged traditional epistemology by emphasizing epistemic norms governed by defeasible reasoning rather than absolute criteria for knowledge. His work in defeasible reasoning earned him the nickname "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and influenced logic, artificial intelligence, and law, introducing concepts like "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat." Later, he developed the OSCAR artificial intelligence system embodying his theories on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture. Pollock also investigated probability and induction in his book *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990). He authored numerous books and papers advancing philosophy, logic, AI, and epistemology, remaining a significant figure in the integration of philosophical reasoning with artificial intelligence.
