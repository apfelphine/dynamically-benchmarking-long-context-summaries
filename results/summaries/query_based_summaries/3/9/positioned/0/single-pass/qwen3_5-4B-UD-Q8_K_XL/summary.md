# **Concise Summary**

**John L. Pollock** was an American philosopher born in 1940 who became a foundational figure in epistemology and the philosophy of artificial intelligence, particularly through his pioneering work on **defeasible reasoning**. After earning a triple-major degree from the University of Minnesota and a PhD from UC Berkeley, Pollock spent most of his academic career at the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. His seminal 1974 book, *Knowledge and Justification*, challenged the traditional view that knowledge consists of justified true belief by introducing the concept of epistemic norms governed by defeasible reasoning—ceteris paribus conditions that admit exceptions. This framework, which he also termed "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning," profoundly influenced fields beyond philosophy, including artificial intelligence and law.

Later in his career, Pollock dedicated significant effort to the **OSCAR** project, an ambitious artificial intelligence software prototype designed to implement his theories on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture. OSCAR, a LISP-based program, aimed to create an "artilect" capable of reasoning about perception, causation, and decision-making while filtering out uninteresting conclusions based on the agent's interests. Pollock also published extensively on nomic probability, subjunctive reasoning, and the logical foundations of rational decision-making. His intellectual legacy is marked by a rigorous synthesis of philosophical logic and computational theory, establishing a robust theoretical framework that continues to shape discussions on rationality, justification, and the nature of intelligent systems.
