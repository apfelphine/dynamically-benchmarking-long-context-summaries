## John L. Pollock – Life and Contributions

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was an influential American philosopher noted for his foundational work in epistemology, defeasible reasoning, and artificial intelligence. Educated at the University of Minnesota (B.S., triple major: physics, mathematics, philosophy) and the University of California, Berkeley (Ph.D., 1965), Pollock’s dissertation introduced early concepts of defeasible reasoning—a recurring theme in his philosophical career. He held faculty positions at SUNY Buffalo, University of Rochester, University of Michigan, and spent most of his career at the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is best known for his book *Knowledge and Justification*, which shifted epistemology toward ceteris paribus norms and defeasible reasoning rather than strict analytic criteria for knowledge. His work established defeasible reasoning in mainstream philosophy, notably distinguishing "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," concepts now central to the literature. In artificial intelligence, Pollock’s ideas were pivotal within non-monotonic reasoning. He developed OSCAR, a LISP-based AI system implementing his theories of defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture, designed to reason about perception, causation, probabilities, planning, and decision-making. Pollock also made significant contributions to the theory of nomic probability and the foundations of induction. His extensive publication record includes seminal books and papers on epistemology, symbolic logic, philosophical semantics, and AI.

**Key Publications:**
- *Knowledge and Justification* (1974)
- *Contemporary Theories of Knowledge* (1987, 1999)
- *How to Build a Person* (1989)
- *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990)
- *Cognitive Carpentry: A blueprint for how to build a person* (1995)
- Numerous influential papers on epistemic justification, defeasible reasoning, moral reasoning, AI, and decision theory.
