John Leslie Pollock was born in 1940 and earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. His doctoral dissertation at the University of California, Berkeley, featured an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which became his primary philosophical contribution. He held faculty positions at institutions including the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock gained significant recognition for his work in epistemology, particularly with his book *Knowledge and Justification*. He became known as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" for his emphasis on defeasible reasoning—epistemic norms governed by ceteris paribus conditions that allow for exceptions. Later in his career, he worked on OSCAR, an artificial intelligence prototype embodying his ideas on defeasible reasoning, intentions, and problem-solving, which was based on his earlier work, *How to Build a Person: A prolegomenon*.
