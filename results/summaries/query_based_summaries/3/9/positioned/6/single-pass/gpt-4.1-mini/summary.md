## Summary of Information About John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (born January 28, 1940, Atchison, Kansas) was an influential philosopher and cognitive scientist known primarily for his pioneering work on defeasible reasoning. He earned a triple major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy at the University of Minnesota and completed his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley, under Ernest Adams. Pollock held academic positions at multiple universities, most notably the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock’s major philosophical contribution centers on epistemology and defeasible (non-monotonic) reasoning, developed extensively from his 1965 dissertation and subsequent work culminating in his book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), which integrated defeasible reasoning into mainstream epistemology. He was known as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and significantly influenced the fields of philosophy and artificial intelligence by formalizing types of defeaters such as undercutting and rebutting defeat. Later in his career, Pollock developed OSCAR, a LISP-based AI project embodying his theories about rational decision-making and defeasible reasoning, aspiring to build an "artilect" capable of human-like cognition. Beyond philosophy, Pollock was an avid mountain biker and founder of a biking club in Southern Arizona.
