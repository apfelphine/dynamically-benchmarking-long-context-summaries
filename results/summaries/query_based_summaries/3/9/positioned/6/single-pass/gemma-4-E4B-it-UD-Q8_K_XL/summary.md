John Leslie Pollock was born in Atchison, Kansas, on January 28, 1940. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961, and his doctoral dissertation from the University of California, Berkeley, was advised by Ernest Adams. Pollock held faculty positions at institutions including SUNY Buffalo, the University of Rochester, the University of Michigan, and the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is recognized in philosophy for his work in epistemology, notably his book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974). He is particularly known as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" for his commitment to defeasible reasoning, which he incorporated into his philosophical mainstream contributions. Later in his career, he worked on the OSCAR software project, an AI prototype implementing his ideas on defeasible reasoning, and researched Nomic Probability.
