## Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was an influential American philosopher recognized for his pioneering work in epistemology and artificial intelligence. After receiving degrees in physics, mathematics, and philosophy, and earning his PhD at UC Berkeley, Pollock became a leading figure at the University of Arizona, notably helping to establish its Cognitive Science Program. His seminal book *Knowledge and Justification* positioned him at the center of philosophical debates about knowledge, introducing the concept of "defeasible reasoning"—norms that admit exceptions and allow knowledge to be justified under ceteris paribus conditions.

Pollock's contributions to defeasible reasoning, particularly his distinctions between "undercutting" and "rebutting" defeat, were foundational and became standard in the literature. He extended these ideas into artificial intelligence through OSCAR, a LISP-based program demonstrating interest-driven, defeasible problem-solving and reasoning. Pollock also explored the intersection of probability and reasoning in *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction*. Over his career, he authored numerous books and articles covering logic, epistemology, philosophy of language, probability, and the logical foundations of decision-making, solidifying his legacy as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and a key bridge between philosophy and AI.
