**Summary of Information on John L. Pollock**

John L. Pollock (born January 28, 1940, in Atchison, Kansas) was a philosopher known primarily for his work in epistemology and defeasible reasoning. He earned a triple major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy at the University of Minnesota (1961) and completed his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley in 1965. Pollock held academic positions at several universities, including SUNY Buffalo, University of Rochester, University of Michigan, and notably the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock’s philosophical contributions include the influential book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), which addressed epistemology through the lens of defeasible reasoning—reasoning that allows for exceptions and revisions. He became renowned as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning," introducing key concepts such as "undercutting" and "rebutting" defeat. Later in his career, Pollock developed *OSCAR*, a LISP-based artificial intelligence program implementing his theories of defeasible reasoning, perception, planning, and decision-making. His work also included *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990), exploring the interaction between defeasible reasoning and probability theory. Pollock published numerous books and papers that bridged philosophy and artificial intelligence.
