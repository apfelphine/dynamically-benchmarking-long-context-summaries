## Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2009), born in Atchison, Kansas, was a philosopher whose foundational work shaped contemporary epistemology and defeasible reasoning. Pollock earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota and completed his Ph.D. at UC Berkeley with a dissertation on analyticity and implication, which introduced the concept of defeasible reasoning—a central theme in his career. Pollock held faculty appointments at several universities, most notably at the University of Arizona where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock’s contributions include the influential book *Knowledge and Justification*, which introduced epistemic norms governed by defeasible (exception-admitting) reasoning, bringing such ideas into the philosophical mainstream. Renowned among philosophers as “Mr. Defeasible Reasoning,” Pollock distinguished between “undercutting defeat” and “rebutting defeat,” concepts that remain core to the field. His later work focused on the AI software project OSCAR, a LISP-based system embodying his theories on interest-driven, defeasible reasoning. Pollock also explored the foundations of statistical inference and direct probability in *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (Oxford, 1990). His substantial bibliography includes books such as *How to Build a Person*, *Cognitive Carpentry*, and numerous pivotal papers in philosophy, logic, AI, and epistemology.
