## John L. Pollock – Summary

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was an American philosopher and cognitive scientist renowned for his foundational work in epistemology and artificial intelligence. He earned his undergraduate degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy at the University of Minnesota, and received his PhD from UC Berkeley with a dissertation on analyticity and implication. Pollock held faculty positions at several universities, most notably at the University of Arizona, where he was a key figure in establishing the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is most celebrated for his contributions to the theory of defeasible reasoning, which challenges rigid analytic criteria for knowledge by emphasizing that epistemic norms admit exceptions. His influential book *Knowledge and Justification* ushered in the mainstream acceptance of this approach in philosophy. In AI, Pollock developed the OSCAR software—an "artilect" implementing his theories on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture. He pioneered concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," now standard in defeasible reasoning literature. His extensive publication record spans books and numerous papers on epistemology, moral reasoning, induction, philosophical semantics, and AI, cementing his legacy as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" among philosophers and AI researchers.
