## Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock was a prominent American philosopher, born in Atchison, Kansas in 1940, with an academic background in physics, mathematics, and philosophy. He earned his doctorate at UC Berkeley and held faculty positions at several universities, notably the University of Arizona where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. Pollock was known for his pioneering work in epistemology and the philosophy of mind, particularly his influential book *Knowledge and Justification*, in which he introduced the concept of defeasible reasoning—reasoning governed by standards that admit exceptions rather than strict analytic criteria.

Pollock became widely recognized for advancing defeasible reasoning, a concept that gained significant traction both in philosophy and artificial intelligence, especially for its clarity and early commitment to non-monotonic logic. He originated key distinctions like "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat" in the literature. Later in his career, Pollock developed OSCAR, a LISP-based AI prototype designed to implement his ideas on defeasible reasoning and interest-based cognition. He also conducted major work on the philosophy of probability and induction, particularly in his book *Nomic Probability and The Foundations of Induction*. Pollock authored numerous influential books and papers spanning symbolic logic, semantics, and rational decision-making, leaving a lasting impact on epistemology, AI, and cognitive science.
