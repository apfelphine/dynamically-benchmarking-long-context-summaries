John L. Pollock is a philosopher known for his work in epistemology and defeasible reasoning. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota and later held faculty positions at several universities, including the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. Pollock is best known for his book *Knowledge and Justification*, which established him in epistemology by introducing defeasible reasoning—ceteris paribus norms that allow for exceptions. This concept, which governs epistemic norms, became central to his philosophical contributions and is widely recognized in the field. He also developed the idea of defeasible reasoning through his work on the "undercutting" and "rebutting" of arguments, which remain foundational in the field. Pollock's later work included a software project called OSCAR, an artificial intelligence prototype based on defeasible reasoning, and he explored related topics such as nomic probability and the foundations of induction. He is often referred to as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" among philosophers.
