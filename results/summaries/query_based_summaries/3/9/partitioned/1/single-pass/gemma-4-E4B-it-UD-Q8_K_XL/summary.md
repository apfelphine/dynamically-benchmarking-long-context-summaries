**John L. Pollock** was a philosopher known for his work in epistemology, notably for his development of "Defeasible Reasoning." His foundational work in this area includes the paper "Defeasible reasoning" in *Cognitive Science* (1987), and his ideas matured in *Knowledge and Justification*. He developed key concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," later expanding to include "self-defeat."

Later in his career, Pollock worked on a software project called **OSCAR**, an artificial intelligence prototype he termed an "artilect." OSCAR was an implementation of his ideas on defeasible reasoning and also incorporated his less-known concepts regarding intentions, interests, and problem-solving strategies. Pollock's career saw him publish extensive works covering topics like knowledge, justification, and logic, including *Knowledge and Justification* and *Cognitive Carpentry*.
