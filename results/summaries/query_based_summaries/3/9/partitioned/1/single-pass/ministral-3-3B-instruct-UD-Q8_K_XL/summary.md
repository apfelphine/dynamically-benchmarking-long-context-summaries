John L. Pollock was a prominent philosopher known for his groundbreaking contributions to **defeasible reasoning** and epistemology. Often referred to as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning," Pollock became influential in artificial intelligence and law due to his early and clear commitment to the concept, which challenges traditional logical frameworks by allowing for exceptions and partial truths. His foundational work, *Defeasible Reasoning* (1987), introduced key distinctions like "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," which remain central to the field.

Pollock’s philosophical work also explored **probability and induction**, particularly in *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990), where he examined how probability can be derived from frequencies and frequencies themselves. His 1967 paper, *"Criteria and Our Knowledge of the Material World,"* marked the beginning of his epistemological research, which critiqued traditional approaches to knowledge and justified belief. His writings on **OSCAR**, an AI project he developed, further advanced his ideas on reasoning systems capable of handling uncertainty and complex decision-making.

Pollock’s intellectual legacy lies in his emphasis on **flexible, context-sensitive reasoning**—a departure from rigid logical systems—making him a key figure in bridging philosophy and artificial intelligence. His work laid the groundwork for understanding how machines and humans can handle incomplete or uncertain information effectively.
