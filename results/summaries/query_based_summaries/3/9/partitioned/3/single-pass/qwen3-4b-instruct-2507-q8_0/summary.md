John L. Pollock was a philosopher and epistemologist known for his foundational work on defeasible reasoning, a framework for reasoning that allows for exceptions and context-dependent conclusions. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota and held academic positions at several institutions, including the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. His seminal book *Knowledge and Justification* established him as a leading figure in epistemology, introducing the idea that epistemic norms are ceteris paribus conditions that can admit exceptions. Pollock's work on defeasible reasoning, first articulated in publications such as "Defeasible Reasoning" and later matured in *Knowledge and Justification*, became central to both philosophy and artificial intelligence. He developed a software project called OSCAR, an AI prototype implementing defeasible reasoning and cognitive architecture, which he referred to as an "artilect." His later works, including *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction*, further explored the relationship between defeasible reasoning and probabilistic inference. Pollock was widely recognized as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and his ideas significantly influenced the development of non-monotonic logic and AI systems.
