# Summary of Information on John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (born January 28, 1940, in Atchison, Kansas) was a philosopher and cognitive scientist known primarily for his pioneering work on defeasible reasoning and epistemology. He held academic positions at several universities, notably spending much of his career at the University of Arizona where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program. His doctoral work at UC Berkeley included foundational ideas on defeasible reasoning, which became central to his philosophical contributions.

Pollock's seminal book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974) significantly influenced epistemology by introducing defeasible reasoning as a normative framework for knowledge that allows exceptions, moving beyond traditional analytic criteria debates like the Gettier problem. He was recognized as a key figure ("Mr. Defeasible Reasoning") in philosophy and artificial intelligence for clarifying non-monotonic reasoning. His paper *Defeasible Reasoning* (1987) and later distinctions such as "undercutting" and "rebutting" defeat have become standard in the field.

In AI, Pollock developed the OSCAR system, a LISP-based artificial intelligence prototype embodying his ideas on defeasible reasoning, intentions, and problem-solving strategies. OSCAR was notable for its "interest-based" approach, efficiently avoiding "uninteresting" conclusions and covering reasoning about perception, change, causation, probabilities, planning, and decision-making. Pollock described its theoretical and practical evolution in his writings, notably in *How to Build a Person*.

Pollock also explored the foundations of probabilistic reasoning in philosophy, as in his book *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990), examining relationships between defeasible reasoning and statistical inference. His extensive publications span symbolic logic, epistemology, philosophical semantics, modal logic, and practical reasoning, reflecting a broad and influential career bridging philosophical theory and AI applications.
