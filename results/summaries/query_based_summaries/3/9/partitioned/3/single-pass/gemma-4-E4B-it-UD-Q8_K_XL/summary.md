John L. Pollock was an academic who earned a triple-major in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. His doctoral dissertation, advised by Ernest Adams, included an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which later became central to his philosophical contributions. He held faculty positions at several universities, including the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock is best known for establishing his reputation in epistemology through his book, *Knowledge and Justification*. His epistemic norms are based on defeasible reasoning—conditions that can admit exceptions. He devoted later career work to the OSCAR project, an AI software prototype that implemented his ideas on defeasible reasoning, intentions, and cognitive architecture, as detailed in his work *How to Build a Person*. His publications covered topics such as nomic probability and the foundations of induction.
