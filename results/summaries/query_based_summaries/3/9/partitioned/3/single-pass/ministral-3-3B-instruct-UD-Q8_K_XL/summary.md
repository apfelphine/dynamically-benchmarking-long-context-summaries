John L. Pollock was a prominent epistemologist and philosopher whose work significantly advanced the study of **defeasible reasoning** and its applications in philosophy and artificial intelligence. Born in 1940, Pollock earned his Ph.D. in philosophy from the University of California, Berkeley, where his dissertation on analyticity and implication laid the groundwork for his later contributions.

His most influential book, *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), established Pollock as a key figure in epistemology by introducing the concept of **defeasible reasoning**—a framework that allows for exceptions and probabilistic judgments rather than rigid, absolute certainty. This work was pivotal in shifting philosophical discourse toward more nuanced and practical approaches to knowledge, aligning with broader trends in AI and cognitive science. Pollock’s ideas on defeasible reasoning influenced fields like artificial intelligence, where non-monotonic logic became essential for systems handling uncertain or incomplete information.

Later in his career, Pollock developed **OSCAR**, an artificial intelligence software prototype designed to reason defeasibly about perception, causation, probabilities, and decision-making. OSCAR embodied Pollock’s broader philosophical and cognitive architectural ambitions, focusing on how machines could simulate human-like reasoning under conditions of uncertainty. Though not widely adopted, OSCAR remains a notable example of Pollock’s innovative approach to integrating philosophical insights into AI systems.

Pollock’s legacy lies in his emphasis on **pragmatic, context-dependent reasoning**—a departure from traditional logical absolutism. His work underscored the importance of flexibility and contextual judgment in both philosophical inquiry and technological design, making him a foundational figure in the philosophy of AI and cognitive science.
