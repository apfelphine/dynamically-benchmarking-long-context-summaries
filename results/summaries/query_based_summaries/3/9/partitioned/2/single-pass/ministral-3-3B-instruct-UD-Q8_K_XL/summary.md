John L. Pollock was a prominent philosopher known for his groundbreaking work in **defeasible reasoning** and epistemology. His most influential contributions include:

- **Nomic Probability and Induction**: Pollock’s foundational work in *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990) explored how defeasible reasoning and probability estimation from frequencies can inform inductive logic. This work builds on his earlier 1983 paper in *Theory and Decision*, which laid the groundwork for his broader theories.

- **Defeasible Reasoning**: Pollock is widely recognized as the architect of **defeasible reasoning**, a framework that allows for logical conclusions that can be undermined or "defeated" by new evidence. His 1987 paper *"Defeasible Reasoning"* in *Cognitive Science* introduced key concepts like *undercutting defeat* and *rebutting defeat*, which became central to AI and philosophical debates on rationality.

- **OSCAR and Rationality**: His software project **OSCAR** (an "artilect") exemplified his ideas on reasoning systems, integrating defeasible logic with cognitive architectures. Pollock also explored broader themes like **intentions, interests, and problem-solving strategies**, contributing to the field of artificial intelligence and cognitive science.

Pollock’s work bridged philosophy and AI, influencing both fields by emphasizing the importance of **flexible, context-sensitive reasoning**—a departure from rigid, monolithic logical systems. His ideas remain foundational in debates on rationality, epistemology, and the design of intelligent systems.
