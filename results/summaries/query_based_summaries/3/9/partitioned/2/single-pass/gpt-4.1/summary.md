## Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was a prominent American philosopher renowned for his pioneering work on defeasible reasoning, epistemology, and artificial intelligence. After earning a triple-major bachelor's degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota and completing his Ph.D. at UC Berkeley, he held faculty positions at SUNY Buffalo, University of Rochester, University of Michigan, and spent most of his career at the University of Arizona, where he helped found the Cognitive Science Program.

Pollock made foundational contributions to epistemology in his book *Knowledge and Justification* by introducing epistemic norms governed by defeasible reasoning, which admit exceptions and move beyond strict analytic criteria for knowledge. His distinction between "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat" became central in the study of defeasible reasoning. Pollock extended these concepts into artificial intelligence through his creation of OSCAR, a LISP-based "artilect" that implemented interest-driven defeasible reasoning and other cognitive architectures. His extensive published work covers topics in probability, induction, moral reasoning, decision theory, and the logical foundations of cognition, notably *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction*. Through his clear commitment to both philosophical rigor and practical implementation, Pollock ushered defeasible reasoning from philosophical obscurity to mainstream acceptance and left a lasting impact on philosophy and AI.
