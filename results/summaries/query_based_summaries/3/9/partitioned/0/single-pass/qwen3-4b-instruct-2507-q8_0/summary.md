John L. Pollock was a philosopher known for his work on defeasible reasoning, a form of non-monotonic logic that allows for the possibility of revising beliefs in light of new information. His influential book *Knowledge and Justification* established him as a key figure in epistemology, introducing the idea that epistemic norms are governed by defeasible reasoning—ceteris paribus conditions that can admit exceptions. Pollock's ideas were shaped by thinkers like Ludwig Wittgenstein and Roderick Chisholm, and he developed a framework that emphasized the role of context and practical reasoning in knowledge and justification.

Later in his career, Pollock developed the software project OSCAR, an artificial intelligence prototype based on defeasible reasoning, which embodied his theories on intention, interest, and problem-solving. His work on nomic probability, explored in *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction*, further expanded his contributions to epistemology by examining how probabilities can be derived from observed frequencies.

Pollock's philosophical legacy is marked by his clarity, early influence, and significant impact on both artificial intelligence and epistemology, earning him the moniker "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" among philosophers.
