John Leslie Pollock (1940–2017) was a prominent American philosopher and epistemologist born in Atchison, Kansas, who earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. He completed his doctoral dissertation at the University of California, Berkeley in 1965, supervised by Ernest Adams, which established his intellectual lineage from Gottfried Leibniz and Immanuel Kant through Ernest Nagel and Patrick Suppes. Pollock held faculty positions at SUNY Buffalo, the University of Rochester, the University of Michigan, and the University of Arizona, where he spent the majority of his career and helped found the Cognitive Science Program. He was also an avid mountain biker who founded a riding club in Southern Arizona.

Pollock is best known for his seminal work in epistemology, particularly the book *Knowledge and Justification* (1974), which shifted the focus of American epistemology away from seeking necessary and sufficient conditions for knowledge toward analyzing defeasible reasoning. This approach introduced concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," which became central to the field and influenced subsequent epistemologists like Ernest Sosa, Roderick Chisholm, and Keith Lehrer. Beyond epistemology, Pollock made significant contributions to the philosophy of artificial intelligence and rationality. He developed the OSCAR project, an artificial intelligence prototype based on LISP that implemented his theories of defeasible reasoning, interests, and strategic problem-solving. His extensive bibliography includes numerous influential papers and books on topics ranging from defeasible reasoning and nomic probability to the logical foundations of goal-regression planning and the theory of direct inference.
