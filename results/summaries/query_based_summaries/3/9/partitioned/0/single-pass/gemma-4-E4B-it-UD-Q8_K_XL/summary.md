John L. Pollock was born John Leslie Pollock on January 28, 1940, in Atchison, Kansas. He received a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy from the University of Minnesota in 1961. His doctoral dissertation from the University of California, Berkeley, advised by Ernest Adams, included an appendix on defeasible reasoning, which became central to his philosophical contributions.

Pollock held faculty positions at several institutions, including the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program. His book, *Knowledge and Justification*, established him in epistemology. He is particularly known as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" for his work in this area, which includes the concepts of "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat." Later in his career, he devoted time to the OSCAR software project, which was an implementation of his ideas on defeasible reasoning and cognitive architectural design.
