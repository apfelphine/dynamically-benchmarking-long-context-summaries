## Summary of John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2009) was a renowned philosopher known for his foundational work in epistemology and artificial intelligence. He earned a triple-major degree in physics, mathematics, and philosophy at the University of Minnesota, and completed his PhD at UC Berkeley. Pollock held academic positions at SUNY Buffalo, University of Rochester, University of Michigan, and spent most of his career at the University of Arizona, where he helped establish the Cognitive Science Program. His influential book *Knowledge and Justification* introduced defeasible reasoning—an approach allowing for epistemic norms that admit exceptions—into mainstream philosophical discourse, markedly impacting philosophical conceptions of knowledge and justification.

Pollock was widely recognized as "Mr. Defeasible Reasoning" and his work bridged philosophy and artificial intelligence, especially non-monotonic reasoning. He formulated crucial concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat" which became standards in the literature. Later, Pollock focused on implementing his theories in AI projects, notably in the creation of OSCAR, a LISP-based cognitive architecture designed as an "artilect" that could reason defeasibly about perception, change, causation, and planning. His book *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* explored the connection between defeasible reasoning and statistical inference. Pollock authored numerous books and papers that remain influential in philosophy, logic, epistemology, and artificial intelligence.
