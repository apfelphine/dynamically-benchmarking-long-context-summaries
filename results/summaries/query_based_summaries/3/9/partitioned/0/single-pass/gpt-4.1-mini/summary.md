# Summary: John L. Pollock

John L. Pollock (1940–2012) was a prominent philosopher known for his foundational work on defeasible reasoning—the study of reasoning processes that can be revised or defeated by new evidence. His 1987 paper "Defeasible reasoning" and his book *Knowledge and Justification* were seminal in bringing defeasible reasoning into mainstream epistemology and artificial intelligence. Pollock introduced key concepts such as "undercutting defeat" and "rebutting defeat," which remain central to the field. Although his ideas influenced AI and legal reasoning, Pollock’s primary interest lay in philosophical rather than jurisprudential aspects of defeasibility.

Later in his career, Pollock developed OSCAR, an AI software prototype embodying his theories of defeasible reasoning as well as novel ideas on intentions and problem-solving strategies. OSCAR aimed to model rational human cognition efficiently by focusing on "interest-based" reasoning. Pollock authored numerous influential philosophical works spanning epistemology, logic, probability, and AI, including *Nomic Probability and the Foundations of Induction* (1990), which expands on statistical inference within a defeasible reasoning framework. His contributions significantly shaped modern understandings of reasoning under uncertainty and the architecture of rational agents.
