The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network designed to address training instability and vanishing gradient issues inherent in the original GAN framework. It redefines the discriminator's strategy set to include only measurable functions with a bounded Lipschitz norm ($|D|_L \leq K$) and modifies the objective function to approximate the Wasserstein distance, specifically $L_{WGAN}(\mu_G, D) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu_G}[D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu_{ref}}[D(x)]$. This modification ensures that the discriminator provides a more stable and informative gradient signal to the generator, preventing the "vanishing gradient" problem that occurs when the discriminator becomes too good at distinguishing real from fake data.

To enforce the Lipschitz constraint without requiring computationally expensive manual weight clipping, WGAN employs three primary techniques: weight clipping, spectral normalization, and gradient penalty. Weight clipping restricts the magnitude of weights within the discriminator to bound the Lipschitz norm. Spectral normalization normalizes the spectral radius of the weight matrices in the discriminator's layers after each update to ensure the Lipschitz constant remains bounded. Alternatively, the gradient penalty method adds a regularization term to the loss function that penalizes deviations of the gradient norm from a specific constant, effectively smoothing the discriminator's decision boundary. These improvements allow WGAN to achieve stable convergence and effectively solve the mode collapse problem often seen in standard GANs.
