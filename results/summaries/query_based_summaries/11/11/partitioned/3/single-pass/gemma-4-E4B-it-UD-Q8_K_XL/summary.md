Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) modifies the standard GAN game by utilizing the Wasserstein metric, which satisfies the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality. This duality establishes that for any fixed generator strategy $\mu_G$, the optimal discriminator $D^*$ maximizes the objective function $L_{WGAN}(\mu_G, D) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mu_G}[D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mu_{ref}}[D(x)]$, and this maximum value equals $K \cdot W_1(\mu_G, \mu_{ref})$, where $W_1$ is the Wasserstein distance and $K$ is a fixed positive constant. Consequently, a good discriminator pushes the generator to minimize the Wasserstein distance between its distribution and the reference distribution, with the optimal generator strategy being $\mu_G = \mu_{ref}$.

Since the discriminator in WGAN is restricted to having a bounded Lipschitz norm ($\|D\|_L \leq K$), methods exist to enforce this constraint. These methods include upper-bounding the Lipschitz norm, which can involve weight clipping, spectral normalization, or employing a gradient penalty term. The use of the Wasserstein distance helps solve the vanishing gradient problem often encountered in standard GANs, especially when the generator distribution is disjoint from the reference distribution.
