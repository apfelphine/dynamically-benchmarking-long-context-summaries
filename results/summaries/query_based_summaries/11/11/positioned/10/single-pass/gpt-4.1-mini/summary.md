### Summary of Wasserstein GAN

Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the original Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework that replaces the Jensen–Shannon divergence used in GANs with the Wasserstein distance (Earth Mover's distance) as a metric for measuring how close the generator's distribution is to the real data distribution. This change is motivated by the fact that the Wasserstein distance provides a more meaningful and smooth gradient when the real and generated data distributions lie on low-dimensional manifolds that do not overlap, addressing problems such as mode collapse and vanishing gradients encountered in standard GANs.

In WGAN, the discriminator—called the "critic"—is no longer restricted to output probabilities but is instead any Lipschitz-continuous real-valued function with bounded Lipschitz norm. The objective function for WGAN is defined as the difference between the expected critic scores over generated data and real data. Leveraging the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, the optimization becomes a minimax game where the generator tries to minimize and the critic tries to maximize this Wasserstein-based objective. Training involves enforcing the Lipschitz constraint on the critic, which can be done by weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalty techniques. This leads to more stable training and better gradient feedback for the generator, improving convergence and generation quality.
