## Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the original generative adversarial network (GAN) framework, designed to improve training stability and address common issues such as vanishing gradients and mode collapse. In the traditional GAN setup, the discriminator's objective effectively estimates the Jensen–Shannon divergence between the reference distribution and the generated distribution, which can lead to degenerate gradients when these distributions are disjoint. WGAN replaces this with the Wasserstein (or Earth-Mover) distance, leveraging the Kantorovich–Rubenstein duality theorem: the discriminator (or "critic") is now constrained to be a K-Lipschitz function, and the training objective becomes the expectation difference between the critic's output on real and generated data. The generator minimizes, and the critic maximizes:  
${\displaystyle L_{WGAN}(\mu_G, D) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_G}\left[D(x)\right] - \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{\text{ref}}}\left[D(x)\right]}$.

Training WGANs involves special techniques to enforce the Lipschitz constraint on the critic, such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, or a gradient penalty term. These changes ensure that the critic provides a meaningful, smooth gradient for the generator even when the generated and real data distributions have limited overlap, leading to more stable and reliable GAN training. The WGAN formulation also enables improved empirical convergence and more interpretable monitoring of generator progress during training.
