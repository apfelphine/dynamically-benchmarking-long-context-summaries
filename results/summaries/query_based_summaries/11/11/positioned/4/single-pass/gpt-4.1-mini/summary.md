**Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)** is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network framework that modifies the original GAN game by replacing the Jensen-Shannon divergence with the Wasserstein distance (earth mover's distance) as the measure of difference between the generated distribution and the real data distribution. This change leads to improved training stability and alleviates issues such as mode collapse and vanishing gradients seen in traditional GANs.

Formally, WGAN defines the discriminator (called the "critic") as a function with bounded Lipschitz norm, and the objective function becomes the difference between the expected critic outputs on generated and real data samples. By Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, optimizing this objective corresponds to minimizing the Wasserstein-1 distance between the generated and real data distributions. Unlike the original GAN discriminator outputting probabilities, the WGAN critic outputs real values, providing smoother gradients for the generator. Training WGANs involves enforcing the Lipschitz constraint on the critic, typically through methods like weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalty. These constraints ensure the critic remains within the set of 1-Lipschitz functions, crucial for the dual formulation. Overall, WGAN improves convergence and sample diversity by providing better gradient signals to the generator compared to the traditional GAN framework.
