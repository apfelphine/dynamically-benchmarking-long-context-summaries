# Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) designed to address training instability issues inherent in standard GANs, such as mode collapse and vanishing gradients. Unlike the original GAN, which relies on the Jensen-Shannon divergence and a discriminator outputting probabilities between 0 and 1, WGAN restricts the discriminator's strategy set to measurable functions with a bounded Lipschitz norm. This constraint ensures that the objective function, based on the Wasserstein metric (Earth Mover's distance), provides a stable gradient signal even when the generated and reference distributions are disjoint. The discriminator is often referred to as a "critic," and its optimization requires specific techniques like weight clipping or spectral normalization to enforce the Lipschitz constraint without altering the gradient magnitude unpredictably.

Training a WGAN involves a zero-sum game where the generator aims to minimize the difference between its output distribution and the reference distribution, while the critic maximizes this difference. Theoretically, this setup guarantees that the discriminator's optimal strategy computes the Wasserstein distance, providing a reliable measure of how far the generator is from the true data distribution. By avoiding the vanishing gradient problem common in standard GANs where the discriminator becomes too good, WGANs offer smoother convergence and more stable training dynamics. Variants like WGAN-GP (Gradient Penalty) further refine the Lipschitz constraint by adding a penalty term to the loss function, ensuring the critic's gradients remain bounded throughout the training process.
