## Summary: Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of Generative Adversarial Network that addresses key limitations of the original GAN framework, particularly issues related to unstable training and vanishing gradients. Instead of using the Jensen–Shannon divergence to measure the difference between the generated and real data distributions, WGAN utilizes the Wasserstein metric (Earth Mover's distance), leveraging the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality. In WGAN, the discriminator (often called the "critic") is constrained to be a K-Lipschitz function, and the game objective is reformulated as: ${\displaystyle L_{WGAN}(\mu _{G},D):=\mathbb {E} _{x\sim \mu _{G}}[D(x)]-\mathbb {E} _{x\sim \mu _{ref}}[D(x)]}$, where the generator tries to minimize and the critic tries to maximize this objective.

The architecture and training of WGAN differ from standard GANs in that the critic's Lipschitz constraint is enforced through various methods, such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalty. These techniques ensure stable training and provide a more useful gradient for the generator, thereby mitigating problems like mode collapse and vanishing gradients observed in classical GANs. The WGAN objective guarantees that the generator is consistently pushed to minimize the Wasserstein distance between its distribution and the reference distribution, leading to improved convergence and higher-quality generative results.
