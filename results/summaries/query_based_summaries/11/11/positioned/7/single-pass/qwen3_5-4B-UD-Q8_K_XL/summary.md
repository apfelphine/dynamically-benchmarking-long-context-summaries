# Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) designed to address training instability and mode collapse issues inherent in standard GANs. Unlike traditional GANs, which optimize the Jensen–Shannon divergence, WGAN utilizes the Wasserstein metric (specifically the 1-Wasserstein distance) as its objective function. This metric is defined by the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality theorem, which allows for a more stable and meaningful distance calculation between probability distributions, even when they are disjoint.

To enforce the mathematical requirements of the Wasserstein distance, WGAN restricts the discriminator's strategy set to include only measurable functions with a bounded Lipschitz norm. This constraint is typically achieved through three primary methods: weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalty. By ensuring the discriminator's Lipschitz constant is bounded, WGAN provides smoother gradients for the generator, enabling more stable training and better sample quality compared to standard GANs, without requiring complex techniques like curriculum learning or two-time-scale update rules.
