**Summary on Wasserstein GAN**

Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the original Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) that addresses training instability and mode collapse by replacing the Jensen–Shannon divergence with the Wasserstein-1 (Earth-Mover) distance as a measure of discrepancy between the real data distribution and the generator distribution. Leveraging the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, the discriminator (also called the critic) is constrained to be a K-Lipschitz function, enabling an objective function where the generator minimizes the Wasserstein distance between distributions, and the critic maximizes it. This leads to smoother gradients and more stable training dynamics compared to the original GAN formulation, where the discriminator provides gradients based on the Jensen–Shannon divergence that can vanish when the distributions have non-overlapping supports.

Training WGANs involves enforcing the Lipschitz constraint on the discriminator via methods such as weight clipping (restricting the discriminator's weights to a fixed interval), spectral normalization (rescaling weights to have bounded spectral norm), or adding gradient penalty terms that encourage gradients to have norm close to a target value. These techniques keep the discriminator within the space of Lipschitz functions required by the theory.

Compared to standard GANs, WGAN provides better gradient feedback for the generator, reducing issues like mode collapse and vanishing gradients. It has been empirically observed that WGANs do not show mode collapse in experiments reported by the authors. Furthermore, WGAN formulations have inspired many subsequent GAN variants with alternative loss functions and regularizations.
