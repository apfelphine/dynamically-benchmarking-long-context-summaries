**Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)** is a variant of the original Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) designed to improve training stability and address problems like mode collapse and vanishing gradients inherent in traditional GANs. It modifies the classical GAN framework by redefining the discriminator (called the "critic" in WGAN) to be a Lipschitz-continuous function with a bounded Lipschitz norm, rather than a probabilistic classifier outputting values in [0,1].

WGAN utilizes the Wasserstein-1 (Earth Mover’s) distance as a measure of divergence between the generated distribution and the true data distribution. By the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, the WGAN objective is formulated as maximizing the difference of expected critic scores between generated samples and real samples, constrained by the critic’s Lipschitz continuity. This formulation yields more meaningful gradients during training, enabling the generator to receive better feedback even when the data and model distributions have non-overlapping supports—an issue where classical GANs often fail due to infinite Jensen-Shannon divergence.

Training WGAN involves minimizing the Wasserstein distance by alternating between optimizing the generator and the critic under the Lipschitz constraint. Practical methods to enforce this constraint include weight clipping, spectral normalization of network weights, and gradient penalty terms. These methods ensure the critic’s function remains within the required space, which is critical for the theoretical guarantees and empirical success of WGAN. Experiments show that WGAN alleviates mode collapse and produces more stable training dynamics compared to original GANs.
