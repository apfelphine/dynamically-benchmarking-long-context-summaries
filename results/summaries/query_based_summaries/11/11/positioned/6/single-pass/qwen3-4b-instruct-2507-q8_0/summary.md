The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) that addresses key issues in training stability and gradient quality. Unlike the original GAN, which uses a Jensen-Shannon divergence as a loss function, WGAN employs the Wasserstein distance, a metric that provides a more meaningful and continuous measure of the difference between the generator's distribution and the real data distribution. This change results in a more stable training process, as the gradient provided by the discriminator is less likely to vanish or explode, especially when the generator distribution is far from the real data distribution. The WGAN objective function is defined as the difference in expected values of a Lipschitz-continuous critic (discriminator) between the generated and real data distributions. To ensure the critic's Lipschitz constraint, WGAN uses techniques such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalty, which help maintain the stability of the training dynamics. This makes WGAN more robust and effective in generating high-quality samples, particularly in complex domains like image generation.
