## Summary: Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the original GAN framework that replaces the traditional objective based on the Jensen–Shannon divergence with the Wasserstein distance (also known as Earth Mover's distance) to measure the difference between the generator's and the reference (real data) distributions. In WGAN, the discriminator (often called the "critic") is defined as a measurable function with bounded Lipschitz norm, rather than outputting a probability. The objective becomes maximizing the difference in expected critic scores between generated and real data samples, specifically:  
${\displaystyle L_{WGAN}(\mu _{G},D) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{G}} [D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{ref}} [D(x)]}$,  
where the generator minimizes and the critic maximizes this objective, subject to the critic being $K$-Lipschitz.

WGAN provides several advantages over the original GAN, including more stable training and a meaningful loss metric that correlates with sample quality. In practice, enforcing the Lipschitz constraint on the critic is accomplished by techniques such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, or adding a gradient penalty term. These methods ensure the critic satisfies the necessary conditions for the dual representation of the Wasserstein distance. Empirically, WGAN is less susceptible to problems like mode collapse and vanishing gradients, leading to improved convergence and generative performance. The discriminator continually provides a useful gradient to the generator, improving sample quality even when distributions are disjoint—a frequent scenario in high-dimensional spaces such as image generation.
