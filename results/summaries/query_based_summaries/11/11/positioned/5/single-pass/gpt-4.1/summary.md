## Wasserstein GAN

The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) modifies the original GAN framework by using the Wasserstein metric (also known as the Earth Mover's Distance) as its measure of divergence between the generated distribution and the true data distribution, rather than the Jensen–Shannon divergence employed in traditional GANs. The game is formalized with the generator producing samples and the discriminator (termed "critic" in WGAN) restricted to functions with a bounded Lipschitz norm. The dual representation theorem (Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality) enables efficient computation: the WGAN's objective is to maximize the critic's ability to distinguish real from generated data while enforcing the Lipschitz constraint, yielding a measure proportional to the Wasserstein distance between distributions.

WGAN provides better gradients for training, avoiding issues like mode collapse and vanishing gradients present in standard GANs, especially when data distributions lie on low-dimensional manifolds. The discriminator in WGAN is trained using methods such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, or gradient penalties to enforce the Lipschitz condition. This improvement results in more stable convergence and mitigates common GAN failure modes, with the generator being consistently pushed to minimize the Wasserstein distance, effectively aligning generated and real data distributions.
