The **Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)** modifies the original GAN framework by introducing two key changes:

1. **Discriminator Strategy Set**: The discriminator is restricted to measurable functions with a bounded Lipschitz norm, denoted as ${\displaystyle \|D\|_{L}\leq K}$ , where $K$ is a fixed positive constant. This ensures the discriminator operates within a constrained space, preventing arbitrary behavior.

2. **Objective Function**: The loss function is redefined as ${\displaystyle L_{WGAN}(\mu _{G},D):=\mathbb{E} _{x\sim \mu _{G}}[D(x)]-\mathbb{E} _{x\sim \mu _{\text{ref}}}[D(x)]}$ . This objective focuses on minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the generator distribution $\mu_G$ and the reference distribution $\mu_{\text{ref}}$, providing a more stable and interpretable gradient signal compared to the original GAN's Jensen–Shannon divergence.

The Wasserstein GAN aims to address issues like mode collapse and unstable convergence by leveraging the Wasserstein metric, which is more robust and differentiable under certain conditions. The discriminator's bounded Lipschitz norm ensures that its gradients are well-behaved, leading to more stable training dynamics. This approach allows the generator to learn representations that are closer to the true data distribution, enhancing the model's ability to generate realistic samples.
