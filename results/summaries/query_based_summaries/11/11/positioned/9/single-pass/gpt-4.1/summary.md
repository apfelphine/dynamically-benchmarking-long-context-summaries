## Wasserstein GAN Summary

Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a type of generative adversarial network (GAN) that replaces the original GAN's training objective, which is based on Jensen–Shannon divergence, with the Wasserstein distance (also known as earth mover's distance). In WGAN, the generator aims to minimize the Wasserstein distance between the generator's distribution and the reference (real data) distribution, while the discriminator (also called the "critic" in this context) provides feedback by outputting a function constrained by the Lipschitz norm. The objective function for WGAN is:  
**L<sub>WGAN</sub>(μ<sub>G</sub>, D) = E<sub>x∼μ<sub>G</sub></sub>[D(x)] − E<sub>x∼μ<sub>ref</sub></sub>[D(x)]**,  
with the optimal discriminator corresponding to the Wasserstein distance between distributions. To efficiently enforce the Lipschitz constraint on the discriminator, practical methods such as weight clipping, spectral normalization, and gradient penalty are used.

A major advantage of WGAN over traditional GANs is improved gradient quality for the generator, leading to more stable training and addressing issues like vanishing gradients and mode collapse. Unlike GANs, where the discriminator may become too powerful and provide no useful gradient when the data and generator distributions are far apart, WGAN maintains informative feedback throughout training. Empirically, WGAN has demonstrated better convergence properties, improved sample diversity, and reduced risk of mode collapse. The architecture has influenced numerous subsequent GAN improvements and is widely used in various generative modeling tasks where stable training and sample quality are priorities.
