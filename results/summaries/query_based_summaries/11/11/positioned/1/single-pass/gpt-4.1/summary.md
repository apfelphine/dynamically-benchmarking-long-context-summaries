## Wasserstein GAN

Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of generative adversarial networks that replaces the classic GAN's reliance on the Jensen–Shannon divergence with the Wasserstein metric (Earth Mover's Distance) to measure the distance between the generator's output distribution and the real data distribution. By the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, the discriminator (critic) in WGAN is constrained to be a real-valued, K-Lipschitz function, and the objective function becomes the expectation of the critic's output on generated samples minus that on real samples. The generator is trained to minimize this objective, while the discriminator maximizes it. The optimal discriminator thus provides a learning signal proportional to the Wasserstein distance between distributions, offering more informative gradients especially in high-dimensional data spaces where real and generated distributions are often disjoint.

For training WGANs, it's crucial to enforce the Lipschitz constraint on the discriminator to ensure valid Wasserstein distance estimates. Methods include weight clipping (restricting discriminator weights to a fixed range), spectral normalization (dividing weights by their spectral norm), and gradient penalty (adding a regularization term penalizing deviations from the desired gradient norm). Compared to standard GANs, WGANs provide improved training stability and alleviate issues such as vanishing gradients and mode collapse, which occur when the discriminator becomes too strong and offers little feedback to the generator. Empirically, WGANs avoid mode collapse, producing more stable and diverse results in generative modeling tasks.
