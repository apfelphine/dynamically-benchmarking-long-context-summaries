Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) that replaces the standard objective function with the Wasserstein distance (also known as the Earth Mover's distance) to measure the divergence between the generated and reference distributions. This modification addresses critical issues in traditional GANs, such as mode collapse, vanishing gradients, and unstable training, particularly when the distributions have disjoint supports. In the WGAN framework, the discriminator is redefined as a "critic" with a bounded Lipschitz norm ($\|D\|_L \leq K$), ensuring the gradient estimates remain stable. To enforce this constraint during training, several methods are employed: weight clipping limits the magnitude of the critic's weights, spectral normalization dynamically adjusts the weights to maintain a Lipschitz constant of one, and gradient penalty adds a regularization term to the loss function to penalize the critic for violating the Lipschitz condition. By optimizing the critic, the generator is pushed to minimize the Wasserstein distance, effectively learning to match the reference distribution without the common pitfalls associated with the Jensen-Shannon divergence used in standard GANs.
