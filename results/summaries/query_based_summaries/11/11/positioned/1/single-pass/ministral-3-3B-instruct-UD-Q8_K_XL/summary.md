The **Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)** is a variant of the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework that addresses key limitations of traditional GANs, particularly mode collapse and unstable training. Unlike standard GANs, which rely on the Jensen-Shannon divergence to measure the distance between distributions, WGAN uses the **Wasserstein distance**, a more robust metric that avoids the issue of infinite divergence when the generator's distribution is far from the target distribution.

In the WGAN game, the generator aims to minimize the **Wasserstein distance** between its generated distribution and the reference distribution, while the discriminator (critic) maximizes this distance by constraining its Lipschitz norm. This constraint ensures that the discriminator provides a meaningful gradient signal to the generator, preventing mode collapse and improving training stability. The Wasserstein distance is computed efficiently using the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, allowing for gradient-based optimization.

Training WGANs involves bounding the Lipschitz norm of the discriminator through methods like **weight clipping**, **spectral normalization**, or **gradient penalty**, ensuring the discriminator remains a valid critic. This approach enables WGANs to generate high-quality samples without the vanishing gradient issues seen in traditional GANs.
