The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) modifies the original GAN framework by replacing the discriminator's role with a critic function constrained to have a bounded Lipschitz norm, specifically aiming to minimize the Wasserstein-1 (Earth Mover’s) distance between the generated distribution and the real data distribution. By leveraging the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, WGAN formulates the GAN game as a zero-sum game where the generator minimizes and the critic maximizes the difference in expected values of this Lipschitz-bounded function under the generated and real data distributions. This provides a meaningful continuous metric for training, overcoming issues like vanishing gradients and mode collapse commonly encountered in classic GANs that utilize Jensen-Shannon divergence.

Training WGANs involves special techniques to enforce the Lipschitz constraint on the critic, such as weight clipping, spectral normalization (which normalizes the spectral norm of weight matrices), and gradient penalty methods that penalize the norm of the gradient of the critic's output with respect to its input. These ensure the critic’s function space remains within the required Lipschitz bounds. Compared to original GANs, the WGAN critic offers more stable and informative gradient signals for the generator, because the Wasserstein distance varies smoothly even when the supports of real and generated distributions do not overlap, leading to improved training stability and sample diversity. The original WGAN paper reported no evidence of mode collapse in experiments.
