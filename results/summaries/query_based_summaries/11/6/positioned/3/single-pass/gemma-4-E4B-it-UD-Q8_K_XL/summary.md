Autoencoders are defined by two components: an encoder family $\text{E}_{\phi}:{\mathcal {X}}\rightarrow {\mathcal {Z}}$ and a decoder family $\text{D}_{\theta}:{\mathcal {Z}}\rightarrow {\mathcal {X}}$, where $\mathcal{X}$ is the space of input messages and $\mathcal{Z}$ is the space of decoded messages. Typically, these are multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). The objective of training an autoencoder is to minimize a loss function, $L(\theta ,\phi )=\mathbb {\mathbb {E} } _{x\sim \mu _{ref}}[d(x,D_{\theta }(E_{\phi }(x)))]$, where $d(x,x')$ measures the reconstruction quality.

Autoencoders can be **undercomplete** ($\dim(\mathcal{Z}) < \dim(\mathcal{X})$), allowing them to compress the message, or **overcomplete** ($\dim(\mathcal{Z}) \ge \dim(\mathcal{X})$). Variations include **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**, which use a variational Bayesian approach and optimize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO); **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**, which enforce sparsity in the latent codes; and **Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs)**, which are trained by intentionally corrupting the inputs. Advanced variations include the **Minimum Description Length Autoencoder (MDL-AE)**.
