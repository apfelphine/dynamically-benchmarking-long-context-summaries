**Autoencoder Summary**

An Autoencoder is a generative model composed of two main functions: an **encoder** ($E_{\phi}: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Z}$) and a **decoder** ($D_{\theta}: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{X}$), where $\mathcal{X}$ is the space of input messages and $\mathcal{Z}$ is the space of encoded/latent messages. Typically, both are implemented as Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs). The objective of training is to minimize a loss function, $L(\theta, \phi) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu_{\text{ref}}}[d(x, D_{\theta}(E_{\phi}(x)))]$, where $d$ is the reconstruction quality function, and $\mu_{\text{ref}}$ is the reference distribution.

The goal of an optimal autoencoder is near-perfect reconstruction; to prevent trivial duplication, the code space ($\mathcal{Z}$) is usually smaller than the message space ($\mathcal{X}$), resulting in an **undercomplete** autoencoder that compresses the message. In the ideal case, an undercomplete autoencoder can generate messages indistinguishable from real messages by feeding its decoder arbitrary codes $z \in \mathcal{Z}$. Variations include the **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**, which models the latent space as a mixture of distributions, and the **Sparse Autoencoder (SAE)**, which enforces sparsity in the latent codes.
