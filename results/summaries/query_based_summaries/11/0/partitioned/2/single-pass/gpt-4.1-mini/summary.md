## Summary: Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)

A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a zero-sum game involving two players: a generator and a discriminator. The generator aims to produce samples matching the reference data distribution, while the discriminator seeks to distinguish real data from generated samples. Mathematically, the discriminator outputs a value between 0 and 1 indicating the likelihood that input data is real, and the game objective involves maximizing discriminator accuracy and minimizing generator error. The optimal discriminator computes the Jensen–Shannon divergence between the real and generated distributions, and the GAN equilibrium is reached when the generator replicates the real distribution perfectly and the discriminator outputs 0.5 for all inputs.

Practically, the generator maps samples from a latent distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) through a neural network to data space, while the discriminator also uses a neural network to classify inputs. Training proceeds via alternating updates, but convergence can be unstable and mode collapse—a failure to capture data diversity—is common. Several GAN variants have been developed to address these issues, including conditional GANs (which condition generation on class labels), GANs with alternative architectures (e.g., convolutional, transformer-based), and GANs with alternative loss objectives (e.g., Wasserstein GAN). Invertible data augmentation methods have been employed to enhance training data, notably in StyleGAN series architectures that progressively grow the generator and discriminator to produce high-resolution images with improved quality and stability. GANs have diverse applications spanning scientific simulation, medical imaging, art generation, and malicious image synthesis, illustrating both their power and potential ethical concerns.
