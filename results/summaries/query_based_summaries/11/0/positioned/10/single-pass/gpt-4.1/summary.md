## Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) Summary

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are a class of generative models defined as a two-player zero-sum game between a generator and a discriminator. Mathematically, the generator seeks to produce samples that match a reference data distribution, while the discriminator aims to distinguish between true data and generated samples. The standard GAN objective is formulated as:  
${\displaystyle L(\mu _{G},D):=\operatorname {E} _{x\sim \mu _{\text{ref}}}[\ln D(x)]+\operatorname {E} _{z\sim \mu _{Z}}[\ln(1-D(G(z)))]}$,  
where $\mu _{G}$ is the generated distribution, $\mu _{\text{ref}}$ is the reference, and $D$ is the discriminator function. GANs are usually implicit generative models and can be implemented with various neural network architectures; commonly, the generator is a deconvolutional neural network and the discriminator a convolutional neural network.

Training GANs involves iterative, independent updates of the generator and discriminator, each guided by their performance in the adversarial game. This can yield unstable dynamics, including mode collapse and vanishing gradients, which motivated improvements like Wasserstein GANs (WGANs). GAN variants include conditional GANs (cGANs), adversarial autoencoders, InfoGANs, CycleGANs, and architectures such as StyleGAN. GANs have seen wide-ranging applications in fields such as science, medicine, art, and computer vision, including image generation, data synthesis, super-resolution, and transfer learning. Evaluation is often performed via metrics like Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). GANs have significantly advanced generative modeling and synthetic data generation across domains.
