A **Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)** is a type of machine learning model defined by a game between two neural networks: a **generator** and a **discriminator**. The generator creates synthetic data samples, aiming to mimic real data, while the discriminator evaluates these samples, trying to distinguish between real and generated data. The generator's objective is to minimize the discriminator's ability to detect fake data, and the discriminator's objective is to maximize its ability to identify fakes. This adversarial process drives both networks to improve iteratively.

Mathematically, the GAN is formulated as a zero-sum game where the generator aims to minimize and the discriminator aims to maximize a loss function involving the log-likelihood of real and generated data. The game converges to a unique equilibrium where the generator perfectly mimics the real data distribution and the discriminator outputs a constant value of 0.5 for all inputs.

In practice, the generator is typically a deconvolutional neural network, and the discriminator is a convolutional neural network. Training involves alternating updates: the discriminator is trained on real and generated samples, and the generator is trained based on the discriminator's feedback. Challenges include **mode collapse** (the generator producing only a limited variety of outputs) and **vanishing gradients** (the generator fails to learn due to a lack of gradient signal). Techniques like the **two-time-scale update rule** and **Wasserstein GAN** have been developed to improve stability.

GANs have diverse applications, including image generation, style transfer, medical imaging (e.g., synthetic MRI generation), scientific simulations (e.g., reconstructing astronomical images), and art (e.g., generating the painting "Edmond de Belamy"). They are also used in scientific research for modeling complex systems and in malicious applications for creating deepfakes. Variants such as **conditional GANs**, **Wasserstein GANs**, and **StyleGAN** extend the framework to handle specific tasks like conditional generation or high-resolution image synthesis.
