Generative adversarial network (GAN) is a zero-sum game involving two players: a generator ($\mu_G$) and a discriminator ($\mu_D$). The generator aims to minimize the objective function by approaching the reference data distribution ($\mu_{\text{ref}}$), while the discriminator aims to maximize the objective. The generator's objective is to produce candidates that the discriminator cannot distinguish from real data, and the discriminator's goal is to correctly identify these synthetic candidates.

In practice, the generator maps from an easily sampled latent space (e.g., multivariate normal distribution) to the true data distribution, while the discriminator evaluates these candidates. Training involves independent backpropagation for both networks: the generator improves by fooling the discriminator, and the discriminator becomes better at flagging synthetic samples. GANs are implicit generative models, meaning they do not explicitly model the likelihood function. Variants include Conditional GANs (allowing conditional generation based on labels) and Wasserstein GANs (WGANs), which use the Wasserstein metric and bounded Lipschitz norms for the discriminator to stabilize training and solve mode collapse.
