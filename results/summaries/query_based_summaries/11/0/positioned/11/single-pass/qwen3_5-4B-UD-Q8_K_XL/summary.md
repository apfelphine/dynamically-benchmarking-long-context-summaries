### Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) Summary

A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a deep learning framework consisting of two competing neural networks: a generator and a discriminator. The generator aims to learn a probability distribution by creating synthetic data samples from a latent space, while the discriminator attempts to distinguish between real data from a reference distribution and the synthetic samples produced by the generator. This setup forms a zero-sum game where the generator's objective is to minimize the discriminator's ability to detect fakes, and the discriminator's objective is to maximize its detection accuracy. Theoretically, the game converges to a Nash equilibrium where the generator perfectly mimics the reference distribution, and the discriminator outputs a constant probability of 0.5, indicating it can no longer distinguish between real and generated data.

To address practical training challenges such as vanishing gradients and mode collapse, several variants and training strategies have been developed. The Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) modifies the objective function using the Wasserstein distance and constrains the discriminator's Lipschitz norm to ensure stable gradient flow. Other variations include Conditional GANs (cGANs) for generating specific classes, InfoGANs for discovering interpretable latent factors, and CycleGANs for translating between unpaired image domains. The architecture has evolved to include Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs) and architectures like StyleGAN, which utilize progressive growth and style-based latent spaces to generate high-resolution, photorealistic images. GANs are widely applied in fields such as medical imaging, scientific simulation, and art generation, though they face ethical considerations regarding the creation of synthetic media.
