## Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) Summary

A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a framework for training generative models, defined as a two-player zero-sum game between a generator and a discriminator. The generator produces samples intended to match a reference data distribution, while the discriminator attempts to distinguish real samples from those produced by the generator. Mathematically, the generator aims to minimize, and the discriminator to maximize, an objective function based on the likelihood of sample origins, ultimately driving the generator's output distribution to mimic the reference. The optimal discriminator computes the likelihood ratio between the reference and the generator distributions and, at equilibrium, the generator reproduces the reference distribution with the discriminator unable to distinguish real from fake samples.

In practice, GANs are typically implemented using neural networks (often convolutional or deconvolutional). Training is complex, often suffering from issues like mode collapse and vanishing gradients, leading to unstable convergence. Variants such as Wasserstein GAN, conditional GANs, CycleGAN, StyleGAN, and others aim to address these challenges and expand GAN functionality, including image translation, style transfer, multimodal generation, or improved training stability. GANs are widely used in diverse applications, from scientific modeling and medical imaging to art and image synthesis, but also raise privacy and misuse concerns due to their powerful synthetic capabilities. GAN performance is commonly evaluated with metrics like Inception Score and Fréchet Inception Distance.
