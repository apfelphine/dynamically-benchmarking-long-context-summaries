# Concise Summary: Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are defined as a zero-sum game between two players: a generator and a discriminator. The generator aims to produce a probability distribution closely matching the reference data distribution, while the discriminator tries to distinguish real data from generated data by outputting values close to 1 for real and 0 for generated samples. Mathematically, the GAN objective function involves minimizing (generator) and maximizing (discriminator) the expected log-likelihoods of these assignments. Practically, the generator maps noise vectors sampled from a latent space (e.g., Gaussian) through a neural network (often deconvolutional for images) to generate candidates, which the discriminator (often convolutional) evaluates. Both networks are trained via backpropagation using labeled real and synthetic samples.

GANs are implicit generative models that do not explicitly define likelihoods or inverse mappings, distinguishing them from methods like flow-based models or autoregressive networks. Theoretical results show the unique equilibrium exists where the generator perfectly mimics the data distribution and the discriminator outputs 0.5 uniformly. GAN training is challenging due to issues like mode collapse (generator producing limited types of outputs) and unstable convergence, often mitigated by techniques such as two time-scale update rules or Wasserstein GAN variants. Evaluation metrics commonly include Inception Score and Fréchet Inception Distance. Numerous GAN variants extend or modify the original framework, including conditional GANs, Wasserstein GANs (which use the Wasserstein metric as the loss to provide better gradients and mitigate mode collapse), multi-player GANs like InfoGAN and Bidirectional GAN, and architectures like CycleGAN for unpaired image-to-image translation. Advanced large-scale implementations include BigGAN and the StyleGAN series, which incorporate progressive growing, style mixing, and adaptive data augmentation. GANs are widely applied in domains such as medical imaging, scientific simulation, art, and synthetic data generation, while also raising concerns about misuse in generating realistic fake images and videos.
