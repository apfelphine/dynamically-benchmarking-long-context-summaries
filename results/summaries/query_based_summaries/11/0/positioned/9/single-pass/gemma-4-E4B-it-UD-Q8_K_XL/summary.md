Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a zero-sum game involving two players: a generator ($\mu_G$) and a discriminator ($D$). The generator aims to produce samples ($\mu_G$) that closely match a reference distribution ($\mu_{ref}$), while the discriminator aims to correctly distinguish between real samples from $\mu_{ref}$ and generated samples from $\mu_G$.

In practice, the generator maps from a simple latent space ($\mu_Z$) to the target data distribution ($\mu_G = \mu_Z \circ G^{-1}$), and the discriminator is implemented as a function $D:\Omega \to [0,1]$. The objective function for the original GAN game is $L(\mu_G, D) := \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mu_{ref}}[\ln D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mu_G}[\ln(1-D(x))]$. A major theoretical result shows that the optimal discriminator computes the Jensen-Shannon divergence, leading to the unique equilibrium where the generator perfectly mimics the reference distribution, and the discriminator outputs $1/2$ deterministically.
