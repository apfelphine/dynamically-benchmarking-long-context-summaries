Based on the provided text, **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** are a class of generative models defined as a zero-sum game between two neural networks: a **generator** and a **discriminator**. The generator's objective is to learn a mapping from a latent space to a data distribution that matches a reference distribution (such as real images), while the discriminator attempts to distinguish between real data samples and those generated by the generator. Theoretically, the optimal discriminator computes the likelihood ratio between the reference and generator distributions, and the game reaches a unique equilibrium when the generator perfectly mimics the reference distribution and the discriminator outputs a constant probability (typically 0.5). In practice, this process often faces challenges such as **mode collapse** (where the generator fails to generalize and produces limited varieties of output) and **vanishing gradients** (occurring when the discriminator becomes too effective, providing no learning signal to the generator). To address these issues, variants like the **Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)** utilize the Wasserstein distance and constrain the discriminator's Lipschitz norm, while training strategies like the **Two-Time-Scale Update Rule (TTUR)** adjust learning rates for stability. GANs have been successfully applied to diverse fields including **medical imaging** for generating synthetic data to preserve privacy, **science** for simulating gravitational lensing and particle physics, and **art** for creating realistic images and facilitating creative workflows.

The text also describes several prominent **GAN variants** that extend the core framework. **Conditional GANs (cGANs)** allow the generator to produce specific samples conditioned on additional information, such as class labels. **CycleGAN** performs unsupervised translation between two domains without paired training data by employing two generators and two discriminators with a cycle consistency loss. **StyleGAN** introduces a progressive growth mechanism and style-based latent manipulation to generate high-resolution images with controllable texture and style, solving issues like "texture sticking." Other variants include **InfoGAN**, which attempts to disentangle the latent space into informative and incompressible parts via mutual information maximization; **Bidirectional GANs (BiGANs)**, which learn both an encoder and a decoder for interpretable generative modeling; and **Adversarial Autoencoders (AAEs)**, which use a discriminator to constrain the latent space. Evaluation of GANs typically relies on metrics such as the **Inception Score (IS)** and **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)** to measure sample quality and diversity.
