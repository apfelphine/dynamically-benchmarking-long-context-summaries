## Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) — Summary

A **generative adversarial network (GAN)** is a machine learning framework based on a two-player zero-sum game—the generator and the discriminator. The generator seeks to produce samples that closely mimic a reference data distribution, while the discriminator attempts to distinguish real data from the generator’s synthetic data. Mathematically, the GAN objective function leverages probability spaces and operates such that the generator minimizes and the discriminator maximizes the loss, pushing the generator’s output distribution to approximate the reference distribution. When the discriminator is optimal, it computes the likelihood ratio and the loss reflects the Jensen–Shannon divergence. Training typically involves alternating updates to both networks with backpropagation, often using randomized latent input for the generator (e.g., Gaussian noise) and a labeled dataset to guide the discriminator. When convergence is ideal, the generator perfectly mimics the reference data, and the discriminator becomes maximally uncertain, outputting ½ for all inputs.

GANs are implicit generative models: they do not provide explicit likelihood functions or latent variable inference as variational autoencoders do. Their core theoretical framework supports many variants, including conditional GANs, Wasserstein GANs, CycleGANs, and StyleGAN series. GANs are renowned for their capabilities in fast data generation, image synthesis, and creative applications such as art, music, and gaming. However, practical training often faces issues like unstable convergence, mode collapse, and vanishing gradients, leading to ongoing research into alternative objectives, architectures, and strategies to stabilize training. GANs have found widespread applications across science, medicine, art, and malicious domains, including scientific simulations, synthetic medical image generation, facial reconstruction, and even deepfake creation.
