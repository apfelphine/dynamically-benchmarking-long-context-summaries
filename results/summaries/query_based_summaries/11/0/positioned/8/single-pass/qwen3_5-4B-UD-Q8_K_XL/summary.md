A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a class of generative machine learning models consisting of two competing neural networks: a generator and a discriminator. The generator's objective is to create synthetic data that mimics the reference data distribution, while the discriminator attempts to distinguish between real data and the generator's output. This setup forms a zero-sum minimax game where the generator aims to minimize the discriminator's ability to detect fakes, and the discriminator aims to maximize its accuracy, theoretically converging to a Nash equilibrium where the generator perfectly mimics the reference distribution.

GANs are distinct from other generative models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) or flow-based models because they do not explicitly model a likelihood function and can produce complete samples in a single pass. However, training GANs is challenging due to issues such as vanishing gradients when the discriminator is too strong, and mode collapse where the generator fails to cover the full diversity of the data distribution. To address these problems, variants like Wasserstein GANs (WGANs) and WGAN-GP have been developed, which replace the standard loss function with the Wasserstein distance and enforce Lipschitz continuity on the discriminator to provide more stable training and better gradient signals.
