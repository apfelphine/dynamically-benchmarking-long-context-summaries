# University of Sydney

Founded in 1850, the University of Sydney is Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university, established to provide access based on merit rather than religious or social status. It was the first institution in the world to admit women on an equal basis with men, granting the first degrees to women in 1885. The university is located on a main campus in the inner-city suburbs of Camperdown and Darlington, featuring historic Gothic and Tudor Revival architecture, and operates several satellite campuses and research facilities, including the Sydney Conservatorium of Music in the CBD, the Camden campus, and the Sydney Dental Hospital. Its governance includes eight faculties and schools, such as Medicine and Health, Engineering, and Law, supported by a significant endowment and numerous endowed professorships.

In recent decades, the university has faced various challenges and developments, including controversies over staff underpayment, financial management practices, and student satisfaction ratings. The university has also grappled with issues regarding sexual harassment and academic misconduct, leading to the establishment of taskforces to address these concerns. Notable alumni include eight Prime Ministers of Australia and numerous Nobel laureates. The university continues to expand its research capabilities, with a focus on areas such as health, engineering, and the arts, and maintains a strong international presence through its global rankings and exchange programs.
