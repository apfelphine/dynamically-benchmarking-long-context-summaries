The University of Sydney is Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university, established in 1850. It is renowned for its academic excellence, with a strong emphasis on research, and has produced numerous notable alumni, including eight Australian prime ministers and several Nobel laureates. The university's main campus is located in Camperdown and Darlington, featuring iconic Gothic and Tudor Revival architecture and a central quadrangle. It offers a wide range of faculties and schools, including Arts and Social Sciences, Business, Engineering, Medicine and Health, and Science. The university is known for its high graduate employment rates and strong international student presence, with over 40% of students coming from outside Australia. 

The university has faced several controversies, including issues related to sexual harassment, staff underpayment, and student housing conditions. In 2025, it had the second-worst student satisfaction rating among Australian universities. The institution has a long-standing tradition of inclusivity, having admitted women on equal terms with men since 1881, and it continues to promote diversity through initiatives like the Charles Perkins Oration and Prize. The university also maintains a significant endowment and a large academic library, the largest in the Southern Hemisphere. It has a robust student life with a student union, clubs, and societies, and a strong focus on innovation and research, particularly in medicine, engineering, and the sciences. The university is consistently ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally, with a strong reputation in fields such as law, medicine, and engineering.
