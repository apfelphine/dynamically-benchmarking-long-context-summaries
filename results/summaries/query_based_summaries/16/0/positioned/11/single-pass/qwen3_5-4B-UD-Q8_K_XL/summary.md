The University of Sydney was established via the University of Sydney Act 1850 on September 24, 1850, and inaugurated in October 1852. It was Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university, receiving a royal charter from Queen Victoria in 1858. The university was notable for admitting women on an equal basis with men starting in 1881. In 1990, it incorporated several colleges and institutes, including the Sydney Conservatorium of Music, Sydney College of the Arts, and Sydney Institute of Education.

The university's main campus spans the inner-city suburbs of Camperdown and Darlington, featuring historic Gothic and Tudor Revival architecture designed by Edmund Blacket and Leslie Wilkinson. Key facilities include the Main Building, Great Hall, and the student-run University of Sydney Union (USU). The institution operates multiple satellite campuses, including locations in the Sydney CBD, Camden, and a Dental Hospital in Surry Hills, alongside specialized facilities such as the Sydney Medical School and the Molonglo Observatory. Financially, the university has faced challenges regarding public funding and student fees, leading to cost-cutting measures and controversies over academic misconduct and staff underpayment.
