# University of Sydney

Founded in 1850, the University of Sydney is Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university, established through the University of Sydney Act 1850. Initially inaugurated in 1852, it was granted a royal charter by Queen Victoria in 1858, conferring degrees with UK equivalent status. The university has a long history of innovation and inclusivity, being among the first globally to admit women on an equal basis with men, starting in 1881. It has received significant historical endowments, most notably the £200,000 bequest from John Henry Challis, which funded seven professorships in the late 19th century. Notable achievements include awarding the first Doctor of Science in Engineering degree in 1924 to John Bradfield, the lead engineer for the Sydney Harbour Bridge, and receiving a Pablo Picasso painting in 2010 to fund endowed chairs at its Charles Perkins Centre.

The university operates across multiple campuses, with its main campus in Camperdown and Darlington housing the majority of faculties, including Arts, Science, Medicine, and Business. Key satellite campuses include the Sydney Conservatorium of Music in the CBD, the Camden campus for bioscience and veterinary science, and the CBD campus for the Business School. The university underwent significant consolidation in 2021 with the closure of the Cumberland campus, moving health disciplines to the purpose-built Susan Wakil Health Building. Its library system, the largest academic library in the Southern Hemisphere, holds over 5.3 million items and is the only state legal deposit library in Australia. The university is highly ranked internationally, often placing in the top 50 globally, and is known for its strong alumni network, having produced eight Prime Ministers and numerous Nobel laureates.

Recent years have seen the university navigate various challenges and controversies, including a 2021 National Student Safety Survey reporting high rates of sexual harassment, a 2022 staff strike demanding improved rights and conditions, and a 2024 protest by students against the Gaza war. Despite these issues, the university maintains a focus on research excellence and student outcomes, with graduates achieving high employment rates and employer satisfaction. The institution continues to evolve through initiatives like the "Global Excellence Strategy" and ongoing campus improvements, aiming to remain a leading center for education and research in Australia and beyond.
