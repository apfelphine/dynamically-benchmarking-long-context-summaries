The University of Sydney, established in 1850, is Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university. It began in the Big Schoolroom of Sydney Grammar School and was granted a royal charter in 1858. The university was among the first in the world to admit women on equal terms with men, with the first female graduates in 1881. Over time, it expanded significantly, incorporating several institutions in 1990, including the Sydney Conservatorium of Music, Sydney College of Advanced Education, and others. The main campus is located in Camperdown and Darlington, featuring a historic Neo-Gothic quadrangle and buildings designed by Edmund Blacket. The university is known for its strong academic reputation, with high graduate employment rates (89.6% employer satisfaction, 80.9% full-time employment), and is consistently ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally. It has a large student body, with a strong emphasis on research and teaching, and is home to notable research centers such as the Charles Perkins Centre. The university is also known for its student life, including the University of Sydney Union (USU), which provides clubs, societies, catering, and recreational spaces. It has faced controversies, including issues with sexual harassment, staff underpayment, and student seating shortages. The university has a diverse student body and a rich history of notable alumni, including eight Australian prime ministers, five chief justices, and numerous Nobel laureates. The university's academic structure includes eight faculties and schools, and it operates a large library system and museums, including the Chau Chak Wing Museum. The university has a long-standing commitment to equity, with initiatives like the Charles Perkins Oration and Prize honoring Indigenous students and leaders.
