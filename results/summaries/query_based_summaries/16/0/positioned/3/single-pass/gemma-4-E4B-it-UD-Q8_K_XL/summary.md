## University of Sydney Summary

The University of Sydney was established on September 24, 1850, following a plan proposed by William Wentworth to expand the existing Sydney College into a university, driven by the belief that it should serve "the child of every class" with merit-based access. It was inaugurated in 1852 and was Australia's first, as well as one of the first public, non-denominational, and secular universities in the British Empire. The university granted a royal charter from Queen Victoria in 1858, granting its degrees rank equal to those from universities in the United Kingdom. Notably, it was among the first in the world to admit women on an equal basis with men, commencing in 1881.

The main campus is located across the inner-city suburbs of Camperdown and Darlington, featuring beautiful architecture and a quadrangle. The university comprises eight faculties and schools, including the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences, the University of Sydney Business School, and the Faculty of Medicine and Health. Its libraries, which include the largest academic library in the Southern Hemisphere, contain extensive collections. The University of Sydney has achieved high national and global rankings, including a position of #47 in the 2024 Aggregate Ranking of Top Universities.
