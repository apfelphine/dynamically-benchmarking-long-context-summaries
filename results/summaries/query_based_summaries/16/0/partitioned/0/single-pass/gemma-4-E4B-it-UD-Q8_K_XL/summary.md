The University of Sydney was established on September 24, 1850, through the passage of the University of Sydney Act 1850 (NSW). It was Australia's first university and one of the earliest public, non-denominational, and secular universities in the British Empire. In 1857, it received a royal charter from Queen Victoria, granting degrees equivalent to those from UK universities. The university was one of the first globally to admit women on an equal footing, starting in 1881.

Throughout its history, the University of Sydney has seen significant development, including the establishment of the PhD research degree in 1951. The university's main campus is located in the inner-city suburbs of Camperdown and Darlington, and features important Gothic and Tudor Revival style architecture. Academically, it is highly ranked globally, attaining positions such as #47 in the 2024 Aggregate Ranking of Top Universities. The university is also noted for its extensive collections, such as the Nicholson Collection of antiquities, and hosts the Charles Perkins Oration, honoring its first Aboriginal graduate.
