The University of Sydney, established in 1850, is Australia's first public, secular, and non-denominational university. It operates a main campus in Camperdown and Darlington, known for its Gothic architecture and quadrangle, with additional campuses in Sydney CBD, Camden, and the medical school in the city. The university has faced controversies including staff underpayment, sexual harassment, environmental littering, and insufficient seating due to high enrolments. It has also been criticized for low student satisfaction, with a 2025 survey ranking it second worst in Australia. The university is known for its strong academic reputation, producing numerous notable alumni including eight Australian prime ministers, five chief justices, and Nobel laureates. It offers a range of faculties and schools, including Arts, Engineering, Medicine, and Business, and maintains a student union (USU) and residential halls. The university has a history of internal management challenges, including staff dissatisfaction and student protests over fee deregulation and funding. It is also recognized for its research and academic excellence, with a strong presence in science, medicine, and the arts.
