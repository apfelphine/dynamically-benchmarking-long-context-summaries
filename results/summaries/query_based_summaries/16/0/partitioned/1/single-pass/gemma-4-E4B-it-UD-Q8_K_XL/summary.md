The University of Sydney was established via the University of Sydney Act 1850 (NSW) on September 24, 1850, and was inaugurated on October 11, 1852. It was Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university, receiving a royal charter from Queen Victoria on February 27, 1858. The university was one of the first to admit women on an equal basis with men starting in 1881.

The University of Sydney comprises eight faculties and schools, including the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences, the University of Sydney Business School, and the Faculty of Medicine and Health. Its main campus is spread across Camperdown and Darlington, and it features numerous affiliated satellite campuses and research stations globally. The institution has faced various controversies over time, including issues surrounding sexual harassment, staff underpayment, and student satisfaction ratings.
