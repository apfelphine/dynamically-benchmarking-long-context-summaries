## University of Jordan Summary

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 in Amman, is the first and largest university in Jordan. Founded by royal decree after extensive national calls for a local institution, it started with a single faculty and 167 students. Over the decades, it expanded rapidly, adding schools in medicine, engineering, law, agriculture, and more. Today, it offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. The university is organized into numerous institutes and research centers, and also includes the Jordan University Hospital—a major teaching and clinical research hospital notable for regional medical innovations and international accreditations.

The university's main campus spans over 1.2 km² in Amman, complemented by an additional branch in Aqaba since 2009 to support southern Jordan’s development. The University of Jordan Library, established alongside the university, holds over a million items and serves as a crucial resource for research and education nationally. The institution enjoys significant international affiliations and maintains hundreds of exchange agreements. Noted for its high admission standards and leading rankings in the Arab world, the university has produced prominent alumni in politics, science, business, and the arts. It is celebrated for its contributions to research, publication of scientific journals, and its strong influence on Jordan’s public life, with over 159,000 graduates since its founding.
