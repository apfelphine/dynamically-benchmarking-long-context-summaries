## University of Jordan Summary

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 by royal decree in Amman, is the oldest and largest university in Jordan, often called "the Mother University." Its main campus is in Jubaiha, Amman, spanning 1,200 dunums. The university also has a branch in Aqaba, opened in 2009 to support regional development. Initially starting with the Faculty of Arts and 167 students, the university has grown significantly, adding scientific, medical, and humanities faculties, and establishing research centers and Jordan University Hospital, the country's first public teaching hospital. Instruction has traditionally been in Arabic for humanities and English for sciences, and the university pioneered a credit hour system in the Arab region. Over the decades, it saw expansions in faculties, research, and student activism, maintaining autonomy while introducing specialized admission programs and blended learning.

Academically, the University of Jordan offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. Notable academic units include the School of Engineering (accredited by ABET), King Abdullah II School of Information Technology, and first-of-their-kind faculties in medicine, nursing, pharmacy, and dentistry in Jordan. The university operates the largest library in Jordan, with over a million print and electronic items, and hosts numerous research institutes and centers specializing in Arabic language, water and environment studies, strategic studies, and Islamic culture. Jordan University Hospital is a major center for clinical education and research. The university ranks highly nationally and regionally, maintaining international affiliations and numerous student-exchange programs. Its alumni network includes prime ministers, ministers, scientists, diplomats, and cultural leaders, reflecting its central role in Jordanian academic and public life.
