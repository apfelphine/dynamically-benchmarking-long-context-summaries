### University of Jordan

The University of Jordan, established by royal decree in 1962, is Jordan’s first and largest university, with its main campus in Jubaiha, Amman, spanning 1,200 dunums. It opened formally in 1965, initially offering instruction primarily in Arabic with a single Faculty of Arts, and began teaching in December 1965. The institution rapidly expanded, adding multiple faculties including Science, Medicine, Engineering, and Law, and developing research centers and a teaching hospital (Jordan University Hospital). A southern branch campus in Aqaba was established in 2009 under King Abdullah II’s patronage to extend higher education access and regional development, featuring modern academic and sports facilities and plans for women's dormitories.

Academically, the university offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across three streams: Scientific, Humanitarian, and Medical faculties. It houses distinguished faculties such as the King Abdullah II School of Information Technology and the first public business school in Jordan to achieve AACSB accreditation. Its library system is one of Jordan’s largest, with over a million print and electronic items. The university supports diverse research through institutes, centers (e.g., for Photonics Research, Islamic Cultural Studies, and Strategic Studies), and a dedicated university press publishing multiple peer-reviewed journals. The University of Jordan plays a leading national role in education, research, and community service, and is ranked among the top universities in Jordan and the Arab world by international rankings, while maintaining extensive international partnerships and a vibrant student life.
