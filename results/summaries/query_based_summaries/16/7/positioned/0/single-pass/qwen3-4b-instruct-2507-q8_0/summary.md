The University of Jordan is a public university established in 1962, with its main campus located in Jubaiha, north of Amman. The campus spans 1,200 dunums and features twelve gates, including a main entrance on University Street. It has a secondary campus in Aqaba, established in 2009 to support regional development and expand higher education. The university was founded following national calls for a national university, with King Hussein bin Talal playing a key role in its establishment. It began teaching in 1965 with 167 students and has since expanded to include 10 faculties and 78 undergraduate programs, with 143 postgraduate programs. The university emphasizes academic freedom, introduced a credit hour system in the Arab world, and established early student organizations like the Samir Al-Rifai Theater. It operates a library with over one million items and has a teaching hospital, Jordan University Hospital (JUH), which offers comprehensive medical services and research. The university has achieved high academic rankings, including being ranked first in Jordan and 11th in the Arab world in the 2021 Webometrics Ranking. It has a strong research output, with over 8,700 research papers, 752 books, and 78 patents reported between 2000 and 2011. The university is known for its international affiliations, including partnerships with institutions in the United States, the Islamic World, and the Mediterranean. Notable faculty and alumni include scholars in politics, law, and science, such as Nasir al-Din al-Asad, Abdelsalam al-Majali, and Kamel Abu Jaber. The university also has a strong student body, with a student union and active student life, and has produced numerous prominent figures in politics, science, and the arts.
