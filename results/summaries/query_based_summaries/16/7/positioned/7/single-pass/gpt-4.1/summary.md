## University of Jordan Summary

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 in Amman, is the oldest and largest university in Jordan, occupying a central role in the country’s higher education landscape. Its main campus spans 1,200 dunums in the Jubaiha district and features extensive academic, recreational, and research facilities, while its southern Aqaba Campus, founded in 2009, supports regional development. From its inception, the university expanded rapidly: beginning with 167 students and a sole Faculty of Arts, it now comprises scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties offering 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs, spread across multiple specialized schools and research centers. The institution introduced the credit hour system (first in the Arab world) and is distinguished for its broad autonomy, innovative research (over 8,700 research papers, 78 patents between 2000–2011), and robust library system.

Academically, the University of Jordan is a leader in Jordan and the region, consistently attaining the country’s highest admission scores and ranking in the top tier of Arab universities within global university rankings (QS, THE, ARWU). Its research enterprise includes discipline-specific journals and scientific collections, notably housing the only known fossil remains of Arambourgiania. The university's teaching hospital, Jordan University Hospital, is the nation’s first university hospital and an accredited leader in medical training and research. Internationally, it maintains hundreds of cooperation agreements, is a member of major university associations, and offers vibrant student and alumni networks. Alumni include multiple prime ministers, ministers, scientists, and notable figures in arts, business, diplomacy, and academia, underscoring the university’s lasting national and regional influence.
