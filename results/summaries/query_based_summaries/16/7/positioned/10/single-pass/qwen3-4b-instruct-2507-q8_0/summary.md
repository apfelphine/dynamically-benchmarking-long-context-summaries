The University of Jordan is a public university established in 1962 in Amman, Jordan, with its main campus located in Jubaiha. It was founded through a royal decree by King Hussein bin Talal, following years of national demand for a national university. The university began operations in 1965 with 167 students, including 17 women, and initially operated from three buildings with a small faculty and budget. Over the decades, it expanded significantly, adding new faculties such as Medicine, Dentistry, Pharmacy, Engineering, Nursing, and Law, and establishing research centers and a teaching hospital, Jordan University Hospital (JUH), which became the country's first university teaching hospital in 1975. The university has a strong academic structure, offering 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across 109 master's and 34 doctoral degrees, organized into scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. It also operates a large library system with over one million items and a range of research centers, including the Center for Photonics Research and the Islamic Cultural Center. The university is known for its high admission scores in the country and ranks among the top universities in Jordan and the Arab world in global rankings, including the Webometrics, QS, and Times Higher Education rankings. It maintains international affiliations, including with the Association of Arab Universities and the Federation of the Universities of the Islamic World, and has a student union that represents student interests. The university's notable alumni include prominent political figures, scientists, and professionals in various fields, such as former Jordanian prime ministers, diplomats, and researchers. The Aqaba branch, established in 2009, further expands its reach and educational offerings.
