## University of Jordan: Summary

The University of Jordan, established by royal decree in 1962 in Amman, is the oldest and largest university in Jordan. Its main campus covers 1,200 dunums and serves as a central hub for higher education in the country, with a branch in Aqaba founded in 2009 to support regional development. Initially operating from three buildings with eight faculty members and 167 students, the university has grown to host thousands of students and numerous faculties. Over decades, it expanded its academic offerings to include 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs, organized into scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. Landmark developments include the establishment of Jordan University Hospital (JUH), specialized research centers, and the University of Jordan Library (UJL) with over one million volumes.

Renowned for both teaching and research, the University of Jordan has achieved regional and international recognition. It was the first in Jordan to adopt a credit hour system and maintains autonomy in its governance. The university regularly ranks highest in national and regional university rankings, and its research output includes numerous patents, publications, and active scientific journals. As a center for student activism, cultural development, and innovation, the university maintains strong international affiliations and exchange programs. Notable alumni and faculty include political leaders, scientists, ministers, and public figures in Jordan and abroad.
