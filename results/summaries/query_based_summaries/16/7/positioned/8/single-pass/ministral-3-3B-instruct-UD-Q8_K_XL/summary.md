The **University of Jordan** was established in 1962 as a national university in Jordan, founded to provide higher education and foster scientific, cultural, and social development. Its founding was a response to the need for a national institution to replace the reliance on foreign universities for higher education. The university was officially inaugurated in 1965, with its first degree conferred in 1965, and quickly became a cornerstone of Jordan’s educational system.

The university is organized into three main streams of faculties: **Scientific, Humanitarian, and Medical (Health) faculties**. The **Scientific faculties** include the School of Science, School of Agriculture, School of Engineering, and the King Abdullah II School of Information Technology. The **Humanitarian faculties** encompass ten schools, such as the School of Arts, School of Sharia (Islamic studies), School of Business, and School of Physical Education. The **Medical faculties** consist of the School of Medicine, School of Nursing, School of Pharmacy, School of Dentistry, and the School of Rehabilitation Sciences.

The **University of Jordan Library (UJL)**, established in 1962, is one of the university’s key resources, housing over one million print and electronic items. It supports faculty, students, researchers, and visiting scholars, and is a depository for United Nations and other international-organization documents. The university has also established several research centers, including the Institute of Archaeology, the Social Work Institute, and the Islamic Cultural Center, among others.

The university has expanded significantly over the years, with the establishment of its **Aqaba branch** in 2009, dedicated to fostering development in the Aqaba region. The university has also been recognized for its academic excellence, ranking among the top universities in Jordan and the Arab world. It has contributed significantly to Jordan’s scientific and cultural landscape, with notable achievements in research, education, and community engagement.
