## University of Jordan

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 by royal decree under King Hussein bin Talal, is the first and largest university in Jordan. Its main campus is situated in Jubaiha, Amman, covering 1,200 dunums (1.20 km²), strategically located near major city landmarks and accessible through twelve gates. The university also has a southern branch campus in Aqaba, founded in 2009 to expand higher education and development in the region, featuring modern academic and recreational facilities, including ongoing construction of a women’s dormitory.

Initially starting with a single Faculty of Arts and 167 students, the university quickly expanded its faculties, adding Science, Economics, Medicine, Engineering, Law, and other disciplines over the decades. It introduced the first credit hour system in the Arab world and organized as a state university with significant academic autonomy. Presently, it offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. The university library, established with the university's founding, houses over one million items, including historic documents and rare archives. It is a leading research institution in Jordan, with various affiliated research centers and a university hospital (Jordan University Hospital) that is noted for early accreditation achievements. The university holds prominent national and regional rankings and maintains numerous international academic affiliations and student exchange agreements. Its alumni include several Jordanian prime ministers, government officials, diplomats, academics, and cultural figures.
