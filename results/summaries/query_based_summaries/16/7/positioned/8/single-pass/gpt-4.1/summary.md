## University of Jordan Summary

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 in Amman, is the oldest and largest university in Jordan. Its main campus, spanning 1.2 km² in the Jubaiha area, was chosen for its central location, scenery, and accessibility. In 2009, the university expanded with a branch in Aqaba, supporting regional educational development. Since its inception, the university has played a pivotal role in higher education and research in Jordan, operating independently with scientific, financial, and administrative autonomy.

The university comprises numerous faculties organized into scientific, humanities, and medical streams, offering 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs. It has established recognized research centers, institutes, and the Jordan University Hospital—a major center for medical education and clinical training. The University of Jordan Library houses over a million print and electronic items, serving as a national document repository. Internationally, the university maintains extensive exchange agreements and is affiliated with several academic organizations. Notable alumni include government leaders, diplomats, scientists, writers, and cultural figures, reflecting the university’s significant impact on Jordan’s intellectual and professional landscape. In recent global rankings, the university is consistently recognized as the top institution in Jordan and among the leading universities in the Arab world.
