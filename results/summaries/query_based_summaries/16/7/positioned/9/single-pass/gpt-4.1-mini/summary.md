## University of Jordan

The University of Jordan, established by royal decree in 1962 and inaugurated in 1965, is the first national university in Jordan, located primarily on a 1.2 km² campus in Jubaiha, Amman. The university's foundation responded to the longstanding demand for a national higher education institution, initially operating with a Faculty of Arts and 167 students. It expanded steadily despite regional political and economic challenges, adding diverse faculties including Science, Economics, Medicine, Engineering, Law, and others. In 2009, it opened a southern branch campus in Aqaba to support regional development. The university emphasizes autonomy and academic freedom, pioneering initiatives such as the credit hour system and offering instruction in Arabic for humanities and English for sciences.

Academically, the University of Jordan offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. Notably, its School of Engineering achieved ABET accreditation, and its business school was the first in Jordan to earn AACSB accreditation. The university hosts numerous research centers and institutes, a sizable library with over one million items, and a teaching hospital—Jordan University Hospital—known for advanced medical procedures and accreditations. Internationally, the university holds memberships in several academic associations and maintains hundreds of bilateral exchange agreements.

The University of Jordan has cultivated prominent alumni across politics, academia, arts, and sciences, including multiple Jordanian prime ministers, government ministers, diplomats, and scholars recognized nationally and internationally. Its faculty has included distinguished scientists, academics, and public figures who have contributed to the university's development and Jordan’s broader societal progress. The university continues to foster research, education, and community engagement aligned with Jordan’s national priorities.
