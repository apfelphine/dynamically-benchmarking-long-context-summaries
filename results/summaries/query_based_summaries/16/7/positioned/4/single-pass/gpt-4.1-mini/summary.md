# Summary: University of Jordan

The University of Jordan, established by royal decree in 1962, is the first and largest university in Jordan, with its main campus located in Jubaiha, Amman, spanning 1,200 dunums. A southern branch campus was founded in Aqaba in 2009 to promote higher education and regional development. The university began with a single Faculty of Arts and 167 students and rapidly expanded, adding multiple faculties including Science, Economics, Medicine, Engineering, and Law, among others. It introduced innovative academic practices such as the credit hour system, and maintains financial, administrative, and scientific autonomy. By the 2010s, it aimed to be a research-focused institution with expanded graduate programs and increased student activism.

Academically, the university offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. Notable faculties include the School of Engineering (ABET-accredited departments), the King Abdullah II School of Information Technology, and the AACSB-accredited School of Business. Its library, established alongside the university, holds over one million items including historical manuscripts and provides extensive services. The university also operates the Jordan University Hospital, a leading teaching and research hospital. The university is recognized regionally in rankings and maintains numerous international partnerships for student exchange and research collaboration. Its alumni include prominent political figures, academics, scientists, artists, and diplomats with significant contributions locally and internationally.
