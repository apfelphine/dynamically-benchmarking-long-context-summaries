# University of Jordan

The University of Jordan (UJ) was established by royal decree in 1962, with its main campus located in Jubaiha, Amman, spanning approximately 1.20 km². The founding reflected national aspirations to create a premier higher education institution in Jordan, addressing the need for local academic opportunities as previously many Jordanians studied abroad. UJ began teaching in 1965 with a single Faculty of Arts and 167 students, expanding rapidly to include multiple faculties across scientific, humanitarian, and medical streams. Notably, the Faculty of Engineering—the largest—achieved ABET accreditation by 2021. The university adopted a credit hour system early and operated independently of the government with scientific, administrative, and financial autonomy. A branch campus was established in Aqaba in 2009 to foster regional development.

Academically, UJ encompasses 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs, including 111 master’s and 38 doctoral degrees. The Scientific faculties cover natural and applied sciences, engineering, agriculture, and IT; the Humanitarian faculties comprise arts, business, law, education, and international studies; and the Medical faculties include medicine, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry, and rehabilitation sciences. The University of Jordan Library, founded alongside the university, holds over one million print and electronic items and serves a large user base including faculty and students. The university also hosts several specialized research centers and institutes, contributing significantly to academic output and innovation.

UJ operates the Jordan University Hospital, founded in 1973 and incorporated into the university in 1975, which serves as a major teaching, research, and clinical facility accredited by international bodies. The university’s research strengths include significant collections of historic documents, manuscripts, and unique fossil remains. Its press publishes numerous peer-reviewed journals across various disciplines. The university holds notable rankings nationally and regionally, including being the top-ranked university in Jordan and among the leading institutions in the Arab world according to various global rankings in recent years.

The university maintains hundreds of international agreements and affiliations with organizations such as the International Association of Universities, the Federation of the Universities of the Islamic World, and the Association of Arab Universities, supporting student exchanges and international cooperation.

Notable faculty and alumni have held prominent roles in academia, government, diplomacy, science, arts, and media. Alumni include multiple Jordanian prime ministers, ministers, diplomats, scholars, and cultural figures. The university fosters a politically active student body, with a structured student union and significant campus activism historically and presently.

The University of Jordan remains a central institution in Jordan’s higher education landscape, symbolizing national development goals and regional academic leadership.
