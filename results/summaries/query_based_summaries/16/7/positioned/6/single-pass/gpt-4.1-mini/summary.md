# Summary: University of Jordan

The University of Jordan (UJ), established by royal decree in 1962 and officially opened in 1965 by King Hussein bin Talal, is Jordan's first and largest public university. Its main campus is located in Jubaiha, Amman, spanning 1,200 dunums, featuring extensive facilities, multiple gates, and nearby student services along University Street. A southern branch opened in Aqaba in 2009, aimed at expanding higher education and regional development, with ongoing infrastructure growth including new dormitories.

Starting with the Faculty of Arts and 167 students in 1965, UJ rapidly expanded through the 1970s to 2000s despite regional and economic challenges, adding numerous faculties including Medicine, Engineering, Law, and business-related schools. It introduced innovative systems like the first credit hour program in the Arab world. Since 2000, the university has focused on research development, internationalization, and student activism, offering over 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties, including the ABET-accredited School of Engineering and AACSB-accredited Business School. The university maintains a significant library with over one million items, various research institutes, and operates Jordan University Hospital, a leading teaching and research medical center.

UJ enjoys prominent national and regional rankings, being top in Jordan and highly rated in the Arab world. It has extensive international affiliations and student exchange agreements with hundreds of institutions, including numerous US universities. Notable faculty and alumni have held high political office, academic distinctions, and contributed broadly in sciences, politics, arts, and diplomacy, including prime ministers, ministers, scholars, and athletes. The student union has evolved with regulations reflecting both student and faculty collaboration, representing a dynamic campus life.
