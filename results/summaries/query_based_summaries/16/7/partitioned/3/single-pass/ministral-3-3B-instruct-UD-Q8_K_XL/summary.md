The **University of Jordan** was established in 1962 as the first national university in Jordan, founded under the patronage of King Hussein bin Talal. Initially modeled on European university structures, it began operations in 1965 with a small faculty and student body, focusing on Arabic and scientific disciplines. Early leadership included key figures like Nasir al-Din al-Asad, who served as the first president (1962–1968).

By the 1970s, the university expanded significantly, adding new faculties such as Medicine, Engineering, and Law, and establishing its teaching hospital, Jordan University Hospital (JUH), in 1975. The university’s academic and administrative autonomy was formalized in the 1990s, aligning with broader national reforms. Today, it operates as a public research university with a diverse range of programs, including 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate degrees across scientific, humanistic, and medical faculties.

The university has a prominent presence in Jordan’s education landscape, offering programs in fields like engineering, information technology, medicine, and social sciences. It has also established international collaborations and research centers, such as the **Jordan Academy of Arabic**, which regulates Arabic language and literature globally. The university’s reputation is bolstered by its contributions to scientific research, with notable achievements in fields like telecommunications, cybersecurity, and medical sciences.

The **University of Jordan** remains a key institution in Jordan’s higher education system, fostering innovation, academic excellence, and social development through its diverse academic offerings and research initiatives.
