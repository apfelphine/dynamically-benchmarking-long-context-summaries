The University of Jordan, established in 1962 as the country's first university, serves as a premier institution for higher education, research, and cultural preservation in Jordan. Situated primarily in the Jubaiha area of Amman, the campus spans over 1,200 dunums and features distinctive architecture, including a clock tower and extensive green spaces. The university has expanded to include a branch campus in Aqaba, established in 2009 to support regional development. Historically, it operated with significant autonomy and introduced innovative practices such as the credit hour system and a student union. The institution is renowned for its diverse faculties, including the largest School of Engineering, the School of Science, and ten humanities schools, alongside five medical faculties.

The University of Jordan boasts a distinguished legacy of notable alumni who have contributed significantly to politics, science, arts, and international affairs. Prominent graduates include former Prime Ministers Bisher Khasawneh and Marouf al-Bakhit, UN Women Executive Director Sima Sami Bahous, and numerous diplomats and researchers. The university is recognized for its research output, having produced thousands of papers and patents, and holds a high ranking in the Arab world. Its library system houses over one million items, and the institution is actively engaged in international collaborations, hosting students from over 100 countries and maintaining partnerships with global universities.
