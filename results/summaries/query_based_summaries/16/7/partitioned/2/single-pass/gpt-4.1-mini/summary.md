# Summary of University of Jordan

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 in Amman at the Jubaiha area, is the country's first and largest public university. Its main campus spans 1,200 dunums (approximately 1.20 km²) and features multiple gates, parking areas, and proximity to major city landmarks. A southern branch campus was inaugurated in Aqaba in 2009 to promote regional development. Over the decades, the university expanded significantly, establishing numerous faculties across scientific, humanitarian, and medical disciplines. The scientific faculties include the Schools of Science, Agriculture, Engineering (largest faculty, all departments ABET accredited), and King Abdullah II School of Information Technology. The humanities faculties encompass ten schools, such as Arts, Sharia, Business (AACSB accredited), Law, International Studies, and others, offering a broad array of non-scientific disciplines. The medical faculties include Schools of Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Dentistry, and Rehabilitation Sciences—the first of their kind in Jordan.

Academically, the University of Jordan offers 78 undergraduate programs and 143 postgraduate programs (109 master's and 34 doctoral degrees), supported by a substantial library established in 1962 with over one million print and electronic resources, including historical manuscripts and United Nations documents. The institution maintains various research centers and institutes focusing on archaeology, Islamic studies, photonics, and language education. From 2000 onward, the university emphasized research growth, establishing an international orientation with multiple patents, books, and paper publications, and expanding graduate offerings to support its mission. It has been active in student political life, with various student organizations shaping campus discourse. The university celebrates its historical legacy and notable faculty and alumni who have contributed significantly to politics, academia, arts, and sciences in Jordan and beyond.
