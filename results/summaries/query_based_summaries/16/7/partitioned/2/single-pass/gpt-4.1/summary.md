## University of Jordan – Overview and Key Features

The University of Jordan, established in 1962 in the Jubaiha area of Amman, is the first and largest public university in Jordan, often referred to as "the Mother University." Its initial focus was on the Faculty of Arts, with 167 students and a handful of faculty, growing rapidly with the addition of new faculties such as Science, Economics and Commerce, Medicine, Engineering, Dentistry, Pharmacy, Nursing, Agriculture, Law, and Physical Education. The university operates autonomously, with academic, financial, and administrative independence, and instruction primarily in Arabic for humanities and English for sciences. Following economic and political developments, the university expanded its infrastructure, research centers, and hospital, notably launching the Jordan University Hospital—a leading teaching and research hospital with regional recognition.

The university has two main campuses: the original campus in Amman, spanning 1.2 square kilometers with modern facilities and green spaces, and the Aqaba branch established in 2009 to support higher education in southern Jordan. Academically, the University of Jordan offers a comprehensive array of programs, comprising 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate degrees across scientific, humanities, and medical faculties. It is distinguished by its research output, innovative centers and institutes—such as the Water and Environment Research Center and the Jordan Academy of Arabic—and a robust library system housing over one million print and electronic items. The university is highly ranked both nationally and regionally; it attracts top admission scores and maintains numerous international affiliations and student exchange agreements.

## Academic Excellence, Alumni, and Student Life

The University of Jordan demonstrates strong scientific research activity, with faculty members contributing patents, publications, and books. It established the region’s first graduate programs and operates research centers focused on strategic, medical, and economic issues. The university’s publishing arm produces prominent peer-reviewed journals in medical, pharmaceutical, engineering, social sciences, and business administration. Student representation is organized through the Student Union, which has evolved over time to reflect both student and faculty input.

Notable alumni and faculty include former prime ministers, ministers, researchers, writers, and public figures from Jordan and the wider Arab region, such as Bisher Khasawneh, Marouf al-Bakhit, Lubna Tahtamouni, and Wadah Khanfar, among others. The institution has fostered academic, cultural, and athletic achievement, evidenced by Nobel Prize nominations, international awards, and Olympic-level athletes. The University of Jordan remains the preeminent institution for higher education in Jordan, recognized for its tradition, comprehensive academic offerings, research impact, prominent graduates, and contributions to national and regional development.
