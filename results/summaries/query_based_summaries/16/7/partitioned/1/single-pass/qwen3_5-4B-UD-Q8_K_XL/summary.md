The University of Jordan (UJ) is a public research university established in 1962 in Amman, Jordan, with its main campus located in the Jubaiha area. Over its history, the university has expanded significantly, establishing an Aqaba branch in 2009 to support regional development and increasing its enrollment and faculty numbers. By 2011, its faculty had produced over 8,700 research papers, and in the early 2010s, it adopted a strategy to become an internationally oriented institution, expanding its graduate offerings to include 111 master's and 38 doctoral programs. The university is organized into three streams of faculties: Scientific, Humanitarian, and Medical. Key institutions include the School of Engineering (the largest faculty), the King Abdullah II School of Information Technology, and the School of Medicine, which was the first medical school in Jordan. The University of Jordan Library (UJL), established in 1962, serves as a major repository for historical documents and international organization records.

The university plays a pivotal role in Jordan's intellectual and political landscape, boasting notable alumni in government, diplomacy, and academia. Its faculty and graduates have included several former Prime Ministers of Jordan and the Palestinian Authority, as well as high-ranking ambassadors and public officials. In science, the university is home to distinguished researchers such as Shaher Momani, a leading figure in fractional calculus, and Lubna Tahtamouni, a biologist recognized for cancer research. The university maintains international affiliations with numerous universities worldwide and has contributed significantly to the Arab world's scientific output, ranking highly in regional research metrics. Additionally, the Jordan University Hospital, a major teaching hospital affiliated with the university since 1975, provides advanced medical services and clinical training.
