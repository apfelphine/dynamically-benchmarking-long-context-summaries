The **University of Jordan** was established in 1962 as a national university, founded to meet the educational needs of Jordan and foster scientific and academic development. Located in Amman, its main campus spans 1,200 dunums, offering a blend of historic and modern academic infrastructure. The university expanded significantly in the 1970s and 1980s, adding new faculties and research centers, including its Aqaba branch in 2009 to promote regional development.

Academically, the University of Jordan offers a wide range of undergraduate and postgraduate programs across **10 faculties**, including **Scientific, Humanities, and Medical faculties**, with a focus on applied sciences, social sciences, and health sciences. Notable programs include the **School of Medicine, School of Engineering, and School of Information Technology**. The university emphasizes research, publishing over **150 peer-reviewed journals**, including internationally recognized titles like the *Jordan Medical Journal* and *Jordan Journal of Agricultural Sciences*.

The university has gained recognition in regional and global rankings, consistently ranking among the top universities in Jordan and the Arab world. It has partnerships with international institutions, including the **International Association of Universities** and the **Federation of the Universities of the Islamic World**. The University of Jordan is also known for its contributions to Jordan’s higher education landscape, including its role in training medical professionals and fostering innovation through research centers.

Student life includes a **Student Union** and various extracurricular activities, though political activism has occasionally led to tensions with administration. The university’s campus features notable landmarks, such as the **Clock Tower** and the **Jordan University Hospital**, which serves as a major teaching and research facility. The university continues to grow, with ongoing efforts to enhance its research capabilities and global academic presence.
