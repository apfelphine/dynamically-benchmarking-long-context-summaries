The University of the Andes (Colombia), founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, was established as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in the country. Modeled after the American university system, it aims to provide a liberal and technical education to produce leaders who contribute to Colombia's development. The university is an autonomous, independent institution governed by a Board of Trustees and operates from a historic campus in Bogota's center, featuring renovated industrial and religious buildings, a central library, and specialized satellite libraries. Its academic structure includes four cycles, allowing students to design interdisciplinary curricula, and comprises 14 academic units, including ten faculties, the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, and the CIDER.

The institution emphasizes academic excellence, pluralism, and critical ethical formation, with a mission to address Colombia's social and civic responsibilities. As of 2022, the university's assets totaled approximately US$426.1 million. It attracts a diverse student body, with 55% male and 45% female enrollment, where 95% of undergraduates are top 1% high school graduates and 30% originate outside Bogota. The university maintains extensive international exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries and is recognized for its faculty, many of whom hold PhDs from top global institutions. Notable alumni include former President of Colombia César Gaviria, artist Fernando Botero, and mathematician Ana María Rey.
