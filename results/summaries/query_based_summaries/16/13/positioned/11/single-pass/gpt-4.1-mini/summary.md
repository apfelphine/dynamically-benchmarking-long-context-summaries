## University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and intellectuals from Colombia’s political elites, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes) is Colombia’s first nonsectarian and politically independent university. Its establishment was influenced by 1930s-40s Liberal reforms and a national demand for skilled professionals to support rapid industrialization and economic growth. Classes began in 1949 with 80 students and a curriculum modeled on American universities, combining liberal and technical education to foster socially responsible leaders. The university prioritized academic neutrality amid political turmoil and implemented a pioneering program allowing students to complete part of their studies abroad at prestigious U.S. institutions.

Legally constituted as a nonprofit corporation in 1948, it achieved official university status in 1964. Governance includes a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Vice-Rectors, Academic Council, deans, and faculty councils, overseeing 14 academic units with approximately 1,600 non-faculty staff. The university focuses on pluralism, diversity, academic excellence, and social commitment. Its campus in Bogotá combines restored industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities, including extensive classrooms, labs, libraries, and a sports center known as La Caneca. The student body is highly selective, with 95% ranking in the top 1% nationally. International students participate through exchange programs with over 119 universities worldwide. The university’s mascot, Seneca—a goat—symbolizes its tradition and spirit. Notable alumni include former Colombian presidents, government ministers, scientists, artists, and public figures contributing broadly to Colombia’s development.
