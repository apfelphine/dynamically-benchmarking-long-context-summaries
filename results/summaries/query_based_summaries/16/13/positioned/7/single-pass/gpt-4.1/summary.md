## University of the Andes (Colombia)

The University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes, Uniandes) was founded in 1948 in Bogotá as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia, aiming to provide professionals equipped with technical and liberal knowledge to aid in national development. Established amidst the country’s modernization efforts and political unrest, it modeled itself after American universities in structure and curriculum, emphasizing academic neutrality and excellence. Its early years saw innovation in academic offerings and internationalization, including partnerships that enabled student exchanges with prominent U.S. universities. Today, the university is governed by a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, and a network of 14 academic units and 20 administrative units, upholding values of pluralism, independence, and a commitment to social responsibility.

Uniandes offers interdisciplinary undergraduate and graduate programs, allowing students to design their own curricular paths. The university has a diverse student body, with 30% from outside Bogotá and 95% of undergraduates being top-performing students. International exchange is facilitated through agreements with over 119 universities in 34 countries. Its campus in Bogotá’s historical center features renovated facilities, libraries holding over 445,000 volumes, numerous laboratories, computer services, and sports amenities. A notable mascot, Seneca the goat, symbolizes student life and tradition. The university has produced leaders in government, science, arts, and business, such as former Colombian presidents and renowned artists and scientists, reflecting its national influence and global connectivity.
