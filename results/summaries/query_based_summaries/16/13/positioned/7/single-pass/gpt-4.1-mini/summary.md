## University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and a group of intellectuals during a period of political transition and industrial growth in Colombia, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes) was established as Colombia’s first nonsectarian and politically neutral university. Modeled after American universities, its mission was to provide highly skilled professionals and leaders capable of contributing to the country's modernization and development. Classes began in 1949 with a small student body and focused on a combination of technical and liberal education aimed at fostering ethical and critical thinking aligned with national progress. Despite political violence in the 1950s, the university maintained strong ties with diverse political groups and implemented programs to send students abroad for advanced study.

The university is legally structured as a nonprofit common-utility corporation with autonomous governance including a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Academic Council, and Deans. It comprises 14 academic units including faculties and interdisciplinary centers. The campus blends renovated religious and industrial buildings with modern facilities in Bogotá’s historical center, enhanced by libraries, laboratories, and sports complexes. Notable features include academic exchange programs with over 119 universities worldwide, a mascot goat named Seneca symbolizing campus tradition, and a distinguished alumni network featuring former presidents, ministers, scientists, artists, and public figures who have significantly influenced Colombia.
