The University of the Andes (Colombia) was founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as a non-sectarian, non-politically affiliated university to meet the need for skilled professionals and leaders in post-war Colombia. It began with 80 students and a small set of faculties, and its early years were marked by a commitment to liberal education and academic freedom. The university adopted a model inspired by American institutions, offering both technical and liberal education. Over time, it expanded through the addition of new faculties, including the Faculty of Aeronautic Sciences, the Institute for Conservation of Natural Resources, and the Preparatory Lyceum of Pereira, though some initiatives were discontinued. The university also established the Cultural Extension Section, which organized academic conferences featuring prominent international speakers, and created the Female Section in 1953. 

The university's governance is structured with a Board of Trustees, a Board of Directors, a Rector (President), and academic councils, ensuring a balance between administrative oversight and academic autonomy. The university's mission emphasizes pluralism, diversity, dialogue, and academic excellence, with a focus on critical and ethical formation for students to strengthen their civic responsibilities. As of 2022, the university's statutes were last amended in 2009. The university has 14 academic units, including 10 faculties, the CIDER, the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, and the Conecta-te Center. 

The campus is located in Bogotá’s historical center and is composed of a mixture of renovated industrial and religious buildings and newly constructed facilities, covering 180,551 sq mt. It includes 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, and five satellite libraries with over 445,000 books. The campus also features a sports center, La Caneca, and various student facilities such as cafeterias, health services, and rest areas. The university has a strong international presence, with over 119 exchange programs with institutions in 34 countries. 

Notable alumni include César Gaviria (President of Colombia), María Ángela Holguín (Minister of Foreign Affairs), Fernando Botero Zea (Ministry of National Defense), and Ana María Rey (theoretical physicist). The university also has a strong faculty, with many professors holding PhDs from top universities worldwide. The university’s student population is 55% male and 45% female, with 95% of undergraduate students being among the top 1% of high school students in Colombia. 

The university's academic structure allows students to design their own curricular programs with the assistance of academic advisors, spanning four cycles: a Basic Professional Cycle, a Cycle of Elective Courses in Socio-humanistic issues, a Professional Cycle, and a Professional Complementary Cycle. International students can attend as visiting students or exchange students, and the university has a strong reputation for academic excellence and research. The university's mission and structure reflect its commitment to academic freedom, critical thinking, and social responsibility.
