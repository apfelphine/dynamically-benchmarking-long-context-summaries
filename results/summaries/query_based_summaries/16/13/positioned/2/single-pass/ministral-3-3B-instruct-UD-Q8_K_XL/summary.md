The **University of the Andes (Colombia)**, commonly referred to as **Uniandes**, was founded in 1948 as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia. Its establishment was driven by the need for skilled professionals to support Colombia’s rapid industrialization and modernization efforts following the Liberal Republic era. Uniandes was modeled after American universities, emphasizing both technical and liberal education to foster intellectual development for practical, altruistic purposes.

### **Key Features**
- **Founding & Early Years (1948–1955):** Founded by Mario Laserna Pinzón, Uniandes initially offered limited programs with a small student body. It quickly gained recognition for its academic rigor and commitment to neutrality, attracting students and faculty from diverse political backgrounds.
- **Structure & Governance:** Uniandes operates under a structured governance model with a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, and an Academic Council. It comprises 14 academic units, including 10 faculties, and employs a faculty with a high proportion of PhDs from top global universities.
- **Campus:** Located in Bogotá’s historical center, Uniandes’ campus includes a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings, with modern facilities such as classrooms, laboratories, and a central library. Notable features include **La Caneca**, a sports center, and a variety of student amenities like cafeterias and health services.

### **Academics & Reputation**
- **Admissions:** Uniandes is highly selective, with a focus on merit-based admissions. It attracts top 1% high school students from Colombia, reflecting its prestige.
- **Research & Innovation:** The university supports faculty research and innovation, with a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary studies. It has a significant endowment and collaborates with international institutions.
- **Global Recognition:** While not widely ranked among the top global universities, Uniandes is recognized for its academic excellence and contributions to Colombian education, particularly in fields like engineering, law, and social sciences.

### **Student Life & Culture**
- **Diversity:** Uniandes has a diverse student body, with a significant proportion of students coming from outside Bogotá.
- **Student Organizations:** The university fosters a vibrant student life through clubs, societies, and cultural events, though political neutrality remains a core value.
- **Notable Alumni:** Uniandes has produced leaders in politics, business, and academia, including former Colombian presidents like César Gaviria and Alejandro Gaviria.

Uniandes remains a key institution in Colombia’s higher education landscape, known for its academic rigor, commitment to pluralism, and contributions to national development.
