## University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón during a period of political and economic change in Colombia, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes) was established as the first nonsectarian and politically independent university in the country. It was created to address Colombia's urgent need for highly skilled professionals to support industrial development and modernization. Inspired by the American university model, Uniandes combined technical education with a liberal arts foundation to develop well-rounded leaders capable of contributing to national progress. Classes began with 80 students, rapidly growing amidst Colombia’s political tensions while maintaining political neutrality.

Organized as a nonprofit corporation since 1948 and legally recognized as a university in 1964, the institution is governed by a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Deans, and Academic Councils. It comprises 14 academic units including 10 faculties and several interdisciplinary centers. The Bogotá campus blends restored industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities, housing over 400 classrooms and extensive libraries. Notably, Uniandes maintains international exchange programs with over 119 universities worldwide. Its faculty largely hold PhDs from top global universities, and about 95% of undergraduates rank in the top 1% nationally. The university mascot is a goat named Seneca, rooted in campus tradition. Prominent alumni include former Colombian presidents César Gaviria and notable ministers, scholars, artists, and public figures who have significantly influenced national and international fields.
