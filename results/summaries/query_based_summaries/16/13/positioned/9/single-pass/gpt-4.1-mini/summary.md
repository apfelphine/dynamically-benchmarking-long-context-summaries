## University of the Andes (Colombia)

The University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes, Uniandes) was founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and others during a period of political and social transformation in Colombia, following decades of Conservative rule and amidst the Liberal Republic’s modernization efforts. It was established as the country’s first nonsectarian and politically neutral university, with the mission to provide highly skilled professionals trained abroad and equipped to lead Colombia’s industrial and social development. Classes began in 1949 with 80 students in a limited number of faculties, adopting an American university model that combined technical and liberal education aimed at fostering critical and ethical awareness.

Throughout its history, the university has maintained an emphasis on academic excellence, diversity, and independence, governed by a Board of Trustees and various administrative bodies. It comprises 14 academic units, including 10 faculties and several specialized schools and centers. Uniandes is located in Bogotá’s historic center on a campus mixing restored industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities, including laboratories, libraries, and sports infrastructure. The student body is predominantly composed of top-ranking Colombian high school graduates, with significant representation from outside Bogotá and a gender ratio of about 55% male to 45% female. The institution maintains an extensive international exchange network with over 119 universities worldwide, facilitating visiting and exchange student programs. The university’s mascot is a goat named Seneca, symbolizing its foundational history and student culture. Notable alumni include former Colombian presidents, government ministers, artists, and scientists such as César Gaviria and Alejandro Gaviria.
