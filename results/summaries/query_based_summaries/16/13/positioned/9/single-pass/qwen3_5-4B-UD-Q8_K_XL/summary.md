The University of the Andes (Colombia) was founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia, following the assassination of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán, which inspired its founders to create an institution for national development. Established with American models, the university began classes in March 1949 with 80 students and quickly expanded to over 400 applicants by 1950. Modeled after American universities, it offers an interdisciplinary curriculum with four cycles: Basic Professional, Socio-humanistic electives, Professional, and Professional Complementary. The campus, located in Bogotá's historical center, covers approximately 180,551 square meters, featuring renovated religious and industrial buildings, a central library, and specialized satellite libraries holding over 445,000 books.

As of 2022, the university is structured with 14 academic units, including 10 faculties, and employs 745 non-faculty staff within academic units plus 853 in administrative units. Its assets totaled approximately COL$1.74 trillion (US$426.1 million) as of December 2020. The student population is 55% male, with 30% originating from outside Bogotá, and 95% of undergraduates are top 1% high school graduates. The university is highly international, with academic exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries. Notable alumni include former President César Gaviria, painter Fernando Botero, and architect Jorge Luis Vaca Forero. The university maintains a mission of academic excellence, pluralism, and critical ethical formation, while its mascot is the goat Seneca.
