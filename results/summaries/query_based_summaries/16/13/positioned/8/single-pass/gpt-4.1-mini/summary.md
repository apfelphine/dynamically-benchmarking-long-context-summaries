# Summary: University of the Andes (Colombia)

The University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes) in Colombia was founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and other intellectual elites during a period of political liberalization and economic growth, responding to a national need for skilled professionals to support industrial development. Modeled after the American university system, Uniandes started with a focus on providing a broad education that combined technical expertise with liberal arts, aiming to cultivate leaders for Colombia’s modernization. Classes began in 1949 with 80 students, expanding rapidly despite the country's political violence by maintaining political neutrality and fostering international academic partnerships, including a notable early three/two program with U.S. universities, facilitating student study abroad.

Legally established as a nonprofit in 1948 and granted university status in 1964, the institution is governed by a Board of Trustees, Rector, Academic Council, and several faculties and interdisciplinary centers. Its mission emphasizes academic excellence, pluralism, and social responsibility toward Colombia’s development. The campus in Bogotá blends renovated industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities such as laboratories, libraries, and sports centers. The student body is highly selective, with 95% of undergraduates coming from the top 1% of Colombian high school graduates, and the faculty mostly holding doctorates from prestigious global universities. The university offers a cross-disciplinary curriculum, has extensive international exchange programs with over 119 universities worldwide, and holds substantial financial assets supporting its educational and research activities. Notable alumni include prominent political leaders, ministers, academics, artists, and professionals influential in Colombia and beyond.
