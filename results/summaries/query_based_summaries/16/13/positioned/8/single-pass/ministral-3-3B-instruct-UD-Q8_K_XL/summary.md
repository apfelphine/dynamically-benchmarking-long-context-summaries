The **University of the Andes (Colombia)**, officially known as *Universidad de los Andes*, was founded in 1949 as Colombia’s first nonsectarian and politically independent university. Established in response to the need for skilled professionals to drive the country’s post-war industrialization and modernization, it was modeled after American universities and aimed to provide both technical and liberal education. The university’s founding principles emphasized critical thinking, ethical formation, and civic responsibility, aligning with Colombia’s evolving social and economic demands.

Located in Bogotá’s historic center, the university’s campus blends restored industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities, housing over 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, and five specialized libraries. Its academic structure includes 14 academic units: 10 faculties, the CIDER (Center for Interdisciplinary Studies on Development), the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, Conecta-te (Center for Innovation in Technology and Education), and the Dean of Students. The university offers interdisciplinary programs, allowing students to design their own curricula, and has partnerships with over 119 universities worldwide for exchange programs.

The University of the Andes is known for its commitment to academic excellence, fostering a diverse student body with 95% of undergraduates being the top 1% of high school graduates. It has a strong focus on research, innovation, and social responsibility, with notable contributions in fields like engineering, medicine, and humanities. The university’s mission is to cultivate critical and ethical leaders who contribute to Colombia’s development and global engagement.
