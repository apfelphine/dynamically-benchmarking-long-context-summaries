The University of the Andes (Colombia) was founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as a non-sectarian, non-politically affiliated institution to address the need for skilled professionals in post-war industrialization. It began with 80 students and a small number of faculties, modeled after American universities, and expanded through the 1950s with a focus on technical and liberal education. The university established international exchange programs and hosted academic conferences with prominent figures like John von Neumann and Arnold J. Toynbee. It maintains a strong commitment to academic excellence, critical thinking, and civic responsibility, as outlined in its mission to foster pluralism, dialogue, and ethical formation. The campus is located in Bogotá’s historical center, featuring renovated industrial and religious buildings and modern academic facilities, including 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, and five satellite libraries. The university’s mascot, Seneca the goat, symbolizes its history and student community, with an annual celebration of "Students' Day" featuring a goat. The university has a diverse student body, with 55% male and 45% female, and 95% of undergraduates being top 1% high school students. It offers 171 undergraduate programs and 143 postgraduate programs across 14 academic units. The university is known for its interdisciplinary academic structure, allowing students to design their own curricula, and has a strong international presence through over 119 exchange agreements. Notable alumni include Colombian political leaders such as César Gaviria and María Ángela Holguín, as well as prominent figures in arts, science, and public service.
