# University of the Andes (Colombia) – Summary

**Institution Identity**
The text refers to the institution as **Universidad de los Andes** or **Uniandes**, located in Bogotá, Colombia. It was founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, approximately a year after his return from Columbia University. It was established as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia, modeled after the American university system. Its primary goals were to provide Colombia with professionals possessing technical knowledge to benefit national development and to prepare students to become leaders in the post-war world. Classes began on March 29, 1949, with 80 students in a small set of faculties, including a college modeled on the American university college.

**History and Administration**
Following the founding, the university maintained a stance of political neutrality despite increasing violence in the country during the 1950s. This neutrality was ensured by the inclusion of government representatives on the Board and the appointment of rectors and staff from both Conservative and Liberal parties. In 1950, a "three/two program" was established, allowing students to spend three years at Uniandes before transferring to American universities (such as MIT, Vanderbilt, and Texas) to complete their degrees; tuition for this period was supported by Colombian industries, American philanthropic institutions, and private individuals. The university was legally established as a common-utility nonprofit corporation in February 1948 and granted University status by the Colombian government in 1964. As of 2022, its statutes were ratified in 2009.

**Structure and Mission**
The university is an autonomous and independent institution governed by organs including the Board of Trustees (the supreme organ with 30–45 members), the Board of Directors, the Rector, Vice-Rectors, the Academic Council, and various Faculty Councils. The Board of Trustees sets general policies, while the Board of Directors manages administration through the PDI (a 5-year development program). The university comprises 14 academic units: 10 faculties, the CIDER (Interdisciplinary Center for Development Studies), the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, Conecta-te (Center for Innovation in Technology and Education), and the Dean of Students. These units house approximately 745 non-faculty staff as of 2021. The university's mission is to foster pluralism, diversity, and dialogue, impart critical and ethical formation to students, and strengthen their awareness of social and civic responsibilities to solve Colombia's problems.

**Campus and Demographics**
The campus is located in Bogotá's historical center, which is noted for housing most of the city's cultural interest buildings, universities, banks, government offices, and cultural meeting places. The infrastructure covers 180,551 square meters and includes approximately 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, five satellite libraries (with over 445,000 books), and 26 computer rooms offering nearly 2,000 computers. In 1956, a metallic cylindrical structure known as "La Caneca" was installed, serving as the sports center (gym, pool, and courts for tennis, squash, and football). The university also has an Internships Center for the Medicine Faculty located at the Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá. As of 2014, the faculty was 70% PhD holders from top global universities. The student population is 55% male and 45% female, with 30% coming from outside Bogotá. Notably, 95% of undergraduate students were ranked in the top 1% of high school students in the country.
