Founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the Universidad de los Andes (Colombia) stands as the nation's first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university. Modeled after the American university system, it opened its doors in March 1949 with 80 students, aiming to provide Colombia with technically skilled professionals and liberal leaders capable of addressing national development needs. Legally established as a nonprofit corporation in February 1948, it received official university status from the Colombian government in 1964. The institution is governed by a Board of Trustees and a Board of Directors, with the Rector serving as its legal representative.

Located in the historical center of Bogotá, the university's campus features a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings and newly constructed facilities covering approximately 180,000 square meters. It houses a central library and five specialized satellite libraries, along with extensive computer labs, sports centers, and health services. The academic structure is highly interdisciplinary, allowing students to design personalized curricula across four cycles: Basic Professional, Socio-humanistic Electives, Professional, and Professional Complementary. Uniandes maintains strong international ties through exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries and has produced numerous notable alumni, including President César Gaviria, Nobel laureate Ana Maria Rey, and renowned artists such as Fernando Botero.
