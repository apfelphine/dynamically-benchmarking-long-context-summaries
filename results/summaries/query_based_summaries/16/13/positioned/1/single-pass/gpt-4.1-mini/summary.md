## University of the Andes (Colombia)

The University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes), founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and other Colombian intellectual elites, is Colombia’s first nonsectarian and politically independent university. Its establishment responded to the urgent need to provide the country with highly skilled professionals to support industrial growth and national development. Modeled after the American university system, Uniandes emphasizes both technical and liberal education to nurture well-rounded leaders. The university began with around 80 students and has since expanded its academic programs significantly while maintaining political neutrality amid Colombia's turbulent mid-20th-century history. It fostered early international cooperation, including a program allowing students to study part of their degree abroad, and hosted renowned international scholars.

Administratively, Universidad de los Andes operates as an autonomous nonprofit corporation with governance structures including a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Academic Council, and Deans across its 14 academic units (10 faculties and several interdisciplinary centers). Its main campus is located in Bogotá's historical center, featuring a blend of renovated historic buildings and modern facilities, including extensive libraries with over 445,000 books, numerous laboratories, recreational and cultural spaces, and affiliated medical facilities. The university hosts a vibrant student life with facilities supporting arts, sports, and health and has an iconic mascot—a goat named Seneca—that symbolizes student culture. Internationally, Uniandes maintains exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries and strives to promote pluralism, academic excellence, and civic responsibility in its mission.
