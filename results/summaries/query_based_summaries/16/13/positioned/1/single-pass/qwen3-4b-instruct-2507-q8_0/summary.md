**University of the Andes (Colombia)**

Founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the University of the Andes (Uniandes) is Colombia’s first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university. It was established to provide technical professionals who would return to the country and contribute to national development, modeled after American universities. The university began with 80 students and expanded rapidly, with a notable early program being the "three/two" system, allowing students to complete three years at Uniandes before transferring to a U.S. university for the final two. 

Uniandes is governed by a Board of Trustees and a Board of Directors, with the rector serving as the chief executive. The university has 14 academic units, including faculties and interdisciplinary centers, and is known for its emphasis on academic excellence, critical thinking, and civic responsibility. It operates in Bogotá’s historical center and includes a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings, with 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, and over 445,000 books in its library system. 

The student population is 55% male and 45% female, with 95% of undergraduates being the top 1% of their high school class. The university offers a flexible academic structure allowing students to design their own curricula across four cycles: Basic Professional, Socio-humanistic Electives, Professional, and Professional Complementary. 

Uniandes has over 119 international exchange partnerships with universities in 34 countries, including notable agreements with Germany, Canada, and the United States. It is recognized for its research in bioenergy, biotechnology, climate change, and public policy, and has been ranked among the top public universities in Colombia. Notable alumni include César Gaviria (former President of Colombia), María Ángela Holguín (Minister of Foreign Affairs), and Fernando Botero Zea (Minister of National Defense). The university also hosts a strong faculty, including prominent academics like Sadhan Kumar Adhikari and Mauro Galetti.
