## University of the Andes (Colombia) – Summary

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and other intellectuals, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes or Uniandes) is Colombia's first nonsectarian, non-politically affiliated university. Created to provide internationally oriented higher education and technical training for national development, Uniandes adopted a model inspired by American universities, emphasizing both liberal and technical education. From its inception, the university maintained political neutrality and broad representation from Colombian society, and established international academic exchange programs with institutions such as the University of Illinois, MIT, and others.

Uniandes is based in Bogotá’s historic center and features a campus comprising renovated historical buildings and new facilities, including libraries with over 445,000 volumes and extensive computing resources. The university has 10 faculties and several interdisciplinary centers, supports a diverse student population (including 30% from outside Bogotá), and encourages interdisciplinary study. Its governance structure includes a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, and Academic Council. Uniandes emphasizes pluralism, academic excellence, and ethical responsibility, with most faculty holding doctorates from leading universities abroad. The campus also includes sports and health facilities, operates a joint internship center for medical students, and is known for its mascot, the goat "Seneca." Its alumni include national presidents, ministers, artists, scientists, and notable figures in Colombian society.
