The University of the Andes (Colombia) was founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as a non-sectarian and non-politically affiliated university to address Colombia's need for skilled professionals and to foster leadership in post-war development. It began with 80 students and a small faculty, and over time expanded through academic programs, international partnerships, and the establishment of 14 academic units, including faculties, a research center, and a school of government. The university is located in Bogotá and is known for its interdisciplinary academic structure, allowing students to design their own curricula across four cycles. It emphasizes academic excellence, critical and ethical education, and social responsibility. The university has a strong international presence, with over 119 exchange partnerships worldwide, and is recognized for its commitment to pluralism, diversity, and dialogue. Its campus includes renovated industrial and religious buildings and modern facilities, with a total of 180,551 sq m of infrastructure. The university's mascot, Seneca, a goat, symbolizes its founding and history. Notable alumni include political leaders such as César Gaviria and María Ángela Holguín, as well as professionals in medicine, law, and the arts. The university is governed by a Board of Trustees, a Board of Directors, and a Rector, with a mission to promote civic responsibility and social engagement.
