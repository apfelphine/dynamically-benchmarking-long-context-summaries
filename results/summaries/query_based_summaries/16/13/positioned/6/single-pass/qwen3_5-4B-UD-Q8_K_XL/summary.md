# University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the Universidad de los Andes was established as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia, modeled after the American university system. Classes commenced on March 29, 1949, with 80 students, aiming to produce professionals capable of leading the nation post-war. The university achieved official university status in 1964 and is governed by a Board of Trustees (30–45 members), a Board of Directors, and an Academic Council. Its academic structure includes 10 faculties, the CIDER (Interdisciplinary Center for Development Studies), the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, and Conecta-te (Center for Innovation in Technology and Education). The institution is legally a common-utility nonprofit corporation with an asset value exceeding COL$1.74 trillion (approx. US$426 million) as of 2020.

Located in Bogotá's historical center, the campus is a mixture of renovated industrial and religious buildings, including a convent and a former women's prison, alongside newly constructed facilities covering approximately 180,551 square meters. The campus features 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, and five specialized satellite libraries holding over 445,000 books. Notable on-site amenities include the "La Caneca," a metallic cylindrical sports center with a gym and pool, and health services. The university boasts a diverse faculty, with 70% of its professors holding PhDs from top global universities, and a student population that is 55% male and 45% female. Approximately 30% of students originate from outside Bogotá, and 95% of undergraduates are from the top 1% of high school graduates in the country. The university maintains international exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries and is known for its emphasis on pluralism, diversity, and the critical analysis of Colombia's social and civic problems.
