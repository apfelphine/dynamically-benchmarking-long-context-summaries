## University of the Andes (Colombia) Summary

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and elite intellectuals, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes, or Uniandes) was established as Colombia’s first secular, nonsectarian, and non-politically affiliated university, with a mission to modernize higher education and provide technically skilled professionals to support the country's development. Classes commenced in March 1949, initially with 80 students, and quickly expanded academic offerings, including unique programs like the three/two program with U.S. universities, enabling students to complete studies abroad. Despite national political turbulence, Uniandes maintained a stance of political neutrality and cultivated close relationships across the political spectrum, helping it remain a stable and innovative institution.

Legally designated a nonprofit corporation and granted university status in 1964, Uniandes is governed by a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, and other academic councils. As of 2022, the university included 14 academic units and 20 administrative units, serving a diverse student body, 30% of whom come from outside Bogotá, with 95% of undergraduates being top achievers. Uniandes espouses pluralism and academic excellence, has an interdisciplinary structure allowing students to shape their curricula, and boasts a highly qualified faculty (70% with PhDs from leading universities). Its urban campus mixes restored historic buildings with new facilities, offers comprehensive resources—including libraries, sports centers, and technology hubs—and features traditions like the Seneca goat mascot. Notable alumni include top government officials, scientists, artists, and entrepreneurs.
