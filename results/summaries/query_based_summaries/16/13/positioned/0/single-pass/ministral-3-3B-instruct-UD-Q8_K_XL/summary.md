The **University of the Andes (Colombia)**, founded in 1949, is Colombia’s first non-sectarian and politically neutral university. Established by intellectuals and elites, its mission was to produce skilled professionals and leaders for the post-war nation, modeled after American universities. Initially housed in rented spaces, it expanded rapidly, offering interdisciplinary education and fostering innovation through partnerships with international institutions.

The university operates under a governance structure with a **Board of Trustees**, **Board of Directors**, and a **Rector**, overseeing 14 academic units, including faculties, interdisciplinary centers, and specialized schools. Its **Statutes** emphasize academic excellence, critical thinking, and social responsibility, fostering pluralism and diversity. The university is renowned for its **critical and ethical formation**, preparing students for civic engagement and problem-solving.

**Academically**, the University of the Andes is structured into four cycles: a **Basic Professional Cycle**, a **Cycle of Elective Courses in Socio-humanistic Issues**, a **Professional Cycle**, and a **Professional Complementary Cycle**. Students can design their curricula with academic advisors, offering flexibility in interdisciplinary studies. The university is known for its **highly qualified faculty**, with 70% holding PhDs from top global universities.

**Student demographics** reflect its elite and diverse intake, with 95% of undergraduates being the top 1% of high school students. The university attracts students from outside Bogotá (30%) and maintains a gender balance of 55% male and 45% female. It hosts over **119 international exchange programs** across 34 countries, promoting global engagement.

**Campus life** includes a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings, housing over **180,000 sq. meters** of infrastructure, including classrooms, laboratories, libraries, and sports facilities like **La Caneca**, a sports center. The university also operates an **Internships Center** for the Medicine Faculty in Bogotá’s northern region.

**Notable traditions** include the adoption of a goat named **Seneca** as a mascot, symbolizing the university’s spirit, and the celebration of **"Students' Day"** with a goat brought to campus. The university has produced leaders in politics, business, and academia, with alumni holding prominent roles in Colombia’s institutions. Its **global reputation** and commitment to innovation and ethical education continue to define its legacy.
