The University of the Andes (Colombia), known locally as Uniandes, was founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in Colombia. Established amidst a backdrop of Liberal Republic modernization efforts and political tension, the university aimed to provide Colombia with skilled professionals capable of leading the nation in the post-war world. Classes commenced on March 29, 1949, with a small enrollment modeled after the American university system, offering both technical and liberal education. By the second academic year, demand had surged, and the university quickly adopted a policy of political neutrality while maintaining good relations with various Colombian administrations through its inclusive Board of Trustees.

Organized as a common-utility nonprofit corporation legally established in 1948 and granted university status in 1964, Uniandes operates with a governance structure including a Board of Trustees, a Board of Directors, a Rector, and an Academic Council. The institution comprises 14 academic units, including 10 faculties and specialized schools, and serves as a major center for research and international exchange, with programs available to over 119 universities worldwide. As of 2022, the university's statutes were last ratified in 2009, and its assets were valued at approximately $426.1 million USD in 2020. The campus, located in Bogotá's historical center, features a mix of renovated religious and industrial buildings, extensive facilities including a central library with over 445,000 books, and sports complexes. The university is renowned for its diverse alumni, including former Presidents of Colombia and prominent figures in government, business, and the arts, and maintains a reputation for academic excellence, pluralism, and ethical formation.
