The University of the Andes (Uniandes) in Colombia is a private, nonsectarian, and non-political institution founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as the first university of its kind in the country. Modeled after the American university system, it began operations in March 1949 with 80 students and a small set of faculties, aiming to provide professionals with technical knowledge and a liberal education to foster national development. Throughout the 1950s, the university maintained political neutrality while establishing an international exchange program and recruiting foreign professors to enhance its academic standing.

As of 2022, Uniandes is governed by a Board of Trustees, a Board of Directors, and an Academic Council, with assets valued at approximately US$426.1 million. The university comprises 14 academic units, including 10 faculties and specialized centers such as the CIDER and the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, serving a diverse student population where 30% originate outside Bogotá. Its campus, located in Bogotá's historical center, features a mixture of restored industrial and religious buildings alongside modern facilities, including the "La Caneca" sports center. The university boasts a prestigious reputation, with notable alumni including former President César Gaviria and Nobel laureate Tu Youyou.
