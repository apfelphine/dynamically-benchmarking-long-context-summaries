The University of the Andes (Colombia) was founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as a non-sectarian and non-politically affiliated university to address Colombia's need for skilled professionals and leaders. It began with 80 students and a small set of faculties, evolving into a major institution with 14 academic units, including the CIDER, the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, and the Conecta-te center. The university's mission emphasizes academic excellence, critical and ethical formation, and social responsibility, fostering pluralism, diversity, and dialogue. It operates in Bogotá's historical center, featuring a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings and newly constructed facilities, with 180,551 square meters of infrastructure. The campus includes 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, and five satellite libraries, housing over 445,000 books. The university is known for its interdisciplinary academic structure, allowing students to design personalized curricula. It has an international student program and academic exchange partnerships with over 119 universities in 34 countries. The university's faculty includes many prominent scholars and researchers, and its notable alumni include César Gaviria (President of Colombia), María Ángela Holguín (Ministry of Foreign Affairs), and Fernando Botero Zea (Ministry of National Defense). The mascot, Seneca, a goat, is a symbol of the university's history and is celebrated annually on "Students' Day." The university has a student population of 55% male and 45% female, with 95% of undergraduates being top 1% high school students. It is recognized for its strong academic and research output, with a focus on social sciences, humanities, and public policy.
