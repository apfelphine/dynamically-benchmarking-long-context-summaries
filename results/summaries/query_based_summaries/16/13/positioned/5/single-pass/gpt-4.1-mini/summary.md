**University of the Andes (Colombia)**

Founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and other intellectuals during Colombia's Liberal Republic period, the University of the Andes (Universidad de los Andes) was established as the country’s first nonsectarian, politically neutral university. Its mission was to foster modernization and produce professionals who could contribute to Colombia’s industrial and social development, modeled after the American university system. It started classes in 1949 with 80 students and expanded rapidly, including initiatives like the "three/two" program with U.S. universities allowing students to study partly abroad. Despite Colombia's political violence in the 1950s, the university maintained neutrality and benefitted from government and private sector support, particularly via scholarships managed by ICETEX.

Operating as a nonprofit corporation with university status granted in 1964, the institution is governed by a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, and Academic Council overseeing 14 academic units including faculties and research centers. As of 2021, it has a substantial administrative staff and assets valued at approximately US$426 million. The university emphasizes pluralism, academic excellence, and ethical instruction aimed at social responsibility. Its campus in Bogotá combines renovated industrial and religious buildings with modern facilities, including extensive libraries and sports amenities. The student body is composed mainly of Colombia’s top high school graduates, with strong support for international exchange. The mascot is a goat named Seneca, symbolizing university spirit. Notable alumni include several Colombian presidents, ministers, academics, artists, and public figures.
