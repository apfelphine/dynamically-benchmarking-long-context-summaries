# University of the Andes (Colombia) Summary

Founded in 1948 and beginning classes in 1949, the University of the Andes (Uniandes) in Bogotá is a private, nonsectarian institution modeled after American universities, emphasizing academic excellence, social responsibility, and political neutrality. Its early years included pioneering curricular innovations, such as interdisciplinary studies and international exchange programs with U.S. institutions, supported by government and private grants. Uniandes' campus blends restored religious and industrial buildings with modern facilities, covering over 180,000 square meters and featuring more than 100 classrooms, numerous laboratories, libraries, and sports amenities. Located in Bogotá's historical center, the university also operates a specialized medical internship center in collaboration with Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá.

Uniandes is governed by a Board of Trustees (including professors and students) that sets general policies, and a Board of Directors managing administration and strategic plans. The academic framework includes 14 units, among them 10 faculties and specialized centers focusing on interdisciplinary studies and innovation in technology and education. Faculty members are predominantly full-time with 70% holding doctorates from top global institutions. The student body is highly selective, with 95% of undergraduates from Colombia's top 1% of high school students, a 55% male and 45% female split, and about 30% from outside Bogotá. The university fosters interdisciplinary curricula, allowing students to design personalized programs across four academic cycles, and maintains exchange agreements with over 119 universities in 34 countries. The university mascot is "Seneca" the goat, a historic symbol cherished by the community.
