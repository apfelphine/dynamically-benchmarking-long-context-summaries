# University of the Andes (Colombia) - Summary

The University of the Andes (Uniandes), founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and intellectuals from Colombia’s traditional parties, was established as the first nonsectarian and politically neutral university in Colombia. It aimed to provide the country with technically skilled professionals educated abroad and prepared to contribute to national development, alongside a liberal education fostering intellectual growth and leadership. The university began academic activities in 1949 with a small student body, expanding rapidly despite Colombia’s political violence. It developed innovative academic programs like the three/two program which allowed students to complete degrees partly abroad in the U.S., supported by various industrial and philanthropic partners.

Uniandes’ Bogotá campus integrates renovated historical religious and industrial buildings with modern facilities, covering over 180,000 square meters and housing extensive libraries, computer labs, and sports centers including the iconic "La Caneca." The campus is located in the city's cultural and business historic center and includes an Internship Center for the Medicine Faculty in partnership with Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá. Its governance is defined by a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Academic Council, and Deans, reflecting a structured organization codified in its statutes. The university’s mascot is “Seneca,” a goat symbolizing campus tradition. Notable alumni include Colombian presidents, ministers, scientists, artists, and beauty queens, underscoring its influence on national leadership and culture.
