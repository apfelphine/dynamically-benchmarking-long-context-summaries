## University of the Andes (Colombia) — Key Summary

The University of the Andes (Uniandes), founded in 1948 in Bogotá by Mario Laserna Pinzón and a group of intellectuals, was established as Colombia’s first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university. Created in response to the country’s need for technical professionals and leaders, it was modeled on the American university system, emphasizing both technical and liberal education. Its foundational mission aimed to foster academic excellence, pluralism, social responsibility, and critical thinking for the benefit of Colombian society. Political neutrality and inclusivity were central to its administration from the beginning, with representatives from multiple political backgrounds ensuring institutional autonomy.

The campus, located in Bogotá’s historic center, features a blend of renovated religious and industrial buildings alongside modern facilities, covering 180,551 sq meters and including classrooms, laboratories, libraries, sports centers (notably La Caneca), cafeterias, and health services. The governance structure comprises a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Vice-Rectors, Academic Council, and Deans. Academically, Uniandes promotes an interdisciplinary approach with 14 units (including ten faculties) and cycles integrating basic, professional, and socio-humanistic studies. The university maintains high academic standards—with a faculty of predominantly full-time academics, most with PhDs from top institutions, and a student body recognized for its excellence (95% of undergraduates were top high school students nationally). Internationalization is facilitated by exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries. Uniandes honors its mascot, Seneca the goat, as a symbol of the institution’s character and traditions. Among its notable alumni are national leaders, artists, scientists, and distinguished professionals who have shaped Colombia’s development.
