The University of the Andes (Colombia), commonly known as Uniandes, is an autonomous, independent, and non-sectarian institution founded in 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón and others to meet Colombia's urgent need for skilled professionals and leaders. Modeled after American universities, it emphasizes pluralism, academic excellence, and the critical analysis of social problems. The university's governance is structured with a Board of Trustees as the supreme organ, a Board of Directors managing administration, and a Rector who serves as the legal representative, overseeing the implementation of a five-year development plan. Academically, Uniandes operates through 14 academic units, including ten faculties, the CIDER, the Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government, and Conecta-te, supported by 20 administrative units and a significant non-faculty staff.

The campus is located in Bogotá's historical center and features a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings, such as a former convent and prison, alongside newly constructed facilities covering over 180,000 square meters. It includes five satellite libraries, numerous laboratories, and sports facilities like La Caneca. Historically, the university began with limited resources but quickly expanded, establishing international exchange programs in the 1950s and fostering a diverse student body where 95% of undergraduates are top 1% high school graduates. Notable alumni include former Presidents of Colombia César Gaviria and María Ángela Holguín, as well as prominent figures like Gabriel García Márquez (though not listed in the specific alumni text provided, the text lists Natalia Castañeda Arbelaez, César Gaviria, and others) and artists Fernando Botero and Luis Caballero. Financially, the university held assets valued at approximately $426.1 million USD as of 2020.
