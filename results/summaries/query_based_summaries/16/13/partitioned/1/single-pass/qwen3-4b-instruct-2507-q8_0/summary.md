The University of the Andes (Colombia) is a private, non-sectarian, and politically neutral institution founded in 1949 in Bogotá. It began with 80 students and was initially housed in rented buildings from the Community of the Good Shepherd, later purchasing land and restoring religious structures. The campus spans 180,551 sq meters and includes 116 classrooms, 219 laboratories, a central library, five satellite libraries, 26 computer rooms, and over 445,000 books. It features a sports center known as La Caneca, a gym, pool, and various courts. The university is located in Bogotá’s historical center, near cultural and governmental institutions.

Its governance is structured with a Board of Trustees (30–45 members, including 2 professors and 2 students), a Board of Directors, a Rector (chief executive), and an Academic Council. The university has 14 academic units, including faculties and specialized schools. As of 2014, 70% of faculty hold PhDs from top global universities. The student body is 55% male, 45% female, with 95% of undergraduates being top 1% high school students in the country. The academic structure allows students to design interdisciplinary curricula across four cycles: Basic Professional, Elective Socio-humanistic, Professional, and Professional Complementary.

International students can attend as visiting students (3 courses per semester) or exchange students (2–6 courses per semester). The university has academic exchange programs with over 119 institutions in 34 countries. The university’s mission emphasizes pluralism, diversity, dialogue, academic excellence, and ethical development, aiming to cultivate socially and civically responsible graduates. Notable alumni include Colombian presidents, ministers, artists, scientists, and public figures. The university’s mascot is Seneca, a goat inspired by the Roman Stoic philosopher, celebrated annually on Students' Day.
