Founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the University of the Andes (Uniandes) in Colombia was established as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in the nation, aiming to provide skilled professionals and liberal education to foster national development. Modeled after the American university system, it quickly gained prominence through its "three/two" program, which allowed students to study in the United States for three years at no cost. By the early 1950s, the university had expanded its academic structure, including the establishment of a Female Section in 1953, and maintained a policy of political neutrality that ensured good relations with various Colombian administrations.

The university is organized into 14 academic units, including 10 faculties, and operates out of a main campus in Bogotá's historical center, covering approximately 180,551 square meters. Its facilities include specialized satellite libraries, extensive computer rooms, and the "La Caneca," a sports center with a gym and pools. Uniandes is known for its interdisciplinary approach, which allows students to design their own curricular programs, and it boasts a strong international presence with exchange programs and academic ties to over 119 universities worldwide. Notable alumni include former President César Gaviria and renowned figures such as painter Fernando Botero and architect Jorge Luis Vaca Forero.
