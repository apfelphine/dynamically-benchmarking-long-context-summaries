Education in New Zealand is a free, compulsory, and secular national system for all citizens and permanent residents, covering primary and secondary schooling from age 5 to 19. The system is primarily composed of state schools (government-owned and funded), state-integrated schools (former private schools that remain religiously affiliated and charge compulsory attendance dues), and private schools. Students follow the New Zealand Curriculum or the Māori-medium Te Marautanga o Aotearoa, with the latter used in Māori-medium schools. While there is no middle school, primary education (Years 1–8) often transitions to secondary education (Years 9–13), though some schools offer full-range programs from Year 1 to 13. Enrollment schemes exist in urban areas to manage overcrowding, prioritizing students within a school's "home zone" or through a lottery.

Tertiary education in New Zealand is regulated within the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and ranges from informal community courses to advanced research-based degrees. Funding is a mix of government subsidies, tuition fees, and student assistance through Student Loans and Allowances. A significant focus has been placed on Māori language revitalization, offering immersion programs (Kura Kaupapa Māori) and subjects in Māori-medium schools to counteract historical suppression of the language. Despite these efforts, the system faces challenges regarding academic performance, with international assessments showing gaps in numeracy and literacy compared to other developed nations, as well as persistent issues with school bullying and the need to improve educational standards.
