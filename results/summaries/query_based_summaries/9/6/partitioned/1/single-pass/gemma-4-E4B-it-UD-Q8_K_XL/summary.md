## Education in New Zealand

**School Structure and Types:**
New Zealand's education system provides free primary and secondary schooling to citizens and permanent residents, compulsory between ages 6 and 16. The system includes state schools (government-owned and funded), state-integrated schools (former private schools integrated into the state system but retaining a "special character"), and private schools. Primary education traditionally spans Years 1 to 8 (with Years 7 and 8 being intermediate), while secondary education covers Years 9 to 13. National curriculum is followed by state and state integrated schools, with the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools.

**Curriculum and Funding:**
Funding for state and state integrated schools is per-student, with additional funds provided based on a school's socio-economic decile rating. State-integrated schools charge "attendance dues" to cover privately owned land and buildings. Tertiary education funding involves government subsidies based on student enrollment and study time, complemented by Student Loans and Student Allowances for low-income students. Māori language education is supported through immersion programs like Kura Kaupapa Māori, recognizing Māori as an official language.

**Educational Standards and Concerns:**
Despite international survey results showing varied rankings, concerns persist regarding student performance in mathematics and general academic achievement. Furthermore, bullying is a recognized issue, with recommendations leading to compulsory anti-bullying programs.
