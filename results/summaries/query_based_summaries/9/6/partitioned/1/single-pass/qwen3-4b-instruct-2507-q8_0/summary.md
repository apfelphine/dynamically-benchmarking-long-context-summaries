**Education in New Zealand** involves a structured system with primary and secondary education divided into various school types based on funding and function. State schools are publicly funded and free, while state-integrated schools, which retain a religious or specialized "special character," charge attendance dues to cover land and building costs. Private schools rely on tuition fees. Alternative schooling includes charter schools (now fully integrated into state system) and home-schooling, which is permitted under specific conditions. 

Schools are categorized by year levels: primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13), with intermediate (Years 7–8) schools serving as a transitional phase. The national curriculum (NZC) is followed in English-medium schools, with Māori-medium schools using Te Marautanga o Aotearoa. Māori language education is actively promoted through immersion schools (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa), and efforts have led to improved outcomes for Māori students in these programs.

The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms with breaks in between. School funding is based on per-student allocations, with additional support for low-decile schools and special needs students via the Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS). State schools may request voluntary "school fees" for extras, while state-integrated schools charge mandatory attendance dues. 

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and includes degrees from universities, polytechnics, and other institutions. Funding combines government subsidies and student fees, with student loans and allowances available to assist with costs. 

Education faces challenges such as declining academic performance in math and science, as seen in international rankings, and persistent issues with bullying, including cyberbullying and physical violence. Despite these, there is a strong emphasis on Māori language and cultural revitalization, and efforts to improve student well-being and equity are ongoing.
