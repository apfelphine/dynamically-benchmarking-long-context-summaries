New Zealand’s education system is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with an optional **Year 13** for advanced students. Primary education is compulsory for all children from age 5, while secondary education is compulsory until age 16, though most students continue until at least 17. The system includes **state schools** (free, government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (formerly private, now government-funded with attendance dues), and **private schools** (tuition-based).

The curriculum follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)**, with core subjects including English, mathematics, science, social sciences, and technology. Students typically earn qualifications like the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)** in Years 11–13, though some schools offer **Cambridge International Examinations (CIE)** or the **International Baccalaureate (IB)**. Māori language education is available through **Māori-medium schools** (full immersion) or as a subject in English-medium schools, reflecting efforts to preserve and integrate Māori cultural heritage.

Key features include:
- **Māori education**: Recognized through **kura kaupapa Māori** (full immersion) and **kōhanga reo** (early childhood immersion).
- **Special education**: Students with disabilities receive support through **Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS)** funding, with some attending specialist schools.
- **Home schooling**: Parents can apply for exemptions if they prove their child is taught "as regularly and as well as in a registered school."
- **Vocational training**: Integrated into the curriculum, with options for practical skills in agriculture, technology, and trades.
- **Funding**: State and state-integrated schools receive per-student funding, with additional support for low-decile schools. Private schools rely on tuition fees.

The system emphasizes **equity and inclusion**, though challenges remain in achieving parity for Māori and Pacific Island students. Recent reforms focus on improving academic standards, particularly in mathematics, and addressing bullying and behavioral issues.
