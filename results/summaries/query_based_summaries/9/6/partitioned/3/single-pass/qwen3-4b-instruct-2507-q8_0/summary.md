# Education in New Zealand

## Funding
State and state-integrated schools receive per-student government funding, with additional support for smaller schools and schools in lower socio-economic deciles. State-integrated schools charge "attendance dues" to cover land and building costs. Private schools rely on tuition fees, with government funding acting as a partial tax rebate. Special needs students receive Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding for curriculum adaptation, teacher aides, and equipment.

## Primary and Secondary Education
All citizens are entitled to free primary and secondary education from age 5 to 19. The system has 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13) education. Students transition to secondary school at age 12–13. The national curriculum (NZC) or Māori-medium curriculum (Te Marautanga o Aotearoa) is followed. Students pursue NCEA, with options for Cambridge or IB. School years run from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms.

## Types of Schools
- **State schools**: Government-funded, free, with no tuition. 
- **State-integrated schools**: Former private schools retaining a special character (e.g., Catholic) and charging attendance dues. 
- **Private schools**: Rely on tuition fees, typically NZ$20,000/year. 
- **Home-schooling**: Permitted with proof of adequate instruction and a government grant.

## Māori Language in Education
Māori language education has been revived since the 1980s. The 1987 Māori Language Act recognized Māori as an official language. Schools offer Māori-medium immersion (kōhanga reo, kura kaupapa) or Māori language in English-medium schools. Enrollment in Māori language programs has remained stable, with kura kaupapa showing better academic outcomes for Māori students.

## Tertiary Education
Funded through government subsidies and student fees. Students access tuition grants, student loans, and allowances. Scholarships are available to promising students, and employers may assist with study. Student loans cover fees and living costs, with interest waived for those living in New Zealand.

## Educational Standards
New Zealand’s performance in international assessments (TIMSS) declined from 18th to 34th in 2012, with poor results in basic arithmetic. Critics argue teaching methods focus on multiple problem-solving strategies without foundational knowledge. However, the Learning Curve report ranked New Zealand 8th globally, citing strong teaching quality and a culture focused on learning. Māori and Pacific students lag behind Pākehā in academic achievement.

## School Bullying
Bullying is widespread, with 20% of high school students reporting cyber-bullying and high rates of physical bullying internationally. The Ombudsman investigated serious incidents and recommended mandatory anti-bullying policies. The government introduced a Positive Behaviour for Learning plan, though results were unsatisfactory.

## Curriculum and Assessment
The national curriculum covers eight learning areas, with subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social sciences. Students in Years 11–13 have greater subject choice, with English or Māori required for literacy and numeracy. Assessment is through NCEA, with optional Cambridge or IB qualifications.

## Teacher Education and Workforce
Teachers are paid directly by the Ministry of Education, with salaries based on qualifications, experience, and workload. Teacher education programs emphasize practical skills and subject knowledge. Teacher satisfaction is moderate, with some stress on workload and classroom management.

## Special Education
Students with special needs attend day specialist schools or receive support in mainstream schools. ORS funding supports curriculum adaptation, teacher aides, and equipment. Special education is integrated into mainstream schools through support services. 

## School Times and Holidays
School days start between 8:00–9:00 am and end around 3:00 pm. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, with four terms and two-week breaks. Summer holidays last six weeks. 

## Manual Training and Vocational Education
Manual training schools were established in the late 19th century but were phased out post-World War II. Manual and technical training is now part of the curriculum. 

## International and Alternative Education
New Zealand offers international and alternative education through charter schools (now integrated), correspondence schools, and special schools. Home-schooling is permitted with government support.
