## Education in New Zealand Summary

**Primary and Secondary Education:**
All New Zealand citizens and residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from age 5 until the calendar year following their 19th birthday, with compulsory education spanning ages 6 to 16. The national curriculum is followed by state and state integrated schools, with the curriculum being the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Schools are categorized as state-owned, state integrated (which may charge compulsory "attendance dues"), or private, which rely mainly on tuition fees. While state and state integrated schools receive government funding per student, low-decile schools receive additional support. Māori language education is available both as a subject within English-medium schools and through immersion in Māori-medium schools like Kura Kaupapa Māori.

**Funding and Tertiary Education:**
Funding for state and state integrated schools includes per-student allocations, with low-decile schools receiving more. State schools are governed by elected Boards of Trustees. In tertiary education, funding is a combination of government subsidies and student fees; enrolled students can access Student Loans and Student Allowances. Special needs students are entitled to Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding.

**Curriculum and Issues:**
The system has faced challenges regarding educational standards, with international surveys showing previous underperformance in mathematics. Bullying is identified as a widespread issue in New Zealand schools.
