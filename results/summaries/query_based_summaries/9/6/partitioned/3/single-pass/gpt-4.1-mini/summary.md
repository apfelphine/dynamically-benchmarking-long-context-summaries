# Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has deep roots in Māori traditions, with early Māori schools (wānanga) focusing on tribal knowledge taught by elders (tohunga). European-style formal schooling began in 1815, initially taught in Māori, but from the late 19th century, government policies promoted English-medium instruction, resulting in a decline in Māori language use. In the late 20th century, Māori language education saw a revival with the establishment of immersive Māori-medium schooling, recognized officially under the Education Act and Treaty of Waitangi, offering programs from early childhood through full immersion at Kura Kaupapa Māori schools.

The education system provides free, compulsory schooling for ages 6 to 16, with most students attending from age 5 to 17 or beyond. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms. Primary education covers Years 1–8, with secondary education spanning Years 9–13. Schools are classified mainly as state, state-integrated (former private schools retaining special character and charging attendance dues), and private schools. State schools are secular and government-funded, with approximately 85% of students enrolled. State-integrated schools are often religiously affiliated and charge fees to maintain their premises, while private schools rely heavily on tuition fees.

The national curriculum (The New Zealand Curriculum for English-medium; Te Marautanga o Aotearoa for Māori-medium) mandates eight learning areas, with most compulsory until Year 10 and specialized qualifications from Year 11 onward, including the National Certificate of Educational Achievement. Home schooling is permitted under exemption, and alternative schooling options like correspondence schools exist for remote areas or specific needs.

Tertiary education encompasses all post-school education and training, regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, ranging from informal community courses to postgraduate degrees.  
Funding mechanisms include government subsidies, loans, and scholarships, supporting access to tertiary study, though concerns exist around rising fees and skill shortages. Special education funding supports students with needs through dedicated schemes.

Overall, New Zealand's education system integrates Māori cultural resurgence, a structured national curriculum, diversified schooling types, and comprehensive tertiary pathways.
