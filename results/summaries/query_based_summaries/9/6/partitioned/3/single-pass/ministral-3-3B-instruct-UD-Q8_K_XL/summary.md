Education in New Zealand is structured around a **free, compulsory, and secular** system, covering primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13) education. All citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free schooling from age 5 until the end of the calendar year following their 19th birthday, with most students starting at age 5 and completing secondary education by age 17. The curriculum follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)**, covering core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies, with additional language learning recommended from Years 7–10.

Funding for state and state-integrated schools comes from the government on a per-student basis, with additional funding for socio-economically disadvantaged areas. State-integrated schools (e.g., Catholic schools) charge compulsory "attendance dues" to cover operational costs, while private schools rely on tuition fees. Tertiary education is regulated through the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, offering a range of programs from vocational training to university degrees, supported by government subsidies and student loans.

New Zealand’s education system emphasizes **inclusivity**, including provisions for students with special needs, though challenges persist in addressing disparities in academic achievement, particularly among Māori and Pacific Island students. Efforts to integrate Māori language and culture into education have gained traction, with Māori-medium schools and immersion programs expanding. The system also faces criticism for high academic pressure, with debates ongoing about balancing rigorous standards with student well-being.
