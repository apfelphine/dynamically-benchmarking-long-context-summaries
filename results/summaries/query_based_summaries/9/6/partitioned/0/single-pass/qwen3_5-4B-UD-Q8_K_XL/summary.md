Education in New Zealand is characterized by a free, compulsory, and secular state system for citizens and permanent residents, covering primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13) schooling. While attendance is mandatory between ages 6 and 16, most students remain in school until age 17. The system is predominantly state-funded, with approximately 85% of students enrolled in state schools. However, the landscape includes state-integrated schools (formerly private), which retain religious or specialist characteristics and charge compulsory attendance dues, as well as private schools funded largely by tuition fees. Funding for state schools varies by socio-economic decile, with poorer areas receiving more resources, and special needs students access additional Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding.

The curriculum is guided by *The New Zealand Curriculum* (NZC) for English-medium schools and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, covering eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, and technology. Māori language education has seen significant revival since the 1980s, with the establishment of *kōhanga reo* (language nests) and *kura kaupapa Māori* (immersion schools) recognizing Māori as an official language under the Treaty of Waitangi. Early childhood education includes licensed centers, playgroups, and *kohanga reo*, all regulated by the Ministry of Education. Tertiary education is accessible via student loans and allowances, though rising fees have sparked concerns regarding skills shortages and debt. Recent challenges include addressing the academic performance gap for Māori and Pacific Island students and improving educational outcomes in rural areas.
