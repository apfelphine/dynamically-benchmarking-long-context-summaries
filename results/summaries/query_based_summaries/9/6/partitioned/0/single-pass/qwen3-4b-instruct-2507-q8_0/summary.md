**Education in New Zealand**  

- **History**: Pre-European, Māori communities used *wānanga* for traditional knowledge transfer, primarily in the Māori language. European-style schooling began in 1815, initially taught in Māori, with the London Missionary Society establishing the first schools. By 1878, a free, compulsory, secular national system was established.  

- **Primary and Secondary Education**:  
  - All citizens have free access from age 5 to 19. Compulsory between ages 6–16, with most students staying until age 17.  
  - School years: 13 levels (Year 1 to Year 13). Primary (Years 1–8), with Years 7–8 as intermediate. Secondary (Years 9–13).  
  - Curriculum: National curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools.  
  - Students pursue NCEA from Year 11 onward; some schools offer Cambridge or IB alongside it.  

- **School Types by Funding**:  
  - **State schools**: Free, government-funded; students pay for stationery, uniforms, and trips.  
  - **State-integrated schools**: Former private schools with religious or cultural special character; charge attendance dues.  
  - **Private schools**: Fee-based; operate independently with minimal government funding.  
  - **Alternative schooling**: Charter schools (now fully integrated into state system). Home-schooling is permitted with government grants.  

- **Māori Language in Education**:  
  - Once suppressed, Māori language education was revived in the 1970s with *kōhanga reo* (language nests) and *kura kaupapa* (immersion schools).  
  - Māori language is now taught in English-medium schools (up to 50% of time) or in full immersion (51%+).  

- **Funding and Enrolment**:  
  - State schools receive per-student funding; low-decile schools get extra support.  
  - Schools may charge voluntary donations; state-integrated schools require mandatory attendance dues.  
  - Special needs students receive Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding.  

- **School Times and Structure**:  
  - School year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms with two-week breaks.  
  - Manual training was introduced in 1895 and integrated into curricula over time.  

- **Tertiary Education**:  
  - Regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.  
  - Includes degrees, diplomas, and vocational training.  

- **Education Standards and Challenges**:  
  - Historically underperformed in international assessments (e.g., TIMSS), with concerns over numeracy and foundational skills.  
  - Bullying is a widespread issue; the government has implemented anti-bullying policies and funding.  
  - Māori and Pacific students face achievement gaps, with targeted initiatives to improve outcomes.  

- **Key Features**:  
  - Emphasis on equity, cultural recognition, and inclusion.  
  - Strong focus on Māori language and culture in education.  
  - A flexible, diverse system with multiple pathways for students.
