**Education in New Zealand**

Historically, Māori education involved *wānanga* run by elders (*tohunga*) to preserve traditional knowledge, which was confined to the chiefly class. Formal European-style schooling began in 1815, initially taught in Māori by missionaries, though advocacy groups later promoted the use of English. The lack of a national system meant early secondary providers were private grammar schools. A free, compulsory, and secular national state education system was finally established on January 1, 1878, modeled partly on the Canterbury system.

Currently, New Zealand has three types of schools: state schools (government-owned and funded, with 85% of students enrolled), state-integrated schools (formerly private schools integrated into the state system and charging "attendance dues"), and private schools (relying heavily on tuition fees). Primary school traditionally runs from Year 1 to Year 8, while secondary school runs from Year 9 to Year 13. The national curriculum is either *The New Zealand Curriculum* (NZC) for English-medium schools or *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Efforts continue to raise the availability of Māori language education, with immersion options available in Māori-medium schools (*Kura Kaupapa Māori*).
