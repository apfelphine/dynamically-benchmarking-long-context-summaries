**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with some schools offering combined programs. **Primary education** is compulsory from age 5, while **secondary education** is compulsory from age 6 to 16, though most students stay until at least 17. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms with short breaks in April, July, and September/October.

The **national curriculum** is implemented through **The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** for English-medium schools and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)** for Māori-medium schools. Students in Years 11–13 primarily work toward the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)**, though some schools offer **Cambridge International Examinations (CIE)** or the **International Baccalaureate (IB)**. Funding for schools includes **state schools** (government-funded, free), **state-integrated schools** (formerly private, retaining special character), and **private schools** (tuition-based). **Alternative schooling** options include home schooling, with a small percentage of students enrolled.

**Early childhood education** (ages 0–5) includes **kindergarten (K–5)**, **Puna Reo**, and **licensed early childhood education centers**, with **Te Whāriki** as the curriculum framework. **Māori language education** is available through immersion programs like **kōhanga reo** and **kura kaupapa**, reflecting New Zealand’s official languages of **Māori, English, and New Zealand Sign Language**. The system emphasizes **inclusive education**, though disparities persist in academic achievement among **Māori and Pacific Island students**.
