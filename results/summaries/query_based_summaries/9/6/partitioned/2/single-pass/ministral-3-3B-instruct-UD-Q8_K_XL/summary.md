New Zealand’s education system is structured around **primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13) schooling**, with a strong emphasis on **Māori language and cultural inclusion** alongside English-medium education. Historically, Māori children were initially taught in Māori-medium schools, but systematic English preference began in 1867, with Māori language education later revived in the 1970s through programs like **kōhanga reo** (immersion preschools) and **kura kaupapa Māori** (Māori-medium schools). Today, Māori students can access education in Māori-medium schools (51%+ Māori language instruction), Māori-language-in-English schools (up to 50% Māori language), or schools with minimal Māori language exposure. Recent reforms highlight improved outcomes for Māori students in Māori-medium schools compared to mainstream education, though enrollment numbers have declined since 2004.

The **national curriculum** (NZC) and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa** (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools guide instruction across eight learning areas, including English, mathematics, science, and technology. State schools are free to citizens and permanent residents, while state-integrated schools (e.g., Catholic schools) charge attendance fees. Private schools operate independently but must meet equivalent standards. Early childhood education includes kindergarten and licensed daycare centers, with a focus on **Te Whāriki**, a curriculum framework promoting holistic development. The system mandates **compulsory education** from age 6 to 16, though most students stay until at least age 17. The **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)** is the primary qualification for secondary school graduates, with options for Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the International Baccalaureate (IB).
