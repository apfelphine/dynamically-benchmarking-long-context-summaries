## Education in New Zealand

**Primary and Secondary Education Structure and Funding**

New Zealand offers free primary and secondary schooling to all citizens and permanent residents, compulsory from the age of six to sixteen. The schooling system comprises 13 year levels. Primary education runs from Years 1 to 8, with Years 7 and 8 often functioning as intermediate. Secondary education spans Years 9 to 13. Schools are categorized by funding: state schools are government-owned and funded, state integrated schools are government-funded but maintain a "special character" (often religious or specialist), and private schools rely mainly on tuition fees.

The national curriculum is The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools, while Māori-medium schools follow Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA). Curriculum outlines eight learning areas, with most compulsory until Year 10. Students from Year 11 onwards typically work toward the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with some schools also offering Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the International Baccalaureate (IB).

**Māori Language and Educational Standards**

Efforts have been made to promote Māori language education, which historically took place through traditional Māori educational systems (wānanga). While English is the dominant language, Māori language education is available through immersion in Māori-medium schools (Kura Kaupapa Māori) or as a subject in English-medium schools. International assessments have shown disparities in academic achievement between Māori/Pacific Island students and Pākehā students.

**Tertiary Education**

Tertiary education is financed through a mix of government subsidies and student fees. Students can access Student Loans and Student Allowances to support tuition and living costs. Funding for tertiary institutions has faced criticism regarding high fees, which some argue is discouraging participation and leading students to seek overseas employment to manage debt.
