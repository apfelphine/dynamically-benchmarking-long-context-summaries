**Education in New Zealand**

- **Primary and Secondary Education**:  
  - Compulsory from age 6 to 16; free for all citizens and permanent residents.  
  - School year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms with breaks in April, July, and September/October.  
  - 13 year levels (Years 1–13): primary (Years 1–8), intermediate (Years 7–8), secondary (Years 9–13).  
  - All state and state-integrated schools follow the national curriculum: *The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)* or *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)* for Māori-medium schools. Private schools must offer a curriculum equivalent to NZC or TMoA.  

- **Māori Language in Education**:  
  - Historically taught in Māori from 1816, but English-medium instruction was prioritized from 1867.  
  - Māori language education was discouraged in the early 20th century, leading to a decline in fluency.  
  - Revival began in the 1970s with the establishment of *kōhanga reo* (language nests) and *kura kaupapa* (Māori-medium immersion schools).  
  - The Māori Language Act (1987) recognized Māori as an official language.  
  - Today, Māori language education is available in immersion schools (51%+ of time) and as a subject in English-medium schools.  

- **School Types by Funding**:  
  - **State schools**: Government-owned and funded; free to citizens.  
  - **State-integrated schools**: Government-funded but charge compulsory "attendance dues" (typically $240–$2,300/year); retain a special character (e.g., religious or cultural).  
  - **Private schools**: Rely on tuition fees; receive limited government funding.  

- **Funding and School Fees**:  
  - State and state-integrated schools receive per-student funding, with additional support for low-decile schools.  
  - Parents may pay voluntary donations for uniforms, trips, and stationery.  
  - Attendance dues are legally required and cover school land and building costs.  

- **Tertiary Education**:  
  - Funded through government subsidies and student fees.  
  - Students access student loans and allowances to cover tuition and living costs.  
  - Funding has been criticized for not keeping pace with inflation, leading to skills shortages.  

- **Educational Standards and Challenges**:  
  - Performance in international assessments (e.g., TIMSS) has declined, with New Zealand ranking poorly in math and science.  
  - Concerns about rote learning and lack of critical thinking.  
  - School bullying is a widespread issue; the government has implemented anti-bullying policies and a $60 million "Positive Behaviour for Learning" plan.  

- **Student Support and Inclusion**:  
  - Special needs students receive Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding for curriculum adaptation, teacher aides, and equipment.  
  - Inclusive education is promoted, with students with disabilities supported in mainstream classrooms or through special education services.  

- **Early Childhood Education**:  
  - Includes kindergarten, licensed early childhood centres, and parent-led services like *Kohanga Reo* and *Playcentre*.  
  - All services must follow the *Te Whāriki* curriculum framework.  

- **Historical Context**:  
  - Pre-European Māori education (*wānanga*) was led by elders and focused on cultural knowledge.  
  - European-style schooling began in 1815; Māori language was central initially, later replaced by English.  
  - A national, secular, free, and compulsory system was established in 1878.  

- **Key Reforms**:  
  - 2017 education reforms extended primary education to eight years (replacing six years of primary + three years of intermediate).  
  - Emphasis on Māori language and cultural inclusion in the curriculum.
