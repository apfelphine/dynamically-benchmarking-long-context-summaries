Education in New Zealand is a free, compulsory, and secular system available to citizens and permanent residents from age five to nineteen. The structure consists of primary education lasting eight years (Years 1–8), often including an intermediate stage for Years 7 and 8, followed by five years of secondary education (Years 9–13). Funding is primarily provided by the government on a per-student basis, with additional allocations for schools in lower socio-economic deciles and for smaller institutions. State schools operate under the national curriculum, while private schools set their own curricula provided they meet minimum standards. A significant portion of the system is dedicated to Māori education, which includes immersion programs (*kōhanga reo* and *kura kaupapa*) and language instruction in English-medium schools, supported by legislation recognizing Māori as an official language and aiming to improve academic outcomes for Māori students.

The system is characterized by a diverse range of school types, including state, state-integrated, private, charter, and home-schooling options. Enrollment management in major urban centers utilizes "home zone" systems to prevent overcrowding, while rural areas often have "open enrolment." Tertiary education is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, offering degrees and vocational training. The education system faces ongoing challenges, including a historical decline in Māori language proficiency, recent concerns regarding math and numeracy standards, and persistent issues with school bullying. Despite these challenges, the system emphasizes self-management for schools and provides specific support for students with special needs through the Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS).
