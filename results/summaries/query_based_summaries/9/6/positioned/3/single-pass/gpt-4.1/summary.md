## Education in New Zealand: Summary

Education in New Zealand is shaped by a history that began with Māori-run wānanga, evolving post-European arrival into a system initially dominated by missionary and provincial schools before establishing a nationwide, free, compulsory, and secular state education system in 1878. Today, education is compulsory from ages 6 to 16, but most children start primary school at age 5. The system comprises early childhood education—including kindergarten, kōhanga reo, and playcentres—followed by 13 years of primary and secondary schooling, with the last two years of primary often taken at intermediate schools. Secondary education runs from Year 9 to Year 13, with most students pursuing the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), though alternative qualifications like Cambridge International Examinations and the International Baccalaureate are also available.

State schools are government-owned and funded, while state-integrated schools retain a special character (often religious or philosophical) and charge compulsory attendance dues, and private schools rely mainly on tuition fees. Māori language and Māori-medium education are integral, with immersion and language instruction options contributing to revitalization and improved outcomes for Māori students. The curriculum encompasses eight learning areas, and special provisions exist for disabled students, teen parents, and home-schoolers. Funding is allocated per enrolled student, with extra support for lower-decile schools and special needs. Tertiary education includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational training, with costs covered by a mix of government subsidy and student loans/allowances. New Zealand’s education system faces challenges including declining performance in mathematics and science, the need to raise Māori and Pacific learners’ achievement to match Pākehā peers, and ongoing concerns about bullying and equity in school enrolment.
