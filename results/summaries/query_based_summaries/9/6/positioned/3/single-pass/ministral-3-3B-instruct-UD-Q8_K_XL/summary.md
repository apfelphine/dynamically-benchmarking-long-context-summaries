**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured around **free, compulsory schooling** from age 5 to 16, with most students continuing until at least age 17. The curriculum is divided into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with a focus on eight core learning areas: English, mathematics, science, arts, health, technology, social sciences, and languages. Schools are categorized into **state, state-integrated (religious-affiliated), and private**, with state schools being the most common (85% enrollment).

Key features include:
- **Māori-medium education**, which has seen improved outcomes for Māori students in immersion schools (kura kaupapa), though mainstream schools still face challenges in addressing disparities.
- **Compulsory attendance** from age 6 to 16, with most students remaining in school until at least 17.
- **Standardized assessments** like the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)** for senior students, alongside international programs like the **International Baccalaureate (IB)**.
- **Funding disparities**, with state schools receiving government support but facing voluntary donations, while private schools rely on tuition fees.
- **Special education** for students with disabilities, supported by the **Ongoing Resourcing Scheme (ORS)**.

The system emphasizes **equality of access** but has faced criticism over **achievement gaps**, particularly for Māori and Pacific Island students, and **infrastructure challenges** in rural areas. Recent reforms aim to improve **STEM education** and **digital literacy**, though broader issues like **teacher shortages** and **funding inequities** persist.
