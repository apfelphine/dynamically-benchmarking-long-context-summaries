**Education in New Zealand**

Traditional Māori education involved *wānanga*, which were run by elders (*tohunga*) to transmit traditional knowledge, songs, chants, tribal history, and spiritual understanding, primarily confined to the chiefly class (*rangatira*), as reading and writing were unknown. Formal European-style schooling began in 1815, with the London Missionary Society establishing the first schools. A free, compulsory, and secular national state education system was established on January 1, 1878, largely modeled after the Canterbury system, following the abolition of the provinces in 1876.

New Zealand citizens and residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from their fifth birthday until the end of the year following their nineteenth birthday. Education is compulsory between ages six and sixteen. The school year runs from late January to mid-December and is divided into four terms. The curriculum follows either The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, covering eight learning areas, with English being the dominant language. Early childhood education is provided through various centre-based (like Kindergarten/kindy) and whānau-led services.
