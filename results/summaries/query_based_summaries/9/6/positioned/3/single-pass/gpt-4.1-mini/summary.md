## Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has deep roots in Māori traditions of passing knowledge through wānanga led by elders, with formal European-style schooling introduced by missionaries in the early 19th century. A national, free, compulsory, and secular education system was established in 1878, influenced by Victorian ideals, with separate schooling for boys and girls common initially. Early childhood education is widespread and can be centre-based or home-based, with licensed services following the national curriculum framework Te Whāriki.

Primary and secondary education covers Years 1 to 13, generally starting at age 5 and continuing until 17 or 18, with compulsory schooling from ages 6 to 16. Schools are categorized as state, state integrated (former private schools retaining a "special character"), or private, each governed by respective funding and enrollment rules. The curriculum is nationally standardized, with qualifications including the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), alongside options like Cambridge and International Baccalaureate. Māori language education has seen revival through immersion schools (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa) and Māori-medium instruction, although English remains dominant. Funding for schools is largely government-driven, supplemented by voluntary donations and compulsory fees for integrated schools. Tertiary education includes vocational and degree programs regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, with government subsidies, loans, and allowances supporting students. Despite relatively high international rankings, concerns remain about literacy and mathematics achievement, particularly among Māori and Pacific students. Bullying is recognized as a significant issue, with government efforts ongoing to implement comprehensive anti-bullying policies and positive behavior programs.
