**Education in New Zealand**  

New Zealand’s education system is free and compulsory for all citizens from age 6 to 16. It is structured into 13 year levels, with primary education (Years 1–8) and secondary education (Years 9–13). The curriculum follows the national curriculum (NZC) or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, covering eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, arts, health, and technology.  

A key feature is the emphasis on Māori language and culture, with immersion programs like kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa offering full immersion in Māori. Māori-medium education is recognized as a vital part of cultural revitalization.  

Schools are categorized by funding: state schools (government-funded, free), state-integrated schools (funded by government with religious or cultural special character), and private schools (fee-based). State-integrated schools charge attendance dues to cover operational costs.  

The system includes early childhood education (kindergarten, playgroups, and kōhanga reo), with a strong focus on inclusive education. Students with special needs attend day specialist schools or are supported through inclusive classrooms.  

Tertiary education is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and includes undergraduate degrees, vocational training, and postgraduate study.  

The system faces challenges such as declining student performance in basic numeracy and literacy, high levels of academic pressure, and concerns over bullying and mental health. Despite these, the system promotes equity, cultural diversity, and student well-being.  

The education system is also marked by a strong commitment to equity, inclusion, and the recognition of Māori language and culture as central to national identity.
