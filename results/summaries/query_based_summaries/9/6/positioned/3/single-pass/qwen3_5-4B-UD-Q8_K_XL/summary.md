# Education in New Zealand

New Zealand's education system is characterized by free, compulsory state schooling for all citizens and permanent residents from age five until the end of the calendar year following their 19th birthday, with compulsory attendance required between ages six and sixteen. The system is organized into 13 year levels: primary education spans Years 1–8 (ages 5–12), often divided into contributing primary (Years 1–6) and intermediate (Years 7–8) schools, while secondary education covers Years 9–13 (ages 13–17). Funding is provided per student, with additional support for low-decile schools based on socio-economic status, though parents are expected to pay for uniforms, stationery, and optional donations.

The curriculum is governed by the national New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* for Māori-medium schools, covering eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, and technology. While English is dominant, there are significant efforts to revitalize Māori language education through immersion programs (*kōhanga reo*, *kura kaupapa*) and Māori-medium schools, supported by legislation recognizing Māori as an official language. The system is diverse, comprising state schools, state-integrated schools (often religiously affiliated), and private institutions, with a heavy emphasis on extracurricular activities and the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA) for senior students. Recent challenges include international ranking drops in mathematics and science, as well as significant issues regarding school bullying and cyber-bullying.
