# Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has its roots in Māori traditional knowledge transmission through wānanga before the arrival of Europeans, followed by formal European-style schooling introduced by missionaries in the early 19th century. The national education system, established in 1878, provides free, compulsory secular education primarily influenced by Victorian ideals, with a structure of eight years of primary education (Years 1–8) and five years of secondary education (Years 9–13). Early childhood education is widespread, including centre-based and parent-led services, all licensed by the Ministry of Education and following the curriculum framework Te Whāriki. Māori language education, revitalized since the 1980s, is available through immersion and bilingual programs, reflecting ongoing efforts to preserve Māori culture within the education system.

The New Zealand Curriculum and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa guide learning across eight areas, with literacy and numeracy being focal compulsory components until Year 10. From Year 11 onward, most students pursue National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with alternatives like Cambridge or International Baccalaureate offered. There are three main school types—state, state-integrated (often religious), and private—with differing funding models. Funding for education is allocated per student, with additional support for low-decile schools and special needs students. Tertiary education encompasses a broad range of qualifications within the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, with funding via government subsidies and student fees; however, concerns exist about rising costs and skills shortages. Educational challenges include relatively low international performance in mathematics, disparities in Māori and Pacific Island educational achievement, and pervasive bullying issues, prompting government initiatives for improved standards and student wellbeing.
