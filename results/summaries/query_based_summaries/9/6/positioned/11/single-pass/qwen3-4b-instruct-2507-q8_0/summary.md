Education in New Zealand is free and compulsory for all citizens from age 5 until the end of the calendar year following their 19th birthday. The system includes early childhood education, primary (Years 1–8), and secondary (Years 9–13) schooling, with a school year running from late January to mid-December. Schools follow the national curriculum (NZC) or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, covering eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, and technology. Students work toward the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA) from Year 11 onward, with some schools offering Cambridge or International Baccalaureate programs.

New Zealand has three main types of schools: state (government-funded and free), state-integrated (government-funded with optional fees), and private (tuition-based). State schools are secular, while state-integrated schools maintain religious or cultural special character. Enrolment is managed through a system of home zones, with schools operating roll limits and enrolment schemes to avoid overcrowding. Māori language education is available through immersion schools (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa) and as a subject in English-medium schools, with evidence showing better outcomes for Māori students in immersion programs.

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and includes degrees and vocational training. Funding for primary and secondary schools is based on per-student allocations, socio-economic decile, and fixed costs, with schools receiving voluntary donations. Special needs students receive Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding. Students rely on government student loans and allowances to cover tuition and living costs. 

The education system faces challenges, including declining performance in basic numeracy and literacy, as seen in international assessments like TIMSS. Critics argue that teaching methods have led to confusion and a lack of foundational knowledge. Bullying is a significant issue, with high rates of cyberbullying and physical violence, prompting government action and calls for stronger, standardized anti-bullying policies. The system emphasizes cultural inclusivity, with a strong focus on revitalizing Māori language and education.
