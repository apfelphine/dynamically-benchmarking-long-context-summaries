Education in New Zealand is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with a focus on **free, compulsory, and secular schooling** for all citizens and permanent residents from age 5 to 19. Primary education is compulsory for all children, with most starting at age 5, and includes eight years of instruction in core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies. Intermediate years (7–8) are integrated into primary schools or standalone intermediate schools, while secondary education spans five years, culminating in the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)**.

The system is divided into **state schools** (government-funded, free), **state-integrated schools** (formerly private, now government-funded but retaining special character, e.g., religious affiliations), and **private schools** (tuition-based). Māori-medium education is available through immersion schools (kura kaupapa) and Māori language programs within English-medium schools, reflecting New Zealand’s bilingual and bicultural policies. Funding for schools is based on socio-economic decile ratings, with smaller schools receiving additional support. Tertiary education is regulated by the **New Zealand Qualifications Framework**, covering a range of post-school programs, from vocational training to university degrees, with government subsidies and student loans supporting access. The education system emphasizes **inclusive practices**, though challenges remain in addressing disparities among Māori, Pacific Island, and other student groups.
