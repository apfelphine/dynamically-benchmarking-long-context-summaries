Education in New Zealand provides free, compulsory schooling from age 5 to 19, with compulsory attendance mandated between ages 6 and 16. The system consists of 13 year levels: primary education spans Years 1–8 (ages 5–12), while secondary education covers Years 9–13 (ages 13–18). The curriculum is nationalized, primarily following the English-medium *New Zealand Curriculum* (NZC) or the Māori-medium *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA), which outline eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, and the arts. While state schools are government-funded and secular, state-integrated schools retain religious or specialist character and charge attendance dues, and private schools rely heavily on tuition fees, typically around NZ$20,000 annually.

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and is funded through a mix of government subsidies and student fees, supported by a Student Loan Scheme that abolished interest for domestic students from 2006. A significant focus has been placed on Māori language revitalization through immersion programs like *kōhanga reo* and *kura kaupapa*, which have shown better academic outcomes for Māori students compared to mainstream schooling. Despite these efforts, challenges persist, including a decline in international mathematics and science rankings, issues with school bullying, and disparities in achievement between Māori/Pacific Island students and Pākehā students. The system also features complex enrollment schemes in urban areas and offers diverse alternatives such as home schooling, correspondence schools, and specialized units for teenage parents and students with special needs.
