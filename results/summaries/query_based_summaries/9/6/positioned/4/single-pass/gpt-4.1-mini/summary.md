# Summary: Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has roots in Māori traditional knowledge transmission through wānanga, focusing on oral cultural teachings conducted by elders (tohunga). Formal European-style education began in 1815 with missionary schools teaching primarily in Māori language. The national education system was formally established in 1878 as free, compulsory, and secular, influenced by Victorian ideals.

Early childhood education includes centre-based (kindergarten, licensed centres) and whānau-led services (kōhanga reo), with licensed providers following the Te Whāriki curriculum. Free primary and secondary education is available to citizens and residents from age 5 to 19, with compulsory schooling between ages 6 and 16. The school year runs late January to mid-December, divided into four terms. Primary education spans Years 1–8 (with Years 7–8 as intermediate) and secondary education covers Years 9–13. The national curriculum (The New Zealand Curriculum for English-medium and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa for Māori-medium schools) encompasses eight learning areas. From Year 11, students work toward the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with options for Cambridge or IB curricula.

Schools are categorized as state (government-owned and free), state-integrated (retaining special character and charging attendance dues), and private (fee-paying). Enrolment schemes exist for certain schools to manage capacity, often based on geographic zones. Māori-medium education has grown with kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa Māori immersion schools, though there has been a decline in overall Māori language education enrolment. Māori students in immersion schools perform notably better in NCEA outcomes compared to mainstream schooling.

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, spanning informal community courses to postgraduate research degrees. Funding for primary and secondary education is based on per-student government allocations adjusted for socio-economic decile ratings, supplemented by voluntary parental donations and compulsory attendance dues in state-integrated schools. Tertiary education funding combines government subsidies and student fees, with many students relying on loans and allowances.

New Zealand's education system has faced challenges in standardized testing performance, particularly in mathematics, prompting government interventions and ongoing debates on teaching approaches. Bullying is recognized as a significant issue, with government efforts to introduce comprehensive policies and funding to address it.

Overall, New Zealand’s education system aims to balance bicultural commitments, accessibility, and quality amidst evolving societal needs.
