## Education in New Zealand

New Zealand's indigenous Māori education systems traditionally passed down knowledge through elders (*tohunga*) in *wānanga*, primarily to the chiefly class, with reading and writing being unknown. Formal European-style schooling was introduced in 1815, initially taught in Māori, before the 1867 *Native Schools Act* articulated a government preference for English instruction for Māori children.

Currently, all New Zealand citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from their 5th birthday until the end of the year following their 19th birthday, with compulsory education running from age 6 to 16. The national curriculum, *The New Zealand Curriculum* (NZC) for English-medium schools and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, outlines eight learning areas. The country features state schools, which are government-funded, and state integrated schools, which maintain a special religious or philosophical character. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.

### Māori Language in Education
There are structured ways to engage with the Māori language:
*   **Māori Medium:** Students are taught the curriculum in Māori for at least 51% of the time.
*   **Māori Language in English Medium:** Students learn Māori as a language subject or are taught the curriculum in Māori for up to 50% of the time.
*   **No Māori Language in Education:** Students are only introduced to Māori via simple words, greetings, or songs.

Recent data indicates that Māori children enrolled in Māori language schools (*kōhanga* and *kura kaupapa*) are excelling, with 58% leaving with NCEA Level 3 compared to 35% in mainstream schooling.
