## Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand is primarily state-funded and governed by the Ministry of Education. Schooling is compulsory for students aged 6 to 16, but most children begin at age 5. The system consists of 13 year levels, with primary education spanning Years 1–8 (including intermediate Years 7–8) and secondary education covering Years 9–13. All citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free education in state or state-integrated schools, with private and home-schooling options also available. State-integrated schools retain a special character, often religious, and may charge attendance dues, while private schools rely heavily on tuition fees.

The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa are the national curricula for English- and Māori-medium schools, respectively, encompassing eight key learning areas. Senior secondary students pursue the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with some schools offering Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the International Baccalaureate (IB). There are active efforts to revitalize Māori language and culture in education, seen in the establishment of kōhanga reo (language nests) and kura kaupapa Māori (immersion schools). Tertiary education includes universities, polytechnics, and wānanga, and is funded by a combination of government support and student loans/allowances. Educational outcomes vary, with Māori and Pacific Island students facing achievement gaps and bullying recognized as a significant challenge in schools.
