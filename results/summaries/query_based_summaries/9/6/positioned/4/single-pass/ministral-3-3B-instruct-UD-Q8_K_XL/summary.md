**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured around a **free, compulsory, and secular** framework, ensuring access to education from age 5 until the end of the calendar year following a student’s 19th birthday. The system is divided into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with the latter also referred to as high school or college. Primary education is compulsory, while secondary education is not mandatory but highly encouraged.

The curriculum is standardized through **The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** for English-medium schools and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)** for Māori-medium schools. Students begin learning core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies from Year 1, with additional languages and arts introduced progressively. From Year 11, students typically pursue the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)**, though some schools offer Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the International Baccalaureate (IB). The system includes three types of schools: **state schools** (government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (religious or specialized but government-funded), and **private schools** (tuition-based).

New Zealand emphasizes **equality of opportunity**, with provisions for **Māori language education** through immersion programs (e.g., *kura kaupapa Māori*). The country has also made strides in **inclusive education**, though challenges remain in ensuring equal access and quality for all students. Funding is allocated based on socio-economic decile ratings, with additional support for special needs students. Tertiary education is regulated within the **New Zealand Qualifications Framework**, offering a range of programs from vocational training to university degrees.
