**Education in New Zealand**  

New Zealand’s education system is structured into early childhood, primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, with a national, free, and compulsory system from age 6 to 19.  

**Early childhood education** includes kindergarten (ages 3–5), Puna Reo, and licensed early childhood centres. Parent-led services like Kohanga Reo and Playcentre are also available. All services must follow the early childhood curriculum *Te Whāriki*.  

**Primary and secondary education** is compulsory from age 6 to 16, with students typically starting at age 5. The system spans 13 year levels (Year 1–13), with Years 1–8 as primary and Years 9–13 as secondary. Students transition to secondary education around age 12–13. The national curriculum (*NZC*) and Māori-medium curriculum (*Te Marautanga o Aotearoa*) cover eight learning areas. Students work toward the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with some schools offering Cambridge or IB qualifications.  

**School types** include state schools (free, government-funded), state-integrated schools (funded with religious or cultural special character), and private schools (fee-based). State-integrated schools charge attendance dues.  

**Māori language education** has seen a revival since the 1980s, with immersion schools (*kura kaupapa*) and Māori-medium programs now widely available. Māori language is taught in both English-medium and Māori-medium schools, with a focus on cultural preservation and language revitalization.  

**Tertiary education** is regulated under the NZQF and includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational institutions.  

**Funding** is per-student, with additional support for socio-economic decile, and voluntary school fees. Special education students receive Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding.  

**Educational standards** have faced criticism, with declining performance in international assessments (e.g., TIMSS), though recent reforms aim to improve numeracy and critical thinking.  

**School bullying** is a widespread issue, with high rates of cyberbullying and physical bullying. The government has introduced anti-bullying policies and a Positive Behaviour for Learning plan.  

**Notable features** include a strong emphasis on Māori language and culture, inclusive education, and a well-established national curriculum. The system is decentralized, with local boards managing schools.
