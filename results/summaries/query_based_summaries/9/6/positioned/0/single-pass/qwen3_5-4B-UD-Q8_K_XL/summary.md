Education in New Zealand evolved from pre-European Māori wānanga systems, where elders taught traditional knowledge to chiefly classes, to a formal European-style system established by missionaries in 1815. A national, free, compulsory, and secular state education system was implemented in 1878, modeled on the Canterbury system, though early efforts included manual training schools and a gradual shift toward English-medium instruction for Māori children. The current system features 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with compulsory attendance between ages 6 and 16, and three main school types: state schools (government-funded), state-integrated schools (government-funded but retaining religious character with attendance fees), and private schools (tuition-funded). The curriculum is defined by *The New Zealand Curriculum* and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa*, emphasizing literacy, numeracy, and science, with a strong focus on revitalizing Māori language education through immersion programs like kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa. Funding is largely per-student based, with additional support for low-decile schools and special needs, while tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.

The system faces ongoing challenges regarding educational standards, with TIMSS and PISA data indicating performance gaps in mathematics and science compared to international peers, leading to reforms such as the Numeracy Development Project. Significant social issues include school bullying, which has prompted government initiatives for positive behavior and comprehensive anti-bullying policies. While the system generally provides equitable access, issues of gender disparity in certain regions, resource allocation in rural areas, and the pressure of competitive university entrance remain. The Ministry of Education oversees a diverse landscape that includes alternative schooling options such as charter schools (now integrated), home schooling, and correspondence schools, aiming to balance academic rigor with holistic development and cultural inclusion.
