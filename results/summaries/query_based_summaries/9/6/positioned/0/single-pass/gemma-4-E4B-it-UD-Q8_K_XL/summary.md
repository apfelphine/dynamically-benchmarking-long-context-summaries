## Education in New Zealand

Historically, pre-European Māori education, known as *wānanga*, transmitted traditional knowledge through elders (*tohunga*) and was initially confined to the chiefly class. Formal European-style schooling arrived in 1815, initially using the Māori language, although later advocacy led to the teaching of English. Early education was decentralized, relying on private grammar schools and provincial government setups before a national system was established. New Zealand implemented a free, compulsory, and secular national state education system on January 1, 1878, largely modeled after the Canterbury system.

The education structure is divided into primary and secondary schooling, with compulsory education running from the 6th to the 16th birthday. All state and state integrated schools adhere to national curricula: the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools, and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. The system includes three types of schools: government-owned state schools, government-funded state integrated schools, and privately funded private schools. Early childhood education is provided through centre-based (e.g., Kindergarten, Licensed Early Childhood Education and Care Centres) or home-based services (e.g., Kohanga Reo, Playcentre).

Furthermore, there are distinct pathways for Māori language education, ranging from Māori Medium (taught in the Māori language for at least 51% of the time) to Māori Language in English Medium. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, involving a combination of government subsidies and student fees.
