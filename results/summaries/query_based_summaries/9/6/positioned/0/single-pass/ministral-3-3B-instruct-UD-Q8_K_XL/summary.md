New Zealand’s education system is structured around a **free, compulsory, and secular** framework, ensuring access to education from age 5 until 19. The system is divided into **early childhood education, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity and cultural diversity.

### **Early Childhood Education**
Early childhood education in New Zealand includes **centre-based and teacher-led services** such as kindergarten (ages 3–5), Puna Reo, and licensed early childhood education centres (ages 0–5). Some services are **whānau-led or parent-led**, including Kohanga Reo and Playcentre. All early childhood education services must follow the **Te Whāriki curriculum framework** and are licensed by the Ministry of Education but are not government-owned.

### **Primary and Secondary Education**
All New Zealand citizens and permanent residents are entitled to **free primary and secondary schooling** from age 5 until the end of the calendar year following their 19th birthday. Education is **compulsory between ages 6 and 16**, though most students stay until at least 17. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms with two-week breaks. The curriculum follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** for English-medium schools and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)** for Māori-medium schools.

Primary education lasts **eight years (Years 1–8)**, with the last two years (Years 7 and 8) known as intermediate. Secondary education spans **five years (Years 9–13)**. Students can choose from **state schools (government-funded), state-integrated schools (religious or specialized), or private schools (tuition-based)**. The system supports **Māori language education** through immersion schools (kura kaupapa) and programs, reflecting efforts to revitalize Māori culture and language.

### **Tertiary Education**
Tertiary education includes **universities, polytechnics, and colleges**, regulated by the **New Zealand Qualifications Framework**. Students can access **student loans and allowances** to cover tuition and living expenses. The system emphasizes **vocational and practical training**, aligning with industry needs.

### **Cultural and Special Education**
New Zealand’s education system actively promotes **Māori language and culture** through programs like **kura kaupapa**, which offer immersion education. Special education supports students with disabilities through **Ongoing Resourcing Scheme (ORS) funding**, ensuring accessibility and inclusion. The system also addresses **gender and cultural diversity**, though challenges remain in ensuring equitable opportunities for all students.
