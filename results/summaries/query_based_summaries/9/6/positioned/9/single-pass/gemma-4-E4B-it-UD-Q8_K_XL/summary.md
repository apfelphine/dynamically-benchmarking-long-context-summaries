In New Zealand, traditional Māori education involved elders (**tohunga**) passing down cultural knowledge such as songs, chants, and tribal history, primarily confined to the chiefly (**rangatira**) class, with reading and writing being unknown. Formal European-style schooling was introduced in 1815. Following the abolition of provinces in 1876, the nation established a free, compulsory, and secular national state education system from 1878, largely modeled on the Canterbury system.

Early childhood education can be centre-based and teacher-led (e.g., Kindergarten or Licensed Early Childhood Education and Care Centres) or centre-based and whānau-led (e.g., Kohanga Reo). All citizens entitled to reside in New Zealand can receive free primary and secondary schooling from their fifth birthday until the end of the year after their nineteenth birthday, with compulsory education spanning ages 6 to 16. The school year runs from late January to mid-December and is divided into four terms. The curriculum is based on The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Tertiary education is unified under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.
