New Zealand’s education system is structured into three main phases: **early childhood, primary, and secondary education**, with a strong emphasis on compulsory schooling from ages 6 to 16. Early childhood education includes kindergarten and pre-school programs, with 50% of early learning teachers required to be qualified. Primary education spans eight years (Years 1–8), with the last two years (Years 7–8) known as intermediate. Secondary education lasts five years (Years 9–13), culminating in the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), though some schools offer alternative programs like the International Baccalaureate (IB) or Cambridge International Examinations (CIE).

The system is divided into **state schools** (free, government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (formerly private, now government-funded but retaining their "special character"), and **private schools** (tuition-based). All state and state-integrated schools follow the national curriculum, while private schools can offer alternative programs. New Zealand also supports **Māori-medium education**, with schools offering immersion programs in the Māori language, and **special education** for students with disabilities. Funding for schools is based on socio-economic decile ratings, with low-decile schools receiving more resources. Tertiary education is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, covering a range of post-school programs, including universities, polytechnics, and vocational training. The system prioritizes inclusivity, equity, and practical skills, with ongoing efforts to improve academic standards and address disparities in educational outcomes.
