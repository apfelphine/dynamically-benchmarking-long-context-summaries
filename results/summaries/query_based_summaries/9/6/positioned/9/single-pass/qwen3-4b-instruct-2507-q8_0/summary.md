Education in New Zealand includes a national curriculum (NZC) and Māori-medium curriculum (Te Marautanga o Aotearoa), with free primary and secondary schooling for citizens from age 5 to 19. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms. Students progress through 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with primary education (Years 1–8) and secondary education (Years 9–13). Schools are categorized as state, state-integrated, or private, with state schools being government-funded and free, state-integrated schools retaining a special character (e.g., religious) and charging attendance dues, and private schools relying on tuition fees. Māori language education is available in immersion schools (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa) and as a subject in English-medium schools. The education system emphasizes inclusivity, with provisions for students with special needs and those in alternative schooling. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, offering degrees and diplomas. Funding for primary and secondary schools includes government per-student allocations, with voluntary school fees and mandatory attendance dues for state-integrated schools. Student loans and allowances support tertiary education, though high fees and inflation have raised concerns. The system faces challenges in academic standards, with international assessments showing declining performance in math and science, and ongoing efforts to improve teaching methods and student outcomes. School bullying is a recognized issue, with government initiatives to address it. The system promotes Māori language and cultural education, with significant improvements in achievement for Māori students in Māori-medium schools.
