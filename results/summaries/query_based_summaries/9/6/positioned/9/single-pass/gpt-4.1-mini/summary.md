# Summary: Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has roots in Māori traditional learning, with wānanga led by elders focused on oral knowledge. European-style formal schooling began in the early 19th century, initially taught in Māori language, before transitioning to English with the establishment of a national secular, free, and compulsory education system from 1878. Early childhood education includes teacher-led centers (kindergartens, daycares) and whānau-led services, all licensed and regulated, with a curriculum framework called Te Whāriki.

Primary and secondary education is free for residents from age 5 to 19, compulsory between 6 and 16. The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into 13 year levels (Year 1 to 13), with primary schooling until Year 8 and secondary schooling from Year 9 to 13. The national curriculum guides all state and integrated schools, emphasizing English or Māori language instruction, mathematics, science, social studies, the arts, health, technology, and languages. From Year 11 onward, students often work towards the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA) or other international qualifications.

Schools in New Zealand are categorized as state (public), state-integrated (former private with special character, often religious), and private schools, with the majority enrolled in state schools. Funding is mainly per-student from government sources, supplemented by voluntary donations or compulsory attendance dues in integrated schools. Māori language education has experienced historical decline but is currently supported through immersion schools (Kura Kaupapa Māori) and bilingual programs. New Zealand also offers diverse schooling options including home schooling, alternative schools, and composite schools serving varied year levels.

Tertiary education covers all post-school education, governed by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, and funded via government grants and student fees. Students commonly access governmental student loans and allowances to support tuition and living costs. Despite reforms and initiatives, New Zealand faces challenges in educational standards, with international assessments showing mixed results, and efforts continuing to improve Māori and Pacific Island student outcomes. School bullying remains a concern, with national efforts directed to develop compulsory anti-bullying policies.
