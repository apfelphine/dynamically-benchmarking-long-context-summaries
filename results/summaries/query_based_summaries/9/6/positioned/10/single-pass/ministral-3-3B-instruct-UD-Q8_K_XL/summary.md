New Zealand’s education system is structured around **compulsory, free, and secular education** from the age of 5 to 19, with a focus on both academic and practical learning. Primary education spans **Years 1–8**, followed by **intermediate (Years 7–8)** and **secondary (Years 9–13)** schooling. The system is divided into **state schools** (government-funded, free), **state-integrated schools** (private but government-funded, retaining religious or cultural character), and **private schools** (tuition-based).

Key features include:
- **National curriculum** (The New Zealand Curriculum for English-medium schools, *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* for Māori-medium schools), emphasizing literacy, numeracy, arts, science, and social sciences.
- **Māori language immersion** programs, with Māori students excelling in bilingual education compared to mainstream schools.
- **Special education** for students with disabilities, including Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS) funding for adapted learning.
- **Home schooling** is permitted with government approval, though rare.
- **Tertiary education** is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, covering vocational training, university degrees, and professional certifications.

Funding is primarily government-based, with state schools receiving per-student allocations, adjusted by socio-economic decile ratings. Private schools rely on tuition fees, while state-integrated schools charge "attendance dues" to cover maintenance costs. The system emphasizes **equality of access**, though challenges remain in achieving parity for Māori and Pacific Island students in academic achievement.
