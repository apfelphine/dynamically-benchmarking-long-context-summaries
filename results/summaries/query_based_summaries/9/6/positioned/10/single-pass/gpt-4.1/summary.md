## Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand is characterized by a comprehensive and inclusive system that spans early childhood, primary, secondary, and tertiary education. Early childhood education includes kindergartens, playcentres, and kōhanga reo, with services licensed or certified by the Ministry of Education and guided by the Te Whāriki curriculum. Primary and secondary education is free for New Zealand citizens and permanent residents from ages 5 to 19 and is compulsory between ages 6 and 16. The schooling structure consists of 13 year levels, with primary education lasting eight years (Years 1–8) and secondary education five years (Years 9–13). State schools, state-integrated schools (often religious or philosophically affiliated), and private schools all operate, with state schools remaining secular and enrolling about 85% of students.

The national curriculum (NZC or Māori-medium TMoA) outlines eight learning areas, with compulsory subjects up to Year 10 and most students working towards the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA) from Year 11 onwards. Māori language education is actively supported and available in both English- and Māori-medium settings, including kōhanga reo (preschool) and kura kaupapa Māori (primary/secondary). Tertiary education encompasses education beyond secondary school, including vocational training and university studies, regulated through the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Schools receive government funding based on enrollment and socioeconomic decile, while private and integrated schools rely more heavily on attendance dues and tuition fees. Educational challenges include ensuring consistent standards, reducing bullying, and improving Māori and Pacific Island achievement.
