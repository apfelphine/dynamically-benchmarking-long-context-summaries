**Education in New Zealand**

Māori traditional knowledge was preserved through *wānanga* run by elders (*tohunga*) before European arrival, where teaching was confined primarily to the chiefly (*rangatira*) class. Formal European-style schooling began in 1815, initially conducted in the Māori language by missionaries. New Zealand established a free, compulsory, and secular national state education system on January 1, 1878, largely modeled after the Canterbury system.

The educational structure includes eight years of primary education (Years 1–8), with the last two years (Years 7 and 8) being intermediate. Secondary education lasts five years (Years 9–13). All state and state integrated schools adhere to the national curriculum: The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Early childhood education options include centre-based (e.g., Kindergarten, licensed centers) and whānau-led (e.g., Kohanga Reo, Playcentre) services, which follow the *Te Whāriki* curriculum framework.

**Key Features of the System:**

*   **Access and Funding:** All New Zealand citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from age five until the end of the year following their 19th birthday.
*   **School Types:** There are state schools (government owned and funded), state integrated schools (government-funded but retain a "special character"), and private schools.
*   **Māori Language:** Efforts are ongoing to raise the availability of Māori language education, available through immersion in Māori-medium schools (*Kura Kaupapa Māori*).
*   **Tertiary Education:** Higher education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, encompassing various degrees. Funding is through a combination of government subsidies and student fees.
