Education in New Zealand is structured around a **free, compulsory, and secular** national system, ensuring access to education from age 5 until the end of the calendar year following the student’s 19th birthday. The system is divided into **early childhood, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**.

### **Early Childhood Education**
- **Age range:** 0–5 years (kindergarten, preschool, or early childhood centres).
- **Curriculum:** Follows *Te Whāriki*, a framework emphasizing holistic development, with at least 50% of staff holding early learning qualifications.
- **Access:** Licensed by the Ministry of Education but not government-owned; parents or whānau (extended family) can lead services.

### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory:** Ages 5–16, though most students start at age 5 and continue until at least 17.
- **Structure:**
  - **Primary (Years 1–8):** Focuses on foundational skills in English, mathematics, science, and social studies. English is introduced from Year 1.
  - **Intermediate (Years 7–8):** Transition phase, often integrated into primary schools or separate intermediate schools.
  - **Secondary (Years 9–13):** Prepares students for NCEA (National Certificate of Educational Achievement), with core subjects including English, mathematics, science, and technology. Students choose electives based on career goals.
- **Curriculum:** Aligned with *The New Zealand Curriculum*, which includes eight learning areas (e.g., English, mathematics, science, technology). Health and physical education are compulsory until Year 10.
- **School Types:**
  - **State schools:** Free, government-funded, and secular.
  - **State-integrated schools:** Former private schools integrated into the state system, retaining their "special character" (e.g., religious or specialized focus).
  - **Private schools:** Charge tuition fees, offering alternative curricula.

### **Tertiary Education**
- **Post-school education:** Includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges, regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.
- **Funding:** Government-subsidized courses with student loans and allowances available for those in financial need.
- **Notable Programs:** Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees, while polytechnics focus on vocational training.

### **Key Features**
- **Māori Language:** Māori-medium education is available through immersion schools (kura kaupapa) and Māori-medium classes in English-medium schools.
- **Inclusivity:** Special education supports students with disabilities, including assistive technology and mainstreaming opportunities.
- **Home Schooling:** Parents can apply for exemptions, though they must prove their child’s education meets state standards.

New Zealand’s education system emphasizes **equality, inclusivity, and practical skills**, with a strong emphasis on literacy and numeracy, though recent reforms have faced criticism for academic pressures and disparities in achievement among Māori and Pacific Island students.
