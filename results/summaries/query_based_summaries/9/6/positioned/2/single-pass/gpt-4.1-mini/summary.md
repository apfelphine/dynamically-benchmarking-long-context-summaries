## Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has roots in Māori traditional knowledge transmission, with formal European-style schooling introduced by missionaries in the early 19th century. The current national education system, established in 1878, provides free and compulsory education from age 6 to 16, with most starting school around age 5 and staying until about 17. The schooling structure consists of eight years of primary education (Years 1–8, with Years 7–8 sometimes called intermediate), followed by five years of secondary education (Years 9–13). The school year runs from late January to mid-December, divided into four terms.

New Zealand’s national curriculum applies to state and state-integrated schools, covering eight learning areas, with compulsory education until Year 10 and national qualifications (NCEA) pursued from Year 11 onward. State schools are government-funded and free, while state-integrated schools retain a "special character" (often religious) and charge compulsory attendance dues, and private schools rely largely on tuition fees. Māori-medium education has grown, supported by legislation and Māori immersion schools (Kura Kaupapa Māori), aimed at revitalizing language and culture. Early childhood education is widely available in teacher-led and parent-led settings, though not government-owned. Tertiary education includes universities and vocational institutions, funded by a combination of government subsidies and student fees, with student loans and allowances available. Challenges in the system include concerns about international assessment outcomes, Māori and Pacific student achievement gaps, and school bullying, which remains a notable issue.
