**Education in New Zealand**  
New Zealand has a free, compulsory, and secular national education system from age 5 to 19. The system is divided into early childhood (kindergarten), primary (Years 1–8), and secondary (Years 9–13) education. All state and state-integrated schools follow the national curriculum (NZC) or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (Māori-medium). The curriculum includes core subjects such as English, mathematics, science, and social studies, with options in foreign languages and Māori.  

Māori language education is available in both immersion and subject-based formats, with increasing enrolment and recognition. Schools are categorized by funding: state (free), state-integrated (with religious or cultural special character), and private (fee-based).  

Secondary education culminates in the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with optional pathways like Cambridge or IB. The education system emphasizes extracurricular activities and student well-being, though it faces criticism for excessive academic pressure and low achievement in international comparisons.  

The tertiary system is structured under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, with universities and polytechnics offering undergraduate and postgraduate degrees. Public funding is allocated per student, with additional support for special needs students via the Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS).  

The system has undergone reforms to promote inclusivity, equity, and student well-being, including the integration of Māori language and culture into education, and efforts to address bullying and student mental health.
