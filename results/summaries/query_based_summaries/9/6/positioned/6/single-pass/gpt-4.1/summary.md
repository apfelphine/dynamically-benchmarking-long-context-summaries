## Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand is shaped by its historical roots, beginning with Māori wānanga for traditional knowledge and later shaped by missionary schools in the early 19th century. A nationally standardized, free, compulsory, and secular state education system was established in 1878. Today, early childhood education is provided through various center- and home-based options, with licensed services required to follow the Te Whāriki curriculum framework. Primary and secondary education is free for New Zealand citizens and permanent residents from age 5 to 19, with compulsory attendance between ages 6 and 16; most students begin school at age 5 and continue until at least age 17. The school year is split into four terms, and schooling consists of Years 1–13, after which students may pursue tertiary education.

All state and state-integrated schools follow the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. School types include state (public), state-integrated (often religious or philosophy-based), and private schools, with state schools enrolling approximately 85% of students. The system emphasizes equal access, but selective enrollment zones exist in urban areas. Māori language education is available through both immersion and language-in-English-medium programs, supporting cultural revitalization. Tertiary education encompasses universities, vocational institutions, and informal learning, and is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Funding for primary and secondary schools is government-based and considers school size and socio-economic rating, while tertiary education is funded by a mix of government subsidy and student fees, supported by student loans and allowances. The quality of education, especially in subjects like mathematics, has at times faced criticism, and there are ongoing efforts to address gaps in achievement, particularly for Māori and Pacific Island students.
