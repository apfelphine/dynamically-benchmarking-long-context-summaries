**Education in New Zealand**  
New Zealand has a national, free, and compulsory education system from age 5 to 18. The system includes early childhood education (kindergarten), primary (Years 1–8), and secondary (Years 9–13) education. The national curriculum, The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC), is followed in English-medium schools, with Māori-medium schools using Te Marautanga o Aotearoa.  

The education system emphasizes inclusivity, with state-integrated schools maintaining religious or cultural special character, and a growing focus on Māori language and culture. Students may attend state, state-integrated, or private schools, with state schools being the majority.  

Early childhood education is available in both centre-based and parent-led settings, including kindergartens, playgroups, and kōhanga reo (language nests).  

Secondary education includes a range of pathways, including academic (NCEA), international (IB, Cambridge), and vocational programs.  

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, with universities and polytechnics offering degrees.  

Funding is per-student-based, with additional support for low-decile schools and special needs students.  

The system faces criticism over student well-being, with concerns about high academic pressure, bullying, and mental health. Efforts are ongoing to improve equity, student engagement, and the integration of Māori language and culture.  

The education system is structured to promote equity, cultural diversity, and student well-being, with a strong emphasis on Māori language and cultural development.
