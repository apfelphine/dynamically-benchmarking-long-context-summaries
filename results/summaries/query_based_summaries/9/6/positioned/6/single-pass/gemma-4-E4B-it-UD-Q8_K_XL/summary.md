Education in New Zealand began with traditional Māori knowledge transfer run by elders (*tohunga*). Formal European-style schooling arrived in 1815. A national state education system was established on January 1, 1878, largely modeled after the Canterbury system, providing free, compulsory, and secular education.

Currently, all New Zealand citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from age five until the year following their 19th birthday. The system comprises state schools, state-integrated schools (which maintain a special religious or philosophical character), and private schools. The national curriculum is either The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Tertiary education is unified under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Māori language education is supported through immersion programs, and reports indicate that Māori students in Māori-medium schools show higher academic achievement compared to those in mainstream schooling.
