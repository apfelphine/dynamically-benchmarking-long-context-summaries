## Education in New Zealand

Historically, Māori ran schools, known as *wānanga*, to transmit traditional knowledge, while formal European-style schooling was introduced in 1815. Early educational structures were established by missionary societies, and the initial lack of a national system led to grammar schools and private institutions serving as the first sizable secondary education providers. A free, compulsory, and secular national state education system was established on January 1, 1878, largely based on the Canterbury system.

New Zealand education is structured across different types of schools: state schools (government-owned), state integrated schools (government-funded but retaining a "special character" often religious), and private schools. All citizens entitled to reside in New Zealand are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from their fifth birthday until the end of the year following their 19th birthday, with compulsory education running from age 6 to 16. The curriculum is either The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Early childhood education can be centre-based or home-based, and all licensed services must follow the *Te Whāriki* curriculum framework.

## Key Features and Structure

**Primary and Secondary Education:**
*   **Primary Education:** Lasts eight years (Years 1–8). Years 7 and 8 are known as intermediate and may be taken at a full primary school, a Year 7–13 secondary school, or a separate intermediate school.
*   **Secondary Education:** Lasts five years (Years 9–13).
*   **Curriculum:** State and state integrated schools follow the national curriculum (NZC or TMoA).
*   **Funding:** State and state integrated schools receive per-student government funding, with low-decile schools receiving more. Private schools rely heavily on tuition fees.
*   **Māori Language Education:** Efforts are underway to increase Māori language availability, with immersion options available through Māori-medium schools (*Kura Kaupapa Māori*).

**Tertiary Education:**
Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Funding is a combination of government subsidies and student fees. Students can access Student Loans and Student Allowances.

**Educational Standards and Issues:**
Despite national efforts, concerns have been raised regarding academic standards, particularly in mathematics and reading, as highlighted by international surveys. Bullying is a widespread issue, prompting recommendations for compulsory anti-bullying programmes.
