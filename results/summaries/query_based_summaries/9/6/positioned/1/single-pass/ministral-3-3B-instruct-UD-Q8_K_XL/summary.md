In **New Zealand**, education is structured into **compulsory primary and secondary schooling** from age 5 to 16, with most students continuing until at least 17. The system is **free for citizens and permanent residents**, divided into **state schools** (government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (religious or specialized, with partial fees), and **private schools** (paid tuition). Education spans **13 year levels** (Years 1–13), with **primary education** covering Years 1–8 (intermediate Years 7–8) and **secondary education** from Years 9–13.

The curriculum follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** for English-medium schools and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)** for Māori-medium schools, covering core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies. Students transition to secondary education at age 12–13, with **NCEA** (National Certificate of Educational Achievement) being the primary qualification for higher education. Specialized programs, such as **Māori-medium education** (kura kaupapa) and **international curricula** (e.g., Cambridge, IB), are also available. The system emphasizes **inclusive education**, though challenges remain in **accessibility, teacher training, and equity**, particularly for Māori and Pacific Island students. Literacy rates are high, with **74.66% of the population literate as of 2022**.
