**Education in New Zealand**  

New Zealand offers a structured, free, and compulsory education system from age 5 to 19. Primary education spans Years 1–8, with Years 7–8 often considered intermediate. Secondary education follows from Year 9 to Year 13, with students choosing between academic (e.g., HAVO/VWO-style) or vocational pathways.  

The national curriculum, *The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)*, is followed in English-medium schools, with *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA)* for Māori-medium schools. All students are assessed annually, and those in Years 11–13 work toward the *National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)*, with options for Cambridge or International Baccalaureate.  

Schools are categorized as state (government-funded), state-integrated (government-funded with religious or cultural special character), or private (fee-based). State schools are free, while state-integrated schools charge attendance dues.  

A key feature is the emphasis on Māori language education, including immersion programs (kōhanga reo, kura kaupapa) and the integration of Māori language into the curriculum.  

The education system is supported by early childhood education (kindergarten, playgroups, or *kohanga reo*), with all children entitled to free primary and secondary schooling.  

The system has faced challenges such as school bullying, with national reports highlighting incidents of verbal and physical abuse, and efforts to improve anti-bullying policies.  

The tertiary education system includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational colleges, with degrees aligned to international standards via the *New Zealand Qualifications Framework*.  

Funding is based on per-student allocations, with additional school fees and attendance dues. Special education services are available for students with disabilities, with support through resource rooms and inclusive practices.  

Overall, New Zealand’s education system emphasizes equity, cultural inclusivity, and student well-being, with a strong focus on Māori language and identity.
