Education in New Zealand has a history rooted in traditional Māori knowledge transfer, such as *wānanga*, where elders taught tribal history, songs, and medicinal plant knowledge, initially confined to the chiefly class. Formal European-style schooling arrived in 1815, with early instruction provided by missionaries in Māori. A pivotal shift occurred when the country established a free, compulsory, and secular national state education system on January 1, 1878, largely modeled after the Canterbury system.

Currently, all citizens and permanent residents are entitled to free primary and secondary schooling from age five until the calendar year following their 19th birthday. The national curriculum, The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools, outlines eight learning areas, though learning languages is recommended from Year 7 to 10. The education system features three types of schools: government-owned state schools, government-funded but religiously or specially character state-integrated schools, and private schools. Tertiary education is governed by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, and funding for both primary/secondary and tertiary education involves a mix of government subsidies, student loans, and fees.
