Education in New Zealand is characterized by a free, compulsory, and secular national system that begins at age 5 and continues until age 19. The curriculum is structured into 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with primary education (Years 1–8) and secondary education (Years 9–13). The national curriculum, Te Whāriki (Māori-medium) or the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC), emphasizes eight learning areas, including English, mathematics, science, arts, and social sciences. Schools are categorized into state, state-integrated, and private types, with state schools being the most common. State-integrated schools maintain a religious or cultural special character and charge attendance dues. 

Māori language education is a significant component, with programs ranging from immersion (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa) to language subjects in English-medium schools. The government supports inclusive education, including special needs programs, and has implemented policies to address bullying and improve student well-being. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, offering degrees and vocational training. Funding for primary and secondary schools is based on per-student allocations, with additional support for socio-economic decile and special needs students. 

The education system has faced criticism for declining international performance in math and science, with reforms introduced to improve numeracy and problem-solving skills. Despite challenges, New Zealand maintains a strong focus on equity, cultural inclusion, and student well-being, with ongoing efforts to modernize teaching methods and address gaps in achievement.
