# Summary of Education in New Zealand

Education in New Zealand has deep roots in Māori traditions, where knowledge was passed orally by elders in wānanga, primarily to chiefly classes. European-style formal education began with missionaries in 1815, using the Māori language initially. A free, compulsory, secular national education system was established in 1878. Early childhood education is widespread, including centre-based and whānau-led programs, with required teacher qualifications and curriculum frameworks like Te Whāriki.

Primary and secondary schooling is mostly free and compulsory from ages 6 to 16, with optional continuation until 19. The school year is divided into four terms, and schools follow the national curriculum (NZC for English-medium, TMoA for Māori-medium). Primary schooling lasts for eight years (Years 1–8), with secondary education covering Years 9–13. There are three main school types: state (free and secular), state-integrated (formerly private, retaining a special character and charging attendance dues), and private schools (fee-paying). Home schooling is possible with exemption.

Māori language education has been revitalized with Māori-medium immersion schools under legal protections, contributing to improved educational outcomes for Māori students. The government funds schools based on socio-economic deciles, with extra resources for lower-decile schools and special needs funding (ORS). Tertiary education encompasses all post-school training and degrees under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, funded by government subsidies, student loans, and allowances. Despite reforms, national assessments have shown mixed results in literacy and numeracy, with ongoing challenges in raising Māori and Pacific Island standards. Bullying in schools remains a concern, leading to government efforts to improve policy and behaviour programs.
