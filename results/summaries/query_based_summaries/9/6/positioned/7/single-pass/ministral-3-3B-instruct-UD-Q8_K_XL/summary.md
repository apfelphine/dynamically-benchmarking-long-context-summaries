**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with compulsory schooling from age 5 to 16. Primary education is free and compulsory, while secondary education is also free but not mandatory. The curriculum is standardized, covering core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies, with additional language and arts education. Students transition to secondary school at age 12–13, where they can pursue the **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)** or international qualifications like Cambridge International Examinations or the International Baccalaureate (IB).

The system includes **state schools** (free, government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (religious or specialized, with attendance fees), and **private schools** (tuition-based). Specialized schools cater to students with disabilities, while alternative education options like home schooling are available. Tertiary education is regulated by the **New Zealand Qualifications Framework**, offering degrees, diplomas, and vocational training through universities, polytechnics, and colleges. Funding for schools is based on student numbers, socio-economic decile ratings, and special needs, with government subsidies supplemented by voluntary donations. The education system emphasizes inclusivity, with efforts to improve outcomes for Māori and Pacific Island students, though challenges remain in addressing disparities and ensuring high-quality instruction.
