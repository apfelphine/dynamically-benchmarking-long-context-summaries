**Education in New Zealand** is characterized by a free, compulsory, and secular national system beginning at age five and ending at age 19. The system includes three main types of schools: state, state-integrated, and private, with state schools being the most common. Education is structured into 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with primary education lasting eight years (Years 1–8) and secondary education (Years 9–13). A national curriculum, The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC), is followed in English-medium schools, with Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) used in Māori-medium schools. 

Māori language education is actively promoted through immersion programs (kōhanga reo and kura kaupapa), with evidence showing improved outcomes for Māori students in these programs. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with special education services available for students with disabilities, including those with intellectual, sensory, or physical impairments. 

Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework and includes a range of pathways from community courses to undergraduate and postgraduate degrees. Funding for primary and secondary education is based on per-student allocations, with additional support for socio-economically disadvantaged schools. Students receive financial assistance through student loans and allowances, with tuition fees generally covered by government subsidies. 

The system has faced criticism for declining international rankings in mathematics and science, attributed to teaching methods that prioritize multiple problem-solving strategies over foundational knowledge. Additionally, concerns about school bullying and student mental health persist, with reports of high levels of cyber-bullying and physical violence. Despite these challenges, New Zealand's education system maintains strong cultural integration, with a focus on Māori language and identity, and a commitment to equitable access and inclusive practices.
