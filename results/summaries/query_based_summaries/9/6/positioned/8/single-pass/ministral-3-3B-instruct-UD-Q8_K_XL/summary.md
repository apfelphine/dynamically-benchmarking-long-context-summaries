**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with **compulsory schooling** from age 6 to 16. Primary education is free and includes a focus on literacy, numeracy, and social skills. Secondary education offers a range of options, including **National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA)**, which is the primary qualification for university entry. Students can choose between **state schools** (free, government-funded), **state-integrated schools** (religious or specialized, with fees for land maintenance), and **private schools** (paid tuition).

The system emphasizes **inclusive education**, with provisions for students with disabilities through **Ongoing Resource Scheme (ORS)** funding. Tertiary education is regulated by the **New Zealand Qualifications Framework**, covering universities, polytechnics, and colleges, with funding through government subsidies and student loans. The curriculum includes **English-medium instruction** (with Māori-medium options) and is designed to foster critical thinking, creativity, and practical skills. Recent reforms have aimed to improve academic standards, particularly in **mathematics and science**, though challenges remain in ensuring equitable outcomes for all students.
