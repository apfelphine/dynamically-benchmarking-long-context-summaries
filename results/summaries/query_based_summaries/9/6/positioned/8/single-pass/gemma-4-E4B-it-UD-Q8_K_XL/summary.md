## Education in New Zealand

New Zealand's educational system provides free primary and secondary schooling for all citizens and permanent residents, covering the period from the 5th birthday until the end of the year following the 19th birthday. Compulsory education runs from the 6th to the 16th birthday. The school year operates from late January to mid-December and is divided into four terms.

The system features 13 year levels; primary education spans eight years (Years 1–8), with the final two years (Years 7 and 8) designated as intermediate. Secondary education covers five years (Years 9–13). Curricula are nationally standardized: The New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. Tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.

### School Types and Funding
Education is provided through three main school types:
*   **State Schools:** Government-owned and funded, these are free for citizens and residents.
*   **State-Integrated Schools:** Former private schools integrated into the state system, they charge compulsory attendance dues to cover costs associated with their private land and buildings.
*   **Private Schools:** These rely heavily on parental tuition fees.

Early childhood education services, such as Kindergarten (kindy) and Licensed Early Childhood Education and Care Centres, are licensed but not government-owned. While the curriculum mandates eight learning areas, students in Years 11 onwards pursue the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), and some schools also offer Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the International Baccalaureate (IB).

### Language and Māori Education
Although English is the dominant language, efforts are underway to enhance Māori language education, one of New Zealand's official languages. This is provided through Māori-medium schools (Kura Kaupapa Māori) or through Māori language subjects taught in English-medium schools. In recent years, statistics show that Māori students in Māori language schools achieve better outcomes than those in mainstream schooling.
