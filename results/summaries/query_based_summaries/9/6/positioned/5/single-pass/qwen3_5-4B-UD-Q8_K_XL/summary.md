# Education in New Zealand

New Zealand operates a state-funded, secular, and free primary and secondary education system accessible to all citizens and permanent residents from age 5 to 19. Education is compulsory between ages 6 and 16, though most students attend until age 17 or 18. The system comprises three main school types: state schools (government-owned and fully funded), state-integrated schools (government-funded but run by religious or specialist groups with mandatory "attendance dues"), and private schools (funded primarily by tuition fees). The curriculum is nationally standardized through *The New Zealand Curriculum* (for English-medium schools) and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (for Māori-medium schools), covering eight learning areas including English, mathematics, science, and social sciences. Secondary education lasts five years (Years 9–13), culminating in the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), while tertiary education is regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.

The system places significant emphasis on Māori language revitalization, offering Māori-medium education through immersion programs (*Kura Kaupapa Māori*) where students are taught 51% or more of the curriculum in Māori. Early childhood education includes licensed centers, parent-led services, and the indigenous *Kohanga Reo* (language nests). While the system has achieved high enrollment rates, it faces challenges such as school bullying, which was identified as a systemic issue requiring a comprehensive policy response, and declining international rankings in mathematics and science. Additionally, the education sector grapples with funding pressures, with state schools relying on government per-student allocations supplemented by voluntary donations, while private schools face high tuition costs that can discourage participation and lead to skills shortages as graduates seek employment overseas to repay student loan debts.
