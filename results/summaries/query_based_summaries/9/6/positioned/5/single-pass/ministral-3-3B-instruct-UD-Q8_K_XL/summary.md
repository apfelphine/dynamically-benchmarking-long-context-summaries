**Education in New Zealand**

New Zealand’s education system is structured into **primary (Years 1–8)**, **intermediate (Years 7–8)**, and **secondary (Years 9–13)** education, with compulsory attendance from age 6 to 16. Primary education is free and compulsory, while secondary education is also free for citizens and permanent residents. The curriculum follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** for English-medium schools and **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa** for Māori-medium schools, covering core subjects like English, mathematics, science, and social studies. Students transition to secondary school at age 12–13, with most continuing until at least age 17.

The system includes **state schools** (government-funded, free), **state-integrated schools** (formerly private, now government-funded but retaining religious or specialized character), and **private schools** (tuition-based). Māori language education is available through immersion programs, with significant progress in improving outcomes for Māori students. Funding is allocated based on socio-economic decile ratings, with smaller schools receiving additional support. Tertiary education is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework, offering a range of post-school programs from vocational training to university degrees. The system emphasizes inclusivity, though challenges remain in addressing disparities in academic achievement, particularly among Māori and Pacific Island students.
