Education in New Zealand is characterized by a free, compulsory, and secular national system that begins at age 5 and lasts until age 19. The education system is divided into primary (Years 1–8) and secondary (Years 9–13) education, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity and the integration of Māori language and culture. Students are taught through the National Curriculum (NZC) or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) in Māori-medium schools. The system includes a range of school types—state, state-integrated, and private—each with distinct funding models, with state schools being the most common. State-integrated schools maintain a special character, often religious or cultural, and charge attendance dues. 

The curriculum includes core subjects such as English, mathematics, science, and social studies, with increasing subject choice in Years 11–12. Students pursue the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA) or international qualifications like the International Baccalaureate. The education system also features a robust support structure for students with special needs, including special schools and inclusive classrooms. 

Funding is based on per-student allocations, with additional support for low-decile schools. Schools may request voluntary donations or "school fees" to cover operational costs, while state-integrated schools charge mandatory attendance dues. The system has faced criticism over educational standards, with declining performance in international assessments, and ongoing efforts to improve teaching methods and student outcomes. Despite these challenges, New Zealand maintains a high level of equity, cultural inclusion, and student well-being.
