Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a notable emphasis on compulsory schooling from ages 5 to 16. Primary education (grades 1–5) is typically in English or Urdu, with subjects including mathematics, science, social studies, religious education, and languages. Middle school (grades 6–8) continues this curriculum, often with single-sex education in rural areas. Secondary education (grades 9–12) is divided into **matriculation (SSC)** and **intermediate (HSSC)**, with standardized exams administered by regional Boards of Intermediate and Secondary Education (BISEs). Students can pursue streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, or commerce.

Tertiary education includes professional degrees (e.g., medicine, engineering) and bachelor’s/master’s programs, with universities offering both public and private institutions. The system faces challenges such as **gender disparity** (only ~18% of women achieve 10+ years of education), **low literacy rates** (60.65% overall, with significant regional variations), and **quality issues** due to underfunding and outdated curricula. Recent reforms aim to improve access and quality, though progress remains uneven. The education sector receives only **~2% of GDP**, with calls for increased investment to address systemic gaps.
