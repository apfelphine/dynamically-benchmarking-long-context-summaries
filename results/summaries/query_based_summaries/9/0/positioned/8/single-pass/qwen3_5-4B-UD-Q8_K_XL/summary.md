# Education in Pakistan

Pakistan's formal education system is primarily inspired by the English model and is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary stages. Primary education consists of eight years, covering junior school (years 1–5) and middle school (years 6–8). While the curriculum includes eight core disciplines such as Mathematics, General Science, and Social Studies, religious education (Islamic Studies) is a mandatory component. However, the system faces significant challenges, with net primary attendance rates hovering around 67% as of 2009, far below the global average. Secondary education begins in year 9 and lasts four years, culminating in the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) or "intermediate" level. Students often choose from streams such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, or commerce, though alternative qualifications like the British GCE O and A levels are also popular in private institutions.

Tertiary education offers a wide variety of professional degrees, including engineering, medicine, law, and commerce, typically requiring four to five years of study. The system distinguishes between "Pass" and "Honours" degrees, with the latter being increasingly preferred. Despite government efforts to raise literacy and access, the system is plagued by qualitative issues, including outdated curricula, overcrowded classrooms, and a severe shortage of qualified teachers. Significant gender disparity remains a critical issue, with female enrollment and literacy rates substantially lower than those of males due to cultural norms, safety concerns, and a lack of infrastructure in rural areas. Furthermore, the education sector receives low public expenditure relative to GDP, and chronic underfunding has led to a reliance on madrasas (Islamic seminaries) in impoverished regions, where students receive free boarding and instruction in religious texts.
