## Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan follows a structure inspired mainly by the English system and is divided into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education (playgroup to grade 5) covers subjects such as arts, sciences, languages (Urdu, English, regional languages), mathematics, Islamic studies, and social studies, with some schools incorporating drama, music, and physical education. Despite this, as of 2009, only about 67.5% of children complete primary education, and attendance rates lag behind global averages. Secondary education (grades 9 to 12) includes national standardized testing (SSC and HSSC exams) with multiple academic streams such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, and commerce. Alternative qualifications like GCE O/A Levels and Advanced Placement are also available, primarily through private institutions. Vocational and technical education is provided through specialized diploma programs equivalent to secondary education.

Tertiary education offers both two- and four-year bachelor's programs, with professional degrees in engineering, medicine, law, and more, accredited by various national councils. Despite its expansion, tertiary education enrollment was just 6% in 2007, with plans to increase this rate. Teacher education programs exist but face criticism for focusing more on memorization than application, coupled with shortages of qualified trainers and resources. Gender disparities in education are significant, influenced by societal patriarchal norms, economic barriers, and infrastructure limitations, although progress has been made in female enrollment and literacy. The education system faces challenges including poor quality, outdated curricula, insufficient funding (around 2% of GDP), and infrastructure deficits. Informal education, including madrasas focusing on Islamic subjects, supplement formal education, especially in impoverished regions. Recent efforts include the promotion of digital and distance learning to improve access, highlighted by innovations during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Notable achievements in science and academia have been made by Pakistani scholars, contributing globally, despite systemic challenges within the educational framework.
