## Education in Pakistan – Concise Summary

Education in Pakistan is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, largely inspired by the English system. Only about 67.5% of Pakistani children complete primary school, with the curriculum including core subjects such as languages, mathematics, sciences, Islamic studies, and Pakistan Studies. Secondary education begins in year 9 and is split into two cycles ending with the Secondary School Certificate (SSC/matric) and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC/intermediate), with streams in pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, computer science, and commerce. Alternative qualifications such as O/A Levels, technical education, and madrassas also exist. Tertiary education includes two-year and four-year bachelor’s programs, master’s, MPhil, and PhD options, but less than 6% of the population holds a university degree. Teacher training faces challenges in quality and application-based instruction.

Major issues include chronic underinvestment—less than 2% of GDP is spent on education, leading to dilapidated infrastructure and insufficient resources. Literacy rates, while rising, remain low compared to regional peers, with adult literacy at 60.65% nationally and significant disparities between urban-rural areas, provinces, and genders. Gender disparity remains a serious problem, especially for women and girls, though some progress has occurred. Other challenges include low teaching quality, outdated curriculum, barriers to equal access, and the influence of religion in both public and private education settings. Distance learning initiatives and technical/vocational education are gaining ground, but poverty and social inequality continue to hinder broad educational development. International schools and partnerships facilitate global curriculum delivery.
