Education in Pakistan includes a structured system with primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education begins at age 6 and lasts for 6 years, covering subjects like arts, computer studies, general science, modern languages, mathematics, religious education, and social studies. Secondary education starts at age 12 and spans four years, with students taking standardized exams (SSC-I and SSC-II) and then progressing to intermediate education (HSSC-I and HSSC-II) in a three-year program. Students can choose from streams such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, commerce, or computer science. Alternative qualifications like GCE, IGCSE, and AP are also available.

Tertiary education includes professional institutes offering degrees in engineering, medicine, law, architecture, pharmacy, and nursing, as well as universities offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD programs. A significant portion of students pursue degrees in arts, sciences, and commerce. Teacher education programs are available, with pathways including 12 years of schooling and a bachelor’s degree in education, or a four-year program leading to a bachelor’s degree. There is also a growing emphasis on technical and vocational education, with institutions like the Punjab Board of Technical Education offering diplomas in engineering and technical fields.

The system faces challenges such as gender disparity, with fewer female students enrolling and lower literacy rates among women. The quality of education is often criticized due to outdated curricula, lack of qualified teachers, and poor infrastructure. Despite these issues, the government has implemented reforms and initiatives to improve access and outcomes, including free and compulsory education for all children. The literacy rate has improved over time, reaching 60.65% in 2023, with significant regional variations. The education system also includes madrassas, which provide religious education and are popular among poorer families, though they are often criticized for promoting religious extremism. Overall, Pakistan's education system is undergoing reforms to address disparities and improve quality.
