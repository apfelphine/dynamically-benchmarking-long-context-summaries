Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with significant regional and gender disparities.

### **Primary Education (Grades 1–8)**
- **Attendance:** Only about **67.5%** of children complete primary education, below the global average of 90%. Pre-school (ages 3–5) includes play groups, nursery, and kindergarten (KG/Prep), followed by junior school (Grades 1–5) and middle school (Grades 6–8).
- **Curriculum:** Core subjects include **Arts, Computer Studies, General Science, Modern Languages (Urdu/English), Mathematics, Religious Education (Islamic Studies), Social Studies, Pakistan Studies**, and provincial/regional languages (e.g., Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto). Some schools offer **drama, music, or physical education** but these are not always examined.
- **Challenges:** Low public expenditure (2.2% of GDP) and limited access to quality education, particularly in rural areas. **Gender disparity** persists, with only **18% of women** achieving 10+ years of schooling. Urban schools often face issues like outdated curricula, lack of qualified teachers, and inadequate infrastructure.

### **Secondary Education (Grades 9–12)**
- **Structure:** Begins at **Grade 9** (matriculation) and lasts four years. Students take standardized exams (SSC-I & SSC-II) to earn a **Secondary School Certificate (SSC)**. After SSC, students enter **intermediate colleges** (Grades 11–12) and take exams (HSSC-I & HSSC-II) to obtain a **Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC)**.
- **Curriculum:** Includes **eight core subjects** (e.g., **Maths, English, Urdu, Islamic Studies, Pakistan Studies, Sciences, and electives like Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Computer Science**) with specialized streams (pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, commerce, computer science).
- **Alternative Paths:** **Technical Education** (e.g., **Technical School Certificate (TSC), Diploma of Associate Engineering (DAE)**) and **A-Level qualifications** (British system) are available but less common.
- **Challenges:** **Low enrollment rates** (e.g., only **45% of secondary students** complete their education). **Gender disparity** persists, with female enrollment rates lower than male counterparts. **Quality issues** include outdated curricula, lack of modern facilities, and teacher shortages.

### **Tertiary Education (Post-Secondary)**
- **University Education:** **6% of Pakistanis** (9% men, 3.5% women) are university graduates. Universities offer **Bachelor’s (Pass or Honours), Master’s (MPhil), and PhD** degrees in fields like **engineering, medicine, law, computer sciences, and arts**. Accreditation bodies include **PEC (Engineering), NCEAC (Computer Sciences), PMC (Medicine), etc.**
- **Teacher Education:** Programs vary from **12 years of schooling + associate degree** to **14–16 years + master’s/PhD**. Criticisms include **lack of practical training, memorization-focused exams, and underqualified instructors**.
- **Vocational & Technical Education:** Focuses on **skilled trades** (e.g., **Civil, Mechanical, Electrical Engineering, Nursing**) through **polytechnics and technical schools**. **Meister Schools** (German-style) are being introduced to improve vocational training.
- **Challenges:** **High tuition costs** discourage students from pursuing higher education. **Universities lack industry-relevant skills**, and **higher education is expensive**, limiting access for many. **Gender gaps** remain, with women underrepresented in STEM fields.

### **Key Issues & Reforms**
- **Gender Disparity:** Despite progress, **women face barriers** due to cultural norms, lack of infrastructure, and high costs. **Low-cost private schools (LCPS)** are being explored as a solution, though male enrollment remains higher.
- **Quality & Relevance:** **Outdated curricula**, poor teacher training, and **lack of modern facilities** hinder educational quality. Reforms like the **Sustainable Development Goals (SDG)** aim for **free and compulsory education** and **access to quality education**.
- **Literacy & Literacy Rates:** **60.65% literacy rate** (2023), with **rural areas lagging behind urban regions**. **Provincial disparities** exist, with **Punjab (66.25%)** and **Sindh (57.54%)** having higher rates than **Balochistan (42.01%)**.
- **International Education:** Pakistan has **439 international schools** (ISC 2015), offering **English-medium curricula** (e.g., **Cambridge, IB, Edexcel**).

### **Conclusion**
Pakistan’s education system faces **critical challenges**—**low enrollment rates, gender disparities, outdated curricula, and inadequate infrastructure**—but reforms like **free primary education, vocational training, and gender-inclusive policies** are being implemented. While progress is being made, **sustained investment, teacher training, and policy reforms** are essential to improve educational outcomes.
