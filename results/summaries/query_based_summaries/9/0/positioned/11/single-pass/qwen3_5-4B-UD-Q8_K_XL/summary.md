Education in Pakistan is a hierarchical system comprising pre-school, primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, heavily influenced by the English educational model. The formal system begins with pre-school education for ages 3–5, followed by junior school (years 1–5) and middle school (years 6–8), where single-sex education is often preferred. The national curriculum covers eight core disciplines, including Arts, Science, Mathematics, and Religious Education (Islamic Studies), though the language of instruction varies between English and Urdu depending on the institution. Attendance remains a significant challenge, with only about 67.5% of children completing primary school due to factors such as poverty, inadequate infrastructure, and gender disparities. Secondary education (years 9–12) culminates in the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) or "intermediate," with students choosing from streams such as pre-medical, engineering, humanities, and commerce.

The tertiary sector includes a diverse range of institutions, from public universities to private colleges, offering degrees in professional fields like medicine, engineering, and law. As of recent data, approximately 6% of the population holds a university degree, a target the government aims to raise to 15% by 2020. The system faces critical issues regarding quality, including outdated curricula, a shortage of qualified teachers, and a lack of practical skills. Additionally, significant gender inequality persists, with female enrollment lagging behind male enrollment due to cultural norms and safety concerns. Alternative education pathways include madrassas (Islamic seminaries) which serve many rural families, and international schools offering foreign curricula like the IB and A-Levels, alongside a growing sector of technical and vocational education.
