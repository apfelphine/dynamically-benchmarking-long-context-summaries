## Education in Pakistan

Formal education in Pakistan is structured into distinct stages: Pre-school (for 3–5 year olds), Junior School (Years 1 to 5), Middle School (Years 6 to 8), and Secondary School (Years 9 to 12). The national system is primarily modeled after the English educational system. The curriculum encompasses eight common disciplines: Arts, Computer Studies and ICT, General Science, Modern languages (Urdu and English), Mathematics, Religious Education (Islamic Studies), Social Studies, and Pakistan Studies. Instruction is determined by the institution's nature, whether it is English-medium or Urdu-medium.

Secondary education requires passing national examinations administered by regional Boards of Intermediate and Secondary Education (BISE) upon completing Year 9 (awarding the SSC) and again upon finishing Year 12 (awarding the HSSC). Alternative qualifications are available through bodies like the General Certificate of Education (GCE) and IGCSE. Pakistan's educational challenges include a low primary school completion rate of about 67.5% and a declining quality of education due to teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and underinvestment, with public expenditure on education at around 2% of GDP. Gender disparity remains a significant issue, as literacy rates are notably lower for women, and patriarchal culture influences educational access.
