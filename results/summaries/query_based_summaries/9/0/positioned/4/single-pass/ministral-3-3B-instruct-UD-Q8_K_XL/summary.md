Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a notable emphasis on compulsory education from ages 5 to 16. Primary education (grades 1–8) is compulsory, though only about **67.5% of children complete it**, with enrollment rates varying significantly across regions. The curriculum follows an English-based model, incorporating subjects like Urdu, Islamic studies, mathematics, science, and social studies, though provincial and regional languages (e.g., Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto) are also taught in respective areas. Secondary education (grades 9–12) is divided into **matriculation (SSC)** and **intermediate (HSSC)** levels, with students choosing specialized streams such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, or vocational tracks. Higher education includes professional degrees (e.g., engineering, medicine, law) and bachelor’s/master’s programs, though access is limited by financial constraints and institutional capacity.

The system faces **critical challenges**, including **gender disparities**—with only **18% of women achieving 10+ years of schooling**—and **low literacy rates**, particularly in rural areas. Public spending on education remains **under 2% of GDP**, and infrastructure gaps persist, leading to poor learning outcomes. Madrasas, which offer Islamic education, also play a significant role, especially in underserved communities. Reforms, such as the **2009 National Education Policy**, aim to improve access, quality, and equity, but systemic issues like teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and socioeconomic barriers continue to hinder progress.
