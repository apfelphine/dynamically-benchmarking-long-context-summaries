## Education in Pakistan: A Concise Overview

Education in Pakistan is structured across primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, with only about 67.5% of children completing primary education. The curriculum is heavily influenced by the British system and covers subjects such as arts, sciences, languages (Urdu and English), mathematics, religious education, and social studies, with regional languages and foreign languages sometimes taught. Secondary education leads to the matriculation certificate after standardized board exams, followed by intermediate education (FSc/FA/ICS or HSSC), which offers specialized streams. Alternative qualifications, such as O/A Levels and AP, are available through international examination boards. Technical and vocational education is also present, offering diplomas and associate degrees. Tertiary education comprises both professional (engineering, medicine, law, etc.) and general degrees, with honours and pass options, and advanced study available up to PhD level.

Pakistan faces substantial challenges in education quality, access, gender disparity, and underfunding. The literacy rate stands at 60.65% as of 2023, with notable urban-rural and male-female gaps. Several reforms have been attempted to address quality, teacher preparation, and gender parity, but issues persist, particularly in rural areas. Female and transgender individuals continue to encounter significant barriers, though some progress and legal protections have been enacted. Education expenditure is chronically low, remaining under 2% of GDP despite policy targets to increase it. While international, religious (madrasas), and non-formal educational options exist, infrastructure, teacher shortages, outdated curricula, poverty, and sociopolitical factors hamper progress. Despite challenges, Pakistan has produced distinguished alumni and continues efforts to improve education access and quality amid ongoing systemic difficulties.
