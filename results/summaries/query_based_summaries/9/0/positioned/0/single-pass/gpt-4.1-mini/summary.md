## Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan follows a multi-tiered formal system inspired largely by the English model. Primary education (years 1–5) is completed by about 67.5% of children, and includes subjects ranging from mathematics and sciences to religious and social studies, with instruction languages varying between English, Urdu, and regional languages. Secondary education lasts four years (years 9–12), culminating in the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC), with optional streams such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, and commerce. Alternative international qualifications like GCE O/A Levels and IGCSE are common, alongside technical and vocational education run by specialized boards, preparing students for engineering and allied fields. Tertiary education offers bachelor's, master's, MPhil, and PhD degrees, regulated by professional councils for various disciplines. Challenges include low attendance rates (66%), gender disparity in enrollment and literacy, declining quality marked by outdated curricula and teacher shortages, and underfunding with education expenditure around 2% of GDP.

Gender disparity is pronounced, with female literacy (42%) lagging behind male literacy (67%) due to socio-cultural barriers, lower enrollment, and limited access especially in rural areas. Though some progress has been made, particularly at secondary and higher education levels, females remain disadvantaged in educational attainment and workforce participation. Madrassas provide religious education to many, especially poorer families, sometimes outnumbering public schools in certain regions. Efforts to improve education have included reforms, distance learning initiatives like Teleschool during COVID-19, and focus on teacher training, though challenges persist related to infrastructure, curriculum relevance, teacher quality, and social equity.
