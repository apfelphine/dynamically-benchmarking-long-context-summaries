# Education in Pakistan

The formal education system in Pakistan is primarily inspired by the English model and is divided into primary, secondary, and tertiary stages. Primary education is compulsory, yet completion rates remain low, with approximately 67.5% of children finishing their studies. The curriculum includes subjects such as mathematics, sciences, languages, and religious education, with the language of instruction varying between English and Urdu depending on the institution. Secondary education lasts four years and culminates in either the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) after year 10 or the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) after year 12, with various alternative qualifications like GCE and IGCSE also available. Tertiary education is accessible at universities and professional institutes, with a significant portion of the population lacking a university degree, though government plans aim to increase this figure over time.

Significant challenges persist within the system, including chronic underfunding, which has historically kept public expenditure on education below 3% of GDP, and a severe gender disparity rooted in a patriarchal society. While literacy rates have risen to 60.65% as of 2023, female enrollment and literacy rates lag significantly behind those of males, with women comprising only 42% of the literate population. Issues such as inadequate infrastructure, cultural barriers, and a lack of female teachers further exacerbate the gap. Additionally, the system faces criticism for declining quality, outdated curricula, and a heavy reliance on rote learning. Alternative educational paths, such as madrasas for Islamic studies and technical vocational training, play a crucial role, particularly for the poorest families, while recent reforms have attempted to address issues through initiatives like free textbooks and teacher training improvements.
