**Education in Pakistan**

Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary (grades 1–8)**, **secondary (grades 9–12)**, and **tertiary (university and vocational)** levels. Primary education is compulsory but enrollment rates remain low, with only **67.5%** of children completing it. Secondary education spans four years, culminating in the **Secondary School Certificate (SSC)** and **Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC)**, with students choosing between academic, vocational, or professional streams. Tertiary education includes professional degrees (e.g., engineering, medicine) and bachelor’s degrees, though higher education remains underfunded, with only **6%** of Pakistanis holding university degrees as of 2007.

The system faces significant challenges, including **gender disparity**—only **18%** of women have completed 10+ years of schooling—and **low literacy rates** (60.65% in 2023). Public expenditure on education is minimal (**2% of GDP**), and infrastructure is often inadequate, particularly in rural areas. Madrasas, which offer Islamic education, are widely used by poorer families, though they are not universally recognized as formal institutions. Recent reforms aim to improve access and quality, but systemic issues persist, including outdated curricula, teacher shortages, and limited resources.
