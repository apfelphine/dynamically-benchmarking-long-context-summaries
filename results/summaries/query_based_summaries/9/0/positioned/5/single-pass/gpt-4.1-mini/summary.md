**Education in Pakistan** is structured into several stages largely modeled on the English system, beginning with pre-school (ages 3–5), followed by primary (years 1–5), middle school (years 6–8), secondary education (years 9–12), and tertiary education at universities and professional institutes. Primary education completion rates are around 67.5%, and net school attendance is about 66%, below global averages. Secondary education culminates in standardized exams administered by regional Boards of Intermediate and Secondary Education (BISE), leading to certification such as the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC). Alternate qualifications like GCE O and A Levels are also prevalent, particularly in private schools. Teacher education faces challenges with insufficient qualified trainers and outdated curricula, contributing to issues in educational quality.

Gender disparity remains a significant challenge; female enrollment and literacy lag behind males, especially in rural areas, due to societal, cultural, and infrastructural barriers. Efforts to reduce this gap have had some impact but persistent challenges include lower female literacy rates and retention in education. Madrassas provide religious education and play a prominent role particularly in underserved regions, often filling gaps left by the formal education system. Additionally, technical and vocational education aims to prepare students for employment, with programs managed by multiple boards offering certificates and diplomas. The country’s literacy rate has improved over decades, reaching about 60.65% overall as of 2023, but remains lower than neighboring countries.

Tertiary education includes universities offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees across various fields; however, access remains limited, and public expenditure on education hovers around 2% of GDP, below targets. Distance learning initiatives such as Teleschool have expanded access especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, yet disparities in digital access persist. Notable Pakistani scholars and scientists have contributed internationally, reflecting pockets of excellence despite systemic challenges. Overall, Pakistan’s education system contends with issues of access, quality, gender inequality, and underfunding amid ongoing reform efforts.
