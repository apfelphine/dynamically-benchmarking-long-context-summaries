## Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan follows a formal structure beginning with pre-school (ages 3–5), progressing to primary (years 1–5), middle (years 6–8), secondary (years 9–12), and tertiary education. The curriculum covers a broad range of subjects including arts, sciences, languages (Urdu, English, provincial, and some foreign languages), mathematics, religious studies, and Pakistan Studies. Secondary students undertake national examinations for the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC), while alternatives such as O/A Levels and technical education are also available. Tertiary education includes professional degrees (e.g., engineering, medicine, law), general bachelor’s and master’s programs, and a growing range of teacher education routes. Non-formal education, vocational training, and Islamic seminaries (madrassas) supplement the mainstream system.

Despite incremental improvements, Pakistan faces significant challenges: low literacy rates (60.65% nationally as of 2023, with notable gender gaps), chronic underinvestment (education spending is around 2% of GDP), deteriorating infrastructure, and persistent gender disparity (low female enrollment and literacy, barriers in STEM and teaching professions). The quality of education is undermined by outdated curricula, teacher shortages, and high dropout rates, especially among vulnerable groups. Recent reforms aim to improve access, quality, and equity, including distance learning initiatives like Teleschool. However, religious content and intolerance may permeate curricula, and minority and gender representation remain problematic. Pakistan’s achievements in higher education and science are notable, but ensuring universal, inclusive, and relevant education remains a major national challenge.
