**Education in Pakistan**

Pakistan’s formal education system is structured into primary (grades 1–5), middle (grades 6–8), secondary (grades 9–10), higher secondary (grades 11–12), and tertiary levels. Primary education completion rates are about 67.5%, with curricula covering diverse subjects including sciences, languages, religious education, and social studies. Secondary education culminates in the Secondary School Certificate (SSC), followed by Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) after grades 11 and 12, with students specializing in streams like pre-medical or commerce. Alternative education boards such as the British GCE O/A Levels and vocational technical education are also prominent. Tertiary education includes bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees across various professional fields, with private expenditure on higher education and significant challenges in teacher quality and infrastructure.

Gender disparities persist throughout Pakistan’s education system, with female enrollment and retention lagging behind males due to social, cultural, and economic barriers, although some progress has been made in recent decades. Madrassas (Islamic seminaries) play a substantial role, especially among poorer families, often providing basic Islamic education alongside limited secular subjects. Quality issues are evident in curricula relevance, teacher training, and infrastructure. Efforts to improve education include distance learning initiatives like Teleschool during COVID-19 and reforms aimed at raising expenditure, though government funding remains low (~2% of GDP). Despite challenges, Pakistan has produced notable scholars in science and technology. Overall, Pakistan’s education system faces significant hurdles in accessibility, quality, gender equity, and modernization.
