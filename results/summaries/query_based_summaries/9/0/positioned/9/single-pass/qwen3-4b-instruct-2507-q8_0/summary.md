**Education in Pakistan**  
Pakistan's education system is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, inspired by the English model. Primary education spans grades 1–8, with pre-school (ages 3–5) including play groups, nurseries, and kindergartens. Secondary education begins at grade 9 and lasts four years, culminating in the Secondary School Certificate (SSC), followed by the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) for intermediate studies. Students can choose from streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, or commerce.  

Tertiary education includes universities offering bachelor's (4–5 years), master's, and doctoral degrees, with a strong emphasis on professional programs in engineering, medicine, law, and business. A significant portion of students pursue vocational training through technical schools or diplomas.  

The system faces persistent challenges, including gender disparity—women have lower enrollment and literacy rates than men, and face cultural and economic barriers. Female participation in higher education remains limited, with only a small fraction of women in leadership roles.  

Religious education is deeply embedded, with Islamic studies a core subject. However, religious content is criticized for promoting intolerance, including negative portrayals of minorities and anti-democratic narratives.  

Education expenditure is around 2% of GDP, with a government target of 7% by 2015, though implementation has been inconsistent. Infrastructure remains inadequate, especially in rural areas, with many schools lacking basic facilities.  

Despite these challenges, Pakistan has notable achievements in science and technology, with prominent alumni such as Abdus Salam (Nobel laureate) and Arfa Karim (young computer prodigy). Literacy has improved, reaching 60.65% in 2023, with higher rates in urban areas.  

The system is undergoing reforms to improve quality, equity, and relevance, including efforts to expand access, reduce gender gaps, and modernize curricula.
