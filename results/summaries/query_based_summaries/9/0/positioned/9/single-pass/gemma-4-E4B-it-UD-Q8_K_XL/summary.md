**Education in Pakistan**

Pakistan's standard education system is largely inspired by the English educational system and is divided into three main stages: pre-school (ages 3–5), primary school (years 1–5), and secondary school (years 6–8). In secondary school, single-sex education is often preferred by the Pakistani community, though co-education is available in cities. The curriculum includes subjects such as Arts, Computer Studies and ICT, General Science (covering Physics, Chemistry, and Biology), modern languages (Urdu and English), Mathematics, Religious Education (Islamic Studies), Social Studies, and Pakistan Studies.

The system faces significant challenges, including low net primary school attendance rates (66% in 2009, below the estimated world average of 90%). Public expenditure on education was 2.2% of GNPs in 2007, with the vast majority (about 88%) allocated to lower-level education. Gender disparity remains a major issue, with women having significantly lower literacy rates than men. Additionally, the quality of education is criticized for being based solely on rote learning, lacking professional and communication skills, and having an outdated curriculum. Many families turn to madrasas for education, which focus heavily on Islamic subjects.
