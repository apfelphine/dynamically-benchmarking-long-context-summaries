In Pakistan, the education system is structured into three primary stages: **primary (grades 1–8)**, **secondary (grades 9–12)**, and **tertiary education**. Primary education is compulsory for children aged 5–12, though only about **67.5%** complete it. The curriculum includes subjects like Urdu, English, mathematics, science, religious studies, and Pakistan Studies, with some schools offering additional languages or vocational training. Secondary education spans grades 9–12, culminating in the **Secondary School Certificate (SSC)** and **Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC)**, with students choosing streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, or commerce. Tertiary education includes professional degrees (e.g., engineering, medicine) and bachelor’s/master’s programs, though access remains limited due to high costs and systemic challenges.

**Key challenges** include low enrollment rates, especially for girls (18% have 10+ years of schooling), gender disparities, and poor quality in public schools. Teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and inadequate infrastructure further hinder progress. Despite reforms like free compulsory education and literacy initiatives, systemic issues persist, with literacy rates at **60.65%** (2023) and regional disparities significant. While Pakistan has made strides in integrating marginalized groups (e.g., transgender rights), broader equity and quality improvements remain critical.
