## Education in Pakistan

The standard national education system in Pakistan is largely inspired by the English educational system and is structured into several stages. Pre-school education caters to children aged 3–5 years through Play Group, Nursery, and Kindergarten. Following this, students progress through junior school (years 1 to 5) and then middle school (years 6 to 8). The curriculum generally covers eight disciplines, including Arts, Computer Studies and ICT, General Science (Physics, Chemistry, Biology), Modern languages (Urdu and English), Mathematics, Religious Education (Islamic Studies), Social Studies, and Pakistan Studies.

Secondary education commences in year 9 and lasts for four years, culminating in the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) after years 9 and 10, and the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) after years 11 and 12. Students may choose various streams for years 11 and 12, such as pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, computer science, and commerce. Alternative qualifications are available through international boards like GCE (Cambridge Assessment) and IGCSE (Pearson PLC).

Key challenges within the Pakistani education sector include a low net primary school attendance rate (66% as of 2009), chronic underinvestment in infrastructure leading to deteriorating learning environments, and a declining quality trend. Furthermore, deep-rooted gender disparity persists, with low female literacy rates and enrollment, often linked to patriarchal societal norms and cultural barriers. The education system is also deeply intertwined with religious instruction, with madrassas playing a significant role, particularly in underserved areas.
