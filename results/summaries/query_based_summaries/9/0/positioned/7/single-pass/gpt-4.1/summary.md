## Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan is structured in several formal stages: pre-school (ages 3–5), primary (grades 1–5), middle school (grades 6–8), secondary (grades 9–10), and intermediate (grades 11–12). After completing secondary school, students sit for standardized national exams (SSC and HSSC) to obtain certifications required for further studies. The curricula include core subjects such as mathematics, sciences, Urdu, English, Islamic studies, and Pakistan Studies, with options for elective subjects. Alternative qualifications like GCE O/A Levels exist, mainly in private schools. Technical and vocational education is available from middle school onward, with specialized diplomas offered by provincial boards. Higher education comprises various professional and general degree programs, though only about 6% of Pakistanis were university graduates as of 2007, with ongoing efforts to improve this figure. Teacher education reform is noted as essential due to shortages and outdated training.

Pakistan's literacy rate stands at 60.65% (2023 Census), with considerable regional and gender disparities—males at 68%, females at 52.84%. Education expenditure remains low at around 2% of GDP, resulting in chronic underinvestment, especially in infrastructure and quality. Gender inequality is persistent, with a lower enrollment and literacy rate for females, although some progress has occurred over the last decades. Significant portions of the system rely on non-formal education, religious schools (madrasas), and distance learning initiatives like Teleschool. The education system has been criticized for rote learning, outdated materials, and limited relevance to market needs. Religious content pervades public education, and school textbooks have been reported to marginalize minorities and reinforce ideological narratives. Despite challenges, some districts, especially in urban Punjab and Karachi, achieve literacy rates above 75%, showing uneven progress across the country.
