Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a notable emphasis on English-medium instruction in urban areas and Urdu-medium in others. **Primary education** spans six years (ages 5–11), beginning with pre-primary (playgroup to KG) and followed by junior (years 1–5) and middle school (years 6–8). Attendance rates are low, with only **67.5%** of children completing primary education. The curriculum includes core subjects like mathematics, science, Urdu, English, Islamic studies, and Pakistan Studies, supplemented by optional subjects such as arts, computer studies, and regional languages.

**Secondary education** starts at age 12 with matriculation (years 9–10) and intermediate (years 11–12), culminating in the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC). Students can choose from streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, computer science, or commerce. The system also offers alternative qualifications like the General Certificate of Education (GCE) or International Baccalaureate (IB). Vocational education, including technical schools and diplomas, provides specialized training in engineering, agriculture, and trades.

**Tertiary education** includes universities offering bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees in fields like engineering, medicine, law, and the arts. Public universities are subsidized, though costs remain high, discouraging access. Teacher education programs vary, with some requiring 12 years of schooling followed by a bachelor’s degree in education. The system faces challenges, including **gender disparities** (only 18% of women complete 10+ years of education), **low literacy rates** (60.65% overall, with significant regional variations), and **underfunding** (education expenditure is ~2% of GDP). Reforms aim to improve quality, reduce dropout rates, and enhance vocational training, though systemic issues persist.
