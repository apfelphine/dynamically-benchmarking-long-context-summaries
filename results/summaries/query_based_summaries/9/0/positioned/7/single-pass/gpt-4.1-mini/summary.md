# Summary: Education in Pakistan

Pakistan's formal education system broadly follows the British model and is divided into pre-school (3–5 years), primary (years 1–5), middle (years 6–8), secondary (years 9–10), and higher secondary education (years 11–12), culminating in tertiary education. Primary education completion is at about 67.5%, with common subjects including arts, sciences, languages (Urdu, English), religious studies, and social studies. Secondary education leads to the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) after years 9 and 10, and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC) after years 11 and 12, with students selecting streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities, computer science, or commerce. Alternative curricula such as the British GCE/O and A Levels and technical diplomas are also prevalent.

Tertiary education offers various bachelor’s and postgraduate degrees across disciplines including engineering, medicine, law, and business, with accreditation bodies overseeing professional degrees. Teacher education faces challenges including shortages of qualified educators and outdated curricula emphasizing rote learning. Gender disparity remains significant, especially in rural and impoverished areas, with female enrollment and literacy rates lagging behind males despite improvements. The education sector suffers from underfunding—receiving around 2% of GDP—and infrastructure deficiencies, with many schools in disrepair. Religious madrasas play an influential role, often filling gaps in formal education but also fostering conservative religious instruction. Recent expansions in distance learning, such as televised lessons during COVID-19, aim to improve accessibility. Literacy rates have been steadily increasing, reaching about 60.65% in 2023, with notable regional and gender gaps persisting.
