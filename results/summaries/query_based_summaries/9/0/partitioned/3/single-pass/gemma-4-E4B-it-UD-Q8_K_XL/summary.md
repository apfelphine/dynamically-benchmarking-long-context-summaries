## Education in Pakistan

Pakistan's educational landscape is characterized by significant disparities and persistent challenges. The national literacy rate stands at 60.65% (2023), which is noted as lower than its neighbors in South Asia, and it is linked to issues such as poverty, lack of basic needs fulfillment, feudalism, and patriarchal leadership, particularly affecting females in rural areas. Key contributors to low educational levels include insufficient government oversight, scarcity of quality institutions, teacher absenteeism, and a general lack of awareness.

The formal education structure includes pre-school education (ages 3–5), junior school (years 1–5), and middle school (years 6–8). Secondary education lasts four years, culminating in the Secondary School Certificate (SSC). Students can also pursue alternative qualifications, such as the General Certificate of Education (GCE) or Advanced Placement (AP). Tertiary education involves professional institutes for Bachelor's degrees (e.g., in engineering, medicine) and universities offering Bachelor of Arts (BA), Bachelor of Science (BSc), and Bachelor of Commerce (BCom). Despite reforms, gender disparity remains a major issue, with male enrollment significantly outpacing female enrollment across various educational levels.
