Pakistan’s education system faces significant challenges and disparities, particularly in **literacy rates** and **gender inequality**. As of 2023, the national literacy rate stands at **60.65%**, with males at **68%** and females at **52.84%**, reflecting a stark gender gap. Urban areas exhibit higher literacy rates (74.09%) compared to rural regions (51.56%).

**Education infrastructure** is a critical issue, with many schools lacking proper facilities. Over **3,000 government school buildings** in Punjab are structurally unsafe, posing risks to students and teachers during monsoon seasons. Despite efforts to improve education, **funding remains insufficient**, with public expenditure on education hovering around **2% of GDP**—one of the lowest in the region. The system also struggles with **teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and limited access to quality education**, particularly in rural and underprivileged areas.

**Gender disparity** persists, with women facing systemic barriers to education. Only **18% of Pakistani women** have received **10 years or more of schooling**, and female enrollment rates are significantly lower than males across all education levels. Cultural norms, economic constraints, and lack of infrastructure often prevent girls from accessing education. While some progress has been made, such as the **legal right to primary education for children aged 5–16**, systemic issues like **parental preference for boys, high tuition costs, and cultural stigma** continue to hinder female education.

**Vocational and technical education** is underdeveloped, with limited opportunities for skill-based training. **Madrasas** (Islamic religious schools) play a significant role in education, particularly in rural areas, but their focus on rote memorization and religious studies often limits academic and professional development. **Higher education** remains accessible to a small percentage of the population, with only **6% of Pakistanis** holding university degrees as of 2007, and women’s representation in higher education is notably low.

**Reforms and challenges** include efforts to increase education expenditure, improve teacher training, and promote gender equality. However, **political instability, economic constraints, and cultural resistance** often hinder progress. The system’s reliance on **public sector funding and traditional educational methods** also limits innovation and adaptability to modern needs. Addressing these issues requires **increased investment, policy reforms, and community engagement** to ensure equitable and quality education for all Pakistanis.
