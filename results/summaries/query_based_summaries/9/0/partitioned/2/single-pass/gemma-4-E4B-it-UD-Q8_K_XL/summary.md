**Education in Pakistan**

Education in Pakistan faces significant challenges, including a low literacy rate of 60.65% (as of 2022), placing it as the second lowest in South Asia after Afghanistan. Key contributing factors to low educational attainment include poverty, lack of basic needs fulfillment, feudal and patriarchist leadership in rural areas, and low access to formal education, particularly for females. While there have been improvements in gender disparity in enrollment ratios across primary, middle, and secondary levels since the 1990s, low female enrollment remains a prevalent issue, contributing to a significant gap in literacy rates between men (around 67%) and women (around 42%).

The national education system is largely inspired by the English educational system. Primary education covers years 1 to 5, followed by middle school (years 6 to 8), and secondary education runs for four years (starting in year 9). The curriculum covers disciplines such as Arts, Computer Studies and ICT, General Science, Mathematics, and Religious Education (Islamic Studies). Despite governmental efforts, the quality of education faces criticism due to outdated curriculum, teacher shortages, and a reliance on cramming rather than skill development. Additionally, the relationship between education and religion is complex, with many poor families turning to madrasas due to state system failures.
