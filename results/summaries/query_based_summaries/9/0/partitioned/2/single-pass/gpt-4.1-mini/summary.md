# Summary of Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan follows a system inspired by the English model, spanning from pre-school (ages 3–5) through primary (years 1–5), middle (years 6–8), secondary (years 9–12), and tertiary education. Primary education completion is about 67.5%, with eight common subjects including Arts, Sciences, Languages (Urdu and English), Mathematics, and Religious Education. Secondary education culminates in nationally standardized exams (SSC and HSSC) covering various streams like pre-medical, pre-engineering, and humanities. Tertiary education offers both two-year Pass and four-year Honours bachelor's degrees, alongside professional degrees in fields such as engineering, medicine, and law. Alternative qualifications include British GCE O and A Levels, and vocational diplomas like the Diploma of Associate Engineering (DAE). Teacher education varies from associate degrees to PhDs but faces criticism for being overly knowledge-based and lacking application focus.

Despite efforts to improve, Pakistan faces significant challenges including low literacy rates (around 60.65%), especially among females with only 18% receiving 10 or more years of schooling. Gender disparity remains severe due to cultural, infrastructural, and socio-economic barriers. Female enrollment is lower, and female teachers and leadership are underrepresented. Quality issues prevail with outdated curricula, teacher shortages, poorly equipped labs, and rote-learning predominating. Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) has gained attention to enhance workforce skills. The COVID-19 pandemic spurred distance learning initiatives like Teleschool to reach remote and disadvantaged students. Pakistan has a notable presence of madrassas serving poor families with religious education, sometimes outnumbering public schools in certain regions. Recent reforms aim to address gender disparities, improve educational quality, and expand access, yet systemic issues persist.
