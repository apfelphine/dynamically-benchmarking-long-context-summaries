Education in Pakistan is characterized by a multi-stage formal system heavily influenced by the English model, yet it faces significant challenges including low enrollment rates and substantial gender disparities. Primary education completion stands at approximately 67.5%, with a net attendance rate of 66% for both sexes, falling below the global average. The curriculum covers disciplines such as science, mathematics, and religion, with language of instruction varying between English and Urdu mediums. Secondary education spans years 9–12 and culminates in the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC), often followed by specialized streams in pre-medical, engineering, or humanities. Tertiary education is expanding, with plans to increase university graduate numbers from 6% to 15% by 2020, though only about 6% of the population currently holds a degree. Accreditation for professional degrees like medicine and engineering is managed by specific councils, while teacher education programs face criticism for being knowledge-based rather than application-oriented.

The system operates alongside diverse non-formal and alternative pathways, including Madrassas (Islamic seminaries), which are popular among the poorest families and sometimes outnumber public schools, and technical/vocational training offered through boards like the Punjab Board of Technical Education. Gender disparity remains a critical issue; women comprise only 42% of the adult literate population and face significant barriers to enrollment, particularly in rural areas, due to cultural norms, lack of safe infrastructure, and parental preference for educating sons. While literacy rates have improved over the decades, with 60.65% of the population literate as of 2023, the quality of education is often cited as declining due to outdated curricula, shortages of qualified teachers, and inadequate facilities. Furthermore, the government has committed to raising education expenditure to 7% of GDP, though historical spending has largely remained stagnant around 2% of GDP.
