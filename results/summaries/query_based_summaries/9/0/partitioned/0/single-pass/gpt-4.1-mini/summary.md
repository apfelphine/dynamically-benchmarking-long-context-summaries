# Education in Pakistan: Summary

Education in Pakistan follows a formal structure inspired by the English system, comprising primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education (grades 1–5) is completed by about 67.5% of children, with curricula covering arts, sciences, languages, religious education, and social studies. Secondary education spans four years (grades 9–12) culminating in national examinations for Secondary School Certificate (SSC) and Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC). Alternative qualifications like GCE O/A Levels and technical/vocational education are also available. Tertiary education includes bachelor's, master's, MPhil, and PhD degrees, with a focus on professional degrees in engineering, medicine, law, and science. Despite reforms, challenges such as outdated curricula, teacher shortages, inadequate infrastructure, and low public expenditure (around 2% of GDP) hinder education quality. Gender disparity remains significant, with female literacy and enrollment rates substantially lower than males, due to cultural norms, safety concerns, and resource limitations, although some progress has been noted in recent decades.

The education system also includes non-formal and informal sectors, vocational training, and Islamic seminaries (madrasas), which are prevalent especially in underserved areas. Distance learning initiatives like Teleschool were launched during COVID-19 to improve access, particularly for girls and rural students. Teacher education requires reforms, emphasizing updated training, human rights awareness, and application-focused methods. Gender disparities extend beyond enrollment to academic performance and representation in STEM fields; systemic barriers and societal attitudes impede female participation and career advancement. While education is a legal right for children aged 5 to 16, significant regional and gender inequalities persist, demanding sustained policy attention and innovative solutions to improve accessibility, quality, and inclusivity.
