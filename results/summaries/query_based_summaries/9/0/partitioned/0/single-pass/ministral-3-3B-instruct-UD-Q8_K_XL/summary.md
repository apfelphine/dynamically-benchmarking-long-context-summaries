Pakistan’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, tertiary, and non-formal education**, with significant challenges and disparities.

### **Primary Education**
- **Completion Rate:** Only **67.5%** of Pakistani children finish primary school, far below the global average of 90%.
- **Curriculum:** Inspired by the English system, it includes subjects like Urdu, English, mathematics, science, Islamic studies, and Pakistan Studies. Provincial and regional languages (e.g., Punjabi, Sindhi) are taught in respective provinces.
- **Gender Disparity:** Female enrollment remains low, with **18%** of women receiving 10+ years of schooling. The **female-to-male ratio** improved from **0.47 (1990)** to **0.74 (1999)**, but disparities persist, especially in rural areas.
- **Infrastructure:** Many government schools lack proper facilities, contributing to low attendance and dropout rates.

### **Secondary Education**
- **Structure:** Secondary education spans **9th to 12th grade**, with standardized exams (SSC and HSSC) determining progression.
- **Vocational Pathways:** Technical education (e.g., DAE in engineering) offers vocational training alongside academic routes.
- **Alternative Qualifications:** Students can pursue **GCE (Cambridge/PEARSON)**, **IGCSE**, or **AP** exams, though these are less common.
- **Gender Disparity:** Women face systemic barriers, including cultural norms, limited teacher roles, and societal stigma, despite some progress in enrollment rates.

### **Tertiary Education**
- **University Enrollment:** Only **6%** of Pakistanis (9% men, 3.5% women) hold university degrees as of 2007, with plans to increase this to **15% by 2020**.
- **Higher Education:** Universities offer **Bachelor’s, Master’s, and PhD** programs in fields like engineering, medicine, law, and the arts. Teacher education programs vary in quality, with many lacking qualified faculty.
- **Vocational Training:** Technical and vocational education (TVET) is expanding, though access remains limited in rural areas.

### **Non-Formal and Informal Education**
- **Madrassas:** Islamic seminaries (madrassas) are popular among poorer families, offering free education and religious instruction. Estimated at **12,000–40,000**, they often outnumber public schools in some regions.
- **Informal Education:** NGOs and corporates provide training in vocational skills, though rural students face internet access barriers during COVID-19.

### **Key Challenges**
- **Funding:** Public expenditure on education is **~2% of GDP**, with **88%** spent on lower-level education, leaving higher education underfunded.
- **Quality:** Outdated curricula, poorly trained teachers, and lack of modern facilities hinder learning outcomes.
- **Gender Inequality:** Women’s education remains underfunded, with systemic discrimination in access, teaching roles, and career opportunities.
- **Literacy Rates:** Pakistan’s literacy rate is **60.65%**, with significant disparities between urban (74.09%) and rural (51.56%) areas.

### **Reforms and Achievements**
- **Education Policy:** The **2009 National Education Policy** aims to increase education spending to **7% of GDP** and improve infrastructure.
- **STEM Representation:** Women in STEM fields (e.g., medicine, engineering) face barriers but show progress in enrollment.
- **International Recognition:** Pakistan’s universities have gained visibility in global rankings (e.g., **NUST** ranked in the **top 100 QS Asia University Rankings** in 2018).

The system remains **fragmented, underfunded, and gender-biased**, requiring significant reforms to address equity, quality, and accessibility.
