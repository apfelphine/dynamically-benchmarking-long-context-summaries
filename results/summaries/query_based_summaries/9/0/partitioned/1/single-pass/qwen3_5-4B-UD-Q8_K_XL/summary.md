# Education in Pakistan

Education in Pakistan faces significant challenges characterized by severe gender disparity, inadequate infrastructure, and low literacy rates. Gender discrimination is most pronounced among the poorest households, with female enrollment and literacy rates consistently lagging behind males (e.g., 42% female literacy versus 67% male literacy). Despite government initiatives and the Millennium Development Goals, the female-to-male enrollment ratio has only improved gradually, and women often face cultural barriers, lack of safe infrastructure, and limited access to higher education, particularly in leadership roles. Additionally, the system suffers from chronic underfunding, with public education expenditure historically hovering around 2% of GDP, leading to overcrowded, unsafe school buildings and outdated curricula that emphasize rote memorization over critical thinking and scientific inquiry. Religious seminaries (madrasas) play a significant role in rural areas, often providing free education but reinforcing traditional gender roles and, in some cases, militant ideologies.

The structure of formal education includes primary (years 1–5), middle (years 6–8), and secondary (years 9–12) levels, with students eventually pursuing tertiary degrees in professional institutes or universities. While Pakistan has produced notable alumni in science and technology, including Nobel laureates and world-class researchers, the overall quality of education remains a concern due to a shortage of qualified teachers, a lack of modern facilities, and a curriculum that is often disconnected from market needs. Recent developments include the introduction of low-cost private schools and digital learning initiatives, such as the Teleschool during the pandemic, aimed at improving access. However, without addressing deep-rooted issues like parental preference for educating boys, political instability, and insufficient budgetary allocations, the system struggles to meet the educational needs of its diverse population.
