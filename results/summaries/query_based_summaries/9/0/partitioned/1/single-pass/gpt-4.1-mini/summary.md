# Education in Pakistan: Summary

Pakistan’s education system, largely inspired by the English model, comprises stages from pre-school through tertiary education. Primary education (years 1–5) follows pre-schooling and is succeeded by middle school (years 6–8), with a preference for single-sex education in many areas. Secondary education (years 9–12) leads to nationally standardized exams, awarding the Secondary School Certificate and Higher Secondary School Certificate. Students can pursue academic or vocational streams, with alternatives like the British GCE qualifications also available. Tertiary education includes professional degrees (engineering, medicine, law, etc.) and general degrees (BA, BSc, BCom), with increasing emphasis on four-year honors programs. Teacher education exists but faces criticism for being knowledge-focused and lacking practical training. Non-formal education and madrassas also play a role, especially among poorer populations.

Despite some progress, Pakistan struggles with significant challenges. Gender disparity remains a major issue, with female literacy at 42% compared to 67% for males and strong socio-cultural barriers limiting girls' access to education. Infrastructure deficits, low government spending (around 2% of GDP), and uneven quality further hinder progress. Religious factors intertwine deeply with education, particularly in rural areas where madrasas are prominent. Literacy rates have improved over time but remain low relative to neighbors. International education and a growing network of private and low-cost private schools offer alternatives but may also exacerbate inequalities. Efforts to reform and increase educational access continue amid socio-political complexities.
