## Education in Pakistan

The Pakistani education system faces significant challenges, particularly concerning gender disparity, as evidenced by the fact that only 18% of Pakistani women have completed 10 years or more of schooling. Although progress has been made in closing the gender gap in enrollment across different levels—with disparities decreasing notably at the secondary level—low female enrollment remains prevalent. Literacy rates show a substantial difference between genders, with men at around 67% versus women at 42%. Major barriers include parental preference for educating boys, lack of safe physical infrastructure for girls, and cultural/religious barriers.

Beyond gender issues, the quality of education is declining due to outdated curricula, teacher shortages, and poorly equipped laboratories, resulting in students lacking professional and communication skills. While the government has set ambitious targets for education expenditure (aiming for 7% of GDP), chronic underinvestment has led to deteriorating infrastructure, such as structurally unsafe government schools. Furthermore, the education system is deeply intertwined with religion, with many families relying on *madrassas* due to the failings of the public system.
