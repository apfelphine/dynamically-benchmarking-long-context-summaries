## Education in Poland

**Compulsory Education and Structure:**
Compulsory education in Poland is structured with primary school typically lasting eight years (grades 1–8). Historically, the system included a three-year junior high school ($\text{gimnazjum}$), but this was abolished by the 2016 "Law on School Education," extending primary school to eight years starting in September 2017. Compulsory education concludes at the age of 18. Contemporary law differentiates between compulsory school ($\text{obowiązek szkolny}$) and compulsory education ($\text{obowiązek nauki}$).

**Reforms and Higher Education:**
A significant organizational reform occurred in 1999, restructuring education to 6 (primary) + 3 (junior high school) + 3 (senior high school) years. Later, the 2017 reform reverted the structure to 8 (primary school) + 4/5 (high school) years. Poland's education system adheres to the Bologna scheme at the tertiary level, primarily comprising a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, although some master's degrees can be longer. University-level grading uses a numeric system from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with grading occurring every semester.
