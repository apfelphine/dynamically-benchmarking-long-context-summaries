**Education in Poland**  
Poland’s education system features compulsory education from age 6 to 18, structured in primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Primary education (6–12 years) was traditionally 6 years (grades 1–6), followed by a 3-year junior high school (gimnazjum), but a 2017 reform extended primary education to 8 years (grades 1–8) and eliminated gimnazjum. Secondary education includes general (liceum) and vocational tracks. Students in general secondary schools take the *Matura* exam, which grants access to higher education. Vocational education is offered through technical schools (5 years) and basic vocational schools (3 years), with some graduates earning the *Matura* after additional study.  

Tertiary education follows the Bologna model, with a three-year bachelor’s degree followed by a two-year master’s. Some programs (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) last longer. Doctoral programs take about three years. The grading system at the university level uses a numeric scale from 2.0 to 5.0, with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade. A failing grade (2.0) allows retaking, and grades are assessed semester-by-semester.  

The curriculum emphasizes core subjects, with foreign languages (English and German) taught in primary and secondary schools. Reforms in the 2017–2019 period shifted the structure to better balance academic and vocational pathways, though concerns remain about historical content in history education, including the removal of "communism" and overemphasis on national and military figures. The system also includes provisions for students with disabilities, who may attend special schools to prepare for the *Matura*.  

Public spending on education is significant, and the system has seen improvements in literacy, with only 1.2% of the population illiterate by 1978. However, challenges remain in teacher training, infrastructure, and equitable access.
