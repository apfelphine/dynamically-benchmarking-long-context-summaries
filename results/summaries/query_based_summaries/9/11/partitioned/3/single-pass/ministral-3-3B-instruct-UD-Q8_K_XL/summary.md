Poland’s education system emphasizes compulsory education from ages 6 to 18, structured into primary, junior high, and upper secondary levels. Primary school (szkoła podstawowa) spans eight years (previously six), starting at age 7, covering core subjects like Polish, mathematics, and foreign languages. Junior high (gimnazjum) was abolished in 2017, merging into primary education, while upper secondary education includes general schools (liceum) for academic preparation, vocational schools (technikum), and specialized schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa) for trade-focused training. The Matura exam, taken after four years of liceum or five years of technical school, grants access to higher education.

Poland follows the **Bologna Process**, with tertiary education divided into a three-year bachelor’s degree and a two-year master’s degree, though some programs (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) require longer cycles. Doctoral studies typically take three years. The system also includes teacher training colleges for primary educators. Grading at universities uses a 2–5 scale, with 5.0 being the highest. Vocational education is managed by post-secondary schools, offering shorter programs (2.5 years). The curriculum has faced reforms, including a 2017 shift from 6+3 to 8+4 years, though debates persist over curriculum content and inclusivity.
