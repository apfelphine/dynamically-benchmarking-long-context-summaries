# Education in Poland

Poland’s education system includes compulsory education starting typically at age six or seven, lasting 12 years, ending around age 18. Primary education (szkoła podstawowa) lasts eight years (grades 1–8), a reform implemented from 2017 replaced the previous system of six years of primary plus three years of junior high (gimnazjum). Before 2017, gimnazjum formed the lower secondary education, lasting three years and covering subjects like Polish, history, languages, sciences, and physical education. Upper secondary education follows compulsory schooling and prepares students for the labor market or higher education. The 1999 reform formerly organized schooling as 6 (primary) + 3 (gimnazjum) + 3 (liceum), but the 2017 reform reverted it to 8 (primary) + 4 or 5 (high school).

Historically, compulsory education was first proposed in the 16th century, introduced in parts of Poland under Prussian and Austrian control in the 19th century, and fully implemented in 1919 after independence. Illiteracy was significantly reduced post-World War II, reaching near eradication by the late 20th century. Polish students often study one or two foreign languages, with English and German being the most common. Education reforms in 1999 improved Poland’s OECD rankings in reading, science, and mathematics. In 2006, a "zero tolerance" school reform was launched to address school violence, granting teachers the status of civil servants and enabling stricter penalties for crimes in schools.
