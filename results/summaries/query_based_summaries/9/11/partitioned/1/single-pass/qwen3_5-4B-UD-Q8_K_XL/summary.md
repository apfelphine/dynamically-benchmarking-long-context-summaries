# Education in Poland

The Polish education system is structured into three main stages: primary, secondary, and tertiary education. **Compulsory education** covers eight years of primary school (grades 1–8), a reform that merged the previous six-year primary system with the three-year junior high school (*gimnazjum*). Following primary education, students pursue **upper secondary education**, which offers various pathways including general secondary schools (*liceum*) leading to the *Matura* qualification, technical schools (*technikum*) for five-year vocational training, basic vocational schools (*zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*) for three-year certificates, and profiled schools (*liceum profilowane*) for specific vocational fields. Specialized schools also exist for students with physical or mental disabilities.

**Tertiary education** in Poland operates under the Bologna Process, consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, with doctoral programs lasting approximately three years. University grading uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with grading occurring twice a semester. The system is characterized by a strong emphasis on the *Matura* exam for university access and a mix of public and private institutions. Historically, Poland established the world's first state ministry of education in 1773 and boasts the Jagiellonian University in Kraków, founded in 1364, as one of the oldest universities in Central Europe. Recent reforms in 2017 have further adjusted the structure to extend primary education and high school duration.
