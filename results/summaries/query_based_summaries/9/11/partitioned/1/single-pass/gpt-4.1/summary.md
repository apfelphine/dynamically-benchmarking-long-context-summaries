## Education in Poland: Concise Summary

Education in Poland is compulsory from the ages of 6 to 18, encompassing twelve years. Primary school (szkoła podstawowa) begins at age six or seven and lasts eight years, after which students enter upper secondary education. Historically, the system underwent major reforms: in 1999, it was restructured into 6 years of primary, 3 years of junior high (gimnazjum), and 3 years of senior high (liceum); in 2017, the structure reverted to an 8-year primary followed by 4/5 years of high school, abolishing gimnazjum. School attendance and literacy have been national priorities since the early 20th century. As of the late 1970s, illiteracy was virtually eradicated in Poland.

Upper secondary education includes general (liceum), vocational (technikum), and special schools. Liceum lasts four years and culminates in the Matura exam, which grants access to higher education. Vocational education is provided by technical schools (five years, leading to the Matura) and basic vocational schools (three years, leading to a competence certificate and, with additional study, eligibility for Matura). Special schools are available for students with disabilities. Tertiary education follows the Bologna model, usually comprising a three-year bachelor's degree and a two-year master's degree, with certain programs (medicine, pharmacy) functioning as long-cycle degrees. Doctoral programs typically last around three years. Polish universities use a numeric grading scale (2.0 to 5.0, with increments of 0.5), and assessment occurs every semester. Students commonly study one or two foreign languages, with English being the most popular.
