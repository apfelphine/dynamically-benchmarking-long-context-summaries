## Education in Poland

**Compulsory and Secondary Education:**
Compulsory education in Poland typically starts around age six, with primary school (szkoła podstawowa) traditionally taking eight years. The structure changed significantly with the 2016 "Law on School Education," which abolished the three-year junior high school (gimnazjum) and extended primary school to eight years, effective September 1, 2017. Upper secondary education prepares students for either direct entry into the labor market or tertiary education. Vocational training is offered through technical schools (technikum) or basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa).

**Tertiary and Language Education:**
Poland adheres to the Bologna scheme, featuring a standard two-cycle structure: a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree. Some specialized fields, such as medicine and pharmacy, require longer programs of four to six years for their respective degrees. Regarding language, students in Polish schools are typically required to learn one or two foreign languages; a reform introduced by Minister Katarzyna Hall mandates that lower secondary students learn two different languages, with one generally taught three times a week (usually English).

**University Grading:**
University-level education in Poland utilizes a numeric grading system ranging from 2.0 (failing grade) to 5.0 (highest grade), with increments of 0.5 points. Grading occurs every semester, and the final grade can be based on a single exam or a semester-long point system.
