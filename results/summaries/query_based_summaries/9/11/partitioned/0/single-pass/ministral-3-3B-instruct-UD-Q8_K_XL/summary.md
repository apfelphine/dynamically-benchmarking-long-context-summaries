Poland’s education system is structured around **compulsory education** from age 7 to 18, divided into three main stages:

1. **Primary School (Szkoła Podstawowa, ages 7–12)**:
   - Typically lasts **8 years** (previously 6 years until 2017), combining core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages.
   - After 2017, the system was reformed to extend primary education to 8 years, replacing the previous 6-year primary school followed by a 3-year junior high school (*gimnazjum*).

2. **Upper Secondary Education**:
   - **General secondary schools (liceum)** provide a 4-year curriculum leading to the *Matura* exam, granting access to higher education.
   - **Vocational and technical education** is offered through **technika** (5 years) and **zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa** (3 years), preparing students for skilled trades or further academic study.
   - **Special schools** cater to students with disabilities, offering tailored education.

3. **Tertiary Education**:
   - Follows the **Bologna Process**, with programs divided into a **3-year bachelor’s degree** followed by a **2-year master’s degree**.
   - Some specialized degrees (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) take **4–6 years**.
   - Vocational education is provided by **szkoła policealna**, with programs lasting **2.5 years**.

**Key Features**:
- **Compulsory education** begins at age 7 and lasts until age 18.
- **Foreign languages** (primarily English) are mandatory in schools.
- **Grading system** at universities uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent), with frequent semester evaluations.
- **History and social studies** emphasize Polish and European-centric perspectives, with recent reforms altering curriculum content.

The system balances academic rigor with vocational training, aiming to prepare students for both higher education and the workforce.
