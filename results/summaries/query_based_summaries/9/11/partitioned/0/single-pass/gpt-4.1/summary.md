## Education in Poland – Summary

Education in Poland is compulsory for children aged 7 to 18, encompassing primary and secondary schooling. The system has undergone several reforms: historically, Poland featured eight years of primary school followed by four years of high school (liceum). In 1999, the structure shifted to six years of primary, three years of junior high school (gimnazjum), and three/four years of senior high school. In 2017, a reform reverted the structure to eight years of primary and four/five years of high school. Primary education begins at age 6 or 7 and is followed by upper secondary options, including general (liceum) and technical schools (technikum), or basic vocational schools. General secondary schools prepare students for higher education through the Matura exam; technical schools last five years and also lead to the Matura, focusing on trades. Vocational schools offer three-year programs granting certificates, with pathways to the Matura via further study.

Tertiary education adheres to the Bologna scheme: a three-year bachelor’s degree followed by a two-year master’s, or some integrated long-cycle master’s programs, with doctoral programs taking about three years. University grading uses a 2.0–5.0 scale, with 3.0 as the lowest passing grade. Foreign language learning is emphasized—English, German, French, and Spanish are the most common. Historically, Poland valued education early, establishing the Jagiellonian University in 1364 and creating the world’s first state ministry of education in 1773. By the late 20th century, illiteracy had all but disappeared, and recent reforms aimed to improve academic standards and adapt curricula to contemporary needs.
