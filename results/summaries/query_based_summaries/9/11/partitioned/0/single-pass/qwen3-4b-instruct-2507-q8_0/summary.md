**Education in Poland**

**Compulsory Education:**
- Children typically start primary school (szkoła podstawowa) at age 6 or 7, after attending kindergarten. 
- Prior to 2017, primary school lasted 6 years (grades 1–6), followed by a 3-year middle school (gimnazjum). 
- In 2017, the "Law on School Education" abolished gimnazjum and extended primary education to 8 years (grades 1–8), making the total compulsory education span 12 years.

**Upper Secondary Education:**
- After completing primary school, students enter upper secondary education, which includes:
  - **General secondary schools (liceum)**: Students take the "Matura" exam, which grants access to higher education.
  - **Technical schools (technikum)**: Offer vocational training and lead to the Matura.
  - **Basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa)**: Provide vocational education for 3 years, with a certificate of competence. Graduates can take the Matura after an additional 2 or 3 years in a general secondary school.
  - **Profiled general secondary schools (liceum profilowane)**: Offer vocational education in fields described by the Polish Classification of Activities (PKD), lasting 4 years.

**Tertiary Education:**
- Poland follows the Bologna system, with a three-cycle structure:
  - **Bachelor's degree (3 years)**: Followed by a **master's degree (2 years)**.
  - Some programs (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) are longer (5–6 years).
- Doctoral programs last about 3 years.
- The diploma for primary school teachers requires 3 years of study at a teacher training college.
- Vocational education is handled by post-secondary schools (szkoła policealna), with programs lasting 2.5 years.

**Grading System:**
- University-level education uses a numeric scale from 2 to 5, with most grades including 0.5 increments.
- A grade of 2.0 is failing; 3.0 is the lowest passing grade, with 5.0 being the highest.
- Students can fail a subject and retake it, with no formal grade of 2.5.

**Foreign Languages:**
- Students typically study one or two foreign languages.
- In 2005/06, 67.9% studied English, 33.3% studied German, and 13.3% studied French.
- A 2017 reform required students in lower secondary schools to study two foreign languages, with English taught three times a week and a second language twice a week.
- Language exam results influence eligibility for upper secondary school entry.

**Historical Context:**
- Poland's education system has roots dating back to the 12th century, with the Jagiellonian University (founded 1364) being one of Europe’s oldest.
- The 1773 Commission of National Education was the world’s first state ministry of education.
- Major reforms occurred in 1999 and 2017, shifting the structure from 6+3+3 to 8+4+5 years, emphasizing core subjects and delaying vocational training.
