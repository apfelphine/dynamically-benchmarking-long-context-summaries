## Education in Poland

Education in Poland begins with compulsory schooling starting around age six, lasting 12 years, typically ending at age 18 after completing high school. The education system has undergone significant reforms; the 1999 reform changed the structure from 8 years primary plus 4 years high school to 6 years primary, 3 years junior high (gimnazjum), and 3 years senior high (liceum). However, the 2017 reform reverted this system back to 8 years of primary school followed by 4 to 5 years of high school. Upper secondary education includes general secondary schools (liceum), vocational and technical education provided by technical schools (technikum) lasting five years, and basic vocational schools lasting three years with options to earn the Matura exam for tertiary education access. Special schools serve mentally or physically handicapped students preparing them for the Matura in three years.

At the tertiary level, Poland follows the Bologna Process with most degrees structured as a three-year bachelor's followed by a two-year master's, with some long-cycle master's programs in fields like medicine, lasting four to six years. Doctoral programs typically last about three years. Vocational post-secondary education lasts around two and a half years. The university grading system uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (highest), with increments of 0.5 and occasional exceeds-expectations grades.

Foreign language education is emphasized, with most students learning one or two languages, commonly English (67.9%) and German (33.3%). Lower secondary schools require learning two foreign languages, with standardized exams impacting progression to upper secondary education. Poland has a long tradition of education dating back to the 12th century, with the Jagiellonian University founded in 1364 as one of Europe's oldest higher education institutions.
