Education in Poland is characterized by a unified, state-funded system with a strong emphasis on core subjects and recent structural reforms. Historically, compulsory education was introduced in 1919 and remains a priority, ending at age 18. A major 1999 reform reorganized the structure from an eight-year primary school followed by four years of high school into a six-year primary, three-year junior high (*gimnazjum*), and three-year senior high (*liceum*) model. In 2017, the PiS government reversed this structure, extending primary education to eight years and abolishing the junior high stage to revert to the pre-1999 system of 8 years of primary followed by 4 or 5 years of secondary education. This reform also shifted the focus of history curricula toward political and military nationalism.

The Polish education system is diverse, offering general secondary schools (*liceum*) that prepare students for university through the national *Matura* exam, technical schools (*technikum*) for vocational training leading to a *Matura*, and basic vocational schools (*zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*) for immediate entry into the workforce. Tertiary education follows the Bologna process, consisting of three-year bachelor's degrees and two-year master's degrees, with doctoral programs lasting approximately three years. The grading system uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with semesters graded individually.
