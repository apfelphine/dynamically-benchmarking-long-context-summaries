Poland’s education system emphasizes compulsory education, starting at age 6 with primary school (grades 1–8), which was reformed in 2017 to extend to eight years. Compulsory education concludes at age 18, with students transitioning to upper secondary education, which includes general secondary schools (liceum), vocational schools (technikum), and specialized schools for the disabled. The curriculum includes core subjects like Polish, mathematics, and foreign languages (English, German, French, etc.), with a focus on critical thinking and practical skills.

The system follows the Bologna Process, with tertiary education structured into bachelor’s (3 years) and master’s (2 years) degrees, though some programs like medicine and pharmacy span five to six years. University-level grading ranges from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (exceeds expectations). Poland has historically prioritized education, with literacy rates improving significantly post-WWII, though challenges persist in rural areas and access to quality education. Recent reforms aim to modernize curricula and reduce overcrowding in schools.
