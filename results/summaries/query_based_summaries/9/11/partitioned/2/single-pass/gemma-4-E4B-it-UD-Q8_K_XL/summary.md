## Education in Poland

**History and Compulsory Education:**
The concept of compulsory education was first proposed in Poland in 1555. After Poland regained independence in 1921, compulsory education was introduced by decree in February 1919 for all children aged 7 to 14. Following World War II, it remained a state priority, and by 1978, the illiteracy rate had dropped to 1.2%. Compulsory education currently ends at age 18. A major organizational overhaul occurred in 1999, changing the structure to 6 (primary) + 3 (junior high) + 3 (senior high) years.

**Modern Reforms and Structure:**
A significant 2017 reform by the PiS government reverted the structure to 8 (primary) + 4/5 (high school) years. In general secondary schools (*liceum*), students can complete the "Matura" after four years to gain access to higher education, or they can attend technical schools (*technikum*) which last five years and also lead to the Matura, focusing on trades like accounting and mechanics. Vocational training is also available through basic vocational schools (*zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*).

**Language Education:**
Students in Polish schools typically learn one or two foreign languages. Following an education reform, students in lower secondary schools were required to learn two foreign languages: one taught three times a week (usually English) and the second taught twice a week. These language examinations contribute to criteria for applying to upper secondary level schools.
