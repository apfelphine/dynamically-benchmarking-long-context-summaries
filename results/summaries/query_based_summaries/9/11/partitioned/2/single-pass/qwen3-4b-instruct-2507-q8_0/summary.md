**Education in Poland**  
Poland’s education system features a comprehensive structure with compulsory education ending at age 18. Primary education (szkoła podstawowa) began at age 6–7 and was previously 6 years long (grades 1–6), followed by a 3-year middle school (gimnazjum). This changed in 2017 with a major reform: primary education was extended to 8 years (grades 1–8), and gimnazjum was abolished. The reform aimed to improve academic performance and align with OECD rankings.  

Upper secondary education includes general schools (liceum), where students take the "Matura" exam to access higher education, and vocational schools (technikum or zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), offering technical training. Vocational programs last 3–5 years and lead to a Matura or a vocational certificate.  

Tertiary education follows the Bologna model: a 3-year bachelor’s degree followed by a 2-year master’s. Some programs, like medicine and pharmacy, last 5–6 years. Doctoral programs take about 3 years. The grading system uses a 2–5 scale, with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade.  

Foreign language instruction is required, with students learning two languages—typically English and another (e.g., German, French)—in lower secondary school. The 2017 reform also revised history curricula, removing references to communism and emphasizing political and military leaders, which has drawn criticism for downplaying the role of lower social classes.  

The system emphasizes national identity, with language and history curricula reflecting Polish-centric narratives. Despite reforms, challenges remain, including teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and concerns over educational equity and student well-being.
