**Education in Poland**

Poland’s education system is structured into **compulsory education** (obowiązek szkolny) and **compulsory education** (obowiązek nauki), with the former covering ages 6–18 and the latter extending to age 21. **Primary school (szkoła podstawowa, ages 6–13)** consists of eight years, replacing the previous six-year system and the three-year **gimnazjum (middle school)**. After primary school, students transition to **upper secondary education**, which includes **general secondary schools (liceum)** for four years leading to the "Matura" exam (required for university admission), **vocational schools (technikum, 5 years)** for technical training, or **basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa, 3 years)** for practical skills. Students with disabilities attend **special schools (szkoła specjalna)**.

Tertiary education follows the **Bologna Process**, with programs divided into **bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and doctoral (3 years)** degrees. The system emphasizes standardized exams, with a **numerical grading scale (2–5)**. Foreign language instruction typically includes **English (67.9% of students)**, with other languages like German, French, and Spanish offered at lower rates. The education system has undergone significant reforms, including the **2017 overhaul**, which extended primary school to eight years and adjusted curriculum content, though debates persist regarding its impact on historical and social education.

Key challenges include **high academic pressure**, **limited access to resources in rural areas**, and **social disparities** affecting educational outcomes. The system also incorporates **inclusive education** for students with disabilities, though implementation varies. Overall, Poland’s education system balances **traditional academic rigor** with **vocational training**, reflecting its commitment to both intellectual and practical development.
