Education in Poland is structured across different levels, starting with primary school (szkoła podstawowa), which typically takes eight years (grades 1–8). Before 2017, this was preceded by a six-year primary school followed by a three-year *gimnazjum* (middle school); this system was reformed to abolish the *gimnazjum* and extend primary school to eight years as of September 1, 2017.

Upper secondary education follows full-time compulsory education, preparing students for the labor market or tertiary education. General education leads to the "Matura" qualification, which grants access to higher education in general secondary schools (*liceum*). Vocational and technical education is primarily provided by technical schools (*technikum*) or basic vocational schools (*zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*). Tertiary education follows the Bologna scheme, typically consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, though some specialized fields, like medicine, have longer cycles. University-level grading uses a numeric system from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest grade).
