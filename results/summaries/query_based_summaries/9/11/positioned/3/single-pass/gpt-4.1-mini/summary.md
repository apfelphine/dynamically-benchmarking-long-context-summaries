**Education in Poland**

In Poland, compulsory education begins typically at age six or seven, with children starting primary school (szkoła podstawowa) which lasts eight years (grades 1–8). Prior to a 2017 reform, primary education lasted six years, followed by a mandatory three-year junior high school (gimnazjum), but gimnazjum was abolished as part of education restructuring that started in 2017. Upper secondary education follows, available through general secondary schools (liceum), technical schools (technikum), or basic vocational schools, with liceum lasting four years and culminating in the "Matura" exam, granting access to tertiary education. Vocational schools offer varied trainings leading to certificates and competency qualifications. Poland's tertiary education conforms to the Bologna process, mainly offering a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, with some long-cycle degrees (four to six years) and doctoral studies thereafter.

University grading in Poland uses a numeric scale from 2.0 to 5.0 (with 2.0 failing), often including 0.5 increments. Students typically study one or two foreign languages in school, primarily English and German, with reforms making two foreign languages obligatory at the lower secondary level. Historically, Poland has had a deep tradition of education dating back to the 12th century, with notable institutions like Jagiellonian University founded in 1364 and the world's first ministry of education established in 1773. Recent educational reforms in 1999 and 2017 have shaped the current structure, including reinstating an eight-year primary school system and revising historical content and curriculum focus. Compulsory education in Poland ends at age 18 after typically 12 years of schooling.
