**Education in Poland**

- **Primary School**: Begins at age 6 or 7, lasting 8 years (grades 1–8). Replaced the previous 6-year primary + 3-year middle school system introduced in 2017.
- **Middle/Secondary School**: Previously known as *gimnazjum*, now abolished; students transition directly to upper secondary education.
- **Upper Secondary Education**: Prepares students for university or direct entry into the workforce. Includes general education (leading to *matura*), vocational schools (technical schools or basic vocational schools), and profiled general secondary schools (liceum profilowane).
- **Tertiary Education**: Follows the Bologna model with a 3-year bachelor’s degree followed by a 2-year master’s degree. Doctoral programs take about 3 years. Teacher training takes 3 years.
- **Grading System**: Uses a 2–5 scale (2 = failing, 5 = excellent); grades are typically assigned at the end of each semester. A failing grade allows students to retake the subject.
- **Foreign Languages**: Students study at least one foreign language (usually English) in lower secondary school; a second language is taught in the final two years.
- **Historical Reforms**: Key reforms include the 1999 restructuring (to 6+3+3) and the 2017 reform (to 8+4+5), which expanded primary education and increased time spent on core subjects.
- **Curriculum and Examinations**: Students take standardized exams at the end of each year, with the *matura* exam at the end of upper secondary school serving as a gateway to higher education.
- **Education Policy**: The Polish government emphasizes national values and history, with curriculum reforms in recent years removing references to communism and focusing on Polish-centric history.
