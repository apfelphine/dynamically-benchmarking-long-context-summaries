# Education in Poland

Poland's education system underwent significant structural reforms, most notably the 2016 "Law on School Education" which abolished the three-year *gimnazjum* (junior high school) and extended primary education to eight years (grades 1–8), effective September 1, 2017. This replaced the previous six-year primary and three-year junior high model. Compulsory education generally lasts until age 18, starting around age six, covering primary, secondary, and vocational tracks. The upper secondary stage includes general education pathways leading to the *Matura* qualification required for university entry, technical schools (five years), and basic vocational schools (three years). Special education has evolved from segregated institutions to an integrated model within ordinary schools, supported by special teachers, though challenges remain for marginalized groups.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna Process, consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, with doctoral programs lasting approximately three years. Public universities offer free tuition for first-time students under age 26, while private institutions charge between €2,000 and €10,000 annually. The grading system uses a scale from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with grades assessed twice a semester. Historically, Poland has a rich educational tradition dating back to the 12th century, including the establishment of the first state ministry of education in 1773 and the oldest university in the country, the Jagiellonian University in Kraków, founded in 1364.
