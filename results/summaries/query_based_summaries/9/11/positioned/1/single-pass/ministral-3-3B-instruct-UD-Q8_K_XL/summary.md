**Education in Poland**

Poland’s education system is structured into **compulsory (obowiązek szkolny)** and **compulsory education (obowiązek nauki)** phases, with the latter extending until age 18. **Primary school (szkoła podstawowa)** lasts eight years (grades 1–8), replacing the previous six-year system introduced in 2017. After primary school, students attend **junior high school (gimnazjum)**, which was abolished in 2019, and replaced by an eight-year primary school followed by **upper secondary education**, which includes **general secondary schools (liceum)**, **vocational schools (technikum)**, and **basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa)**. The **Matura** exam, taken at the end of upper secondary education, grants access to higher education.

Higher education follows the **Bologna Process**, with three main levels: **bachelor’s (licencjat)**, **master’s (magister)**, and **doctoral (doktorat)** degrees. Tertiary education includes universities, vocational schools, and specialized institutions. The system emphasizes **vocational training** alongside academic education, with a focus on practical skills. The curriculum includes mandatory subjects like **Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages**, with English often introduced early. Recent reforms have aimed to modernize the system, though debates persist over curriculum content, particularly in history and social studies.
