## Education in Poland – Summary

Education in Poland is compulsory until age 18 and is structured into several stages. Children typically begin primary school (szkoła podstawowa) at age seven or six, following kindergarten attendance or a readiness assessment. Primary school lasts eight years (grades 1–8) since a 2017 reform that replaced the previous system of six-year primary school plus three-year junior high school (gimnazjum, abolished in 2019). Upper secondary education, taken after primary school, includes general secondary schools (liceum, four years leading to the Matura exam for university access), as well as vocational and technical schools (technikum, five years) and basic vocational schools (three years), with options for further education and Matura eligibility. There are special schools for handicapped students, and foreign language education is widespread with English and German being most popular. Classes for national minorities exist, and recent reforms have increased the emphasis on Polish history and nationalism.

At the tertiary level, Poland follows the Bologna model: three-year bachelor's degrees, two-year master's degrees, and doctoral programs lasting about three years. Some professional degrees require longer integrated programs. The university grading system uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent). Poland has a rich educational history, with Jagiellonian University established in 1364 and the world's first state ministry of education founded in 1773. Compulsory education was introduced nationally in 1919 and now runs from ages 6 to 18. Major system reforms occurred in 1999 and 2017, with improvements noted in international rankings for reading and science. Despite progress, issues such as violence in schools and challenges in curriculum development remain under active review and reform.
