Education in Poland begins with primary school (szkoła podstawowa), typically starting at age 6 or 7, and lasting eight years (grades 1–8). Before 2017, primary school was followed by a three-year middle school (gimnazjum), but this was abolished and replaced by an extended primary school system. The curriculum includes core subjects such as Polish language, history, mathematics, science, and foreign languages, with students required to take standardized exams in humanities, science, and foreign languages at the end of the lower secondary level.

Upper secondary education includes general education (liceum) and vocational tracks. General education leads to the "Matura" qualification, which is required for university admission. Vocational education is provided through technical schools (technikum) and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), which offer certificates and, in some cases, the Matura qualification. Profiled general secondary schools (liceum profilowane) offer vocational education in specific fields. Special schools for developmentally disabled children were replaced by elementary and practical schools, which focus on practical skills and self-sufficiency.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna system, with a three-year bachelor's degree (Bc) and a two-year master's degree (MgA, Mgr, or Ing). Students must pass a final exam and present a thesis. The education system has undergone reforms, including changes to foreign language requirements and exam systems, with a focus on improving access and quality. The grading system uses a numeric scale from 2 to 5, with 5.0 being the highest grade. Poland's education system emphasizes a balance between academic and practical training, with a strong focus on language instruction and international curriculum alignment.
