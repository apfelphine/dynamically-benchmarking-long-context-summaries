## Education in Poland

Compulsory education in Poland begins typically at age six or seven with eight years of primary school (szkoła podstawowa). This system replaced the earlier structure where primary school lasted six years, followed by a mandatory three-year gimnazjum (junior high school), which was abolished starting in 2017 following educational reforms. The eight-year primary school is the current standard, ending compulsory education at age 15.

Upper secondary education offers various pathways including general secondary schools called liceum, which last four years and culminate with the "Matura" exam granting access to higher education. Technical and vocational education is provided by technical schools (technikum) lasting five years and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa) lasting three years. The Matura exam is a graduation requirement from general and technical secondary schools, enabling students to pursue tertiary education.

Tertiary education follows the Bologna Process with bachelor’s degrees (usually three years) and master’s degrees (two years), with some long-cycle programs for fields like medicine and pharmacy. Doctoral programs usually take about three years to complete. Teacher training requires a three-year college program, with vocational education offered at post-secondary schools lasting around two and a half years. The Polish university grading system ranges from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (highest passing), with some increments and special grades varying by institution.

Foreign language education in schools often includes English as the primary foreign language, followed by German, French, Spanish, and Russian. Students typically learn one or two foreign languages, with language exams contributing to progression criteria. The educational reforms of 1999 and 2017 respectively restructured the education system from 6+3+3 years to 8+4/5 years, and reversed earlier system changes, affecting school organization and curricula accordingly.
