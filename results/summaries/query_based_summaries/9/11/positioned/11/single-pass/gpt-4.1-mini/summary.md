# Education in Poland

Compulsory education in Poland begins typically at age six or seven with primary school (szkoła podstawowa), which currently lasts eight years (grades 1–8). This structure replaced the earlier system that comprised six years of primary school followed by three years of junior high school (gimnazjum), which was abolished starting in 2017 following a 2016 educational reform. Until 2019, gimnazjum served as the lower secondary level covering grades 7–9, with a curriculum including Polish, history, foreign languages, sciences, and technology. Students concluded gimnazjum with evaluations based on tests in multiple disciplines. The 2017 reforms extended primary education to eight years and phased out gimnazjum by 2019.

Upper secondary education prepares students either for direct entry into the labor market or for higher education. General secondary schools (liceum) offer four-year programs culminating in the Matura examination, which grants access to universities. Vocational and technical education is provided through five-year technical schools (technikum) that also lead to the Matura, and three-year basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), offering certificates of competence in various trades. Mentally or physically handicapped students may attend special schools preparing them for Matura in three years.

Polish tertiary education adheres to the Bologna Process, typically involving a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree. Some master’s degrees are awarded after extended single-cycle programs of four to six years (e.g., medicine). Doctoral studies generally require about three years. Teacher training for primary education involves three years at teacher training colleges, while vocational education is managed by post-secondary schools with roughly two-and-a-half-year programs. The university grading system uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent), with some institutions awarding intermediate or exceeding-expectations grades.

Foreign language instruction is widespread, with English being the most studied language, followed by German, French, Spanish, and Russian among others. Lower secondary students traditionally learned two foreign languages, with examinations at multiple proficiency levels.

Historically, Poland established one of Europe’s oldest universities, the Jagiellonian University in 1364, and introduced compulsory education in 1919 for children aged 7 to 14. Educational reforms in 1999 shifted the structure to 6+3+3 years (primary, junior high, senior high), improving OECD rankings. The 2017 reforms reverted this to an 8+4/5 system, aiming to extend primary education and alter secondary education durations.

Poland has addressed school violence through a "zero tolerance" policy initiated in 2006, enhancing legal protections for teachers and introducing measures to sanction aggressive pupils and their guardians.
