## Education in Poland

Education in Poland is compulsory from primary through upper secondary levels. Children typically begin primary school (szkoła podstawowa) at age seven or six, with eight years of study now required following a 2016 reform that abolished the previous six-year primary and three-year junior high (gimnazjum) structure. After completing primary education, students enter upper secondary schools, which include general secondary (liceum) and vocational/technical schools (technikum, basic vocational schools), all preparing for the "Matura" examination needed for higher education entry. Technical schools last five years and also lead to the Matura, while basic vocational schools last three years and offer certification in various trades, with pathways to further academic qualifications.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna scheme: a three-year bachelor's degree, followed by a two-year master's, with some fields offering longer unified programs (e.g., medicine, pharmacy). Doctoral programs usually require around three years post-master's. University grading uses a scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent), and grading occurs each semester. Polish schools emphasize foreign language learning: in 2005/06, most students studied English, with German, French, and Spanish also common. Historical education reforms in 1999 and 2017 notably restructured organizational levels and curriculum, with contemporary law upholding compulsory education until age 18.
