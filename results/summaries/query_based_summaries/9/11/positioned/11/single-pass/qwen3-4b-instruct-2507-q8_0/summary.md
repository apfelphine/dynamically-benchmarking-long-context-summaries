Education in Poland features a compulsory education system starting at age 6 or 7, with primary school (podstawówka) lasting eight years (grades 1–8) since the 2017 reform, replacing the previous six-year primary and three-year junior high (gimnazjum) system. Upper secondary education offers general pathways through liceum (leading to the Matura exam for university access) or vocational tracks via technical schools (technikum) or basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa). Tertiary education follows the Bologna Process with a three-year bachelor's and two-year master's structure, and a grading system from 2.0 to 5.0 (with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade). Foreign language education is mandatory, requiring two languages (typically English and another) in lower secondary school. The system has undergone major reforms, including the 1999 shift to a 6+3+3 structure and the 2017 expansion to 8+4/5, with changes in curriculum content and emphasis. Violence in schools has been addressed through a "zero tolerance" policy, granting teachers civil servant status and stricter penalties for reporting failures.
