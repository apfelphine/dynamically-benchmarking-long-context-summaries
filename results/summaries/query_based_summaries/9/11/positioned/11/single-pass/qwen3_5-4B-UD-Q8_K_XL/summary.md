Education in Poland is structured to provide 12 years of free, compulsory general education, with the primary school system expanded from six to eight years effective September 1, 2017. This reform abolished the intermediate "gimnazjum" (junior high school) stage, merging its functions into primary education and extending the duration of upper secondary education to four or five years. The current system consists of an eight-year primary school (grades 1–8) followed by upper secondary education, which includes general secondary schools (*licea*) leading to the *Matura* exam, technical schools (*technikum*) for vocational training, and basic vocational schools (*zasadnicze szkoły zawodowe*) offering three-year certificates.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna Process, consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, with doctoral programs typically lasting three years. The grading system uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with increments of 0.5. While the system emphasizes core subjects like Polish language, history, mathematics, and foreign languages, it has faced criticism regarding historical curriculum changes under recent governments, such as the removal of references to communism and the Non-Aligned Movement. Compulsory education ends at age 18, and the system is supervised by the Ministry of Education and Science, though challenges persist regarding teacher quality and infrastructure.
