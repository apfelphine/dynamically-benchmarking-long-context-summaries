In Poland, the education system is structured with **compulsory education** starting at age six, divided into **primary school** (grades 1–8) and **upper secondary education**, which includes general, vocational, and technical tracks. Primary school, previously six years, now spans eight years, with the transition to upper secondary education beginning after completing primary school.

Upper secondary education prepares students for either direct entry into the labor market or further education. General education is offered through **liceum** (high schools), where students complete a four-year program leading to the "Matura" exam, granting access to higher education. Vocational and technical education is provided by **technikum** (technical schools) and **zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa** (basic vocational schools), with the latter offering three-year programs culminating in a vocational certificate. Special schools cater to students with disabilities. The system follows the **Bologna Process**, with tertiary education structured into a three-year bachelor’s degree followed by a two-year master’s degree, though some programs span four to six years. The grading system at universities ranges from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent), with additional increments.
