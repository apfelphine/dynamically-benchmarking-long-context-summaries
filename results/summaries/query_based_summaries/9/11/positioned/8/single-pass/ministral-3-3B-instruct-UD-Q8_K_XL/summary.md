Poland’s education system is structured around **compulsory education**, which begins at age six and lasts until age 18. The system is divided into three main stages:

- **Primary School (szkoła podstawowa, grades 1–8):** Children start at age six, with attendance mandatory after completing kindergarten. The curriculum includes core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (primarily English). The reform of 2017 extended primary education from six to eight years, eliminating the separate three-year *gimnazjum* (junior high school) phase.

- **Upper Secondary Education:** After primary school, students can pursue either **general education** (leading to the *Matura* exam for university admission) or **vocational/technical education**. General education is offered through *liceum* (grammar schools), which take four years and prepare students for higher education. Vocational schools (*technikum* and *zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*) provide specialized training in trades, lasting three to five years, with graduates earning a vocational diploma.

- **Tertiary Education:** Poland follows the **Bologna Process**, offering bachelor’s (3 years) and master’s (2 years) degrees, with some programs lasting longer (e.g., medicine, 6 years). Universities are divided into public and private institutions, with public universities offering free education for domestic students. The grading system ranges from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent), with additional increments (e.g., 5.5) for outstanding performance.

**Key Features:**
- **Compulsory Education:** Mandatory from ages 6–18, with free public schooling.
- **Language:** Polish is the primary language of instruction, though English is widely taught.
- **Vocational Focus:** Strong emphasis on vocational training, with options for technical and professional diplomas.
- **Reform:** Recent changes (2017) extended primary education and streamlined upper secondary education, though debates continue over academic standards and curriculum content.

The system balances academic and vocational pathways, aiming to prepare students for higher education or direct entry into the workforce.
