## Education in Poland

**Compulsory Education (Primary and Junior High):**
*   Primary school (szkoła podstawowa) typically lasts eight years (grades 1–8). Students must have attended kindergarten in the preceding school year to start primary school.
*   Until 2019, junior high school (gimnazjum) covered lower secondary education and lasted three years. The 2016 "Law on School Education" abolished the *gimnazjum*, extending primary school to eight years, effective September 1, 2017.
*   The education system is structured such that students who complete the eight years of primary school then transition to upper secondary education.

**Upper Secondary and Tertiary Education:**
*   Upper secondary education prepares students for either direct entry into the labor market or for tertiary education. This education takes several forms: general secondary schools (liceum), technical schools (technikum), and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa).
*   Technical schools last five years and lead to the Matura, while basic vocational schools last three years and grant a certificate of competence. Profiled general secondary schools offer vocational education in four years.
*   Poland follows the Bologna scheme, with most tertiary programs consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, although some fields (like medicine and pharmacy) have longer, unique-cycle programs. Doctoral programs take about three years.
*   University-level grading uses a numeric system from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest grade), with grading occurring every semester.
