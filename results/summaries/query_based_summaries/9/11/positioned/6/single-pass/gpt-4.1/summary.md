## Education in Poland

Education in Poland is structured around compulsory primary and upper secondary schooling, with children typically starting primary school at age six or seven. The primary education system was reformed in 2016, abolishing the previous three-year junior high school (gimnazjum) and creating an eight-year primary school model. Upper secondary education includes general, technical, and vocational tracks; general education (liceum) prepares for university entry via the Matura exam, while technical schools (technikum) and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa) focus on trades and lead to vocational qualifications. All students learn one or two foreign languages, with English being the most common.

Tertiary education follows the Bologna scheme: a three-year bachelor’s, two-year master's, and typically a three-year doctoral program, with some fields offering long-cycle master's degrees (e.g., medicine). Poland has a numeric university grading system from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (highest), with grades determined each semester. The country has a long history of educational achievement, with Jagiellonian University founded in 1364 and the creation of the world’s first state education ministry in 1773. After major reforms in 1999 and 2017, Poland's educational rankings improved internationally. Compulsory education lasts until age 18, and significant efforts have been made to reduce illiteracy since the early 20th century. The system also includes educational provisions for national minorities and students with disabilities.
