Poland’s education system is structured with **compulsory education** starting at age six, divided into **primary school (szkoła podstawowa, grades 1–8)** and **upper secondary education**. Primary school, previously six years, was extended to eight years in 2017, eliminating the separate three-year *gimnazjum* (junior high school) phase. Students then transition to upper secondary education, which includes general secondary schools (*liceum*), vocational schools (*technikum*), and specialized schools for students with disabilities.

**Tertiary education** follows the **Bologna Process**, with most programs divided into a **three-year bachelor’s degree** followed by a **two-year master’s degree**. Some specialized fields (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) require longer programs. The system emphasizes academic rigor, with a numeric grading scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent). Foreign language instruction is mandatory, with English being the most widely taught. The education system has undergone reforms, including a shift from a nine-year compulsory education model (pre-2017) to an eight-year primary school and four-year upper secondary education. Recent debates focus on curriculum content, particularly in history and social studies, reflecting broader national educational priorities.
