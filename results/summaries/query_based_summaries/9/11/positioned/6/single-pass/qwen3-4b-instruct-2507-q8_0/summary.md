**Education in Poland**

Poland's education system features a structured progression from primary to tertiary education, with significant reforms shaping its modern form.

**Primary Education:**  
Children typically begin primary school (szkoła podstawowa) at age 6 or 7. Prior to 2017, primary school lasted six years (grades 1–6), followed by a three-year middle school (gimnazjum). The 2016 "Law on School Education" reformed this structure, extending primary education to eight years (grades 1–8) and abolishing gimnazjum. This change began on 1 September 2017, aligning primary education with the new national framework.

**Secondary Education:**  
After primary school, students enter upper secondary education, which includes two main pathways:  
- **General education** (liceum): Students pursue a four-year program leading to the *matura*, a national qualification required for higher education.  
- **Vocational education**: Offered through technical schools (technikum) or basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), these programs last five or three years, respectively, and prepare students for employment. Graduates from vocational schools can also take the *matura* after an additional two or three years of study.

**Tertiary Education:**  
Poland follows the Bologna Process, with most programs structured in two cycles: a three-year bachelor’s degree followed by a two-year master’s degree. Some programs, such as medicine and pharmacy, are longer (five to six years). Doctoral programs take about three years. Teacher training requires a three-year program at a teacher training college.

**Grading System:**  
University-level education uses a numeric system from 2 to 5, with most grades including 0.5 increments. A score of 3.0 is the lowest passing grade, and 5.0 is the highest. A failing grade (2.0) means a student must retake the subject, though it is commonly used and not a major barrier to progression.

**Foreign Languages:**  
Students typically study one or two foreign languages. By 2005/06, English was the most common foreign language (67.9%), followed by German, French, and Russian. The 2016 reform required students in lower secondary schools to study two foreign languages, with English taught three times a week and a second language twice a week.

**History and Reforms:**  
Poland has a long history of educational development, with the Jagiellonian University (founded in 1364) being one of Europe’s oldest. The 1999 reform expanded time in core subjects and delayed vocational study, improving educational outcomes. The 2017 reform extended primary education and reduced the role of middle schools, emphasizing a more cohesive and nationally aligned curriculum.

**Violence and Safety:**  
In response to a 2006 case of school-based sexual abuse, the Polish government introduced a "zero tolerance" policy. Teachers now have civil servant status, and violent acts against them are punishable by law. Schools can send aggressive students to community service, and parents may face fines for non-reporting.

Overall, Poland’s education system is characterized by a strong national framework, significant reform efforts, and a focus on both academic and vocational pathways, with a commitment to equity and quality in teaching and learning.
