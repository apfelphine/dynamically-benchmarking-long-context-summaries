Poland’s education system is structured into **compulsory education** (ages 6–18) and **tertiary education**, with a strong emphasis on academic rigor and standardized testing.

### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary School (Szkoła Podstawowa, ages 6–12):** Originally six years (grades 1–6), it was extended to eight years (grades 1–8) in 2017 under the **Law on School Education**, eliminating the separate **gimnazjum (junior high school)** system. Students learn core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (typically English and German).
- **Junior High School (Gimnazjum, abolished in 2017):** Previously a three-year mandatory stage (grades 7–9), it was replaced by the expanded primary school curriculum.
- **Upper Secondary Education:** After primary school, students choose between **general secondary schools (liceum)** for university preparation, **vocational schools (technikum or zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa)** for technical training, or **profilowane liceum** for specialized vocational education. The **Matura** exam (equivalent to a high school diploma) grants access to higher education.

### **Tertiary Education**
Poland follows the **Bologna Process**, offering a **two-cycle system**:
- **Bachelor’s Degree (3 years):** Followed by a **Master’s Degree (2 years)**.
- **Doctoral Studies (3 years):** For advanced research.
Specialized programs include teacher training (3 years) and vocational education (e.g., post-secondary schools for 2.5 years).

**Grading System:** Universities use a numeric scale from **2.0 (fail)** to **5.0 (highest)**, with additional increments (e.g., 5.5 or 6.0 for "exceeds expectations").

**Foreign Languages:** English is the most widely taught, followed by German, French, and Spanish. The system emphasizes early exposure, with two foreign languages required in junior high.

**Education Reforms:** Key changes include the **2017 primary school extension**, **increased vocational training**, and **standardized testing** to ensure academic rigor. The system remains highly competitive, with significant pressure on students, particularly in university admissions.
