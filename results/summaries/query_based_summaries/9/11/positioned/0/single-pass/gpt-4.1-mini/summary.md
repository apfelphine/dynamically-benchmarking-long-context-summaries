# Education in Poland

Education in Poland begins with compulsory primary school (szkoła podstawowa), typically starting at age six or seven. Since 2017, primary education lasts eight years (grades 1–8), following a reform that abolished the three-year junior high school (gimnazjum) previously in place. Upper secondary education offers various pathways including general secondary schools (liceum) where students study for four years and take the Matura exam granting access to higher education; technical schools (technikum) lasting five years and culminating in the Matura with vocational training; and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa) with three-year vocational education. Special education schools also exist for physically or mentally handicapped students. Tertiary education follows the Bologna Process with three-year bachelor’s and two-year master’s degrees, alongside long-cycle programs in fields like medicine.

Polish education emphasizes foreign language learning, typically requiring one or two languages in lower secondary school, with English being the most prevalent. The grading system at university level uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (highest), with intermediate half-points. Historically, Poland’s educational system is among Europe’s oldest, with foundational institutions such as the Jagiellonian University (est. 1364) and the world’s first state ministry of education established in 1773. Significant reforms occurred in 1999 and 2017, reshaping school structures and curricula. The 2017 reform returned to an 8-year primary education structure, extended high school length, and introduced a Polish-centered historical syllabus. The Ministry of Education has also enacted measures to address school violence, such as introducing “zero tolerance” policies and increasing legal protections for teachers.
