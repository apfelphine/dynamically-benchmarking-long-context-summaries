**Education in Poland**

Poland’s education system is structured into **compulsory primary education** (szkoła podstawowa, grades 1–8) and **upper secondary education**, which includes general secondary schools (liceum), vocational schools (technikum), and specialized schools for students with disabilities. Since 2017, primary education has been extended to eight years, replacing the previous six-year primary followed by a three-year gimnazjum (junior high school). Upper secondary education prepares students for either the labor market or higher education, with options including vocational training, technical education, or academic studies leading to the "Matura" exam, which grants access to tertiary education.

**Tertiary education** follows the Bologna Process model, with most programs divided into a three-year bachelor’s degree followed by a two-year master’s degree. Some master’s degrees are granted after long-cycle programs (4–6 years). Doctoral studies typically take three years. Teacher education programs require three years of study at teacher training colleges. The system emphasizes a numeric grading scale (2.0–5.0), with frequent retakes for failing grades. Foreign language instruction typically includes one or two languages, with English being the most common.

**Key reforms** include the 1999 overhaul, which shifted education from 8+4 to 6+3+3 years, and the 2017 reform, which extended primary education to eight years and added an extra year to upper secondary education. The system has faced criticism for its emphasis on rote learning, limited vocational opportunities, and disparities in educational quality between public and private schools. However, Poland has made progress in improving literacy rates and expanding access to education, though challenges remain in addressing gender disparities and ensuring equitable resources across regions.
