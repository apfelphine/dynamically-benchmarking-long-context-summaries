**Education in Poland**

- **Primary School**: Children typically start at age 6 or 7, with an 8-year primary education (grades 1–8) introduced in 2017, replacing the previous 6-year primary + 3-year middle school system. Pre-primary education (kindergarten) is required before entry. The curriculum includes subjects like Polish, mathematics, foreign languages, science, history, geography, and physical education. A new policy prohibits mobile phone use during class, and physical punishment is banned.

- **Middle/Secondary School**: Replaced by a unified 8-year primary school system in 2017. Previously, a 3-year middle school (gimnazjum) was followed by upper secondary education. The new structure includes general education leading to the Matura (a national qualification), vocational education via technical schools (technikum) or basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), and profiled general secondary schools (liceum profilowane) for specific fields.

- **Tertiary Education**: Follows the Bologna model with a 3-year bachelor's degree followed by a 2-year master's. Doctoral programs take about 3 years. Teacher training requires a 3-year bachelor's degree. The grading system uses a numeric scale from 2 to 5, with 5.0 being the highest. A failing grade (2.0) is common, and students can retake failed subjects.

- **Foreign Languages**: Students typically learn one or two foreign languages, with English being the most common. By 2005/06, 67.9% of students studied English. The reform introduced in 2016 requires students to learn two foreign languages, with one taught three times a week and the second twice a week.

- **History**: Poland has a long tradition of education, with the Jagiellonian University founded in 1364. The first compulsory education was introduced in 1919, and by 1978, illiteracy had dropped to 1.2%. Major reforms in 1999 and 2017 restructured the system, emphasizing national and historical content, and reducing the emphasis on foreign languages and global perspectives.

- **Reforms and Controversies**: The 2017 reform shifted the system to an 8-year primary school, eliminating the middle school. Critics argue that the curriculum promotes a nationalist, Eurocentric view, removes discussions of non-aligned movements, and emphasizes political and military leaders over social classes. The reform has been criticized for neglecting historical perspectives and promoting a narrow curriculum.

- **Violence and Discipline**: In response to a 2006 student suicide, the government introduced a "zero tolerance" policy, giving teachers civil servant status and allowing them to punish violent acts. Headteachers can send aggressive students to community service, and parents may face fines. Teachers who fail to report violence can face prison sentences.

- **Gender and Social Issues**: The education system has seen progress in gender parity, though female enrollment remains lower in some areas. The curriculum includes social studies and ethics, with a focus on civic responsibility and national identity. The system promotes gender equality in education, though societal norms may still influence participation in certain fields.
