Poland’s education system is structured around **compulsory education**, beginning at age 6 with **primary school (szkoła podstawowa)**, which now spans eight years (previously six) due to a 2017 reform. After primary school, students attend **junior high school (gimnazjum)**, a three-year program that prepares them for upper secondary education. The **2017 reform** abolished the traditional three-year junior high school, merging it with primary school into an eight-year primary phase, while extending upper secondary education by one year.

Upper secondary education in Poland includes **general secondary schools (liceum)**, which lead to the **Matura** exam for university admission, and **vocational schools** (technikum and zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa), offering specialized training in trades and professions. The system follows the **Bologna Process**, with tertiary education structured into **bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and doctoral programs (3 years)**. Grading is based on a numeric scale from 2 to 5, with additional increments (e.g., 2.5, 3.5).

Poland’s education system emphasizes **compulsory schooling** (obowiązek szkolny) and **compulsory education** (obowiązek nauki), which ends at age 18. The curriculum integrates **foreign languages** (primarily English, with optional German, French, etc.), and **religious education** (or ethics) is mandatory. Recent reforms have focused on **standardizing education quality**, though challenges remain, including **infrastructure gaps** and **disparities in funding** between urban and rural areas. The system also addresses **gender disparities**, though progress has been slow, particularly in ensuring equal access and opportunities for girls.
