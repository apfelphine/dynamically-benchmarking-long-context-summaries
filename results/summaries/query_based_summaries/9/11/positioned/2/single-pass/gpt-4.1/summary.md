## Education in Poland

Education in Poland is overseen by the Ministry of Education and operates a compulsory structure starting typically at age seven, although children may begin at six after attending kindergarten in the previous year. The primary school (szkoła podstawowa) currently spans eight years (grades 1–8) since the 2017 reform, which abolished the previous gimnazjum (middle school) and reverted to the 8-year primary structure. Upper secondary education (liceum, technikum, or vocational schools) prepares students for the "Matura" examination, granting access to tertiary education. Vocational routes include technical schools (five years, leading to Matura and skilled trades) and basic vocational schools (three years, granting a competence certificate), with paths available to continue education and achieve Matura qualification.

Tertiary education follows the Bologna framework: a three-year bachelor's degree and a two-year master's degree, though some specialist programs last up to six years. Doctoral degrees typically take about three years. University grading uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (highest), with grades awarded every semester and retake opportunities common. Students in Poland often study one or two foreign languages, with English and German being most popular, and there are schools serving national minorities. Historically, Poland has a tradition of education dating back to the 12th century, with the Jagiellonian University founded in 1364. The system has undergone multiple reforms, notably the 1999 and 2017 reorganizations, and compulsory education now usually ends at age 18 after 12 years (starting around age 6). The curriculum and structure remain influenced by historical, cultural, and political developments, with significant progress in literacy and access over the last century.
