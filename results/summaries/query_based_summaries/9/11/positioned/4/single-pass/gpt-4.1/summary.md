## Education in Poland

Education in Poland is structured through a comprehensive system of compulsory and optional schooling. Children typically begin primary school (szkoła podstawowa) at age six or seven, completing eight years of study. Prior to 2017, this was a six-year program followed by a three-year junior high school (gimnazjum), but reforms implemented from 2017 onwards abolished gimnazjum, reverting to the 8-year primary model. After primary education, students enter upper secondary schools, which include general secondary schools (liceum), technical schools (technikum), vocational schools, and special schools for disabled students. Liceum and technikum lead to the Matura exam—a key qualification for university entry. Technical schools last five years and combine vocational training with the Matura, while basic vocational schools last three years and offer trade certification.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna Process, encompassing a three-year bachelor's degree and a two-year master's degree, with longer integrated master's programs for fields like medicine. Doctoral programs last about three years, and university grading uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent). Foreign language learning is emphasized, commonly including English, German, French, Spanish, Russian, and Italian, with the option for national minority language schools. Historically, Poland has prioritized education since the 12th century and established prominent institutions like Jagiellonian University (1364). The education system underwent notable reforms in 1999 and 2017, expanded compulsory schooling to age 18, and continues to adapt both curricula and organizational structures.
