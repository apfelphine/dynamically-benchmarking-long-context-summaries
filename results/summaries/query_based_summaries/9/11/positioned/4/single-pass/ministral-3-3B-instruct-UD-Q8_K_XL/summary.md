**Education in Poland**

Poland’s education system is structured into three main stages: **compulsory education (obowiązek szkolny)**, **general secondary education**, and **tertiary education**.

### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary School (szkoła podstawowa, grades 1–8)**: Begins at age 6 or 7, with kindergarten (pre-school) not mandatory but often attended. The curriculum includes core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (primarily English, German, or French). In 2017, the system was reformed to extend primary education to 8 years instead of the previous 6, eliminating the separate **gimnazjum (middle school)** stage.
- **Compulsory Duration**: 12 years (8 years of primary school + 4 years of secondary education).

### **General Secondary Education**
- **General Secondary Schools (liceum)**: Offer a **Matura** exam, which grants access to higher education. Students study core subjects like Polish, mathematics, foreign languages, history, and sciences, with a focus on academic preparation.
- **Vocational and Technical Education**: Provided by **technika** (5 years) and **zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa** (3 years), leading to vocational certificates. Graduates can later pursue the Matura exam in a general secondary school or technical school.
- **Special Schools**: For students with disabilities, offering adapted curricula.

### **Tertiary Education**
- **Bachelor’s Degree (3 years)**: Follows the **Bologna Process** model, aligning with European standards.
- **Master’s Degree (2 years)**: Required for advanced professional roles.
- **Doctoral Studies (3 years)**: For research-focused careers.
- **Teacher Training**: Requires 3 years of study at a teacher training college.
- **Vocational Education**: Offered at **szkoła policealna** (2.5 years), focusing on practical skills.

### **Key Features**
- **Grading System**: Numeric (2–5), with 5.0 being the highest.
- **Foreign Languages**: English is the most widely taught, followed by German, French, and Spanish.
- **Reforms**: Recent changes have extended primary education to 8 years and introduced stricter academic standards, though debates continue over curriculum content and educational philosophy.

Poland’s education system balances academic rigor with vocational training, aiming to prepare students for both higher education and the workforce.
