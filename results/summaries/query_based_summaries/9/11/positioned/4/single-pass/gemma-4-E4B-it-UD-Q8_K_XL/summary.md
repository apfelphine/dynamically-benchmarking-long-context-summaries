### Education in Poland

**Primary and Junior High School**
In Poland, primary school (szkoła podstawowa) typically lasts eight years (grades 1–8). Students usually begin school at age six, though some may start at seven, provided they have attended kindergarten beforehand. Prior to the 2016 "Law on School Education," the system involved a six-year primary school followed by a mandatory three-year gimnazjum (middle school). The 2017 reform abolished the gimnazjum, extending the primary school to eight years. Junior high school, which previously covered lower secondary education, taught subjects such as Polish language, history, civics, two foreign languages, mathematics, and sciences.

**Upper and Tertiary Education**
Upper secondary education prepares students for employment or tertiary education. General education can be pursued in general secondary schools (liceum), where passing the "Matura" grants university access. Vocational education is provided through technical schools (technikum) or basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa). Poland adheres to the Bologna scheme, structuring most tertiary programs into a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, though some fields (like medicine) feature longer, unique programs. University-level grading uses a numeric system from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest grade), assessed every semester.
