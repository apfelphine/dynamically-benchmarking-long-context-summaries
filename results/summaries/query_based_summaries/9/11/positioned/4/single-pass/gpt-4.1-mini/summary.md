**Education in Poland**

Compulsory education in Poland typically begins with primary school (szkoła podstawowa), which students start at age six or seven. Since reforms beginning in 2017, primary education lasts eight years (grades 1–8), replacing the previous model of six years of primary school followed by three years of junior high school (gimnazjum), which was abolished. Until 2019, gimnazjum provided lower secondary education covering various subjects, but it has now been phased out. Upper secondary education includes general secondary schools (liceum) lasting four years, where students can earn the Matura qualification granting access to higher education. Vocational and technical training is offered mainly through technical schools (technikum) lasting five years, also leading to the Matura, and basic vocational schools (zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa) lasting three years, granting a certificate of competence. Special schools exist for mentally or physically handicapped students.

At the tertiary level, Poland conforms to the Bologna process with a two-cycle system: a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree. Some long-cycle programs (four to six years) exist for fields like medicine and pharmacy. Doctoral programs typically last about three years. The grading system at universities uses a numeric scale from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest), with increments of 0.5; passing grades start at 3.0. Students usually study one or two foreign languages, with English, German, French, Spanish, Russian, and Italian among the most common. Educational reforms in 1999 and 2017 reorganized schooling structures and curricula, affecting length and content of primary, secondary, and upper secondary education. Historically, Poland has a strong tradition in education dating back to the 12th century, with the Jagiellonian University established in 1364 as one of Europe’s oldest universities.
