Poland’s education system is structured around **compulsory education**, which begins at age six and lasts until age 18. The system is divided into three main stages:

1. **Primary School (Szkoła Podstawowa, Grades 1–8)** – Mandatory for all children, covering core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (primarily English). Since 2017, primary education has been extended to eight years, replacing the previous six-year primary school followed by a three-year *gimnazjum* (junior high school).

2. **Upper Secondary Education** – Students can pursue either:
   - **General Secondary School (Liceum, 4–5 years)** – Prepares students for university with a *matura* exam, granting access to higher education.
   - **Vocational Schools (Technikum, 5 years; Zasadowicza Szkoła Zawodowa, 3 years)** – Focus on practical training in trades, professions, or technical fields, with graduates receiving a vocational certificate or *matura* qualification.

3. **Tertiary Education** – Follows the **Bologna Process** model, with most programs structured into a **3-year bachelor’s degree** followed by a **2-year master’s degree**. Some specialized programs (e.g., medicine, pharmacy) take longer. Higher education is offered by universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools, with public institutions generally offering lower tuition than private ones.

### Key Features:
- **Grading System**: Uses a numerical scale from **2.0 (fail)** to **5.0 (excellent)**, with no grade 2.5. Exams are frequent, and retaking is common.
- **Foreign Languages**: English is the most widely taught foreign language, though German, French, and Spanish are also options.
- **Reforms**: Recent changes (2017) extended primary education to eight years and restructured upper secondary education, emphasizing core subjects and vocational pathways. The system remains influenced by **Confucian and European educational traditions**, with a focus on academic rigor and practical skills.

Poland’s education system balances **compulsory academic training** with **vocational opportunities**, aiming to prepare students for both higher education and the workforce.
