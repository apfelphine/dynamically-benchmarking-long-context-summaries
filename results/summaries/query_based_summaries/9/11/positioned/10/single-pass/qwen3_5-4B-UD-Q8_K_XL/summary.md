# Education in Poland

Poland's education system underwent a significant structural reform in 2017, merging the previous six-year primary school and three-year junior high school (*gimnazjum*) into an eight-year mandatory primary education phase (*szkoła podstawowa*). This change, driven by the 2016 "Law on School Education," was intended to delay vocational training and improve academic continuity. The system now flows from eight years of primary school into upper secondary education, which includes general secondary schools (*liceum*) lasting four to five years that prepare students for university via the *Matura* exam, as well as vocational tracks like technical schools (*technikum*) and basic vocational schools (*zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa*) that lead to professional certificates or the *Matura* through additional study. Compulsory education ends at age 18, covering 12 years of learning, and the country follows the Bologna process for tertiary education, consisting of three-year bachelor's degrees followed by two-year master's degrees, alongside doctoral programs and specialized teacher training.

The Polish educational landscape is characterized by a strong emphasis on core subjects and foreign languages, with students typically learning English and often a second language such as German or French. While Poland has historically been a leader in literacy since the late 19th century, the 2017 reforms have faced criticism regarding their content, particularly in history, where the curriculum has been criticized for removing references to communism and the Non-Aligned Movement while focusing heavily on nationalism and political elites. Despite these debates, the system maintains high standards, with university grading on a 2.0 to 5.0 scale and a focus on semester-based assessments that allow for retakes, ensuring flexibility for students.
