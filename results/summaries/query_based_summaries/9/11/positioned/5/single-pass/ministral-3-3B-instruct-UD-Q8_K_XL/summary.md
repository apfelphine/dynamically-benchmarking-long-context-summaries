Poland’s education system is structured into three main stages: **compulsory education**, **upper secondary education**, and **tertiary education**.

### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary School (Szkoła Podstawowa, ages 6–13)**: Mandatory for eight years (previously six years until 2017). Students learn core subjects like Polish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (typically English, German, or French). The curriculum emphasizes foundational skills and prepares students for upper secondary education.
- **Junior High School (Gimnazjum, abolished in 2017)**: Previously a three-year mandatory middle school (ages 12–15) leading to a diploma. The system was reformed in 2017, merging it into the eight-year primary school structure.
- **Compulsory Education Duration**: Officially ends at age 18, though most students complete it by age 13.

### **Upper Secondary Education**
- **General Secondary Schools (Liceum)**: Four-year programs leading to the *Matura* exam, granting access to higher education. Subjects include Polish, foreign languages, mathematics, sciences, and humanities.
- **Vocational and Technical Schools (Technikum, Zasadnicza Szkoła Zawodowa)**: Offer vocational training in fields like engineering, healthcare, or business. Graduates receive a vocational diploma, allowing entry into the workforce or further study.
- **Special Schools (Szkoła Specjalna)**: For students with disabilities, providing tailored education and vocational training.

### **Tertiary Education**
- **Bachelor’s Degree (3 years)**: Follows the Bologna Process, with programs like engineering, medicine, and business.
- **Master’s Degree (2 years)**: Advanced study in specialized fields, often requiring a thesis.
- **Doctoral Studies (3–6 years)**: Research-based degrees leading to PhDs, typically in academic or professional fields.
- **Teacher Training**: Requires a three-year program at a teacher training college (*Szkoła Pedagogiczna*).

### **Key Features**
- **Grading System**: Numeric (2–5), with 5.0 being the highest. Exams are rigorous, and repetition is common.
- **Foreign Languages**: Mandatory in most schools, with English being the most widely taught.
- **Compulsory Education**: Free and mandatory for eight years, with a focus on foundational skills and critical thinking.
- **Vocational Pathways**: Offered alongside academic routes, providing practical training for careers.

Poland’s education system balances academic rigor with vocational opportunities, ensuring a well-rounded education for its citizens.
