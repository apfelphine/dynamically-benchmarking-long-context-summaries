Education in Poland is structured with compulsory education lasting eight years at the primary school level (grades 1–8). Following this, upper secondary education is pursued in general secondary schools (liceum) or through vocational/technical schools (technikum or zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa). General secondary schools allow students to pass the "Matura" for university entry, while technical schools focus on trades like accounting and mechanics.

Tertiary education adheres to the Bologna scheme, typically consisting of a three-year bachelor's degree followed by a two-year master's degree, although some specialized programs, such as medicine or pharmacy, require longer cycles. University-level grading utilizes a numeric system from 2.0 (failing) to 5.0 (highest grade), assessed semesterly. Furthermore, Polish schools mandate the learning of one or two foreign languages, with reforms requiring students in lower secondary schools to study two distinct languages.
