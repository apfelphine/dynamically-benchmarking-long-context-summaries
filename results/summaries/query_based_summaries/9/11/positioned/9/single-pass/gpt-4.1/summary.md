## Education in Poland

Education in Poland is structured around compulsory primary education, which begins at age six or seven and lasts eight years, followed by various forms of upper secondary schooling. Reforms in 2016–2017 eliminated the three-year junior high schools (gimnazjum) and extended primary school to eight years. Upper secondary education takes place in general secondary schools (liceum), technical schools (technikum), or basic vocational schools, offering pathways to the Matura exam, technical qualifications, or vocational certifications. Education for students with disabilities is provided through special schools. Compulsory education generally ends at age 18.

Tertiary education in Poland follows the Bologna Process, with three-year bachelor's programmes and two-year master's programmes, and some integrated, longer master's programmes for specific fields. Doctoral programmes usually last around three years. University grading uses a scale from 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (top mark). Foreign language instruction, especially English and German, is widespread, with students learning one or two foreign languages in school. Poland has a long history of valuing education, with the Jagiellonian University founded in 1364 and the world's first state education ministry established in 1773. Recent reforms aimed to align Poland’s educational structure with European standards and further improve academic outcomes.
