Donald Knuth was a renowned computer scientist born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, who earned his PhD in mathematics from Caltech in 1963. His most significant contribution to the field is *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAOCP), a monumental series that rigorously analyzes algorithms and computer science. Knuth developed the TeX typesetting system and Metafont to achieve high-quality typesetting for his works, and he also pioneered the methodology of literate programming, which treats code as documentation. Beyond TAOCP, he authored *Concrete Mathematics* and other works, including a mathematical novelette on Surreal numbers and religious texts. Knuth is a devout Lutheran who has received numerous accolades, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize, and he is known for his humorous reward system for finding errors in his books.

Knuth's academic career included stints at the University of Oslo, Burroughs Corporation, and Stanford University, where he served as the Fletcher Jones Professor of Computer Science. He proposed the term "algorithmics" to better describe the discipline of computer science and has held membership in prestigious academies such as the National Academy of Sciences and the Royal Society. His work extends into recreational mathematics, and he appears in various media discussing topics ranging from his software patents opinions to his personal views on technology, such as his preference against email. Knuth continues to work on completing the TAOCP series and has written extensively on the intersection of computer science and religion.
