Donald Knuth is a computer scientist and author, best known for his monumental book series, *The Art of Computer Programming* (*TAOCP*), which details fundamental algorithms. He originated the concept of "algorithmics" as a name for computer science and was instrumental in developing digital typesetting by creating TeX and Metafont after becoming frustrated with phototypesetting limitations. Knuth also introduced "literate programming," a methodology where programs are conceived as works of literature, which he embodied in the WEB system.

In addition to his technical writings, Knuth has authored *Concrete Mathematics: A Foundation for Computer Science*, which evolved from a course he introduced at Stanford in 1970. His works also include *Surreal Numbers*, a mathematical novelette. Knuth has received numerous honors, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize. Personally, he is a Lutheran and a composer, and he has expressed strong opposition to software patents for trivial solutions.
