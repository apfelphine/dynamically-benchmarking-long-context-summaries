Donald Knuth is a renowned computer scientist and author, best known for his monumental work *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAOCP), a multi-volume series that establishes foundational principles in computer science. Born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, he earned a PhD in mathematics from the California Institute of Technology and began his career at Caltech before joining Burroughs Corporation. He later moved to Stanford University, where he became a professor and continued his influential work in algorithms, programming, and computer science.

Knuth developed TeX and Metafont for digital typesetting, creating a system that revolutionized the typesetting of mathematical and technical documents. He also pioneered *literate programming*, a methodology that treats programs as works of literature, emphasizing clarity and explanation over mere code. His work on the *WEB* system and its evolution into *CWEB* has had a lasting impact on software documentation.

A prolific writer, Knuth has authored numerous books and papers, including *Concrete Mathematics* and *Surreal Numbers*. He is also known for his contributions to recreational mathematics, his interest in religious topics (e.g., *3:16 Bible Texts Illuminated*), and his strong opposition to trivial software patents. He has received numerous prestigious awards, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize, and is recognized as one of the most influential figures in computer science. Knuth's legacy is marked by his rigorous standards, deep mathematical insight, and commitment to clarity and beauty in both theory and practice.
