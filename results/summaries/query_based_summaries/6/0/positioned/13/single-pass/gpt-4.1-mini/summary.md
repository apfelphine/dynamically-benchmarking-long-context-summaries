**Donald Knuth** was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and showed early ingenuity in problem-solving, such as winning a word rearrangement contest in eighth grade. He earned a physics scholarship to Case Institute of Technology, where exposure to the IBM 650 computer sparked his interest in programming. After switching to mathematics, he obtained both Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees in 1960, and later earned a PhD in mathematics from Caltech in 1963, under Marshall Hall.

Knuth’s early work included developing an ALGOL compiler for Burroughs Corporation and creating simulation languages. He became a consultant for Burroughs and taught at Caltech before moving to Princeton and then Stanford in 1969, where he became a professor of computer science. Knuth is best known for authoring *The Art of Computer Programming*, a comprehensive multi-volume series originally planned as one volume but expanded due to the subject’s breadth. He also developed the TeX digital typesetting system and introduced the concept of literate programming. Knuth’s influence in computer science spans algorithm analysis, programming languages, and digital typography.

He is married to Nancy Jill Carter and is an accomplished organist and composer. Known for his humor, Knuth offered monetary rewards for errors found in his works and authored writings connecting computer science and religion. His honors include the first ACM Grace Murray Hopper Award (1971), Turing Award (1974), National Medal of Science (1979), Kyoto Prize (1996), and foreign membership of the Royal Society (2003). Knuth is a Distinguished Fellow of the British Computer Society and a member of the National Academy of Sciences and Engineering.
