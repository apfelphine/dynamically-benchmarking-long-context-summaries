Donald Knuth is a renowned computer scientist and author best known for *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAoCP), a multi-volume reference work that established rigorous standards for the field. He proposed the term "algorithmics" as a discipline name and developed the TeX typesetting system and Metafont font design, embodying his philosophy of "literate programming" through the WEB system. Knuth's work in algorithm analysis and combinatorial generation earned him the Turing Award in 1974 and numerous other honors, including the National Medal of Science and the Kyoto Prize. A Lutheran and organist, he also authored *3:16 Bible Texts Illuminated* and has maintained a lifelong commitment to open-source documentation and error correction, famously paying a finder's fee for typos in his books.

In addition to his technical contributions, Knuth has made significant impacts on the culture of computer science through his advocacy for structured documentation, his creation of the CWEB programming system, and his humorous approach to software quality. He is also noted for his personal life, including his adoption of the Chinese name Gao Dena to connect with programmers in China, and his diverse interests in music, mathematics, and recreational puzzles. His legacy is cemented by his role as a mentor and a foundational figure whose work on algorithms, data structures, and typesetting continues to influence computer science and mathematical typography globally.
