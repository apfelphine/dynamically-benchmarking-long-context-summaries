Donald Knuth was a computer scientist and author, best known for his foundational work in the field. He began his academic career in physics at the Case Institute of Technology, where he was exposed to the IBM 650. After switching to mathematics, he received his Ph.D. from the California Institute of Technology. Knuth worked as a consultant for Burroughs Corporation, where he suggested an extension to the symbol table in ALGOL, which became the basis for DEFINE in Burroughs ALGOL.

He is most famous for authoring *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAOCP), a monumental work that he developed over many years and structured across seven volumes. Knuth was instrumental in the development of digital typesetting, creating TeX and Metafont. He also developed the concept of literate programming, wherein programmers treat code as literature. Knuth has received numerous honors, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize.
