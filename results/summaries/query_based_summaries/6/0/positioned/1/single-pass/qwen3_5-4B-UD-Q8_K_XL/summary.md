Donald Knuth was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and earned his Bachelor of Science and Master of Science degrees from the Case Institute of Technology in 1960. He later received his PhD in mathematics from the California Institute of Technology in 1963, with a thesis on Finite Semifields and Projective Planes. After working briefly as a consultant for Burroughs Corporation, Knuth joined the faculty of the California Institute of Technology in 1963. He is best known for his seminal work *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAoCP), a comprehensive series of volumes that established the field of computer science as a rigorous discipline. Knuth also invented the TeX typesetting system and Metafont, which revolutionized the design and presentation of mathematical formulas, and developed the methodology of literate programming, which emphasizes explaining code alongside its implementation.

Throughout his career, Knuth held positions at the Institute for Defense Analyses and Stanford University, where he served as Fletcher Jones Professor of Computer Science. He was awarded the Turing Award in 1974 and later received the title "Professor of The Art of Computer Programming" in 1990, a distinction he retained until his retirement in 1993. Knuth is also recognized for his contributions to recreational mathematics, including the invention of Surreal Numbers, and his advocacy for the importance of clear documentation and algorithmic analysis in computer science. His works have been influential in shaping the theoretical foundations of computing and continue to be used as standard texts in universities worldwide.
