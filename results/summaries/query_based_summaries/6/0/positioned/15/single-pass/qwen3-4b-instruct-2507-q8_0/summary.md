Donald Knuth is a renowned computer scientist, author, and educator known for his foundational contributions to computer science. Born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, he earned a B.S. and M.S. in mathematics from Case Institute of Technology and a Ph.D. in mathematics from the California Institute of Technology. He is best known for his monumental work *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAOCP), a multi-volume series that systematically presents the theory and practice of computer programming. Knuth developed the TeX and Metafont typesetting systems to address the limitations of existing digital typesetting, and introduced the concept of literate programming, which emphasizes writing programs as works of literature. He also authored *Concrete Mathematics*, a foundational text on mathematical tools for computer science, and *Surreal Numbers*, a mathematical novelette on John Horton Conway's number system. Knuth is a strong advocate for the scientific rigor of computer science and has written extensively on topics including algorithms, programming languages, and the philosophy of computer science. He received numerous prestigious awards, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize. Knuth is also known for his distinctive humor, such as offering finder's fees for typographical errors in his books and using a fictional "Bank of San Serriffe" for rewards. He is a Fellow of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Philosophical Society, and has been recognized globally for his contributions to computer science and mathematics.
