Donald Knuth, born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, to a Midwestern Lutheran German heritage, is a renowned computer scientist and author best known for his seven-volume work, *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAoCP), first published in 1968. He received his degrees from the Case Institute of Technology and the California Institute of Technology, where he earned a PhD in mathematics in 1963. His academic career includes positions at Caltech, the Institute for Defense Analyses, and Stanford University, where he became a Professor of Computer Science and later Professor Emeritus in 1993. Knuth is also a pioneer in digital typesetting, having created the TeX system and Metafont to address the limitations of phototypesetting for his books, and he developed the methodology of literate programming, which treats programs as works of literature.

Beyond his technical contributions, Knuth has made significant impacts in recreational mathematics, computer architecture, and philosophy. He authored *Concrete Mathematics* (with Graham and Patashnik), *Surreal Numbers*, and *MMIX: A RISC Computer for the New Millennium*. He is a strong advocate for open-source and free software, famously paying finder's fees for typographical errors in his books, and has expressed strong opinions against software patents. A Lutheran who also serves as an organist and composer, Knuth has explored the intersection of faith and technology in works such as *3:16 Bible Texts Illuminated*. His work has been recognized with numerous prestigious awards, including the ACM Turing Award (1974), the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize.
