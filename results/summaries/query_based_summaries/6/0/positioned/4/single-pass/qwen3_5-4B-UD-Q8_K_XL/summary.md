Donald Knuth was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and earned his B.S. and M.S. from the Case Institute of Technology before receiving his Ph.D. in mathematics from the California Institute of Technology in 1963. He joined the faculty at Stanford University in 1969, where he is a Professor Emeritus of The Art of Computer Programming. Knuth is renowned for *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAoCP), a comprehensive seven-volume series that established the discipline as a rigorous mathematical field. He also created the TeX typesetting system and METAFONT, pioneering literate programming as a methodology that emphasizes documenting code as literature. A prolific writer, he authored over 1,300 papers and several other works, including *Concrete Mathematics* and *Surreal Numbers*. Knuth received numerous accolades, including the ACM Turing Award in 1974, the National Medal of Science, and election to the National Academy of Sciences. He is known for his critical views on software patents and his advocacy for the term "algorithmics."

Beyond his technical contributions, Knuth is celebrated for his meticulous writing style and dedication to simplicity. He famously used a Montblanc fountain pen to hand-write his manuscripts, known as EWDs (EWD reports), which are numbered and often circulated privately. He introduced the concept of "forks" in the APL language during his time at Jsoftware and later developed the J programming language. Knuth was also a Lutheran who composed music, including *Fantasia Apocalyptica*, and adopted the Chinese name Gao Dena in 1977 to connect with Chinese programmers. He received the Turing Award in 1974 and the National Medal of Science in 1979, and his work continues to influence computer science globally.
