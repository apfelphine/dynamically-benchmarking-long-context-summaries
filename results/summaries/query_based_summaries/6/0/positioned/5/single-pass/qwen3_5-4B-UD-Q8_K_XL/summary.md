Donald Knuth was a prominent American computer scientist and mathematician born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, who is best known for his monumental seven-volume series, *The Art of Computer Programming* (TAoCP), and the TeX typesetting system. Born to a Lutheran German family, Knuth attended Case Institute of Technology and later earned a PhD in mathematics from Caltech in 1963. He made significant contributions to the development of programming languages, notably influencing the design of the Burroughs ALGOL compiler and advocating for the use of "literate programming," a methodology that treats code as a work of literature. Knuth founded the TeX typesetting system to ensure high-quality formatting for his own complex mathematical works, a tool that later became standard in academia. He proposed the term "algorithmics" to describe the discipline of computer science and was a strong advocate for open-source software, famously paying a finder's fee of 256 cents for errors in his books.

A prolific writer and thinker, Knuth held the title of Professor of The Art of Computer Programming at Stanford University, where he served as a Fletcher Jones Professor. His work extended beyond computer science to include recreational mathematics, the study of surreal numbers, and religious texts, including a project examining the Bible's chapter 3:16. He received numerous prestigious awards, including the ACM Turing Award in 1974, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize. Knuth was also an accomplished organist and composer, having written a piece titled *Fantasia Apocalyptica*. His Chinese name, Gao Dena, was adopted in 1977 to connect with the growing computer science community in China. A member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Philosophical Society, Knuth is revered for his rigorous approach to mathematical analysis and his dedication to clarity in scientific communication.
