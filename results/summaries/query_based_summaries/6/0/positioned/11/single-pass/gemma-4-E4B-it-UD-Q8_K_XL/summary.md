**Donald Knuth** was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and later studied physics at the Case Institute of Technology. While there, he became acquainted with the IBM 650 computer and began developing his interest in programming, deciding to switch his focus from physics to mathematics. After receiving two degrees from Case in 1960, he worked for Burroughs Corporation, where his ideas led to an extension of the symbol table in Burroughs ALGOL, forming the basis of the DEFINE command adopted by other languages.

He later developed *The Art of Computer Programming* (*TAOCP*), initially planning a single volume but concluding it required seven volumes to cover the subject thoroughly. Knuth has also been instrumental in the creation of TeX and Metafont for digital typesetting, and he developed the methodology of literate programming, viewing programs as works of literature. Knuth has received numerous accolades, including the Turing Award, the National Medal of Science, and the Kyoto Prize.
