## Summary: Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and electrical engineer known for her seminal contributions to VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) chip design methodology and computer architecture. Born in New York, Conway faced gender dysphoria from an early age and began a gender transition in the late 1950s, eventually completing her transition in 1968. After early work at IBM on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project—where she contributed to inventions foundational to superscalar computer architecture—Conway was fired by IBM due to her transition but later received a formal apology and lifetime achievement recognition.

Post-transition, Conway rebuilt her career through roles in industry and research, including significant work at Xerox PARC where she co-developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology alongside Carver Mead. This methodology revolutionized chip design, establishing scalable design rules and rapid prototyping via pioneering multiproject wafer technology institutionalized as MOSIS. She also played key roles in academia and government research, teaching at MIT and directing DARPA’s Strategic Computing Initiative before becoming a professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan. Her contributions underpinned the growth of the semiconductor industry, electronic design automation, and technology startups.

Beyond engineering, Conway was an outspoken transgender rights activist, publicly sharing her story from 1999 onward to promote awareness, support, and inclusion for transgender individuals in science and technology. She advocated for trans rights in employment and healthcare and challenged misinformation within academic and clinical communities. Conway passed away in 2024, leaving a lasting legacy both as a technical innovator and civil rights advocate. Her work has been widely recognized through numerous prestigious awards including IEEE Fellow, membership in the National Academy of Engineering, and induction into multiple halls of fame.
