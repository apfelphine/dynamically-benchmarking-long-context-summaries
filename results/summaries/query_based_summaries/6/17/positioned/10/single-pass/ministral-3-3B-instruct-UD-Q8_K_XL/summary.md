Lynn Conway is a pioneering figure in computer science, best known for her groundbreaking contributions to **Very-Large-Scale Integration (VLSI)** system design and the development of foundry-based chip manufacturing. Born in 1938, Conway initially worked at IBM in the 1960s, where she helped pioneer **multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling**, a foundational concept in modern CPU design. After IBM, she became a key architect at Xerox PARC, where she co-founded the **Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS)**, a system that enabled universities and small companies to prototype and fabricate large numbers of custom VLSI chips.

Conway’s work revolutionized chip design by introducing **dimensionless, scalable design rules** and **internet-based rapid prototyping**, addressing the escalating complexity of VLSI systems. Her collaboration with Carver Mead led to the publication of *Introduction to VLSI Systems* (1980), which became a standard textbook in chip design and remains influential in academia and industry. She also introduced the **Conway effect**, a term describing how marginalized groups—such as women and people of color—are often overlooked in historical accounts of technological innovations.

Beyond VLSI, Conway was a trailblazer in **transgender rights activism**, advocating for gender-affirming healthcare, employment protections, and visibility for transgender individuals in high-tech fields. She publicly shared her story in the early 2000s, contributing to broader discussions on gender identity and transitioning in professional settings. Conway’s legacy includes numerous awards, such as the **IBM Lifetime Achievement Award (2020)**, the **National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023)**, and the **Computer Pioneer Award (2009)**. She passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
