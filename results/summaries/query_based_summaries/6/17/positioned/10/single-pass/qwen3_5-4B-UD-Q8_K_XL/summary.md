Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and transgender activist born in New York who transitioned in the 1960s before being fired by IBM for her gender identity. She co-founded the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, which revolutionized chip design by introducing scalable, dimensionless rules that simplified the creation of complex integrated circuits. Her work laid the foundation for the modern foundry model, enabling universities and startups to access state-of-the-art digital technology through the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS). Beyond her technical innovations, Conway is renowned for her advocacy for transgender rights, including her public disclosure of her transition, her creation of resources for trans women, and her leadership in securing LGBT inclusion in the IEEE Code of Ethics.

Conway's legacy extends to her influence on the electronic design automation industry and her recognition as a singular force behind the development of the chip foundry sector. She received numerous honors, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, the National Inventors Hall of Fame induction, and the IBM Lifetime Achievement Award. Her contributions to VLSI systems have been acknowledged by institutions such as the Computer History Museum and the National Academy of Engineering, cementing her status as a revolutionary figure in both computing science and transgender activism.
