Lynn Conway was a pioneering figure in computer science, known for her significant contributions to integrated circuit design. Early in her career at IBM Research, she was part of the architecture team for the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, and the ACS architecture is considered by the Computer History Museum to be the first "superscalar" design.

After completing her gender transition in 1968, Conway continued her career as a digital system designer and computer architect. At Xerox PARC, she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and, in collaboration with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead, co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*. This work, integrating her MPC79/MOSIS innovations, formed the basis of the Mead–Conway revolution in VLSI design, which led to the institutionalization of the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system. Conway later worked at DARPA and taught at the University of Michigan, retiring as professor emerita in 1998. She is recognized with numerous honors, including being named a Fellow of the IEEE and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
