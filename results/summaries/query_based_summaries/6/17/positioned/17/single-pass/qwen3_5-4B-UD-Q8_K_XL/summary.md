Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer whose work fundamentally transformed the design of Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) systems. Born in New York and raised as a boy, she faced significant barriers as a transgender woman in the tech industry, including being fired from IBM in 1968 for her transition plans. After completing her transition, she worked in stealth mode until joining Xerox PARC in 1973, where she co-founded the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) and developed the "Mead-Conway" design methodology. This approach introduced scalable, dimensionless design rules that simplified chip design and democratized access to state-of-the-art technology, establishing the foundry model used in the semiconductor industry today.

Conway's legacy extends beyond her technical innovations to her advocacy for transgender rights and her observation of the "Conway effect," a phenomenon where marginalized groups are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation. She received numerous accolades, including induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2023 and the Computer Pioneer Award in 2009. In 2020, the IBM Corporation publicly apologized for firing her and awarded her a Lifetime Achievement Award. Conway passed away on June 9, 2024, at the age of 86, leaving behind a profound impact on both the architecture of modern electronics and the recognition of diverse voices in science and technology.
