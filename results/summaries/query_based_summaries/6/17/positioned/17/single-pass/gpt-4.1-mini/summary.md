## Summary of Information on Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (January 2, 1938 – June 9, 2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and electrical engineer known for her seminal contributions to computer architecture and VLSI (Very-Large-Scale Integration) design. After early education at MIT and Columbia University, she joined IBM Research in 1964, working on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project where she helped invent multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling—an early superscalar architecture.

Following her gender transition in 1968, Conway was fired from IBM but restarted her career, working at Computer Applications, Inc. and Memorex before joining Xerox PARC in 1973. At PARC, she led the LSI Systems group, co-founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology, and co-authored the influential textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. This work sparked the Mead–Conway revolution, standardizing VLSI design methodology globally and fostering the founding of MOSIS, a prototype fabrication service that empowered rapid chip prototyping. Conway also taught a foundational VLSI design course at MIT and later contributed as a key architect of DARPA’s Strategic Computing Initiative. She retired from the University of Michigan in 1998.

Conway was a prominent transgender rights activist from the late 1990s onward, advocating for awareness, inclusion, and medical resources while sharing her personal story publicly. She highlighted the "Conway effect," describing systemic underrecognition of innovations by marginalized groups. Throughout her career, she received numerous honors including IEEE Fellowship, National Academy of Engineering membership, the IEEE Computer Society’s Computer Pioneer Award (2009), IBM Lifetime Achievement Award (2020), and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023). She passed away at her Michigan home from a heart condition at age 86.
