## Lynn Conway: Summary

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering American computer scientist and electrical engineer. After earning degrees at Columbia, Conway began her career at IBM, where she contributed crucial concepts to the design of advanced supercomputers, including inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling—a key superscalar architecture innovation. Following her gender transition in 1968, Conway was dismissed by IBM, later receiving a formal apology and Lifetime Achievement Award from the company in 2020. She restarted her career in stealth mode and went on to work at Memorex, then became a leading figure at Xerox PARC, where she founded the multiproject wafer technology and co-authored the influential textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. Her innovations, such as scalable chip design rules and the development of the MOSIS system, transformed VLSI chip design, democratized access to advanced chip fabrication, and fostered the rise of university-based digital design education and the electronic design automation industry.

Conway's later roles included serving as a key architect of DARPA’s Strategic Computing Initiative and as a professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan. She received numerous honors, including membership in the National Academy of Engineering and the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal. Near retirement, Conway became a prominent transgender rights activist, sharing her life story, advocating for trans inclusion in professional organizations, and documenting transgender successes. She coined the term "Conway effect" to describe the underrecognition of marginalized inventors. Her career and activism inspired generations, while her technical contributions remain foundational in modern computing and chip design. Conway died in June 2024 at the age of 86.
