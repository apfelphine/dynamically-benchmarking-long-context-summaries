# Summary of Information on Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and electrical engineer notable for her fundamental contributions to computer architecture and very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design. Born in Mount Vernon, New York, she struggled with gender dysphoria and underwent gender transition in 1968, after which she restarted her career anonymously due to societal challenges, including being fired by IBM in 1968 upon revealing her transition plans. IBM later publicly apologized and honored her lifetime achievements.

Early in her career at IBM, Conway worked on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, contributing to the first superscalar computer design. She later joined Xerox PARC, where she led the "LSI Systems" group and co-developed the foundational Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, which revolutionized chip design education and industry practices. Together with Carver Mead, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, a widely adopted textbook standardizing chip design approaches. Conway founded the multiproject wafer (MPW) technology and co-developed MOSIS, an internet-based infrastructure enabling rapid prototyping and fabrication of chip designs.

From 1978, Conway taught VLSI design at MIT and later worked with DARPA as a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative. She was a professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan until her retirement in 1998. Her contributions spurred growth in electronic design automation, startup innovation, and established novel educational and design paradigms. She coined the "Conway effect" describing societal overlooking of contributions by marginalized innovators.

Conway was also a dedicated transgender rights activist from 1999, advocating for awareness, medical and employment rights, and criticizing pathologization of transgender identities. She publicly shared her story and supported other transgender individuals, was involved in activism against controversial gender theories, and participated in cultural events promoting transgender visibility.

Throughout her life, Conway received numerous accolades including the IEEE Fellowship (1985), membership in the National Academy of Engineering (1989), the IEEE Computer Society Computer Pioneer Award (2009), and, with Mead, the Electronics magazine Award for Achievement (1981). In 2020, she received the IBM Lifetime Achievement Award. Lynn Conway passed away in June 2024 at age 86.
