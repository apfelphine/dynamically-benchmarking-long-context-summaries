Lynn Conway was a pioneering figure in computer science and engineering, renowned for her groundbreaking contributions to Very-Large-Scale Integration (VLSI) design. Born in 1938, she initially pursued a gender transition in the 1950s but ultimately detransitioned due to lack of social and medical support. Conway began her career at IBM in the 1960s, where she worked on advanced supercomputer architecture, including the development of multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling—a concept later recognized as foundational to modern superscalar designs.

After IBM, Conway transitioned into academia and industry, notably at Xerox PARC, where she co-founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and collaborated with Carver Mead on VLSI design methodology. Her work on scalable design rules and internet-based infrastructure for chip prototyping led to the creation of the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), revolutionizing chip design. Conway’s contributions were so impactful that she was awarded the Electronics 1981 Award for Achievement alongside Carver Mead.

Conway’s career also included significant roles in the U.S. Department of Defense’s Strategic Computing Initiative and her tenure at the University of Michigan, where she specialized in visual communications and hybrid internet interfaces. She championed the concept of the "Conway effect," highlighting the historical underrepresentation of marginalized innovators, such as women and people of color, in technological advancements. Her legacy includes the invention of the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, which became a cornerstone of modern chip design and manufacturing.

Beyond her technical contributions, Conway was a vocal advocate for transgender rights, working to raise awareness, protect trans rights, and promote understanding of gender identity. She was a key figure in the campaign against J. Michael Bailey’s controversial work on transsexualism and was recognized for her efforts in the transgender community. Conway passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
