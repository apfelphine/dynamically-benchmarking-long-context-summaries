## Lynn Conway — Concise Summary

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was an influential American computer scientist, electrical engineer, and transgender rights activist. After earning engineering degrees at Columbia University, she joined IBM in 1964 and invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, a foundational concept for superscalar computer architecture. Following her gender transition and subsequent dismissal from IBM in 1968, Conway restarted her career, eventually working at Memorex and then Xerox PARC. At PARC, she pioneered the multiproject wafer (MPW) technology and, collaborating with Carver Mead, co-authored "Introduction to VLSI Systems" (1980), a textbook that revolutionized very-large-scale integration (VLSI) chip design and was adopted widely in academia and industry. She also developed scalable design rules and initiated the MOSIS system, enabling accessible prototyping and fabrication of VLSI chips, thus catalyzing the growth of the electronic design automation industry and the foundry model for chip manufacturing.

Conway held positions at MIT, DARPA (designing the Strategic Computing Initiative), and served as professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan, where she specialized in control systems and internet interfaces. Near retirement, Conway publicly shared her transgender experience and became a prominent advocate for trans rights, using her personal website to provide guidance and resources to transgender individuals and challenge pathologizing theories. She received numerous honors, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, election to the National Academy of Engineering, and the IBM Lifetime Achievement Award. Her later life included significant contributions to equality in technology, and she passed away in 2024 at age 86.
