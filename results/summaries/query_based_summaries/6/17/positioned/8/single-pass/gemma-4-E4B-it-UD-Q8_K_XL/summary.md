Lynn Conway was born on January 2, 1938, in Mount Vernon, New York, and was raised as a shy child who experienced gender dysphoria. After attending MIT and beginning an attempted gender transition in 1957, she withdrew in 1959. After working as an electronics technician, she earned B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University in 1962 and 1963.

Conway joined IBM Research in 1964, where she was part of the architecture team for the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, contributing to the invention of multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, which the Computer History Museum noted as potentially the first "superscalar" design. After IBM fired her in 1968 due to her intention to transition, she completed her transition and restarted her career. At Xerox PARC starting in 1973, she led the "LSI Systems" group and founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology. Collaborating with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, and her contributions to the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology and the institutionalization of the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system were groundbreaking, helping to drive the VLSI design revolution. She later joined DARPA in 1983 and taught at the University of Michigan until her retirement as professor emerita in 1998.
