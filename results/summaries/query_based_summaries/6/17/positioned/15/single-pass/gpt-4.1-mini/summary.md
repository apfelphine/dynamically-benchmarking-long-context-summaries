## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and electrical engineer known for her groundbreaking work in advanced computer architectures and very-large-scale integration (VLSI) chip design. Born in Mount Vernon, New York, she initially studied at MIT but withdrew during her first gender transition attempt due to lack of support. She later earned B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University and joined IBM Research in 1964, contributing to the design of the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) supercomputer, including the first "superscalar" architecture with out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling.

After completing her gender transition in 1968, Conway faced employment challenges but relaunched her career stealthily, working at Memorex and then Xerox PARC, where she led the LSI Systems group. Collaborating with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead, she co-authored the seminal textbook "Introduction to VLSI Systems," which revolutionized chip design methodologies and was widely adopted in academia and industry. She pioneered multiproject wafers (MPW) and co-developed infrastructure that led to MOSIS, a service enabling rapid prototyping of integrated circuits, significantly impacting the semiconductor industry.

Subsequently, Conway was a visiting associate professor at MIT and later joined DARPA and the University of Michigan, contributing further to strategic computing initiatives and visual communications. Besides her technical achievements, she was a vocal transgender rights activist, advocating for trans inclusion, medical support, and employment protections. Conway received numerous honors, including IEEE Fellow, Computer History Museum Fellow, and the National Academy of Engineering membership.

Her career and activism highlighted barriers faced by "othered" innovators, a phenomenon she termed the "Conway effect," emphasizing how social biases obscure recognition of contributions from marginalized groups. Conway passed away in 2024, leaving a lasting legacy in computer engineering and transgender advocacy.
