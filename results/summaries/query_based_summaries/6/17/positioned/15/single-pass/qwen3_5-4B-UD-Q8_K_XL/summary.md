# Lynn Conway

**Lynn Conway** (born 1938) is a pioneering computer scientist, mathematician, and engineer known for her revolutionary contributions to Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) chip design. Born in Mount Vernon, New York, Conway experienced gender dysphoria as a child and undertook a gender transition in the late 1960s, a process that was interrupted by her firing from IBM in 1968 due to her intention to transition. Upon re-entering the industry under a new name, she joined Xerox PARC in 1973, where she co-authored the seminal textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. This work established the Mead–Conway design methodology, which introduced dimensionless, scalable design rules and the concept of multiproject wafers (MPW). These innovations democratized access to advanced chip design tools, enabling universities and small companies to develop state-of-the-art digital technology and fostering the growth of the electronic design automation industry and the foundry model.

Conway's influence extended beyond technical design to the broader technological landscape. She invented the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), an internet-based infrastructure that allowed for rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication of chip designs, effectively institutionalizing the Mead–Conway methods. Her work on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project at IBM in the 1960s involved inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, an early form of superscalar architecture. After leaving Xerox for DARPA in 1983, she served as a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative and later joined the University of Michigan. Conway is also recognized as a transgender rights activist, coining the "Conway effect" to describe the overlooking of non-traditional innovators in history. Her legacy includes the establishment of the foundry model, the widespread adoption of VLSI design methodologies, and numerous awards including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, the National Inventors Hall of Fame induction, and the Lifetime Achievement Award from IBM.
