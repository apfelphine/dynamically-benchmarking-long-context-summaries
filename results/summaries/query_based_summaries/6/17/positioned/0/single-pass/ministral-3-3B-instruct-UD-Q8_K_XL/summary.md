Lynn Conway was a pioneering figure in computer science and engineering, known for her groundbreaking contributions to VLSI (Very-Large-Scale Integration) systems and her advocacy for transgender rights. Born in 1938 in New York, Conway faced gender dysphoria and detransitioned after early gender transition attempts, which she later overcame. She earned degrees in electrical engineering from MIT and Columbia University, working at IBM before being fired in 1968 for her gender transition, later receiving an IBM Lifetime Achievement Award for her work.

Conway’s most notable achievements include co-authoring *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, which revolutionized chip design methodologies. She founded the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system, a foundational tool for rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication of chip designs. Her work in VLSI systems significantly influenced the electronic design automation industry and spurred advancements in technology startups.

Beyond her technical contributions, Conway was a vocal advocate for transgender rights, sharing her story publicly to raise awareness and challenge societal perceptions. She co-founded initiatives like "Transsexual Women's Successes" to provide role models and resources for transgender individuals. Conway’s legacy includes the "Conway effect," a term she coined to describe the underrepresentation of marginalized groups in historical accounts of innovation. She received numerous awards, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, the National Academy of Engineering’s Presidential Appointment, and the IBM Lifetime Achievement Award. Conway passed away in 2024.
