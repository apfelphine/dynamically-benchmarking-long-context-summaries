## Lynn Conway: Key Contributions and Legacy

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering American computer scientist and electrical engineer, widely recognized for her foundational innovations in VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) chip design. After early research at IBM, where she contributed to the design of an advanced supercomputer and invented dynamic instruction scheduling for the superscalar architecture, Conway emerged as a central figure at Xerox PARC. There, she led the development of the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and, together with Carver Mead, co-authored "Introduction to VLSI Systems"—a seminal textbook that became a global standard for chip design, catalyzing the Mead–Conway revolution in microelectronics. Conway also invented scalable design rules and the MOSIS system for rapid chip prototyping, dramatically democratizing access to state-of-the-art fabrication for academia and startups alike.

Conway’s career extended to DARPA as a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative, and later to the University of Michigan as a professor and associate dean. She was honored with numerous awards, including election to the National Academy of Engineering, the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. Beyond her technical work, Conway became a prominent advocate for transgender rights, particularly after publicly sharing her story in 1999, and she coined the "Conway effect" to describe how marginalized innovators are overlooked. In 2020, IBM apologized for firing her upon her transition—also awarding her the Lifetime Achievement Award. Conway died in June 2024, leaving an enduring legacy in computer science, engineering education, and social advocacy.
