## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was born in 1938 and studied at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology before withdrawing and later earning B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science. In 1964, she was recruited to IBM Research, where she was part of the architecture team designing the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, contributing to the invention of multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, which the Computer History Museum stated appeared to be the first 'superscalar' design.

After being fired by IBM in 1968 following her revelation of her intention to transition, Conway resumed her career in stealth-mode. She later joined Xerox PARC in 1973, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. Her contributions included inventing dimensionless, scalable design rules and institutionalizing the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system in 1981. Conway eventually joined DARPA in 1983 and later became a professor at the University of Michigan, retiring in 1998. She also coined the term "Conway effect" to describe how people "othered" by society are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation.
