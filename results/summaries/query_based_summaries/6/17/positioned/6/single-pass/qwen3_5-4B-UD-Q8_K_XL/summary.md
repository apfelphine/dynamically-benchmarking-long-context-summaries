Lynn Conway was born in Mount Vernon, New York, in 1938 and experienced gender dysphoria from a young age. After graduating from high school in 1955, she attempted a gender transition at MIT but withdrew due to a lack of support and eventually detransitioned. She later earned degrees from Columbia University and joined IBM in 1964, where she contributed to the Advanced Computing Systems project. Despite her technical achievements, IBM fired her in 1968 after she revealed her intention to transition; IBM publicly apologized for this in 2020.

Upon transitioning in 1968, Conway adopted a new identity and worked as a digital system designer at Memorex before joining Xerox PARC in 1973. There, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a groundbreaking textbook that established the Mead-Conway VLSI design methodology, which revolutionized chip design by introducing scalable, dimensionless design rules. She also founded the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), an infrastructure for rapid chip prototyping. Later, she joined DARPA as a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative and became a professor at the University of Michigan, where she continued her research in visual communications and control systems until her retirement in 1998. Conway is renowned for her pioneering work in VLSI, her advocacy for transgender rights, and her recognition as a revolutionary figure in computer science.
