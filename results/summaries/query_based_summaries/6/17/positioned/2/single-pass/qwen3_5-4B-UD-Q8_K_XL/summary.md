# Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was born in Mount Vernon, New York, in 1938 to a family of German descent. She attended the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) as a boy, attempting a gender transition in 1957, but withdrew due to a lack of support. She later earned her B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science in 1962 and 1963, respectively.

In 1964, Conway joined IBM Research in Yorktown Heights, where she worked on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project alongside John Cocke, inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling. Although she hoped to transition on the job, IBM fired her in 1968 after she revealed her intention to transition. In 1967, she began hormone replacement therapy with Harry Benjamin's assistance, eventually completing her transition in 1968.

After her transition, Conway worked as a digital system designer at Memorex from 1969 to 1972, where she co-authored the groundbreaking textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, which became a standard in chip design. She then joined Xerox PARC in 1973, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and co-invented the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system, which enabled rapid prototyping for small companies. In 1983, she joined DARPA as a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative before moving to the University of Michigan in 1985, where she specialized in visual communications and control systems.

Conway is renowned for her contributions to Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) design, including the invention of dimensionless, scalable design rules that simplified chip design. She also coined the term "Conway effect" to describe the phenomenon where people "othered" by society, such as women and people of color, are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation. Beyond her technical achievements, Conway was a prominent transgender rights activist, advocating for equal employment opportunities and the de-pathologization of transgender identities. She passed away on June 9, 2024, at the age of 86.
