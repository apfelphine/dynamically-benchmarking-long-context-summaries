Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer who made significant contributions to the field of very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design. She co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a foundational textbook that became widely adopted in universities and influenced the development of the VLSI industry. Conway invented dimensionless, scalable design rules that simplified chip design and created a new infrastructure for rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication through the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system. This system allowed researchers and small companies to access advanced chip design tools, fostering innovation and the growth of the electronic design automation industry.

Conway also played a key role in the development of the foundry model for chip design and manufacturing, which enabled a decentralized approach where design could be outsourced to specialized fabrication facilities. Her work helped spur the rise of influential technology startups in the 1980s and 1990s. In addition to her technical achievements, Conway was a trailblazing advocate for transgender rights and gender equality. She publicly came out as a transgender woman in the 1990s, sharing her story with colleagues and the public, and became a prominent figure in transgender rights activism. She worked to protect and expand trans rights, promoted understanding of gender identity, and advocated for equal employment opportunities in the high-tech industry.

Throughout her career, Conway received numerous awards and honors, including the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, the IEEE Fellow award, the John Price Wetherill Medal, and the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal. In 2020, she was awarded the IBM Lifetime Achievement Award for her work at IBM and later in the field. In 2023, she was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. Her legacy is widely recognized for both her groundbreaking contributions to computer science and her courage in advocating for gender equity and inclusion.
