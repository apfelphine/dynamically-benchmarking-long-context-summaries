## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was a computer architect and digital system designer. After earning degrees from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science (B.S. and M.S.E.E. in 1962 and 1963), she joined IBM Research in 1964. While there, she contributed to the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, and the ACS architecture is considered the first "superscalar" design.

Following her gender transition in 1968, Conway restarted her career as a contract programmer and later worked as a digital system designer and computer architect at Memorex. Her tenure at Xerox PARC starting in 1973 was highly significant, where she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. In PARC, she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and, with Mead, developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, which led to the creation of the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system in 1981. Conway later joined DARPA in 1983 and taught at the University of Michigan before retiring as professor emerita in 1998. She coined the term "Conway effect" to describe how marginalized groups are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation.
