# Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer known for her revolutionary contributions to Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) design. Born in 1938 and raised as a boy, she transitioned to womanhood in 1968, a journey that included a significant gender identity crisis and professional challenges, notably being fired by IBM in 1968 for revealing her intention to transition. She later founded the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), an internet-based infrastructure that enabled rapid prototyping of chip designs for universities and small companies, democratizing access to advanced technology.

Conway's most enduring legacy lies in the Mead-Conway VLSI design methodology, co-developed with Carver Mead, which introduced dimensionless, scalable design rules and the concept of "design by example." This approach transformed chip design from a craft reliant on hand-drawn schematics into a systematic engineering discipline, fostering the growth of the electronic design automation industry and establishing the foundry model for chip manufacturing. Her work also established the field of computer architecture as a distinct discipline, and she is recognized as a transgender rights activist for her advocacy and for coining the "Conway effect" to describe the overlooking of non-traditional innovators in historical accounts.
