Lynn Conway was born in Mount Vernon, New York, in 1938. Raised in Hartsdale and White Plains, she experienced gender dysphoria and attempted a gender transition in her youth, withdrawing from MIT in 1959. After completing her education at Columbia University, she joined IBM in 1964, where she worked on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling. In 1968, she transitioned and restarted her career as a contract programmer at Computer Applications, Inc., later working at Memorex. At Xerox PARC, she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a foundational work in chip design. She developed scalable design rules and created the MOSIS (Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service) system, which provided rapid prototyping and fabrication for chip designs. Her work revolutionized VLSI design, establishing the foundry model and enabling the growth of the electronic design automation industry. She joined DARPA in 1983 and later taught at the University of Michigan, where she specialized in visual communications and control systems. Conway coined the "Conway effect," describing how marginalized individuals are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation. She was a key figure in transgender rights activism, advocating for equal opportunities and against the pathologization of transgender people. She received numerous awards, including the National Academy of Engineering membership, the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, the John Price Wetherill Medal, and the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal. In 2023, she was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. She died in June 2024 at age 86.
