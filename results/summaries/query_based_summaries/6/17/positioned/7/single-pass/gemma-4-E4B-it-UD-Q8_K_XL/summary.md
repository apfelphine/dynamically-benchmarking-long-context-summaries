**Lynn Conway** was a pioneer in computer science, recognized for her foundational contributions to VLSI (Very Large-Scale Integration) design. She worked with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead at Xerox PARC, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology. Collaborating with Mead, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, a standard textbook in chip design, and was instrumental in the development of the Mead–Conway revolution in VLSI.

Conway further invented dimensionless, scalable design rules to simplify chip design and created a new internet-based infrastructure for rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication, which became the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system in 1981. She left Xerox to join DARPA, becoming a key architect of the Strategic Computing Initiative. She later became a professor at the University of Michigan, specializing in visual communications and control systems, and retired in 1998. She is known for coining the term "Conway effect" to describe the overlooking of innovators who do not fit traditional perceptions of engineers.
