## Summary: Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering American computer scientist and electrical engineer, renowned for her groundbreaking work in computer architecture and very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design. After earning B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University in the early 1960s, she joined IBM Research in 1964, where she contributed to the Advanced Computing Systems project, inventing multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, foundational to modern superscalar processors.

Following her gender transition in 1968 and subsequent dismissal by IBM, Conway rebuilt her career at Computer Applications, Inc., Memorex, and Xerox PARC. At Xerox PARC, she led the LSI Systems group and co-developed the Mead-Conway VLSI design methodology, which revolutionized chip design by introducing scalable, dimensionless design rules and pioneering multiproject wafer technology. This methodology catalyzed the growth of electronic design automation, semiconductor foundries, and numerous tech startups.

Conway also served as a professor at MIT, played a key role at DARPA’s Strategic Computing Initiative, and was a professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan until her retirement in 1998. Her legacy includes both technical innovations and advocacy for transgender rights; later in life, she publicly shared her story, worked on trans rights activism, and was recognized with numerous honors, including IBM’s Lifetime Achievement Award and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. She passed away in June 2024.
