Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer known for her foundational contributions to Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) chip design. She co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a groundbreaking textbook that became a standard in the field and helped establish the foundry model for chip design. She invented dimensionless, scalable design rules that simplified chip design and developed the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), a prototype infrastructure for rapid, low-cost prototyping of chip designs. Her work fostered the growth of the electronic design automation industry and influenced the development of numerous startups in the 1980s and 1990s.

Conway also made significant contributions to computer science through her invention of the "Conway effect," a phenomenon where individuals marginalized by society—such as women and people of color—are overlooked in historical accounts of technological innovations. She used this term to highlight the lack of recognition for women and minorities in the field of engineering and technology.

Beyond her technical achievements, Conway was a prominent transgender rights activist. She publicly came out about her gender transition in the late 1990s and used her platform to advocate for equal opportunities, employment protections, and the elimination of the pathologization of transgender people by the psychiatric community. She contributed to the campaign against J. Michael Bailey's controversial research on transsexualism and supported broader trans rights initiatives.

Conway's personal life included a marriage to Charles "Charlie" Rogers, with whom she shared a home in Jackson, Michigan. She died from a heart condition at her home on June 9, 2024, at the age of 86. Her legacy includes numerous awards and honors, including being named a Fellow of the IEEE, a recipient of the National Academy of Engineering's membership, and an inductee into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2023. Her work continues to inspire efforts toward greater inclusion and recognition in the fields of technology and engineering.
