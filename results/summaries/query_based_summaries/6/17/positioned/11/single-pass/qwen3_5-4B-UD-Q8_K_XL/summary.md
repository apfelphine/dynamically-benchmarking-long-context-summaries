# Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and transgender woman known for her revolutionary contributions to Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) design. Born in 1938 in New York and raised as a boy, she transitioned in the 1960s, initially facing discrimination at IBM where she was fired in 1968 for revealing her intention to transition. She later worked at Xerox PARC, where she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, establishing the "Mead-Conway" design methodology. This approach introduced scalable, dimensionless design rules that simplified chip design and became a standard textbook used in nearly 120 universities. Conway also founded MOSIS, a prototype infrastructure for rapid prototyping of chip designs, which democratized access to state-of-the-art digital technology for universities and small companies.

Beyond her technical innovations, Conway is a prominent advocate for transgender rights and social justice. She coined the term "Conway effect" to describe how society overlooks the contributions of marginalized groups in historical accounts of innovation. After sharing her life story publicly in 1999, she dedicated her career to activism, providing medical resources, publishing role models for transgender individuals, and lobbying for the inclusion of transgender people in the IEEE code of ethics. Her legacy includes numerous awards, such as the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame, and she is recognized as a singular force behind the development of the modern foundry model for chip manufacturing.
