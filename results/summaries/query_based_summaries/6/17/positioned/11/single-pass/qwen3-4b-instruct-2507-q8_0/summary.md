Lynn Conway was born in Mount Vernon, New York, in 1938. She developed an early interest in astronomy and excelled in math and science. After earning a B.S. and M.S.E.E. from Columbia University, she joined IBM in 1964, where she contributed to the design of advanced supercomputers. In 1968, she was fired after revealing her intention to transition gender. She later transitioned and restarted her career in stealth mode, working at various tech firms. At Xerox PARC, she co-founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a foundational work in chip design. She also developed the MOSIS system, which enabled rapid prototyping of integrated circuits. In 1983, she joined the University of Michigan, where she specialized in visual communications. Conway is renowned for her work in VLSI chip design and for co-inventing the "Conway effect," a phenomenon where marginalized individuals are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation. She was a key figure in the development of the foundry model for chip design and received numerous awards, including the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, the John Price Wetherill Medal, and the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal. She was also recognized for her contributions to transgender rights and activism. Conway passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
