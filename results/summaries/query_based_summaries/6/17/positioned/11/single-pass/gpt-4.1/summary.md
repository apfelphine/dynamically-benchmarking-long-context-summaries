## Lynn Conway: Concise Summary

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering American computer scientist, electrical engineer, and transgender activist. After earning degrees at Columbia, Conway began her career at IBM, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, a foundational concept for supescalar CPU architectures. In 1968, IBM terminated her employment after she announced her intention to transition. Conway restarted her career in stealth mode and subsequently held positions at Memorex, Xerox PARC, DARPA, and the University of Michigan.

At Xerox PARC, Conway fundamentally transformed chip design by collaborating with Carver Mead to create the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology and the multiproject wafer (MPW) prototyping technology. Their textbook, *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, became a standard in academia and industry, igniting the VLSI revolution and the rise of the chip foundry model. Conway's inventions—scalable, dimensionless design rules and the MOSIS system—enabled rapid prototyping and democratized access to cutting-edge semiconductor technology. Her legacy includes a broad impact on the development of the electronic design automation industry and widespread advancement of computing hardware.

In later life, Conway became a prominent advocate for transgender rights and visibility, sharing her experiences and supporting trans individuals in STEM. She received numerous awards, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, membership in the National Academy of Engineering, and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. Conway died in June 2024 at the age of 86, remembered both for her technical achievements and her activism.
