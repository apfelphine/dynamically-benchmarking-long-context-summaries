Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer whose groundbreaking contributions reshaped the field of Very Large-Scale Integration (VLSI) design. Born in 1938, she faced gender dysphoria and transitioned in 1968, despite facing significant challenges, including being fired by IBM for her gender transition. Conway’s career spanned groundbreaking work at IBM, Xerox PARC, and DARPA, where she developed foundational methodologies for VLSI design, including scalable design rules and the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), which democratized access to advanced digital technology for universities and small companies.

Conway’s innovations revolutionized chip design, fostering the growth of the electronic design automation industry and establishing the foundry model for chip manufacturing. She introduced the concept of the **Conway Effect**, highlighting how marginalized groups, including women and people of color, are often overlooked in historical accounts of innovations. Beyond her technical contributions, Conway was a vocal advocate for transgender rights, working to raise awareness, protect, and expand trans rights, and promoting understanding of gender identity and transition processes.

Her legacy includes numerous awards, such as the **IBM Lifetime Achievement Award (2020)**, the **Computer Pioneer Award (2009)**, and the **National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023)**. Conway’s work remains foundational in computer science, particularly in VLSI design and the broader impact of her advocacy for inclusivity and innovation.
