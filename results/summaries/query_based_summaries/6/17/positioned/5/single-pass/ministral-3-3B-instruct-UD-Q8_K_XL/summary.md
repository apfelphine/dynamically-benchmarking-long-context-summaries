Lynn Conway is a pioneering figure in computer science and engineering, best known for her groundbreaking contributions to **very-large-scale integration (VLSI)** design and architecture. Born in 1938, Conway initially struggled with gender dysphoria and gender transition, which led to her eventual career shift from academia to industry. After transitioning in 1968, she became a trailblazer in computing, working at IBM, Xerox PARC, and later at DARPA and the University of Michigan.

Conway is credited with co-inventing **dimensionless, scalable design rules** for chip design, which revolutionized the complexity and efficiency of VLSI systems. She also pioneered **Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS)**, a foundry model for rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication of chip designs, democratizing access to advanced digital technology for universities and small companies. Her work with Carver Mead at Xerox PARC led to the foundational textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, which became a standard in chip design education.

Beyond VLSI, Conway played a crucial role in advancing **transgender rights and visibility**. She publicly shared her story in the late 1990s, advocating for transgender inclusion in technology and science. Her activism helped shape discussions around gender identity, employment protections, and the pathologization of transgender individuals. She was also a vocal critic of transphobic research and advocacy, notably opposing figures like J. Michael Bailey.

Conway received numerous awards, including the **IBM Lifetime Achievement Award (2020)**, the **IEEE Computer Pioneer Award (2009)**, and the **National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023)**. Her legacy includes the **Conway Effect**, a term she coined to describe the historical underrepresentation of marginalized groups in computing history, and her advocacy for **equitable representation in STEM fields**. She passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
