Lynn Conway was a pioneer in computer science, holding a B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science, after which she worked as an electronics technician. In 1964, she joined IBM Research and contributed to the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, where she helped invent multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, and the ACS architecture is considered the first "superscalar" design.

After completing her gender transition in 1968, Conway continued her career at Xerox PARC, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology. Collaborating with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead on very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design methodology, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, a standard textbook in chip design. Her innovations included dimensionless, scalable design rules and the institutionalization of the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system in 1981. Conway later joined DARPA in 1983 and became a professor at the University of Michigan in 1985.
