Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and transgender woman who overcame significant gender dysphoria and societal barriers to become a leading figure in Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) design. Born in 1938, she faced dismissal from IBM in 1968 for attempting a gender transition but eventually returned to the field under a new identity, co-authoring the groundbreaking textbook *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. At Xerox PARC, she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and established the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), which revolutionized how chip designs are prototyped and fabricated. Her innovations in scalable design rules and the "Mead–Conway revolution" laid the foundation for the modern foundry model of chip manufacturing.

Beyond her technical achievements, Conway is renowned for coining the "Conway effect," a term describing how innovators who do not fit traditional stereotypes (such as women and people of color) are often overlooked in historical accounts. A vocal advocate for transgender rights, she publicly came out in 1999, shared her story widely, and engaged in activism to combat the pathologization of transgender identities. Her legacy includes numerous awards, including the National Inventors Hall of Fame induction in 2023, and her work remains a cornerstone of the semiconductor industry, influencing everything from personal computers to modern electronics.
