**Lynn Conway** was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer known for her foundational work in VLSI (Very-Large-Scale Integration) chip design. She co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, which became a standard textbook in the field and helped establish the foundry model for chip design and manufacturing. She invented the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology, enabling rapid prototyping and short-run fabrication of chip designs, and developed scalable design rules that simplified chip design. Her work significantly influenced the growth of the electronic design automation industry and the rise of influential technology startups.

Beyond her technical contributions, Conway was a prominent advocate for transgender rights. After coming out publicly in 1999, she became a key figure in transgender activism, promoting awareness, protecting trans rights, and challenging the pathologization of transgender identities in the psychiatric community. She criticized research practices, such as those in J. Michael Bailey's book *The Man Who Would Be Queen*, and worked to promote understanding of gender identity and transition.

Conway received numerous awards, including the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, the IEEE Computer Society's Computer Pioneer Award, and the National Academy of Engineering membership. In 2020, she was awarded the National Academy of Engineering's highest honor, the Lifetime Achievement Award, and inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2023. She also coined the term "Conway effect" to describe the phenomenon where marginalized groups, such as women and people of color, are overlooked in historical accounts of innovation.

She was a dedicated educator and mentor, teaching a renowned VLSI design course at MIT and mentoring students in the field. Conway passed away on June 9, 2024, at the age of 86, leaving behind a legacy of technical innovation and advocacy for social justice.
