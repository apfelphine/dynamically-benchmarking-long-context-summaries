## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was born on January 2, 1938, in Mount Vernon, New York. After attending MIT and Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science, she was recruited by IBM Research in 1964 to work on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, and her work on the ACS architecture is considered by the Computer History Museum to have been the first "superscalar" design.

Conway's career was marked by her efforts toward gender transition and professional contributions to computer architecture. After IBM fired her in 1968 following her revelation of her intention to transition, she resumed her career. She joined Xerox PARC in 1973, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology. Collaborating with Ivan Sutherland and Carver Mead, she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, and her innovations led to the Mead and Conway revolution in VLSI design. She was instrumental in developing dimensionless, scalable design rules and institutionalizing the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system in 1981.
