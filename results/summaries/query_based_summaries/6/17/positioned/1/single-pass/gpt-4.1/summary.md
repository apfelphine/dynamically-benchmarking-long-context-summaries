## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist whose key contributions revolutionized integrated circuit design. After earning engineering degrees from Columbia University, she joined IBM in 1964, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, leading to the first superscalar computer architecture. Conway transitioned gender in 1968, subsequently losing her job at IBM (an action for which IBM apologized decades later). Post-transition, she resumed her career in stealth, eventually joining Xerox PARC, where she led the LSI Systems group and, alongside Carver Mead and Ivan Sutherland, co-created the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology and the multiproject wafer (MPW) technology. Their influential textbook "Introduction to VLSI Systems" and the MOSIS fabrication infrastructure democratized chip design, transforming academia and industry, catalyzing electronic design automation, the foundry model, and tech startups.

Conway held academic posts at MIT and the University of Michigan, directed DARPA's Strategic Computing Initiative, and coined the "Conway effect" to describe the historical erasure of innovators from "othered" groups. Later in life, she became a prominent transgender rights activist, sharing her story and providing resources for the community, and advocating for equal opportunities and inclusion in STEM. Conway received numerous awards—including election to the National Academy of Engineering, the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame, and multiple honorary doctorates—and her legacy endures through seminal writings, patents, and the profound transformation she brought to technology and society. She died in June 2024 at age 86.
