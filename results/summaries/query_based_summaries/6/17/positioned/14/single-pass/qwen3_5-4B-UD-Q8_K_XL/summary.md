**Lynn Conway** was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer known for revolutionizing Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) chip design. Born in 1938 to a gay man and a woman, Conway experienced severe gender dysphoria in her early life and transitioned in 1968 after being fired from IBM for revealing her intention to transition. Post-transition, she co-founded the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS), a crucial infrastructure that allowed universities and small companies to fabricate chip prototypes, democratizing access to state-of-the-art digital technology. Along with Carver Mead, she developed the Mead-Conway VLSI design methodology, which introduced dimensionless, scalable design rules and a new software toolset that simplified complex chip design. This methodology became the foundation for the modern "foundry" business model, where designers submit specifications to specialized factories for manufacturing, and established the standard for electronic design automation.

Beyond her technical innovations, Conway is a prominent advocate for transgender rights and diversity in STEM. She coined the term "Conway effect" to describe how individuals who do not fit traditional gender norms are often overlooked in historical accounts of innovation. She has been recognized with numerous awards, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame, and the 2023 National LGBTQ Wall of Honor. Her work fundamentally shifted the approach to semiconductor design, fostering the growth of the electronic design automation industry and enabling the rapid technological advancements of the 1980s and beyond.
