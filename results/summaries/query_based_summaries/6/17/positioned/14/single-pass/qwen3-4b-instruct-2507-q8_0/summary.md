**Lynn Conway** (born January 2, 1938) was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer known for her foundational contributions to very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design. She worked at IBM, where she helped develop the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) architecture, considered one of the first "superscalar" designs. After being fired in 1968 for disclosing her intention to transition gender, she transitioned and restarted her career in stealth mode, eventually joining Xerox PARC in 1973, where she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead. Her invention of dimensionless, scalable design rules and the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system revolutionized chip design and established the foundry model for semiconductor manufacturing. Her work laid the foundation for the modern electronic design automation industry and influenced numerous startups.

Conway also coined the term **"Conway effect"** to describe how marginalized groups, such as women and people of color, are overlooked in historical accounts of technological innovation. She became a prominent advocate for transgender rights, sharing her story publicly in the 1990s and working to expand trans rights, protect employment protections, and challenge the pathologization of transgender identities. She was a key figure in the campaign against J. Michael Bailey’s controversial research on transsexuality.

She received numerous awards, including the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, Fellow of the IEEE, and the National Academy of Engineering membership. In 2020, IBM publicly apologized for firing her and awarded her the Lifetime Achievement Award. In 2023, she was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. Conway died on June 9, 2024, at age 86.
