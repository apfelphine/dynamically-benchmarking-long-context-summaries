## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and electrical engineer known for her groundbreaking work in VLSI chip design and computer architecture. Educated at MIT (initially) and later earning B.S. and M.S.E.E. degrees from Columbia University, Conway joined IBM Research in 1964, working on the Advanced Computing Systems project where she contributed to the first superscalar processor design through inventions such as multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling. After transitioning in 1968, Conway continued her career in stealth-mode, holding positions at companies like Memorex and Xerox PARC. At Xerox PARC, she led the LSI Systems group and co-founded the multiproject wafer (MPW) technology, collaborating with Carver Mead on the seminal "Introduction to VLSI Systems," a textbook that revolutionized chip design and education.

Her work was foundational in creating dimensionless, scalable design rules simplifying chip design and helping to establish the Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS) system, which democratized access to VLSI fabrication and catalyzed the growth of electronic design automation and tech startups. She later contributed as a visiting professor at MIT and served in DARPA's Strategic Computing Initiative and as professor and associate dean at the University of Michigan. Conway coined the "Conway effect," highlighting how innovators marginalized by society are often overlooked in historical accounts. After coming out publicly in 1999, she became an active transgender rights advocate, supporting healthcare, employment protections, and combating pathologization of transgender people. Conway received numerous honors including IEEE Fellow (1985), National Academy of Engineering member (1989), Computer Pioneer Award (2009), and a Lifetime Achievement Award from IBM (2020). She died on June 9, 2024, at age 86.
