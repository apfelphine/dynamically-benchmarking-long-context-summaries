Lynn Conway is a pioneering figure in computer science and engineering, best known for her groundbreaking contributions to **Very-Large-Scale Integration (VLSI) design**. Born in 1938, Conway initially pursued a career in electronics and worked at IBM in the 1960s, where she co-developed the **Advanced Computing Systems (ACS)** project—a precursor to superscalar computing architecture. However, her career took a significant turn when she transitioned to **gender transition** in 1968, a process supported by pioneering medical research, including work by Harry Benjamin.

After completing her transition, Conway resumed her career in stealth mode, working as a contract programmer and later as a digital system designer at **Memorex** and **Xerox PARC**. Her most notable achievements include co-authoring *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, which revolutionized chip design by introducing scalable, dimensionless design rules and innovative methodologies like **Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service (MOSIS)**. This work laid the foundation for modern foundry-based chip design, influencing universities and industries worldwide.

Conway’s contributions extended to **transgender rights activism**, advocating for visibility and inclusion of transgender individuals in STEM fields. She played a key role in exposing the **Conway Effect**—a phenomenon where marginalized innovators are overlooked in historical narratives. Her advocacy led to increased recognition of her work, including awards such as the **IBM Lifetime Achievement Award (2020)**, the **National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023)**, and honorary doctorates from institutions like **Princeton University (2023)** and **Syracuse University (2024)**.

Beyond her technical and activist work, Conway’s legacy includes her advocacy for **equitable representation** in technology, her pioneering role in VLSI design, and her enduring influence on both engineering and transgender rights movements. She passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
