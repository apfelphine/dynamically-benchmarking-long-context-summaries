## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist born in 1938 in Mount Vernon, New York. After overcoming personal challenges—especially related to gender identity—she made foundational contributions to computer architecture, notably at IBM where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling for the ACS supercomputer project, a precursor to superscalar design. Despite being dismissed from IBM in 1968 due to her transition, Conway rebuilt her career at Memorex and, most significantly, at Xerox PARC. There, she led the development of scalable VLSI design rules and co-authored "Introduction to VLSI Systems" with Carver Mead. Her work revolutionized chip design through the multiproject wafers (MPW) concept and the creation of MOSIS, an infrastructure enabling rapid prototyping and manufacturing access to universities and startups. These innovations underpin the rise of electronic design automation and the foundry model in modern semiconductor industries.

Beyond her technical achievements, Conway held key academic and governmental positions, including professorship at the University of Michigan and leadership at DARPA's Strategic Computing Initiative. She received numerous prestigious awards from IEEE, the National Academy of Engineering, and other institutions, and was recognized as a Computer History Museum Fellow and National Inventors Hall of Fame inductee. Conway was also a prominent transgender activist, publicly sharing her story and advocating for equal rights and representation and influencing industry and academic policies on LGBT inclusivity. She died in 2024 at age 86, leaving a legacy of innovation in both technology and social progress.
