# Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and VLSI designer who made groundbreaking contributions to the field of integrated circuit design. Born in 1938, she faced significant gender barriers in academia and industry, eventually withdrawing from MIT in 1959 due to a lack of support for her gender transition before detransitioning. She later transitioned again in 1967 and, despite being fired by IBM in 1968 for her intention to transition, went on to become a leading figure in chip architecture. At Xerox PARC, she co-founded the Multiproject Wafer (MPW) technology and co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, a foundational textbook that revolutionized how chips are designed and manufactured. Her work introduced scalable, dimensionless design rules and established the "foundry model," where companies design chips for third-party fabrication rather than building their own facilities, a practice that dominates the modern semiconductor industry today.

Beyond her technical innovations, Conway is celebrated for her advocacy regarding transgender rights and for coining the "Conway effect," which describes how innovators who do not fit traditional societal norms are often overlooked in historical accounts. She played a key role in advancing transgender inclusion in the IEEE code of ethics and was recognized as one of the "Stonewall 40 trans heroes." Conway received numerous prestigious awards, including the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame, and passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
