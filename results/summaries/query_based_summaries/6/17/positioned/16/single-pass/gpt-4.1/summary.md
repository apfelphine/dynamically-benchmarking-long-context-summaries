## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway was an influential computer scientist and engineer best known for her pioneering work in very-large-scale integration (VLSI) chip design. Born in 1938, Conway experienced gender dysphoria from a young age and transitioned in the late 1960s, which led to her dismissal from IBM despite her significant contributions, including the invention of multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling for supercomputers. After transitioning and restarting her career in stealth-mode, Conway joined Xerox PARC, where she led the LSI Systems group and, in collaboration with Carver Mead, co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, a foundational textbook that revolutionized chip design teaching and practice. She invented scalable, dimensionless design rules and the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology, which became institutionalized as the MOSIS prototype system, making state-of-the-art chip fabrication accessible to universities and small companies.

Conway’s later career included positions at DARPA and the University of Michigan, influencing the US Strategic Computing Initiative and specializing in visual communications and user interface design. Her innovations drove the growth of the electronic design automation industry and the foundry model, directly enabling tech startups and broadening access to advanced electronic design. In addition to her technical achievements, Conway was a prominent transgender rights activist, using her visibility and advocacy to promote trans inclusion and equality in the tech industry and academia. She received numerous awards and honors, including induction into the National Academy of Engineering, the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, and a Lifetime Achievement Award from IBM, and was posthumously recognized after her death in June 2024.
