Lynn Conway was a key architect of the United States Department of Defense's Strategic Computing Initiative after leaving Xerox in 1983. She later joined the University of Michigan, where she specialized in visual communications and designing control systems for hybrid internet and broadband-cable user interfaces, before retiring in 1998. Her research, particularly with Carver Mead, was central to the development of the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology and the MOSIS prototype, which drove the VLSI chip design revolution throughout the 1980s.

Conway is also known for coining the term "Conway effect" to describe how people "othered" by society, such as women and people of color, are often overlooked in historical accounts of innovations. Regarding her personal journey, Conway began coming out in 1999 about her gender transition and subsequently became involved in transgender rights activism, advocating for equal opportunities in high-technology industries.
