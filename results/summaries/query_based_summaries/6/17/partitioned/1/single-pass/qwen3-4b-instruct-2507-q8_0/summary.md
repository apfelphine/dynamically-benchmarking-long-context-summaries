Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and engineer who revolutionized VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) chip design. She co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, which became a foundational textbook in the field. Her work introduced scalable design rules and established the MOSIS (Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service) infrastructure, enabling rapid prototyping and fabrication of chips, and democratizing access to advanced semiconductor technology. This innovation profoundly influenced the development of the electronics industry and helped spur the rise of numerous technology startups.

Conway's contributions were recognized with numerous awards, including the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award, the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal, and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. She was a Fellow of the IEEE and a member of the National Academy of Engineering.

Beyond her technical achievements, Conway was a prominent transgender rights activist. After publicly coming out in 1999, she advocated for trans rights, equality in employment, and the elimination of pathologization of transgender people in the psychiatric community. She was a key figure in the campaign against J. Michael Bailey's book *The Man Who Would Be Queen*, and was named one of the "Stonewall 40 trans heroes" in 2009. She also contributed to the inclusion of transgender rights in the IEEE code of ethics.

She was born in Mount Vernon, New York, and experienced gender dysphoria as a child. After being denied access to her children following her transition and facing professional discrimination at IBM, she transitioned and rebuilt her career in stealth mode, eventually joining Xerox PARC, where she co-developed the Mead–Conway VLSI methodology and founded the MOSIS program. Conway retired from active teaching in 1998 and continued her activism and public speaking until her death in 2024.
