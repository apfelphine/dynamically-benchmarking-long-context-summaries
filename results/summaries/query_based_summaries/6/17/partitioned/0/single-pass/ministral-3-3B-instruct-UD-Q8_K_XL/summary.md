Lynn Conway was a pioneering figure in computer science and VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) technology, known for her groundbreaking contributions to integrated circuit design. Born in 1938, she faced gender dysphoria and gender transition challenges early in life, which influenced her career trajectory. After detransitioning in 1959, she pursued education and a career in electronics, eventually transitioning professionally in 1968 under a new identity.

Conway’s most notable achievements include co-authoring *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead in 1980, which revolutionized chip design methodologies. She developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, which introduced scalable design rules and innovative techniques for chip fabrication, significantly impacting the electronic design automation industry. Her work laid the foundation for the foundry model in chip manufacturing and spurred the development of influential technology startups.

Conway’s career spanned IBM, Xerox PARC, and DARPA, where she played key roles in strategic computing initiatives. She was recognized for her contributions to VLSI technology, earning awards such as the IEEE EAB Major Educational Innovation Award (1984), the John Price Wetherill Medal (1985), and the Computer Pioneer Award (2009). Her advocacy for transgender rights and visibility further cemented her legacy as a trailblazer in both technology and social justice.

Conway’s legacy includes her influence on the field of VLSI, her pioneering work in gender transition and professional identity, and her enduring impact on the broader tech community. She passed away in 2024 at the age of 86.
