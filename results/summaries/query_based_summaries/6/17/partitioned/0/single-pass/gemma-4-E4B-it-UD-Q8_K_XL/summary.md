Lynn Conway was a pioneering figure whose career spanned electronics, computer architecture, and groundbreaking contributions to VLSI design methodology. Early in her career, she worked at IBM Research on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project, where she invented multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, and she was fired in 1968 after revealing her intention to transition.

Following her transition, Conway worked at Xerox PARC, where she founded the multiproject wafers (MPW) technology and collaborated with Carver Mead on Very-Large-Scale Integration (VLSI) design methodology, co-authoring *Introduction to VLSI Systems*. She later joined DARPA and eventually the University of Michigan. Conway coined the term "Conway effect" to describe how society overlooks innovators who do not fit outdated perceptions of engineers. In later life, she became involved in transgender rights activism, providing resources and advocating for equal employment protections.
