## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering computer scientist and transgender rights activist. Born in New York, Conway initially studied at MIT but withdrew due to lack of support for her gender transition. She later earned degrees in electrical engineering from Columbia University. Conway joined IBM Research in 1964, contributing groundbreaking work on the Advanced Computing Systems (ACS) project that pioneered multiple-issue out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling, an early form of superscalar architecture. After transitioning and facing job loss at IBM in 1968, she restarted her career quietly, working at Computer Applications, Inc., Memorex, and later leading the LSI Systems group at Xerox PARC. There, she co-developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, revolutionizing chip design and founding foundational concepts utilized by universities and industry, including the MOSIS infrastructure for chip fabrication.

Conway was a visiting professor at MIT in 1978 and later worked at DARPA and the University of Michigan, focusing on visual communications and control systems. Widely recognized for her technical contributions, she received numerous awards, including IEEE Education and Computer Pioneer awards, election to the National Academy of Engineering, and posthumous honors such as induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. Beyond her engineering work, Conway was a vocal transgender rights advocate who supported many transgender individuals, critiqued pathologization in psychiatry, and campaigned against controversial typologies and research like those by J. Michael Bailey. She was honored as a "Stonewall 40 trans hero" and helped achieve inclusive policies in organizations such as IEEE. Conway died in 2024 at age 86, leaving a lasting legacy both in VLSI technology and transgender activism.
