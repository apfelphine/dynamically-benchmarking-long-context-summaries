Lynn Conway is a pioneering figure in computer science and engineering, best known for her groundbreaking contributions to **very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design** and the development of the **MOSIS (Metal Oxide Semiconductor Implementation Service)** prototype system. Born in 1938, Conway initially pursued a career in electronics but faced challenges due to gender dysphoria and societal constraints. After transitioning in 1968, she resumed her career in stealth mode, working as a contract programmer and later as a digital system designer.

Her most significant work came at **Xerox PARC**, where she co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* with Carver Mead, establishing foundational design methodologies that revolutionized chip engineering. Conway introduced scalable design rules and innovative infrastructure for rapid prototyping, which became the basis for the MOSIS system, democratizing access to advanced digital technology for universities and small companies. This work earned her recognition, including the **Electronics 1981 Award for Achievement** and the **Computer Pioneer Award** from the IEEE.

Beyond VLSI, Conway was a trailblazer in **transgender rights activism**, advocating for equal opportunities and protections for transgender individuals in high-technology fields. She was a vocal critic of pathologizing transgender identities and supported initiatives to include transgender perspectives in scientific and professional communities. Conway’s legacy includes her advocacy for diversity in STEM, her pioneering work in computing, and her enduring influence on the fields of VLSI design and gender-inclusive innovation. She passed away in 2024.
