Lynn Conway was a pioneering computer scientist and transgender rights activist. Born in 1938, she experienced gender dysphoria as a child and began her gender transition in the 1950s, facing significant challenges including being fired from IBM in 1968 due to her transition. After completing her transition, she rebuilt her career in stealth mode, working at various technology firms before joining Xerox PARC in 1973. There, she co-developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology, a revolutionary approach to integrated circuit design that introduced scalable, dimensionless design rules and enabled rapid prototyping through the MOSIS foundry system. Her work became foundational in the field of VLSI, influencing academic curricula and industry practices globally. Conway also advocated for transgender rights, promoting gender identity awareness, and was recognized with numerous awards, including the National Inventors Hall of Fame induction in 2023. She was named one of the "Stonewall 40 trans heroes" in 2009 and a "21 Transgender People Who Influenced American Culture" by Time Magazine in 2014. Her legacy includes both technical innovation and activism, highlighting the intersection of engineering and social justice.
