# Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński was a pioneering Polish computer scientist and engineer who made significant contributions to the field of computing during the communist era in Poland. After serving in the Home Army and surviving the Warsaw Uprising, he worked at the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he designed the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, a vacuum-tube computer that improved weather forecast precision by 10%. His most celebrated achievement was the **AKAT-1**, completed in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. This device, praised for its innovative design and aesthetics, earned Karpiński a UNESCO scholarship to the United States in 1960, where he met leading computer scientists like Claude Shannon and John von Neumann.

Following his return, Karpiński developed the **Perceptron**, one of the first such devices in the Communist bloc, completed in 1964. Later, he designed the **KAR-65** (1968), a high-speed synchronous computer using germanium transistors, and the innovative **K-202** minicomputer (1973), which utilized Intel 4004 chips and memory segmentation. Despite the K-202's technical excellence and potential to compete with international giants like the DEC PDP-11, the project faced severe political obstacles, including censorship, accusations of fraud, and a lack of state support, leading to only 30 units being produced. Karpiński's life was marked by his controversial role as a secret informant for the Polish security services (Służba Bezpieczeństwa), which he claimed was limited to reporting on Western technological progress. He passed away in 2010, posthumously receiving high Polish honors for his engineering achievements.
