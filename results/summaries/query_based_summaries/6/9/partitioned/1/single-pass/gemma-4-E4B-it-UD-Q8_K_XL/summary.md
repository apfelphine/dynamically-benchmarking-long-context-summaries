## Jacek Karpiński Summary

Jacek Karpiński faced initial issues with communist security services due to his past involvement in the Home Army, though he avoided imprisonment. After accepting a job at the Polish Academy of Sciences' Institute of Basic Problems in 1955, he worked on the design of a prototype ultrasonography device under Leszek Filipkowski. His first independent project was the AAH — Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics, which he constructed in 1957 with a team of five to calculate Fourier integrals for long-term weather forecasts. His most notable achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959 with Janusz Tomaszewski, which was recognized as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors.

Following the success of AKAT-1, Karpiński was nominated by Poland for the UNESCO worldwide award for young engineers in 1960, leading to a scholarship in the United States. He later implemented a perceptron in 1964, inspired by his American experience. He was involved in the development of KAR-65 in 1968, a mathematical machine for analyzing CERN data. Subsequently, he orchestrated the founding of Microcomputers' Construction Plant in 1970, leading to the innovative minicomputer K-202. Despite the technical excellence of K-202, it never reached mass production, and its ultimate fate is debated among critics.

In later life, Karpiński was revealed to have worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa since 1961, primarily collecting technological data. He moved to the countryside near Olsztyn in 1978 before returning to Poland in 1990. He passed away in Wrocław in 2010, and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2010.
