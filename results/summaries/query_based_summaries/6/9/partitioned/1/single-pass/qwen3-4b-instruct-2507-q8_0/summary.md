Jacek Karpiński was a Polish computer engineer and inventor, born in 1927 in Turin, Italy. After surviving World War II as a member of the Home Army, he pursued a career in computer science, beginning at the Polish Academy of Sciences. He designed several significant machines, including the KAR-65, a high-speed computer developed in the 1960s, and the K-202, a minicomputer introduced in 1970. The K-202, built using advanced microchips and featuring innovative memory segmentation, was designed to be small, affordable, and modular, with a high level of performance and reliability. Despite technical excellence, the K-202 failed to achieve commercial success due to political resistance, administrative mismanagement, and a lack of state support. Karpiński also faced controversies for his cooperation with the Polish security services (Służba Bezpieczeństwa), where he provided technological intelligence on U.S. computer systems and industry. Later in life, he lived in Switzerland and Poland, worked as an advisor, and attempted various business ventures. He was posthumously honored with the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta. Karpiński's legacy remains a subject of debate, with some viewing him as a visionary whose work was stifled by political and bureaucratic obstacles.
