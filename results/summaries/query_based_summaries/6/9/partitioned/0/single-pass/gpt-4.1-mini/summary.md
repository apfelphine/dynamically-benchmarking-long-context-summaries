# Summary of Information on Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a prominent Polish computer engineer and innovator, born into a family of Polish intellectuals and mountaineers. After World War II, despite political suspicions due to his Home Army background, he made significant contributions to computer engineering in Poland. Early in his career, he worked on projects like the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957, intended to improve weather forecasting, and designed AKAT-1 in 1959, the world’s first transistor-based differential equations analyzer.

Karpiński developed several pioneering computing machines, notably the KAR-65 in 1968, an asynchronic computer using Polish transistors that was the fastest in Poland at the time, and the K-202 minicomputer (early 1970s), which incorporated advanced features like memory segmentation with paging and used Intel 4004 chips. Despite K-202’s technological innovations and superior performance compared to contemporaries like DEC's PDP-11, it never achieved mass production due to political and economic obstacles, including lack of support from communist authorities, competition with state-backed projects like the Riad family, and possibly Karpiński’s personal traits and resistance to compromise. Only about 30 K-202 units were produced, with a few preserved in museums.

Karpiński’s career was also marked by controversies; he worked as a secret informant for Polish intelligence during the 1960s, mainly reporting on technological developments abroad, especially during his UNESCO-sponsored scholarship in the United States where he met leading scientists. Later, he faced surveillance and restrictions by the same intelligence services. After withdrawing from major computer projects, he pursued other ventures including work in Switzerland and attempted business projects upon returning to Poland. He was recognized posthumously with high Polish state honors for his engineering achievements.
