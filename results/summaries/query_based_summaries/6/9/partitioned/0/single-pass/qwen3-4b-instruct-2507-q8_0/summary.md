Jacek Karpiński was a Polish computer scientist and engineer born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was a pioneering aeronautic engineer, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a respected physician and pioneer in winter mountaineering. Karpiński joined the Home Army during World War II, participating in resistance operations, including the Warsaw Uprising, where he survived a near-fatal wound and was left paralyzed. After the war, he worked in various institutions, including the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he developed the AKAT-1, the world’s first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, and the AAH, a device that improved weather forecasting precision by 10%. He also designed the KAR-65, a fast Polish computer used in particle physics research, and the K-202, a minicomputer that outperformed Western competitors like the PDP-11 in speed and features. Despite its technical excellence, the K-202 project failed to reach mass production due to political opposition, administrative incompetence, and economic constraints. Karpiński also worked as a secret informant for the Polish intelligence service, collecting technological data during his travels. Later in life, he lived in rural Poland and Switzerland, and returned to Poland in the 1990s. He was awarded the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009 and the Commander's Cross in 2010. He died in 2010. His legacy includes significant contributions to computer engineering, and his work remains a subject of historical and technical debate.
