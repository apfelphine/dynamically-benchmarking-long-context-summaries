Jacek Karpiński was a Polish intellectual and engineer born on April 9, 1927, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother was a physician who became Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish underground paramilitary organization, the Gray Ranks, and later participated in the Home Army. He was seriously injured in the Warsaw Uprising and survived, though he remained paralyzed afterward.

In his post-war career, Karpiński made significant contributions to computer engineering. He co-constructed AKAT-1 in 1959, recognized as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he was involved in the K-202 project, a minicomputer of original architecture that was technologically advanced, utilizing Intel 4004 chips and featuring memory segmentation with paging. Despite the technical success of his projects, Karpiński also worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa. He died on February 21, 2010.
