# Summary: Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer engineer and pioneer in early computing technology. Born into a family of intellectuals and alpinists, he participated actively in the Polish resistance during World War II, sustaining serious injuries that he overcame to continue his work post-war. After studying electronics and engineering, he developed several important computing devices, including the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, and notably engineered the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, AKAT-1, in 1959. His work earned him a UNESCO scholarship to the United States, where he engaged with leading scientists and resisted offers to remain abroad, returning to Poland to further develop his inventions.

Karpiński's most significant creations include the KAR-65 computer, completed in 1968, which was the fastest Polish computer of its time, and the breakthrough K-202 minicomputer project in the early 1970s, which was technically innovative and competitive internationally. Despite moderate political support, Karpiński faced opposition from state monopolists and political factors within communist Poland, which severely limited the commercial success and mass production of his inventions, leading to the K-202 project’s termination and his marginalization. Afterward, he moved to agriculture and later worked in Switzerland before returning to Poland in the 1990s as an advisor and entrepreneur. His legacy is mixed with controversies, including his cooperation as an informant for Polish intelligence and debates over the scale and impact of his engineering achievements, yet he remains recognized for his pioneering contributions to Polish computing history.
