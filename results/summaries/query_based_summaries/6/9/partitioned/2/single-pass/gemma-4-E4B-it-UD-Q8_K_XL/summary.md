Jacek Karpiński's career involved significant contributions to engineering and computer science. After leaving the Institute of Automatics due to a disagreement with Węgrzyn, he joined Jerzy Pniewski's team at the Polish Academy of Sciences' Institute of Physics. In 1965, Karpiński designed a scanner, leading to the development of KAR-65 in 1968, a machine he finished with a team. KAR-65 was an asynchronous computer using a dedicated operation system designed by Karpiński, achieving 100,000 operations per second, making it the fastest Polish computer at the time.

Later, Karpiński founded his own institution in 1970 to develop the K-202, a minicomputer of original architecture. He served as technical director of the Microcomputers' Construction Plant, orchestrating a venture with British private partners. The K-202 was highly innovative, utilizing 1971 Intel 4004 chips, and performed nearly a million operations per second, surpassing competitors like DEC's PDP-11. Despite its technical success, K-202 never achieved mass production, and Karpiński faced censorship regarding his work.
