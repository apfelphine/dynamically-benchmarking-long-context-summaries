Jacek Karpiński was a Polish engineer whose career included significant contributions to computing. His early work involved designing the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957, a vacuum tube computer used for calculating Fourier integrals. His most notable breakthrough was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which was recognized as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors.

Later, Karpiński worked on the K-202 minicomputer project, a technically advanced, asynchronous, microchip-based machine that surpassed competitors like the DEC's PDP-11. Despite this innovation, K-202 never achieved mass production. Following his technical pursuits, Karpiński moved to the countryside near Olsztyn in 1978 and later moved to Switzerland in 1981 to work on Nagra tape recorders. He returned to Poland in 1990 and served as a computer science advisor to Andrzej Olechowski and Leszek Balcerowicz, before passing away in Wrocław in 2010.
