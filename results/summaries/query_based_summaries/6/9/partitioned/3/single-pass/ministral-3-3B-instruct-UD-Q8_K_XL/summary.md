Jacek Karpiński was a Polish engineer and inventor whose career spanned the mid-20th century. Born in 1927, he was deeply involved in the development of early computing technology in Poland. After World War II, he worked on various projects, including the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a vacuum-tube-based device used for calculating Fourier integrals to improve weather forecasting, which was destroyed in an accident.

Karpiński’s most notable achievement was the AKAT-1, completed in 1959, the world’s first transistor-based differential equations analyzer. This innovative machine, designed in collaboration with engineer Janusz Tomaszewski, was celebrated for its advanced functionality and aesthetic design, featuring artwork by prominent Polish artists. AKAT-1 was widely recognized for its pioneering role in Polish computing history and remains preserved in the Museum of Technology in Warsaw.

Despite his technical brilliance, Karpiński faced political challenges. His work on the K-202, a modular minicomputer project with British partners, was abandoned due to opposition from high-ranking officials, including potential sabotage or administrative hurdles. The project was rebranded as Mera 400 and never reached commercial success, with only 30 prototypes produced. Karpiński’s later life involved shifting roles, including working on animal husbandry in rural Poland, collaborating with Swiss engineers, and advising on computer science at universities.

Karpiński’s legacy is marked by his contributions to early computing, though his career was overshadowed by political and institutional obstacles. His work on AKAT-1 and the K-202 project remains significant in the history of Polish technology and computing. Additionally, he was a secret informant for the Polish security services, Służba Bezpieczeństwa, from 1961, providing intelligence on technological advancements in the West.
