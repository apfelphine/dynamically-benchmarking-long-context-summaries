Jacek Karpiński was a Polish intellectual and aeronautic engineer born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization, sustaining severe injuries from an accidental bomb explosion, which led to the loss of sight in both eyes and threatened hand-amputation. He later participated in the Home Army and the Warsaw Uprising, being shot and left paralyzed.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued education in Poland, eventually working on technological projects. A key achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which is recognized as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His career included a UNESCO scholarship in the United States where he met leading computer scientists like Claude Shannon. Later, he designed the perceptron in 1964. In 1970, he co-founded Microcomputers' Construction Plant (Zakład Budowy Mikrokomputerów) to build the K-202 minicomputer, an innovative machine built with Intel 4004 chips, which featured memory segmentation with paging. Despite its technical success, K-202 did not achieve mass production.
