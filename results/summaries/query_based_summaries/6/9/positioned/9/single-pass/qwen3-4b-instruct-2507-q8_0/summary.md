Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was a prominent aeronautic engineer and inventor, while his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a respected physician and later Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. The family moved to Warsaw in 1934, where his father died in 1939 during an expedition to Nanda Devi in the Himalayas.

During World War II, Karpiński joined the Gray Ranks, a Polish underground paramilitary organization, and participated in numerous resistance operations, including the Sieczychy Operation and Operation Kutschera. He was also involved in the Warsaw Uprising, where he was wounded and left paralyzed. He was awarded the Cross of Valour three times.

After the war, Karpiński completed high school and earned a degree from the Warsaw University of Technology. He worked at the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he developed the AAH—Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics, a device to improve weather forecasting. In 1959, he co-developed AKAT-1, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, which was widely recognized and praised for its innovative design and aesthetic appeal.

Karpiński received a UNESCO scholarship in the United States in 1960, where he visited major technological centers and met leading computer scientists. Upon returning, he designed the Polish perceptron, one of the first such devices in the world. In 1965, he developed the KAR-65, a computer that could perform 100,000 operations per second and was used in scientific research until 2000.

In 1970, Karpiński founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant (Zakład Budowy Mikrokomputerów) to develop the K-202, a minicomputer that was innovative in design and performance. Despite its technical excellence, the project failed to reach mass production due to political and administrative challenges. Only 30 units were produced.

Karpiński later moved to Switzerland, where he worked on Nagra tape recorders. In the 1990s, he served as an advisor in computer science and tried to start business ventures. In 2009, he was awarded the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta, and in 2010, he was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta. He died on February 21, 2010, in Wrocław, Poland. Karpiński is remembered for his significant contributions to computer engineering and his pioneering work in the development of early computing systems.
