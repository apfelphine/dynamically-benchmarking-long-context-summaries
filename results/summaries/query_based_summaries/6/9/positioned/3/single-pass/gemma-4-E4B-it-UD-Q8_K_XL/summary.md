Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father was a prominent aeronautic engineer and inventor. His life was significantly impacted by the outbreak of World War II, during which he joined the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts, the Gray Ranks, and later resumed activities in the Home Army.

Karpiński was severely injured in 1943 while working on homemade bombs for sabotage operations, losing sight in both eyes, though his eyes fully recovered and his hand was saved, albeit not regaining total control. He participated in numerous field operations, including Sieczychy Operation and Operation Kutschera, and was shot during the Warsaw Uprising. After the war, he attended university, graduating in 1951, and later worked on projects such as the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, and later achieved a career breakthrough with the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. He also undertook a scholarship to the United States in 1960, where he met leading computer scientists, before eventually returning to Poland.
