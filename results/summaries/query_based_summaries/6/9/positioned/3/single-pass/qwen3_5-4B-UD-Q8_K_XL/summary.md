Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist renowned for pioneering work in the development of early computers and programming languages within the People's Republic of Poland. Born in Turin to Polish intellectuals and alpinists, he joined the Polish resistance during World War II after losing his sight and a hand in a bomb explosion. Post-war, he collaborated with Józef Lityński to build the AAH, an early vacuum-tube computer for weather forecasting, before his breakthrough achievement: constructing AKAT-1 in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors.

Karpiński's most significant contribution was the design of KAR-65 in 1968, a high-speed asynchronous computer capable of 100,000 operations per second, which utilized Polish germanium transistors and a dedicated operating system. Later, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to develop the K-202, a minicomputer featuring innovative architecture including memory segmentation and floating-point representation. Although the K-202 achieved technical excellence comparable to Western competitors like the DEC PDP-11, its production was limited to 30 units due to political opposition, bureaucratic hurdles, and a lack of industrial support. Karpiński also played a key role in Poland's history of espionage by serving as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa from 1961. He died in Wrocław in 2010 and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta.
