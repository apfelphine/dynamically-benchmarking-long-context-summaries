**Jacek Karpiński**

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a pioneering Polish computer engineer born into a family of intellectuals and mountaineers. After enduring severe wartime injuries as a member of the Polish resistance during World War II, he excelled academically and graduated from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. Early in his career, he developed innovative computing devices such as the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) and AKAT-1, the world’s first transistor-based differential analyzer. His notable achievements include designing the KAR-65, Poland's fastest computer in the late 1960s, and the highly innovative K-202 minicomputer in the early 1970s, which featured advanced architecture and modularity and was faster than contemporaries like DEC's PDP-11.

Despite technical successes, Karpiński faced political and institutional obstacles within communist Poland, hindering mass production of the K-202. He was pressured by competing state enterprises and suffered censorship, limiting recognition of his work. After leaving active computer engineering in the late 1970s, he briefly worked in Switzerland and returned to Poland in the 1990s as a governmental advisor. Controversially, Karpiński served as a secret informant for Poland's security services during the Cold War, primarily providing technological intelligence, though his cooperation diminished in the 1970s. Karpiński is remembered as a brilliant but stubborn innovator whose contributions represented lost opportunities for Polish technological advancement. He died in Wrocław and was posthumously honored with prestigious state awards.
