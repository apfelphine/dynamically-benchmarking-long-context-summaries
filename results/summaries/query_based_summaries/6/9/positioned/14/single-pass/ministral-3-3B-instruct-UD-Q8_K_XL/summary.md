Jacek Karpiński was a Polish engineer and inventor, born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of intellectuals and mountaineers. His father, Adam Karpiński, was a renowned aeronautical engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician and pioneer of winter mountaineering. Karpiński’s early life was marked by tragedy, including the death of his father during an expedition in the Himalayas and the outbreak of World War II, which forced him to join the Polish underground resistance.

After the war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career in computer engineering began with the development of the **AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics)**, a pioneering vacuum-tube-based computer used for weather forecasting, which improved forecast accuracy by 10%. His most significant contributions came later with the **AKAT-1**, the world’s first differential equations analyzer using transistors, and the **KAR-65**, a fast, custom-built computer for data processing.

Karpiński’s most ambitious project was the **K-202**, a minicomputer of original architecture, designed with Intel 4004 microchips. Though commercially unsuccessful due to political and economic constraints, K-202 showcased advanced features like memory segmentation and floating-point arithmetic. Despite controversies surrounding his alleged collaboration with Polish intelligence, Karpiński’s legacy includes pioneering work in early computer design and his advocacy for innovative, modular computing systems.

He passed away in 2010, leaving behind a lasting impact on Polish and global computer engineering.
