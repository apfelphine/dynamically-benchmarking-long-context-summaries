Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, in 1927, into a family of Polish intellectuals. He served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization during World War II and later participated in the Home Army, including in the Warsaw Uprising, where he was shot and left paralyzed.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career achievements include constructing the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957 and developing AKAT-1 in 1959, which is noted as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His major breakthrough was the creation of K-202 in 1973, a minicomputer that utilized Intel 4004 chips. He later worked as an advisor on computer science in the 1990s and died in Wrocław in 2010. He has also been revealed to have worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa since 1961.
