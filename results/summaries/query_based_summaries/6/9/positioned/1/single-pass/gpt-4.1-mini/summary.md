Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a pioneering Polish computer engineer and inventor, renowned for significant early developments in computing technology. Born into an intellectual family with a strong mountaineering legacy, his career was shaped by wartime experiences and remarkable resilience, including recovery from severe injuries sustained during World War II resistance activities. After the war, he completed his education at Warsaw University of Technology and began working on innovative computational devices.

Karpiński’s notable achievements include building Poland’s first computer prototypes such as the AAH and the transistor-based differential analyzer AKAT-1. He developed early implementations of neural-inspired perceptron devices (1964), designed the KAR-65 computer (1968), and led the creation of the K-202 minicomputer (1973), which featured advanced architecture and performance competitive with Western contemporaries. Despite technical excellence, his projects faced political and institutional obstructions during communist-era Poland, limiting their industrial deployment and commercial success. Late in life, he transitioned away from computing, engaged in animal husbandry, and briefly worked in Switzerland. Posthumously, Karpiński has been recognized as a visionary engineer whose innovations were ahead of their time, though debates about the full extent of his impact and the causes of his projects' suppression continue.
