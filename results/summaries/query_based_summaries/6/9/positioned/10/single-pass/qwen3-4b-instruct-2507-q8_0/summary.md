Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, while his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician and later became Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. The family moved to Warsaw in 1934.

During World War II, Karpiński joined the Gray Ranks, a Polish underground paramilitary organization, and participated in numerous resistance operations, including the Warsaw Uprising. He was awarded the Cross of Valour three times. After the war, he completed his education and began working in engineering.

Karpiński's major achievements include the development of the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a device to improve weather forecasts, and the AKAT-1, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. He also designed the KAR-65, a computer that could perform 100,000 operations per second, and the K-202, a minicomputer with innovative features like memory segmentation and paging.

In 1960, Karpiński was awarded a UNESCO scholarship to study in the United States, where he met leading computer scientists such as John Eckert and Claude Shannon. He returned to Poland and continued his work in computer science, contributing significantly to the development of early Polish computing systems.

Karpiński faced political and administrative challenges in the implementation of his projects, particularly the K-202, which never reached mass production due to opposition from established computer manufacturers and internal bureaucratic resistance. Despite this, his work laid foundational elements for the development of computing in Poland.

He later moved to Switzerland and worked on Nagra tape recorders. In the 1990s, he served as an advisor in computer science and attempted to launch business ventures. He died in 2010 in Wrocław, Poland. He was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta, the third-highest honor in Poland.
