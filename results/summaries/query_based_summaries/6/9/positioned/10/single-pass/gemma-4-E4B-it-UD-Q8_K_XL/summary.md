Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, to Polish intellectuals and alpinists. During World War II, he was involved in the Polish underground resistance, severely injuring himself in early 1943, which resulted in temporary blindness and a partial hand injury. He subsequently participated in the Warsaw Uprising and was awarded the Cross of Valour three times for his service.

After the war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career included building the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) computer in 1957, which improved weather forecast precision. His most significant achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. He later visited the United States as part of a UNESCO scholarship, where he met computer scientists like Claude Shannon. Karpiński was also involved in building a Polish perceptron in 1964 and designed the KAR-65 computer in 1968. He founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant in 1970, leading the development of the K-202 minicomputer, which featured groundbreaking technology such as memory segmentation with paging.
