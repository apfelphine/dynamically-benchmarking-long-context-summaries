# Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist and engineer renowned for his pioneering work in the design of early computers in the Communist bloc. Born in Turin, Italy, to a family of alpinists and engineers, he served in the Polish Home Army during World War II, where he was severely injured in an explosion but survived. Post-war, he studied at Warsaw University of Technology and worked under communist security services as a secret informant collecting data on Western technological progress, a revelation that later sparked controversy regarding his legacy.

Karpiński's engineering achievements include the construction of several innovative machines. In 1957, he built the AAH, an analytical analyzer of harmonics that improved weather forecast precision. His breakthrough came in 1959 with AKAT-1, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, and in 1965 with KAR-65, a fast asynchronous computer designed to process data from particle physics experiments. In 1970, he founded a company to develop the K-202, a highly innovative minicomputer utilizing Intel 4004 chips and memory segmentation. Although only 30 prototypes were produced due to political interference, economic constraints, and a lack of industrial support, the K-202 was technically superior to its contemporaries like the DEC PDP-11. Karpiński retired to the countryside, returned to Poland in the 1990s to advise government officials on computer science, and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2010.
