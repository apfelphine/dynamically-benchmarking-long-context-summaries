Jacek Karpiński was a Polish engineer and inventor born in 1927, known for pioneering contributions to early computer science. His family background included prominent figures in mountaineering and engineering, with his father, Adam Karpiński, being a renowned aeronautic engineer and inventor.

During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish resistance, participating in operations such as the Warsaw Uprising, where he was wounded. After the war, he attended university in Warsaw and later worked at the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he developed groundbreaking early computers. His most notable achievements include:

- **AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics)**: A vacuum-tube-based computer built in 1957 to improve weather forecasting accuracy.
- **AKAT-1**: The world’s first differential equations analyzer using transistors, completed in 1959.
- **KAR-65**: A transistor-based mathematical machine, completed in 1968, which was one of the fastest Polish computers of its time.

Karpiński was awarded the UNESCO scholarship in the United States in 1960, allowing him to study advanced technological centers. He later developed a **perceptron**, a neural network model, in 1964. His ambitious project, **K-202**, a minicomputer of original architecture, was planned but never fully realized due to political and economic constraints.

Karpiński was also a secret informant for the Polish intelligence services, providing technological intelligence during his travels abroad. He died in 2010, leaving behind a legacy of innovative contributions to early computer engineering. His work laid foundational groundwork for Polish and global advancements in computing.
