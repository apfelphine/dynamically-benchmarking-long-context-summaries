Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist renowned for his pioneering work in computer architecture and artificial intelligence during the mid-20th century. Born into a family of Polish intellectuals in Turin, Italy, Karpiński suffered severe injuries in 1943 during World War II, losing sight in both eyes and sustaining a hand injury, yet he recovered fully enough to continue his resistance activities and eventually pursue a career in engineering. After recovering and completing his education in Poland, he developed the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, a vacuum-tube computer that improved weather forecast precision, and the groundbreaking **AKAT-1** in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, which was praised for its aesthetic design and innovation.

Karpiński's most significant achievement was the design of the **KAR-65**, a high-speed asynchronous computer capable of 100,000 operations per second, which became the fastest computer in Poland at the time. Following a dispute at the Institute of Automatics, he founded the **K-202** project in 1970, creating a highly innovative minicomputer featuring floating-point arithmetic, memory segmentation, and the ability to handle multiple devices simultaneously. Although only 30 units were produced before the project was halted due to political interference and bureaucratic opposition, the K-202 demonstrated capabilities that rivaled international leaders like the DEC PDP-11. Karpiński's legacy also includes his posthumous recognition with the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta and his contributions to the history of Eastern European computing.
