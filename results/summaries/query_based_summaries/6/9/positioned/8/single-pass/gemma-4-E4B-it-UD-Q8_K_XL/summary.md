Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, on April 9, 1927, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. During World War II, despite his youth, Karpiński served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization and later participated in the Home Army, sustaining severe injuries including loss of sight and a shot in his backbone during the Warsaw Uprising, after which he remained paralyzed.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career focused on engineering innovations, notably creating the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, and achieving a career breakthrough with AKAT-1 in 1959—the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he designed the perceptron and developed the KAR-65, a machine for processing CERN data. In 1970, he established his own institution to create the K-202, a minicomputer of original architecture, which was highly innovative but never reached mass production.
