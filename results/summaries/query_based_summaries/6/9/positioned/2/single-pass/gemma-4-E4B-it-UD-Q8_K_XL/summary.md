Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother was a physician. Karpiński's life was profoundly altered by World War II, during which he served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization, suffering severe injuries from an explosion in early 1943, which caused him to lose sight in both eyes. He resumed resistance activities in the Home Army and participated in the Warsaw Uprising, sustaining a gunshot wound to his backbone, which resulted in paralysis.

After the war, he completed his education, graduating from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career breakthroughs include designing the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957 and, most significantly, co-constructing AKAT-1 in 1959, which was recognized as the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His success led him to a UNESCO scholarship in the United States, where he visited major technological centers, including Harvard, and he later designed the perceptron in 1964. Later, in 1965, he designed KAR-65, a mathematical machine for CERN data, and in 1970, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to build the K-202 minicomputer. Karpiński died on February 21, 2010, in Wrocław.
