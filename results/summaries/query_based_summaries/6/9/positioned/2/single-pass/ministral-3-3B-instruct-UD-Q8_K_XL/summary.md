Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish engineer and inventor renowned for pioneering advancements in early computer technology. Born into a family of intellectuals and alpinists, he served in the Polish resistance during World War II, enduring severe injuries and eventual paralysis. Post-war, he pursued engineering studies, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951.

Karpiński’s most notable contributions include the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a vacuum-tube-based device developed in 1957 to improve weather forecasting accuracy. His breakthrough came with the **AKAT-1**, the world’s first differential equations analyzer using transistors, completed in 1959. This work earned him recognition and a UNESCO scholarship in the U.S. in 1960, where he studied advanced computing techniques.

Later, Karpiński designed the **Perceptron**, a neural network-inspired learning device, in 1964, and the **KAR-65**, a high-speed mathematical machine, in 1968. His ambitious **K-202** minicomputer project, intended for mass production, faced political and economic challenges, limiting its success. Despite these setbacks, Karpiński’s innovations laid foundational work in early Polish computing.

Karpiński’s later years were marked by activism and entrepreneurship, including work on advanced communication systems and a small-scale animal husbandry venture. He was posthumously honored with the **Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta** (2009) and the **Commander’s Cross of Polonia Restituta** (2010) for his contributions to engineering. His legacy includes his role as a secret informant for the Polish intelligence services, though his primary focus remained on technological innovation.
