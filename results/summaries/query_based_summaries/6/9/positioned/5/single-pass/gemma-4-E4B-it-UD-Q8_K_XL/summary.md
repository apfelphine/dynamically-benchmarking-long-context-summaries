Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, on April 9, 1927, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization and later in the Home Army, participating in field operations and the Warsaw Uprising. He was severely injured during the Uprising, receiving a gunshot wound to his backbone, which left him paralyzed, and he was awarded the Cross of Valour three times.

After the war, Karpiński pursued education, graduating from the University of Technology in 1951. His career achievements include building the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957, and his major breakthrough, AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His later work involved designing a minicomputer, K-202, in 1970, which incorporated features like memory segmentation with paging. Karpiński later served as an advisor on computer science and was recognized with the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009, before passing away in 2010.
