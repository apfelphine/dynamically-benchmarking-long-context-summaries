Jacek Karpiński was a Polish computer scientist and engineer born in 1927, known for developing several pioneering computer systems in Poland during the communist era. His early life was marked by the death of his father, a renowned alpinist and aeronautic engineer, during an expedition in 1939. During World War II, Karpiński joined the Polish Home Army's Gray Ranks and later the Zośka battalion. He survived the Warsaw Uprising, where he was paralyzed by a gunshot wound to the backbone, recovering through hiking trips with his brother.

In the post-war period, Karpiński graduated from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951 and worked under pressure from communist security services, which monitored his past as a former Home Army member. He conducted groundbreaking research at the Polish Academy of Sciences, building the AKAT-1 (1959), the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, and the KAR-65 (1968), a high-speed computer capable of 100,000 operations per second. He also designed a perceptron in 1964, one of the first in the Communist bloc. His most significant achievement was the K-202 minicomputer (1973), which utilized Intel 4004 chips, floating-point arithmetic, and memory segmentation. Despite its technical superiority over Western competitors like the PDP-11, the K-202 failed to achieve mass production due to political interference, administrative sabotage, and lack of state support. Karpiński later worked in Switzerland and returned to Poland in the 1990s, where he died in 2010.
