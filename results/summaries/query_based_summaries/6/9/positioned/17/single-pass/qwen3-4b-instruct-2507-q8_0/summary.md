Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was a pioneering aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda, was a physician and university dean. The family moved to Warsaw in 1934, and Karpiński’s father died in 1939 during a Himalayan expedition. During World War II, Karpiński joined the Polish underground, serving in the Gray Ranks and the Home Army, where he participated in major operations including the Warsaw Uprising. He was awarded the Cross of Valour three times.

After the war, he studied at the Warsaw University of Technology, graduating in 1951. His first major project, the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), improved weather forecasting precision by 10%. In 1959, he co-developed AKAT-1, the world’s first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, praised for its innovation and aesthetic design. This work earned him a UNESCO scholarship to the United States, where he met leading computer scientists like Claude Shannon and John Eckert.

Inspired by his U.S. experience, Karpiński designed Poland’s first perceptron in 1964, one of the world’s earliest such devices. After leaving the Institute of Automatics, he developed KAR-65, a high-speed computer used for analyzing data from CERN, which operated for 20 years. In 1970, he founded a minicomputer project, K-202, which was technologically advanced—using microchips, memory segmentation, and floating-point arithmetic—and outperformed major Western competitors. However, due to political resistance, lack of industrial support, and administrative sabotage, only 30 units were produced, and the project was ultimately abandoned.

Karpiński later lived in Switzerland and returned to Poland in 1990, advising on computer science and attempting business ventures. He died in 2010. His work remains controversial, with debates over the scale of his innovations and the political forces that suppressed his achievements. He was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta. Karpiński is also portrayed in Roman Bratny's novel *Lot ku ziemi* as Marek Zych.
