Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, on April 9, 1927, to Polish intellectual and alpinist pioneers. His life was dramatically altered by World War II; at age fourteen, he joined the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts, suffering severe injuries in 1943 which resulted in the loss of sight in both eyes and a hand injury. Despite these setbacks, he resumed resistance activities in the Home Army and participated in the Warsaw Uprising, sustaining a gunshot wound that left him paralyzed.

Following the war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His engineering career focused on advanced computational devices, notably constructing the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, which aimed to improve weather forecasting. His most significant achievement was the AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he built the perceptron in 1964 and developed the KAR-65 in 1968. In 1970, he established the Microcomputers' Construction Plant (Zakład Budowy Mikrokomputerów) to build the K-202, an advanced minicomputer utilizing 1971 Intel 4004 chips, although this project never achieved mass production. Karpiński later became involved in controversial activities, as he worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa starting in 1961. He died in Wrocław on February 21, 2010.
