Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, in 1927, to Polish intellectuals. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother was a physician. During World War II, he joined the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts and later participated in the Home Army resistance effort.

After sustaining severe injuries during wartime sabotage and the Warsaw Uprising, Karpiński resumed his education and university studies. His career was marked by significant technological contributions, including the AAH—Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics—and the groundbreaking AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he developed a perceptron and designed the KAR-65 machine. Despite his engineering successes, his projects faced institutional and political challenges in Communist Poland.
