# Summary of Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer engineer and inventor, born in Turin to a family of intellectuals and mountaineers. After serving in the Polish resistance during World War II, including the Warsaw Uprising during which he was wounded, he pursued electrical engineering studies and graduated from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. Karpiński worked on pioneering computing projects in Poland, constructing early machines such as the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics, 1957), AKAT-1 (1959), the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, and later the KAR-65 (1968), a high-speed asynchronous computer, and the innovative K-202 minicomputer (1973), notable for its advanced architecture and modularity.

Despite technical successes and international recognition, including a UNESCO engineering award that allowed him to study in the US, Karpiński's projects often faced political and institutional obstacles in communist Poland, limiting their production and dissemination. His career was marked by disputes with authorities and censorship, impacting the commercial fate of his inventions like the K-202. Later in life, he moved away from engineering to animal husbandry and business ventures. Post-communism, revelations emerged about his cooperation with Polish intelligence services during his travels abroad. He was honored posthumously with prestigious awards in Poland, and his legacy is remembered as a visionary computer pioneer whose work anticipated global technological trends.
