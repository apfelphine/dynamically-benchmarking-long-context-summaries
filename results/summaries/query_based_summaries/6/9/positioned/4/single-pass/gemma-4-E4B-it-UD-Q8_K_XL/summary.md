Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. Karpiński's life was profoundly affected by his father's death in 1939 and the outbreak of World War II, during which he served in the Polish underground paramilitary organization, the Gray Ranks.

During the Warsaw Uprising, Karpiński was shot with a 9mm caliber gun and survived, but he was left paralyzed. Post-war, he resumed education, eventually graduating from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career breakthrough was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Subsequently, he developed the perceptron and, in 1968, finished KAR-65, a mathematical machine. In 1970, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to develop the K-202 minicomputer, which featured innovations like memory segmentation with paging, though it never reached mass production. Karpiński later became a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa.
