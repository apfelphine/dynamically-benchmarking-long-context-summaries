Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Gray Ranks and later in the Home Army. He was severely injured in 1943 while working on homemade bombs, losing sight in both eyes, although his vision eventually recovered. He was shot during the Warsaw Uprising and remained paralyzed afterward.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued higher education and later made significant contributions to computer science. His breakthrough achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His career also included developing the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) and leading the project for the K-202 minicomputer. He was a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa starting in 1961. Jacek Karpiński passed away on February 21, 2010.
