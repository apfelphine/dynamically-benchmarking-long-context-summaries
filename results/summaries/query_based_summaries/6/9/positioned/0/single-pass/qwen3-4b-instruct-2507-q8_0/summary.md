Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician. The family moved to Warsaw in 1934, where his father died during an expedition in the Himalayas. Karpiński joined the Gray Ranks during WWII, participating in sabotage operations and surviving a bombing that caused him to lose sight in both eyes. He later became involved in the Home Army, participating in key operations such as the Warsaw Uprising, where he was wounded and left paralyzed. After the war, he attended high school in Radomsko and later studied at the Warsaw University of Technology. 

He worked at the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he developed the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) and later the AKAT-1, a pioneering differential equations analyzer based on transistors. The AKAT-1 was praised for its design and aesthetic appeal. Karpiński received a UNESCO scholarship to study in the United States, where he worked with leading computer scientists. He later developed the perceptron, a device inspired by Frank Rosenblatt's ideas, and the KAR-65, a computer designed for data processing. Despite political challenges and censorship, his work influenced early computing in Poland. 

In later life, he moved to Switzerland and returned to Poland in the 1990s, advising on computer science. He was awarded the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009 and posthumously received the Commander's Cross in 2010. Karpiński was also a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa, providing technological intelligence. His work is widely recognized as innovative, though some of his achievements have been debated in terms of their technical superiority and the political context of their development.
