# Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist renowned for pioneering work in automated computing, particularly for developing the AKAT-1, the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, and the KAR-65, a high-speed scientific computer. His early career included significant contributions to harmonic analysis with the AAH device and the creation of the world's first perceptron in Poland. In 1970, he founded his own institution to develop the K-202, a highly innovative minicomputer featuring advanced architecture and modular design, though its limited production was hindered by political constraints and competition.

Throughout his life, Karpiński faced controversies regarding his dual role as a scientific pioneer and a secret intelligence informant for the Polish security services, which he maintained until the fall of communism. Despite setbacks in his later commercial ventures, including failed business attempts after moving to Switzerland, he received the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009. He died in Wrocław in 2010, posthumously receiving the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta, while his legacy remains celebrated for introducing key technologies that anticipated modern computing trends.
