Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was a renowned aeronautic engineer and inventor, while his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician and Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. Both were pioneers in winter mountaineering in the Tatra Mountains. Karpiński’s childhood was marked by tragedy, including the death of his father during an expedition to Nanda Devi in the Himalayas.

During World War II, Karpiński, then a teenager, joined the Gray Ranks, a Polish underground paramilitary organization, and later the Home Army. His wartime experiences included severe injuries and near-paralysis after being shot during the Warsaw Uprising. Despite these hardships, he continued his involvement in the resistance, earning the Cross of Valour three times.

After the war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from high school in one year and later from the University of Łódź and Warsaw University of Technology. He worked initially in industry but faced challenges due to his past in the Home Army. His career shifted significantly with groundbreaking contributions to computer science. He developed the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, a pioneering computer for weather forecasting, and later the AKAT-1, the world’s first differential equations analyzer based on transistors in 1959.

Karpiński’s most notable achievement was the K-202, a minicomputer of original architecture, which he designed in 1970. Though K-202 faced political and economic obstacles, it represented a significant leap in Polish computer technology. His later years were marked by a move to Switzerland, where he worked on Nagra tape recorders, and he returned to Poland in 1990, serving as an advisor on computer science. He was posthumously awarded the Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta for his contributions to computer engineering.

Additionally, it was revealed that Karpiński worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa (Security Service) since 1961, providing technological data, though he maintained that his cooperation was limited to reporting on state-of-the-art American facilities.
