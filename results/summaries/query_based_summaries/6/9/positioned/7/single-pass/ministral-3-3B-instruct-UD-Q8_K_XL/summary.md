Jacek Karpiński was a Polish engineer and inventor born in Turin, Italy, in 1927. His family background included prominent intellectuals and alpinists, with his father, Adam Karpiński, being an aeronautical engineer and inventor. Karpiński’s early life was marked by World War II, where he served in the Polish underground resistance movement, the Home Army, and participated in the Warsaw Uprising, earning the Cross of Valour three times.

After the war, Karpiński studied at the Warsaw University of Technology and later worked at the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he developed pioneering computing devices. His most notable achievements include:

- **AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics)**: A vacuum-tube-based computer built in 1957, which improved weather forecasting accuracy.
- **AKAT-1**: The world’s first differential equations analyzer using transistors, completed in 1959, showcasing advanced computational capabilities.
- **Perceptron**: A learning device inspired by Frank Rosenblatt’s ideas, completed in 1964, one of the first known in the Communist Bloc.
- **KAR-65**: A transistor-based mathematical machine, completed in 1968, designed for data processing from CERN experiments, though its development was hindered by censorship.

Karpiński also worked on the **K-202**, a minicomputer of original architecture, which was innovative in its use of microchips, floating-point representation, and memory segmentation. Despite its technical excellence, K-202 failed to achieve mass production due to political and bureaucratic obstacles.

Later in life, Karpiński faced controversies, including accusations of working as a secret informant for the Polish intelligence services (Służba Bezpieczeństwa) from 1961 onward. He later moved to Switzerland, where he worked on Nagra tape recorders, and returned to Poland in 1990, where he served as an advisor on computer science. He died in Wrocław in 2010. Karpiński was posthumously awarded the Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta and later the Commander’s Cross of Polonia Restituta for his contributions to computer engineering.
