Jacek Karpiński was born in Turin, Italy, in 1927 to Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother was a physician. Karpiński's early life was marked by significant events, including his father's death in a Himalayan expedition in 1939.

During World War II, Karpiński was involved in the Polish underground resistance, sustaining severe injuries that led to blindness and partial hand damage. After the war, he pursued education, graduating from Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His major career achievements include constructing the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957 and, most notably, AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His later work involved establishing Microcomputers' Construction Plant in 1970 to develop the K-202 minicomputer.
