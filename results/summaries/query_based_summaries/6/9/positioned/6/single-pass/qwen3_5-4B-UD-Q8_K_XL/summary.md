**Jacek Karpiński** (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist born in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was a prominent aeronautic engineer and inventor of climbing equipment, while his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, became the Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. After losing his sight in both eyes and suffering hand injuries during a World War II sabotage operation while in the Gray Ranks and Home Army, Karpiński recovered and resumed his resistance activities. Following the war, he studied at the University of Łódź and Warsaw University of Technology, graduating in 1951.

Karpiński made significant contributions to computer engineering in Poland. His first independent project, the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, was a vacuum-tube computer that improved weather forecast precision. His breakthrough achievement was the **AKAT-1** in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, which was praised for its aesthetic design. Later, he developed the **perceptron** in 1964, one of the first such devices in the world and the first in the Communist bloc. In 1968, he completed the **KAR-65**, a fast asynchronic computer capable of 100,000 operations per second. In 1970, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to build the **K-202**, a minicomputer utilizing Intel 4004 chips and memory segmentation. Despite its technical excellence and innovative features, the K-202 failed to reach mass production due to political opposition and competition from the Riad project. Karpiński died on February 21, 2010, in Wrocław, Poland.
