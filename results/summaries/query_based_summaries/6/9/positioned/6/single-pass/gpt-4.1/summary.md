## Jacek Karpiński – Concise Summary

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer engineer renowned for his pioneering work in computer design and artificial intelligence. Surviving severe injuries sustained as a teenage resistance fighter during WWII, he later excelled in his studies, earning a degree from Warsaw University of Technology. His early projects included the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) and AKAT-1, the world’s first transistor-based differential equations analyzer, which garnered international recognition and a UNESCO scholarship in the United States. Karpiński interacted with prominent figures such as John Eckert and Claude Shannon and contributed to Polish intelligence by reporting on American technological advances.

Karpiński’s notable achievements include building one of the world’s first perceptrons and designing the KAR-65, then Poland’s fastest computer. His most famous innovation, the K-202 minicomputer, was highly advanced—using Intel 4004 chips, asynchronous processing, and memory segmentation with paging, making it faster than Western competitors like PDP-11. Despite its technical excellence, K-202 was never mass-produced due to political and industrial opposition within the communist regime. Later, Karpiński worked in Switzerland and returned to Poland, where he continued to innovate but faced business setbacks. He was honored with the Officer’s and Commander's Crosses of Polonia Restituta. His legacy remains debated, with some viewing him as a lost opportunity for Polish modernization, and his life is chronicled in several cultural works.
