Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish engineer and inventor, renowned for pioneering contributions to early computer science. Born into a family of intellectuals and alpinists, his early life was marked by tragedy, including the death of his father during an expedition in the Himalayas. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish Home Army, enduring severe injuries and later becoming a key figure in the Warsaw Uprising.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued engineering studies and worked on early computing projects. His most notable achievements include the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a vacuum-tube-based computer used for weather forecasting, and the **AKAT-1**, the world’s first differential equations analyzer using transistors. These projects earned him international recognition and a UNESCO scholarship in the U.S. for his engineering innovations.

Karpiński’s most ambitious project was the **K-202**, a minicomputer of original architecture, which he developed with British partners. Though commercially unsuccessful due to political and economic constraints, K-202 was innovative in its use of microchips, floating-point representation, and memory segmentation. Karpiński later worked on ultrasonography devices and other scientific instruments before retiring to a rural life in Poland.

Despite his groundbreaking work, Karpiński’s legacy is overshadowed by controversies, including his alleged collaboration with Polish intelligence services. He was posthumously awarded the Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta and later the Commander’s Cross of Polonia Restituta for his contributions to computer engineering. His inventions remain influential in the history of early computing.
