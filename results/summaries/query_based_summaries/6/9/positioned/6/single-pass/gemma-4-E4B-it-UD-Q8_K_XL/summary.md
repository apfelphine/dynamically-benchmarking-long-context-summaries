Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician. Karpiński was severely injured in 1943 while working on homemade bombs for a Polish underground sabotage operation, resulting in loss of sight in both eyes and a partial hand injury. Despite this, he resumed his activities in the Home Army and participated in the Warsaw Uprising, surviving being shot in the backbone, but remaining paralyzed afterward.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. He began his independent work with the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957. His most significant career achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His later work included designing the perceptron in 1964, and the KAR-65 computer in 1968, a fast, asynchronic machine. In 1970, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant, leading to the K-202, a highly innovative minicomputer. Karpiński was also a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa since 1961. He died on February 21, 2010, in Wrocław.
