Jacek Karpiński was an Italian-born Polish intellectual and aeronautic engineer who was involved in pioneering fields of science and technology. His father was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother was a physician. During World War II, Karpiński served in the Polish underground paramilitary organization, the Gray Ranks, and was severely injured in a bombing operation, resulting in blindness in both eyes and a partial hand injury.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued a career in scientific research. His major achievements include constructing the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) computer in 1957, which helped improve weather forecast precision, and the breakthrough achievement of AKAT-1 in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Following a UNESCO scholarship trip to the United States, he implemented a perceptron in Poland in 1964. Later, he designed the KAR-65 computer in 1968, and in 1970, established the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to build the K-202 minicomputer, which was technically innovative but never reached mass production. He died in 2010 and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta.
