**Summary of information regarding Jacek Karpiński:**

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer engineer and inventor, born into a family of Polish intellectuals and mountaineers. During World War II, he actively participated in the Polish resistance, sustaining serious injuries but recovering to continue his efforts. Post-war, he quickly completed high school and graduated from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. Karpiński made pioneering contributions to early computer technology, including building the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957, the transistor-based differential equations analyzer AKAT-1 in 1959, and the perceptron in 1964. He also designed scanners and the KAR-65 computer, Poland’s fastest at the time. His most notable achievement was the K-202 minicomputer (early 1970s), innovative for its use of Intel 4004 microchips, asynchronous operation, memory segmentation with paging, and high performance. Despite technical excellence, political and institutional resistance limited its production and adoption, resulting in only about 30 units built.

Karpiński’s work garnered international recognition, including a UNESCO engineering scholarship to the US, where he visited major technological centers and met leading scientists. Later, he worked in Switzerland on audio technology before returning to Poland and advising on computer science. Controversially, Karpiński cooperated as an informant with Polish intelligence during the Cold War, providing technological information, but later his relationship with the authorities soured. He died in 2010 and was posthumously honored with the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta. His career is seen as a mixture of pioneering innovation hindered by political and institutional challenges, with his legacy debated among historians and engineers.
