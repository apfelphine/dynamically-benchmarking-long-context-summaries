Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist born in Turin, Italy, to a family of intellectuals and alpinists. After sustaining severe injuries during World War II while working on homemade bombs for the Polish Home Army, he survived the war and recovered his vision, though he never regained full motor control of his injured hand. His post-war career was marked by significant contributions to computer engineering, including the design of the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer (AKAT-1) in 1959, and the perceptron, one of the first such devices in the Communist bloc, completed in 1964. Karpiński is perhaps best known for the K-202 minicomputer, an innovative asynchronous machine featuring memory segmentation and floating-point representation that utilized Intel 4004 chips. Despite its technical excellence and superior performance to contemporary Western competitors like the PDP-11, the K-202 failed to reach mass production due to political sabotage, administrative incompetence, and a lack of commercial backing from state officials. Karpiński served as an advisor to the Polish government in the 1990s and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2010. His work remains a subject of historical debate regarding the intersection of engineering achievement and political obstructionism in the People's Republic of Poland.
