# Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer scientist and engineer renowned for pioneering contributions to the development of early computers in Poland. Born in Turin to aeronautic engineer Adam Karpiński and physician Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, he survived severe injuries during World War II while working with the Polish Home Army. After recovering, he pursued higher education in Łódź and Warsaw, graduating from the Warsaw University of Technology in 1951. His career began with the design of the Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics (AAH) in 1957, which improved weather forecast precision, followed by the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors.

Karpiński's most significant achievement was the design of the KAR-65 computer in 1968, a high-speed, asynchronous machine capable of 100,000 operations per second, which was used in the Institute of Physics until 1988. Later, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant to develop the K-202 minicomputer, an innovative, modular system utilizing Intel 4004 chips and floating-point representation that was technically superior to contemporary Western models like the DEC PDP-11. However, despite its capabilities, the K-202 failed to reach mass production due to political opposition, lack of state backing, and bureaucratic sabotage within the communist regime. Karpiński spent his later years in Switzerland and returned to Poland in 1990 to advise on computer science reforms, receiving the Officer's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009 and posthumously the Commander's Cross in 2010.
