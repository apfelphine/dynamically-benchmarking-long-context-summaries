Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor.

Karpiński's life was significantly impacted by World War II, during which he served in the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts organization. Following severe injuries from an accidental bomb explosion in 1943, he continued resistance activities. After the war, he pursued higher education in Poland, graduating in 1951. His significant contributions to engineering included constructing the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957 and, most notably, AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he led the development of the minicomputer K-202 in 1970, which was highly innovative despite failing to reach mass production. He also implemented the perceptron, a device designed to discern and recognize objects and shapes, in 1964.
