Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, a prominent aeronautic engineer, and mother, a physician, were pioneers in winter mountaineering. Karpiński joined the Gray Ranks during World War II and served in the Home Army, participating in numerous operations, including the Warsaw Uprising, where he survived a bullet wound and was left paralyzed. After the war, he studied at the Warsaw University of Technology and worked in the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he designed the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) and later the AKAT-1, the world’s first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. His work on the perceptron and the KAR-65 computer further advanced Polish computing. In 1960, he was awarded a UNESCO scholarship to study in the United States, where he met leading computer scientists. Upon returning, he led the development of the K-202 minicomputer, a technically innovative machine that was hindered by political and administrative obstacles. After its failure, he moved to Switzerland and later returned to Poland, advising on computer science. Karpiński faced controversy for working as a secret informant for the Polish intelligence service. He died in 2010 and was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta.
