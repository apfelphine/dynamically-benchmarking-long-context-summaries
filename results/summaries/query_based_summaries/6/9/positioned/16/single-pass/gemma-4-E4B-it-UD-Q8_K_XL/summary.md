Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, to Polish intellectuals. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician and Dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw. Following his father's death in 1939, Karpiński joined the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts during World War II and participated in the Warsaw Uprising. He sustained a gunshot wound during the Uprising, which left him paralyzed.

Post-war, Karpiński pursued higher education, graduating in 1951. His career included developing the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, and his most significant achievement was constructing AKAT-1 in 1959, which was the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he implemented a perceptron in 1964 and designed the KAR-65 computer in 1968. In 1970, he founded Microcomputers' Construction Plant, which produced the K-202 minicomputer in 1973. Karpiński was later revealed to have worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa. He died on February 21, 2010, in Wrocław.
