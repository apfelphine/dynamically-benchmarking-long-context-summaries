Jacek Karpiński was born on April 9, 1927, in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and mountaineers. His father, Adam 'Akar' Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor. During World War II, Karpiński joined the Polish underground paramilitary boy scouts and later resumed resistance activities with the Home Army. He was severely injured in 1943 while working on homemade bombs, losing sight in both eyes, though his eyes later recovered. He was shot during the Warsaw Uprising and remained paralyzed afterward.

Post-war, Karpiński studied at Warsaw University of Technology, graduating in 1951. His early projects included the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics) in 1957, a vacuum-tube computer designed to calculate Fourier integrals. His most significant achievement was the construction of AKAT-1 in 1959, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors. Later, he built the perceptron in 1964. In 1968, he designed KAR-65, a machine for computing scanned data. Subsequently, he founded the Microcomputers' Construction Plant and developed K-202, a highly innovative, asynchronous minicomputer built entirely with microchips. Karpiński died in Wrocław on February 21, 2010.
