## Jacek Karpiński: Key Contributions and Life

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a pioneering Polish computer engineer known for his innovative work in computer design under challenging political and economic circumstances. After joining the resistance during World War II, he overcame severe war injuries and completed his education, ultimately working at the Polish Academy of Sciences. Karpiński built several notable devices including the AAH for weather forecasting, AKAT-1 (the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer), and one of the first perceptrons in the Communist bloc. His KAR-65 computer was the fastest in Poland, designed specifically for processing CERN data.

Karpiński's most prominent achievement was the K-202 minicomputer, developed between 1970–1973. The K-202 was highly advanced for its time, featuring modular design, microchip integration, memory segmentation with paging, and the ability to perform close to a million operations per second. Despite its technical excellence, K-202 was never mass-produced, largely due to political obstruction, industrial monopoly pressures, and the prioritization of Soviet-aligned computer projects. Only about 30 units were built, with most operating in Polish institutions. Later in life, disappointed by the lack of support in Poland, Karpiński moved abroad and attempted various business ventures. He was posthumously awarded high Polish honors for his contributions. Karpiński's career was also marked by controversy due to his work as an informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa, mostly focused on technical intelligence. His legacy remains debated, with some viewing him as a symbol of unrealized technological potential in Communist-era Poland.
