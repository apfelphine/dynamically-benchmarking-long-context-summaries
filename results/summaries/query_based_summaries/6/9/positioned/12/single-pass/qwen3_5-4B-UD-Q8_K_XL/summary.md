# Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a pioneering Polish computer scientist and engineer renowned for developing several of the country's first advanced computing systems. Born into a family of intellectuals and alpinists, he survived severe injuries during World War II, losing his sight and hand, before joining the Home Army and later studying at the Warsaw University of Technology. His career began with the **AAH** (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a vacuum-tube computer built in 1957 to improve weather forecasting. His breakthrough achievement came in 1959 with **AKAT-1**, the world's first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, which was celebrated for its innovative design and aesthetic appeal.

In 1964, Karpiński implemented one of the first perceptrons in the Communist bloc, and in 1968 he completed **KAR-65**, a mathematical machine capable of 100,000 operations per second that processed data from CERN's bubble chambers. His most significant contribution was the **K-202 minicomputer**, launched in 1970 as a commercially viable, microchip-based system featuring memory segmentation and floating-point arithmetic. Although only 30 units were produced before the project was abandoned due to political interference and a lack of industrial backing, the K-202 was technically superior to contemporary competitors like the DEC PDP-11. Karpiński continued his work in Switzerland and later Poland, where he passed away in 2010; he was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2010.
