# Summary: Education in Germany

Germany's education system is state-controlled, with secondary education divided into lower and upper levels, and multiple school types including Gymnasium (prepares for university), Realschule, Hauptschule, Gesamtschule (comprehensive), and Sonderschule (special needs). The Gymnasium concludes with the Abitur examination, typically after grade 12 or 13, qualifying students for higher education. The school structure includes an orientation phase in grades 5 and 6 to determine the appropriate secondary school path. Foreign language instruction, particularly English, is compulsory in secondary education. The dual education system (Duale Ausbildung) combines vocational schooling with apprenticeships in companies, providing practical and theoretical training.

Primary education begins between ages 5 to 7, with options including state, Montessori, Waldorf, and religious schools. Homeschooling is illegal under compulsory schooling laws. Public schooling dominates, but private schools exist, including Ersatzschulen, which are government-subsidized and must adhere to educational standards without socioeconomic segregation. Special schools serve children with disabilities but are debated due to concerns about segregation. Universities in Germany, mostly public and tuition-free, offer internationally recognized degrees with opportunities for bachelor's, master's, doctorate, and state examinations (Staatsexamen) for professions like medicine and law. Admissions generally require the Abitur or equivalent qualification, with some variations and additional exams for non-traditional candidates. Education is decentralized, leading to variation across the 16 states, but federal standards aim to ensure quality. The educational attainment and school allocation are influenced by social class and immigrant background, with ongoing debates about educational equity and tracking.
