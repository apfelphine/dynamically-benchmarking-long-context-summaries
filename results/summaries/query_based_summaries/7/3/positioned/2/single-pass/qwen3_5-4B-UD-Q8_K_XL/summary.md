Germany's education system is decentralized, with the 16 federal states (*Länder*) holding constitutional sovereignty over educational policy, leading to significant variations in curriculum and structure across the country. Secondary education is primarily divided into three tracks: *Gymnasium* (preparing students for university, lasting 8–9 years with the *Abitur* exam), *Realschule* (intermediate level leading to *Mittlere Reife*), and *Hauptschule* (vocational focus leading to *Hauptschulabschluss*). A growing number of states utilize *Gesamtschulen*, which combine these tracks into a comprehensive system. Vocational training is highly valued through the dual education system (*Duale Ausbildung*), where apprentices alternate between theoretical instruction at a *Berufsschule* and practical work in companies.

Tertiary education consists of *Universitäten* (research universities awarding doctorates) and *Fachhochschulen* (Universities of Applied Sciences focusing on practical skills). Public universities generally charge only nominal semester fees (€150–€350) for domestic students, though long-time students and non-EU citizens may face higher fees. The standard degree structure follows the Bologna process with three-year bachelor's and two-year master's degrees. Admission to universities typically requires the *Abitur*, though alternative pathways exist for vocational graduates. The system is characterized by a strong emphasis on research, high international rankings, and a cultural respect for skilled craftsmen and vocational qualifications.
