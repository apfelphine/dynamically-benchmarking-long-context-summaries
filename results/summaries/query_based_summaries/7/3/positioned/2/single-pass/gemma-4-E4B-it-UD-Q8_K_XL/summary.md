**Education in Germany**

German secondary education is divided into lower and upper levels. The lower-secondary education prepares students for upper-secondary education, which generally leads to vocational training. There are five subtypes of schools: Gymnasium, Realschule, Hauptschule, Gesamtschule, and Sonderschule. The Gymnasium prepares students for higher education and concludes with the Abitur, while the Realschule and Hauptschule prepare students for vocational education with exit examinations like the Mittlere Reife (after grade 10) and Hauptschulabschluss (after grade 9), respectively. Comprehensive schools (Gesamtschule) combine elements of the above.

The education system features a dual education system (Duale Ausbildung), which integrates in-service training in a company with theoretical education at a state school. The 16 states have exclusive responsibility for education, leading to variations in curricula. Public universities generally charge only small semester fees (€150–€350), and financial aid is available through the state-funded BAföG. Literacy rates are high, exceeding 99% for those aged 15 and above. While students from private schools have been observed to underperform in PISA tests relative to state schools when socioeconomic class is factored in, the system features a strong tradition of vocational training and a commitment to educational autonomy at the state level.
