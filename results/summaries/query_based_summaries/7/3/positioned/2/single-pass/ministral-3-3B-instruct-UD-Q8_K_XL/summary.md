**Education in Germany**

Germany’s education system is decentralized, managed by the 16 states (*Länder*), with compulsory schooling from age 6 to 16. The system is divided into **primary education** (Grundschule, ages 6–10) and **secondary education**, which includes five main types of schools:

- **Gymnasium** (ages 10–18): Prepares students for university with the *Abitur* exam, offering a broad academic curriculum.
- **Realschule** (ages 10–14): Provides intermediate education leading to the *Mittlere Reife* exam, allowing students to pursue vocational training or further education.
- **Hauptschule** (ages 10–9): Focuses on vocational readiness, culminating in the *Hauptschulabschluss* exam.
- **Gesamtschule** (ages 10–16): A comprehensive school offering a mix of academic and vocational paths, with varying exit qualifications.
- **Sonderschule** (ages 6–18): Specialized schools for students with special educational needs.

After secondary education, students can enter vocational training (*duale Ausbildung*), combining classroom instruction with on-the-job experience, or pursue higher education. The system emphasizes bilingual education, with English as a core subject, and includes extracurricular activities like sports, music, and arts.

Germany’s education is highly regarded internationally, with students consistently performing well in global assessments like PISA. However, challenges include social disparities in school quality, regional differences in curricula, and debates over tracking students by ability. The system also incorporates a strong emphasis on vocational training, making it unique among Western nations.
