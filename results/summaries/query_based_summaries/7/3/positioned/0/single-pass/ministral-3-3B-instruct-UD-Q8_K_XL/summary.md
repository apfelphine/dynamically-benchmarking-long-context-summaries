Germany’s education system is highly decentralized, with responsibility for education primarily held by the 16 states (*Länder*). The system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a strong emphasis on vocational training and the dual education system (*Duale Ausbildung*).

### **Primary Education**
- **Duration**: Typically 4–6 years (elementary school), depending on the state.
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on foundational subjects like German, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (often starting in primary school).
- **Compulsory**: Free and mandatory for children aged 6–10 (or 6–12 in Berlin and Brandenburg).

### **Secondary Education**
Germany’s secondary education is divided into five main school types, each leading to different qualifications:

1. **Gymnasium** (Academic Track)
   - Prepares students for university entry with the *Abitur* exam after grades 12–13.
   - Historically, the *G8* model (8-year Gymnasium) was phased out in 2019, replaced by *G9* (9-year Gymnasium).

2. **Realschule** (Intermediate Track)
   - Offers the *Mittlere Reife* after grades 10–11, allowing students to pursue vocational training or further education.

3. **Hauptschule** (Vocational Track)
   - Focuses on practical skills, leading to the *Hauptschulabschluss* after grade 9, preparing students for entry-level vocational jobs.

4. **Gesamtschule** (Comprehensive School)
   - Integrates students from different tracks, offering a mix of academic and vocational options. Some provide the *Abitur* after 8 years.

5. **Sonderschule** (Special Needs Schools)
   - Serves students with disabilities, though some may integrate into mainstream schools.

**Vocational Training**:
- The *Duale Ausbildung* system combines classroom education with on-the-job training, offering apprenticeships in various fields.
- Students attend vocational schools (*Berufsschulen*) twice weekly while working in companies.

### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: Public institutions offering degrees (e.g., *Bachelor*, *Master*, *Ph.D.*), with tuition fees capped at €60–500 per semester.
- **Fachhochschulen (Universities of Applied Sciences)**: Focus on practical, career-oriented education, offering diplomas and bachelor’s/master’s degrees.
- **Vocational Training**: Includes apprenticeships, vocational schools, and specialized training programs.

### **Key Features**
- **Decentralization**: Each state (*Länder*) sets its own curriculum, exams, and school policies.
- **Compulsory Education**: Free and mandatory for children aged 6–10 (or 6–12 in Berlin and Brandenburg).
- **Vocational Focus**: Strong emphasis on vocational training and the dual education system.
- **PISA Performance**: German students consistently score above the OECD average in reading, mathematics, and science, though disparities exist between regions and socioeconomic backgrounds.

### **Recent Reforms**
- **Inklusion (Inclusion)**: Efforts to integrate students with disabilities into mainstream schools.
- **Digitalization**: Increased use of technology in classrooms, including online learning and digital resources.
- **Language Policy**: Bilingual education in some subjects, with mandatory English lessons in primary school.

Germany’s education system balances academic and vocational paths, ensuring a well-rounded education while preparing students for diverse career opportunities.
