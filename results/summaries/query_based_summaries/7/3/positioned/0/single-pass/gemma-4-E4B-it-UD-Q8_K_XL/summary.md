## Education in Germany

Germany's education system is decentralized, with responsibility resting with the individual states ($\text{Länder}$). Secondary education is divided into lower and upper tiers. The secondary school types include the *Gymnasium*, which prepares pupils for higher education with the *Abitur* examination; the *Realschule* and *Hauptschule*, which prepare pupils for vocational education with exams like *Mittlere Reife* or *Hauptschulabschluss*; the *Gesamtschule* (comprehensive school), which combines elements of the above; and *Förder- or Sonderschulen* for students with special educational needs. Vocational training is supported by the *Duale Ausbildung* (dual education system), which combines in-service company training with state school instruction.

The system features compulsory education from age 6 to 16, with an early orientation phase in some states guiding students toward one of the various academic or vocational paths. While public universities charge minimal semester fees, some states have introduced tuition fees for certain student groups. The structure of the system is subject to continuous reform, with recent trends focusing on increased use of technology and local school philosophy.
