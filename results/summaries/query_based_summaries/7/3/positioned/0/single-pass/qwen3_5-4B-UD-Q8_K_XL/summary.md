Based strictly on the provided text, education in Germany is a decentralized system where responsibility for education and professional training lies with the 16 federal states (Länder), which hold constitutional sovereignty over the field. The system is characterized by a strict separation of church and state, where religious education is provided by churches in cooperation with the state, and compulsory school prayers are constitutionally prohibited. Education is divided into preschool (Kindergarten/Kita), primary (Grundschule), and secondary stages. Secondary education is traditionally tripartite, comprising the *Gymnasium* (academic track leading to the *Abitur* and university), the *Realschule* (leading to *Mittlere Reife* and vocational paths), and the *Hauptschule* (leading to *Hauptschulabschluss* and vocational training). A significant portion of the population follows a dual education system (*Duale Ausbildung*), which combines theoretical instruction at a vocational school (*Berufsschule*) with practical on-the-job training in companies, making Germany unique globally for the high value placed on skilled craftsmanship over academic degrees.

Higher education in Germany consists primarily of public universities (*Universitäten*), which have the right to confer doctorates, and universities of applied sciences (*Fachhochschulen*), which focus on practical skills and do not award PhDs in their own right. Public universities are funded by the states and generally do not charge tuition fees, though semester fees and fees for long-time students exist; however, Baden-Württemberg reintroduced tuition fees for non-EU students in 2017. Admission to universities typically requires the *Abitur*, though alternative routes exist for vocational graduates. The system has faced international criticism regarding low PISA rankings and a high socio-economic gradient in academic attainment, though reforms since the early 2000s have included efforts to improve teacher training, digitalization, and the introduction of English as a mandatory foreign language in primary schools.
