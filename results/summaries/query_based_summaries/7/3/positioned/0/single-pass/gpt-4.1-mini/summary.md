## Education in Germany

Germany’s education system is primarily administered at the state (Länder) level, with a structured division into preschool, primary, secondary, and tertiary education. Preschool education (Kindergarten or Kita) is available but non-compulsory for children aged 2 to 6. Primary education (Grundschule) typically lasts four years, followed by secondary education which is split into lower and upper secondary schools. Secondary education offers multiple pathways: Gymnasium (academic track preparing for university with the Abitur exam), Realschule (intermediate track), Hauptschule (vocational track), Gesamtschule (comprehensive school combining tracks), and Sonderschulen (special schools for students with special needs). Early tracking into these school forms occurs around grade 4, influencing future academic and career trajectories.

The dual education system (Duale Ausbildung) is a hallmark of German education, blending vocational schooling with company-based apprenticeships, enabling practical and theoretical learning. Tertiary education consists of public universities (Universitäten) able to confer doctoral degrees and Fachhochschulen (Universities of Applied Sciences) focused on applied studies that usually do not award doctorates. University entrance requires the Abitur or equivalent qualifications, with various admission regimes including free admission, local restrictions, and nationwide quotas for certain competitive fields. Tuition fees are generally low or absent at public universities, though some states have introduced fees for certain students. Germany’s education quality scores above OECD averages in reading, mathematics, and science, but debates continue about early tracking, inclusion, and equal opportunity across social classes and immigrant populations.
