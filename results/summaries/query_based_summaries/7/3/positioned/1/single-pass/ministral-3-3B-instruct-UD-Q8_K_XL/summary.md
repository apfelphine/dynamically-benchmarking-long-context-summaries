Germany’s education system is structured around **state autonomy**, with each of the 16 federal states (Länder) managing its own curriculum, school policies, and teacher qualifications. Education is **compulsory** from age 6 to 16, with a focus on foundational skills in primary school (Grundschule) and structured pathways in secondary education (Gymnasium, Realschule, Hauptschule, Gesamtschule).

**Primary Education (Grundschule, ages 6–10 or 12 in Berlin/Brandenburg):**
- Focuses on core subjects like German, mathematics, science, and foreign languages (often starting with English).
- Students transition into secondary education after Grade 4 or 6, depending on the state, based on academic recommendations.
- No formal grading in early grades; later grades introduce letter grades (A–D) and numerical scores (1–10).

**Secondary Education:**
- **Gymnasium (academic track):** Prepares students for university admission via the **Abitur** exam after 12 or 13 years (G8/G9).
- **Realschule (intermediate track):** Offers the **Mittlere Reife** after 10 years, enabling vocational training or further education.
- **Hauptschule (vocational track):** Provides the **Hauptschulabschluss** after 9 years, leading to entry-level vocational training.
- **Gesamtschule (comprehensive):** Blends academic and vocational paths, with flexible grading and potential for Abitur after 8 years.

**Vocational Training (Duale Ausbildung):**
- Combines classroom instruction with on-the-job training in companies, offering practical skills alongside academic education.
- Students attend a Berufsschule (vocational school) 2–3 days per week while working full-time.

**Higher Education:**
- Universities (Universitäten) and Fachhochschulen (Universities of Applied Sciences) offer degrees, with public institutions charging minimal fees (semester fees).
- Admission to universities typically requires the **Abitur** or equivalent qualifications, with some states offering alternative pathways for skilled workers.

**Key Features:**
- **Decentralized governance:** State autonomy ensures varied curricula and school structures across regions.
- **Compulsory education:** Mandates 10–12 years of schooling, with emphasis on foundational literacy and numeracy.
- **Vocational integration:** Strong dual education system bridges academic and practical training.
- **Grading and tracking:** Early academic recommendations influence future educational paths, though comprehensive schools aim to reduce rigid tracking.

Germany’s system balances academic rigor with vocational readiness, reflecting its emphasis on both theoretical knowledge and practical skills.
