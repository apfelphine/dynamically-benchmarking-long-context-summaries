# Education in Germany

Germany's education system is largely decentralized, with each of the 16 federal states (Länder) having sovereignty over education. The school system divides secondary education into lower and upper levels, further split into distinct types of schools: Gymnasium (prepares for university, finishing with the Abitur exam after grade 12 or 13), Realschule (intermediate education with Mittlere Reife exam after grade 10), Hauptschule (vocational orientation, finishing after grade 9), Gesamtschule (comprehensive school combining tracks), and Sonderschule/Förderschule for students with special educational needs. After secondary school, students often enter the dual education system (Duale Ausbildung), combining vocational training in companies with vocational school attendance. Foreign language education is mandatory, with English widely studied and other languages depending on the state. Kindergarten (ages 2 to 6) is widespread but not compulsory or free, while homeschooling is illegal due to compulsory schooling laws. School uniforms are generally not used. 

Tertiary education includes public universities and Fachhochschulen (Universities of Applied Sciences). University admission typically requires the Abitur or equivalent entrance qualifications. Tuition fees are generally low or non-existent for public universities, though fees exist for long-term students and international students in some states. The Bologna Process introduced bachelor’s and master’s degrees, replacing traditional Diplom and Magister degrees. German universities emphasize academic freedom, allowing students to choose courses freely, and research is conducted at universities and a range of specialized institutes (Max Planck, Fraunhofer, etc.). Social class and immigrant background strongly influence academic attainment, with children from more affluent or academic families more likely to attend Gymnasium. Gender differences are notable, with males more often in special education and post-graduate roles but underperforming at lower levels. Approximately 15% of university students are international. Overall literacy exceeds 99% among those aged 15 and above.
