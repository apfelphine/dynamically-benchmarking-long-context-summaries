# Summary: Education in Germany

Education in Germany is primarily the responsibility of the individual states (Länder), resulting in a decentralized system with variations across regions. The school system is divided into primary education (Grundschule), followed by secondary education, which includes several tracks: Gymnasium (academic, leading to university via the Abitur exam), Realschule (intermediate, ending with Mittlere Reife), Hauptschule (vocational-oriented, ending with Hauptschulabschluss), and Gesamtschule (comprehensive combining other tracks). The dual education system uniquely blends vocational school and apprenticeships in companies. Language learning is compulsory, with English being most common, and some Gymnasien offer Latin or other languages. Attendance at private schools (Ersatzschulen) is protected by the constitution, subsidized, and accounts for roughly 9% of students. Special schools serve children with disabilities, though mainstreaming is increasing. Homeschooling is illegal, emphasizing children's social rights.

Tertiary education features over a million new students annually, with about 400,000 obtaining the Abitur and 30,000 doctoral degrees awarded each year. Universities (Universitäten) have the exclusive right to confer doctorates and emphasize research, while Fachhochschulen (Universities of Applied Sciences) focus on practical skills and do not grant doctoral degrees. Admission typically requires the Abitur or equivalent qualifications, with alternative pathways including aptitude tests and vocational experience. Tuition fees at public universities were abolished by 2014 in all states, though semester fees for administration and student services remain. Scholarships and state-funded loans (BAföG) support financially disadvantaged students. International students constitute about 15% of the cohort, facing different tuition arrangements. Recent reforms have introduced bilingual education, increased practical teacher training, and enhanced digital infrastructure in schools. Germany’s education system balances tradition with modern reforms, striving to maintain academic freedom and practical skills development.
