## Education in Germany

German education is decentralized, with the 16 federal states (Länder) holding constitutional responsibility for their respective school systems. Secondary education is divided into lower and upper tiers, categorized into subtypes such as the Gymnasium (aimed at higher education), Realschule, Hauptschule, Gesamtschule (comprehensive school), and Sonderschule (special school). The system involves an early segregation of students based on academic achievement, a characteristic noted in PISA results, which show a high socio-economic gradient in student performance.

At the tertiary level, the German system includes *Universitäten* (which hold the right to confer doctorates) and *Fachhochschulen* (Universities of Applied Sciences), with the latter focusing on applied research and employability. Most public universities charge minimal semester fees, though long-time students and non-EU/EEA students in some states are subject to tuition fees. The vocational track, known as the dual education system (*Duale Ausbildung*), combines practical in-service company training with theoretical instruction at a state school, and it remains a highly valued part of German society.
