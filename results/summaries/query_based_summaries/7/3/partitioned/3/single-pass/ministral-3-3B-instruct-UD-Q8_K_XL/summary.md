Germany’s education system is highly decentralized, with responsibility for education falling under the states (Länder). The system is structured into three main phases: **primary education**, **secondary education**, and **tertiary education**.

**Secondary Education** in Germany is divided into five main types of schools: **Gymnasium**, **Realschule**, **Hauptschule**, **Gesamtschule**, and **Sonderschule**. Each type prepares students for different career paths:

- **Gymnasium** (academic track) leads to the **Abitur**, enabling students to attend university.
- **Realschule** and **Hauptschule** focus on vocational training, with the **Mittlere Reife** and **Hauptschulabschluss** certifications, respectively.
- **Gesamtschule** combines elements of the above, offering flexibility.
- **Sonderschule** serves students with special educational needs.

The system emphasizes **dual education**, where students alternate between classroom learning and practical training in apprenticeships. This approach is highly valued, offering a blend of theoretical and hands-on experience.

**Tertiary Education** in Germany includes universities and **Fachhochschulen** (universities of applied sciences). Universities confer doctorates and habilitations, while Fachhochschulen offer practical degrees. Public universities generally do not charge tuition fees, though students pay a semester fee covering administrative costs. Scholarships and financial aid, such as **BAföG**, support students from diverse backgrounds.

Germany’s education system is noted for its **academic freedom**, rigorous research focus, and emphasis on practical skills, particularly through apprenticeships. The system remains a model for other countries, balancing academic rigor with vocational training.
