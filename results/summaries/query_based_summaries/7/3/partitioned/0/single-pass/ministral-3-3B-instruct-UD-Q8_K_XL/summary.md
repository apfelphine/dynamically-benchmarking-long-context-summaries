Germany’s education system is highly decentralized, with responsibility for schools and education policies assigned to the 16 federal states (*Länder*). The system is structured into **primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a strong emphasis on vocational training through the **dual education system** (*Duale Ausbildung*).

### **Secondary Education**
Germany’s secondary education is divided into **five main types of schools**, each leading to different qualifications:

1. **Gymnasium** – Prepares students for higher education, culminating in the **Abitur** (university entrance qualification) after grades 12 or 13. Historically, the **G8** model (Abitur in 8 years) was phased out in 2019, reverting to **G9** (12 years).

2. **Realschule** – Offers an intermediate qualification, the **Mittlere Reife**, after grades 10 or 11. Students can then pursue vocational training or further education at a **Fachoberschule** (vocational high school).

3. **Hauptschule** – Focuses on vocational training, leading to the **Hauptschulabschluss** after grade 9. Students typically enter vocational schools afterward.

4. **Gesamtschule** – A comprehensive school combining elements of the above, offering flexibility in curriculum and qualifications. Some can lead to the **Abitur** after 8 years.

5. **Sonderschule** – Specialized schools for students with special educational needs, though integration (*Inklusion*) is increasingly encouraged.

Students transition between these paths in **grade 5 or 6** (depending on the state) based on academic performance and recommendations. The system ensures that students receive tailored education aligned with their abilities and career aspirations.

### **Vocational Training & Tertiary Education**
- **Dual Education System (*Duale Ausbildung*)**: Combines classroom learning with on-the-job training, allowing students to earn a **Berufsabschluss** (vocational certificate) after 3–3.5 years.
- **Hochschulen (Universities & Fachhochschulen)**: Public universities offer **Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees** (Bologna Process reforms), while **Fachhochschulen** (universities of applied sciences) focus on practical training.
- **Tuition Fees**: Public universities typically do not charge tuition, though students pay a **semester fee** (~€150–€350). Some states (e.g., Baden-Württemberg) reintroduced tuition fees for non-EU students.

Germany’s education system balances academic rigor with vocational readiness, ensuring students are prepared for both higher education and the workforce.
