## Education in Germany Summary

German education is decentralized, with the 16 states ($\text{Länder}$) holding constitutional sovereignty over its systems. Secondary education is divided into lower and upper stages. The system features several school types: Gymnasium (aimed at higher education, culminating in the $\text{Abitur}$), Realschule (intermediate focus, culminating in $\text{Mittlere Reife}$), Hauptschule (preparing for vocational education, culminating in $\text{Hauptschulabschluss}$), Gesamtschule (a comprehensive alternative), and $\text{Förderschulen}$ or $\text{Sonderschulen}$ for special educational needs. A dual education system, $\text{Duale Ausbildung}$, combines in-service training in a company with study at a state school for vocational courses.

Tertiary education is characterized by a distinction between $\text{Universität}$ (institutions with the right to confer doctorates) and $\text{Fachhochschulen}$ (Universities of Applied Sciences), which focus on practical application. Most public universities are tuition-free, though students must pay a semester fee. The Bologna Declaration introduced standardized degrees, including Bachelor's and Master's. While the system emphasizes academic freedom, it also faces ongoing debates regarding the socio-economic gradient in achievement across different school types.
