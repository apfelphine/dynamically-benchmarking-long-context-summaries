## Education in China

**Higher Education**
China's higher education system, which boasts the world's highest number of institutions, has undergone significant modernization. In the 1980s, universities gained greater autonomy, allowing them to use tuition and service income for their own development. Traditionally, national entrance examinations remain central to student recruitment, although various admissions criteria, including academic records and preferential treatment for minority or disadvantaged candidates, have been introduced. Since 1986, enrollment policies shifted to align with national modernization needs, involving state quotas and employer requests, though this system has evolved. More recently, the system has diversified, moving toward greater decentralization, diversification, and openness, with top universities aiming for world-class status through initiatives like Double First-Class Construction.

**Basic and Adult Education**
Basic education is mandated by the Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education, which covers six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education. Curriculum elements include Chinese, mathematics, and mandatory political studies emphasizing communist values. While urban schools often run on a six-day schedule, rural schools maintain flexible schedules tailored to agricultural needs. Adult education has seen various forms, such as night schools and correspondence/television universities, which have expanded significantly. Furthermore, China has focused heavily on integrating Information and Communications Technology (ICT) into education through top-down planning, aiming to provide quality educational resources to all adults.
