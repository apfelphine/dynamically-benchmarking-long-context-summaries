## Education in China

Education in China has undergone significant evolution, shifting from an initial focus on improving population-wide literacy in the early People's Republic era. Since the Cultural Revolution, the system has been geared toward economic modernization. Key reforms included the devolution of basic education responsibility to local governments in 1985, the call for nine years of compulsory education, and a renewed emphasis on science and technology training, particularly from the early 1980s onward. Currently, basic education includes pre-school, nine-year compulsory education (elementary to junior high school), standard senior high school, and special education.

The education system is characterized by intense competition, which is visible in the college entrance examination (Gaokao). While the government mandates nine years of compulsory education, regional inequalities persist, with rural areas facing challenges like teacher shortages. Modern trends include a push for lifelong learning, the development of vocational and technical education, and the integration of Information and Communications Technology (ICT) into education. Furthermore, the central government maintains tight control over the system, evidenced by the rigorous examination and censorship of all curricula and textbooks to align with the directives of the Chinese Communist Party.
