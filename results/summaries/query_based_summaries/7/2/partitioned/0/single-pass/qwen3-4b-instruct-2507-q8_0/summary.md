**Education in China**  

China’s education system emphasizes universal access, modernization, and economic development. Since the founding of the People’s Republic, education has evolved from basic literacy campaigns to a comprehensive, state-led system. The **Nine-Year Compulsory Education Law (1986)** established universal primary and junior high education, covering 85% of the population, though rural areas still face challenges in access and quality due to teacher shortages and infrastructure gaps.  

The curriculum integrates political education, with a strong focus on socialist values, and includes mandatory subjects such as Chinese, mathematics, and ideological studies. **Vocational and technical education** has been prioritized to meet economic demands, with a growing number of vocational schools and technical training programs. The **Gaokao** (national college entrance exam) remains the primary gateway to higher education, with intense competition and high stakes.  

Higher education has expanded rapidly, with over 3,000 universities and more than 44 million students enrolled. The **Double First-Class Construction** initiative aims to elevate elite institutions to world-class status. The government invests heavily in education, with education spending increasing annually, and promotes lifelong learning through adult education, distance learning, and online platforms.  

**Reforms under Xi Jinping** include reducing homework burdens, limiting private tutoring, and promoting health and physical education in schools. The system also emphasizes ideological indoctrination, with mandatory political education and the inclusion of "Xi Jinping Thought" in curricula. Despite high enrollment, concerns remain over regional inequality, teacher shortages, and the overemphasis on rote memorization and standardized testing.  

China ranks among the world’s top nations in university numbers and research output, with leading institutions like Peking University and Tsinghua University gaining global recognition. However, criticism persists over academic rigidity, regional disparities, and the quality of education in rural areas.
