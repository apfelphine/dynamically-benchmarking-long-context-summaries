# Education in China

Education in China has undergone significant reforms since 1949, beginning with a focus on literacy improvement, expanding elementary and secondary enrollment substantially. The post-Cultural Revolution era reoriented education towards economic modernization, emphasizing science, technology, and vocational training aligned with national development goals. The 1986 Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education mandated six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education, with allowances for local adaptation, although rural and minority areas still face challenges in access and quality.

Primary education varies between urban key schools, which follow rigorous full-time schedules, and rural schools, which adapt to agricultural cycles with flexible scheduling and limited curriculums. Secondary education is split into junior (compulsory) and senior levels, with senior secondary education preparing students for university via competitive entrance exams (Gaokao and Zhongkao). Vocational and technical education has expanded, though its quality and public perception remain mixed. Private education accounts for about 20% of students, with increased governmental regulation and party oversight. Higher education is the largest globally, featuring over 3,000 institutions and initiatives like the Double First-Class university plan aiming for world-class standards. Entrance to universities relies heavily on standardized exams, complemented by scholarships and expanding international study opportunities.

Adult education includes part-time, correspondence, and television universities, serving workers and those who missed formal schooling. Literacy campaigns and language reforms have aimed to unify and simplify education, though regional and socioeconomic disparities persist. English education begins around age 10 but faces criticism for rote-focused teaching. Government efforts in ICT, teacher training, and online education seek modernization, while issues such as academic censorship, educational inequality, and stress from competition remain challenges. Education policies increasingly emphasize ideological education, with reforms under Xi Jinping reinforcing party control and curriculum content consistent with socialist values.
