# Education in China: Summary

Education in China has evolved significantly since the founding of the People's Republic in 1949, with a focus initially on improving literacy and later on modernization aligned with economic development. The 1986 Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education guarantees six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education, although disparities remain between urban and rural areas in access and funding. Basic education includes preschool, compulsory schooling, senior high school (non-compulsory), and special education for disabled children. A dual emphasis on academic and vocational education exists, with vocational training expanding since the 1970s, but vocational graduates still face challenges in employment quality. “Key schools” historically concentrated resources for top students but contributed to educational inequality.

Higher education, the world's largest by enrollment, underwent reforms from the late 1970s to enhance autonomy, funding, and quality, emphasizing science and technology critical for modernization. Entrance examinations remain central, with ongoing reforms to diversify admissions. The government promotes lifelong learning and adult education via various part-time and distance education modalities. Recent policies under Xi Jinping have implemented tighter ideological education, limited private tutoring (to reduce educational inequality and student burden), and strengthened Communist Party control over private schools. China also hosts a growing number of international schools and foreign students, and invests in building world-class universities through initiatives like the Double First-Class University Plan. Despite progress, critiques persist regarding rote learning emphasis, regional disparities, and pressures linked to highly competitive exams.
