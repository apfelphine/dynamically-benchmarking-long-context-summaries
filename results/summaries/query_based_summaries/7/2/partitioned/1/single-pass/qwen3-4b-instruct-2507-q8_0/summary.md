**Education in China** is characterized by a strong emphasis on compulsory education, with a nine-year basic education system (six years of primary and three years of junior high) implemented nationwide. The system has seen significant expansion, with primary and secondary enrollment rates rising sharply since the 1980s, and a focus on modernization, science, and technical skills. Urban and key schools operate on a rigorous schedule, while rural schools adapt to agricultural cycles and offer a more limited curriculum. 

Vocational and technical education has been prioritized to meet economic demands, with a growing number of vocational schools and training programs. The Gaokao (National College Entrance Examination) remains the central mechanism for university admission, though reforms have introduced alternative pathways, including independent enrollment and private school admissions. 

Higher education has grown to become the largest in the world, with over 3,000 institutions and more than 44 million students. The government promotes elite institutions through the "Double First-Class Construction" initiative and emphasizes ideological education, including Marxism and national identity. 

Adult and lifelong learning is supported through distance education, correspondence courses, and open universities. However, challenges persist, including regional disparities, rural-urban divides, gender inequality, and the intense pressure on students due to exam-based systems. The government continues to address these issues through policy reforms, increased funding, and efforts to improve teacher quality and access to education.
