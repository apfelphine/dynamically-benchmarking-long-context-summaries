## Education in China

### Primary and Compulsory Education
China mandates nine years of compulsory education, which combines six years of primary education and three years of junior high school education. Within primary schools, urban settings generally adhere to a six-day full-time schedule, while rural schools operate on more flexible schedules accommodating agricultural seasons. The curriculum in primary schools includes Chinese, mathematics, physical education, music, drawing, and instruction in nature, history, and geography, alongside moral training that emphasizes patriotic values. The government has made strides in increasing enrollment, with primary school enrollment reaching about 96% in 1985, and literacy rates have fallen significantly.

### Secondary and Higher Education
The secondary education system includes junior high school (ages 12–15) and senior high school (grades 10–12). Senior secondary education offers academic or vocational tracks. The government has strongly emphasized vocational and technical education to meet modernization needs, though this sector faces competition for funding with general education. Higher education is the largest in the world, with over 3,000 colleges and universities. Historically, the system has shifted between ideological priorities and practical economic modernization goals. Modern reforms have introduced greater diversification and openness, but intense competition, particularly for university admission (via the Gaokao), remains a defining feature, alongside growing concerns over regional inequality.
