## Education in China – Summary

Education in China is structured as a nine-year compulsory system, comprising six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education, with ambitious goals for universal literacy and modernization. The Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education (effective since 1986) guarantees tuition-free access for children, with differentiated implementation across regions based on local conditions. While urban schools typically offer comprehensive curricula on a full-time schedule, rural schools adapt to agricultural cycles and resource constraints, often resulting in shorter school years and simpler subjects. A significant focus exists on bridging educational quality gaps between urban and rural areas. Political and moral education, teaching love of country and Communist values, starts from kindergarten and permeates all stages, while English instruction and rigorous testing play major roles. Special education for gifted, disabled, or slow learners has grown but remains a lower government priority.

At the secondary level, China offers both academic and vocational tracks, with ongoing reforms to strengthen technical education for continued economic growth. Private education and international schools have expanded, providing options outside the traditional public system. Entrance to senior secondary and higher education is highly competitive, primarily determined by standardized exams like the Zhongkao and the Gaokao. Higher education has seen massive growth and reform, emphasizing scientific research, national prestige, and global rankings, though rural and less affluent students face significant barriers. Adult education, including part-time, distance, and online learning, is widely available, aiming for lifelong educational access. Despite impressive literacy gains and international accolades, China’s education system faces challenges: regional and gender disparities, stress on memorization over creativity, limited academic freedom, and persistent inequalities for migrants and minorities. Recent policies also feature strict regulation of private tutoring and emphasize ideological education under Xi Jinping, including Marxism and patriotic curriculum content.
