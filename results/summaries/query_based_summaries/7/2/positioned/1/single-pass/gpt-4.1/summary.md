## Education in China – Summary

Education in China is characterized by a strong centralized policy framework emphasizing mass literacy, compulsory primary and junior secondary education, and high-stakes examinations. The system consists of six years of primary (elementary), three years of junior secondary (middle school), and three years of senior secondary (high school) education, with nine years (primary and junior secondary) being compulsory. Educational reforms from the late 20th century onward shifted focus towards economic modernization, science, and technology, accompanied by increased government investment and decentralization of administration to local authorities. Enrollment rates are high, with 98.9% at the primary level and over 94% at junior high, but regional disparities persist, especially between urban and rural areas.

Secondary education includes a rigorous high school system geared towards the Gaokao, China’s university entrance examination, as well as a substantial vocational track designed to address workforce needs. Higher education in China is the largest in the world, with over 3,000 institutions and more than 44 million enrolled students. Prestigious universities, such as those in the C9 League, have gained international recognition, and the government’s Double First-Class initiative aims to create world-class institutions. Despite noteworthy achievements in international assessments (such as PISA), the system faces ongoing challenges, including educational inequalities, stress from standardized testing, regional and gender disparities, and a need for more creative, problem-solving skills. Supplemental private tutoring was widespread until recent policy reforms sought to reduce its prevalence. Adult and online education have expanded access. Political content permeates curricula, particularly in history and political studies, with textbook approval closely overseen by the Chinese Communist Party.
