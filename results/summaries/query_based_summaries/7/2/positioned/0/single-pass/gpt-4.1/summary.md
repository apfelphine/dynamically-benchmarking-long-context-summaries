## Education in China

China’s education system has undergone significant transformations since 1949, beginning with efforts to improve literacy rates through mass campaigns and expanding access to elementary and secondary education. The Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education, enacted in 1986, ensures tuition-free, compulsory schooling for 6 years of primary and 3 years of secondary education, though implementation varies according to local economic conditions, and rural-urban inequalities persist. The curriculum emphasizes Chinese, mathematics, political studies, and increasingly, English, alongside moral and ideological instruction reflecting government priorities. Vocational and technical education has expanded to meet modernization goals, while notable achievements in international science olympiads highlight academic success. Teacher training and qualification programs have also grown in response to the large student population.

In recent decades, China’s government has initiated major reforms to promote educational equity, support disadvantaged and minority groups, regulate private and supplemental education, and foster lifelong learning through adult and online programs. Higher education has experienced massive expansion, boasting over 3,000 colleges and universities and 44 million students as of 2021, along with internationally recognized elite universities. Policy emphasis includes integrating socialist values, limiting excessive homework and private tutoring (Double Reduction Policy), and investing heavily in educational infrastructure. Despite these gains, the system faces criticism for intense competition, memorization-focused pedagogy, censorship, and enduring regional and gender disparities in access and outcomes.
