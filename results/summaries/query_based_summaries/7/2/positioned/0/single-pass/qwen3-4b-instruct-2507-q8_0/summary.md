**Education in China**  

China's education system has evolved significantly since the founding of the People's Republic, focusing initially on mass literacy and universal access to basic education. By 1985, enrollment in primary and secondary schools had grown dramatically, with elementary school enrollment tripling and secondary school enrollment increasing by 8.5 times. A major policy shift in 1985 emphasized nine-year compulsory education, leading to a nationwide expansion of basic education and the establishment of the State Education Commission.  

The system has since prioritized economic modernization, with a strong focus on science and technology, vocational training, and producing skilled workers. This shift was driven by Deng Xiaoping’s strategy for economic development, which emphasized the importance of science and technology and led to the adoption of international learning and research.  

China has achieved high enrollment rates, with over 200 million students in primary and secondary education and a near-universal literacy rate. Chinese students consistently perform well in international assessments, especially in mathematics, science, and reading, with Shanghai students topping the OECD's PISA rankings. China ranks first in medal counts at major science Olympiads.  

The system includes a rigorous academic structure, with a strong emphasis on standardized testing, such as the Gaokao, which determines university admissions. The government has implemented reforms to reduce excessive academic pressure, including the "Double Reduction Policy" (2021), which limits homework and private tutoring.  

Despite achievements, challenges remain, including regional inequality, high student stress, and concerns about rote memorization. Rural students often face poorer educational resources and lower quality instruction. The system also faces criticism for academic censorship, with strict control over textbooks and curricula, and for the underrepresentation of students with disabilities and minorities.  

Private education has grown rapidly, with over 56 million students in private schools by 2021, and the government has begun nationalizing some for-profit institutions.  

Higher education is the largest in the world, with over 3,000 institutions and more than 44 million students. The "Double First-Class Construction" initiative aims to elevate elite universities to world-class standards.  

Overall, China’s education system is highly centralized, state-driven, and focused on producing a large, skilled workforce to support economic growth, though it continues to face criticism regarding equity, creativity, and student well-being.
