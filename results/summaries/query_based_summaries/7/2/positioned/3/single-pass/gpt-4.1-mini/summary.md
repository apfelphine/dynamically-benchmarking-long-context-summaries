# Education in China

Education in China has evolved significantly since 1949, shifting from improving basic literacy to supporting economic modernization. The government established a nine-year compulsory education law in 1986, combining six years of primary and three years of junior secondary education. Despite funding and resource disparities, especially in rural areas, enrollment rates in primary and junior high schools have reached high levels. Senior secondary education remains non-compulsory, with students choosing academic or vocational paths, and admissions often relying on competitive entrance exams like Zhongkao for high school and Gaokao for university.

China's higher education system is the largest globally, with over 3,000 institutions and tens of millions enrolled. Efforts such as the "Double First-Class" initiative aim to develop world-class universities. Adult education, open and distance learning, and online education have been expanded as alternatives for broader access. The government tightly controls curricula and textbooks, emphasizing ideological education aligned with the Communist Party’s principles. Private education has grown but is subject to recent nationalization and increased party oversight. Challenges include regional disparities, intense competition, emphasis on rote learning, gender inequality, and academic censorship. International education programs and foreign universities have increased presence, while supplemental tutoring, once widespread, has been curtailed by the government to reduce educational inequities.
