### Education in China

The focus of education in the early years of the People's Republic of China was improving population-wide literacy, which saw elementary school enrollment triple and secondary school enrollment increase by a factor of 8.5 within the first sixteen years of communist governance. Since the end of the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976), the system has been geared towards economic modernization, with vocational and technical skills becoming paramount for meeting national modernization goals.

China mandates nine years of compulsory education (six years of primary education and three years of junior high school education), established by the Law on Nine-Year Compulsory Education. While primary education was intended to be tuition-free, financial difficulties can still cause students to drop out. The system includes various levels, from pre-school to senior high school. Academically, while some provincial districts (like Shanghai and Beijing) show high performance in international assessments like PISA, there are noted regional inequalities, and the system is heavily controlled by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), which regulates curricula and textbooks to align with party directives.
