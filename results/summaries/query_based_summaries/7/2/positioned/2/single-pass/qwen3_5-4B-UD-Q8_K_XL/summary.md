**Education in China** is a state-managed system prioritizing economic modernization and political stability, characterized by a nine-year compulsory education framework and a highly competitive university entrance examination known as the *Gaokao*. The system is divided into elementary, junior high, and senior high school stages, with a strong emphasis on rote memorization and standardized testing, though recent reforms aim to reduce homework burdens and curb excessive off-campus tutoring under the "Double Reduction" policy. Higher education is vast, with the government focusing on elite institutions through the "Double First-Class" initiative to produce scientific talent, while regional disparities between urban and rural areas remain significant. The curriculum is strictly controlled by the Chinese Communist Party, integrating ideological education and ensuring alignment with national policies.

**Education in India** operates under a concurrent list framework where both central and state governments share responsibility, leading to diverse policies and a large private school sector that complements government institutions. The traditional structure follows a 10+2 pattern, but the 2020 National Education Policy (NEP) proposes a 5+3+3+4 model to foster holistic development, foundational literacy, and vocational training. Access to education is legally mandated for children aged six to fourteen, yet challenges persist regarding teacher absenteeism, infrastructure deficits, and gender inequality, particularly in rural areas. The system is heavily influenced by a culture of competitive entrance examinations for prestigious engineering and medical colleges, driving the growth of private coaching institutes and creating immense academic pressure.

**Education in Germany** features a decentralized system where states (*Länder*) hold constitutional authority, resulting in varied school structures and curricula. It is known for its tripartite secondary education system, which tracks students early into *Gymnasium* (academic), *Realschule* (vocational-academic), or *Hauptschule* (vocational), alongside comprehensive schools. A cornerstone of the system is the *Duale Ausbildung* (dual education system), which combines classroom learning with practical apprenticeships in companies, providing a highly skilled workforce. While Germany excels in research and international rankings, it faces ongoing debates regarding early student tracking, social inequality, and the need to balance academic rigor with student well-being.

**Education in Greece** is structured around a nine-year compulsory education mandate, followed by non-compulsory lower and upper secondary levels (Gymnasium and Lyceum). The system emphasizes a centralized examination process, with the *Pan-Hellenic Examinations* determining university admission based on a weighted average of grades and exam scores. Vocational education is integrated through the EPAL system, offering dual diplomas combining academic and technical training. Recent reforms have aimed to improve accessibility and quality, particularly through initiatives like Public Onassis Schools, though the system continues to face challenges related to corruption, bullying, and the enforcement of free tuition policies in public universities.
