# Education in China

Education in China has undergone extensive development since the founding of the People's Republic in 1949, focusing initially on improving literacy through formal schooling and campaigns. The education system currently comprises nine years of compulsory education—six years of primary followed by three years of junior secondary education—after which students may attend senior secondary school or vocational schools. The government has prioritized expanding access, improving quality, and aligning education with economic modernization goals, emphasizing science, technology, vocational, and technical education.

China boasts a vast number of educational institutions, including over 3,000 colleges and universities serving millions of students, with prestigious universities part of initiatives like the Double First-Class Construction aimed at developing world-class institutions. Despite progress, challenges remain, including regional inequalities, especially between rural and urban areas, disparities in resources and opportunities for minorities and disabled students, and concerns about the heavy emphasis on rote memorization and standardized testing restricting creativity. Recent reforms under Xi Jinping’s leadership emphasize socialist values in curricula, limit excessive homework and private tutoring, and involve tight control over educational content. Private and international education are growing but regulated to align with party directives. Online education and adult education have expanded access, particularly in underserved areas, supported by ICT development. China ranks highly in global assessments and university rankings, reflecting both the scale and the quality of its educational system.
