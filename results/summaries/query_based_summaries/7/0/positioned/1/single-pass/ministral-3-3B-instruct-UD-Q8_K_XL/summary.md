**Education in Greece**

Greece’s education system is structured into **pre-primary, primary, lower secondary (Gymnasium)**, and **upper secondary (Lyceum)** stages, with a focus on compulsory education up to age 15. Preschool (2 years) is mandatory, starting at age 4, and includes pre-kindergarten and kindergarten, with no formal grading until Year 3. Primary education (6 years) is compulsory, transitioning students to Gymnasium (lower secondary) at age 12. Gymnasium lasts 3 years, leading to the **"Απολυτήριο Γυμνασίου"** (Gymnasium leaving certificate), which grants admission to Lyceum (upper secondary).

Upper secondary education (3 years) is non-compulsory, culminating in the **"Απολυτήριο Λυκείου"** (Lyceum leaving certificate), required for higher education. Students choose from specialized streams (e.g., Humanities, Sciences, Vocational) and must pass the **Pan-Hellenic Examinations** for university admission. Vocational pathways, such as **Vocational Lyceum (ΕΠΑΛ)**, offer specialized diplomas alongside general education. The system emphasizes a **20-point grading scale**, with English mandatory in all stages. Specialized schools (e.g., Music, Art Lyceums) cater to specific interests, while **Public Onassis Schools** provide free education in disadvantaged areas. The **Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF)** classifies education levels (1–8), aligning with European standards.
