**Education in Greece**  
Greece follows a structured, multi-stage education system with compulsory education from age 6 to 15. The system includes **preschool** (ages 4–6), **primary school** (ages 6–12), and **secondary education** (ages 12–18), divided into lower and upper secondary levels.  

- **Preschool** is compulsory and lasts two years: Pre-kindergarten (age 4–5) and Kindergarten (age 5–6). Students begin at age 4, with registration required by May. English is introduced in Kindergarten.  
- **Primary education** (6 years, ages 6–12) is compulsory and includes grades 1–6. Students are awarded the *Apolytirio Dimotikou* (primary school leaving certificate), which grants automatic admission to the lower secondary level.  
- **Lower secondary education** (ages 12–15) is compulsory and includes three grades (1st–3rd Gymnasium). Students receive the *Apolytirio Gymnasium* (lower secondary leaving certificate).  
- **Upper secondary education** (ages 15–18) is non-compulsory and includes three years of Lyceum (General, Art, Music, or Vocational), with the *Apolytirio Lykeiou* (high school diploma) required for university admission.  

The system features a **National Qualification Framework (HQF)** that aligns qualifications across formal and non-formal education, with levels from 0 to 8. Vocational education is available through **EPAL** (Vocational Lyceum), **EPAS** (Apprenticeship), and **SAEK** (Advanced Vocational Training), offering diplomas at HQF levels 3–5.  

Higher education includes **public universities** (tuition-free) and **private institutions** (with accreditation from HAHE). The **Pan-Hellenic Examinations** are required for university admission.  

The system emphasizes a balanced curriculum, including mandatory English and core subjects, with grading on a 1–20 scale. There is a strong focus on **digital education**, **extracurricular activities**, and **student participation** through student councils and school-based programs.  

Notable features include **public Onassis Schools** (for disadvantaged students), **dual education pathways**, and the **International Baccalaureate (IB)** program, set to launch in 2026–2027.  

Challenges include **corruption**, **student bullying**, and **inequality in access**, particularly in rural areas. The system is highly structured and centralized, with clear progression paths and standardized assessments.
