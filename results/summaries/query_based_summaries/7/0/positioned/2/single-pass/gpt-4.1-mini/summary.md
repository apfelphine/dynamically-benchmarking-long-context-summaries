# Education in Greece

Education in Greece is structured into distinct stages beginning with compulsory preschool (ages 4–6), followed by primary school (Dimotiko) lasting six years (ages 6–12). Primary education culminates in the "Apolytirio Dimotikou" certificate granting automatic admission to lower secondary education (Gymnasium). Gymnasium is compulsory and spans three years (ages 12–15), after which students obtain the "Apolytirio Gymnasium" certificate enabling entry to upper secondary education (Lyceum). Lyceum lasts three years (ages 15–18) and is non-compulsory, ending with national Pan-Hellenic Examinations that are prerequisites for higher education admission.

The Greek secondary system includes various specialized institutions such as Model, Music, Art, Ecclesiastical, Vocational Lyceums (EPAL), and Experimental schools. Vocational education pathways are offered at upper secondary and post-secondary levels with diplomas aligned to the Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF). Post-secondary vocational training includes Schools of Advanced Vocational Training (SAEK) and apprenticeship classes, providing graduates with recognized qualifications that may allow progression to higher education.

Higher education consists of public and private Higher Educational Institutes (HEIs) including universities and specialist academies. Public HEIs are mostly autonomous, tuition-free, and provide some English-taught programs; private HEIs must obtain accreditation and government licensing. The National Qualification Framework (HQF) in Greece aligns qualifications from primary through tertiary education into eight levels connected to European frameworks. Non-formal education and lifelong learning opportunities are also actively promoted through online platforms, adult education centers, and specialized training institutions.
