## Education in Greece

**Educational Structure and Levels:**
The Greek education system is structured across several compulsory and non-compulsory stages. Preschool education is compulsory for two years, comprising Pre-kindergarten (ages 4–5) and Kindergarten (ages 5–6). Primary education, known as *Dimotiko*, is compulsory for six years, spanning ages 6 to 12, and culminates in a primary school leaving certificate. Lower secondary education is compulsory for three years in the *Gymnasium* (ages 12–15). Upper secondary education is non-compulsory and lasts three years in the *Lyceum* (ages 15–18).

**Curriculum and Higher Education:**
Students begin learning English as part of their core curriculum in Kindergarten. Primary school students are not officially graded in the first two years, while subsequent years use alphabetical rankings and then numerical grades (1 to 10). The *Gymnasium* curriculum mandates English as a core subject for all three years, with students choosing between French or German as a second foreign language. For higher education, the public *HEIs* are mostly autonomous and are funded by the government, with many public universities offering programs without tuition fees. Admission to these HEIs requires secondary school graduation and passing the national standardized "Pan-Hellenic Examinations."

**Specialized and Vocational Education:**
The system includes various specialized and vocational options, such as Vocational Lyceums (*EPAL*) and the *School of Advanced Vocational Training* (*SAEK*). Vocational programs often incorporate dual-diploma studies, combining classroom theory with workplace training. Non-formal education options are available through platforms like GUnet, which provides free online courses.

**Challenges and Controversies:**
The Greek education sector faces issues concerning corruption in public schools and reports of student abuse, including physical and verbal violations. Historically, corporal punishment was widely used in public schools until it was banned.
