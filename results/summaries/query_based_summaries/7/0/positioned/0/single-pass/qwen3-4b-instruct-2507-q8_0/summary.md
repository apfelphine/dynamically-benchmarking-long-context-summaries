**Education in Greece**  
Greece's education system is structured into preschool, primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Preschool (ages 4–6) is compulsory and includes Pre-Kindergarten and Kindergarten, with a two-year program. Primary education (ages 6–12) lasts six years and is compulsory, with no grades in the first two years and a grading system from A to D starting in grades 3–6. Secondary education is divided into lower and upper levels: the Gymnasium (ages 12–15) is compulsory and leads to the "Apolytirio Gymnasium" qualification, while the Lyceum (ages 15–18) is non-compulsory and leads to the "Apolytirio Lykeiou" qualification, required for higher education.  

Grading systems vary by level, with primary schools using descriptive assessments, secondary schools using a 1–10 scale (later extended to 1–20), and the Lyceum using a 20-point system. The system includes specialized tracks such as vocational schools (EPAL), music, art, and sports, with dual diplomas and apprenticeship programs. Students must pass the Pan-Hellenic Examinations (national standardized tests) for university admission, with scores weighted by subject and grade point averages.  

The system is supported by the Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF), which standardizes qualifications across levels. Notable features include the presence of public Onassis Schools, which offer enhanced curricula in STEM and social sciences, and the upcoming introduction of the International Baccalaureate (IB) in 2026–2027. Criticisms include widespread corruption, bullying, and physical punishment, with ongoing reforms aimed at improving quality and equity.
