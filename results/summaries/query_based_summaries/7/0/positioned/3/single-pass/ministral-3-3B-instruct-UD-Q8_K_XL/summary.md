Greece’s education system is structured into **formal education stages**, beginning with **preschool (pre-kindergarten and kindergarten)**, which is compulsory for two years (ages 4–6). This is followed by **primary school (Dimotiko)**, a six-year compulsory program (ages 6–12), culminating in a primary school leaving certificate.

After primary school, students enter **lower secondary education (Gymnasium)**, a three-year compulsory program (ages 12–15), leading to the gymnasium certificate. Following this, students can pursue **upper secondary education (Lyceum)**, a three-year non-compulsory program (ages 15–18), resulting in a high school diploma. The system includes specialized tracks such as **vocational lyceums (EPAL)**, offering dual diplomas in both general and vocational education, and **model and experimental schools** with unique curricula.

Greece’s **National Qualification Framework (HQF)** classifies education levels from 1 (primary school) to 8 (doctoral studies), aligning with European standards. The education system emphasizes **compulsory education**, with significant attention to **vocational training** and **specialized education** for diverse student needs. While the system is structured to ensure access to education, it faces challenges related to **corruption, bullying, and disparities in resources** across different educational levels.
