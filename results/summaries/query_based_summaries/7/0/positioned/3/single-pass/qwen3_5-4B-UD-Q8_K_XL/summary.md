Education in Greece is a unified system where primary education is compulsory for six years, leading automatically to lower secondary education known as the *gymnasium* (grades 7–9). The *gymnasium* is also compulsory for three years and concludes with a qualification that grants automatic admission to upper secondary education, the *lyceum* (grades 10–12), which is non-compulsory. Admission to the *lyceum* is competitive and requires passing the Pan-Hellenic Examinations (university matriculation exams), which are weighted averages of secondary school grades and standardized national tests. The *lyceum* is further divided into general and vocational tracks, with vocational high schools (*epagelmatiko lykeio*) offering specialized orientation groups leading to dual diplomas combining academic and vocational qualifications.

Higher education in Greece is provided by public and private Higher Educational Institutes (HEIs), primarily universities and specialized academies. Public universities generally offer free tuition for undergraduate studies, with limited exceptions for postgraduate programs, and provide free meals for most students. The system follows a three-cycle structure: first-cycle (bachelor's degree, Level 6), second-cycle (master's degree, Level 7), and third-cycle (doctoral degree, Level 8). Vocational training is integrated throughout the system, ranging from apprenticeship programs (*mathitia*) and post-lyceum apprenticeships to advanced vocational schools (*sae*), designed to provide practical skills alongside academic qualifications. The National Qualification Framework (HQF) aligns Greek qualifications with European standards, facilitating mobility and recognition across the EU.
