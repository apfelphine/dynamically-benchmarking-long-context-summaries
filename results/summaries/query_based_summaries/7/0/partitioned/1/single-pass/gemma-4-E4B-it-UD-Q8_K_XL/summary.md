**Structure and Levels of Education in Greece:**
Greek education follows a tiered structure. Preschool, which is compulsory and lasts two years (one year of Pre-kindergarten and one year of Kindergarten), prepares children for primary school. Primary education (Dimotiko scholeio) is compulsory for six years, with grades 1 through 6. Lower Secondary Education is compulsory in the Gymnasium, lasting three years (ages 12-15), and is followed by non-compulsory Upper Secondary Education (Lyceum) for three years (ages 15-18).

**Curriculum and Languages:**
Students begin learning English as part of the core curriculum in kindergarten. In primary school, grades 1 and 2 do not receive official grades; grades 3 and 4 are ranked alphabetically, while from grade 5 to 6, numerical grades (4 being the lowest, 10 the highest) are used. In lower secondary education, English is mandatory for all three years, and students can choose French or German as a second foreign language.

**Secondary and Vocational Education:**
The system includes various types of upper secondary schools, such as the General Lyceum (ΓΕΛ), Vocational Lyceum (EPAL), and Special Lyceum. The Vocational Lyceum (EPAL) utilizes a dual-diploma system, combining a general High School Diploma with a Diploma of Vocational Education and Training at HQF Level 4. Post-lyceum students can pursue a one-year apprenticeship class to upgrade their qualification to Level 5.

**Higher Education:**
Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) are primarily public. While most public HEIs offer free education, about 25% of postgraduate programs are tuition-free. Private Higher Education Institutions must be accredited by the Hellenic Authority for Higher Education (HAHE) and obtain an operating license from the Greek Ministry of Education.

**Other Educational Provisions:**
Non-formal education includes free online courses offered by the Greek Academic Network (GUnet) and the Citizens' Digital Academy, as well as specialized programs like the Open Courses available in public HEIs.

**Controversies:**
Concerns have been raised regarding corruption within the Greek public sector education system, instances of student bullying, and the controversial historical practice of corporal punishment in schools.
