# Summary of Education in Greece

Education in Greece comprises a structured, multi-level system starting from preschool (compulsory 2 years: Pre-kindergarten and Kindergarten) through primary school (6 compulsory years), lower secondary school (Gymnasium, compulsory 3 years for ages 12–15), and upper secondary school (Lyceum, 3 non-compulsory years for ages 15–18). Primary education concludes with the "Apolytirio Dimotikou" certificate, which grants automatic admission to Gymnasium. Upon completing Gymnasium, students receive the "Apolytirio Gymnasium" certificate that allows entry into Lyceum. Upper secondary education culminates in the "Apolytirio Lykeiou" certificate, enabling higher education admission via nationally regulated Pan-Hellenic Examinations.

The Greek secondary education includes various types of schools: general, vocational (EPAL), special, music, art, model, experimental, and ecclesiastical schools. The Vocational High School (EPAL) offers both general and specialized education streams with qualifications aligned to European and national standards, often including options for apprenticeships and dual vocational training. Grading generally uses a 20-point scale, and foreign languages such as English are mandatory from early education stages.

Higher education in Greece is provided mostly by public autonomous Higher Educational Institutions (universities and specialized academies) with recent legislation allowing the establishment of accredited private institutions. Public higher education is tuition-free or is low cost for most students, with some postgraduate programs partly tuition-waived. English-taught programs are available at select universities. Non-formal education initiatives include online open courses, lifelong learning centers, and specialized training programs.

The National Qualifications Framework (HQF) in Greece aligns education levels from primary school certificates (Level 1) to doctoral degrees (Level 8), covering formal and recognized vocational qualifications. The Greek education system includes various initiatives to support digital literacy, active citizenship, and lifelong learning.

Challenges include corruption in the public education sector, historic use of corporal punishment (banned in early 2000s), and occasional incidents of bullying and abuse reported in schools. Despite these issues, Greece maintains a comprehensive, regulated education system encompassing formal schooling, vocational training, higher education, and non-formal learning opportunities.
