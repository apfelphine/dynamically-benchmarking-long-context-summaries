**Education in Greece** features a structured, multi-tiered system with compulsory education from age 4 to 18. The system includes:

- **Preschool (Kindergarten)**: Mandatory for children aged 4–6, lasting two years (Pre-Kindergarten and Kindergarten), with English introduced as a core subject.
- **Primary Education (Dimotiko)**: Compulsory for 6 years (ages 6–12), with no grades until Year 3–4, followed by a grading system from A to D and then numerical scores (1–10).
- **Secondary Education**:
  - **Lower Secondary (Gymnasium)**: Compulsory until age 15, leading to the *Apolytirio Gymnasium* (equivalent to a high school diploma).
  - **Upper Secondary (Lyceum)**: Non-compulsory, lasting 3 years (ages 15–18), leading to the *Apolytirio Lykeiou* (high school diploma), with a 20-point grading scale.
  - **Vocational High Schools (EPAL)**: Offers dual diplomas, combining a *Vocational High School Diploma* and a *Diploma of Vocational Education and Training (Level 4)*, with pathways to higher education through a Post-High School Year Apprenticeship Class (Level 5).
- **Vocational Training**: Includes specialized programs such as EPAS (Apprenticeship), SAEK (Advanced Vocational Training), and other vocational schools, providing practical training and leading to recognized qualifications.
- **Higher Education**: Public universities and institutions offer degrees (Bachelor's, Master's, Doctorate) under a national qualification framework (HQF), with a strong emphasis on research and academic freedom.
- **National Framework**: The Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF) aligns Greek qualifications with European standards, ensuring comparability across education levels.
- **Controversies**: The system faces criticism for corruption, bullying, and the historical use of corporal punishment, which was banned in 1998 (primary) and 2005 (secondary).

The system emphasizes a balance between academic and vocational pathways, with a strong focus on practical skills and national integration, while ongoing challenges include administrative corruption and social equity.
