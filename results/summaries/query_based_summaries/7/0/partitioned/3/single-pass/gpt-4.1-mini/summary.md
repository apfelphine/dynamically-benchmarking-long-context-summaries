## Education in Greece

Education in Greece is structured across preschool, primary, secondary (lower and upper), vocational, and higher education stages. Preschool education is compulsory for two years, consisting of Pre-kindergarten (age 4–5) and Kindergarten (age 5–6), often attached to primary schools. Primary education lasts six years (ages 6–12), culminating in the Απολυτήριο Δημοτικού (Primary School Leaving Certificate), which grants automatic admission to lower secondary education (Gymnasium).

Lower secondary education (Gymnasium) is compulsory until age 15 and lasts three years. It offers various types such as Special, Evening, Ecclesiastical, Model, and Music Gymnasiums. Completion awards the Απολυτήριο Γυμνασίου (Lower Secondary School Leaving Certificate), providing access to upper secondary education (Lyceum). Upper secondary education (Lyceum), which is non-compulsory, lasts three years (ages 15–18) and includes several types: General, Vocational (EPAL), Music, Art, Model, Experimental, and Ecclesiastical Lyceums. Graduates receive the Απολυτήριο Λυκείου (High School Diploma), which is necessary for higher education admission via national Pan-Hellenic Examinations.

Vocational education is well developed, with several school forms such as Vocational High Schools (EPAL) offering dual diplomas (general high school diploma and vocational specialization diploma). EPAL students may also pursue a Post-lyceum Year Apprenticeship Class for additional vocational qualifications, potentially leading to higher education. Other vocational institutions include Apprenticeship Vocational Schools (EPAS) managed by the Public Employment Service, Experimental Vocational Schools (PEPAS), Vocational Training Schools (ESK), Laboratory Centres (ΕΚ), and Schools of Advanced Vocational Training (SAEK), which replaced the Institute of Vocational Training (IEK). These institutions provide structured education with certifications aligned with the National Qualification Framework (HQF), facilitating formal recognition and progression routes.

Higher education in Greece comprises public and newly authorized private Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). Public HEIs are mostly autonomous and tuition-free, with some postgraduate programs charging fees. They offer programs typically lasting four years (undergraduate), with additional postgraduate and doctoral studies. Several universities also provide English-taught programs. Private colleges mostly operate non-formal foreign degree programs under validation agreements with foreign institutions.

Non-formal education includes various online and lifelong learning initiatives by public authorities and organizations, offering free courses and training through MOOCs and specialized frameworks.

Overall, the Greek education system integrates formal compulsory education, diverse vocational tracks, and tertiary education under a regulated national framework aligning with European standards, supporting academic progression and vocational qualifications.
