Education in Greece is structured around a National Qualification Framework (HQF) with 8 levels, aligned with the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). Compulsory education lasts nine years, consisting of six years of Primary School (Dimotiko) and three years of Lower Secondary School (Gymnasium). Primary education is automatic in progression, while Gymnasium offers various types, including special, evening, and experimental schools, culminating in the "Apolytirio Gymnasium" certificate. Upper Secondary Education (Lyceum) is non-compulsory and lasts three years, leading to the "Apolytirio Lykeiou" diploma. Admission to Higher Education Institutes (HEIs) requires this certificate and passing the Pan-Hellenic Examinations, a standardized national test. The system also includes vocational pathways such as Vocational Lyceums (EPAL), which offer dual-track programs combining classroom instruction with workplace apprenticeships, and Apprenticeship Vocational Schools (EPAS) operated by the Public Employment Service (DYPA).

Higher education is primarily provided by public HEIs, which are free of tuition fees for most students, though postgraduate programs may incur costs. Private Higher Education Institutions are permitted under recent legislation but must be accredited by the National Authority for Higher Education (HAHE). The curriculum emphasizes modern Greek and English, with foreign languages like French or German as second choices. Vocational training is integrated throughout the system, from specialized schools like the School of Advanced Vocational Training (SAEK) to post-lyceum apprenticeships that allow graduates to access undergraduate programs. Non-formal education is supported through digital platforms and open courses, while issues such as corruption, corporal punishment bans, and student bullying have been subjects of national debate and reform efforts.
