# Summary: Education in Greece

Education in Greece is structured across multiple levels, beginning with compulsory preschool (2 years: Pre-kindergarten for ages 4–5 and Kindergarten for ages 5–6), followed by primary education (Dimotiko) lasting 6 years for ages 6–12. Primary graduates automatically advance to compulsory lower secondary education (Gymnasium), a 3-year program for ages 12–15 culminating in the "Apolytirio Gymnasium" certificate (HQF level 3). Upper secondary education (Lyceum), non-compulsory and lasting 3 years (ages 15–18), awards the "Apolytirio Lykeiou" diploma (HQF level 4), necessary for higher education entry. The grading scale in secondary education operates on a 1–20 system, with 20 equivalent to an A or 100%.

Vocational education features prominently, especially through Vocational High Schools (EPAL), which offer dual qualifications: a High School Diploma (Apolytirio Epagelmatikou Lykeiou) and a Vocational Education and Training Diploma (Ptychio) at HQF level 4. EPAL students specialize in one of nine Orientation Groups and subsequent specific specializations in grade 3, with options to upgrade qualifications through Post-High School Year Apprenticeship classes (Level 5) combining classroom and workplace training. Additionally, other vocational schools include the Vocational School (EPAS) operated by the Public Employment Service offering 2-year apprenticeship programs (HQF level 3), Advanced Vocational Training Schools (SAEK) for adult post-secondary education (HQF level 5), and public Schools of Tourism Education. The Greek Qualification Framework (HQF) aligns with European frameworks, offering recognized certifications from primary to tertiary education levels. Access to higher education requires successful completion of the Pan-Hellenic Examinations based on the Lyceum studies.
