## Education in Greece

The Greek education system is structured into primary, lower secondary (gymnasium), and upper secondary (lyceum) stages, with compulsory education lasting nine years. Primary education covers six years (ages 6–12), where grading begins in the third year using letters (A–D) before shifting to a 1–10 numerical scale. Lower secondary education, known as the gymnasium, is compulsory for three years (ages 12–15) and provides automatic admission to upper secondary schools. Upper secondary education, or the lyceum, is a non-compulsory three-year program (ages 15–18) culminating in the "Apolytirio Lykeiou" (high school diploma), which serves as a prerequisite for university admission. The lyceum requires students to select from three elective "Orientation Groups" (Humanities, Science and Health, or Economic and Computer Studies) starting in the second year, with final admission to higher education determined by performance in the Pan-Hellenic Examinations.

Beyond general education, Greece offers diverse vocational pathways, most notably the Vocational Lyceum (EPAL). The EPAL program provides a dual-diploma qualification consisting of a high school diploma and a specialized vocational diploma (Level 4), allowing students to focus on specific career tracks such as engineering, health, arts, or business administration. Graduates may further enhance their credentials through the Post-lyceum Year Apprenticeship Class, a dual-track system combining classroom instruction with workplace training to earn a Level 5 diploma, which facilitates direct entry into undergraduate higher education programs. Vocational training also includes Apprenticeship Vocational Schools (EPAS) operated by the Public Employment Service (DYPA), featuring a dual learning model with alternating periods of theoretical study and paid work experience, typically catering to students aged 15 to 23.

The tertiary education sector comprises public and private Higher Education Institutes (HEIs), including universities and universities of applied sciences (Fachhochschulen). Public universities are largely tuition-free for domestic students, charging only a nominal semester fee, while private institutions operate under a licensing framework established in 2024. The system is regulated by the Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF), which aligns qualifications with European standards, ensuring a cohesive structure from primary education through to doctoral degrees. Recent reforms have also introduced new educational initiatives, such as the "Active Citizen Actions" program and plans to launch the International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme in specific public high schools.
