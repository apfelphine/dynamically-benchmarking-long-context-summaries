**Education in Greece** includes a structured system from preschool through higher education.  

**Preschool** (ages 4–6) is compulsory and split into Pre-Kindergarten (Προνηπιαγωγείο) and Kindergarten (Νηπιαγωγείο), with English introduced in kindergarten.  

**Primary education** (ages 6–12) lasts 6 years and is compulsory. Students receive an "Apolytirio Dimotikou" (primary school leaving certificate) upon completion, granting automatic admission to lower secondary education.  

**Lower secondary education** (Gymnasium, ages 12–15) is compulsory and includes three academic years. Students earn an "Apolytirio Gymnasium" certificate (HQF level 3), which grants entry to upper secondary education.  

**Upper secondary education** (Lyceum, ages 15–18) is non-compulsory and lasts 3 years. Students take the "Pan-Hellenic Examinations" and earn an "Apolytirio Lykeiou" (high school diploma, HQF level 4). This certificate is required for admission to higher education.  

**Vocational High Schools (EPAL)** offer dual diplomas: a high school diploma (HQF level 4) and a Vocational Education and Training Diploma (HQF level 4). Students can pursue a Post-High School Year Apprenticeship Class, upgrading their qualification to HQF level 5, which allows entry into higher education.  

The education system is governed by the Hellenic Qualification Framework (HQF), linked to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF), with clear levels for formal and non-formal qualifications. A 20-point grading system is used in secondary schools, with 20 equivalent to an A.  

The curriculum emphasizes modern Greek, mathematics, sciences, foreign languages (English, French, or German), and physical education, with subject requirements varying by orientation group in the Lyceum.
