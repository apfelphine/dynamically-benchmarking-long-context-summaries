Education in Greece is structured into compulsory primary and secondary levels, followed by non-compulsory upper secondary education and tertiary institutions. Compulsory education consists of six years of primary school (grades 1–6, ages 6–12) and three years of lower secondary education known as the *Gymnasium* (grades 7–9, ages 12–15). Students then transition to upper secondary education, the *Lyceum* (grades 10–12, ages 15–18), which is non-compulsory and lasts three years. Admission to the Lyceum requires a Gymnasium certificate, and the Lyceum certificate is necessary for university entry. The system utilizes a 20-point grading scale in upper secondary schools, and students must pass the Pan-Hellenic Examinations (university matriculation exams) to access higher education.

The Greek educational framework includes various specialized types of schools, such as Vocational Lyceums (EPAL), Model Lyceums, and Evening schools for adult learners. Vocational education offers dual-track apprenticeship programs combining classroom theory with practical workplace training. Tertiary education is provided by public Higher Education Institutes (HEIs) and private colleges, with most public universities offering tuition-free attendance for undergraduate students. The National Qualification Framework (HQF) aligns Greek qualifications with international standards, and the system emphasizes a progression from primary through secondary levels to higher education, supported by mechanisms like the Post-High School Year Apprenticeship Class for advanced vocational training.
