# Studienstiftung

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is a German foundation dedicated to promoting academic excellence, initiative, and responsibility among students. Established in 1948, it provides scholarships for bachelor's, master's, and doctoral studies, offering both financial support (ranging from a €300 monthly allowance to need-based amounts up to €855 for undergraduates and €1,550 for PhDs) and academic resources such as summer schools, language courses, and mentoring. Admission is based on merit and social commitment, with candidates selected through nominations by schools, universities, or professors, as well as via a national assessment test. The foundation also supports international study and offers internal programs for scholars and alumni, including partnerships with institutions like KAUST and the Beethoven House.

As of March 2023, the Studienstiftung had 14,888 scholars across Germany, representing approximately 0.48% of the student population. While public universities like Heidelberg University (657 scholars) and Hannover Medical School (78 scholars) maintain smaller but significant proportions of their student bodies, private institutions such as Bucerius Law School and Hertie School show higher percentages (8.41% and 2.49%, respectively). The foundation has a distinguished alumni network that includes Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists, and prominent figures in politics, science, and arts. Notable recipients include Manfred Eigen (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1962), Reinhard Genzel (Nobel Prize in Physics, 2020), and Peter Scholze (Fields Medal, 2018).
