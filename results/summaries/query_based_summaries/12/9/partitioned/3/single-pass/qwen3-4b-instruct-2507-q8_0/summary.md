**Studienstiftung Overview**

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (German Students' Foundation) is a German organization providing scholarships for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students. Founded in 1925 and re-established in 1948, it offers financial and academic support to students studying full-time at German universities, EU institutions, or in Switzerland, without requiring German or EU citizenship or age restrictions.

**Scholarship Details**
- **Financial Support**: Includes a monthly allowance of €300, need-based scholarships (up to €855/month), and additional funds for students with children. PhD scholars receive €1,550/month (€1,650 from 2025) plus a €100 research allowance. Payments are tax-free under German income tax law. Visits abroad and tuition fees are partially covered.
- **Academic Support**: Offers summer schools, research groups, language courses, workshops, and mentoring by liaison professors. Scholars must submit regular study reports and can apply for funding to organize conferences or events.
- **Summer Academies**: Annual two-week subsidised summer academies are a central event format. Scholars can also organize shorter academies with support.

**Internal & Open Programmes**
- **Internal Scholarships**: Includes the KAUST-Studienstiftung programme (for STEM research internships or Master/PhD studies at KAUST, Saudi Arabia) and Beethoven House scholarships for young composers.
- **Open Programmes**: Collaborative initiatives such as the McCloy Academic Scholarship Program, ERP-Stipendienprogramm, and Carlo-Schmid-Programm, accessible without prior Studienstiftung scholarship.

**Statistics (2023)**
- Approved 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships.
- 71.5% received a €300 study allowance, 16.4% received partial need-based scholarships, and 11.1% received the maximum need-based scholarship.
- 55.4% of scholars in 2020 were women.

**Admission Process**
Scholarship holders are nominated through various channels:
1. **School nomination** (30 senior students per school)
2. **Professor nomination** (from universities)
3. **Examination board nomination** (1 in 50 second-year students)
4. **National/federal competition winners**
5. **Self-nomination via assessment test**
6. **Alumni nomination**
7. **Musical arts nomination** (for fine arts, music, and performing arts)

**Leadership**
- Notable past chairmen include Adolf Grimme (1948–1963), Karl Theodor Bleek (1963–1967), Rudolf Sieverts (1967–1980), Werner Maihofer (1980–1982), Manfred Eigen (1982–1993), Gerhard Roth (2003–2011), and Reinhard Zimmermann (2011–2023). Current chair is Michael Hoch (2023–).
