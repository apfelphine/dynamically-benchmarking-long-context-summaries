# Summary of Studienstiftung

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally founded in 1925 and reestablished in 1948, is a German scholarship foundation that supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates with both financial assistance and academic enrichment. Scholarships are offered across various study phases—including bachelor's, master's, and doctoral levels—without restrictions on citizenship or age, provided part of the study is conducted at a university within Germany, the EU, or Switzerland. Admission occurs via diverse channels such as school nominations, university professor endorsements, competition winners, assessment tests, and alumni nominations. Once admitted, scholars receive a monthly study allowance (around €300), with additional need-based support up to €855 for undergraduates and higher stipends (currently €1,550, rising to €1,650 plus €100 research allowance in 2025) for doctoral candidates, including provisions for students with children. Financial awards are tax-exempt under German law.

Beyond financial support, the Studienstiftung offers extensive academic programs including summer schools, research groups, mentoring by liaison professors, language courses, and workshops aimed at fostering intellectual excellence, initiative, and social responsibility. Scholars also have opportunities to organize conferences and partake in specialized gatherings, such as queer scholar meetings supported since 2014. The foundation runs distinct internal and open scholarship programs in partnership with institutions like the Saudi Arabian KAUST and the Beethoven House. As of 2023, the Studienstiftung had granted over 2,000 scholarships including doctoral awards, with more than half of the scholars being women, reflecting its commitment to fostering diverse academic leadership in Germany and beyond.
