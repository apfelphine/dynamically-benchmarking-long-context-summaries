## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes: Overview and Key Features

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is Germany’s largest and oldest scholarship foundation, initially founded in Dresden in 1925 and re-established in Cologne in 1948. It is dedicated to supporting exceptionally talented and committed students and doctoral candidates through a competitive, multi-channel admission process, including nominations by schools, university professors, examination boards, national or cooperation competitions, self-application via assessment test, alumni nomination, and special pathways for musicians and artists. Admission is based on principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility, with selection focusing on academic achievement, motivation, extracurricular activities, and social engagement. The foundation does not require German/EU citizenship or set age limits, and a portion of studies must be in Germany, the EU, or Switzerland.

## Scholarship and Funding Programs

Studienstiftung scholarships for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students consist of financial support (including a monthly allowance, needs-based scholarship, insurance subsidies, and additional child support) governed by German Education Ministry regulations, with payments tax-free. Academic support encompasses summer schools, research groups, language courses, workshops, mentoring, and opportunities for scholars to host conferences and events. Numerous internal and open scholarship programs also exist, including international partnerships (e.g., KAUST, McCloy, ERP, Carlo-Schmid, Max Weber). In 2023, Studienstiftung issued 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral awards; most recipients (71.5%) received a study expense allowance of €300 per month, and 55.4% were women. Notable alumni span Nobel, Leibniz, and Fields Medal laureates, public figures in politics, science, business, arts, and media.
