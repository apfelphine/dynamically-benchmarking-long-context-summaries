**Studienstiftung Overview**

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (German People's Scholarship Foundation) is a German scholarship organization providing financial and academic support to undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students. Established in 1925 and re-founded in 1948, it offers general scholarships based on the German state support system (BAföG), with eligibility based on academic merit, social commitment, and initiative. Students are nominated through various channels: high schools, universities, examination boards, national competitions, self-identification via an assessment test, or by alumni.

**Scholarship Support**
- **Financial Support**: Includes a monthly allowance (€300), subsidies for health and long-term care insurance, and need-based scholarships (up to €855/month) calculated according to BAföG. Additional funds are available for students with children. PhD scholars receive a monthly award of €1,550 (€1,650 from 2025) plus a research allowance of €100. Financial support is regulated by German Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF) guidelines. Visits abroad are supported via grants or overseas allowances, and tuition fees may be partially covered.
- **Academic Support**: Includes summer schools, research groups, language courses, workshops, and mentoring by liaison professors. Scholars must submit academic reports during their first semesters and annually thereafter. They may also apply for funding to organize conferences and events. The Studienstiftung hosts two-week subsidised summer academies, which are its central event format. Special programs include the KAUST-Studienstiftung (for STEM students) and musical scholarships via the Beethoven House.

**Internal and Open Programs**
- **Internal Programs**: Include the KAUST-Studienstiftung (for STEM students) and musical scholarships for young composers.
- **Open Programs**: Collaborative programs with external organizations such as the McCloy Academic Scholarship Program, ERP-Stipendienprogramm, and the Carlo-Schmid-Programm. Participation does not require a prior regular scholarship.

**Statistics and Impact**
- As of 2023, the Studienstiftung approved 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships.
- In 2023, 71.5% of scholars received a €300 allowance, 16.4% received a partial need-based scholarship, and 11.1% received the maximum need-based scholarship.
- In 2020, 55.4% of scholars were women.
- Scholar participation varies by university, with notable shares at institutions like Heidelberg (2.25%), Freiburg (1.44%), and Bucerius Law School (8.41%).

**Notable Alumni**
- In natural sciences: Manfred Eigen (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1962), Gerd Faltings (Fields Medal, 1986), Reinhard Genzel (Nobel Prize in Physics, 2020), and Peter Scholze (Fields Medal, 2018).
- In social sciences: Ulrich Beck, Michèle Tertilt, and Reinhard Zimmermann (Leibniz Prize).
- In business and civil service: Andreas von Bechtolsheim (Sun Microsystems), Hans-Paul Bürkner (BCG), and Frauke Petry (politician).
- In arts and journalism: Hans-Jürgen von Bose, Ulrike Meinhof, and Frank Schirrmacher.

The Studienstiftung emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility, fostering leadership and societal contribution among its scholars.
