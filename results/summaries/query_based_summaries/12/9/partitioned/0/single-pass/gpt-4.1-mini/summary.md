# Summary of Studienstiftung

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally founded in 1925 and re-established in 1948, is Germany’s premier scholarship foundation offering both financial and academic support to exceptionally talented bachelor's, master's, and doctoral students. Scholarships are awarded based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility without restrictions on age or citizenship, provided that studies are conducted full-time, partly at a university in Germany, the EU, or Switzerland. Admission channels include nominations by schools, professors, examination boards, achievements in competitions, assessment tests, alumni recommendations, and for musicians and artists. Scholarship durations vary, typically up to the sixth semester with possible extensions contingent on academic performance and personal circumstances. Doctoral candidates also have access to scholarships with certain global eligibility conditions.

Financial support involves a monthly allowance (e.g., €300 for most scholars, €1,550 for PhD candidates as of October 2025), subsidies for insurance, funding for study abroad, and is tax-free under German law. Academic aid comprises summer schools, research groups, language courses, workshops, mentoring by liaison professors, and opportunities to organize conferences. The Studienstiftung also offers internal and open scholarship programs in partnership with other institutions. As of 2023, approximately 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships were awarded annually, with a roughly equal gender distribution among scholars. Notable alumni include several Nobel laureates and esteemed academics across natural sciences, social sciences, politics, business, arts, and journalism. The share of Studienstiftung scholars among university populations typically ranges around 0.5%, with some private universities exhibiting higher proportions.
