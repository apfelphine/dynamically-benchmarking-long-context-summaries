The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (Studienstiftung) is a prestigious German scholarship program founded in 1925, initially as a department of the Deutsche Studentenschaft. After its dissolution under National Socialism, it was revived in 1948 as a registered association in Cologne. The Studienstiftung supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates through both financial and academic support, adhering to the principles of "excellence, initiative, and responsibility."

**Admission** is based on high academic achievement and social commitment, with eligibility determined by criteria similar to those of the German state support system (BAföG). Applicants are nominated by schools, professors, examination boards, national competitions, or self-nominations through an assessment test. Scholarships are awarded for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral studies, with financial support including monthly allowances, health insurance subsidies, and tuition fee coverage. The program also offers specialized summer academies, mentoring, and networking opportunities.

**Notable alumni** span diverse fields, including natural sciences (e.g., Nobel Prize winners like Manfred Eigen and Gerd Faltings), social sciences (e.g., sociologist Ulrich Beck), business and NGOs, politics, arts, and journalism. The Studienstiftung has expanded its reach, collaborating with international institutions like KAUST and the Max Weber scholarship program in Bavaria. Its mission remains rooted in fostering leadership and academic excellence among Germany’s most promising students.
