## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes — Overview

The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (German National Academic Foundation) is Germany's largest and oldest scholarship foundation, originally established in Dresden in 1925, dissolved under National Socialism in 1934, and re-founded in Cologne in 1948. The foundation is led by a president and secretary general, with notable leadership changes over the years. Its admission process is highly selective (based on "excellence, initiative and responsibility") and operates through nominations from schools, professors, examination boards, winners of designated competitions, self-assessment tests, alumni, and as a special route for musicians and artists. Admission is open to exceptionally talented students at universities in Germany, other EU countries, or Switzerland, regardless of citizenship or age.

## Program Structure and Scope

Studienstiftung provides general scholarships for undergraduate, graduate, and PhD students, combining financial support (monthly allowances and need-based stipends up to €855, and for PhD scholars, €1,550–€1,650 per month plus research allowance) and extensive academic programming (summer academies, research groups, language courses, workshops, mentoring, and funding for conferences/events). Subsidies are available for health insurance, students/PhDs with children, and study visits abroad. Internal programs include partnerships such as the KAUST-Studienstiftung program and music scholarships, while open programs (like McCloy, ERP, Carlo-Schmid) operate with other organizations. In 2023, Studienstiftung supported 14,888 scholars (including 1,262 doctorates), representing about 0.48% of German university students. The foundation counts many Nobel laureates, business leaders, politicians, artists, and journalists among its alumni.
