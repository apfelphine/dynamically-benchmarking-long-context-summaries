## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes: Overview and Key Facts

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is Germany’s largest and oldest scholarship foundation for highly talented students and doctoral candidates, founded in Dresden in 1925. After dissolution during the Nazi era, it was re-established in Cologne in 1948. The foundation’s mission is to support "excellence, initiative and responsibility" among scholars through both financial and academic sponsorship. Admission is highly selective and can occur through nominations by schools, university professors, examination boards, winners of major competitions, self-application via assessment tests, alumni, or for musicians and artists via university nomination. In 2023, 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships were awarded, with about 0.48% of all students in Germany supported.

Studienstiftung scholarships provide a monthly allowance of €300 and need-based stipends of up to €855 (undergraduate/graduate), plus health insurance subsidies and additional funds for students or doctoral candidates with children. Doctoral candidates receive higher stipends. Academic support includes summer schools, research groups, mentoring, and funds for students to organize conferences. The foundation offers internal and open scholarship programs in cooperation with partner organizations, such as the KAUST partnership and the Max Weber scholarship in Bavaria. Studienstiftung scholars are represented in all academic disciplines, and many alumni have received major scientific awards, held political office, or achieved prominence in the arts, journalism, and business.
