The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is a German foundation established in 1948 in Cologne to support talented students through financial and academic assistance. It offers general scholarships for bachelor's, master's, and doctoral studies, as well as specific doctoral scholarships, based on criteria similar to the German state support system (BAFöG). Eligibility requires full-time study at a university in Germany, an EU country, or Switzerland, with no restrictions on citizenship or age. Admissions occur through various channels, including nominations by schools, professors, examination boards, and alumni, as well as an open assessment test. Scholarship holders receive a monthly allowance, health insurance subsidies, and access to academic enrichment programs such as summer schools, research groups, and mentoring. The foundation also manages internal programs for selected scholars and alumni, and open programs in cooperation with other organizations, including the KAUST partnership and the Max Weber scholarship for Bavaria.

In 2023, the Studienstiftung awarded 2,179 general scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships, representing approximately 0.48% of all university students in Germany. The scholarship portfolio includes a €300 monthly allowance for most scholars, with need-based funding available up to €855 monthly, and higher stipends for PhD students (€1,550 monthly). Financial support covers tuition fees, travel, and study abroad costs, and scholarship payments are tax-free. Academic support encompasses subsidized summer academies, conferences, and language courses, alongside mandatory reporting on studies and activities. The foundation is led by a President and Secretary General, with a leadership history spanning from Adolf Grimme (1948–1963) to Michael Hoch (2023–present). Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists, and prominent figures in politics, science, and the arts, reflecting the foundation's commitment to excellence, initiative, and responsibility.
