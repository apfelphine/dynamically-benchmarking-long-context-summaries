The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (German Students' Foundation) is a German scholarship organization founded in 1925 as a department of the Deutsche Studentenschaft. It was dissolved under National Socialism in 1934 and re-established in 1948 in Cologne as a registered association. The foundation offers financial and academic support to undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students, with eligibility based on BAFöG criteria. Students must study full-time, with at least part of their studies in Germany, another EU country, or Switzerland. The foundation emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility in its selection process.

Scholarship holders are admitted through various channels, including school nominations, professor nominations, examination board nominations, participation in national or federal competitions, self-nomination via an assessment test, and alumni nominations. Admission is not limited by age or nationality, and students may receive funding until the end of their studies, with extensions possible based on academic performance or exceptional circumstances.

The scholarship includes a monthly allowance of €300, a need-based basic scholarship (up to €855/month), and additional support for students with children. Academic support includes summer schools, research groups, language courses, and mentoring. The foundation also offers internal and open scholarship programs, such as the KAUST-Studienstiftung and the McCloy Academic Scholarship Program.

As of 2023, the foundation approved 2,179 undergraduate and 301 doctoral scholarships. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates Manfred Eigen and Robert Huber, and prominent figures in politics, business, and the arts. The foundation's mission is to foster excellence and civic responsibility among future leaders through educational support.
