## Studienstiftung Summary

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, Germany's largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation, was established in Dresden in 1925. After being dissolved during the Nazi era, it was re-founded in Cologne in 1948. The Studienstiftung provides general scholarships for bachelor's, master's, and doctoral studies to exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates, based on the core principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission is highly competitive and can occur through nomination by schools, professors, examination boards, winners of national competitions, self-application via assessment tests, alumni nomination, or via specific programs for musicians and artists. The foundation supports studies primarily at German universities but also in other EU countries or Switzerland, and does not require German/EU citizenship or impose age limits.

The scholarship offers substantial financial support (up to €855/month for students, €1,550 increasing to €1,650/month for PhDs from October 2025, plus allowances and overseas grants) and extensive academic support, such as mentoring, summer schools, research groups, and workshops. In 2023, about 0.48% of German university students were Studienstiftung scholars; more than 14,800 were supported nationwide. The foundation also administers specialized internal and open scholarship programs and runs the Max Weber scholarship in Bavaria. Studienstiftung alums include numerous Nobel laureates, business leaders, politicians, artists, and scholars, underlining its reputation as a leading institution for fostering academic and professional excellence in Germany.
