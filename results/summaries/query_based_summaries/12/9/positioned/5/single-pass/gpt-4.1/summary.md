## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes — Summary

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, Germany's oldest and most prestigious scholarship foundation, was established in Dresden in 1925, dissolved under National Socialism in 1934, and re-founded in Cologne in 1948. Its mission centers on supporting exceptionally talented undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students based on the principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission is highly selective and occurs via several channels, including nominations from schools, professors, examination boards, national competition winners, alumni, and a competitive assessment test. Nominees undergo rigorous selection seminars assessing academic achievement, motivation, and social commitment. The program is open to students regardless of citizenship or age, provided part of their studies is undertaken at a university in Germany, another EU country, or Switzerland.

Scholarships consist of financial support—such as a €300 monthly allowance and needs-based stipends up to €855 (and €1,550/€1,650 plus research allowances for PhD students)—and comprehensive academic support including summer schools, research groups, workshops, and mentoring. Additional funds are available for scholars with children and those undertaking studies abroad. The Studienstiftung also runs internal and cooperative scholarship programs, such as the Max Weber Program in Bavaria and international collaborations like the KAUST partnership. In 2023, there were 14,888 scholarship recipients (including 1,262 doctoral candidates), representing about 0.48% of university students in Germany. The foundation's alumni include Nobel laureates, leaders in science, business, politics, and the arts.
