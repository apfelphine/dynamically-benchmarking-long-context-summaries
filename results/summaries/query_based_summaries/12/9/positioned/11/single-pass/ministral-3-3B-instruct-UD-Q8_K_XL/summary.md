The **Studienstiftung** is a prestigious German scholarship program founded in 1925, initially as a department of the *Deutsche Studentenschaft*. It was revived in 1948 as a registered association in Cologne and remains one of Germany’s most influential merit-based scholarships. The program supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates across various fields, emphasizing excellence, initiative, and responsibility.

### **Admission and Selection**
The Studienstiftung offers scholarships to students and doctoral candidates based on merit, regardless of nationality or citizenship. Admission occurs through several channels:
1. **School nominations**: High school principals can nominate 30 senior students annually.
2. **University nominations**: Professors can nominate students directly.
3. **Examination boards**: Universities can nominate students from the second academic year.
4. **Competitions**: Winners of national or federal competitions (e.g., *Bundeswettbewerb Informatik*, *Jugend forscht*) may qualify.
5. **Assessment test**: Since 2010, students can apply via a standardized test followed by a selection seminar.
6. **Alumni nominations**: Past scholars may nominate suitable candidates.
7. **Support for artists and musicians**: Universities can nominate students in the fine arts, music, and performing arts.

Scholarship holders are selected based on academic excellence, social commitment, and potential for leadership. The program operates without strict quotas, ensuring a diverse and high-caliber cohort.

### **Scholarship Benefits**
The Studienstiftung provides both financial and academic support:
- **Financial Support**: A monthly allowance of €300, plus need-based subsidies (up to €855/month) for health and long-term care insurance. PhD scholars receive €1,550–€1,650/month, including research allowances.
- **Academic Support**: Summer schools, research groups, language courses, and mentoring programs. Scholars can apply for funding to organize their own conferences and events.
- **Summer Academies**: Annual two-week subsidized summer academies, a central event format for scholarship holders.
- **Doctoral Scholarships**: Highly qualified PhD students receive funding under the guidelines of the German Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF).

### **Statistics and Notable Alumni**
As of 2023, the Studienstiftung awarded **2,179 undergraduate/graduate scholarships** and **301 doctoral scholarships**, with **71.5% of scholars being women**. The program supports students across Germany’s public and private universities, including elite institutions like Heidelberg, Freiburg, and Bucerius Law School.

Notable alumni include:
- **Science & Medicine**: Martin Beneke (physicist, Leibniz Prize 2008), Reinhard Genzel (Nobel Prize in Physics 2020).
- **Social Sciences**: Ulrich Beck (sociologist), Michèle Tertilt (economist, Leibniz Prize 2019).
- **Business & NGOs**: Andreas von Bechtolsheim (Sun Microsystems co-founder).
- **Politics & Law**: Fritz Kuhn (Green Party co-chairman), Christine Teusch (first female German Minister of Education).
- **Arts & Journalism**: Mechthild Bach (soprano), Frank Schirrmacher (journalist).

The Studienstiftung is renowned for fostering leadership and innovation, with alumni occupying key roles in academia, government, business, and the arts. Its mission aligns with Germany’s commitment to meritocracy and excellence in education.
