## Summary of Studienstiftung

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally founded in Dresden in 1925, was reestablished as a registered association in Cologne in 1948 after its dissolution in 1934 during National Socialism. It is Germany’s premier scholarship foundation offering both financial and academic support to exceptionally talented undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students, irrespective of citizenship or age. Scholarship candidates are admitted through diverse avenues, including school nominations, university professor nominations, academic competitions, assessment tests, and alum recommendations, with rigorous selection seminars involving interviews and group assessments. The foundation emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility in alignment with German state support criteria (BAFöG).

Financially, the scholarship provides monthly allowances (up to €855 for general scholars and €1,550 for PhD candidates, increasing to €1,650 plus research allowance after 2025), health subsidies, and grants for overseas study and tuition partial payments. Academically, it offers summer schools, research opportunities, language courses, mentoring, and encourages scholars to organize conferences. The Studienstiftung also runs internal and open scholarship programs in partnership with institutes like KAUST and Beethoven House. As of 2023, it supports around 14,888 scholars, comprising undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students, representing roughly 0.48% of the German student population. Notable alumni span natural sciences (including multiple Nobel laureates), social sciences, politics, arts, and journalism, reflecting the foundation's broad impact across German and international academia and public life.
