The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (German Students' Foundation) is a German organization established in 1925, dissolved under National Socialism in 1934, and re-founded in 1948 in Cologne. It offers scholarships to outstanding students pursuing undergraduate, graduate, or doctoral studies, with eligibility based on academic merit and the principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility. The foundation supports students through financial aid (including a monthly allowance, health insurance, and need-based subsidies) and academic enrichment, such as summer schools, workshops, and mentoring. 

Scholarship holders are selected through various channels, including nomination by schools, professors, or examination boards, and via an assessment test. Admission is open to students from any nationality, with no age or citizenship requirements, and full-time study at a German, EU, or Swiss university. Scholars typically receive funding from the 6th semester onward, with extensions based on academic performance or exceptional personal circumstances.

In 2023, the foundation approved 2,179 undergraduate/graduate and 301 doctoral scholarships. It has a strong academic presence, with notable alumni in natural sciences (e.g., Gerd Faltings, Peter Scholze), social sciences (e.g., Ulrich Beck), business, politics, and the arts. The foundation also runs internal and open scholarship programs in collaboration with institutions like KAUST and the Beethoven House, and supports research and community engagement through its academic initiatives.
