## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes: Overview

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is Germany's largest and most selective scholarship foundation, founded in 1925 in Dresden. Dissolved during National Socialism and re-established in Cologne in 1948, the organization is governed by a succession of presidents and secretaries general. It offers general scholarships for bachelor's, master's, and doctoral studies, providing both financial (monthly allowances, insurance subsidies, child supplements, tax-free payments) and academic support (summer schools, research groups, mentoring, language courses, and subsidized events). Applications are open to exceptional students and PhD candidates of any citizenship or age studying full-time at universities in Germany, other EU countries, or Switzerland. Admission channels include nominations by schools, professors, examination boards, alumni, competition winners, cooperation partners, and an assessment test, as well as targeted support for musicians and artists.

## Programs, Reach, and Impact

Studienstiftung funds can extend to exchange programs, research internships, and joint scholarships such as KAUST-Studienstiftung or the Max Weber scholarship in Bavaria. In 2023, 2,179 general scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships were approved, with a scholar population of 14,888—about 0.48% of German university students. More than half of scholars are women, and most receive an expense allowance, with a minority receiving need-dependent funds. Notable alumni include numerous Nobel laureates and leaders in science, business, academia, politics, and the arts. Academic reporting is mandatory, and event organization is supported. The foundation enjoys a strong network across German and select private universities.
