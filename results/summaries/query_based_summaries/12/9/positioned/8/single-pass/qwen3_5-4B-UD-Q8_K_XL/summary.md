**Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is a German foundation established in 1948 in Cologne, succeeding a predecessor dissolved during the Nazi era in 1934. It provides financial and academic support to exceptionally talented bachelor's, master's, and doctoral students who demonstrate excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Eligibility is generally tied to the German state support system (BAföG), though citizenship is not required, and applicants must study full-time, with at least some coursework in Germany, the EU, or Switzerland. Admission occurs through various channels, including nomination by schools (one in 30 students), university professors (one in 50 students), examination boards, winners of national competitions, or self-assessment tests. Since 2010, candidates may also apply via a standardized assessment test, which determines cognitive ability, followed by a selection seminar evaluating motivation and social commitment. The foundation offers a monthly allowance (starting at €300) and need-based support up to €855, along with subsidies for insurance, research grants, and overseas study. In 2023, it approved 14,888 scholarships, representing approximately 0.48% of German university students.

The Studienstiftung also manages internal programs for scholars and alumni, such as partnerships with KAUST for STEM research and residencies for musicians. It runs open scholarship programs in cooperation with external organizations, including the McCloy Academic Scholarship Program, the ERP-Stipendienprogramm, and the Carlo-Schmid-Programm. Additionally, the foundation administers the Max Weber scholarship for students in Bavaria. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates in physics, chemistry, and medicine, as well as prominent figures in science, business, politics, and the arts, reflecting the foundation's role in fostering international academic exchange and leadership.
