## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes: Summary

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, founded in Dresden in 1925 and re-established in Cologne in 1948, is Germany’s largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation. Dedicated to supporting exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates based on "excellence, initiative, and responsibility", the Studienstiftung offers a rigorous selection process through school, university, and competition nominations, along with self-application options. Its scholarships are open to full-time university students in Germany, other EU countries, or Switzerland, regardless of citizenship or age. Selection involves multi-day seminars and academic assessment, with admissions not limited by quotas.

Studienstiftung scholarships provide both financial and academic support: scholars receive a €300 monthly allowance, need-based stipends up to €855 per month, plus insurance and family support; doctoral stipends are €1,550 (€1,650 from October 2025) plus research allowance. Academic support includes summer schools, research groups, mentoring, and conference funding. Additional opportunities include internal programs, arts and music support, and jointly financed scholarships such as the Max Weber Program. In 2023, 14,888 scholars were supported, making up 0.48% of Germany’s student population; 55.4% were women. Notable alumni span fields from Nobel laureates and leading academics to prominent figures in politics, journalism, business, the arts, and non-governmental organizations.
