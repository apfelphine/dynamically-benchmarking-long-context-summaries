## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes: Overview

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, known as the Studienstiftung, is Germany’s oldest and largest scholarship foundation dedicated to supporting academically gifted and socially committed undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students. Founded in Dresden in 1925, dissolved in 1934 under National Socialism, and re-established in Cologne in 1948, it has since operated as a registered association. The program is open to full-time students at universities in Germany, other EU countries, or Switzerland; no citizenship or age requirements apply. Admission is highly competitive and occurs through school, university, or professor nomination, self-assessment tests, success in national/international competitions, or nomination by alums and cooperation partners. In 2023, around 14,888 scholars (including 1,262 doctoral scholars) were supported, representing approximately 0.48% of German university students.

## Scholarships and Support

Studienstiftung scholarships provide financial support consisting of an allowance (€300/month) plus a need-based stipend up to €855/month for undergraduates and graduates, and €1,550/month (to increase to €1,650 in October 2025) plus a research allowance for PhD candidates. Additional payments are available for scholars and doctoral candidates with children or for study abroad. Scholarship payments are tax-free. Academic support includes mentoring, summer schools, research groups, workshops, and conferences, with reporting requirements for scholars. Studienstiftung also operates internal and open scholarship programs, such as partnerships with KAUST, McCloy, ERP, and the Carlo-Schmid-Programme, as well as the Max Weber Scholarship for Bavarian students. The foundation boasts a distinguished alumni network spanning Nobel laureates, renowned scientists, public figures, artists, and leaders in academia, politics, and business.
