The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (German Endowment Fund) is Germany's oldest and largest scholarship foundation, established in 1948 to support exceptionally talented and socially committed students in Germany, Switzerland, and the EU. Founded in 1925, it was dissolved during the Nazi era and re-established in Cologne in 1948, operating under the principles of "excellence, initiative, and responsibility." The foundation provides financial support, including a monthly allowance ranging from €300 to €855 for undergraduates and €1,550 for PhD students, alongside academic support such as summer schools, language courses, and mentoring. Admissions are based on academic achievement and social commitment, with candidates selected through various channels including nominations by schools, professors, examination boards, and a national assessment test. In 2023, the Studienstiftung awarded 14,888 scholarships, representing approximately 0.48% of German university students.

The foundation also offers internal programs for current scholars and alumni, such as the KAUST-Studienstiftung partnership for STEM researchers, and open scholarship programs in collaboration with external organizations like the McCloy Academic Scholarship Program and the Max Weber scholarship for Bavaria. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize laureates in physics, chemistry, and medicine, Fields Medalists, and prominent figures in politics, business, and the arts. The Studienstiftung maintains a diverse gender balance, with women comprising over 50% of its scholars, and focuses on fostering international understanding and academic excellence across the European continent.
