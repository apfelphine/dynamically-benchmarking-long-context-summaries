# Summary on Studienstiftung

Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally founded in Dresden in 1925, was dissolved under National Socialism in 1934 and re-established as a registered association in Cologne in 1948. It provides scholarships based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility, supporting full-time university students and doctoral candidates primarily in Germany, the EU, or Switzerland, with no citizenship or age restrictions. Admission is competitive and can occur via nomination by schools, professors, examination boards, competition winners, assessment tests, alums, or arts faculties, with selection involving seminars assessing academic and personal qualities.

The scholarship includes financial support comprising monthly allowances (up to €855 for students and €1,550 for PhD candidates) plus insurance subsidies, as well as academic support through summer schools, workshops, mentoring, and funding for research and conferences. The Studienstiftung also offers internal and open scholarship programs through partnerships, such as with KAUST and the Bavarian Elite Support Act. As of 2023, around 14,888 scholarships were awarded, with notable alumni spanning natural sciences (several Nobel laureates), social sciences, business, politics, arts, and journalism.
