## Summary of Studienstiftung

The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally established in Dresden in 1925, was dissolved under National Socialism in 1934 and re-established as a registered association in Cologne in 1948. It functions as Germany's largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation, selecting exceptionally talented undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students based on principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission pathways include nominations by secondary schools, university professors, examination boards, winners of national competitions, alumni, and assessment tests. In 2023, approximately 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships were awarded.

The scholarship offers both financial and academic support, including monthly allowances, health insurance subsidies, and funding for study-related activities such as summer schools, workshops, and mentoring. PhD scholars receive higher monthly stipends and additional research allowances. Academic support also encourages extracurricular engagement and scholarly events organized by recipients. The foundation maintains specialized internal and open scholarship programs—including partnerships with institutions like KAUST and the Beethoven House—and welcomes applicants studying full-time in Germany, the EU, or Switzerland, without strict age or citizenship requirements. Notable alumni span natural sciences, social sciences, business, politics, arts, and journalism, including multiple Nobel laureates and leaders in academia and public service.
