The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (Studienstiftung) is a prestigious German scholarship program founded in 1925, initially as a department of the Deutsche Studentenschaft. After being dissolved under National Socialism, it was reestablished in 1948 as a registered association in Cologne. The Studienstiftung supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates through both financial and academic support, adhering to the principles of **excellence, initiative, and responsibility**.

### **Admission and Selection**
Eligibility is determined by criteria similar to those of the German state’s **BAföG** (Federal Training Assistance Act) and is open to students from any country, regardless of age or citizenship. Admission occurs through several channels:
1. **School nominations**: High school principals can nominate up to one student per class.
2. **University nominations**: Professors can nominate students directly.
3. **Examination boards**: Universities can nominate students based on academic performance.
4. **Competitions**: Winners of national or federal competitions (e.g., *Bundeswettbewerb Informatik*, *Jugend debattiert*) may be considered.
5. **Assessment test**: Applicants can self-nominate via an assessment test, followed by a selection seminar.
6. **Alumni nominations**: Past scholars can recommend students.
7. **Artists and musicians**: Universities can nominate students in the fine arts, music, and performing arts.

Scholars are selected based on academic excellence, social commitment, and personal qualities. The selection process includes interviews and group discussions during a three-day seminar.

### **Scholarship Benefits**
The Studienstiftung offers **general scholarships** for bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral studies, including:
- **Financial support**: A monthly allowance of €300, plus need-based funding up to €855/month (similar to BAföG). PhD scholars receive €1,550/month (plus a research allowance of €100).
- **Insurance coverage**: Health and long-term care insurance.
- **Travel grants**: Support for international visits and partial tuition fee coverage.
- **Tax-free**: Scholarship payments are exempt from income tax under German law.
- **Academic support**: Access to summer schools, research groups, language courses, workshops, and mentoring by liaison professors. Scholars can organize their own conferences and apply for funding.

Additionally, the Studienstiftung provides **internal scholarship programs** (e.g., partnerships with KAUST, the Beethoven House) and **open scholarship programs** (e.g., McCloy Academic Scholarship, ERP-Stipendienprogramm) in collaboration with other organizations.

### **Notable Alumni**
The Studienstiftung has produced leaders in academia, politics, business, and the arts, including:
- **Natural Sciences**: Nobel Prize winners like **Manfred Eigen** (chemistry) and **Reinhard Genzel** (physics).
- **Social Sciences**: Sociologists like **Ulrich Beck** and economists such as **Michèle Tertilt**.
- **Business & NGOs**: Founders of companies like **Andreas von Bechtolsheim** (Sun Microsystems) and **Alexander Dibelius** (Goldman Sachs).
- **Politics & Public Service**: Politicians like **Fritz Kuhn** (Green Party) and **Karl Schiller** (former German Finance Minister).
- **Arts**: Composers like **Matthias Pintscher** and writers such as **Juli Zeh**.

### **Statistics**
As of 2023, the Studienstiftung awarded **2,179 scholarships** for undergraduate/graduate studies and **301 doctoral scholarships**, supporting **14,888 scholars** out of Germany’s **2.87 million students**. The scholarships are distributed across public and private universities, with notable concentrations in fields like law, medicine, and the humanities.

The Studienstiftung remains a cornerstone of Germany’s merit-based scholarship system, fostering talent and fostering a culture of responsibility and innovation.
