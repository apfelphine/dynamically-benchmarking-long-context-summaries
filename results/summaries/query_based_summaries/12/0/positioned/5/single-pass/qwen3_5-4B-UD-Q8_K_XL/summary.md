# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program is a professional exchange initiative supported by the United States Department of State, designed to provide summer internship placements, career training, and financial assistance to Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the United States. Established by the United States Congress in the 1992 fiscal year (Pub. L. 102–138, Sec. 227) and named in honor of the Freedom Support Act of 1992 (Pub. L. 102–511, Sec. 801), the program aims to foster international understanding through direct professional engagement. Historically administered by the International Research & Exchanges Board and various American Councils, the program underwent a significant renewal and scope expansion in 2015 when it was launched by Cultural Vistas.

Named after Edmund S. Muskie, a prominent U.S. Senator and former Governor of Maine, the scholarship honors his legacy in diplomacy and public service. Muskie, born to Polish immigrant parents in 1914, rose to become the first Polish American nominated by a major party for the vice presidency. His political career was marked by significant achievements in environmental legislation, including the Clean Air Act and Clean Water Act, and civil rights advancement. He served as a U.S. Senator from 1959 to 1980, chaired the Senate Budget Committee, and briefly served as Secretary of State under President Jimmy Carter in 1980–1981, where he managed the diplomatic response to the Iran hostage crisis. Following his retirement, he continued his involvement in public service and diplomacy, receiving the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1981.
