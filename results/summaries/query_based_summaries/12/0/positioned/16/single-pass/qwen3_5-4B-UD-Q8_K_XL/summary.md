The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** is a professional exchange initiative supported by the United States Department of State, designed to provide summer internship placements, career training, and financial assistance to Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the United States. Established by the United States Congress in fiscal year 1992 (Pub. L. 102–138, Sec. 227) and named in honor of the late U.S. Senator Edmund S. Muskie, the program was originally named the Freedom Support Act of 1992 (Pub. L. 102–511, Sec. 801). Historically administered by the Institute for International Education (formerly the International Research & Exchanges Board) and various American Councils, the program underwent a significant renewal with a new format and scope in 2015, now managed by Cultural Vistas.

The program's primary objective is to foster international understanding and strengthen diplomatic ties through direct engagement between U.S. scholars and students from Eurasian nations. By facilitating immersive experiences within U.S. academic and professional environments, the Muskie Internship Program aligns with Muskie's lifelong legacy of diplomacy, environmental protection, and civil rights advocacy. It serves as a critical vehicle for cross-cultural exchange, offering participants the opportunity to gain firsthand insight into American society while contributing to broader goals of global cooperation and educational advancement.
