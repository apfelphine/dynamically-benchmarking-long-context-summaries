# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program is a professional exchange initiative supported by the United States Department of State, established by the United States Congress in fiscal year 1992 (Pub. L. 102–138, Sec. 227) and named following the Freedom Support Act of 1992. Designed to facilitate career training and financial support for Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the United States, the program was originally administered by the International Research & Exchanges Board and the American Council of Teachers of Russian, as well as the American Council for Collaboration in Education and Language. In 2015, the program was relaunched with a renewed format and scope under the administration of Cultural Vistas.

Named in honor of U.S. Senator Edmund S. Muskie, the program reflects his legacy as a pivotal figure in American diplomacy, environmental legislation, and civil rights. Muskie, who served as Governor of Maine, a U.S. Senator, and Secretary of State, is celebrated for his bipartisan approach, advocacy for the "Muskie Act" (Clean Air Act), and efforts to stabilize the government in Cambodia. His contributions to federal budget reform earned him the title "father of the federal budget process," while his work during the Iran hostage crisis and diplomatic negotiations with the Soviet Union solidified his status as a key architect of U.S. foreign policy. The Muskie Internship Program continues his commitment to fostering international understanding through educational exchange.
