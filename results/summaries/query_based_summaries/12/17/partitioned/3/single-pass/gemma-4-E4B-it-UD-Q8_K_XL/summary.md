King's Scholar is a designation associated with several prestigious educational institutions. At **King's Ely**, scholarships were historically given to twelve boys annually, but after Queen Elizabeth II requested their inclusion, the scholarship is now awarded to twelve high-achieving girls and boys from the lower sixth, based on GCSE grades, with most scholars achieving over eight grade '9's. Scholars at King's Ely wear a red gown in cathedral services and are members of the Ely Cathedral foundation for life.

At **King's School, Canterbury**, King's Scholars are students who achieved exceptional grades in their GCSEs or passed the scholarship exam. All academic scholars now wear a white gown to cathedral services and gain the right to walk down the aisle first. **King's School, Worcester** offers the highest scholarship based on academic or musical attainment; scholars appointed in the Lower Remove are titled King's Scholars (regardless of gender), while female scholars in the Lower Sixth are titled Queen's Scholars.
