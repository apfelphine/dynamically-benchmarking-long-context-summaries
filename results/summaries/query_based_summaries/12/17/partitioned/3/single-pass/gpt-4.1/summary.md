## King's Scholar: Overview and Traditions

The title "King's Scholar" historically refers to students awarded prestigious scholarships at select educational institutions, notably Eton College and several King's Schools in England. Established by royal foundation, such as Edward II's writ to Cambridge in 1317 and Henry VI’s re-founding of Eton College in 1440, King's Scholars were originally provided education free of charge and distinguished from fee-paying students. At Eton, King's Scholars—known as "Collegers" or "tugs"—are selected via a competitive examination and occupy the original college house, wearing black academic gowns over their uniforms, and partaking in unique traditions and formalities, including separate dining in College Hall and specific privileges within school life.

Further use of the term extends to King's Schools at Canterbury, Ely, Worcester, and others, where King’s Scholarships are awarded for academic or musical excellence and may include a reduction in school fees. Modern traditions at these schools may include ceremonial admission (e.g., in cathedrals), gown wearing, special events, and lifelong privileges such as the right to marry or be buried within cathedral grounds. Notably, at Westminster School, scholars are called King's Scholars when the monarch is male and Queen's Scholars when female, following similar selection and tradition patterns. At King's College Cambridge, the term is used for undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, entitling them to ceremonial recognition and certain privileges.

## Notable King's Scholar Details

Across these institutions, King’s Scholars are recognized for their academic distinction and adherence to historic traditions specific to each school. In recent times, the scholarships may be subject to means-testing and may no longer guarantee full fee waivers, but they remain highly prestigious. At Eton, approximately 70 King's Scholars are enrolled at any time; at other King's Schools, the number and nature of scholarships may vary and, since 1973, Queen’s Scholarships may also be awarded, reflecting advancements in co-education. King's Scholars are often identified by particular clothing (gowns, ties) and post-nominal letters (KS). Notable alumni include influential figures in politics, literature, science, and public life.
