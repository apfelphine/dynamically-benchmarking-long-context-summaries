The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic scholarship at the **King’s Schools** in England, established by King Henry VIII in 1541. Originally awarded to all pupils at these schools, the scholarship now recognizes exceptional academic or musical attainment. Scholars receive fee discounts and are granted special privileges, such as admission to the **King’s Scholar book** in Ely Cathedral, participation in traditions like the **Hoop Trundle**, and wearing distinctive red gowns and ties during school services.

The scholarships are awarded at **King’s Ely**, **King’s School Canterbury**, **King’s School Worcester**, and other King’s Schools. At **King’s Ely**, scholars are admitted as members of the **Cathedral Foundation** and sign their names in a special book during a choral evensong ceremony. At **King’s School Canterbury**, scholars wear white gowns and have the right to walk down the aisle at cathedral services first. At **King’s School Worcester**, scholars are titled **King’s Scholars** (for males) or **Queen’s Scholars** (for females), depending on the reigning monarch, and are initiated into the **Worcester Cathedral Foundation**. The scholarships emphasize academic excellence and are accompanied by fee reductions, with some schools offering additional privileges like access to cathedral services and leadership roles.
