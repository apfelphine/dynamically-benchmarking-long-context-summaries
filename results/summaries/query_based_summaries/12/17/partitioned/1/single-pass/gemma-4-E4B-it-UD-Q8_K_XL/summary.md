## King's Scholar Summary

The term "King's Scholar" is used across several distinct institutions:

*   **Eton College:** Historically, a Foundation Scholarship at Eton College provided a full and unconditional waiver of all school fees, but since the 1960s, it has become subject to means testing, meaning part or all of the fees may be waived based on financial circumstances. King's Scholars are identified by the post-nominal letters KS appended to their surnames.
*   **King's College Cambridge:** This term refers to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees. Such scholars receive a prize of £350 and are invited to attend a ceremony in the King's College Chapel, followed by a free, formal dinner.
*   **The King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester):** These schools award King's Scholarships in honor of King Henry VIII. The specific nature of the award varies by school; for example, at King's Ely, King's Scholars and high-achieving girls are awarded scholarships, and they participate in traditions like the "Hoop Trundle." At King's School, Worcester, the scholarship is the highest, awarded for academic or musical attainment and often comes with a fee reduction.

Additionally, the term "King's Scholar" is used in connection with **Westminster School**, where foundation scholars are designated Queen's Scholars when a reigning Queen is on the throne, and King's Scholars when a reigning King is in power.
