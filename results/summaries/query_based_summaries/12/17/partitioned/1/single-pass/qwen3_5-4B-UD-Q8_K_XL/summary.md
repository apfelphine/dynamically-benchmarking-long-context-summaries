# King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" primarily refers to students at Eton College who have passed the College Election examination and received a Foundation Scholarship, granting them a post-nominal "KS" and admission into the historic "College" house. While historically a full and unconditional fee waiver, since the 1960s the scholarship has been subject to means testing, though it still retains the post-nominal letters. Distinct from these are "Oppidan Scholars" (OS), who are academically distinguished Oppidans but receive no financial benefit. The title is also applied to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees, and to students at the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) awarded in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts. Since 1973, the King's Schools have also awarded Queen's Scholarships in recognition of the reigning monarch.

Historically, King's Scholars at Eton were housed in the central college building, eating together in College Hall, and playing the Wall Game for five years, distinguishing them from fee-paying "Oppidans." At King's Schools, scholars are initiated into cathedral foundations and wear specific gowns and ties, retaining privileges such as the right to be buried within the cathedral grounds or to marry there. The King's Scholar designation at Eton and the King's Schools originates from the founding by King Henry VIII, who established these institutions as charities for "poor, scholarly boys" and endowed them with scholarships.
