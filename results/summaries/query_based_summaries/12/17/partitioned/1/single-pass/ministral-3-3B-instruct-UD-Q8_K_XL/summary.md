The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at Eton College, awarded to students who excel in scholarship and are admitted into the oldest house, **College**, through competitive examination. These scholars, known as "Collegers" or colloquially as "tugs," are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown and are housed in the central college buildings, which include School Yard and Chapel. The tradition dates back to King Edward II’s establishment of the first King’s Scholars at Cambridge in 1317, and Eton’s King’s Scholars have historically been a select group of academically gifted boys, though the scholarship has evolved to include girls under Queen Elizabeth II’s patronage.

At **King’s College Cambridge**, King’s Scholars are undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, receiving a £350 prize and attending a ceremonial dinner in the college chapel. This tradition stems from scholarships endowed by King Henry VI, linking King’s Colleges at Cambridge and Eton. The scholarships at the **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) were re-endowed by King Henry VIII in 1541, offering King’s Scholarships to students based on academic or musical excellence, often accompanied by fee discounts. These schools also grant Queen’s Scholarships under Queen Elizabeth II’s statute.
