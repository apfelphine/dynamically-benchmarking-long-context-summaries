**King's Scholar** refers to a prestigious academic scholarship awarded to students at several institutions in the UK, primarily at Eton College, Westminster School, King's College Cambridge, and the King's Schools (Canterbury, Ely, and Worcester). 

At **Eton College**, King's Scholars (or "Collegers") are selected through competitive examinations and reside in the original College building. They wear a black academic gown and participate in unique traditions, including meals in College Hall and the Wall Game. Oppidans (non-scholar students) are distinguished by their different housing and traditions. 

At **Westminster School**, the title is "King's Scholar" when a reigning king is in office, and "Queen's Scholar" when a queen is reigning, with scholars being selected through a competitive examination known as "The Challenge" and admitted to the oldest house, College.

At **King's College Cambridge**, the term is used for undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, who receive a £350 prize and attend a ceremony in the King's College Chapel, where they sign a book. 

The **King's Schools** (Canterbury, Ely, Worcester) award scholarships based on academic or musical achievement. These scholars are admitted to the cathedral foundation, gain privileges such as the right to marry or be buried in the cathedral, and wear gowns during services. At Worcester, scholars are entitled to a unique tradition where the Senior Scholar demands a holiday from the judge.

These scholarships are historically linked to the founding of the schools by Henry VIII and are rooted in a tradition of academic excellence and service to the community.
