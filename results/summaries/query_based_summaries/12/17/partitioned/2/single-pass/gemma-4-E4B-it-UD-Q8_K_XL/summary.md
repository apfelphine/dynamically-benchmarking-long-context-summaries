## King's Scholar Summaries

**At Eton College:**
A King's Scholar at Eton College is an individual who has passed the College Election examination and been awarded a Foundation Scholarship, granting admission to the house known as "College." This house consists exclusively of King's Scholars, who wear a black academic gown over the uniform. Historically, this scholarship provided a full and unconditional waiver of all school fees, though since the 1960s, the award has been subject to means testing, meaning part or all of the fees may be waived based on financial circumstances.

**At King's and Queen's Scholars at Westminster School:**
At Westminster School, foundation scholars are designated as Queen's Scholars when a Queen reigns and King's Scholars when a King reigns. These scholars are selected via a competitive examination known as "The Challenge," are housed in the oldest house (College), and participate in wearing gowns during Abbey services.

**At King's College Cambridge:**
The term "King's Scholar" also applies to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees. These scholars receive a £350 prize and are entitled to attend a ceremony in the King's College Chapel to sign their names in the King's Scholar book. A formal dinner is also held during the Michaelmas term for these scholars.

**At the King's Schools:**
The seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) award King's Scholarships in honor of King Henry VIII. While they now generally recognize academic or musical attainment, the scholarship is often accompanied by a discount on school fees. In Worcester, male scholars appointed in the Lower Sixth are titled King's Scholars, while female scholars appointed in the Lower Sixth are titled Queen's Scholars.
