The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction awarded at several prestigious institutions in the UK and internationally, primarily tied to historical royal patronage and educational excellence.

### **King's Scholars at King's College Cambridge**
At **King’s College Cambridge**, the term "King’s Scholar" refers to undergraduates who achieve **first-class degrees**. These scholars receive a **£350 prize**, attend a formal ceremony in the college chapel where their names are inscribed in the *King’s Scholar Book*, and are entitled to a **three-course dinner** with their respective Directors of Studies. The scholarship is a historical legacy from King Henry VI, who founded both King’s College and Eton College, reflecting their shared origins.

### **King's Scholars at King's Schools**
The **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) were re-endowed by **King Henry VIII** in 1541. Originally, all pupils at these schools were funded as King’s Scholars, but today, the scholarship is awarded to students with **exceptional academic or musical attainment**, often accompanied by a **fee discount**. King’s Scholars at these schools are members of the **foundation of their respective cathedrals**, wear academic gowns during services, and enjoy privileges such as **marriage in the cathedral** and **burial within the grounds**.

### **Key Distinction**
While the **King’s Scholar** designation at Cambridge is tied to academic excellence (first-class degrees), the **King’s Schools** scholarships emphasize **academic and musical distinction** and are tied to the historical legacy of royal patronage. Both reflect the deep-rooted tradition of royal scholarships in British education.
