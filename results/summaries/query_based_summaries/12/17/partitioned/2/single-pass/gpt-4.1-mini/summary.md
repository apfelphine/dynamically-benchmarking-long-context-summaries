# Summary of Information on "King's Scholar"

The title "King's Scholar" historically originates from 1317 when King Edward II established 12 boys from his household to study at Cambridge at royal expense, and later in 1440, King Henry VI founded Eton College as a free charity school for "poor, scholarly boys," admitting an increasing number of King's Scholars housed in the central College building. At Eton College today, a King's Scholar (also known as a "Colleger" or "tug") is a student who passes the College Election examination and is awarded a Foundation Scholarship, living in the original College house wearing a traditional black academic gown. Approximately 70 King's Scholars are admitted (about 14 per year at age 13), who share most school activities with fee-paying Oppidans but have unique privileges such as eating in College Hall, taking part in ancient formal traditions, and usually playing the Wall Game throughout their schooling.

Beyond Eton, several historic King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) also award King's Scholarships, originally as fully endowment-funded distinctions, now generally recognizing academic or musical excellence often with fee discounts. At these schools, King's Scholars enjoy privileges including wearing distinctive gowns during cathedral services, admission ceremonies in the local cathedrals, and lifelong association with the Cathedral foundations, including rights to marry or be buried in cathedral grounds. At King's School Canterbury, for example, scholars receive white gowns and procession privileges; at King's School Worcester, distinctions extend to male and female scholars (Queen's Scholars for females during a reigning queen), with traditions like the Senior Scholar petitioning for a holiday (Judge's Day) and entitlement to ecclesiastical grounds privileges.  
In summary, King's Scholars are academically distinguished students selected through examinations or merit, afforded historic privileges centered on education within cathedral foundations, especially at Eton College and various King's Schools across England.
