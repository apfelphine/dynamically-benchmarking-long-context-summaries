The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious scholarship at **Eton College**, established in 17th-century England to provide full or partial financial support to academically gifted students. Originally awarded exclusively to boys, the scholarship expanded to include girls in 1973, aligning with Queen Elizabeth II’s visit to Eton. King’s Scholars are selected based on academic excellence, typically achieving high grades in their GCSEs, and are granted privileges such as access to the King’s College Chapel, special gowns, and a reduced fee structure. They are also part of the oldest house at Eton, known as "College," and participate in unique traditions like the "Hoop Trundle," a race across the school grounds. The scholarship is named after **King Henry VIII**, who re-endowed the King’s Schools in 1541, including Eton, to support students through education.
