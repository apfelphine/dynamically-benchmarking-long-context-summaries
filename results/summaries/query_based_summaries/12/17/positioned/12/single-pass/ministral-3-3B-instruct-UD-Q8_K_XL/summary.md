The **King’s Scholar** program is a prestigious academic scholarship at **King’s College, Cambridge**, and **Eton College**, historically reserved for exceptional students from the royal household and later expanded to include outstanding young men and women. At Eton, King’s Scholars (or "Collegers") are selected through a rigorous examination and admitted to the oldest house, College, where they receive full scholarships and live in the central part of the school. The scholarship traditionally waived all fees but has increasingly been means-tested. Notable alumni include prominent figures in politics, academia, and the arts, such as Boris Johnson, Harold Macmillan, and Stanley Mordaunt Leathes.

At **King’s College Cambridge**, the term "King’s Scholar" refers to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, receiving a £350 prize and the right to attend a formal dinner and sign their name in the King’s Scholar book. This tradition stems from scholarships endowed by the college’s founder, King Henry VI. Additionally, the **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) award King’s Scholarships based on academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts. These schools also grant Queen’s Scholarships for female students, reflecting the historical gender distinctions in education.
