The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at **King’s College, Cambridge**, awarded to undergraduates who achieve a **first-class degree**. This honor is granted annually to students who demonstrate exceptional academic excellence, with recipients receiving a **£350 prize** and the privilege of attending a formal dinner with their respective Directors of Studies. The tradition stems from King’s College’s founding by **Henry VI**, who endowed scholarships for outstanding students, mirroring similar programs at Eton College and other institutions.

At **Eton College**, the King’s Scholar (or "Colleger") is a select group of students who pass rigorous examinations and are admitted to the oldest house, **College**, where they live and study alongside distinguished scholars. These students, aged 13–18, wear a distinctive black gown and share privileges such as access to ancient traditions and formal academic attire during services. The scholarship is historically tied to financial support, though modern scholarships may be subject to means testing.

Additionally, King’s Scholarships are awarded at **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), where high-achieving students receive academic or musical recognition, often accompanied by fee discounts. These schools, re-endowed by **Henry VIII**, also honor King’s Scholars with privileges like burial within their cathedral grounds. The term reflects the enduring legacy of royal patronage in British higher education.
