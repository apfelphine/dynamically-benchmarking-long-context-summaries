## King's Scholar: Overview and Historical Origins

The title "King's Scholar" traces its origins to 1317, when King Edward II established the first King's Scholars at the University of Cambridge, sending 12 boys from his household to study under a master. In 1440, King Henry VI founded Eton College as a charity school, increasing the number of King's Scholars to 70 by 1443. At Eton, King's Scholars—known as "Collegers" or "tugs"—are selected through the College Election examination and awarded a Foundation Scholarship, living in the original College house and distinguished by wearing black academic gowns. They participate in unique traditions and receive privileges that set them apart from fee-paying "Oppidans." Historically, the scholarship provided full waiver of school fees, but is now subject to means testing. Notable Eton King's Scholars include Boris Johnson, George Orwell, and John Maynard Keynes.

The term "King's Scholar" is also used at other historic British institutions. At Westminster School, the foundation scholars are titled King's or Queen's Scholars depending on the reigning monarch, selected by competitive examination and housed in College. At King's College, Cambridge, the title is historically reserved for undergraduates who attain first-class degrees, where scholars are recognized in a chapel ceremony. Additionally, The King's Schools (re-founded by Henry VIII in 1541) award King's Scholarships for academic or musical achievement, often with fee reductions and ceremonial privileges such as special gowns and the right to marry or be buried within cathedral grounds.

## Key Traditions and Privileges

King's Scholars across institutions share ceremonial roles, distinctive dress (such as gowns or ties), and access to historic privileges linked to the foundations' royal origins. Admission is typically awarded through examination-based selection, and scholars may participate in ancient traditions like the Hoop Trundle at King's Ely or special processions at King's School Canterbury. While initially limited to boys and focused on financial support, many King's Scholar programs have evolved to include girls and adapt to modern educational structures. Notable King's Scholars have gone on to make significant contributions in politics, literature, science, and other fields.
