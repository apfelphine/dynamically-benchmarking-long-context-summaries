## King's Scholar Summaries

**King's Scholar at Eton College:**
*   The title is awarded to students who pass the College Election examination and receive a Foundation Scholarship, admitting them into the "College," the oldest house within the original college buildings.
*   There are about 70 King's Scholars at any time, with approximately 14 admitted annually at age 13.
*   King's Scholars wear a black academic gown and eat meals in College Hall, sharing some aspects of life with Oppidans while participating in certain ancient formal traditions.
*   Historically, the scholarship provided a full waiver of all school fees, but since the 1960s, the scholarship has been subject to means testing, and as of 2021, it does not affect the size of fees.

**King's Scholar at King's College Cambridge:**
*   This term is used for undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees.
*   Recipients receive a prize of £350 and are entitled to attend a ceremony in the King's College Chapel, where they sign their name in the King's Scholar book.
*   These scholars also dine for free with their respective Directors of Studies during the subsequent Michaelmas term, unless they attain a first-class degree in their final year.

**King's Scholars at the King's Schools:**
*   The seven King's Schools award King's Scholarships (or Queen's Scholarships during the reign of a Queen) in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a discount on school fees.
*   **King's Ely:** Awards scholarships to boys and girls from the lower sixth based on GCSE grades. Scholars participate in the annual "Hoop Trundle" tradition and are members of the Cathedral foundation.
*   **King's School, Canterbury:** Scholars are admitted after passing the scholarship exam or achieving exceptional GCSE grades. All academic scholars wear a white gown to cathedral services.
*   **King's School, Worcester:** The King's Scholarship is the highest award, typically given based on exam results or GCSE attainment. Female scholars in the Lower Sixth are titled Queen's Scholars in honor of Elizabeth II.
