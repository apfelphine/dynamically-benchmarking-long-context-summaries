The **King's Scholar** program is a prestigious scholarship tradition at Eton College, established in 1317 by King Edward II. It selects 70 exceptional students annually, aged 13–18, who demonstrate outstanding academic ability and leadership potential. These scholars, known as "Collegers" or "tugs," are housed in the historic College building, distinguished by wearing a distinctive black academic gown. The scholarship covers full tuition and living expenses, though financial need is increasingly considered. Historically, King’s Scholars have included notable figures such as Boris Johnson, Aldous Huxley, and James Arbuthnot.

At **King’s College Cambridge**, the term "King’s Scholar" refers to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, receiving a £350 prize and attending a formal ceremony in the college chapel. This tradition, linked to King Henry VI’s founding of the college, remains a symbolic honor.

At **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), King’s Scholarships are awarded to high-achieving students, often based on academic or musical excellence, and may include fee discounts. These schools also grant Queen’s Scholarships for female applicants, reflecting their historical co-education reforms. Notable alumni include scholars and leaders in academia, law, and public service.
