# King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" primarily refers to students at Eton College, England, who are awarded a Foundation Scholarship and admitted into the school's original house known as "College." There are approximately 70 King's Scholars at any given time, aged 13 to 18, distinguished by wearing a black academic gown over their uniform. Admission is determined by passing a competitive College Election examination. While historically providing a full waiver of fees, the scholarship has been subject to means testing since the 1960s, though fees may still be partially waived based on financial circumstances. Scholars are granted a post-nominal "KS" and share many aspects of school life with other students, including lessons and sports, though they eat in College Hall and play the Wall Game for five years. The tradition dates back to 1440 when King Henry VI established Eton College, and the number of scholars was increased to 70 starting in 1443.

The title "King's Scholar" is also used in other contexts. At King's College Cambridge, it refers to undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, granting them a prize of £350 and a ceremony in the King's College Chapel. Similarly, the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), re-founded by King Henry VIII, award King's Scholarships in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts. By statute of Queen Elizabeth II, these schools also award Queen's Scholarships for female students. Additionally, Westminster School designates its foundation scholars as King's Scholars when a King is reigning and Queen's Scholars when a Queen is reigning. Historically, the term also appeared in the United States, where the "King's Scholar" program was originally a Florida state scholarship based on academic merit, later restructured into levels such as Florida Academic Scholar (FAS) and Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), though this program has since been replaced or significantly altered by other initiatives.
