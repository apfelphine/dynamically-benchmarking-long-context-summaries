The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic title with historical origins in the United Kingdom. Established in 1317 by King Edward II, the first King's Scholars were sent to study at the University of Cambridge. In 1440, King Henry VI founded Eton College, initially known as the King's College of our Lady of Eton, to provide free education to poor, scholarly boys. This institution evolved into a system where students admitted as "King's Scholars" (or "Collegers") are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown and are selected through a competitive examination. The term is also used at other institutions, including King's College Cambridge, where it is awarded to undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, and at the seven King's Schools, where it recognizes academic or musical attainment. Notable individuals associated with the title include politicians, scientists, artists, and leaders such as George Orwell, John Maynard Keynes, and Sir Harold Evans. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and service to society, with various traditions and privileges, including access to specific buildings, special events, and lifelong memberships in school foundations.
