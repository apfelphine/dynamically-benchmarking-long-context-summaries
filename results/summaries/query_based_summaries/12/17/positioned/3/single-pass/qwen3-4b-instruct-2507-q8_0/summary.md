**King's Scholar** refers to a distinguished student at several prestigious institutions in the UK and beyond, with distinct meanings at each:

- **Eton College**: A student selected through a competitive examination ("The Challenge") and admitted to the central "College" (known as a "Colleger" or "tug"). They wear a black academic gown and are entitled to privileges such as eating in College Hall and certain formal traditions. The scholarship, historically, provided full and unconditional waiver of fees, though means testing has since been introduced.

- **Westminster School**: Similar to Eton, a student selected through a competitive examination and admitted to the oldest house, "College," and wears gowns during school Abbey services.

- **King's College, Cambridge**: A term for undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, who receive a £350 prize and attend a formal ceremony in King's College Chapel, where they sign their name in the King's Scholar book.

- **King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester)**: Awarded to students who demonstrate academic or musical attainment, often with a reduction in school fees. The scholarship is granted in recognition of the reigning monarch, and students are members of the school's foundation.

The tradition at Eton is particularly notable for its long-standing history, with a fixed number of scholars (around 70) selected each year, and a strong emphasis on personal and academic development. Notable former scholars include prominent figures in politics, law, science, and the arts, such as Harold Macmillan, John Maynard Keynes, and Boris Johnson. The scholarship remains a significant part of the school's identity and educational philosophy.
