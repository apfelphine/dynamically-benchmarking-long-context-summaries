The **King’s Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at Eton College, established in 1317 by King Edward II. It is awarded to exceptional students who pass rigorous examinations and are admitted to the oldest house at Eton, known as "College." King’s Scholars (often called "Collegers" or "tugs") are distinguished by a black academic gown and live in the central college buildings, sharing academic and social life with the school but enjoying unique privileges, such as attending formal traditions and reduced fees under means-testing. The scholarship is historically tied to financial support for poor, scholarly boys, though modern scholarships are increasingly means-tested.

Notable alumni include figures like **Boris Johnson**, **Robert Walpole**, and **Sir David Attenborough**, reflecting the scholarship’s historical and cultural significance. The King’s Scholar program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and a commitment to public service, aligning with Eton’s legacy of producing influential leaders in politics, science, and the arts.
