## King's Scholar

The "King's Scholar" designation dates back to 1317 when King Edward II sent twelve boys from his household to study at Cambridge, establishing the tradition. Most notably at Eton College, King's Scholars—known as "Collegers" or "tugs"—are students who pass a competitive exam and receive a Foundation Scholarship, residing in the historic "College" house for five years. There are around 70 King's Scholars at any time, distinguished by their academic gowns and participation in unique Eton traditions such as dining in College Hall and playing the Wall Game throughout their tenure. Originally, the scholarship covered all school fees unconditionally, but today it may offer fee waivers based on financial need. King's Scholars at Eton use the post-nominal "KS" and have a long history of notable alumni in diverse fields.

Beyond Eton, the title "King's Scholar" is also granted at Westminster School (as Queen’s Scholars when appropriate), selected by competitive examination and granted distinctive privileges. At King's College, Cambridge, undergraduates achieving first-class degrees earn the King's Scholar title, a monetary award, and ceremonial recognition. In several King's Schools re-founded by Henry VIII—including Ely, Canterbury, and Worcester—King's Scholarships are awarded for academic or musical achievement and usually come with various privileges and sometimes fee reductions. Traditions associated with King's Scholars may include special attire, ceremonial events in cathedrals, and lifelong membership privileges within the cathedral foundation.
