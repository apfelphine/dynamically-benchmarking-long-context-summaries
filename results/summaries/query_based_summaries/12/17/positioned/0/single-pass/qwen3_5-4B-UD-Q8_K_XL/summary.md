The term "King's Scholar" refers to students admitted to prestigious institutions bearing the King's name. At Eton College, a King's Scholar, historically known as a "Colleger" or "tug," is admitted after passing a competitive examination and receiving a Foundation Scholarship. There are approximately 70 scholars per year, who live in the original central college buildings, wear black academic gowns, and enjoy specific privileges such as dining in the College Hall and playing the Wall Game for five years. Historically, the scholarship provided a full fee waiver, though since the 1960s it has been subject to means testing.

The title is also applied to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees, granting them a prize and the right to sign the King's Scholar book. At Westminster School, scholars are designated as King's Scholars during a reigning King's tenure. Additionally, the term is used for scholarships at the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), which were re-founded by King Henry VIII. These institutions award King's Scholarships based on academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts, and scholars are entitled to various traditional privileges, including special attire and the right to be married or buried in their respective cathedrals.
