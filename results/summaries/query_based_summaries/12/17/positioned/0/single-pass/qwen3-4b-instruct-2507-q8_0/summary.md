**King's Scholar** refers to several distinct educational and academic programs across different institutions in the United Kingdom and the United States:

1. **King's Scholars at Eton College**: Selected through competitive examinations, these scholars are awarded a Foundation Scholarship and reside in the central "College" buildings. They wear a black academic gown and are known colloquially as "tugs." They share school life with Oppidans (non-scholar students) and participate in formal traditions, including meals in College Hall and the Wall Game. Historically, the scholarship was fully funded, but since the 1960s, means testing has been introduced.

2. **King's Scholars at Westminster School**: Named when a reigning King is in office, these scholars are selected through "The Challenge" examination and reside in the oldest house, College. They wear gowns during school services in Westminster Abbey.

3. **King's Scholars at King's College Cambridge**: Undergraduate students who achieve first-class degrees receive the title of King's Scholar, earning a £350 prize and attending a formal ceremony in King's College Chapel. They are entitled to a three-course dinner with their Directors of Studies.

4. **King's Scholars at the King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester)**: Established by Henry VIII in 1541, these schools award King's Scholarships based on academic or musical excellence. Originally all students were funded, but now the scholarship is awarded based on achievement and often includes a fee discount. At Ely, scholars are admitted in a special choral evensong and participate in traditions like the "Hoop Trundle."

These programs emphasize academic excellence, leadership, and community service, with distinct traditions and privileges tied to each institution.
