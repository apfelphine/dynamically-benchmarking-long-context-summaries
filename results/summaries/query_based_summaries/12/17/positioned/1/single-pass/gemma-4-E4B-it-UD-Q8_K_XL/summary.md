## King's Scholar

**Historical Origins and Purpose:**
The concept for a scholarship allowing students from Royal Frederik University (now the University of Oslo) to study one year at Wadham College in Oxford originated in 1919 through the efforts of Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne. The scholarship was established to foster closer educational association between the youth of Norway and Britain. For many years, Norwegian students were awarded the Norway Scholarship, though funding proved inadequate over time.

**King's Scholar in Different Institutions:**
The term "King's Scholar" is used in several contexts:
*   **Eton College:** A King's Scholar (also called a "Colleger") must pass the College Election examination and is admitted into the College house, consisting solely of King's Scholars aged 13 to 18. These scholars are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown.
*   **King's College Cambridge:** This term refers to undergraduates who attain first-class degrees, granting them a prize and the right to attend a formal ceremony in the King's College Chapel.
*   **The King's Schools (Canterbury, Ely, Worcester):** These schools award scholarships in the King's name, often recognizing academic or musical attainment. For instance, at King's Ely, scholars are admitted as members of the Cathedral foundation.
*   **Westminster School:** Foundation scholars are designated as Queen's Scholars when a Queen reigns, and King's Scholars when a King reigns.
