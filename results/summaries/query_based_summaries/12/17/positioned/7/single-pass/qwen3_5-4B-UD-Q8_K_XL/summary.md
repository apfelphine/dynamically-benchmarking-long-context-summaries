# King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" refers to students admitted to specific prestigious institutions under the patronage of a reigning monarch, with the most prominent example being the "Collegers" at Eton College. At Eton, a King's Scholar is a student who passes the College Election examination and receives a Foundation Scholarship, allowing them to live in the original central college buildings. There are approximately 70 scholars at any given time, aged 13 to 18, who are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown over their uniform. Historically, this scholarship provided a full waiver of fees, though since the 1960s it has been subject to means testing; as of 2021, the scholarship does not affect the size of fees, though part or all may be waived based on financial circumstances.

The title "King's Scholar" is also used at other institutions, including King's College Cambridge, Westminster School, and the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester). At King's College Cambridge, the term applies to undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, receiving a prize of £350 and the right to sign the King's Scholar book. At Westminster School, the title depends on whether there is a reigning King or Queen. Similarly, the King's Schools, re-founded by King Henry VIII, award scholarships in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts, though female scholars in the Lower Sixth at these schools are now titled "Queen's Scholars" in honor of the reigning monarch.
