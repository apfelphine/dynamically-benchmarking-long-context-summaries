## King's Scholar Summary

The term "King's Scholar" applies to several distinct institutions. At the University of Cambridge, the term refers to undergraduates who attain first-class degrees and receive a prize of £350, allowing them to attend a formal ceremony in the King's College Chapel.

At Eton College, a King's Scholar (or "Colleger") is one who has passed the College Election examination, been awarded a Foundation Scholarship, and admitted to the College house. Historically, this scholarship waived all school fees, though since the 1960s, it has been subject to means testing. Furthermore, the term is used for scholars at the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester and Worcester), where the scholarship is awarded in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a fee discount.
