The **King’s Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at Eton College, established in 1317 by King Edward II. These scholars are selected through a rigorous examination process and granted a Foundation Scholarship, allowing them to live in the historic "College" house, where they share a communal lifestyle with other King’s Scholars. The scholarship traditionally provides full or partial fee waivers, though financial support is now subject to means testing. King’s Scholars are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown and are part of a select group of students who receive specialized privileges, including access to unique traditions and academic opportunities.

The distinction is also recognized at other institutions, such as Westminster School and King’s College Cambridge, where scholars are awarded prizes and recognized for academic excellence. Notably, King’s Scholars have included prominent figures in politics, academia, and the arts, reflecting the program’s legacy of nurturing exceptional talent.
