The **King's Scholar** program is a prestigious academic distinction at **King’s College, Cambridge**, awarded to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees. This honor includes a prize of **£350** and the privilege of attending a ceremony in **King’s College Chapel**, where scholars sign their names in the **King’s Scholar Book**. The ceremony is followed by a formal dinner where scholars dine for free with their respective **Directors of Studies (DoS)**. The tradition stems from scholarships established by **King Henry VI** in 1440, originally for Eton College, though the modern King’s Scholar designation is specific to Cambridge’s King’s College.

Additionally, **King’s Scholars** at **Eton College** are distinguished students who pass the **College Election examination** and receive a **Foundation Scholarship**, living in the historic **College House** (often called "the Tugs") and participating in unique traditions like the **Wall Game**. Notable alumni include figures like **Boris Johnson** and **Harold Macmillan**. The program emphasizes academic excellence and long-standing tradition, reflecting its deep historical roots in British scholarship.
