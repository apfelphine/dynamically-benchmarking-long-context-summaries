# King's Scholar

The concept of a "King's Scholar" has historical applications across several institutions, with distinct traditions:

*   **University of Cambridge (King's College):** The term refers to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees. These scholars receive a prize of £350 and are entitled to attend a ceremony in the King's College Chapel, where they sign the King's Scholar book.
*   **Eton College:** At Eton, a King's Scholar (or "Colleger") is one who passes the College Election examination and is admitted into the "College" house. These students wear a black academic gown and remain in College for five years, though the scholarship is increasingly subject to means testing.
*   **The King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, etc.):** These seven schools, re-endowed by King Henry VIII, award King's Scholarships in recognition of academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a fee discount. At King's Ely, scholars are awarded based on GCSE grades and participate in a traditional "Hoop Trundle."

Additionally, the title "King's Scholar" is used by the **King's Schools at Westminster School** to denote foundation scholars, and historically, it was the designation for undergraduates at **King's College Cambridge** receiving a prize.
