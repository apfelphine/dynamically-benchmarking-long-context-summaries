The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic honor established historically at several institutions in the UK:

- At **Cambridge**, the first King's Scholars were established in 1317 by King Edward II, initially comprising 12 boys from his household.
- At **Eton College**, founded in 1440 by King Henry VI, King's Scholars (called "Colleger" or "tugs") are selected through a competitive examination and admitted to the original college buildings. They wear a black academic gown and are distinguished from fee-paying students, known as "Oppidans."
- At **Westminster School**, scholars are known as King's Scholars (or Queen's Scholars during a Queen's reign) and are selected through a competitive exam, residing in the oldest house, College.
- At **King's College Cambridge**, undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees receive the title of King's Scholar, with a £350 prize and a formal ceremony.
- At the **seven King's Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester), established by Henry VIII in 1541, King's Scholarships are awarded based on academic or musical excellence, often with a fee discount. These schools also grant Queen's Scholarships in honor of the reigning monarch.

Notable past King's Scholars include figures such as George Orwell, John Maynard Keynes, Boris Johnson, and Stephen Wolfram, reflecting the program’s legacy of fostering intellectual and public service excellence.
