The King's Scholar is a title awarded to students at several prestigious institutions in the UK, rooted in historical traditions. At Eton College, it is given to students who pass a competitive examination and are admitted to the oldest house, College, wearing a distinctive black gown. These students, known as "Collegers" or "tugs," share a unique school life with the rest of the students, including meals in College Hall and participation in traditions like the Wall Game. The scholarship, originally a full waiver of fees, now includes means testing, though it still offers financial support. At Westminster School, the title is similarly awarded to students who pass a competitive exam and are admitted to the College house, with the title changing to "Queen's Scholar" when a queen is reigning. At King's College Cambridge, the title is given to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, granting them a prize and a ceremony in the college chapel. The seven King's Schools—Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester—were re-founded by Henry VIII and award scholarships based on academic or musical achievement, often accompanied by a fee discount. These institutions honor their scholars with privileges such as burial rights in cathedral grounds and participation in traditional events like the Hoop Trundle at Ely. The title reflects a long-standing tradition of academic excellence and leadership, with many notable alumni in politics, arts, and public service.
