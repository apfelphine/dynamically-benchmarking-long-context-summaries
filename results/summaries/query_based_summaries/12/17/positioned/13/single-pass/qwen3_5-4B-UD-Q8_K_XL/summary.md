# King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" refers to students admitted to specific institutions in the United Kingdom and the United States under distinct historical and academic frameworks. In the United Kingdom, the title is most notably associated with **Eton College**, where a King's Scholar (often called a "Colleger" or "tug") is a student admitted to the school's oldest house, known as "College." These scholars, typically ranging in age from 13 to 18, are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown over their uniform and are the only students residing in the original central college buildings. They share life with Oppidans (fee-paying students) in lessons and sports but maintain separate dining arrangements in the College Hall and play the Wall Game throughout their five-year tenure. The scholarship, originally a full waiver of fees, has become means-tested since the 1960s, though King's Scholars retain the post-nominal letters "KS."

The term also applies to undergraduates at **King's College, Cambridge**, who receive a prize of £350 and attend a signing ceremony in the King's College Chapel upon achieving a first-class degree; this is a historical remnant of the college's founder, Henry VI. Additionally, the title is used at the **King's Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), which were re-founded by King Henry VIII. At these schools, King's Scholarships are awarded based on academic or musical attainment and often come with fee discounts. Notably, at King's Ely, King's Scholars are admitted alongside Queen's Scholars (awarded to girls) every year, while at King's School, Worcester, female scholars in the Upper Sixth are titled "Queen's Scholars." The title is also used at **Westminster School** for students admitted to the oldest house, known as "College," who wear gowns during services in Westminster Abbey.
