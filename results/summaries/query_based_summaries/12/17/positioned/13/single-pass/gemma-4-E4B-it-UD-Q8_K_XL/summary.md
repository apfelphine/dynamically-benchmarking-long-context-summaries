**King's Scholar** refers to a scholarship established by King Edward II at the University of Cambridge on July 7, 1317, initially providing 12 boys from the king's household with an education. The institution's history also includes King Henry VI establishing Eton College in 1440, which provided free education to "poor, scholarly boys," leading to the designation of King's Scholars.

At Eton College, a King's Scholar (or "Colleger") is one who has passed the College Election examination, receives a Foundation Scholarship, and is admitted to the original and oldest Eton house, "College." These scholars are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown and remain in College for five years. The term "King's Scholar" is also used for undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees, receiving a prize of £350. Furthermore, the term applies to award recipients at the seven King's Schools, which award scholarships in recognition of academic or musical attainment.
