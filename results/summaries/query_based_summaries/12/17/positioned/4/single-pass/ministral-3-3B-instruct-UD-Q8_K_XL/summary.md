The **King’s Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at **Eton College**, awarded to exceptional students who excel in scholarship and character. Established in **17th-century England**, the King’s Scholars are selected through competitive examinations and admitted to the oldest house at Eton, known as "College." They receive full scholarships, covering all school fees, and are distinguished by wearing a distinctive black academic gown. The tradition emphasizes intellectual excellence, moral integrity, and a commitment to service, with notable alumni spanning politics, academia, and the arts.

At **King’s College Cambridge**, a King’s Scholar is recognized for achieving a **first-class degree**, receiving a £350 prize and attending a formal ceremony in the college chapel. The distinction is also associated with the **King’s Schools** in Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester, where it signifies academic or musical excellence, often accompanied by fee discounts. The tradition reflects the historical legacy of King Henry VI, who endowed these institutions, and underscores a commitment to fostering outstanding scholars.
