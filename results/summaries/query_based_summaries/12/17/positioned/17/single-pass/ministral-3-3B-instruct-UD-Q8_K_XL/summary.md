The **King’s Scholar** program is a prestigious academic scholarship system rooted in historical traditions, primarily associated with **Eton College** and **King’s College Cambridge**, though it extends to other institutions like the **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester).

### **Eton College King’s Scholars**
- **Selection:** King’s Scholars at Eton are chosen through a competitive examination called the **College Election**, where students demonstrate academic excellence and character.
- **Admission:** Around **14 boys per year** are admitted at age 13, living in the **College** house—a traditional boarding area within the original college buildings. They wear a distinctive **black academic gown** over their school uniform.
- **Privileges:** Scholars enjoy unique privileges, including free meals in **College Hall**, participation in ancient traditions (e.g., playing the **Wall Game** for five years), and access to exclusive academic and social opportunities.
- **Financial Support:** Historically, the scholarship provided full tuition waivers, though modern scholarships may be means-tested. King’s Scholars are distinguished by the post-nominal letters **KS**.

### **King’s College Cambridge Scholars**
- **Selection:** Undergraduates at King’s College Cambridge who achieve **first-class degrees** are awarded the title of King’s Scholar.
- **Privileges:** They receive a **£350 prize**, attend a formal dinner in the **King’s Scholar Book**, and dine free with their Director of Studies. The tradition stems from scholarships endowed by King Henry VI.

### **King’s Schools (Canterbury, Ely, Worcester, etc.)**
- **Selection:** Scholarships are awarded based on **academic or musical attainment**, often accompanied by fee discounts. King’s Ely, for example, selects **12 boys and girls annually** from GCSE results, while King’s Worcester awards scholarships to high-achieving students in the Lower Remove or Sixth Form.
- **Privileges:** King’s Scholars at these schools gain access to **cathedral foundations**, wear special gowns, and have unique rights like marrying in the cathedral or being buried within its grounds.

The program emphasizes **academic excellence, tradition, and community**, with King’s Scholars at Eton and Cambridge being particularly renowned for their intellectual contributions and leadership roles.
