# King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" refers to a specific designation used in British educational institutions, primarily Eton College, Westminster School, and the historic King's Schools, to denote students admitted on full foundation scholarships. At Eton College, these students, known as "Collegers," are selected through a competitive examination called "The Challenge" and admitted to the school's oldest house, College, at age 13. They live in the ancient central buildings, share meals in College Hall, and are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown over their uniform, a tradition deriving from the Latin *Gens Togata* ("toga'd people"). Historically, this scholarship provided a full waiver of fees, though since the 1960s it has been subject to means testing. Other institutions, such as the King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), re-established these scholarships in 1541 by Henry VIII. While originally endowment-funded for all pupils, they now generally recognize academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts.

The designation also appears in other contexts within the British education system. At Westminster School, the term varies based on the reigning monarch: students are called "Queen's Scholars" during a reign of a Queen and "King's Scholars" during a reign of a King, both selected via competitive examination and wearing gowns at Abbey services. Similarly, at King's College Cambridge, the term "King's Scholar" is bestowed upon undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees, granting them a prize of £350, free dining in Michaelmas term, and the right to sign the King's Scholar book. In Worcester, the title depends on gender and academic entry; while all Lower Remove scholars are King's Scholars, female scholars entering the Lower Sixth are titled "Queen's Scholars" in honor of Elizabeth II. These distinctions highlight the enduring legacy of royal patronage in British education, linking academic excellence and scholarship directly to the monarchy.
