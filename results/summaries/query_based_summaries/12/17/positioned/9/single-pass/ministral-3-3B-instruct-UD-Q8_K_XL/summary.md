The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction associated with **Eton College** and **King’s College Cambridge**, though its origins trace back to **King Edward II’s establishment of the first King’s Scholars at Cambridge in 1317**. At Eton, a King’s Scholar (or "Colleger") is a student who has passed the College Election examination and is awarded a Foundation Scholarship, living in the oldest house, "College," from ages 13 to 18. These scholars are distinguished by a black academic gown and are granted privileges such as attending formal traditions not available to other students (Oppidans). The scholarship historically provided full tuition waivers but has increasingly been subject to financial means testing.

At **King’s College Cambridge**, the term "King’s Scholar" refers to undergraduates who achieve a **first-class degree**, receiving a £350 prize and attending a ceremony where they sign their name in the King’s Scholar book. This tradition stems from scholarships endowed by King Henry VI, linking Eton and Cambridge as sister colleges. The scholarship is not tied to a specific house but signifies academic excellence. Notably, King’s Scholars are also granted access to King’s College Chapel and formal dinners with their Directors of Studies.
