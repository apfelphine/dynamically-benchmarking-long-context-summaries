The **King's Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at **King's College, Cambridge**, awarded to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees. This honor confers a prize of £350 and grants access to a formal dinner with the college’s Directors of Studies, where scholars sign their names in the **King’s Scholar Book**. The tradition stems from scholarships established by **King Henry VI** in 1317, linking King’s College to the broader legacy of Cambridge’s academic excellence.

At **Eton College**, King’s Scholars (also called "Collegers") are selected through competitive examinations and distinguished by wearing a distinctive black academic gown. They reside in the oldest house, **College**, and share privileges such as exclusive dining in **College Hall** and participation in unique traditions like the **Wall Game**. Historically, the scholarship provided full fee waivers, though modern versions incorporate financial need assessments. Notable alumni include figures like **Boris Johnson**, **Harold Macmillan**, and **Robert Walpole**.

At **Westminster School**, King’s Scholars are similarly chosen through competitive exams, wear gowns during services in **Westminster Abbey**, and are part of the oldest house, **College**. The scholarship reflects the school’s long-standing tradition of academic excellence.

At **King’s Schools** (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester), King’s Scholarships are awarded based on academic or musical merit, often accompanied by fee discounts. These schools, re-endowed by **King Henry VIII**, grant Queen’s Scholarships to high-achieving female students, honoring the reigning monarch. Scholars at these institutions enjoy privileges like marrying in the cathedral and being buried within its grounds.
