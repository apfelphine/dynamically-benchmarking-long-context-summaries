The **King's Scholar** is a title awarded to students at several institutions in the UK, with distinct traditions and privileges:

1.  **Eton College:** A King's Scholar (or "Colleger") is selected through a competitive examination and admitted to the oldest house, College. They wear a black academic gown and are distinguished by their status, with about 70 scholars at any time. The title is a traditional "tug" (from Latin *Gens Togata*), and scholars are often involved in school traditions like the "Hoop Trundle."

2.  **Westminster School:** Scholars are called "Queen's Scholars" when a Queen is reigning and "King's Scholars" when a King is reigning. They are selected by a competitive exam ("The Challenge"), board at the oldest house (College), and wear gowns during services in Westminster Abbey.

3.  **King's College, Cambridge:** Undergraduates who achieve a first-class degree are awarded the title of King's Scholar. They receive a £350 prize and attend a ceremony in King's College Chapel, signing their name in the King's Scholar book.

4.  **The King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester):** These schools, re-endowed by Henry VIII, award King's Scholarships based on academic or musical attainment. Originally, all pupils were scholars; now, the title is awarded to high-achieving students, often with a fee discount. The title is sometimes accompanied by a special gown and privileges, such as the right to marry or be buried in the cathedral grounds.

Notable alumni from these programs include prominent figures in politics, science, arts, and business, such as Boris Johnson, John Maynard Keynes, and Stephen Wolfram.
