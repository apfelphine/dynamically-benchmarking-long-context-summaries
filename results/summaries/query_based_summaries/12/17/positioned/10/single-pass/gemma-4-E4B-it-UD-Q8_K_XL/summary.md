## King's Scholar

The term "King's Scholar" has several distinct meanings across different institutions:

*   **At Eton College:** A King's Scholar is a student who has passed the College Election examination and been awarded a Foundation Scholarship, granting admission to the original and oldest Eton house, "College." These scholars wear a black academic gown and are distinguished by the post-nominal letters KS.
*   **At King's College Cambridge:** This term refers to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees. These scholars receive a prize of £350 and are entitled to attend a formal ceremony in the King's College Chapel.
*   **At the King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester):** These schools award King's Scholarships in honor of King Henry VIII. The scholarship generally recognizes academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a reduction in school fees.

Historically, King Edward II established the first King's Scholars at the University of Cambridge in 1317. At Eton, the statutes provide for 70 King's Scholars, with about 14 being admitted annually at age 13.
