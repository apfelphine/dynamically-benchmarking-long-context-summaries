**King's Scholar** refers to several distinct programs and designations:

*   **University of Cambridge/Eton College:** King's Scholars were first established at the University of Cambridge by King Edward II in 1317, sending twelve boys to study there. Later, King Henry VI established Eton College, where a King's Scholar ("Colleger" or "tug") is awarded a Foundation Scholarship, gaining admission to the central College house, consisting solely of King's Scholars aged 13 to 18.
*   **King's College Cambridge:** The term also applies to undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees, entitling them to a prize and a formal dinner ceremony.
*   **The King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, etc.):** In these seven schools, the King's Scholarship recognizes academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a discount on fees. At King's Ely, the scholarship was extended to include high-achieving girls in 1973, and at King's School, Canterbury, scholars now wear a white gown to cathedral services.

In essence, the term denotes an honor bestowed through various historical institutions across England and Cambridge for scholarly or academic distinction.
