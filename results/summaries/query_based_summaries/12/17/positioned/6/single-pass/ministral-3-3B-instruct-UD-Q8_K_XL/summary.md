The **King’s Scholar** is a prestigious academic distinction at **King’s College, Cambridge**, awarded to undergraduates who achieve a **first-class degree**. Recipients receive a prize of £350 and are invited to a formal ceremony in King’s College Chapel, where they sign their name in the **King’s Scholar Book**. The ceremony includes a three-course dinner, during which scholars dine for free alongside their respective Directors of Studies.

This tradition stems from scholarships originally endowed by **King Henry VI**, who founded King’s College in 1478. The title reflects the college’s historical connection to royal patronage, though the modern King’s Scholar designation is distinct from the broader **King’s Scholars** at Eton College, where the term refers to students admitted to the oldest house through competitive examinations.
