The University of Cambridge, founded in 1209, developed from an intellectual center that attracted scholars fleeing Oxford. Its colleges, initially endowed fellowships, became a key feature of the institution, with the first being Peterhouse in 1284. The university formalized its structure with the Cambridge University Act 1856, which introduced subjects like theology, history, and modern languages.

Cambridge has achieved global leadership in various fields, notably mathematics through the highly competitive Mathematical Tripos. The institution's academic structure is collegiate, featuring 31 colleges, each self-governing, while central faculties and departments manage the overall academic program. The University of Cambridge has a distinguished history in politics, having educated numerous British Prime Ministers, and in science, with alumni including Isaac Newton and Charles Darwin.
