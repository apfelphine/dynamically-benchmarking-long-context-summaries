# Summary on University of Cambridge

The University of Cambridge, founded in 1209 by scholars migrating from Oxford, is renowned for its collegiate system comprising 31 self-governing colleges, each with distinct academic strengths and traditions. The university is organized into six broad schools encompassing over 150 faculties and departments, with teaching methods including small-group supervisions unique to Cambridge and Oxford. Its academic year is divided into three terms (Michaelmas, Lent, Easter), with degrees awarded at formal ceremonies managed by Regent House. Cambridge is a global leader in numerous disciplines, notably mathematics, physics, humanities, and life sciences, producing famous alumni such as Isaac Newton, Charles Darwin, Stephen Hawking, and multiple Nobel laureates.

Admissions to Cambridge are highly competitive, with rigorous requirements and a distinctive Winter Pool system to balance college allocations. The university actively engages in widening access through foundation courses for disadvantaged students. Its extensive library system includes Cambridge University Library and numerous faculty and college libraries, supporting research and scholarship. Cambridge maintains a significant cultural presence through museums, formal hall traditions, and numerous student societies including the prestigious Cambridge Union and Footlights comedy club. The university is an integral part of Cambridge city, known for its historic architecture and its role in fostering high-tech industries in the "Silicon Fen." It regularly ranks among the top universities worldwide and remains a central institution in British academia and beyond.
