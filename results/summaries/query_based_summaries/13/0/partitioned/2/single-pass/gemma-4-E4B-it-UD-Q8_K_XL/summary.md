The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university structured around 31 self-governing colleges, which provide housing, welfare, and undergraduate teaching, while the university centrally handles lectures, research, and degree awarding across more than 150 departments. The academic year is divided into three terms: Michaelmas (October-December), Lent (January-March), and Easter (April-June). Undergraduates are required to "keep term" within three miles of St Mary the Great's Church during eight-week full terms.

The university holds significant academic distinction, having produced alumni who include Isaac Newton and Charles Darwin. Its pedagogical system features unique supervisions, where small student groups meet weekly with teaching staff. Cambridge also maintains extensive resources, including 116 libraries, with Cambridge University Library holding over eight million volumes, and operates various specialized museums and a botanical garden. Furthermore, the University of Cambridge Press & Assessment is the world's oldest printer and publisher, and the institution has a strong reputation, being ranked highly globally as of 2025.
