## University of Cambridge

The University of Cambridge, founded in 1209, is a collegiate research university composed of 31 self-governing colleges, each with its own endowments, property, and teaching staff. Colleges provide housing, welfare, social functions, and undergraduate supervision, often via small group supervisions unique to Cambridge and Oxford. The university oversees academic programs, conducts research through over 150 departments and faculties grouped into six broad schools, and provides central administration led by the Chancellor and Vice-Chancellor. Cambridge has a total endowment (excluding colleges) of approximately £3.7 billion, with the 31 colleges collectively holding about £4.6 billion. It is a member of prestigious groups like the Russell Group and the League of European Research Universities, and is closely linked to the Silicon Fen high-tech cluster.

Academically renowned for its Mathematical Tripos and strong emphasis on mathematics and sciences, Cambridge offers a rigorous education with a strong tutorial/supervision system. Its academic year is divided into three terms, with particular traditions such as Formal Halls and May Balls celebrated by students. The university maintains numerous libraries, including the Cambridge University Library with over eight million volumes, and eight museums. It publishes through Cambridge University Press & Assessment, one of the world’s oldest and largest academic presses. Cambridge ranks consistently among the top global universities, noted especially for arts and humanities, engineering, natural sciences, and social sciences. Its alumni and faculty include 70 Nobel laureates, 11 Fields Medalists, and numerous leaders in politics, science, and the arts. The university continues to adapt through initiatives to widen access and address campus issues such as sexual harassment and academic freedom.
