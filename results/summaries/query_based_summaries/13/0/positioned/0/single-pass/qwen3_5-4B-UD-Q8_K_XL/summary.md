The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university founded in 1209, established by scholars fleeing political instability at Oxford. Its structure consists of 31 self-governing colleges, with Peterhouse being the oldest, founded in 1284. Historically focused on mathematics, classics, and canon law, the curriculum expanded under Prince Albert to include modern languages, history, and natural sciences. The university is renowned for its high standards in mathematics, particularly the highly competitive Mathematical Tripos, and has produced numerous Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists, and influential figures in science, politics, and literature. It holds parliamentary representation and maintains a strong economic impact through the "Cambridge Phenomenon," a cluster of high-tech and biotech companies.

Academic life at Cambridge is characterized by the "term system," where students must reside within three miles of the city center for nine terms during their undergraduate studies, and by the traditional "supervision" system, involving small-group weekly tutoring. The university is the only UK institution to still operate female-only colleges (Girton and Newnham). While it has faced criticism regarding sexual harassment and diversity, it continues to admit women in significant numbers, with a nearly balanced gender ratio among its total student population. The campus, centered around the River Cam, features historic buildings like King's College Chapel and houses major research facilities, including the Cavendish Laboratory and the University Library, which holds over eight million volumes.

In terms of administration, the university is governed by Regent House, comprising the Chancellor, High Steward, and senior academic staff, which holds ultimate authority over policy. The Chancellor serves a ceremonial role, while the Vice-Chancellor acts as the principal administrative officer. Financially, the university is supported by a substantial endowment, research grants, and tuition fees. It is a member of the Russell Group and the G5, ranking highly in global university league tables. The university has a complex history with the city of Cambridge, known as the "town and gown" relationship, which has shifted from frequent conflict to a symbiotic economic partnership. Notable alumni include Isaac Newton, Charles Darwin, Stephen Hawking, and many British Prime Ministers.
