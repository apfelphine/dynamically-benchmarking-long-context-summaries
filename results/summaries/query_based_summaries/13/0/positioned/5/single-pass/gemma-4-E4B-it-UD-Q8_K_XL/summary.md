The University of Cambridge was founded in 1209, inspired by scholars fleeing conflict from the University of Oxford. Its structure is collegiate, meaning its 31 colleges are self-governing, holding their own endowments and property, although the faculties, departments, and research centers belong to the university. The university is legally structured as an exempt charity and a common law corporation, with its central administration overseen by the Chancellor and the Vice-Chancellor, who serves as the de facto principal administrative officer.

Cambridge has historically excelled in mathematics, with its Mathematical Tripos examination producing famous scientists. The university is a member of the Russell Group, the G5, and the League of European Research Universities. Academically, it houses faculties across disciplines, including Arts and Humanities, Biological Sciences, Clinical Medicine, and Physical Sciences. Furthermore, the university is renowned for its strong traditions, such as the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, and its extensive collection of libraries and museums.
