The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university, legally structured as an exempt charity and a common law corporation. Its 31 colleges are self-governing institutions, each possessing its own endowment and property, which provide housing, welfare, and undergraduate teaching. While faculties and departments handle lectures, research, and degree awarding, undergraduate supervision is provided within the colleges through small group teaching sessions.

The university has historically emphasized mathematics, maintaining the highly competitive Mathematical Tripos, which produced prominent figures like James Clerk Maxwell and G. H. Hardy. Modern periods saw curriculum expansion beyond mathematics and classics, with Prince Albert reforming the curriculum to include modern era history and the natural sciences. Cambridge maintains numerous specialized academic bodies, including six main schools (Arts and Humanities, Biological Sciences, etc.), and is a member of the Russell Group. Its prestige is reflected in its world rankings, with its arts and humanities and engineering programs ranked among the world's best.
