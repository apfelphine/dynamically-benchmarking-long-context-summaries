The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university located in Cambridge, England, with a history dating back to 1209. It is renowned for its academic excellence, particularly in mathematics, sciences, and humanities, and is considered one of the world's most prestigious institutions. The university is composed of 31 colleges, each with its own governance, endowments, and student community, and operates under a central administration led by the Chancellor and Vice-Chancellor. The university's academic structure includes 150 departments and faculties, with teaching organized through small-group supervisions and a rigorous curriculum, including the competitive Mathematical Tripos. Research is a core strength, with significant contributions in fields such as physics, biology, and computer science, and the university is a member of the Russell Group and the League of European Research Universities.

Cambridge has a long-standing tradition of producing Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists, and influential figures in science, politics, literature, and the arts. Its student life includes formal halls, May Balls, and a wide range of societies and clubs, with student representation through JCRs and MCRs. The university emphasizes academic integrity through its Honor Code and has a strong tradition of public engagement, including debates, research, and public lectures. It has faced scrutiny over historical ties to eugenics and sexual misconduct, leading to institutional reforms and apologies. Cambridge is also recognized globally for its research output, with high rankings in world university surveys, and continues to expand through new campuses and research initiatives.
