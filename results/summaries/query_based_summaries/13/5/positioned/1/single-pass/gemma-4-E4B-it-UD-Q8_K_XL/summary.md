University College London (UCL) was founded on February 11, 1826, as London University, an alternative to Oxford and Cambridge. In 1830, the London University School was established, which would later become University College School. UCL was incorporated by royal charter as University College, London on November 28, 1836.

The college gained significant milestones, including admitting women to the faculties of Arts and Law and Science in 1878. Following the reconstitution of the University of London in 1898, UCL merged into the university in 1907, formally becoming University College London. UCL was granted its own taught and research degree awarding powers in 2005 and adopted the initialism UCL for external communications. The university continues to expand, with new campuses such as UCL East in Stratford, and maintains strong affiliations, being a founding member of the Russell Group.
