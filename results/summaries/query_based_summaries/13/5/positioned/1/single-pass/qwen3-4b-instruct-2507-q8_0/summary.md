University College London (UCL) was founded in 1826 as London University, a joint-stock company without legal university status or the right to award degrees. It gained recognition as a college under a royal charter in 1836, becoming University College, London. In 1907, it merged with the University of London, losing its legal independence but gaining greater influence. In 1976, it regained its legal independence under a new charter, officially becoming University College London. UCL is a member of the University of London and the Russell Group, and has expanded through mergers, including with the Institute of Archaeology, the Institute of Laryngology & Otology, and the Royal Free Hospital Medical School. 

UCL's main campus is located in Bloomsbury, with a secondary campus, UCL East, in Stratford. The university has a strong focus on research, with a significant income from research grants and contracts, and is ranked among the top universities globally. UCL has a diverse student body, with a high proportion of international students and a strong commitment to sustainability, including a strategy to achieve net zero carbon emissions by 2030. It has also addressed historical issues, including its role in eugenics, and has established initiatives to promote diversity and inclusion. The university is known for its academic excellence, with a strong reputation in fields such as medicine, engineering, and the sciences.
