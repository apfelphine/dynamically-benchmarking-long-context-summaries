University College London (UCL) was established on February 11, 1826, as London University, initially operating as a joint stock company to provide an alternative to the Anglican universities of Oxford and Cambridge. Although Jeremy Bentham is often regarded as its "spiritual father," UCL was incorporated by royal charter as University College, London, on November 28, 1836.

Historically, UCL gained the right to award degrees to women in 1878, admitting women to the faculties of Arts, Law, and Science, though medical and engineering faculties remained barred initially. Following the University of London's reconstitution in 1909, UCL merged into the university. In 2005, UCL was granted its own taught and research degree-awarding powers. UCL maintains its primary campus in Bloomsbury, with a second campus, UCL East, located at the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park in Stratford. The university is a founding member of the Russell Group and the League of European Research Universities.
