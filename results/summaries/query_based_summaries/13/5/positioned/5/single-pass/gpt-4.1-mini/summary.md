**University College London (UCL)** was founded on 11 February 1826 as London University, an alternative to the Anglican universities of Oxford and Cambridge. Initially a joint stock company without university status or degree-awarding powers, it became University College, London in 1836 when incorporated under a royal charter. UCL was a founding college of the University of London and pioneered admitting women to faculties like Arts, Law, and Science in 1878, although medicine and engineering admitted women later. Over time, UCL expanded by merging with various specialized institutes and medical schools, and it regained legal independence in 1976.

In 2005, UCL gained its own degree-awarding powers and adopted the initialism “UCL” for branding. It launched UCL East in Stratford as a new campus from 2018 onward and has been investing heavily in its Bloomsbury campus. UCL is a major research institution with an annual income exceeding £2 billion, strong global partnerships (including a significant collaboration with Yale University), and membership in leading academic groups like the Russell Group and the League of European Research Universities. UCL houses eleven faculties covering arts, sciences, medicine, engineering, and social sciences with many notable research centers. The university maintains extensive library collections, several museums, and student accommodations for approximately 7,000 residents.  

UCL is highly selective with a diverse student body of around 47,000 students, including a majority of international students and a female majority. It runs widening access programs and contextual offers to promote inclusion. The student union, founded in 1893, supports a wide range of societies and sports clubs. UCL has a secular tradition but provides multi-faith facilities and chaplaincy. Notable alumni include Francis Crick (DNA co-discoverer), Joseph Lister (surgery pioneer), and Peter Higgs (Higgs boson theorist). UCL academics and alumni have been awarded 33 Nobel Prizes and 3 Fields Medals. The university has also faced and addressed historic links with eugenics, and recent student activism includes protests related to geopolitical issues.
