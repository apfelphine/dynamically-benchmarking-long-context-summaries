University College London (UCL) was founded in 1826 as London University, an alternative to Oxford and Cambridge, and gained university status in 1836 under the name University College, London. It became a school of the University of London in 1907 and, in 1976, was granted formal independence as University College London. UCL achieved full degree-awarding powers in 2005 and was granted university status in 2023. The university is primarily based in the Bloomsbury area of Central London, with a second campus, UCL East, in Stratford. It operates across eleven faculties and has a strong research profile, with significant contributions in fields such as neuroscience, engineering, and medicine. UCL is known for its diverse student body, with a majority of international students and a high proportion of female students. It has a long-standing commitment to inclusivity, including a history of student activism, diversity initiatives, and efforts to address historical ties to eugenics. UCL is globally ranked among the top universities, consistently appearing in the top 25 in major international rankings, and is recognized for its sustainability efforts, research output, and impact on the UK economy.
