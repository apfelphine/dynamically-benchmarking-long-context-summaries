## University College London Summary

University College London (UCL) began as London University on February 11, 1826, established as an alternative to Oxford and Cambridge. While Jeremy Bentham is widely regarded as its "spiritual father," its initial form was a joint stock company. Classes in medicine started in 1828, and the institution was incorporated by royal charter as University College, London, on November 28, 1836, when it became affiliated with the University of London.

UCL underwent significant changes, including the foundation of the Slade School of Fine Art in 1871 and the admission of women to the faculties of Arts and Law and Science in 1878. Following the University of London's reconstitution, UCL merged into the university in 1907, formally becoming University College London. In 1976, a new charter restored its legal independence, and in 2005, UCL gained its own taught and research degree awarding powers. Today, UCL is primarily based in Bloomsbury, Central London, and also operates a second campus, UCL East, at Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park.
