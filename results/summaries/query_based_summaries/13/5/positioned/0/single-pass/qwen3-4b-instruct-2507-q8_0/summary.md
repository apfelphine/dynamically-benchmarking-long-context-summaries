**University College London (UCL)** is a leading research university in the UK, founded in 1826 as London University, with a mission to provide education outside the Anglican-dominated Oxford and Cambridge universities. Initially a joint-stock company without degree-awarding powers, it gained royal charter status in 1836 as University College, London, becoming affiliated with the University of London. In 1900, it merged with the University of London, and in 1976, it regained legal independence under the name University College London (UCL). In 2005, UCL was granted full degree-awarding powers, and its branding was simplified to use the initialism "UCL."

UCL's main campus is located in Bloomsbury, central London, with additional sites including UCL East in Stratford and other facilities in central London and beyond. The university is organized into eleven faculties and maintains a large library system, including the UCL Main Library and specialized collections. UCL has a strong research profile, with significant research income and a high proportion of world-leading research (4* ratings) in the 2021 Research Excellence Framework (REF). It is known for its contributions to science, medicine, and engineering, including the development of key technologies such as the first programmable computer and the eugenics laboratory.

UCL is highly selective, with an average entry tariff of 189 UCAS points. It is ranked among the top universities in the UK and globally, with a strong reputation in research, teaching, and student life. The university is known for its diverse student body, with over 40% international students, and has a long history of student activism, including protests over tuition fees, housing, and social justice issues. UCL has also faced scrutiny over its historical ties to eugenics and controversial events, leading to formal apologies and internal reviews.

The university is governed by a council and academic board, with the President and Provost as the chief academic and administrative officer. It has a strong commitment to sustainability, with initiatives such as the Positive Climate and Wild Bloomsbury programs, and has been recognized for its environmental efforts. UCL has a rich history of notable alumni and faculty, including Nobel laureates, and is involved in global partnerships and research collaborations. The student life at UCL is vibrant, featuring a large student union, numerous societies, and a strong tradition of activism and social engagement.
