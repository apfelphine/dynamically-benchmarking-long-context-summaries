University College London (UCL), founded in 1826, was established as an alternative to the Anglican universities of Oxford and Cambridge, initially operating as a joint-stock company before gaining full university status in 2023. Located primarily in Bloomsbury, Central London, UCL is a federal university comprising eleven faculties, including the Bartlett (Built Environment), Brain Sciences, and Medical Sciences, alongside numerous schools, departments, and institutes. The institution is renowned for its research output, having been ranked second for research power and market share by Times Higher Education and Research Professional News, and is a founding member of the Russell Group and the 'golden triangle' of UK research-intensive universities.

UCL has a significant history of innovation, playing a pivotal role in the early development of the Internet and establishing the first international node of the ARPANET. Its campus features historic buildings designed by William Wilkins and Alfred Waterhouse, as well as modern developments such as UCL East in Stratford and the UCL Australia campus in Adelaide. The university is committed to sustainability, having achieved BREEAM Outstanding certification for its Student Centre and set targets for net-zero carbon emissions by 2030. While it maintains a diverse student body with a high proportion of international students, particularly from Asia, UCL has faced criticism and legal challenges regarding sexual harassment, eugenics history, and recent protests over the Gaza war and housing costs.
