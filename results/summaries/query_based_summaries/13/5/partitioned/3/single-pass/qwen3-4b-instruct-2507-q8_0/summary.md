**University College London (UCL)** is a leading research university in the UK, established in 1826 as a secular alternative to Oxford and Cambridge. It gained full university status in 2023 after being granted a supplemental charter, becoming a de facto independent university within the University of London federation. UCL's main campus is in Bloomsbury, London, with a second campus, UCL East, located in Stratford, opened in 2022. The university operates 26 halls of residence, guaranteeing accommodation for first-year students and care-leavers, and offers a wide range of student housing options.

UCL is known for its commitment to widening access, offering contextual offers through programs like Access UCL and a foundation year for disadvantaged students. It also provides intensive preparatory courses for international students and operates a student-led summer school for underprivileged applicants. The university is recognized for its diverse student body, with a significant international presence, and has a strong reputation in research, ranked among the top 25 globally in several rankings, including the QS World University Rankings and Times Higher Education.

Academically, UCL is organized into eleven faculties and over 150 departments, with a strong focus on interdisciplinary research and teaching. It has a robust research portfolio, generating over £538 million in research income in 2023/24, and holds the second-largest patent portfolio in the UK. UCL's research centers include the Sainsbury Wellcome Centre for Neural Circuits and Behaviour and the Centre for the Study of the Legacies of British Slave-ownership. The university is also a major contributor to innovation, with UCL Business supporting spin-off companies and intellectual property licensing.

Student life at UCL is vibrant, featuring over 250 clubs and societies, a strong student union, and a range of sports and cultural activities. The Students' Union, founded in 1893, provides services such as clubs, societies, sports facilities, and advice. UCL has a long-standing tradition of student activism, including protests on issues such as climate change, social justice, and university policies. The university has also faced scrutiny over historical ties to eugenics and slavery, leading to formal apologies and the renaming of buildings.

UCL's governance structure includes a council and academic board, with the President and Provost as the principal officer. The university emphasizes sustainability, with initiatives such as the Positive Climate and The Loop programs, and is recognized as a leader in environmental responsibility. UCL is a member of the Russell Group, the League of European Research Universities, and the U7+ Alliance, and has partnerships with institutions globally, including Yale University.
