University College London (UCL) was originally founded in 1826 as London University, initially as a joint stock company, and was commonly regarded as having Jeremy Bentham as its "spiritual father." The institution was incorporated by royal charter as University College, London, on November 28, 1836, after accepting a government proposal to become a college under the University of London examining body.

UCL underwent significant evolution, merging into the University of London in 1907 and formally adopting the name University College London after gaining legal independence in 1976. The university has expanded its presence through new campuses, such as UCL East at the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park in Stratford, and maintains a global reach through partnerships. Academically, UCL is recognized internationally, holding positions in the top rankings of major global university league tables.
