University College London (UCL), founded in 1826 as London University, was established as an alternative to the Anglican universities of Oxford and Cambridge. Initially operating without legal recognition, it gained university status in 1836 by joining the University of London. UCL historically struggled with admitting women, which was not permitted until 1878 in Arts, Law, and Science, though full access to Medicine and Engineering occurred later during the World War I era. The institution underwent significant structural changes, merging with other institutes and hospitals to form UCL Medical School and UCL Hospitals NHS Trust, before regaining its legal independence in 1976. It eventually achieved full university status in 2023, granting it the power to award its own taught and research degrees.

UCL is primarily located in the Bloomsbury area of Central London, with a second major campus, UCL East, situated in the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park in Stratford. The university is organized into eleven faculties and maintains extensive partnerships globally, including affiliations with institutions in Australia, Asia, and North America. It is a founding member of the Russell Group and the Alan Turing Institute, and is recognized for its strong research output, particularly in biomedical sciences and engineering. UCL has a diverse student body with a significant international population, notably from China, and has implemented various widening access schemes to support disadvantaged applicants. The university also operates several notable museums, including the Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology and the Grant Museum of Zoology, and is renowned for its historical contributions to science, medicine, and the early development of the internet.
