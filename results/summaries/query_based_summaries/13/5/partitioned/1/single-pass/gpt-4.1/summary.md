## University College London (UCL) – Summary

University College London (UCL), founded in 1826 as "London University," is a prominent public research university located in the Bloomsbury area of central London. Established as a secular alternative to Oxford and Cambridge, UCL was a pioneer in allowing students of all religious backgrounds to study together and, later, was among the first institutions in Britain to admit women to higher education. Over time, UCL became a constituent college of the University of London and grew substantially through mergers and expansions. In 2023, it was granted independent university status while remaining within the federal University of London.

UCL now comprises eleven faculties and hosts nearly 47,000 students, with a large international community. The campus features historic and award-winning buildings, with a significant presence in Bloomsbury and a new UCL East campus in Stratford. UCL is globally recognized for its research excellence, ranking second in Britain for research power in the latest REF, and offers extensive resources such as 18 libraries, notable museums, and the country's first fully open access university press. The university boasts 33 Nobel laureates among its alumni and staff, and its notable figures include Francis Crick, Joseph Lister, and Mahatma Gandhi. UCL’s student life is vibrant, with over 250 clubs, strong sports traditions, active student union campaigns, and a commitment to widening participation and diversity.
