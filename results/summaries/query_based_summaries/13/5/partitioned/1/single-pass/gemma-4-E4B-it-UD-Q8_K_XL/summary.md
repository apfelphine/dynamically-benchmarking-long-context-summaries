University College London (UCL) was established on February 11, 1826, initially as London University, to provide an alternative to the Anglican universities of Oxford and Cambridge. It became formally incorporated as University College, London, in 1836, and later became a school of the University of London. UCL gained its own taught and research degree-awarding powers in 2005 and was officially granted university status in 2023.

UCL has multiple campus locations, including the historic Bloomsbury area, which features notable buildings like the grade I listed UCL Main Building. It also maintains a second campus, UCL East, in Stratford, east London, and has associated sites elsewhere in Central London. The university emphasizes environmental initiatives through its Strategy for Sustainable UCL, which includes goals for carbon neutrality by 2030. In terms of research, UCL was ranked highly for both research power and market share in 2023/24, and it operates several key research centers, such as the Sainsbury Wellcome Centre for Neural Circuits and Behaviour.
