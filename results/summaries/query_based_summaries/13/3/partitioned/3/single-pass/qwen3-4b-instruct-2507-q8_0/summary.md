The University of Edinburgh is a prominent Scottish institution with a rich history dating back to 1583, when it opened as Tounis College. It evolved into a leading research university, recognized for its contributions to science, medicine, and academia. The university is organized into three colleges—Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Science and Engineering; and Medicine and Veterinary Medicine—each with multiple schools and research centers. It operates across several campuses, including the Central Area, King's Buildings, BioQuarter, Easter Bush, and Western General, housing key academic and medical facilities. The university is governed by the University Court, General Council, and Senatus Academicus, with a principal, rector, and chancellor serving as key officials. 

Student life is vibrant, featuring active student unions, societies, and sports, including the Scottish Boat Race and the Scottish Varsity rugby match. The university is known for its strong student activism, with groups like People & Planet and the Edinburgh University Students' Association organizing protests on issues such as divestment from arms and fossil fuel companies. In 2024, a student-led protest camp in Old College demanded divestment from companies linked to Israel's actions in Gaza, leading to a university review of investment policies. 

The university has produced numerous notable figures in science, medicine, literature, and politics, including Sir James Black, Sir Robert Robinson, and Sir Charles Murray, and is associated with foundational scientific discoveries such as the structure of DNA and the development of antiseptic surgery. It is also a member of the Russell Group and the Coimbra Group, and has a strong international reputation, with over 300 partner institutions globally. The university's academic profile includes high research output, with 90% of its research judged as world-leading or internationally excellent, and is ranked among the top universities in the world in various subject-specific rankings.
