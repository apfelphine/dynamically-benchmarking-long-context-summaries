The University of Edinburgh, established in 1582 as a secular institution, is a historic research university in Scotland known for its strong ties to the Scottish Enlightenment and its influential medical school, which founded medical programs at major US universities. The university is organized into three colleges—Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Science and Engineering; and Medicine and Veterinary Medicine—and is a founding member of the Russell Group. Its campuses include the Central Area, King's Buildings, the BioQuarter, Easter Bush, and the Western General site, featuring notable buildings such as the Main Library, McEwan Hall, and the Roslin Institute, where researchers created the first cloned mammal, Dolly the sheep.

Student life at Edinburgh is vibrant and politically active, characterized by significant student activism, the presence of various campaigning societies, and three student-run co-operatives focused on housing, food, and sustainability. The university boasts a distinguished alumni network, including Nobel Prize laureates, Olympic gold medalists, and prominent figures in politics, arts, and science. Academically, it is highly ranked globally, particularly in subjects like Nursing, Veterinary Medicine, and Computer Science, and maintains strong international collaborations and research partnerships.
