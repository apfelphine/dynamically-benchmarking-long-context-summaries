The University of Edinburgh, founded in 1583 as Tounis College, is the oldest university in Scotland. It began as a secular institution established by the Edinburgh Town Council, with a focus on liberal arts and divinity. The college was named after the town, and its early leadership included Robert Rollock, who served as principal and taught the first cohort. In 1617, King James VI visited and renamed it "King James' College." After a period of conflict with the town council, the university adopted a Dutch-style system of professorships in the 18th century, leading to the formal establishment of faculties such as Law, Arts, and Medicine. The university became a center of the Scottish Enlightenment, producing key figures like Adam Smith, David Hume, and Joseph Black. In the 19th century, it expanded its facilities and established the first department of nursing in Europe in 1955. 

The university's campus spans five main sites in Edinburgh, including the Central Area, King's Buildings, BioQuarter, Easter Bush, and Western General. Notable buildings include Old College, the oldest on campus, and the newly constructed Chancellor's Building in the BioQuarter. The university is known for its strong research output, with over 20 Nobel laureates affiliated with it, and is a leading institution in medical research and education. It is also renowned for its contributions to science, including the discovery of chloroform, the development of antiseptic surgery, and the creation of the first medical school in the English-speaking world.

The university is governed by a tripartite structure: the University Court, the General Council, and the Senatus Academicus. The Chancellor, Rector, and Principal are key leadership roles, with the Principal serving as the chief executive. The university has a diverse student body, with over 7,000 students enrolled annually, and is known for its student activism, including campaigns for social justice, environmental sustainability, and divestment from companies linked to controversial policies. The university has a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary research and is a member of the Russell Group and the Coimbra Group. 

The University of Edinburgh is ranked among the top universities globally, with high research output and strong international reputation. It has a long-standing tradition of academic excellence, with a strong focus on teaching and research, and has produced numerous influential alumni in science, politics, and the arts. The university is committed to sustainability and has implemented a range of environmental initiatives, including a zero-waste program and a carbon-neutral campus goal. It also has a strong presence in public health and medical research, with a focus on innovation and collaboration. The university continues to expand its global reach through international partnerships and exchange programs.
