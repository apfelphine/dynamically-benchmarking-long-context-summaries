The University of Edinburgh was established via a royal charter on April 14, 1582, after earlier efforts to build a college. It opened its doors on October 14, 1583, initially covering liberal arts and divinity. The university gradually assumed the name "University of Edinburgh" during the 17th century.

Key developments include the restructuring in 1708, which established a Dutch-style system of Professors, and the founding of faculties like Law (1707), Arts (1708), and Medicine (1726). During the 18th century, it was a center of the Scottish Enlightenment, attracting figures such as Adam Smith and David Hume. After 275 years of town council governance, the Universities (Scotland) Act 1858 gave the university full authority over its own affairs. The university is organized into three Colleges: Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences (CAHSS), Science & Engineering (CSE), and Medicine & Vet Medicine (CMVM).
