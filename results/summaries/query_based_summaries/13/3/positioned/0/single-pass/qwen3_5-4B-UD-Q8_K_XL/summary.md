The University of Edinburgh, founded in 1582 as Tounis College and renamed King James' College, is Scotland's oldest university and one of the world's leading research institutions. Established by royal charter through the efforts of the Edinburgh Town Council, it was unique for its time as a secular institution founded by municipal authorities rather than the Church. Throughout its history, the university has been a central hub of the Scottish Enlightenment, producing key figures such as Adam Smith, David Hume, and Joseph Black, and is renowned for its medical school, which has influenced medical education globally. The institution expanded significantly in the 20th century with the development of satellite campuses like King's Buildings, the BioQuarter, and Easter Bush, and recently added the Moray House School of Education and Sport after merging the Moray House Institute in 2019.

Currently, the University of Edinburgh is organized into three Colleges—Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Science & Engineering; and Medicine & Vet Medicine—and maintains five main campuses in Edinburgh, including the historic Central Area and the modern King's Buildings. It is a founding member of the Russell Group and holds a top-tier ranking globally, often cited as one of the best universities in the world for research power. The university boasts a diverse student body with significant international representation and has a rich tradition of student life, including the oldest purpose-built student union in the world, Teviot Row House, and the oldest student newspaper, The Student. Its alumni include numerous Nobel Prize winners, heads of state, and influential figures in science, politics, and the arts, cementing its status as a prestigious global academic center.
