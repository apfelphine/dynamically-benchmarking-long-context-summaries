## University of Edinburgh

The University of Edinburgh was founded in 1583 as Tounis College by secular authorities, receiving a royal charter in 1582. Initially focused on liberal arts and divinity, it evolved through the 17th and 18th centuries amid political and religious upheavals, notably participating in the Scottish Enlightenment with figures like Adam Smith, David Hume, and Joseph Black. The university expanded substantially with the establishment of faculties of Law, Arts, and Medicine and gained full governance independence in 1858. It pioneered women's higher education with the Edinburgh Seven in 1869, who campaigned despite institutional resistance. The university expanded its medical and scientific reputation internationally, founding various schools and campuses, including the BioQuarter and Easter Bush for medical and veterinary studies. 

Today, the university is organized into three colleges encompassing 21 schools covering arts, sciences, medicine, and veterinary medicine, with notable centers like the Roslin Institute (famous for cloning Dolly the sheep). Its principal campuses include the historic Central Area, King's Buildings for science and engineering, and specialized medical campuses. Governed by a University Court, General Council, and Senatus Academicus, it is chaired by Chancellor Princess Anne and led by Principal Sir Peter Mathieson. The university has strong research output, ranking highly nationally and internationally, and boasts over 12,600 staff. It maintains large library collections, offers extensive student exchange programs, and hosts a vibrant student life with numerous cultural, sporting, and activist groups. Edinburgh’s alumni and faculty have contributed significantly to diverse fields such as medicine, economics, literature, and science, with 20 Nobel laureates affiliated. The university also sees active student engagement in political and ethical causes, including recent protests over investments linked to the Gaza conflict.
