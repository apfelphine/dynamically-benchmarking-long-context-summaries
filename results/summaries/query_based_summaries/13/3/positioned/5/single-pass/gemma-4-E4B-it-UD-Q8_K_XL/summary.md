The University of Edinburgh has a history stretching back to 1557, when Bishop Robert Reid endowed a college in Edinburgh, which eventually received a royal charter in 1582. Named Tounis College, it opened in 1583 and initially focused on liberal arts and divinity. Its early development was marked by a power struggle between the university and the town council, which was gradually resolved by the Universities (Scotland) Act 1858, granting the university full autonomy.

Historically, the university was a center of the Scottish Enlightenment, producing key figures such as Adam Smith, David Hume, and Joseph Black, and was recognized as one of the world's preeminent scientific institutions. The Edinburgh Seven, the first matriculated female undergraduates at any British university, began studying medicine there in 1869. Today, the university is organized into three Colleges: Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences (CAHSS), Science & Engineering (CSE), and Medicine & Vet Medicine (CMVM). It is a member of the Russell Group, and its research capacity is highly recognized, with 90% of its research activity judged to be 'world leading' or 'internationally excellent' in the 2021 REF.
