The University of Edinburgh, founded in 1580, is a leading research university in the UK with a strong emphasis on science, medicine, and the humanities. It has a historic campus in Edinburgh, including Old College, the Main Library, and the McEwan Hall, and a modern BioQuarter medical campus. The university is organized into three colleges—Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Science and Engineering; and Medicine and Veterinary Medicine—each with distinct academic strengths. It is a member of the Russell Group and the Coimbra Group, and has a strong reputation in research, particularly in medicine and the natural sciences, with 90% of its research rated as world-leading or internationally excellent. The university is known for its notable alumni, including Nobel laureates, and has contributed to major scientific advancements such as antiseptic surgery, evolution, and the structure of DNA. Student life includes a vibrant student union, debate societies, performing arts, and activism, with student protests over issues like investment in arms and divestment from Israel. The university is also recognized for its environmental initiatives and sustainability efforts, and its student body is diverse, with a significant proportion of international students. The university has a long-standing tradition of academic excellence, with a rigorous teaching model and a strong focus on research and innovation.
