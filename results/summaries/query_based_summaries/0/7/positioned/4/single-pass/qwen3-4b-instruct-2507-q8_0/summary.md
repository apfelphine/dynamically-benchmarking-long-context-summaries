**Frankenstein** is a novel by Mary Shelley, first published in 1818. It tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who, driven by ambition and a desire to conquer life, creates a sentient creature from assembled body parts and electricity. Repelled by his creation, Victor abandons it, leading the creature to suffer isolation, rejection, and a profound sense of alienation. The creature, who learns to read and speak and develops a deep emotional capacity, eventually seeks revenge on Victor, leading to a tragic cycle of violence and loss. The novel explores profound themes of creation, identity, guilt, and the consequences of playing God, with the creature often seen as a tragic figure akin to Adam or a fallen angel, in contrast to Victor, who embodies the hubris of scientific ambition. The story is set in the early 19th century, with elements of Romanticism and Gothic fiction, and has since become a foundational work in science fiction and literature.
