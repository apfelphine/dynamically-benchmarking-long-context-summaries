**Frankenstein** is a novel centered on Victor Frankenstein, an upper-class Genevese man who, after studying various sciences, creates a grotesque humanoid creature through an ambiguous life-giving process involving stolen body parts. The creature, rejected by Victor and feared by humanity, learns language and culture by observing a family, but faces rejection and misunderstanding. Vengeful and embittered, the creature kills Victor's younger brother William and frames a family servant, leading to tragic consequences. Tasked by the creature to create a female companion, Victor ultimately destroys the incomplete female, prompting the creature to vow revenge. Subsequent tragic events include the creature murdering Victor's friend Henry and wife Elizabeth, and Victor's pursuit of the creature ending in the Arctic, where both meet their demise.

Mary Shelley's background deeply influenced the novel, with her personal experiences of loss and her intellectual environment shaping themes of loss, guilt, and the limits of human ambition. The work draws heavily from classical and contemporary literature, such as Milton's *Paradise Lost*, and scientific ideas of the time. It explores themes of humanity, creation, and responsibility, with the creature symbolically compared to both Adam and the fallen angel Lucifer. Since its 1818 publication, *Frankenstein* has become a seminal Gothic and science-fiction work, widely studied and adapted, sometimes leading to misconceptions (e.g., the creature being called 'Frankenstein'). Its enduring legacy includes critical acclaim and numerous cultural references across media.
