Frankenstein is a novel by Mary Shelley, first published in 1818. It tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who discovers a method to create life by assembling body parts and using electricity. After bringing a grotesque creature to life, Victor is horrified and abandons it. The creature, rejected by society and his creator, learns to read and speak, develops emotions, and becomes aware of his own existence. It suffers from loneliness and bitterness, eventually killing Victor's brother William and framing a servant for the crime. Victor later creates a female companion for the creature, but before completing her, he destroys her. The creature kills Victor's fiancée Elizabeth on their wedding night. In a final confrontation, Victor pursues the creature to the Arctic, where he dies, and the creature mourns his death before departing. The novel explores themes of creation, responsibility, isolation, and the consequences of defying nature. Shelley's personal experiences, including the loss of her mother and her first child, are believed to have influenced the story's emotional depth. The novel is considered a landmark work in Romantic and Gothic literature and one of the first science fiction novels. It has inspired numerous adaptations in film, stage, and other media, and remains a significant influence in modern literature and popular culture.
