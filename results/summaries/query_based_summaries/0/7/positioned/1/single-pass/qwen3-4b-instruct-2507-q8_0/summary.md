Frankenstein is a novel by Mary Shelley, first published in 1818. It tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who, inspired by alchemical and scientific studies, attempts to create life from dead body parts and electricity. After successfully assembling a creature, Victor is horrified by its appearance and abandons it. The creature, left to fend for itself, learns to read, speak, and survive, eventually developing a sense of self and morality. It observes a family, learns their language, and eventually reveals itself to the blind father, who treats it kindly. When the family discovers the creature, they are horrified and chase it away. The creature later saves a young girl from drowning, only to be shot by her father, who mistakes it for a threat. 

Driven by anger and grief, the creature seeks revenge. It kills Victor's younger brother William and frames the family's servant, Justine, for the murder. Victor, suspecting the creature, does not intervene and Justine is executed. The creature then warns Victor to create a female companion, which he agrees to. However, while working on the female creature, Victor destroys the incomplete version, fearing it might become a threat. The creature, now vengeful, kills Henry Clerval, Victor's friend, in a separate act of revenge. 

Victor, devastated, marries Elizabeth Lavenza, a childhood friend. On their wedding night, the creature murders her, fulfilling its threat. Victor's father, Alphonse, dies of grief, and with no family left, Victor vows revenge and pursues the creature into the Arctic. Chasing the creature across the ice, Victor nearly dies from exposure and hypothermia, but is rescued by Captain Robert Walton, who is leading an expedition to the North Pole. Victor recounts his story to Walton, who encourages him to continue the pursuit. Victor, weakened, dies aboard the ship. As the ship departs, the creature mourns Victor's death and tells Walton he plans to burn himself on a pyre. 

In a future where humanity has gone extinct and Manhattan is buried under glacial ice, mechas have evolved into advanced forms. A group known as the Specialists, interested in humanity, find and resurrect David and Teddy, the mecha child and his teddy bear from the film A.I. They reconstruct the Swinton family home from David's memories. They explain via an interactive version of the Blue Fairy that David cannot become human. However, they recreate Monica Swinton through genetic material from a strand of hair kept by Teddy. This version of Monica can live for only one day and cannot be revived. David spends his happiest day with Monica, and as she falls asleep, she tells David she has always loved him. David lies down beside her and closes his eyes as Teddy watches over them. 

The novel explores themes of creation, responsibility, isolation, and the consequences of defying nature. The creature is often interpreted as a composite of Percy Shelley and Thomas Paine, reflecting themes of rebellion and desire for love and connection. The story is influenced by Mary Shelley's personal experiences, including the loss of her mother and the death of her first child. The creature's journey from being a "wretched devil" to a being capable of love and empathy reflects the novel's central moral inquiry into the nature of humanity and the consequences of playing God. The title "Modern Prometheus" refers to the myth of Prometheus, who stole fire from the gods and gave it to humanity, a theme echoed in Victor's creation of life. The novel is considered a landmark work in Romantic and Gothic literature and is often regarded as the first true science fiction story.
