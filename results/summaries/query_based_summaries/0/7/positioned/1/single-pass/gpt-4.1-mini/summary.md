## Summary of Frankenstein

**Frankenstein** is a novel by Mary Shelley that tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a scientist from an upper-class Genevese family who becomes obsessed with creating life. After studying alchemy and modern sciences like chemistry and electricity at the University of Ingolstadt, Victor assembles and brings to life a large humanoid creature from stolen body parts. Repelled by his creation, Victor abandons it, leading the creature to learn language and human customs secretly, but face rejection and violence from society. The creature, embittered, seeks Victor and demands a female companion; Victor initially agrees but destroys the unfinished female out of fear. The creature retaliates by killing Victor’s loved ones, culminating in a deadly pursuit to the Arctic where both meet their ends.

Mary Shelley's personal losses and her parents’ intellectual legacies heavily influenced the novel’s themes of creation, responsibility, and alienation. The creature’s characterization as both Adam and the fallen angel evokes biblical and literary allusions, especially to Milton’s *Paradise Lost*. Although popularly linked to electrical reanimation and pieced-together body parts, such imagery stems from later adaptations rather than the original book, where the life-giving process remains ambiguous. *Frankenstein* was published anonymously in 1818 and received mixed contemporary reviews but quickly gained popularity, inspiring numerous adaptations and academic discourse. Today, it is regarded as a foundational work of Romantic, Gothic, and science fiction literature, exploring profound questions about humanity, ethics, and social exclusion.
