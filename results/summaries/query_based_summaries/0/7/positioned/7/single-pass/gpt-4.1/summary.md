## Frankenstein – Concise Summary

**Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus** is a novel by Mary Shelley first published anonymously in 1818. Drawing on her experiences and literary influences such as Milton's *Paradise Lost*, the myth of Prometheus, and contemporary scientific discussions about life creation and electricity, Shelley crafted the story during the "Year Without a Summer" in 1816, in Geneva alongside Percy Bysshe Shelley and Lord Byron. The novel tells the tale of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who becomes obsessed with reanimating life and succeeds in animating a grotesque creature assembled from dead body parts. Horrified by his creation, Victor flees, setting in motion a series of tragic events. The creature, rejected and tormented by society, seeks vengeance by murdering Victor's loved ones and ultimately leading Victor to his own death in the Arctic.

The book explores themes of creation, isolation, prejudice, and the consequences of defying nature. It is considered a landmark work of Romantic and Gothic literature, and is recognized as one of the earliest science fiction novels. Initially met with mixed critical reception, *Frankenstein* quickly achieved popular success, inspiring numerous theatrical adaptations and becoming one of the most influential novels in literature and popular culture. Shelley's work has prompted extensive psychoanalytic and feminist criticism, and its character—the Creature, often misidentified as "Frankenstein"—has become iconic through countless film, television, and comic book adaptations. Today, *Frankenstein* is revered for its exploration of existential questions and its enduring impact on science fiction and horror genres.
