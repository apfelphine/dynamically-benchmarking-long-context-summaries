**Frankenstein** is a novel by Mary Shelley, first published in 1818. It tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist obsessed with alchemy and modern sciences, who discovers a way to create life by assembling body parts from dead bodies and using electricity. After bringing the creature to life, Victor is horrified and flees, abandoning it. The creature, initially rejected and lonely, learns to read, speak, and write, and eventually develops a deep sense of empathy and a desire for love and connection. It seeks out Victor, who is his creator, and demands to be accepted. After a series of tragic events—including the murder of Victor's younger brother William and the framing of Victor's friend Justine—Victor becomes consumed by guilt and fear. He later agrees to create a female companion for the creature, but the project ends in disaster when the creature kills Victor's friend Henry and ultimately murders Victor's fiancée, Elizabeth. In a final act of defiance, the creature saves Victor's life and departs, leaving Victor to live with the consequences of his actions. The novel explores themes of creation, responsibility, isolation, and the consequences of defying nature. The creature is often mistakenly referred to as "Frankenstein," though the name belongs to the creator. The story has become a foundational work in science fiction and Gothic literature, influencing countless adaptations in film, television, and other media.
