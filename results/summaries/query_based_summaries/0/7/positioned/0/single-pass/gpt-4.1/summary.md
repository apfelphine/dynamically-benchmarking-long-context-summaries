## Frankenstein – Concise Summary

**Plot and Characters:**  
*Frankenstein* follows Victor Frankenstein, a young Genevan who becomes obsessed with science and creates a living creature from dead body parts. Repulsed by his grotesque creation, Victor abandons him, leading the Creature to seek understanding and companionship while experiencing rejection and cruelty from humanity. The Creature learns to speak, reads books like *Paradise Lost*, and discovers the details of his origin. Feeling embittered, he kills Victor’s brother William and frames their servant. He demands Victor make him a female companion, but Victor ultimately destroys the second creation, prompting the Creature to exact revenge by killing Victor's loved ones. The pursuit culminates in the Arctic, where Victor dies and the Creature vows to destroy himself.

**Authorship, Influences, and Reception:**  
*Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus* was written by Mary Shelley during the "Year Without a Summer" in 1816, inspired by discussions on science, galvanism, and the origins of life. Literary influences include John Milton's *Paradise Lost*, Ovid's *Prometheus* myth, and her parents' works. The Creature in the novel remains unnamed, often erroneously called Frankenstein due to later adaptations. The novel was first published anonymously in 1818, credited to Shelley in subsequent editions, and achieved immediate popularity, especially through melodramatic adaptations. Over time, it has been recognized as a foundational work in science fiction, Romantic, and Gothic literature, exploring themes of loss, guilt, and the consequences of defying nature.
