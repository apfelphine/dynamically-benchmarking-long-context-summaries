**Frankenstein** is a novel by Mary Shelley, centered on Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist obsessed with alchemy and modern sciences. After his mother's death, Victor travels to Ingolstadt to study, where he discovers a method to create life by assembling body parts from cadavers and using electricity. He brings the creature to life, but is horrified by its appearance and flees, returning to find it gone. The creature, initially named "the wretch," learns to speak and read, and discovers his origin from Victor. After being rejected by society, he kills Victor's younger brother, William, and frames Victor's servant, Justine, for the murder. Victor, devastated, vows revenge and pursues the creature, eventually following it to the Arctic.

During their confrontation, the creature asks Victor to create a female companion, which Victor agrees to. Victor later discovers the creature's true nature and learns that it is not merely a monster but a being with emotions and a desire for love and connection. The creature's suffering and isolation mirror Victor's own guilt and failure to protect his creation. Victor's attempts to destroy the female creature are thwarted, and the creature ultimately kills Victor on the wedding night, leading to a tragic ending. The story explores themes of ambition, creation, guilt, and the consequences of defying nature, drawing on influences from Romanticism, Enlightenment thought, and scientific advancements of the time. The novel's subtitle, *The Modern Prometheus*, draws a parallel between Victor's creation and the myth of Prometheus, who brought fire to humanity, symbolizing both the potential and peril of human innovation. The creature's identity and suffering have led to widespread interpretations, including views of him as a composite of Percy Shelley and Thomas Paine, and as a symbol of alienation and the human condition. The story remains a foundational work in science fiction and Gothic literature, influencing countless adaptations and discussions on ethics, identity, and the limits of scientific progress.
