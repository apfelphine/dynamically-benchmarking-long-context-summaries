# Frankenstein

**Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus**, written by Mary Shelley in 1816, is a Gothic novel centered on Victor Frankenstein, a scientist obsessed with creating life. After assembling a creature from stolen body parts, Victor awakens it but flees in terror, leaving the monster to learn language and society on its own. The creature, who feels rejected and abandoned, becomes embittered and kills Victor's younger brother, framing his servant for the crime. Victor eventually creates a female companion to prevent the monster from repeating his isolation, but the creature murders his new wife and kills Victor himself. The narrative concludes with Victor dying in the Arctic to pursue his creator, only to be found by the creature, who mourns him and vanishes.

The novel draws heavily from contemporary scientific debates regarding galvanism and the works of John Milton and Percy Shelley. Shelley's personal tragedies, including the deaths of her mother and children, influenced the themes of loss and guilt. While later film adaptations, particularly the 1931 version, popularized the imagery of electricity reanimating a corpse, Shelley's original text ambiguously describes the process of imbuing life. The work is often cited as a landmark of Romantic and Gothic literature and is frequently recognized as one of the first true science fiction novels.
