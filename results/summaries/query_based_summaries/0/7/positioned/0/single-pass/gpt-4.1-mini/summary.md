# Summary of Frankenstein

Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who discovers a method to create life by assembling parts from cadavers. After animating his grotesque creation, Victor is horrified and abandons the creature, which then experiences rejection, learns language and human culture, and ultimately seeks vengeance against Victor by targeting his family. The creature demands a female companion for companionship, but Victor destroys the unfinished partner out of fear, inciting further violence—including the deaths of Victor’s brother William, his friend Henry Clerval, and his wife Elizabeth. Pursuing the creature to the Arctic, Victor dies after recounting his tale; the creature mourns him and plans to end its own life.

Shelley developed *Frankenstein* during the "Year Without a Summer" in 1816, when confined indoors with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Lord Byron, competing to write ghost stories. The novel’s themes reflect Mary Shelley’s personal tragedies, including loss and guilt, and draw on various literary influences such as Milton’s *Paradise Lost*. Unlike popular depictions, Shelley’s original creature was not reanimated by electricity but through a mysterious vital principle. Published anonymously in 1818, *Frankenstein* achieved immediate popular success and has since become a landmark Gothic and science fiction novel, influencing countless adaptations and shaping cultural perceptions of the "monster" often mistakenly called Frankenstein.
