**Frankenstein** is a novel by Mary Shelley centering on Victor Frankenstein, a youth from an upper-class family who becomes obsessed with alchemy and modern science. After his mother's death, he attends the University of Ingolstadt, where he discovers a method to create life. Victor assembles a grotesque humanoid creature from body parts stolen from graves and brings it to life by an ambiguous process. Repulsed by his creation, Victor abandons it. The creature, possessing consciousness and learning language and emotions by observing a family, faces rejection and cruelty from humans, which leads to tragic consequences including murder and revenge. Victor builds a female companion for the creature but destroys it out of fear. This fuels the creature's vengeance, culminating in the deaths of Victor’s loved ones and a final pursuit to the Arctic, where Victor dies. The creature, mourning Victor, intends to end his own life.

Shelley's creation was influenced by her own life experiences, her parents’ writings, and the scientific and philosophical ideas of her time. The novel draws themes from works like Milton’s *Paradise Lost* and Coleridge’s *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*, and incorporates debates on life, death, and humanity’s boundaries. The Creature is often mistakenly called Frankenstein; in the original text, it remains unnamed, referred to by terms such as "monster" or "creature." The subtitle "The Modern Prometheus" reflects the mythological parallel of defying natural order and suffering consequences. Published anonymously in 1818 and later revised by Shelley, *Frankenstein* quickly gained popularity and became a cornerstone of Romantic, Gothic, and science fiction literature. It has since inspired numerous adaptations in film, stage, television, comics, and cultural references, solidifying its status as a seminal work exploring the ethical and existential implications of human scientific ambition.
