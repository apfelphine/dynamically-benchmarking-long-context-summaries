**Frankenstein**

Mary Shelley’s *Frankenstein* (1818) follows Victor Frankenstein, a scientist obsessed with creating life through alchemy and modern science. After assembling a grotesque humanoid creature from stolen body parts, Victor brings it to life, only to be horrified and flee. The creature, initially fearful of humans, learns to survive and eventually seeks revenge against Victor and humanity. Victor, driven by guilt and a desire for revenge, pursues the creature across Europe and the Arctic, ultimately dying aboard a ship while the creature contemplates self-destruction.

The novel explores themes of creation, responsibility, and the consequences of defying nature. Victor’s hubris and the creature’s tragic journey highlight the moral dilemmas of scientific ambition and the ethical implications of playing god. The story’s blend of Gothic horror and philosophical inquiry has cemented its status as a foundational work of science fiction and literature.
