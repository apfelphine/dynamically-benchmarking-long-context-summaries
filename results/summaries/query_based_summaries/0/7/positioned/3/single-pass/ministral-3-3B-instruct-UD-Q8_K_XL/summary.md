**Frankenstein** centers on Victor Frankenstein, a Genevese scientist obsessed with reanimating life through alchemy and modern science. After constructing a grotesque humanoid creature from stolen body parts, Victor is horrified by its appearance and flees. The creature, initially repelled by humans, learns to survive and eventually discovers its origins, revealing Victor’s identity. It then seeks revenge against Victor’s family, killing William, Victor’s son, and framing Justine for the crime. Victor, consumed by guilt, returns to Geneva and marries Elizabeth, only to be haunted by the creature’s vengeful threats. The creature, now embittered, pursues Victor to the Arctic, where Victor nearly dies and is rescued by Captain Robert Walton. Victor dies aboard Walton’s ship, and the creature, now despondent, contemplates self-destruction.

The novel explores themes of creation, responsibility, and the consequences of defying nature. Victor’s hubris in attempting to play God leads to the creature’s tragic existence, highlighting the moral and ethical dilemmas of scientific ambition. The story is deeply influenced by Mary Shelley’s personal experiences, including her mother’s death, her father’s political views, and her own struggles with mortality and identity. The creature’s identity as both a fallen angel and a composite of Victor’s flaws underscores the novel’s central question: what does it mean to be human?
