# Summary of Information on "Frankenstein"

Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist from an upper-class Genevese family who becomes obsessed with creating life through scientific means. After assembling and animating a large, grotesque creature from stolen body parts using an undisclosed process, Victor is horrified by his creation and abandons it. The creature, initially benevolent and eager to learn, experiences rejection and violence from humans, fostering a deep bitterness. It educates itself by observing a family and reading books, including *Paradise Lost*, and ultimately confronts Victor. The creature demands a female companion to alleviate its loneliness, but Victor destroys the unfinished mate, leading to tragic consequences: the creature kills Victor’s loved ones in revenge. The pursuit ends with Victor’s death in the Arctic and the creature mourning his creator before disappearing.

Shelley's novel was heavily influenced by Enlightenment and Romantic ideas, classical literature such as *Paradise Lost* and *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*, and contemporary scientific debates about life and electricity (galvanism). The creature is portrayed with complex humanity, often identifying with Adam and the fallen angel from Milton’s work, reflecting themes of creation, rejection, and existential suffering. Published anonymously in 1818 when Mary was only 20, *Frankenstein* became an immediate popular success, spawning numerous adaptations and critical analyses. It is recognized as a foundational Gothic and science fiction work, exploring profound questions about creation, responsibility, and what it means to be human.
