## Frankenstein Summary

Mary Shelley's novel, *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus*, details the story of Victor Frankenstein, who, obsessed with alchemy and modern sciences like chemistry and electricity, discovers a way to create life. After assembling a grotesque humanoid creature from stolen materials, Victor is repelled by his creation and flees. The newly conscious creature learns language and humanity by observing a family, becoming embittered by human rejection, and eventually seeks out Victor to ask for a female companion. After Victor agrees, he becomes fearful and destroys the incomplete female creature, leading the original Creature to murder Victor's friend, Henry Clerval. The creature subsequently murders Victor's fiancée, Elizabeth Lavenza, on their wedding night, prompting Victor to vow revenge.

Victor's relentless pursuit of the creature leads him to the Arctic, where he nearly dies from exhaustion and hypothermia, receiving aid from Captain Robert Walton, who encourages him to continue the chase. The Creature, having mourned Victor's death, departs, planning to burn himself on a pyre. The novel is heavily influenced by literary works such as John Milton's *Paradise Lost* and Samuel Taylor Coleridge's *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*. The title itself is debated, with some scholars suggesting Mary Shelley's inspiration came from visiting Frankenstein Castle near Darmstadt where alchemist Johann Konrad Dippel had experimented with human bodies.
