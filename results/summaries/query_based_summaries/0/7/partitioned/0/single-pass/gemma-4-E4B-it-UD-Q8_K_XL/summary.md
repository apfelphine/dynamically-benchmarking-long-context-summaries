# Frankenstein Summary

Victor Frankenstein, obsessed with modern sciences after his mother's death, discovers a method to create life by assembling materials from charnel houses. Upon animating the grotesque humanoid creature, Victor is repulsed and flees. The creature learns language and literacy by observing a family, eventually learning the truth of its origin and creator. Embittered by humanity, the creature confronts Victor in Geneva, murders Victor's brother William, and frames the servant Justine. Later, the creature demands that Victor create a female companion, to which Victor consents. After Victor destroys the incomplete female creature, the original creature vows revenge, murdering Victor's bride, Elizabeth Lavenza, on their wedding night.

Consumed by vengeance, Victor pursues the creature to the Arctic, where he is rescued by Captain Robert Walton. Victor recounts his story, and before he dies, the creature appears aboard Walton's ship. The novel explores themes of creation, isolation, and the consequences of defying nature. Mary Shelley drew inspiration for the novel from her own experiences of loss and guilt, as well as scientific ideas circulating among contemporaries like Giovanni Aldini and Johann Konrad Dippel.
