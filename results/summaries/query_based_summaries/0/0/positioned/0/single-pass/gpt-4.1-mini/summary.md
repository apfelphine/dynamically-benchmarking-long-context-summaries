# Summary of *Star Trek: The Motion Picture*

*Star Trek: The Motion Picture* (1979) is a science-fiction film set in the 23rd century where the starship Enterprise, under former Captain James T. Kirk—now an admiral who re-assumes command—is tasked to intercept a massive alien energy cloud called V’Ger heading toward Earth. The film explores tensions between Kirk and the current commanding officer Willard Decker, the mysterious nature of V’Ger (eventually revealed to be a sentient NASA probe, Voyager 6, upgraded by machines), and the merging of Decker with the probe to form a new life form, saving Earth. The returning cast includes William Shatner as Kirk, Leonard Nimoy as Spock, and others from the original series, with key new characters like Persis Khambatta's Ilia and Stephen Collins as Decker.

The production faced a complex development history, originally aiming for a television revival (Phase II) before transitioning to a feature film under director Robert Wise. Numerous script drafts were rejected; the final story was adapted from the Phase II pilot "In Thy Image." The film featured extensive new set designs for the Enterprise and V’Ger, innovative models, and updated costumes by Robert Fletcher aiming for realism and less garish visuals than the original series. Technological consultation came from NASA and Isaac Asimov, ensuring scientific plausibility. The special effects, initially hampered by Robert Abel & Associates, were successfully completed by Douglas Trumbull. The film's score, composed by Jerry Goldsmith, was praised and helped define the film's atmosphere.

Released in December 1979, the film broke opening weekend box office records and earned over $139 million worldwide but received mixed critical reviews, with some criticizing its pacing and focus on special effects over character development. It garnered several award nominations, including three Academy Awards, and was followed by sequels that regained critical favor. The 2001 Director’s Edition re-release improved critical reception by refining pacing and effects, and remains a significant entry in the Star Trek franchise.
