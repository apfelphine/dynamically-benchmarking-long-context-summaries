**Star Trek: The Motion Picture** is a 1979 film directed by Robert Wise, based on the original *Star Trek* television series, which features a return of the original cast including William Shatner as Admiral Kirk and Leonard Nimoy as Spock. The plot centers on an alien entity called V'Ger, a sentient living machine evolved from a lost NASA probe named Voyager 6, which is traveling toward Earth to learn all that can be learned. After the USS Enterprise intercepts V'Ger and suffers damage, Spock performs a telepathic mind meld with the entity, discovering it needs a physical "Creator" to complete its evolutionary purpose. The crew realizes humans are the Creator, and Captain Willard Decker sacrifices himself by merging with V'Ger to save Earth, creating a new life form.

Production was complex, involving significant delays and a transition from a planned Phase II television series. Director Robert Wise and producer Jerry Isenberg faced creative conflicts with Gene Roddenberry regarding the script, particularly the ending, which was controversial until NASA scientist Robert Jastrow's interview suggested the plausibility of mechanical life. The film is renowned for its visual effects by Douglas Trumbull and John Dykstra, utilizing "in-camera" techniques and models to depict the Enterprise and V'Ger without computer-generated imagery. While it received mixed critical reviews for its pacing and dialogue, it was a box office success and won the Saturn Award for Best Special Effects, influencing future filmmakers by proving that high-budget, serious science fiction could be commercially viable.
