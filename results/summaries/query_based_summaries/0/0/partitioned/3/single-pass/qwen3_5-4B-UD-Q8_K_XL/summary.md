# Star Trek: The Motion Picture

**Star Trek: The Motion Picture** (1979) was a landmark production that transitioned the *Star Trek* franchise from television to the feature film format, despite facing significant production challenges and creative friction. The film was directed by Robert Wise, who had limited prior experience with the franchise, while Gene Roddenberry, the series creator, exerted intense control over the script and story direction. This dynamic led to numerous rewrites, a budget that ultimately reached $44 million (the largest for a U.S. film at the time), and a complex special effects campaign managed by Douglas Trumbull after the initial vendor, Robert Abel and Associates, was fired for failing to meet deadlines. The film introduced major set redesigns, including a forced-perspective engine room and new uniforms by designer Robert Fletcher, and utilized innovative sound design by Frank Serafine, who employed digital synthesizers and acoustic recordings to create distinct audio textures for V'Ger and other elements.

Released on December 6, 1979, the film became a massive box office success, grossing over $139 million worldwide and setting records for its opening weekend. It received mixed critical reception, often criticized for its dialogue-heavy plot, slow pacing, and reliance on spectacular visuals that overshadowed the narrative, though it was nominated for three Academy Awards (including Best Visual Effects and Best Original Score) and won the Saturn Award for Best Special Effects. The film's legacy is defined by its controversial ending, where Willard Decker sacrifices himself to merge with the sentient entity V'Ger, creating a new life form. Following its initial release, the film underwent several revisions, including a 1983 "Special Longer Version" for television and a 2001 "Director's Edition" that restored deleted scenes and remastered the sound, eventually leading to a remastered 1080p high-definition release in 2009 and a 4K Ultra HD release in 2021.
