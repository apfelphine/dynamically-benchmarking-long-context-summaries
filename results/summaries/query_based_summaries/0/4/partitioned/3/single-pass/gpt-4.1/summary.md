## The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy – Summary

*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*, created by Douglas Adams, originated as a radio comedy in 1978 before expanding into novels, a TV series, a stage production, comics, audiobooks, video games, and a major film. The story centers on Arthur Dent, an ordinary Englishman thrust into intergalactic adventures after Earth is destroyed to make way for a hyperspace bypass. He journeys alongside Ford Prefect (an alien and researcher for the Guide), Zaphod Beeblebrox (eccentric President of the Galaxy), Trillian (the only other human survivor), and Marvin the Paranoid Android. A hallmark of the series is its absurdist humor, philosophical satire, and the Guide itself—an electronic encyclopedia offering witty, occasionally inaccurate advice for galactic travelers. Notable plot elements include the revelation that Earth was a supercomputer built to discover the Ultimate Question to life, the universe, and everything, for which the answer is infamously "42."

The series has seen numerous adaptations and expansions: five main novels (the "trilogy in five parts," selling over 14 million copies), stage shows, television, record albums, comic books, and a 2005 feature film. Its legacy includes spin-off merchandise ("Hitch-Hikeriana"), an annual fan event ("Towel Day"), ongoing references in science fiction and popular culture, and influence on real-world technology predictions like electronic guidebooks and real-time translation (echoing the "Babel Fish"). Radio and audiobook adaptations feature original and new cast members, while a sixth novel, *And Another Thing...*, was later written by Eoin Colfer. The Hitchhiker's Guide remains a landmark of comedic science fiction for its inventive storytelling, enduring characters, and distinctively British wit.
