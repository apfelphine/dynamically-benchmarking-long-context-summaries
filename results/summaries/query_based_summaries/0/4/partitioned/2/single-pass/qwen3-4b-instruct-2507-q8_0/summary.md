The *Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* is a science fiction series created by Douglas Adams, featuring the adventures of Arthur Dent, a hapless Englishman, after Earth is destroyed by the Vogons to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Accompanied by Ford Prefect, Zaphod Beeblebrox, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian, Arthur travels through the galaxy, learning that Earth was a supercomputer designed to answer the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," which is "42." The story includes a mix of humor, absurdity, and philosophical musings, with the Guide serving as a central narrative device. The series has been adapted into multiple formats, including radio, television, film, stage, and books. The original radio series, broadcast in 1978–1980, was followed by several new radio series in the 2000s, with the most recent series (the Hexagonal Phase) airing in 2018. The film adaptation, released in 2005, features a different plot from the books and radio series, with a focus on the romantic triangle between Arthur, Zaphod, and Trillian. The series has also inspired a range of merchandise, including towels, books, and games, and has a lasting cultural impact, with the number 42 becoming a pop culture reference. The series is known for its wit, satire, and imaginative storytelling, and remains a beloved classic in science fiction.
