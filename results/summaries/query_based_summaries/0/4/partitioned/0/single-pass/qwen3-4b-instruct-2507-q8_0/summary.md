**The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy** is a science fiction franchise created by Douglas Adams, featuring a humorous and philosophical exploration of space, life, and human existence. The series centers on Arthur Dent, a hapless Earthling who survives the Vogons' destruction of his planet, and his adventures with companions Ford Prefect, Zaphod Beeblebrox, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian. The narrative is structured across multiple adaptations, including a radio series, novels, television episodes, and films, with the core plot involving the discovery that Earth was a supercomputer designed to determine the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," which was found to be "42."

The books, which are described as a "trilogy in five parts," follow a consistent but evolving storyline, with the radio series and films adapting and reworking the material. The franchise features a distinctive tone, blending satire, absurdity, and existential themes. The title's spelling varies across editions, with Adams later stating he preferred "Hitchhiker's Guide" and advocating for a single, consistent spelling. The series has inspired numerous adaptations, including audiobooks, video games, comic books, and stage productions, and has become a cultural phenomenon, with references such as the number 42 and the "Towel Day" celebration.

The franchise's legacy includes a rich body of media, including the 2005 film adaptation, which features a unique plot that diverges from the books, and a long-running radio series that has seen multiple phases. The franchise has also been recognized for its influence on popular culture, with references in music, film, and literature. The stories explore themes of bureaucracy, the futility of human endeavors, and the search for meaning in a vast, indifferent universe. The franchise remains a beloved and enduring work of science fiction, celebrated for its wit, originality, and philosophical depth.
