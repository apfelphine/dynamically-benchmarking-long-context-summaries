# The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy

Douglas Adams' *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* is a satirical science fiction series originally conceived as a radio show but expanded into novels, television, film, and video games. The narrative follows Arthur Dent, an ordinary Englishman whose house is demolished by Vogons to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Rescued by Ford Prefect, an alien researcher for the titular electronic guidebook, Arthur travels through the galaxy with his new friends: Zaphod Beeblebrox, the eccentric Galactic President; Marvin the Paranoid Android; and Trillian. Their journey reveals that Earth was a supercomputer designed to answer the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," which the computer Deep Thought calculated to be "42." Throughout the story, the Guide provides humorous entries on everything from the dangers of the Vogons to the best way to boil an egg, while the characters navigate absurd galactic politics and existential crises.

The franchise has evolved through multiple adaptations, each with distinct versions of the plot. The original 1978 radio series, produced by the BBC, featured Peter Jones as the voice of the Guide and was noted for its innovative sound design. Subsequent radio phases, TV versions, and the 2005 film adaptation (voiced by Martin Freeman and Stephen Fry) further expanded the lore, introducing new characters like the sentient guide Mark II and the villain Agrajag. The story is structured around a trilogy of novels that correspond to different phases of the radio series, though they occasionally diverge in detail. The work is celebrated for its humor, philosophical depth, and enduring cultural impact, influencing everything from video games to pop culture references, with the number 42 becoming a ubiquitous symbol of the absurdity of the universe.
