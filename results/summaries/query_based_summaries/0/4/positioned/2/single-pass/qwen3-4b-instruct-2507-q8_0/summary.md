The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy is a science fiction series following the adventures of Arthur Dent, a hapless Englishman, after Earth is destroyed by the Vogons to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Dent is rescued by Ford Prefect, a researcher for the eponymous guidebook, and joins a group of travelers including Zaphod Beeblebrox, the eccentric Galactic President, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian, a woman Arthur once met. As they journey through the galaxy, they encounter various characters and situations, including the revelation that Earth was actually a giant supercomputer designed to determine the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," which was ultimately found to be "42." The series explores themes of existentialism, the nature of intelligence, and the absurdity of human endeavors, with a recurring humorous tone. The narrative is punctuated by excerpts from the Guide, which serves as a central element of the story. The series spans multiple formats, including radio, novels, television, film, and stage, and has become a cultural touchstone for its witty commentary on science, technology, and the human condition.
