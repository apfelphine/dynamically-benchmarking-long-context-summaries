The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy is a science fiction series following the adventures of Arthur Dent, a hapless Englishman, after the destruction of Earth by the Vogons, a bureaucratic alien race. Dent is rescued by Ford Prefect, an alien researcher for the eponymous guidebook, and meets Zaphod Beeblebrox, a eccentric and semi-cousin of Ford who is the Galactic President. The group also encounters Marvin the Paranoid Android, a depressed robot, and Trillian, a woman Arthur once met at a party in Islington, who becomes the only other human survivor of Earth's destruction. 

Throughout their travels, Arthur learns that Earth was actually a giant supercomputer created by another supercomputer, Deep Thought, which was built to answer the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything." After eons of calculations, the answer was found to be "42." Deep Thought then designed Earth to determine what the actual question was, but before the answer could be communicated, Earth was destroyed by the Vogons. Arthur becomes a target of the descendants of Deep Thought's creators, who believe his mind holds the answer.

With the help of his friends, Arthur escapes and they decide to have lunch at The Restaurant at the End of the Universe before embarking on further adventures. The series has been adapted into various formats, including radio series, novels, a television series, a film, stage shows, and video games. The books and radio series are known for their humor, satire, and philosophical themes, with the number 42 becoming a cultural reference in popular culture. The series has a strong legacy, with its themes of existentialism, human nature, and the absurdity of bureaucracy resonating with audiences. The story's blend of humor, science fiction, and philosophical inquiry has made it a timeless classic in the genre.
