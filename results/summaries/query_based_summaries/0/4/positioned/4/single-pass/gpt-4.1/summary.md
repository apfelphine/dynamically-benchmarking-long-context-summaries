## The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy: Summary

*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* is a multi-format science fiction comedy series created by Douglas Adams, originally beginning as a BBC radio show in 1978. The central story follows Arthur Dent, an Englishman who is rescued by his alien friend Ford Prefect just before Earth is destroyed by bureaucratic aliens, the Vogons, to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Arthur's intergalactic adventures introduce him to Ford, the eccentric Galactic President Zaphod Beeblebrox, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian, the only other human survivor. The group uncovers the cosmic mystery that Earth was a supercomputer designed to find the Ultimate Question, the answer to which—famously—is "42". The series is known for its absurd humor, satirical take on science fiction, and philosophical undertones.

The story has expanded across numerous adaptations: six radio series (including adaptations of all five novels and a sixth series based on Eoin Colfer's authorized sequel), five main novels ("a trilogy in five parts"—1979–1992), stage shows, vinyl LPs, comics, audiobooks, video games, a 1981 BBC television miniseries, and a 2005 feature film starring Martin Freeman, Mos Def, Sam Rockwell, Zooey Deschanel, Alan Rickman, and Stephen Fry. Each version features distinct plot developments and interpretations, with Adams continually revising and reimagining the material. The franchise is celebrated for its satirical predictions, enduring cultural legacy (such as "Towel Day" and the significance of the number 42), and wide array of merchandise and references in popular culture.
