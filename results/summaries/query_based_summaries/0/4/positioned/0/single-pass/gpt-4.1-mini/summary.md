# Summary of *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*

*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* is a comedic science fiction series created by Douglas Adams that exists in multiple formats, including radio, novels, television, stage, film, audiobooks, and games, featuring variations and inconsistencies due to Adams's frequent rewrites. Central to the story is Arthur Dent, a hapless Englishman whose life is upended when Earth is destroyed by bureaucratic aliens, the Vogons, to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Rescued by Ford Prefect, an alien researcher for the eponymous guidebook, Arthur embarks on adventures across the galaxy with characters including the eccentric Galactic President Zaphod Beeblebrox, the depressed robot Marvin, and Trillian, the only other surviving human. A key plot revelation is that Earth is a supercomputer designed to discover the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," whose answer is found to be "42," but the actual question remains unknown.

The series originated from a 1978 BBC radio comedy, notable for pioneering stereo sound effects and narrated by the voice of the Guide. The title itself evolved over time, with Adams preferring the spelling "Hitchhiker's Guide." The novels, referred to humorously as a "trilogy in five parts," adapt and extend the radio stories, with the first book published in 1979 becoming a bestseller. The series also includes a 1981 BBC television adaptation, multiple stage productions, a 2005 feature film that diverges significantly in plot and tone, and various audiobook and game adaptations. The work's legacy includes fan events such as Towel Day, merchandise known as "Hitch-Hikeriana," and cultural references to its humor and philosophical themes, especially the iconic number 42.
