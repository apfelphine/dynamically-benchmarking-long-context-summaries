## The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy: Summary

**The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy** originated as a 1978 BBC radio series created by Douglas Adams and has since expanded into novels, a television series (1981), a feature film (2005), stage adaptations, audiobooks, comics, and video games. The story centers on Arthur Dent, an Englishman who survives Earth's destruction at the hands of the bureaucratic Vogons. Joined by Ford Prefect, Zaphod Beeblebrox, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian, Arthur travels through space, discovering that Earth was a supercomputer designed by Deep Thought to find the "Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything," to which the answer is "42." The series is known for its absurdist humor and inventive narration, often delivered by the Guide itself.

The franchise is notable for its multiple conflicting adaptations, each with unique plot twists and details, but always maintaining the core ensemble and themes of interstellar misadventure and satire. The books form an "increasingly inaccurately named trilogy" of five (later six, with Eoin Colfer's 2009 addition), each exploring further cosmic oddities. The works helped anticipate real-world advances (e.g., tablet computers and instant translation), spawned extensive merchandise, inspired annual "Towel Day" fan observance, and popularized "42" as a cultural meme. Various versions consistently feature excerpts from the fictional Guide—a digital book produced by Megadodo Publications—and highlight Adams's witty critique of bureaucracy, technology, and philosophy.
