**Transformer (Deep Learning)**  
A deep learning architecture introduced in 2017, the Transformer revolutionized natural language processing (NLP) and beyond by replacing recurrence with self-attention mechanisms. It processes sequences in parallel, enabling faster training and superior performance in tasks like machine translation, text generation, and speech recognition. Key components include tokenization, embeddings, multi-head attention, and feedforward layers. Variants such as BERT (encoder-only) and GPT (decoder-only) dominate modern NLP. The architecture has been extended to vision (Vision Transformers), multimodal tasks, and generative modeling, demonstrating broad applicability in AI.  

**Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)**  
A neural network architecture designed for grid-structured data (e.g., images), CNNs use convolutional layers to detect local features, followed by pooling and fully connected layers for classification. Inspired by biological vision, CNNs exploit spatial locality and share weights to reduce parameters. They excel in image recognition, with applications in video analysis, speech processing, and drug discovery. Despite limitations in handling long-range dependencies, they remain foundational in computer vision.  

**Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)**  
A two-player game between a generator and a discriminator, GANs produce realistic data by training the generator to fool the discriminator. The generator learns to create data that resembles real samples, while the discriminator improves at distinguishing real from synthetic data. GANs are used for image generation, style transfer, and medical imaging, though they face challenges like mode collapse and unstable training. Variants such as WGAN, StyleGAN, and CycleGAN enhance stability and quality.  

**Hopfield Network**  
A recurrent neural network with associative memory, Hopfield networks store patterns via Hebbian learning and retrieve them through energy minimization. They are used for optimization problems and memory systems, though classical versions have limited storage capacity. Modern variants (Dense Associative Memories) improve capacity and robustness through higher-order interactions.  

**Vision Transformer (ViT)**  
A Transformer-based model adapted for computer vision, ViT replaces convolutional kernels with self-attention over image patches. It achieves state-of-the-art performance in image classification and has spurred innovation in CNNs. Variants like MAE and DINO enable self-supervised learning, while Swin and TimeSformer improve efficiency and video understanding.  

**Autoencoder & Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**  
Autoencoders compress data into latent representations and reconstruct it, used for dimensionality reduction and anomaly detection. VAEs introduce probabilistic modeling with latent variables, enabling generative capabilities. Variants like β-VAE and CVAE enhance disentanglement and conditional generation.  

**Graph Neural Network (GNN)**  
A framework for processing graph-structured data, GNNs use message passing to propagate node features through edges. Applications span social networks, protein folding (e.g., AlphaFold), cybersecurity, and computer vision. Despite limitations in handling heterophilic graphs (low homophily), GNNs are pivotal in graph-based AI.  

These architectures collectively form the backbone of modern AI, each excelling in specific domains through unique design principles and mathematical foundations.
