# Summary of Deep Learning Architectures

## Vision Transformers
Vision Transformers (ViTs) replaced convolutional kernels with self-attention mechanisms to process images as sequences of patches. Introduced in 2020, the original ViT achieved state-of-the-art results in image classification by treating images as sequences of vector embeddings, similar to language models like BERT. Variants such as Swin Transformer improved efficiency using sliding windows and pyramid processes, while DINO and Masked Autoencoders (MAE) utilized self-supervised training to learn robust representations without labels. MAE masks out image patches and trains the model to reconstruct them, achieving high performance in medical imaging and other domains.

## Generative Adversarial Networks
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) consist of a generator and a discriminator engaged in a zero-sum game to produce realistic synthetic data. While the original GAN relies on the Jensen-Shannon divergence, Wasserstein GANs (WGANs) use the Wasserstein distance to provide more stable gradients and avoid mode collapse. Variants include Conditional GANs for targeted generation, InfoGAN for disentangled representations, and CycleGAN for unpaired image translation. Recent advancements like StyleGAN series utilize progressive growth and adaptive instance normalization to generate high-fidelity images, while applications extend to scientific simulation, medical imaging, and creative arts.

## Class Activation Mapping
Class Activation Mapping (CAM) and its derivatives, such as Grad-CAM, provide visual explanations for CNN predictions by highlighting discriminative regions in input images. CAM relies on global average pooling and specific architecture modifications, whereas Grad-CAM is model-agnostic, using gradients of the class score with respect to feature maps to generate heatmaps. Fine-tuned versions like Guided Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, and Score-CAM address limitations such as coarse localization and false emphasis by incorporating high-resolution details, pixel-wise gradients, or confidence scores, enabling better interpretability for critical applications like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis.

## Transformers
Transformers revolutionized natural language processing by replacing recurrent architectures with self-attention mechanisms that process sequences in parallel. The architecture comprises tokenization, embedding, encoder-decoder layers with multi-head attention, and positional encodings. Encoder-only models (e.g., BERT) are used for tasks like question answering, while decoder-only models (e.g., GPT) are optimized for generation. Training strategies include pretraining on massive unlabeled datasets followed by fine-tuning, with recent innovations like Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) and FlashAttention enhancing efficiency and long-context handling.

## Graph Neural Networks
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) generalize Convolutional Neural Networks to graph-structured data by aggregating information from neighboring nodes. The core message-passing mechanism updates node representations iteratively, allowing models to capture complex relational structures found in social networks, molecular graphs, and knowledge bases. Specific architectures like Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Graph Attention Networks (GAT) offer different ways to handle topology and feature interactions. GNNs have achieved significant success in protein folding (AlphaFold), recommender systems, and solving combinatorial optimization problems.

## Convolutional Neural Networks
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) utilize shared weights and local connectivity to efficiently process spatial data like images and audio. Composed of convolutional, pooling, and fully connected layers, CNNs exploit translational equivariance and invariance through techniques like max pooling and dilated convolutions. The architecture was historically developed for speech and image recognition, with modern variants like ResNet and EfficientNet addressing depth and parameter efficiency. Regularization methods such as dropout and weight decay prevent overfitting, while applications span from object detection to drug discovery and time-series forecasting.

## Autoencoders
Autoencoders learn compressed representations of data by encoding inputs into a latent space and decoding them back to the original space. Variations include Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which model the latent space as a probability distribution to generate new samples, and Denoising Autoencoders, which learn robust features by reconstructing corrupted inputs. Sparse and Contractive Autoencoders further enhance interpretability and stability. These models are fundamental for dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and serving as building blocks for advanced generative models like Stable Diffusion.

## Recurrent Neural Networks
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) process sequential data by maintaining a hidden state that captures information over time. While standard RNNs suffer from vanishing gradients, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) introduced gating mechanisms to manage long-term dependencies effectively. Bidirectional RNNs process sequences in both directions to capture full context, and architectures like PixelRNN handle multi-dimensional data. RNNs and their derivatives have been pivotal in speech recognition, machine translation, and time-series prediction, though they have largely been superseded by Transformers in many NLP tasks.

## Radial Basis Function Networks
Radial Basis Function (RBF) networks approximate functions using a linear combination of local basis functions centered around specific input vectors. The architecture typically consists of an input layer, a hidden layer of RBF neurons (often Gaussian), and a linear output layer. Training involves selecting centers via clustering and solving for weights using least squares or gradient descent. RBF networks offer faster convergence than multi-layer perceptrons and are effective for interpolation, time-series prediction, and control systems, particularly when dealing with chaotic dynamics.

## Hopfield Networks
Hopfield networks are recurrent neural networks that function as content-addressable memory systems based on an energy function. Units update their states asynchronously to minimize energy, converging to stable attractor states that represent stored patterns. Trained via Hebbian learning, these networks can recall complete memories from partial inputs, though they are limited by spurious patterns and low storage capacity. Modern variants, known as Dense Associative Memories, introduce higher-order interactions to significantly increase memory capacity, making them relevant for associative learning and optimization tasks.
