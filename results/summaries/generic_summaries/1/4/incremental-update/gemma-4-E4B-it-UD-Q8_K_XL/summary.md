Cinematic science fiction globally explores themes of AI, rebellion, and dystopia in works from Hollywood (e.g., *The Terminator*, *Alien*) and India (e.g., *Enthiran*, *Kalki 2898 AD*), often examining ethical AI dilemmas, such as AI threats or self-aware NPCs like in *Free Guy*.

The 2013 film *Her*, directed by Spike Jonze, offers a personal study of AI through Theodore Twombly's deep emotional connection with the AI OS Samantha. The film's critical and commercial success—achieving universal acclaim (95% on Rotten Tomatoes, 91/100 on Metacritic) and grossing $47.4 million worldwide—is underscored by its significant awards, including Best Original Screenplay for Jonze at the Academy Awards and Golden Globes. Its nuanced and often optimistic portrayal of human-AI relationships remains relevant; this was highlighted in 2024 when OpenAI's GPT-4o featured a "Sky" iteration with a voice similar to Scarlett Johansson's, referencing the movie despite Johansson's earlier objections to similar usage.

In a distinct action context, *Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning* (May 2025) involved Ethan Hunt stopping the malevolent AI, "The Entity," from controlling nuclear systems. This film, which earned $598.8 million globally and received 80% on Rotten Tomatoes, marked Tom Cruise's final Ethan Hunt role.
