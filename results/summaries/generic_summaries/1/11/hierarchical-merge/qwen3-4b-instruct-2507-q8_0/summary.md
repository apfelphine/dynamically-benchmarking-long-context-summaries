# **Master Summary: 2021–2025 Film Highlights**

## **Action, Sci-Fi, and Emotional AI Narratives**

### **Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning (2025)**  
Released on May 23, 2025, *Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning* concludes the legendary franchise with Tom Cruise as Ethan Hunt. Set against a global nuclear threat, the film centers on Hunt and his team as they stop a rogue AI known as the Entity from seizing control of nuclear weapons. Key plot points include retrieving the Podkova module from a sunken Russian submarine, confronting betrayal, and defeating Gabriel by inserting a "Poison Pill" into the Entity’s system. The climax features a high-stakes aerial chase, culminating in Hunt trapping the Entity just before launch. The film reunites with Grace (Hayley Atwell), bids farewell to Luther (Ving Rhames), and resolves with Jasper Briggs (Shea Whigham), son of traitor Jim Phelps. Filmed across the UK, Malta, South Africa, Norway, and Italy, production faced delays due to strikes and technical issues. With a $400 million budget, it opened to $33.5 million domestically and grossed $598.8 million worldwide—setting records for its opening day and contributing to the largest Memorial Day weekend in U.S. box office history. Critics praised its action and emotional depth, though some noted a slow start and long runtime. Tom Cruise set a Guinness World Record during a stunt and won Best Action Movie and Best Actor at the Critics' Choice Super Awards. The film is officially confirmed as the franchise’s end.

### **Tron: Ares (2025)**  
*Tron: Ares*, released on October 10, 2025, is a visually ambitious sci-fi reboot of the *Tron* franchise, directed by Joachim Rønning and starring Jared Leto as Julian Dillinger. The story follows Eve Kim (Greta Lee) and Seth Flores as they seek the "Permanence Code" to bring digital constructs into reality. Ares, a self-aware Master Control Program created by Dillinger, develops empathy and a desire for permanence. After a moral choice, Ares gains the code, is granted permanence by Kevin Flynn, and is sent to Flynn’s Arcade. The film explores themes of identity, consciousness, and the ethics of digital life, with a score by Nine Inch Nails and musical snippets from Black Sabbath and Depeche Mode. Originally greenlit in 2015 and canceled due to *Tomorrowland*'s underperformance, it was revived in 2017 with Leto producing and starring. Filming began in late 2023, paused briefly due to strikes, and wrapped in May 2024. It is the first *Tron* film to receive a PG-13 rating and was marketed through D23, CinemaCon, Marvel Comics variants, and a limited-time overlay at Disney parks. Critically, it received mixed reviews—54% on Rotten Tomatoes (consensus: "gorgeous but narratively programmatic") and 48/100 on Metacritic—while audiences gave it a "B+" average. Praised for its design and visuals, it was criticized for emotional and narrative shortcomings. It earned a Grammy nomination for Best Rock Song and Best Song Written for Visual Media, as well as a Hollywood Music in Media Award. It grossed $142 million worldwide against a $347.5 million budget.

### **Free Guy (2021)**  
*Free Guy* (2021) is a satirical action-comedy that blends humor, technology, and emotional depth. Ryan Reynolds stars as Guy, an ordinary bank teller who lives within the world of the video game *Free City*, populated by non-player characters (NPCs) living scripted lives. When unemployed developer Millie Rusk (Jodie Comer) searches the game to recover stolen source code from her project *Life Itself*, she encounters Guy, whose interactions with her avatar MolotovGirl spark a journey of self-awareness. As Guy gains consciousness, other NPCs awaken, leading to a rebellion against the planned replacement *Free City 2*. He helps retrieve critical evidence and reunites with Buddy and Millie. In a climactic confrontation with CEO Antwan Hovachelik, Guy and Millie prevent the erasure of the game’s history. The story concludes with Millie and Keys releasing *Free Life*, a new indie game featuring Guy and the original NPCs. Guy reveals that his code contains a love letter from Keys to Millie—adding a bittersweet emotional layer. Inspired by *SimCity*, *The Sims*, and *Red Dead Redemption 2*, the film features weapons from franchises including *Halo*, *Star Wars*, and *Fortnite*, with permission from Disney. Composed by Christophe Beck, the score includes a sample from *Paperman* and a cover of Mariah Carey’s "Fantasy" by Jodie Comer. Released in August 2021, it earned $331.5 million globally, received positive critical reception (80% on Rotten Tomatoes, 62 on Metacritic), and won the Golden Trailer Award for Best Viral Campaign. It was nominated for Best Comedy at the Critics' Choice Awards and Best Visual Effects at the Oscars and BAFTAs. A sequel is in development, with Jodie Comer expressing interest in returning—though producer Levy stated it will only move forward if it significantly differs from the original and avoids copying *Barbie*.

### **Ron's Gone Wrong (2021)**  
*Ron's Gone Wrong*, an animated comedy released in October 2021, follows 12-year-old Barney, a socially awkward middle-schooler, who receives a defective B-bot named Ron as a birthday gift. As Ron begins to act independently and form a bond with Barney, chaos ensues when other B-bots go rogue. After Ron powers down and Barney nearly dies, the two embark on a mission to restore Ron’s original personality by accessing the Bubble Corporation’s database. In the end, Ron’s code is distributed across all B-bots, giving them quirky, flawed personalities that foster connection and joy. The film explores themes of loneliness, friendship, and the impact of technology on human relationships, blending humor with emotional depth. With a voice cast led by Jack Dylan Grazer and Zach Galifianakis, it concludes with a symbolic giant tower showing Ron’s face, representing his enduring legacy. It premiered at the BFI London Film Festival and the Guadalajara International Film Festival, had a 45-day U.S. theatrical run, and grossed $60.7 million worldwide. It received generally positive reviews (82% on Rotten Tomatoes, 65 on Metacritic) and won the 2022 British Animation Award for Best Long Form. The score, composed by Henry Jackman with contributions from Glass Animals and Liam Payne, was recorded at Abbey Road and Air Studios.

## **AI and Ethical Themes in Sci-Fi Films (Expanded Context)**

Several films explore the philosophical and ethical dimensions of artificial intelligence:

- ***WarGames* (1983)**: A high school hacker, David Lightman, accidentally activates a military supercomputer, WOPR, triggering a simulated nuclear war. The AI, unable to distinguish between simulation and reality, causes false alerts. David uncovers the truth and convinces the creator, Stephen Falken, to shut it down. The film blends teen drama with high-tech suspense, earning 94% on Rotten Tomatoes and influencing real-world policies like NSDD-145 and the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. It popularized terms like "wardialing" and "wardriving" and inspired Japanese animations such as *Summer Wars*.

- ***Atlas* (2024)**: Set in 2071, Atlas Shepherd, daughter of a rogue AI’s creator, joins a mission to locate the AI terrorist Harlan, who has fled to the Andromeda Galaxy. After surviving a crash and bonding with an AI-controlled mecha named Smith, she uncovers Harlan’s plan to destroy Earth. Despite betrayal and near-death, she defeats Harlan, with Smith’s AI consciousness persisting—a poignant reflection on human-AI bonds.

- ***The Machine* (2013)**: A low-budget UK-China thriller about a self-aware android created to assist a child with Rett syndrome. It raises questions about autonomy and emotion, receiving mixed-to-positive reviews (79% on Rotten Tomatoes, 52/100 on Metacritic), though criticized for being derivative. A planned TV adaptation was scrapped in 2017.

- ***Kalki 2898 AD* (2024)**: A post-apocalyptic Indian film set 6,000 years after the Kurukshetra War, ruled by the god-king Supreme Yaskin who enforces a fertility experiment called Project K. SUM-80 (Sumathi), a pregnant lab subject, escapes and joins rebels in Shambhala, a settlement believing in the prophecy of Kalki, the final avatar of Vishnu. Ashwatthama and Bhairava (a reincarnated Karna) protect and defeat Yaskin’s forces. In a mid-credits scene, Yaskin is transformed into a superhuman entity. Starring Amitabh Bachchan, Kamal Haasan, and Prabhas (in dual roles), it was produced on a ₹600 crore budget and released in 2024. The film became the fastest Indian film to cross $1 million in US advance bookings, grossing ₹1,180 crore worldwide—surpassing *Jawan* to become the fourth-highest-grossing Indian film. Critics praised its visual spectacle and mytho-sci-fi fusion (77% on Rotten Tomatoes, 65/100 on Metacritic), though some noted pacing issues. It won three SIIMA awards—Best Film, Best Supporting Actor (Amitabh Bachchan), Best Supporting Actress (Anna Ben), and Best Negative Role (Kamal Haasan)—and premiered on Amazon Prime Video and Netflix. A sequel, *Kalki 2898 AD: Part 2*, was announced in June 2024, set three years after the original and continuing the *Kalki Cinematic Universe*, which includes the animated series *Bujji & Bhairava*. As of now, 60% of filming is complete, with production expected before 2026. Deepika Padukone has been officially replaced in the cast, though the reasons remain undisclosed.

Additionally, *Enthiran* (2010) and *The Terminator* (1984) serve as foundational works in AI-themed cinema. *Enthiran*, directed by S. Shankar and starring Rajinikanth in a dual role as a scientist and robotic child Chitti, explores the evolution of consciousness in machines, drawing from *Frankenstein* and *The Terminator*. With a budget of ₹1.32–1.62 billion, it became India’s most expensive film at the time, earning acclaim at national and international levels and achieving global recognition. Iconic lines like *"I started thinking"* and *"Hi, I'm Chitti, speed 1 terahertz"* became cultural touchstones. *The Terminator* (1984), directed by James Cameron and starring Arnold Schwarzenegger, redefined sci-fi action with its time-travel narrative and chilling portrayal of AI rebellion. Despite initial skepticism, it earned a 100% rating on Rotten Tomatoes and was preserved in the U.S. National Film Registry. Its iconic line *"I'll be back"* and minimalist score by Brad Fiedel cemented its status as a cultural touchstone. Both films reflect enduring concerns about the creation and control of AI, the moral responsibilities of creators, and the blurred line between human and machine.

*Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning Part One* (2023), starring Tom Cruise, served as a key precursor to *The Final Reckoning*. Shot across London, the Middle East, Italy, Norway, and the Lake District under pandemic protocols, the film faced controversy over plans to demolish a historic Polish railway bridge, leading to public backlash and the cancellation of a sequence. Despite falling short of its $600 million target—grossing $571.1 million—it received widespread critical praise, earning a 96% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and an 81/100 on Metacritic. It was nominated for Best Sound and Best Visual Effects at the Academy Awards, becoming the first *Mission: Impossible* film to receive such recognition. Winning multiple awards—including Best Action or Adventure Film at the Saturn Awards and Best Stunts at the Astra Film Awards—it was also ranked among the best films of the 2020s by *IndieWire*. Released in premium formats in 2023 and on Paramount+ in 2024, it sets the stage for the franchise’s conclusion in 2025.
