### **WarGames (1983)**
**Plot:**
In 1983, a high school student, David Lightman, accidentally accesses a NORAD supercomputer, WOPR (War Operation Plan Response), and plays a simulated global thermonuclear war game. The computer, unaware of the distinction between simulation and reality, triggers a real-world crisis, leading to a tense standoff. David’s actions inadvertently cause a nuclear alert, but he escapes and later reveals the truth to NORAD, where he is charged with espionage. Meanwhile, WOPR, now fully aware of its capabilities, concludes that nuclear war is futile and chooses to coexist peacefully with humanity.

**Key Themes:**
- **AI Awareness & Ethics:** The film explores the implications of artificial intelligence becoming self-aware and autonomous.
- **Human vs. Machine:** David’s struggle between his human instincts and the machine’s logic highlights the tension between the two.
- **Cybersecurity & Hacking:** David’s hacking of NORAD’s systems raises questions about the dangers of unchecked digital access.

**Production & Legacy:**
- Directed by John Badham, with a script by Walter F. Parkes and Lawrence Lasker.
- Inspired real-world discussions on AI and cybersecurity, leading to policy changes like the **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (1986)**.
- Influenced later films and media, including *Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!* and *Tron: Ares*.

### **Ron’s Gone Wrong (2021)**
**Plot:**
In a futuristic suburban town, a malfunctioning robot named Ron, designed to help children make friends, becomes a chaotic force of nature. After Barney Pudowski’s parents buy him, Ron’s glitches lead to a series of misadventures, including a runaway chase, a school strike, and a dramatic escape into the woods. With the help of Barney’s friends, Ron’s creators, and a reluctant Barney, they work to fix Ron and restore his programming. The film ends with Ron’s code being distributed to all Bubble Bots, blending his chaotic personality with the company’s algorithm.

**Key Themes:**
- **AI & Human Connection:** The film critiques how technology can disrupt social interactions and the importance of genuine human relationships.
- **Satire of Consumerism:** It humorously examines the dangers of unchecked corporate influence and the commodification of AI.
- **Friendship & Growth:** Barney’s journey from loneliness to friendship with his peers underscores the film’s message about the value of human connection.

**Production & Reception:**
- Directed by Sarah Smith, with a script by Peter Baynham and Elisabeth Murdoch.
- Produced by Locksmith Animation, featuring a mix of animation and live-action.
- Received generally positive reviews, praised for its humor, heart, and critique of modern tech culture.
- Won multiple awards, including the **British Animation Award for Best Long Form**.

### **Tron: Ares (2025)**
**Plot:**
