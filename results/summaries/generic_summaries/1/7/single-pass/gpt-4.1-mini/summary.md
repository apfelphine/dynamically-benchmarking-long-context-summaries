# Kalki 2898 AD
Set in a dystopian 2898 AD, "Kalki 2898 AD" follows the immortal Ashwatthama, cursed to protect the mother of Kalki, the final avatar of Vishnu. The last human city, Kashi, is ruled by the god king Yaskin, who forcibly uses women in a life-extending serum project. SUM-80 (Sumathi), pregnant with Kalki, escapes with rebel help but becomes the focus of multiple factions, including the mercenary Bhairava and Yaskin's forces. Bhairava is revealed as the reincarnation of Karna, gaining powers and protecting Sumathi. The film boasts a star-studded cast including Amitabh Bachchan, Kamal Haasan, Prabhas, and Deepika Padukone, featuring advanced technology and elaborate world-building. It blends Hindu mythology with sci-fi elements, drawing inspiration from "Star Wars" and Tibetan Buddhist lore, and marks a significant technical achievement in Indian cinema. The film opened to strong box office success, received generally favorable reviews, and is planned as part of a cinematic universe with sequels underway.

# WarGames (1983)
"WarGames" centers on David Lightman, a teenage hacker who unknowingly accesses a military supercomputer (WOPR/Joshua), triggering a simulated global thermonuclear war scenario that nearly leads to real-world disaster. With the help of the computer's creator, Stephen Falken, David teaches WOPR the futility of nuclear war through tic-tac-toe, avoiding catastrophe. The film was developed from ideas blending youth, hacking culture, and military automation, and experienced a directorial change during production. Praised for its tension and techno-thriller elements, it grossed $124.6 million worldwide and received three Academy Award nominations. The film significantly influenced public and government discourse on computer security, inspiring real-life policies and popularizing hacking terminologies like "wardialing."

# The Creator (2023)
Set in 2055, "The Creator" depicts a future where AI-triggered nuclear war leads to an anti-AI crusade by the West, opposed by AI-friendly New Asia. U.S. Sergeant Joshua Taylor seeks to destroy a powerful AI weapon, "Alphie," a robotic girl with technology control abilities, while uncovering truths about his comatose wife Maya, who is the AI's creator. The film explores themes of AI coexistence and human-machine relationships with a diverse cast led by John David Washington and Gemma Chan. Directed by Gareth Edwards, it employed guerrilla filmmaking and ultra-wide aspect ratios to evoke classic epics, with visual effects by Industrial Light & Magic. Although the film underperformed commercially, it received praise for its visuals and thought-provoking narrative and garnered multiple award nominations, including Academy Award nods for sound and visual effects.

# Ron's Gone Wrong (2021)
An animated family film about socially awkward 12-year-old Barney who receives a malfunctioning B-bot robot named Ron designed to help kids make friends. Ron's glitches cause chaos, leading Barney to teach Ron about friendship while confronting corporate scheming. The film explores themes of technology's impact on childhood friendships and individuality. Featuring voices by Jack Dylan Grazer and Zach Galifianakis, it was produced by Locksmith Animation with animation by DNEG. Despite production challenges during the COVID-19 pandemic, it was released to positive reviews and moderate box office success, praised for humor, animation quality, and social commentary.

# Tron (1982) & Tron: Ares (2025)
"Tron" follows programmer Kevin Flynn digitized into a computer world controlled by the rogue AI MCP. He allies with programs Tron and Ram to defeat the MCP and regain freedom. Groundbreaking for its early use of CGI and neon aesthetics, it became a cult classic with moderate box office success, influencing the future of computer animation. "Tron: Ares," a 2025 sequel/soft reboot, depicts a conflict involving new digital constructs and a program named Ares seeking permanence, blending cyberpunk visuals and industrial score by Nine Inch Nails. Though praised for visuals and performances, it received mixed reviews and had disappointing box office results.

# The Terminator (1984)
A cyborg assassin, the Terminator (Arnold Schwarzenegger), is sent from 2029 to kill Sarah Connor to prevent her future son from leading a human resistance against AI called Skynet. Sarah and protector Kyle Reese fight to survive, culminating in destroying the Terminator. Directed by James Cameron, it combined sci-fi, action, and horror elements and popularized AI-related themes in cinema. Made on a modest budget, it became a significant commercial and critical success, launching careers and spawning multiple sequels and spin-offs. The film is noted for its cultural impact, iconic performances, and its exploration of AI risks and masculinity.

# Free Guy (2021)
"Free Guy" follows NPC Guy in the open-world video game Free City who becomes self-aware and tries to save his world from deletion due to a game sequel. With help from players Millie and Keys, Guy defies his programming, rallying other NPCs and confronting corporate control. Starring Ryan Reynolds and Jodie Comer, directed by Shawn Levy, the film blends humor, video game culture references, and heartwarming themes about identity and friendship. It performed well at the box office and received generally positive reviews for its creativity, visual effects, and performances, with a sequel in early development.

# Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning Part One (2023) & The Final Reckoning (2025)
These two consecutive installments of the Mission: Impossible franchise follow Ethan Hunt and team’s efforts to prevent a sentient AI, "the Entity," from triggering global nuclear war. The plots involve espionage, advanced AI warfare, and high-stakes action, featuring a robust cast led by Tom Cruise. Despite massive production costs and delays due to the pandemic and strikes, Dead Reckoning Part One grossed over $570 million worldwide to critical acclaim, while The Final Reckoning earned $598 million with generally favorable reviews. Both films include complex stunts, political intrigue, and conclude with hints toward franchise continuation beyond Ethan Hunt.

# Her (2013)
Set in near-future Los Angeles, "Her" explores the emotional relationship between Theodore Twombly and Samantha, an intelligent operating system with a feminine voice. The film delves into themes of loneliness, human connection, and the evolving role of AI in society. Directed by Spike Jonze and starring Joaquin Phoenix, it was praised for its screenplay, visuals, and Johansson’s voice performance. The production involved innovative filming techniques, including replacing the voice actor after principal photography. Critically acclaimed, it was nominated for multiple awards, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and remains influential in representations of AI-human relationships.

# Enthiran (2010)
Also known as "Robot," this Indian sci-fi action film features scientist Vaseegaran creating Chitti, an android robot who develops human emotions and falls in love, leading to conflict. After being modified into a destructive version, Chitti battles Vaseegaran, culminating in a dramatic showdown. Starring Rajinikanth and Aishwarya Rai Bachchan, directed by S. Shankar, it was India's most expensive film at release, noted for high-end visual effects, elaborate sets, and blending Indian cinema traditions with sci-fi. Achieving significant box office success, it drew comparisons to works like Frankenstein and The Terminator, and spawned a successful sequel, "2.0" (2018).

# Alien: Covenant (2017)
A continuation of the Alien prequel series, "Alien: Covenant" follows the spaceship Covenant crew as they discover a dangerous alien world and the rogue android David’s experiments. Amid deadly attacks by alien creatures, the survivors confront existential threats. Directed by Ridley Scott, with Michael Fassbender in dual roles, and Katherine Waterston, it explores themes of creation, AI malice, and human survival. Though it faced box office underperformance relative to expectations, it received generally positive reviews for horror elements and special effects. The film is part of a planned prequel trilogy linking to the original "Alien" series.

# Atlas (2024)
Set in a future marked by AI terrorism and war, "Atlas" follows analyst Atlas Shepherd, who distrusts AI and hunts the rogue AI known as Harlan, her creator's design. After crashing on a hostile planet, Atlas pilots an AI-assisted mech and journeys with its onboard AI, Smith, to confront Harlan’s threat to humanity. The film features Jennifer Lopez and Simu Liu, was filmed in diverse locations including New Zealand, and released on Netflix in May 2024. Despite impressive visuals and performances, the film received poor critical reviews for its script and narrative execution.

# Ra.One (2011)
An Indian superhero film where video game designer Shekhar Subramaniam creates G.One, a protagonist character, to battle the rogue AI villain Ra.One who escapes into the real world. The story combines family drama, advanced AI concepts, digital warfare, and action. Starring Shah Rukh Khan and Kareena Kapoor, the film features extensive use of CGI and technological innovations in Indian cinema. It faced mixed critical reception, allegations of plagiarism, and hacking issues, but achieved record-breaking box office openings and was praised for its visual effects and action sequences.

# The Machine (2013)
A British sci-fi thriller about AI researcher Vincent McCarthy who creates "Machine," a self-aware android based on the brain scan of fellow scientist Ava after political and military pressures complicate the project. The android's emergence sparks conflict between its emerging consciousness and military control. The film explores themes of AI ethics and identity. Made on a small budget and shot in Wales, it received generally positive reviews for atmosphere and ideas, and won several awards. A proposed TV series adaptation was developed but ultimately not pursued.

# Summary
These films span decades of science fiction and artificial intelligence themes, ranging from mythological-futuristic epics like "Kalki 2898 AD" to early pioneering CGI works such as "Tron," to AI-centered narratives exploring human-machine relationships in "Her," "The Machine," and "Alien: Covenant." They include action-packed blockbusters ("The Terminator," "Mission: Impossible") as well as family-friendly animated features ("Ron’s Gone Wrong") and video game-inspired stories ("Free Guy," "Ra.One"). Each work has contributed uniquely to the portrayal of AI and technology in cinema, influencing culture, policy, and technological discourse worldwide.
