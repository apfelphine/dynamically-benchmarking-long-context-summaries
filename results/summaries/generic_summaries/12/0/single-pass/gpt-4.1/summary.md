## Scholarship and Fellowship Programs

### Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
The Muskie Internship Program, named after the influential Maine politician and U.S. Secretary of State Edmund S. Muskie, provides internships, career training, and financial support for Eurasian graduate students in the U.S. Established in 1992, it aims to foster professional development and cultural exchange. Muskie's legacy also includes notable achievements in environmental legislation, civil rights, and the federal budget process.

### Marshall Scholarship
Created in 1953 as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship enables intellectually distinguished young Americans to study at any UK university. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and strengthening U.S.-UK relations. Selection is highly competitive, involving university endorsement and regional interviews. Scholars receive funding for postgraduate study and are expected to act as cultural ambassadors.

### Fulbright Program
Founded in 1946, the Fulbright Program is the largest international educational exchange program, supporting students, scholars, teachers, and professionals in academic and cultural exchanges between the U.S. and 160+ countries. Grants cover various disciplines, with selection based on academic merit, leadership potential, and adaptability. The program operates through bilateral partnerships and commissions; recent years have seen political challenges and funding concerns.

### Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902, the Rhodes Scholarship is one of the world's oldest and most prestigious postgraduate awards, supporting study at Oxford University. It aims to foster leadership, academic excellence, and public service across diverse backgrounds. While historically criticized for exclusionary practices, the program has expanded globally and become more inclusive, though controversies over its founder's legacy persist. Selection is highly competitive, with a focus on both intellect and character.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Created in 1964 as a British memorial to JFK, the Kennedy Scholarship funds up to 10 British postgraduates annually for study at Harvard or MIT. Scholars are selected for academic achievement, communication ability, and public service potential. The Trust is managed by prominent trustees and continues to honor the spirit of transatlantic partnership.

### Harkness Fellowship
Initiated in 1925, the Harkness Fellowships originally supported international study across multiple fields and now focus on health policy for leaders from nine countries. Fellows spend a year conducting research at U.S. institutions and attending policy seminars. The program is administered by the Commonwealth Fund and aims to foster international collaboration.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Germany’s largest and oldest scholarship foundation, Studienstiftung supports academically gifted students and doctoral candidates based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission occurs through nomination, competition, or self-assessment, with financial and academic support provided. The foundation offers specialized programs and is notable for its influential alumni across science, politics, and arts.

### Bolashak
Established in 1993 by Kazakhstan's government, the Bolashak Scholarship funds Kazakh students for study at leading global universities in over 30 countries, with the goal of modernizing Kazakhstan through Western education and values. Selection emphasizes language competence, and alumni often hold key positions domestically.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program
Founded by the State of Florida, Bright Futures provides merit-based scholarships with multiple award levels for post-secondary study in Florida. The program funds tuition for students meeting academic and service requirements and has evolved through several reforms addressing accessibility and funding.

### National Merit Scholarship Program
A U.S. academic competition for high school students based on PSAT/NMSQT performance, the National Merit Scholarship Program recognizes top students with scholarships funded by the NMSC, corporations, and colleges. Finalists are selected through academic achievement, recommendation, and personal essays. The associated National Achievement Scholarship for Black Americans now operates through UNCF.

### King's Scholar
“King's Scholar” designates recipients of historic and academic scholarships at prestigious UK schools (e.g., Eton College, Westminster, Cambridge, and King's Schools) dating back to the 14th century. Awarded for academic excellence or competition, the title carries unique privileges and traditions, with many notable alumni across fields.

### Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by Chinese businessman Koo, the Shuping Scholarship assists academically strong, economically disadvantaged students in China, emphasizing service and self-governance. It has supported thousands of scholars and established enduring foundations to continue its mission.

### United Negro College Fund (UNCF) & Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
UNCF, established in 1944, provides scholarships to students (mainly at HBCUs) and is supported by major donors and fundraising events. TMCF, founded in 1987, focuses on supporting public HBCUs through scholarships, capacity building, and partnerships. Both foundations are central to Black higher education in the U.S.

## Other Major Organizations

### British Council
Established in 1934, the British Council is the UK's primary organization for cultural diplomacy, English language promotion, educational exchange, and arts. Operating in 100+ countries, it administers programs like IELTS testing, school partnerships, and cultural festivals. It has faced geopolitical challenges, the closure of offices, and criticism over policies, outsourcing, and accountability.

### Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Inspired by the landmark anthologies edited by Margaret Busby, the New Daughters of Africa Award provides scholarships for African women pursuing higher education, funded through book proceeds and author support. The anthologies have significantly expanded the visibility of Black women's writing globally.
