# Final Summary

## Edmund S. Muskie

Edmund S. Muskie, a Maine-born son of Polish immigrants and graduate of Bates College and Cornell Law, served in the Navy Reserve during WWII. His extensive political career included roles as state legislator, bipartisan Governor focusing on environment and growth, five-term U.S. Senator advancing environmental protection, civil rights, and fiscal reform, and opposition to Vietnam escalation and Nixon’s executive overreach. Nationally, he was the 1968 Democratic Vice Presidential nominee, a 1970s presidential hopeful targeted by covert attacks, Secretary of State (1980–81), and a member of the Iran-Contra Tower Commission. His legacy endures in environmental and civil rights leadership, reflected by the Presidential Medal of Freedom and the Muskie Internship Program promoting public service ideals.

## Major Scholarship and Fellowship Programs

### Marshall Scholarship

Founded in 1953 to honor George C. Marshall, the program supports distinguished American postgraduates to study at leading UK institutions based on academic excellence and leadership. The Association of Marshall Scholars enhances UK–US relations, honors outstanding contributions with the Marshall Medal, and counts among alumni Supreme Court justices, Nobel laureates, Pulitzer winners, CIA chiefs, and world leaders.

### King’s Scholars Tradition

Since 1317 at Cambridge and expanded through Eton and other King’s Schools, King’s Scholars receive prestigious status and financial support through competitive exams. Alumni include British Prime Ministers and influential cultural figures.

### Miss America Pageant

Established in 1919 to boost Atlantic City tourism, the pageant featured the iconic swimsuit competition until abolished in 2018. It evolved inclusively with the first Black winner in 1983 and broadened eligibility for marginalized groups. The pageant has faced political controversies, leadership reforms, COVID-19 disruptions, and bankruptcy filings amid ongoing disputes, including a historic transgender winner in 2022.

### Norway Scholarship

Launched in 1919 for Norway–UK academic exchange, interrupted by war, then revived and expanded to sciences and social sciences with a diverse recent scholar body.

### Fulbright Program

Created in 1946 to promote peace via global academic and cultural exchanges, the Fulbright Program expanded under the Fulbright–Hays Act (1961) and includes multi-level study, research, teaching, and cultural exchange opportunities. Recent challenges include bans on Russian candidates and US funding freezes sparking resignations, pointing to increasing politicization. The program features Hubert H. Humphrey fellowships, global governance through bilateral commissions, and extensive alumni networks.

### J. William Fulbright Prize and Alumni

This prize honors landmark contributions to global understanding, awarded to Nobel laureates and world leaders. The Fulbright Program has over 325,000 diverse alumni including numerous laureates and pioneers.

### Kennedy Scholarship

Established in 1964 to honor President John F. Kennedy, it funds British study at Harvard or MIT with trustees appointed by the UK Prime Minister. Selection prioritizes intellect and public service; notable alumni include prominent UK politicians.

### British Council

Founded in 1934 to promote UK culture and English language worldwide, playing vital roles in wartime and postwar diplomacy. Operating in over 100 countries, it invests in education, social change, and the arts and co-administers IELTS. The council faces financial and operational challenges under Chair Paul Thompson and CEO Scott McDonald.

### Bright Futures Scholarship (Florida)

A merit-based tuition program inspired by Georgia’s HOPE, Bright Futures has faced criticism for disparities and was reformed to tighten eligibility and diversify award categories, including academic and vocational scholarships.

### Rhodes Scholarship

Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes to foster UK–US and English-speaking world unity at Oxford, the Rhodes Trust now manages the globally expanded and inclusive program covering full tuition and living expenses. Despite debates over its colonial legacy, it boasts internationally eminent alumni and inspired numerous global scholarships.

### National Merit Scholarship Program

A US high school academics program using PSAT/NMSQT to award about 8,000 corporate- and university-sponsored scholarships annually. While criticized for scope and transparency, it has produced notable figures like Bill Gates, Elena Kagan, Jeff Bezos, and Ted Cruz. University of Florida leads in National Merit scholar enrollment.

### Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* Anthology and Award

The 1992 anthology celebrated over 200 African-descended women writers and remains foundational to Black feminist literature. Contributors include Maya Angelou and Toni Morrison. This work inspired the *New Daughters of Africa* (2019) anthology and an associated literary award supporting African women scholars.

### Bolashak Scholarship Program (Kazakhstan)

Launched in 1993 by President Nazarbayev to send Kazakh youth for Western education, it has awarded over 12,800 scholarships by 2018, with alumni prominent in government and business sectors.

### Harkness Fellowship

Founded in 1925, refocused in 1997 on US–UK health policy research, offering scholarships, seminars, and policy dialogues. Alumni include Nobel laureates and leaders in health policy.

### United Negro College Fund (UNCF) and Member HBCUs

Since 1944, UNCF supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and Black students, raising substantial funds from major donors. It encompasses over 50 HBCUs varying in size, affiliation, and mission, mostly in the Southern US.

### Shuping Scholarship (Shanghai)

Founded in 1939 during the Japanese invasion to aid poor capable students with comprehensive support, revived in 1986 with a sustained endowment and expanded reach.

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF) and Member Schools

Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs, TMCF has raised over $500 million and acquired Opportunity Funding Corporation to foster entrepreneurship. Serving 55 member institutions including law and medical schools, it operates under CEO Harry L. Williams.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (German National Academic Foundation)

Originally founded in 1925, suppressed in 1934, and re-founded in 1948, it supports exceptionally talented German and EU students through multiple admission pathways regardless of citizenship or age. Scholarships provide comprehensive academic, financial, and mentoring support, including travel and event funding. Reporting requirements and community engagement are integral. As of 2023, over 14,800 scholars enrolled, comprising about 0.5% of German university students, with strong representation in both public and private institutions.

### Studienstiftung Notable Alumni

The foundation counts numerous distinguished alumni:

- **Natural Sciences:** Nobel laureates (e.g., Manfred Eigen, Reinhard Genzel), Fields Medalists (Gerd Faltings, Peter Scholze), and multiple Leibniz Prize recipients (e.g., Martin Beneke, Magdalena Götz).

- **Social Sciences:** Prominent sociologists (Ulrich Beck), economists (Ulrike Malmendier), and legal historians (Reinhard Zimmermann).

- **Business and NGOs:** Influential figures like Sun Microsystems co-founder Andreas von Bechtolsheim and executives from Daimler, Goldman Sachs, McKinsey, and Boston Consulting Group.

- **Politics and Civil Service:** Politicians including Fritz Kuhn, Frauke Petry, Karl Schiller, and first female minister Christine Teusch; judges like Andreas Paulus.

- **Arts:** Renowned composers, writers (Hans Magnus Enzensberger, Juli Zeh), musicians (Igor Levit), and architect Frei Otto.

- **Journalism:** Prominent journalists including Petra Gerster, Claus Kleber, Frank Schirrmacher, and Ulrike Meinhof.
