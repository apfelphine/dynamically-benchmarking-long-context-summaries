## Overview of Major International Scholarships, Fellowships, and Academic Programs

---

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund & United Negro College Fund

- **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**: Founded in 1987, TMCF supports 55 public HBCUs and PBIs with institutional advocacy, scholarships, and capacity-building grants, having raised over $500 million. Backed by major corporate donations, it also acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to enhance entrepreneurship within HBCUs. Leadership has included Harry L. Williams (CEO since 2018). Member institutions range from 4-year universities to law and medical schools.

- **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**: Established in 1944, UNCF funds scholarships open to all ethnicities but primarily benefits African-American students at 37 private HBCUs. UNCF is renowned for its fundraising (over $200 million from the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars" and significant corporate/philanthropic gifts) and its iconic motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste." Alumni include MLK Jr., Alexis Herman, and Spike Lee. The fund administers scholarships and advocates for long-term HBCU sustainability.

### Global and Governmental Scholarship Programs

- **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**: Germany's largest merit-based scholarship provider for university students and postgraduates, founded in 1925, dissolved under the Nazis, and re-established post-WWII. Selection is via nomination, competitive exams, or achievements in competitions. Scholars receive financial and academic support, with extensions for doctoral candidates and access to specialized programs and summer academies. As of 2023, over 14,000 scholars enrolled.

- **Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)**: Established in 1993 to promote Western education among Kazakh youth. Over 12,800 awarded by 2018, with rigorous selection and language testing; the program mostly benefits ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements. Recipients study at prestigious universities worldwide and return to hold key roles in Kazakhstan’s organizations.

- **Shuping Scholarship (China)**: Founded during WWII to support scholars from poor families in Shanghai, later expanded to Beijing and Huzhou. The scheme combines financial aid with community service and extracurricular activities, funded by the Koo family and an endowment managed by local alumni. It has awarded scholarships to thousands since its inception and fosters lifelong service and alumni involvement.

### Academic Merit and College Access

- **National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)**: Utilizes PSAT/NMSQT scores to recognize and award high school students. The progression is from Commended Scholar to Semifinalist and Finalist, with about 8,000 of 15,000 Finalists earning scholarships annually. The concurrent National Achievement Scholarship targeted Black American students until 2015. Notable alumni include Bill Gates, Mae Jemison, and Elena Kagan. The selection method has drawn criticism for its reliance on standardized tests.

- **Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)**: State-funded merit program aiding post-secondary education for Florida residents, covering up to 100% of tuition for top academic achievers. The program has faced criticism and policy changes regarding standardized test score thresholds, leading to fluctuating eligibility and impact on minority students.

### Prestigious Fellowship Programs

- **Harkness Fellowship**: Since 1925, provides international scholars, particularly in health policy, opportunities to study and research in the US, promoting transatlantic collaboration. Now focused on healthcare policy, fellows participate in notable research institutions and governmental briefings.

- **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**: Linked to the influential “Daughters of Africa” and “New Daughters of Africa” anthologies, these awards support African women in higher education in the UK. The anthologies have significantly increased visibility for Black women writers and inspired global literary engagement.

---

### International Scholarship Comparisons

- **Kennedy Scholarship (UK → USA)**: Memorial to JFK, enabling British scholars to study at Harvard or MIT with selection criteria similar to Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships; alumni include politicians, economists, and academics.

- **Rhodes Scholarship**: Since 1902, supports global postgraduates at Oxford based on academic excellence, leadership, and commitment to service. It has evolved to include women and a more diverse pool, though it’s been subject to criticism for its colonial legacy and selection outcomes.

- **Marshall Scholarship**: Established in 1953 to recognize US leadership in postwar Europe, it funds up to 50 scholars annually for postgraduate study at any UK university. Selection prioritizes intellectual achievement and leadership, fostering Anglo-American understanding.

- **Norway Scholarship**: Since 1919, enables Norwegian students from Oslo to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Managed by committee, it has supported dozens of scholars across various fields.

---

### Cultural Exchange and Academic Cooperation

- **British Council**: Since 1934, the British Council promotes English language education and UK culture globally, administers exams (notably IELTS), and runs large-scale educational and arts programs. Funded as a public charity, it has faced diplomatic, financial, and political challenges, as well as criticism regarding commercial priorities and outsourcing.

---

### Historic Academic Scholarships

- **King’s Scholar**: Originating in medieval England, King’s and Queen’s Scholarships support top students at elite schools (Eton, Westminster, Ely, etc.) and Cambridge. Scholars receive honors, privileges, and a tradition of academic excellence.

---

### The Fulbright Program

- **Fulbright Program**: Founded in 1946 to advance international understanding through educational exchange between the US and other countries. It funds scholars, students, teachers, and professionals across diverse fields, with bilateral commissions and notable alumni—89 Pulitzer and 62 Nobel laureates, plus heads of state. Recent years have witnessed political interference and international disruptions (notably the end of the program in Russia).

---

### Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

- **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**: Named for the influential US Senator and Secretary of State known for environmental legislation and diplomatic accomplishments, this program provides professional internships and training to Eurasian scholars in the US, continuing Muskie’s legacy in public service and global engagement.

---

### Miss America Pageant

- **Miss America**: Since 1921, the Miss America competition has evolved from a bathing beauty contest to a scholarship and “talent” competition. The organization has navigated issues of racial and gender inclusion, changing social norms, scandals, and format revisions—including and then partially reversing “Miss America 2.0”, which de-emphasized physical appearance. Notable alumni and winners have grappled publicly with the pageant’s shifting role and legacy.

---

## Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Social Mobility and Diversity**: All programs strive, to varying extents, to advance social mobility, academic excellence, cross-cultural understanding, and—over time—greater accessibility for women, minorities, and marginalized communities.
- **Criticisms and Controversy**: Many programs face public scrutiny—regarding selection bias, transparency, historic exclusion, commercial priorities, and their adaptation to shifting societal values.
- **Impact**: Alumni from these scholarships and fellowships have become heads of state, Nobel and Pulitzer laureates, civil rights leaders, influential academics, and industry innovators, underscoring their global impact.
