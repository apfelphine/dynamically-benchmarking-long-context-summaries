- **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**: Supports public HBCUs with over $500 million in funding, offering scholarships, capacity building, and programmatic support. It differs from the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) in scope and focus, supporting 55 HBCUs and emphasizing entrepreneurship and talent identification. It received major donations from Apple, Koch Foundation, and Boeing.

- **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**: Founded in 1944, it provides scholarships to African American students, with over 65,000 recipients in 2015. It has a strong history of supporting alumni in business, politics, and health. In 2025, MacKenzie Scott donated $70 million to support HBCU financial stability. The "A mind is a terrible thing to waste" motto is widely recognized.

- **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**: A German scholarship for undergraduate and graduate students, offering financial support, academic development, and summer academies. It emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility, with over 2,000 scholars in 2023.

- **Bolashak Scholarship**: Established in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s first president, it enables youth to study abroad and develop leadership. Over 12,000 scholarships have been awarded, with a focus on promoting Western democratic values.

- **National Merit Scholarship Program**: A U.S.-based program with rigorous academic requirements, offering merit-based scholarships. It has faced criticism for its reliance on standardized tests and gender bias. Notable alumni include Bill Gates, Stephen Hawking, and Barack Obama.

- **Harkness Fellowship**: A prestigious health policy fellowship in the U.S., established in 1925. It supports health policy research and international collaboration, with notable alumni in medicine, law, and public service.

- **Kennedy Scholarship**: A British program enabling students to study at Harvard or MIT. It emphasizes public service and leadership, with notable alumni in politics and diplomacy.

- **Rhodes Scholarship**: A global program founded in 1902, promoting international understanding through postgraduate study. It is highly selective, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%. It has a long history of producing leaders in government, academia, and civil society.

- **Marshall Scholarship**: Established in 1953, it enables American students to study in the UK. It is known for its academic excellence and leadership potential, with a strong focus on fostering U.S.-UK relations.

- **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**: A Florida-based merit-based program, initially criticized for subsidizing wealthier students. It has undergone reforms, including changes in score requirements and the addition of new scholarship levels.

- **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**: A scholarship supporting African women, launched in 2019. It promotes African women’s voices in literature and education, with notable alumni in writing and activism.

- **Fulbright Program**: A U.S. international exchange program promoting mutual understanding. It has faced political interference and controversy, with over 325,000 alumni, including Nobel laureates and U.S. officials.

- **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**: A U.S. Department of State initiative supporting Eurasian scholars. It aims to foster international academic and professional development.

- **Miss America Pageant**: A long-standing U.S. beauty pageant with evolving formats, including a shift to focus on personal development and social issues. It has faced criticism for sexism and racism, with notable milestones in diversity and inclusion.
