# Philanthropic and Educational Organizations

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). It has raised over \$500 million for scholarships, capacity building, and programmatic support. TMCF acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) in 2013 to enhance entrepreneurship curricula at HBCUs. Key donors include Apple (\$50 million in 2015) and the Charles Koch Foundation (\$25.6 million in 2017).

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944 by William J. Trent, the UNCF raises money for HBCUs, though its scholarships are open to all ethnicities. Prominent alumni include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Major philanthropic contributions include a \$70 million donation from MacKenzie Scott in 2025 and a \$120 million donation from Reed Hastings in 2020. The UNCF motto is "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

## Studienstiftung
This German foundation offers general scholarships for bachelor's and master's studies based on the principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility." Eligibility requires full-time study in Germany, an EU country, or Switzerland, without requiring German or EU citizenship. Admission is through multiple channels, including school, professor, and examination board nominations, or via an assessment test.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by Koo, this scholarship was established to support financially needy, academically gifted students, reflecting the motto "Return to the Society what you have gained from the society." The scholarship has been revived since 1986 and continues to encourage recipients toward service to their communities. Koo donated HK\$10 million to the Shanghai Education Development Foundation in 1995 to ensure long-term funding.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This program screens over 1.5 million entrants using the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT). Recognition levels include "Commended," "Semifinalist," and "Finalist." Most Merit Scholarship awards are funded by NMSC, supplemented by corporate sponsors. The National Achievement Scholarship Program transitioned to the UNCF in 2015, redirecting funds to support college graduates.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida scholarship program, which evolved from its HOPE Scholarship emulation, awards different levels of funding based on academic merit, not financial need. Levels include Florida Academic Scholar (FAS) and Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS). The program has undergone several overhauls, notably in 2018, to adjust requirements and award amounts.

# International Exchange and Prestigious Fellowships

## Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program aims to foster international peace and understanding through educational exchange, operating on a bi-national basis. It offers grants for U.S. citizens to study abroad (U.S. Student Program) and for foreign nationals to study in the U.S. (Foreign Student Program). The program is administered by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs. The J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding recognizes extraordinary contributions to global understanding.

## Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953, the Marshall Scholarship aims to enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK while strengthening British-American understanding. Scholars must be American citizens, and selection emphasizes intellect, character, and potential to contribute to society. The program's scope has expanded, with a 25% funding increase announced in 2023 to 50 places annually.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 on the will of Cecil John Rhodes, this highly selective scholarship promotes unity within the British Empire and strengthens diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. Since 2018, it is open globally. Selection criteria include scholastic attainments, moral character, and leadership potential, with the global acceptance rate standing at 0.7%.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 as a memorial to John F. Kennedy, this scholarship offers funding for students to attend Harvard or MIT. The selection process involves a national competition, a long-list, short-list, and an interview stage with the trustees. The trustees consider intellectual attainment, readiness to express oneself, and suitability of the proposed course of study.

## Harkness Fellowship
Since 1997, the Harkness Fellowships focus exclusively on health care policy. Fellows spend a year conducting research at American institutions, gaining insight into the U.S. healthcare system. Fellows attend a structured seminar program involving policy briefings on Capitol Hill and international symposiums.

## Norway Scholarship
This scholarship, initiated in 1919, allows Norwegian students to study at Wadham College in Oxford. Initially, its funding was inadequate, but efforts in the late 1970s and 1990s led to significant fundraising to meet rising costs.

## British Council
This organization, sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, promotes the UK and English language globally. It offers educational programs, runs examinations (including IELTS), and facilitates cultural exchange. It operates in over 100 countries but has faced controversies regarding its closures in Russia and Syria, and concerns over outsourcing functions to the Indian diaspora.

## Erasmus+
From 2014 to 2020, the British Council co-administered nearly €1 billion of the Erasmus+ program, which provides educational, training, youth, and sports opportunities for young people across Europe.

# Arts, Literature, and Culture

## Daughters of Africa
Edited by Margaret Busby, this anthology, published in 1992, gathers over 200 women writers of African descent, presenting a comprehensive view of their literary endeavors across continents and centuries. It was lauded for destroying misconceptions about the lack of literary output by these women and inspired a companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, in 2019. The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students.

## Miss America Pageant
Originating in 1920 in Atlantic City, the pageant has evolved from a beauty contest to a "competition," with significant changes reflecting cultural shifts. It has faced scrutiny over issues of racism and sexism, and its format has changed several times; notably, the 2018 "Miss America 2.0" removed judging based on physical appearance, though some of these changes have since been reversed.

# Politics, Law, and Civil Service

## Edmund S. Muskie
Muskie had a significant political career, serving as Governor of Maine (1955–1959) and U.S. Senator (1959–1980). He is widely recognized as the "father of the 1960s environmental movement in America," driving the passage of the Clean Air Act and the Clean Water Act. As Secretary of State (1980–1981), he managed negotiations during the Iran hostage crisis and engaged in diplomacy concerning the Soviet Union.

## J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding
This award honors individuals who have made significant contributions to international understanding. Its inaugural recipient was Nelson Mandela in 1993, and it continues to recognize global leaders and figures in various fields.

# Miscellaneous Scholarship/Programmatic Notes
*   **King's Scholars:** This title refers to various awards, including those at Eton College, where the scholarship is awarded based on examination, and at King's College Cambridge for obtaining first-class degrees.
*   **McCall MacBain Foundation:** This foundation donated £75 million toward the Rhodes Trust's fundraising efforts in 2013.
*   **Bolashak:** Instituted by Nursultan Nazarbayev in 1993, this Kazakh scholarship is intended to instill Western, democratic values in the youth, with graduates often holding high positions in Kazakh organizations.
*   **Cosmic/Scientific Achievements:** Notable alumni from these various programs include Nobel laureates in chemistry and physics (e.g., Roger Tsien, Manfred Eigen), and figures in technology and medicine (e.g., Ray Dolby).
