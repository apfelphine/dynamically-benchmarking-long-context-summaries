### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**

#### **History and Mission**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** was established in **1987** under Dr. N. Joyce Payne’s leadership, initially funded by major corporations like Sony, Miller Brewing, and the NBA. It evolved from a scholarship fund to a **501(c)(3) nonprofit** supporting **public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)** through **programmatic support, capacity building, and scholarships**. Unlike the **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**, which supports a broader range of institutions, TMCF focuses exclusively on **55 member schools**, including **HBCUs, Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), and community colleges**.

#### **Leadership and Structure**
- **Current Leadership**: Harry L. Williams (President & CEO, 2018–present).
- **Historical Leadership**: Johnny C. Taylor Jr. (2010–2018), Dwayne Ashley (1999–2010), and Johnny Parham (1994–1999).
- **Member Schools**: Includes **42 HBCUs (e.g., Tuskegee University, Howard University)**, **3 PBIs**, **7 Historically Black Community Colleges**, and **1 technical college**.

#### **Funding and Impact**
- **Funding**: Over **$500 million** raised to date, including major donations from **Apple ($50M, 2015)**, **Charles Koch Foundation ($25.6M, 2017)**, and **The Boeing Company ($6M, 2018)**.
- **Acquisitions**: Acquired **Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC)** in 2013, expanding its focus on **talent identification and entrepreneurship**.

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**

#### **History and Purpose**
Founded in **1944** by activists like **William J. Trent, Frederick D. Patterson, and Mary McLeod Bethune**, UNCF was created to address **educational inequities** for African Americans. Unlike TMCF, UNCF supports **900+ colleges and universities**, with **65,000+ students annually** receiving grants and scholarships. In **2008**, UNCF rebranded to focus on **African diaspora writers**, publishing *Daughters of Africa* (1992) and its sequel, *New Daughters of Africa* (2019).

#### **Scholarships and Legacy**
- **Scholarships**: Open to all ethnicities but predominantly awarded to African Americans, supporting students at member institutions and beyond.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Nobel Peace Prize laureates**.
- **Fundraising**: Key initiatives include the **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars** (telethon raising over **$200M**), the **Walk for Education**, and the **Cypresswood Golf Tournament**.

#### **Controversies and Criticisms**
- **Fundraising Controversies**: Criticized for **political interference** (e.g., ending support for the Koch Foundation over political stance).
- **Scholarship Redistribution**: In **2025**, billionaire philanthropist **MacKenzie Scott** donated **$70M** to UNCF to address **HBCU funding disparities**.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
#### **History and Scholarship Model**
- **Founded in 1925** as a **German elite scholarship program**, revived after WWII in **1948** under **Adolf Grimme**.
- **Eligibility**: Open to **exceptionally talented students** (academic, social, and extracurricular excellence), with no citizenship requirements.
- **Selection Process**: Three-day seminars with interviews and group discussions, with **26.6% of nominees** admitted.

#### **Scholarship Benefits**
- **Financial Support**: Monthly stipends (€300–€1,650), health insurance, and **tax-free** awards.
- **Academic Support**: Summer academies, research groups, and mentorship programs.
- **Doctoral Scholarships**: Available for **prosocial PhD students** with research funding.

#### **Statistics and Demographics**
- **Scholarship Recipients**: **2,179 undergraduates and 301 doctoral scholars** in **2023**.
- **Gender Diversity**: **55.4% women** in 2020.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes **Nobel Prize winners (e.g., Manfred Eigen, Gerd Faltings)** and **political leaders (e.g., Angela Merkel, Gerhard Schröder)**.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
#### **History**
- **Founded in 1939** by **Koo Soh Bing**, a philanthropist, to support **poor but academically gifted students** in Shanghai.
- **Unique Features**: Continuous scholarships (renewable until graduation), **free medical care, library access, and extracurricular activities** (e.g., typing, martial arts).

#### **Legacy**
- **Revived in 1986** with support from **government and alumni**, now awarding **700+ scholarships annually**.
- **Philanthropic Impact**: Donated **HK$10M (1995)** to the **Shanghai Education Development Foundation**.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
#### **Selection Process**
- **Entry Requirements**: Must be a **U.S. citizen/permanent resident**, take the **PSAT/NMSQT (junior year)**, and meet academic criteria.
- **Rounds**:
  1. **PSAT/NMSQT**: Top **50,000** scores advance to **National Merit Semifinalists**.
  2. **Semifinalists**: **16,000** selected based on state quotas.
  3. **Finalists**: **15,000** advance after SAT verification, academic record review, and essays.
  4. **Scholarships**: **8,000** receive **$2,500 awards**; **4,600** receive **college-sponsored scholarships**.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Transparency Issues**: Critics argue the **selection process lacks full transparency**.
- **Criticism of Recipients**: Some scholars enter **business/law** instead of public service, raising concerns about **social mobility**.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
#### **History**
- **Created in 1997** to emulate **Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship**, initially covering **42,000+ scholarships** ($70M+).
- **Reforms**: **2011–2021** saw **SAT score increases**, reduced scholarship coverage (e.g., **20% of Florida high school graduates** in 2015–16).

#### **Scholarship Levels**
1. **Florida Academic Scholar (FAS)**: **100% tuition coverage** (SAT ≥ 1330).
2. **Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS)**: **75% tuition coverage** (SAT ≥ 1190).
3. **Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV)**: **Career-focused programs** (SAT ≥ 24/25).
4. **Academic Top Scholar (ATS)**: **Bonus funding** for top-performing students.

#### **History**
- **Founded in 1902** by **Cecil Rhodes**, initially for **British students** to study in the U.S.

- **Politics**: **Bill Clinton (U.S. President)**, **Wasim Sajjad (Pakistan President)**.
