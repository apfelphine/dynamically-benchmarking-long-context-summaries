### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**

#### **History**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** was modeled after Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship, initially awarding over 42,000 scholarships worth $70 million. At its peak in 2008, it supported 39% of Florida high school graduates, including 94% of incoming freshmen and 70% of undergraduates at the University of Florida. Originally merit-based, it offered two levels: **A Level** (100% tuition coverage) and **B Level** (75% coverage). Critics accused it of subsidizing wealthy families using lottery funds, and the program faced cuts in 2011, raising SAT score requirements and reducing awards. By 2015, it covered only 20% of graduates, with average annual payments of $2,000. The program underwent a major overhaul in 2018, renaming levels to **Florida Academic Scholar (FAS)** and **Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS)**, and introducing new awards like the **Academic Top Scholar (ATS)**. Recent controversies include debates over funding, eligibility, and its impact on minority students.

#### **Scholarships**
**Eligibility** requires U.S. citizenship, Florida residency, high school graduation (or GED), no felony convictions, and acceptance into a Florida post-secondary institution. Scholarships are categorized into four levels:
- **FAS**: Requires a weighted GPA of 3.50, SAT/ACT scores (1330+), and 100 volunteer hours.
- **FMS**: Requires a weighted GPA of 3.00, SAT/ACT scores (1190+), and 75 volunteer hours.
- **Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV)**: Requires a GPA of 3.00 in career education courses, SAT/ACT scores (24+ in reading/writing, 24+ in math), and 30 volunteer hours.
- **Gold Seal CAPE Scholars (GSC)**: Requires 5 post-secondary credit hours through Career and Professional Education (CAPE) certifications, 30 volunteer hours, and eligibility for vocational programs.

**Renewal** requires maintaining a GPA (3.00 for FAS, 2.75 for others) and enrolling in at least six semester hours per term. Scholarships are renewable for up to five years or a specified number of semester hours.

**Award amounts** vary by level:
- **FAS**: 100% tuition at public institutions, comparable amounts at private institutions.
- **FMS**: 100% tuition at Florida College System member institutions; 75% at others.
- **GSV**: $39/credit hour for career certificate programs, $48/credit hour for technical degree programs.
- **GSC**: $39/credit hour for career certificate programs, $49/credit hour for technical degree programs.
- **ATS**: Additional $44/credit hour on top of FAS scholarship.

#### **Additional Information**
- Scholarships can be deferred for up to five years, including military service.
- FAS/FMS students can receive funding for up to one semester of graduate study if they complete their bachelor’s degree in seven semesters or fewer.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**

#### **Creation**
Instituted in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, the **Kennedy Scholarship** was established by the British government in collaboration with the Kennedy family. It allows students to attend **Harvard University** or **Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)**. The scholarship was designed to foster international understanding and support young leaders, with a focus on public service.

#### **Selection**
The selection process involves a national competition, with longlists and shortlists followed by interviews. Evaluators consider intellectual attainment, readiness to express themselves, and the suitability of the proposed course of study. Notable criteria include originality of mind, commitment to public service, potential for leadership, and the ability to overcome adversity.

#### **Notable Alumni**
The scholarship has produced prominent figures across politics, economics, journalism, arts, and academia, including:
- **Ed Balls** (politician)
- **Dame Kate Bingham** (former head of UK Government’s COVID-19 Vaccine Task Force)
- **Kwasi Kwarteng** (politician and historian)
- **Stephen Breyer** (U.S. Supreme Court Associate Justice)
- **Nobel Prize winners** like **Mervyn King** (former Governor of the Bank of England) and **Roger Tsien** (Nobel Prize in Chemistry).

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**

#### **Program Entry Requirements**
To enter the competition, students must:
- Be enrolled full-time in high school and planning to enroll in college in the fall.
- Be a U.S. citizen or a lawful permanent resident.
- Take the **Preliminary SAT/NMSQT** in their junior year.

#### **Competition Rounds**
1. **PSAT/NMSQT**: Students are screened based on the Selection Index score (critical reading + math + writing skills). Top scorers receive a **Letter of Commendation** or advance to the semifinalist stage.
2. **Semifinalist**: Semifinalists are chosen based on state quotas and submit SAT scores, academic records, and recommendations.
3. **Finalist**: Semifinalists who meet additional criteria advance to the finalist stage, where they are considered for scholarships.

#### **Scholarships**
- **National Merit $2,500 Scholarships**: Awarded to finalists judged to have the strongest academic records, leadership, and potential.
- **College-Sponsored Merit Scholarships**: Provided by specific colleges for finalists who plan to attend those institutions.
- **Special Scholarships**: Offered by corporate sponsors to finalists based on specific criteria.

#### **Criticism**
Critics argue that the program relies solely on the PSAT/NMSQT, which may not fully capture a student’s potential. The **National Association for College Admission Counseling (NACAC)** has criticized the program for its reliance on standardized tests, while **FairTest** has raised concerns about transparency and the lack of public information on scholarship winners by year and state.

### **Norway Scholarship**

#### **History**
The **Norway Scholarship** was established in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at **Wadham College, Oxford**. The scholarship was funded by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a former British vice-consul in Norway, who raised funds to support students studying in the UK. The program resumed after World War II and has since supported students from Norway and other countries.

#### **Past Recipients**
The scholarship has supported students from various fields, including law, economics, medicine, and the arts. Notable recipients include:
- **G. Astrup-Hoel** (Law)
- **Trygve Leivestad** (Old English Law)
- **Sara Shah** (Medicine)

### **Bolashak Scholarship**

#### **Description**
The **Bolashak Scholarship** was established in 1993 by the first President of Kazakhstan, **Nursultan Nazarbayev**, to foster Western democratic values among Kazakh youth. The scholarship enables students to study abroad, with a focus on developing leadership and professional skills. Over **12,831 students** have received the scholarship since its inception.

#### **Ethnic Distribution**
The scholarship has historically favored Kazakh students, with 93.6% of candidates being of Kazakh ethnicity. However, recent statistics reflect a more diverse ethnic composition, reflecting broader societal changes.

#### **List of Universities**
The scholarship supports students at leading universities worldwide, including:
- **Australia**: Australian National University, Curtin University
- **Canada**: McGill University, University of Toronto
- **China**: Peking University, Tsinghua University
- **Germany**: Heidelberg University, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München
- **Japan**: University of Tokyo, Kyoto University
- **USA**: Harvard University, Stanford University

### **King’s Scholar**

#### **Historical Origins**
The **King’s Scholar** program originates from **King Edward II’s 1317 establishment** of the first King’s Scholars at the University of Cambridge. The program has evolved over centuries, with notable changes in the selection process and privileges.

#### **King’s Scholars at Eton College**
At Eton College, King’s Scholars are selected through competitive examinations and receive a **Foundation Scholarship**. They are distinguished by wearing a black academic gown and live in the oldest house at Eton, known as "College." Notable alumni include:
- **Boris Johnson** (Prime Minister of the United Kingdom)
- **James Arbuthnot** (politician)
- **Eric Blair (George Orwell)**

#### **King’s Scholars at King’s College Cambridge**
King’s Scholars at King’s College Cambridge receive a prize of £350 and attend a ceremony in the King’s College Chapel. They are entitled to a three-course formal dinner with their respective Directors of Studies.

#### **King’s Scholars at the King’s Schools**
The King’s Schools at Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester award King’s Scholarships based on academic or musical attainment. Scholars are often accompanied by fee reductions.

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**

#### **Scholarships**
UNCF-administered scholarships are open to students from its member institutions and others, with a focus on supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). Notable alumni include:

#### **Fundraising and Legacy**
UNCF has received significant donations, including from **John F. Kennedy** (Pulitzer Prize for *Profiles in Courage*) and **Walter Annenberg**. The organization has also hosted high-profile events like the **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars**, which has raised over $200 million for scholarships.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**

#### **Overview**
The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** is a professional exchange program supported by the U.S. Department of State. It provides summer internships, career training, and financial support to scholars and graduate students from Eurasian countries studying in the United States. The program was established in 1992 and is named in honor of former U.S. Secretary of State Edmund Muskie.

#### **Legacy**
Edmund Muskie’s career spanned politics, diplomacy, and public service, with significant contributions to environmentalism, civil rights, and international relations. His legacy includes the establishment of the **Edmund S. Muskie School of Public Service at the University of Southern Maine** and the **Edmund S. Muskie Distinguished Public Service Award**. Muskie was awarded the **Presidential Medal of Freedom** by President Jimmy Carter.

### **Marshall Scholarship**

#### **History**
The **Marshall Scholarship** was established in 1953 as a living memorial to **George C. Marshall**, the U.S. Secretary of State who spearheaded the Marshall Plan. The scholarship enables young Americans to study in the UK, fostering international understanding and cooperation.

#### **Objectives**
The scholarship aims to:
1. Enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK.
2. Contribute to the advancement of knowledge in science, technology, the humanities, and social sciences.
3. Strengthen British-American understanding through cultural exchange.

#### **Selection Criteria**
Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. The selection process includes university endorsements, regional interviews, and final evaluations by selection committees.

#### **Scholarship Terms**
Marshall Scholars may study any full-time postgraduate course at a UK university. The scholarship covers university and college fees and provides a quarterly maintenance stipend. Scholars are expected to engage in cultural exchange and contribute to the advancement of knowledge.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable recipients include:
- **Stephen Breyer** (U.S. Supreme Court Associate Justice)
- **Roger Tsien** (Nobel Prize in Chemistry)
- **Nannerl Keohane** (President of Duke University and Wellesley College)

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**

#### **History**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (Studienstiftung) is a prestigious scholarship program in Germany that supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates. Founded in 1925, it was revived after World War II and has since supported over 2,000 scholars annually.

#### **Admission**
The Studienstiftung accepts students nominated by schools, professors, or through assessment tests. Scholars are selected based on academic excellence, social commitment, and leadership potential.

#### **Scholarships**
The program offers financial support, including monthly allowances and subsidies for health and long-term care insurance. Scholars also receive academic support, such as summer schools, research groups, and mentoring.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable alumni include:
- **Manfred Eigen** (Nobel Prize in Chemistry)
- **Gerd Faltings** (Fields Medalist)
- **Reinhard Zimmermann** (Leibniz Prize winner)

### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History**
The **Rhodes Scholarship** was established in 1902 under the terms of Cecil John Rhodes’ will. It aims to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the United States. The scholarship is administered by the **Rhodes Trust** in Oxford.

#### **Selection Criteria**
The scholarship is awarded based on:
- Literary and scholastic attainments
- Energy to use one’s talents to the full
- Truth, courage, devotion to duty, and leadership potential

#### **Scholarship Terms**
Rhodes Scholars may study any full-time postgraduate course at a UK university. The scholarship covers university and college fees and provides a monthly maintenance stipend. Scholars are expected to engage in cultural exchange and contribute to public service.

#### **Controversies**
The Rhodes Scholarship has faced criticism over its founder’s white supremacist views and its historical exclusion of women and Black Africans. Recent efforts have been made to address these issues, including the introduction of global scholarships and discussions about renaming the program.

#### **Notable Scholars**
Notable recipients include:
- **Stephen Breyer** (U.S. Supreme Court Associate Justice)
- **Nobel Prize winners** like **Mervyn King** and **Roger Tsien**
- **Political leaders** such as **Bill Clinton** and **Wasim Sajjad** (President of Pakistan)
