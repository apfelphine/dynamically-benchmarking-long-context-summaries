# International Scholarship and Fellowship Programs

## Rhodes, Marshall, Kennedy, Harkness, and Other Prestigious Awards

### Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the Rhodes Scholarship aims to promote unity within the British Empire and foster international understanding by enabling outstanding students to study at the University of Oxford. Originally restricted to male applicants, it became open to women in 1977 and now admits scholars globally. Selection is highly competitive and based on academic achievement, character, leadership, and public-spiritedness. Over its history, the scholarship has inspired similar programs worldwide and has produced numerous leaders in government, academia, medicine, and civil rights.

### Marshall Scholarship
Founded in 1953 as a British gesture of gratitude for U.S. wartime aid, the Marshall Scholarships enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to pursue postgraduate study at any UK university. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and the strengthening of UK–US relations. Selection involves rigorous university endorsement and regional interviews. The scholarship is compared favorably to the Rhodes, offering similar access and flexibility across UK institutions.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Created in 1964 as a national British memorial for President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship supports graduate study at Harvard or MIT for British students. Selection is based on academic distinction and leadership potential, with notable alumnae serving in politics, academia, journalism, and the arts. A permanent memorial site at Runnymede, England, symbolizes the program’s ideals.

### Harkness Fellowship
Begun in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, Harkness Fellowships reciprocated Rhodes Scholarships by allowing British and Commonwealth scholars to study in the U.S. The program now focuses exclusively on health policy, supporting international collaboration between healthcare professionals from select countries and U.S. research institutions.

### Fulbright Program
Established in 1946, the Fulbright Program is the largest international exchange program globally, supporting educational exchanges for students, scholars, and professionals between the U.S. and over 160 countries. Grants span research, teaching, and professional development in most disciplines, aiming to promote mutual understanding and world peace. The program is administered by bi-national commissions, the U.S. Department of State, and various partner organizations. Despite its robust legacy, recent years have witnessed political controversy, funding freezes, and operational challenges, particularly regarding U.S. government interference in selection processes.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Founded in Germany in 1925, the Studienstiftung supports academically and socially outstanding scholars in higher education, with a focus on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission follows nomination or direct application and entails financial and academic support for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students. The foundation offers various internal and co-funded programs and maintains a significant alumni network in science, business, politics, and the arts.

### Bolashak
Launched in 1993, Kazakhstan’s Bolashak Scholarship aims to foster Western values and modern expertise by sending students to leading global universities. Selection is competitive and requires proficiency in the Kazakh language. Awardees have contributed substantially to state and private sectors upon their return.

### Shuping Scholarship
Established in 1939 in Shanghai by philanthropist Koo Soh Bing, this scholarship has supported disadvantaged but high-achieving students, offering financial aid, extracurricular activities, and leadership training. The foundation operates in Shanghai, Beijing, and Huzhou, continually expanding its awards and alumni network.

### Norway Scholarship
Originating in 1919, the Norway Scholarship enables selected students from the University of Oslo to study at Wadham College, Oxford, with recipients pursuing diverse academic fields over the decades. The scholarship has persisted through financial and political challenges, now managed by a local alumni committee.

# Major Scholarship Foundations Supporting Diversity and Higher Education

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944, UNCF provides scholarships primarily to African-American students at historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), but awards are open to all ethnicities. It has raised significant funds through donations, grants, and televised events, supporting educational equity and institutional sustainability. Its membership comprises numerous HBCUs across the U.S., many with strong religious and social affiliations.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports public HBCUs with scholarships, programmatic support, and advocacy. The fund partners with major corporations and government agencies, and merged with the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to enhance entrepreneurship opportunities.

# National Scholarship Competitions

## National Merit Scholarship Program (U.S.)
This highly competitive program identifies top high school students based on PSAT/NMSQT scores. Approximately 1.5 million entrants are narrowed to 15,000 Finalists, of whom 8,000 receive scholarships annually. The program has been both lauded and criticized for its reliance on standardized testing. Notable alumni include leaders in government, science, and business.

# Scholarships Promoting Access and Academic Excellence

## Bright Futures Scholarship (Florida)
Funded via state lottery proceeds, the Bright Futures Scholarship provides significant aid to Florida students pursuing post-secondary education. Awards are merit-based and cover tuition at varying levels depending on GPA, standardized test scores, and service hours. The program has faced changes due to budget cuts, shifting eligibility requirements, and criticism for inequitable access, but continues to benefit thousands of students yearly.

# Miss America Scholarship
Founded in 1921 as a beauty pageant, Miss America evolved into a competition offering scholarships to women emphasizing talent, leadership, and education. Though facing numerous changes in format and leadership, it remains a source of scholarship funding for young women across the country.

# Notable Literary Scholarship

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Inspired by Busby’s groundbreaking anthologies, this scholarship supports African women in higher education by funding study at SOAS, University of London, and has fostered a network of writers of African descent whose works span genres, generations, and continents.

# International Cultural and Educational Programs

## British Council
Established in 1934, the British Council promotes British culture and English education overseas through exams, cultural exchange, academic partnerships, and arts programs. Operating globally, it faces challenges regarding financial sustainability, geopolitical tensions, and controversies related to cultural diplomacy and inclusion.
