### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
- **History & Structure**: Established to emulate Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, it initially funded 42,000 students with $70 million. It was merit-based, with A-level (100% tuition) and B-level (75% tuition) scholarships, plus a vocational track. In 2011, funding cuts raised SAT requirements and reduced award amounts, disproportionately impacting Black and Latino students. In 2014, the U.S. Department of Education investigated for racial bias, finding disparate impacts without discriminatory intent. A 2018 overhaul renamed levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) and added the Academic Top Scholar (ATS) award. In 2021, the book stipend was removed due to budget cuts.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Students must be U.S. citizens/residents, graduate from Florida schools or earn a GED, have no felony convictions, submit a Florida Financial Aid Application, and be accepted into a Florida post-secondary institution. Specific requirements vary by level (e.g., GPA, SAT/ACT scores, volunteer work hours).
- **Award Amounts**: FAS covers 100% of tuition and fees; FMS covers 75% at public institutions; GSV and GSC cover per-credit fees. ATS adds $44/credit at semester institutions.
- **Renewal & Deferral**: Scholarships are renewable for up to five years, with GPA requirements (FAS: 3.0, FMS/GSV/GSC: 2.75). Students can defer for up to five years or for military service.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
- **Origins & Purpose**: Created in 1964 to honor President John F. Kennedy, it provides scholarships to students for study at Harvard or MIT. Funded by the UK government and supported by the Kennedy family, it aims to foster international understanding.
- **Selection & Criteria**: Based on intellectual attainment, readiness, and suitability for study at Harvard or MIT. Evaluations consider originality, public service potential, and resilience.
- **Notable Scholars**: Includes prominent figures such as Ed Balls, David Miliband, and Nicholas Boles, who have served in government and public service.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
- **Entry & Competition**: Requires PSAT/NMSQT scores; top 1% of scorers are recognized as Commended. Semifinalists (16,000) are selected by state-based qualifying scores; Finalists (15,000) are chosen based on academic records, essays, and recommendations.
- **Scholarships**: Offers $2,500 awards to Finalists, with additional awards from corporate sponsors. Students receive scholarships at any regionally accredited U.S. college.
- **Criticism**: Criticized for relying solely on a single test (PSAT) to screen applicants, with concerns over transparency and fairness. The National Association for College Admission Counseling (NACAC) and FairTest have challenged the selection process.

### **Norway Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1919 by Arthur Jayne to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford. Initially funded with $60,000, it evolved through fundraising efforts. No scholars were appointed during the 1970s–1980s due to funding issues.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Students must have completed 16 college-prep courses and meet academic standards. A minimum score on the CLT (95 for FAS, 82 for FMS) is required.
- **Past Scholars**: Include notable figures such as G. Astrup-Hoel, Johan Nordahl Brun Grieg, and Iver B. Neumann.

### **Bolashak Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Instituted in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s President Nazarbayev to promote Western democratic values. Initially offered to 785 students, it expanded to 12,831 by 2018.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence, leadership, and a commitment to public service. A strong focus on national development and economic growth.
- **Ethnic Distribution**: 93.6% of candidates were of Kazakh ethnicity, due to language requirements.

### **King's Scholar**
- **Origins**: Established in 1317 by King Edward II at Cambridge; later expanded to Eton College in 1440. A King's Scholar is a student selected through competitive exams and awarded a Foundation Scholarship.
- **Structure & Benefits**: Students at Eton are known as "Collegers" and wear black gowns. They receive financial support and privileges, including access to college facilities and special traditions.
- **Notable Scholars**: Includes figures like John Maynard Keynes, Boris Johnson, and William King.

### **UNCF Scholarships**
- **History & Mission**: Founded in 1944 to support African American students, it has grown into a major provider of scholarships. It now supports over 4 million African Americans through its member institutions.
- **Eligibility**: Open to all ethnicities, with a majority of recipients being African American. Students must attend UNCF member institutions.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Alexis Herman, Spike Lee, and General Chappie James.

---

### **Marshall Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1953 to honor George C. Marshall, it enables intellectually distinguished American students to study in the UK. The program emphasizes understanding of British society and fostering transatlantic relations.
- **Selection & Criteria**: Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. A minimum GPA of 3.7 is required.
- **Notable Scholars**: Includes Stephen Breyer, Robert Oden, and Thomas Friedman, who have gone on to prominent roles in government and academia.

---

### **Studienstiftung**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1925 in Dresden, it supports German and international students with financial and academic aid. Re-established in 1948 after WWII.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Open to students from all nationalities, with full-time study at German or EU universities. Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence and social commitment.
- **Scholarship Benefits**: Includes a monthly allowance, health insurance, and access to summer academies, research groups, and mentoring.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates like Manfred Eigen and Reinhard Genzel.

### **Miss America Pageant**
- **History & Evolution**: Originated in 1920 as a beauty pageant in Atlantic City, it evolved into a cultural and social institution. The 1970s saw the inclusion of African American and Asian American contestants, with significant controversies over racism and sexism.
- **Controversies**: Faced protests in the 1960s over its perceived sexism and racism. In 2018, the "Miss America 2.0" format was introduced, removing the swimsuit competition and emphasizing personal development.
- **Recent Changes**: In 2023, the format reverted to include a swimsuit competition, and the pageant moved from Atlantic City to other locations.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai to support poor, academically strong students. It emphasizes service to society and self-reliance.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Applicants must come from poor families with strong academic records. A continuous scholarship is offered until graduation.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent figures in education, healthcare, and public service.

---

### **Fulbright Program**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1946 to promote peace and mutual understanding through educational exchange. It operates in over 160 countries.
- **Program Components**: Includes student, scholar, and teacher exchanges in various fields. The Fulbright–Hays Program supports research and training in foreign languages and area studies.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, and U.S. government officials.

---

### **Harkness Fellowship**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness to enable British graduates to study in the U.S. Now focused on health care policy and practice.
- **Eligibility & Requirements**: Open to professionals in health policy, business, and the arts. Fellows spend a year conducting research at U.S. institutions.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent figures in science, medicine, and public policy.

---

### **British Council**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1934 to promote British culture and language abroad. Operates in over 100 countries.
- **Activities**: Offers English language teaching, examinations (e.g., IELTS), and cultural exchange programs. Runs initiatives like "Peacekeeping English" and "UK-India Year of Culture."
- **Controversies**: Criticized for outsourcing to Indian centers and for handling the Afghan teacher crisis post-2021.

---

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
- **Purpose & Impact**: A literary anthology celebrating the work of women of African descent. The 2019 companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, features 200 contributors and includes a scholarship for African women students.
- **Legacy**: Has inspired a global movement to recognize and uplift Black women’s voices in literature and public life.

---

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1902 to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties with the U.S. and Germany. It is one of the world's most prestigious postgraduate scholarships.
- **Selection & Criteria**: Based on academic excellence, moral character, and leadership potential. Open to postgraduate students globally since 2018.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for its colonialist legacy and exclusion of Black Africans. Recent changes include the inclusion of transgender and non-binary scholars.
- **Notable Scholars**: Includes leaders in law, science, and public service, such as Bill Clinton and Sir Alimuddin Zumla.
