# Scholarships and Fellowship Programs Summary

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)
Established to emulate Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, Bright Futures is a merit-based program funding college tuition for Florida students. Initially covering full tuition for "A level" scholars and 75% for "B level," it faced criticism for predominantly benefiting wealthy families. Reforms in 2011 raised SAT score requirements and reduced award amounts, disproportionately affecting minority and low-income students. The Department of Education found racial disparities but no intent to discriminate. In 2018, the program rebranded scholarship levels (e.g., Florida Academic Scholar) and restored award values. It requires academic achievements, standardized test scores, volunteer or work hours, and is renewable under GPA maintenance rules. Award amounts vary by scholarship level and institution type, with some covering full tuition and others providing per-credit funding primarily for vocational studies.

## Kennedy Scholarship (United Kingdom)
Created in memory of President John F. Kennedy, the Kennedy Scholarship supports ten British postgraduate students annually to study at Harvard or MIT. Established through a UK parliamentary act in 1964, it combines a living scholarship with a permanent memorial in Runnymede, England. Selection emphasizes intellectual merit, leadership potential, public service commitment, and course suitability. Trustees, appointed by the UK Prime Minister, include notable academics, diplomats, and ambassadors. Alumni have held prominent roles in politics, academia, journalism, and arts.

## National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)
A prestigious academic competition primarily based on PSAT/NMSQT results, it identifies about 50,000 high-scoring students with distinctions as Commended Students, Semifinalists, and Finalists. Scholarships totaling around 8,000 awards are given through corporate sponsors, colleges, and NMSC funds and require high school eligibility, standardized test scores, and academic achievement. Criticism focuses on over-reliance on the PSAT and lack of transparency in score requirements. The program also included the National Achievement Scholarship for Black American students, which ended in 2016 and transitioned support to college graduates via the United Negro College Fund.

## Norway Scholarship
Founded in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford's Wadham College, the Norway Scholarship promotes Anglo-Norwegian cultural ties and academic exchange. Funded initially by private donations and revived with institutional support in the late 20th century, it has supported diverse disciplines. The program includes alumni associations and holds annual events, with notable Norwegian figures benefiting historically.

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Instituted in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev to foster Western education and values among Kazakh youth, Bolashak has awarded over 12,800 scholarships worldwide. Despite its goal to encourage democratic ideals, scholars often face challenges implementing changes at home. The scholarship includes a competitive selection process emphasizing Kazakh language proficiency, which leads to a majority of ethnically Kazakh recipients. Bolashak supports studies in numerous global universities across continents.

## King's Scholar (UK)
Dating back to 1317, King's Scholars are students awarded scholarships to prestigious British schools such as Eton College and King's College Cambridge, with origins tied to royal patronage. At Eton, King's Scholars live in the central college, enjoy distinct traditions, and wear academic gowns. Similar scholarships exist at Westminster School and the King's Schools in various English cities, offering academic privileges and historic ties to cathedrals. Notable King's Scholars have included influential political, literary, and scientific figures.

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944 to improve funding for Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), UNCF provides scholarships accessible to all but predominantly awarded to African Americans. It has raised significant funds through high-profile donations and events, including Lou Rawls' annual telethons and support from philanthropists like MacKenzie Scott and Reed Hastings. UNCF supports numerous member HBCUs across the U.S., contributing to the education and advancement of many prominent Black leaders in various fields.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program and Legacy
Named after prominent American politician Edmund Muskie, the program offers summer internships and career support to Eurasian scholars in the U.S. Muskie (1914–1996) was a Maine governor, U.S. senator, vice-presidential candidate, and Secretary of State known for environmental legislation (Clean Air and Water Acts), civil rights advocacy, and foreign policy efforts during the Iran hostage crisis and Soviet-Afghan war. His legacy includes advancing environmental and civil rights policies, shaping U.S. budget processes, and leaving an enduring political and public service impact.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Founded in 1987 to support public HBCUs, TMCF raises funds for scholarships, capacity building, and advocacy for 55 member institutions. It differs from UNCF by focusing on public HBCUs and has received substantial grants from major corporations. It acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation to enhance entrepreneurship education and talent identification. Its leadership includes noted educators and executives.

## Marshall Scholarship (UK)
Established in 1953 as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George Marshall, this program awards American students scholarships for postgraduate study at any UK university. It emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and fostering UK-US relations. Selection is highly competitive, with about 3-4% acceptance among endorsed applicants, and scholars receive full tuition and living expenses for usually two years. Alumni include prominent academics, public officials, and Nobel laureates. The scholarship parallels other international scholarships like the Rhodes and Gates Cambridge.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Founded initially in 1925 and re-established post-WWII, it is Germany's largest scholarship foundation, providing financial and academic support to exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates regardless of nationality. Admissions occur via school or university nominations, competitions, and self-assessment tests. Scholarships offer allowances, insurance subsidies, and academic enrichment through seminars and mentoring. It runs internal and cooperative scholarship programs and supports arts and sciences extensively.

## Miss America Pageant (USA)
Originating in 1921 as a beauty contest in Atlantic City to boost tourism, it evolved into a scholarship pageant emphasizing talent and education. Early years were racially segregated; the first African-American contestant appeared in 1970, and the first African-American winner in 1983. The pageant faced feminist protests during the 1960s and recent controversies over inclusivity and governance. It underwent format changes in 2018 focusing less on physical appearance, which were partially reversed by 2023. The pageant continues to award scholarships and promote various social causes.

## Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 by industrialist Koo to assist academically talented but financially disadvantaged secondary students in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship provided continuous tuition support and extracurricular enrichment, including medical aid and charitable engagement. Despite interruptions during wartime and political upheavals, it was re-established in the 1980s and continues to support thousands, fostering civic responsibility and leadership among recipients. Governance involves alumni associations and a dedicated foundation.

## Fulbright Program (USA and global)
Created in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international educational exchange and mutual understanding post-WWII, it funds a wide range of student, scholar, teacher, and professional exchanges globally. It involves bilateral commissions managing program administration in partner countries. The program has supported over 325,000 alumni, including Nobel laureates and heads of state. Recent challenges include funding suspensions and political interference claims. It encompasses degree, research, language teaching, and professional development grants.

## Harkness Fellowship (Commonwealth and Allied countries)
Initiated in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, Harkness Fellowships support mid-career professionals from Commonwealth nations to study health care policy and practice in the U.S., promoting policy exchange and research collaboration. Administered by the Commonwealth Fund with additional support from various foundations, Harkness Fellows engage in intensive research and seminar programs at major American institutions. The program is prestigious in health policy circles and serves multiple countries including Australia, Canada, Germany, and the UK.

## British Council (UK)
Established in 1934 to promote UK culture, English language, education, and international relations, the British Council operates worldwide via offices and partnerships in over 100 countries. It organizes English teaching, cultural exchanges, exams like IELTS, and mobility programs such as Education UK and Erasmus+. The Council faced operational challenges in Russia and controversies related to outsourcing, labor disputes, and political activities. It is a UK charity and public body sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, with substantial income from grants, services, and contracts.

## Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* Anthology and Award
Published in 1992, *Daughters of Africa* is a comprehensive anthology collecting writings by over 200 women of African descent globally, spanning centuries and genres. Praised for its scope and literary quality, it has influenced black feminist scholarship and literary studies. In 2019, a sequel *New Daughters of Africa* was published, continuing to highlight diverse African women's voices. The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students in higher education, reinforcing the anthology’s legacy in empowering future generations.

## Rhodes Scholarship (UK)
Founded in 1902 under Cecil Rhodes's will to foster Anglo-American unity and educate future leaders, Rhodes Scholarships are highly prestigious postgraduate awards to study at Oxford University. The program now spans over 20 constituencies, including the U.S., Commonwealth countries, and beyond, with stringent selection criteria emphasizing intellect, character, leadership, and commitment to service. Historically male-only and criticized for colonialist ties, women became eligible in 1977, and racial diversity has increased over time. Notable alumni have impacted politics, civil rights, arts, and sciences. The Rhodes Scholarship has inspired many international counterparts, such as the Marshall and Kennedy Scholarships.

# Overall Summary
This comprehensive overview presents major international scholarship and fellowship programs, highlighting their histories, selection criteria, purposes, notable alumni, and controversies. Programs like the Rhodes, Marshall, Kennedy, and Fulbright Scholarships emphasize international academic exchange and leadership development, often fostering transatlantic and global ties. Nationally focused programs such as Bright Futures (Florida), Bolashak (Kazakhstan), and Shuping (China) provide educational opportunities targeting merit and underprivileged populations. Organizations such as the British Council and UNCF support broader cultural, educational, and community development goals. Collectively, these programs contribute to academic advancement, cross-cultural understanding, and leadership around the world.
