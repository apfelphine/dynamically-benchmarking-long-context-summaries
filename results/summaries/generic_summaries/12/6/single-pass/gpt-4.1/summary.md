# Overview of Scholarship, Fellowship, and Award Programs

---

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

**Daughters of Africa** is a landmark anthology edited by Margaret Busby, first published in 1992, compiling the work of over 200 female writers of African descent. Acclaimed for its breadth, historical significance, and literary quality, the anthology is praised for illustrating the interconnectedness of African women's experiences and contributions, as well as for dispelling myths about the limited literary output of Black women prior to the 1980s. It has influenced educational curricula, inspired companion anthologies, and served as an essential reference for Black feminist literature.

**New Daughters of Africa** (2019) expands upon the original, featuring an additional 200 writers and organizing their contributions by decade of birth to demonstrate generational links. The anthology has been lauded for its diversity and depth, offering a social and cultural world history through Black women's writing. It supports the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award, a scholarship for African women in partnership with SOAS, London.

## Harkness Fellowship

Founded in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowships reciprocated the Rhodes Scholarship, later expanding internationally. Initially open to graduates and civil servants from the Commonwealth and later broadened, since 1997 it focuses exclusively on health policy, offering twelve-month fellowships at major U.S. research institutions. Fellows gain exposure to U.S. health systems and participate in international policy seminars throughout their tenure. The program is notable for its prestige and international collaboration, producing distinguished alumni in academia, science, medicine, government, and the arts.

## Rhodes Scholarship

Established in 1902 per Cecil Rhodes's will, the Rhodes Scholarship is one of the world’s most competitive international postgraduate awards, now open globally. Its criteria emphasize academic excellence, energy, leadership, and character. Historically restricted to men and the white British Commonwealth, it became coeducational in 1977 and gradually more inclusive. Recipients may pursue any full-time postgraduate study at Oxford University. The scholarship, often seen as a route to leadership, has produced numerous heads of state, public intellectuals, researchers, and advocates. It maintains a tradition of fostering debate on equality, inclusion, and the legacy of its founder.

## Shuping Scholarship

Founded in 1939 by businessman Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship sought to support academically talented yet financially disadvantaged students. Scholars benefited from tuition, extracurricular activities, and opportunities for service and leadership. After decades of expansion and revival post-1986, it now supports hundreds annually, advocating not only academic success but also community involvement. The program is managed through alumni associations and sustained by generous donations and a foundation. Its alumni have contributed significantly to China’s modernization and public service.

---

## British Council

Established in the 1930s, the British Council is a UK public body dedicated to international cultural and educational exchange, with operations in over 100 countries. It supports English language education, administers major standardized tests (IELTS), and organizes student mobility, arts, and cultural programs. Despite international praise, it has faced criticism for outsourcing, handling of staff welfare, and strategic closures. It has weathered attacks and diplomatic challenges, adapting its services accordingly. The Council receives government funding and generates income from services. It is noted for its influence but also for debate over its priorities and operational transparency.

---

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes

Germany's most selective and largest scholarship foundation, established in 1925 and revived post-WWII, promotes academic excellence, initiative, and responsibility. It provides financial and academic support including stipends, summer schools, workshops, and mentorship. Selection is via nomination, competitions, and assessment tests, emphasizing not just intellect but also social commitment. The Studienstiftung supports undergraduates, graduates, and doctoral candidates, with internal and external partnership programs. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, leaders in science, politics, arts, and business.

---

## Fulbright Program

Created in 1946 to foster international understanding, the Fulbright Program is the world's largest educational exchange initiative. It funds U.S. and foreign scholars, students, and professionals for study, teaching, or research abroad. Grants cover nearly all disciplines and include specialized programs for language and scientific research. Governed by bilateral commissions and the U.S. State Department, Fulbright has produced scores of Nobel laureates, heads of state, and prominent cultural figures. It has recently faced political interference, leading to board resignations and program disruptions.

---

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)

Founded in 1987, TMCF supports students at public HBCUs through scholarships, professional development, and institutional fundraising. It receives major corporate and philanthropic support and merged with the Opportunity Funding Corporation to expand entrepreneurship opportunities. TMCF advocates for 55 member schools and has raised over $500 million, focusing on academic success, talent identification, and capacity building. Its mission is distinct from the United Negro College Fund, serving predominantly public institutions.

---

## Miss America Scholarship

Since 1921, the Miss America competition evolved from a beauty contest to a scholarship program emphasizing talent, education, and leadership. The organization has faced shifts in contest format, inclusion, scandal, financial challenges, and changing public perceptions. It awards scholarships, celebrates diverse winners, and periodically adapts its format in response to cultural trends and criticism.

---

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)

Founded in 1944, UNCF unites historically Black colleges for fundraising to address educational inequities. It provides scholarships, institutional support, and capacity building to member colleges and students, funded through high-profile donations and events like the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars. Its motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste," is widely recognized. UNCF remains a crucial source of opportunity and advocacy for Black students and institutions.

---

## Marshall Scholarship

Established in 1953 as a British gesture of gratitude for the Marshall Plan, the Marshall Scholarship enables American students to pursue postgraduate study at any UK university. Selection emphasizes intellect, leadership, and ambassadorial potential. Roughly 40 scholars are chosen annually through a highly competitive process. Alumni distinguish themselves in government, academia, journalism, and science. The Association of Marshall Scholars fosters transatlantic dialogue through events such as the Marshall Forum.

---

## Bolashak Scholarship

Launched in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s government, the Bolashak (“Future”) Scholarship offers merit-based support to Kazakh students for study at leading universities worldwide, with the expectation that graduates contribute to national development. Over 12,000 scholars have benefited, many rising to positions of leadership in government and business. Selection involves rigorous language and psychological testing, with scholarships offered for a diverse range of programs and countries.

---

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

Named after the U.S. statesman Edmund S. Muskie, the program supports Eurasian graduate students and scholars in the U.S. with internships and training in professional fields. It is part of broader Muskie Fellowships established by the Freedom Support Act of 1992 to promote educational and cultural exchange and public service.

---

## National Merit Scholarship Program

This U.S. program recognizes academic achievement based on PSAT scores, advancing students through rounds from Commended to Finalist status, with about 8,000 recipients annually. Scholarships are funded by the National Merit Scholarship Corporation, corporations, and colleges, with many universities offering additional funding. The discontinued National Achievement Scholarship Program focused on Black American youth. Critics cite reliance on standardized testing and lack of transparency.

---

## King’s Scholar

Dating to the 14th century, King's Scholars are those awarded foundation scholarships at elite institutions such as Eton College and King's College Cambridge, based on competitive academic examination or merit. The title carries distinct traditions, privileges, gowns, and at Cambridge, also denotes undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees. King's Scholarships are also awarded at cathedral schools and have adapted to include women and broader academic or musical excellence.

---

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)

Started in the late 1990s, Bright Futures is a merit-based scholarship for Florida residents attending in-state colleges, with requirements for GPA, standardized test scores, volunteer service, and application submission. Award amounts and eligibility criteria have fluctuated over time, with recent adjustments restoring full funding for top scholars. The program includes several award levels and renewal policies and remains pivotal for post-secondary access in Florida.

---

## Norway Scholarship

Founded in 1919, the Norway Scholarship enables University of Oslo students to study at Wadham College, Oxford, fostering cultural and academic ties between Norway and Britain. Funded by donations and managed by a committee, the scholarship supports a variety of disciplines and has operated intermittently, now celebrated through annual events and a vibrant alumni network.

---

## Kennedy Scholarship

Established in 1964 as part of the UK’s official memorial to President John F. Kennedy, the scholarship funds British graduates for postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. Selection mirrors that of Marshall and Rhodes Scholarships, emphasizing academic achievement, leadership, originality, and public service. Trustees include eminent scholars and statesmen. Alumni include ministers, diplomats, economists, journalists, and academics who have distinguished themselves in diverse fields.
