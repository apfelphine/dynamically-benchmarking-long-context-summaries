# Literary and Scholarly Anthologies

## *Daughters of Africa* (Original Edition)
This landmark anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, is a comprehensive introduction to the diverse body of Black women's writing from around the world. Critics have hailed it as a literary first, gathering global works of fiction, poetry, memoir, and essays. The anthology challenges the historical misconception that Black women's literary endeavor began only in the 1980s, tracing continuities across continents and centuries. It has received numerous accolades, including inclusion in *Sacred Fire: "QBR" 100 Essential Black Books*, and is considered an invaluable text for studies of women and African descent writers.

## *New Daughters of Africa* (2019)
A companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, expands on the predecessor by featuring an additional 200 writers, primarily those who came of age following the first anthology's publication. Contributors are arranged by birth decade to illustrate generational links and chart the black feminist literary canon. Reviews praise its "glorious portrayal of the richness and magnitude of the spiritual well" from which these writers draw inspiration, presenting a clear sense of evolution in style and subject matter.

# Academic and Philanthropic Awards

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Associated with the 2019 anthology, this award is designed to benefit African women students. The initiative was funded by Margaret Busby and the publisher, with all contributors waiving their fees. The first recipient was Idza Luhumyo from Kenya in 2020.

## Harkness Fellowship
Established in 1918 by Anna Harkness, the Commonwealth Fund initiated these fellowships to enable British graduates to study in the U.S. The program has since focused exclusively on health care policy and practice. Fellows conduct year-long research at prestigious U.S. institutions and participate in seminars, including a Policy Briefing on Capitol Hill.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes, this scholarship aims to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. While initially open only to single men, women were admitted in 1977. The selection emphasizes intellectual and moral character, though the program faces ongoing criticism regarding Rhodes's colonialist views and the focus of recipients' careers.

## Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953, this scholarship supports intellectually distinguished young Americans studying in the UK to foster mutual understanding between the U.S. and Britain. Unlike the Rhodes Scholarship, Marshall Scholars must be American citizens. The program emphasizes academic distinction and leadership potential, and its alumni often achieve high levels of success in academia and government.

## Fulbright Program
Originating in 1945, the Fulbright Program aims to increase global understanding through educational exchange. It operates on a bi-national basis, providing grants for U.S. persons to study abroad and for foreign nationals to study in the U.S. Key components include the U.S. Student Program and the Fulbright Foreign Student Program, and the program is overseen by the U.S. Department of State.

## Studienstiftung
Founded in Dresden in 1925, the Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes supports exceptionally talented German students and doctoral candidates based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility." Admissions occur through multiple channels, including school, professor, or examination board nominations, and the program offers financial and academic support.

## UNCF
The United Negro College Fund was established in 1944 to address funding inequities in higher education for African Americans. Despite its focus on HBCUs, UNCF scholarships are open to all ethnicities. The fund has raised significant capital through large donations, including $70 million from MacKenzie Scott in 2025.

# Historical and Societal Context

## King's Scholars
The tradition of King's Scholars began with King Edward II establishing students at Cambridge in 1317. Today, the term refers to students at Eton College who pass the College Election examination, while the name is also used for high-achieving undergraduates at King's College Cambridge.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, this scholarship was established to support poor, academically excellent students, embodying the motto, "Return to the Society what you have gained from it." Initially providing full tuition and comprehensive support, the program resumed in 1986 and continues to promote service to the community.

## British Council
This charity, sponsored by the UK government, promotes English language and British culture globally. It operates in over 100 countries. The Council has faced controversies related to political interference, outsourcing to the Indian diaspora, and closures in conflict zones like Syria and Russia.

# Miscellaneous Programs

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida state program emulates HOPE Scholarship, funding education based on academic merit rather than financial need. It has undergone several overhauls since its inception, including changes in minimum SAT scores and benefit levels, leading to criticisms regarding potential racial bias.

## Bolashak
Instituted by Nursultan Nazarbayev of Kazakhstan in 1993, "Bolashak" translates to "Future." It sends young people overseas to learn Western, democratic values, with many graduates subsequently holding senior positions in state and private Kazakh organizations.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Named after JFK’s assassination, this scholarship provides a living memorial by funding study at Harvard or MIT. Selection criteria focus on intellectual attainment, readiness to express oneself, and potential for public service, maintaining standards comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall scholarships.
