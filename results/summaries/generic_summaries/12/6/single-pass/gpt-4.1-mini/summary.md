# Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

**Daughters of Africa** is a landmark international anthology edited by Margaret Busby, praised for its comprehensive collection of writings by women of African descent spanning centuries and continents. It features over 200 contributors, including notable authors like Maya Angelou and Toni Morrison, and is widely regarded as an invaluable literary and cultural reference, inspiring new anthologies and academic courses. The companion volume, **New Daughters of Africa** (2019), continues this legacy, featuring another 200 writers and organized by decade of birth to highlight generational links. This anthology has received strong critical acclaim and inspired the creation of the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award, supporting African women scholars.

# Harkness Fellowship

Established in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the **Harkness Fellowships** enable healthcare professionals from several countries to conduct research in the United States. Focused since 1997 solely on health care policy and practice, the program offers a 12-month tenure at prestigious U.S. institutions, enriched by seminars and briefings. Funded by the Commonwealth Fund and partner foundations across different countries, the fellowship boasts notable alumni in diverse fields, including academia, government, and the arts.

# Rhodes Scholarship

Founded by Cecil Rhodes in 1902 to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen Anglo-American ties, the **Rhodes Scholarship** is a prestigious postgraduate award administered by the Rhodes Trust. Originally restricted to male applicants and select regions, it is now open worldwide and to all genders. Selection criteria emphasize scholarly excellence, character, leadership, and commitment to service. Despite controversies over Rhodes's colonialist legacy, gender exclusion history, and recipient career paths, it remains highly competitive and has produced influential leaders across sectors. The Rhodes model has inspired numerous international scholarships.

# Shuping Scholarship

Founded in 1939 by Shanghai businessman and philanthropist Koo in memory of his father, the **Shuping Scholarship** supports academically talented but financially disadvantaged students in China. The scholarship includes a study and activity center, free medical and vocational services, and promotes volunteerism and moral education. Despite interruptions during wartime, the scholarship resumed in the 1980s, expanding to numerous schools and tertiary institutions, now supporting thousands yearly. The foundation continues to be managed by alumni associations and funded through trusts and donations.

# British Council

**British Council**, established in 1934, is the UK’s organization for international cultural and educational exchange. It operates globally to promote knowledge of the UK, English language teaching, arts, education, and societal cooperation. The Council has faced geopolitical challenges (notably in Russia), security attacks, and controversies including staff grievances and policy criticisms. It offers English language exams (notably IELTS), administers programs like Erasmus+, and promotes cultural projects worldwide. It is a charity and public body sponsored by the UK Foreign Office, with a significant global footprint.

# Studienstiftung

The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is Germany’s largest scholarship foundation, established in 1925 and re-founded in 1948, supporting academically excellent and socially committed students without citizenship or age restrictions. It offers financial aid, comprehensive academic support (summer schools, mentoring), and selective internal/external fellowship programs. Applicants can be nominated by schools, professors, competitions, or self-apply via assessment tests. The foundation supports tens of thousands annually and has many distinguished alumni, including multiple Nobel laureates and renowned academics.

# Fulbright Program

Founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright, the **Fulbright Program** is the largest U.S. international educational exchange initiative aimed at promoting global peace and mutual understanding. It provides scholarships for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals to study, teach, or conduct research abroad and in the U.S., across many disciplines. It operates via bi-national commissions, U.S. embassies, and partner organizations. Despite recent political and operational challenges, Fulbright remains a prestigious program with notable alumni worldwide and administers various specialized awards including the Fulbright–Hays programs.

# Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)

Founded in 1987, the **Thurgood Marshall College Fund** is a nonprofit supporting public historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and predominantly Black institutions by providing scholarships, capacity building, and advocacy. It supports 55 member schools and has raised over $500 million. The fund has acquired sister organizations to enhance entrepreneurship education and talent development. TMCF receives major corporate donations and continues to expand its programs under dynamic leadership.

# Miss America

**Miss America Pageant**, established in 1921 to promote Atlantic City tourism, evolved into a national scholarship competition emphasizing talent and education. Historically marked by periods of racial exclusion and evolving social attitudes, it saw racial breakthroughs with contestants like Cheryl Browne and Vanessa Williams. The pageant shifted focus from physical beauty to inclusivity with "Miss America 2.0" reforms in 2018 but has since reversed some changes. It has been embroiled in controversies regarding leadership and representation but continues as a cultural institution awarding scholarships.

# UNCF (United Negro College Fund)

The **UNCF**, founded in 1944 by leading African American educators and activists, is a major nonprofit organization supporting historically Black colleges and universities through fundraising and scholarships open to all ethnicities. It has helped produce prominent alumni in multiple fields and raised significant funds via events like the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars. Recent large philanthropic donations by figures like MacKenzie Scott and Netflix founder Reed Hastings have bolstered its capacity to address funding disparities in higher education.

# Marshall Scholarship

Created in 1953 as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, the **Marshall Scholarship** enables American students to study at any UK university. It prides itself on academic excellence and leadership potential, with flexible tenure and wide university choice compared to Rhodes scholarships. Selection is highly competitive and emphasizes intellectual and personal promise to strengthen UK-US relations. The associated **Association of Marshall Scholars** hosts forums and events on transatlantic issues, featuring prominent scholars and leaders.

# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

Named after Edmund Muskie, prominent American politician and diplomat, this program (established 1992) provides summer internships and career training for Eurasian graduate students and scholars in the U.S. Muskie’s legacy spans environmental policy, civil rights, and diplomacy. The internship program fosters professional development and cultural exchange, continuing Muskie’s commitment to public service and international understanding.

# National Merit Scholarship Program

The **National Merit Scholarship Program** identifies and awards high-achieving U.S. high school students via the PSAT/NMSQT exam. It progresses through Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist stages, culminating in scholarships awarded largely based on academic ability, extracurricular activities, and recommendations. The program also included the National Achievement Scholarship for Black American students, now transitioned to the UNCF. The program is influential in college admissions and merit awards but faces criticism regarding its over-reliance on standardized testing.

# King's Scholar

**King's Scholars** are prestigious scholarship recipients historically linked to educational foundations established by English monarchs, such as those at Eton College and Westminster School. At Eton, King's Scholars are selected via competitive examination, housed in “College,” and granted distinctive traditional privileges and attire. Comparable schemes exist at other historic institutions. The term also denotes high academic achievers at King's College Cambridge and affiliated schools, preserving centuries-old educational traditions.

# Bright Futures Scholarship Program

Florida’s **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** provides merit-based scholarships to high school graduates meeting academic, test score, and service requirements. It includes multiple award levels covering varying tuition amounts and applies to a wide array of public and private Florida post-secondary institutions. Over time, funding and eligibility standards have shifted, sometimes generating controversy around socio-economic and racial impacts. The program emphasizes academic achievement, community involvement, and continuous renewal standards.

# Norway Scholarship

Established post-World War I to foster educational exchange between Norway and Oxford University, the **Norway Scholarship** funds Norwegian students to study for a year at Oxford colleges. Initiated through private fundraising and sustained by alumni and Norwegian officials, it has faced funding challenges but remains a prestigious award promoting Anglo-Norwegian academic ties. Numerous scholars have been supported since its inception, across varied disciplines.

# Kennedy Scholarship

Created in memory of U.S. President John F. Kennedy, the British **Kennedy Scholarship** funds UK graduate studies at Harvard or MIT. Overseen by trustees appointed by the UK Prime Minister, it emphasizes intellectual attainment, public service potential, and originality. Selection is highly competitive and comparable to Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships. Notable alumni span politics, economics, journalism, academia, and the arts, reflecting the program’s prestigious reputation and influence.
