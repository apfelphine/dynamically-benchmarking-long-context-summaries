### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**

#### **Reception & Legacy**
The *Daughters of Africa* anthology (1992) was widely celebrated for its groundbreaking compilation of works by women of African descent, spanning fiction, poetry, memoir, and essays. Praised for its breadth and inclusivity, it was hailed as a landmark in African literature, restoring marginalized voices to the forefront of literary history. The anthology’s impact was further amplified by its inclusion in prestigious lists, such as *Sacred Fire: QBR*’s "100 Essential Black Books" and the Royal African Society’s "50 Books by African Women That Everyone Should Read." Its influence extended to inspiring the 2019 sequel, *New Daughters of Africa*, which continued to celebrate the rich literary contributions of women from Africa and the diaspora.

#### **Contributors**
The anthology featured over 200 women, including literary luminaries like **Zora Neale Hurston**, **Toni Morrison**, **Alice Walker**, **Nobel laureates** such as **Wole Soyinka**, and contemporary voices like **Ayòbámi Adébáyò** and **Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie**. Notable contributors included **Grace Akello**, **Ama Ata Aidoo**, **Buchi Emecheta**, **Nawal El Saadawi**, and **Jamaica Kincaid**, among others.

#### **Influence & Legacy**
The anthology’s success spurred similar initiatives, including the German-language *Töchter Afrikas* (1994) and the *Wasafiri* magazine’s recognition of its influence. It also inspired the establishment of the **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**, a scholarship fund benefiting African women students. The anthology’s legacy endures in its role as a foundational text for African feminist literature, fostering dialogue on identity, culture, and global representation.

*New Daughters of Africa* expanded on the original anthology by featuring an additional 200 writers, maintaining a focus on the intersection of sisterhood, tradition, race, and identity. The anthology was organized by the birth decades of the contributors, emphasizing generational continuity in African feminist literature. Notable additions included writers like **Ayòbámi Adébáyò**, **Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie**, **Toni Morrison**, **Zadie Smith**, **Nnedi Okorafor**, **Sapphire**, and **Warsan Shire**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Diversity & Representation**: The anthology highlighted the vast range of experiences and voices within the African diaspora, from literary giants to emerging talents.
- **Scholarship & Awards**: The anthology’s proceeds supported the **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**, a scholarship for African women students.
- **Global Impact**: It was published in multiple editions, including UK (Myriad Editions), US (Amistad/HarperCollins), and South Africa (Jonathan Ball Publishers), and featured in academic and literary circles worldwide.

#### **Reception & Influence**
Critics praised the anthology for its depth, diversity, and the way it reinforced the legacy of African feminist writing. It was recognized for its role in promoting global understanding and appreciation of African women’s contributions to literature and culture.

### **Harkness Fellowship**

#### **Current Program**
Fellows spend a year at U.S. institutions like Harvard, Johns Hopkins, or Stanford, engaging in research, seminars, and policy discussions. The program emphasizes cross-cultural exchange and the development of long-term professional networks.

#### **Notable Alumni**

### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History & Founding**
The **Rhodes Scholarship** was established in 1902 under the terms of Cecil Rhodes’ will, aiming to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties with the U.S. The scholarship is administered by the Rhodes Trust and is one of the most prestigious international fellowships.

#### **Selection Criteria & Terms**
Applicants must demonstrate exceptional academic achievement, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. Scholarships cover tuition, living expenses, and maintenance, with a typical tenure of two years. The scholarship is open to citizens of over 60 countries, including Australia, Canada, and the U.S.

#### **Controversies & Legacy**
Criticisms have centered on Rhodes’ colonialist legacy, the exclusion of women until 1977, and debates over its impact on public service versus private careers. Despite these controversies, the Rhodes Scholarship remains a cornerstone of international education, with alumni contributing significantly to global leadership in politics, academia, and business.

### **Shuping Scholarship**

#### **History & Founding**
The **Shuping Scholarship** was founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, a philanthropist and businessman, to support academically gifted students from poor families. The scholarship provided full tuition, medical care, and extracurricular activities, fostering a culture of service and education.

#### **Impact & Legacy**
Over 6,000 students have benefited from the scholarship, many of whom went on to become leaders in education, business, and government in China. The program continues to support students through its **Shuping Scholarship Foundation**, ensuring long-term sustainability and impact.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**

#### **Overview & Selection**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is one of the most prestigious academic awards in the U.S., recognizing high-achieving high school students. It begins with the **Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT)**, followed by rounds of selection for semifinalists and finalists. ### **King’s Scholar**

#### **Historical Origins & Traditions**
The **King’s Scholar** program is a prestigious scholarship at **King’s College Cambridge** and **Eton College**, where students are selected for their academic excellence and awarded a scholarship to study at the college. #### **Notable Alumni**
Notable King’s Scholars include **Boris Johnson**, **Harold Macmillan**, **Aldous Huxley**, **Winston Churchill**, and **George Orwell**. The program continues to produce leaders in academia, politics, and the arts.
