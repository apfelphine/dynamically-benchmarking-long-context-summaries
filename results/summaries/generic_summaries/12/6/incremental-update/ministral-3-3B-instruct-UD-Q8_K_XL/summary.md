**Summary:**

*Daughters of Africa* (1992) and *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), edited by Margaret Busby, gather over **200 Black feminist voices**, spanning historical figures like **Hatshepsut and Sojourner Truth** to contemporary leaders such as **Alice Walker, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, and Zadie Smith**, while supporting scholarship through awards (e.g., the **New Daughters of Africa Award**) and initiatives like *The Black Curriculum* and Ghana’s **LLOATAD Library**.

The **Rhodes Scholarship** (1902), originally a **colonial male-only program**, expanded globally by **2018** to **64 countries**, excluding women until **1977** and Black Africans until **1991**. Despite its **0.7% acceptance rate**, it now operates in **21 regions**, with allocations like **32 for the U.S. and 10 for Southern Africa**. Criticized for its colonial legacy, it supports **social justice initiatives**, including the **first transgender scholar (2019)**. Recent funding (e.g., **£75M in 2013**) reflects evolving priorities, though debates persist over exclusions.

The **Kennedy Scholarship** (1964), established to honor **President JFK’s ideals of liberty**, selects scholars based on **intellectual ability, public service potential, and resilience**, with notable recipients like **Ed Balls, Dame Kate Bingham, and figures such as economists Ros Altmann and Mervyn King, journalists Stephanie Flanders and Anatole Kaletsky, and artists like poet Hannah Sullivan**. Administered by the **UK Kennedy Memorial Trust**, it supports interdisciplinary excellence across **economics, law, journalism, arts, and academia**, including jurists like **Mary Arden and Nicholas Hamblen (UK Supreme Court Justices)**. Trustees oversee operations, mirroring programs like the **Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships**.

The **Miss America Pageant** (1921) evolved from a racially segregated contest into a platform for representation debates, with key figures like **Cheryl Browne, Vanessa Williams, and Brían Nguyen** challenging norms. Scandals and financial struggles reshaped it, while **Miss America 2.0 (2019)** abolished physical judging but later reversed reforms, reintroducing **swimsuit judging** and expanding to **28 contestants**. Recent winners include **transgender champion Brían Nguyen** and diverse figures like **Emma Broyles and Grace Stanke**.

**UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**, founded in **1944** to support **HBCUs**, now serves all students while maintaining majority Black enrollment. Founded by **William J. Trent, Frederick D. Patterson, and Mary McLeod Bethune**, it operates through initiatives like the **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars telethon** and **Walk for Education**, with **100+ member HBCUs** across the U.S., including **Tuskegee, Morehouse, and Spelman**, fostering **educational equity**, particularly in **Tennessee, Texas, and Virginia**. Its scholarship programs include the **National Merit and Achievement Scholarships**, now integrated into the **Achievement Capstone Program (2016)**.

Florida’s **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** (1997–present) provides **100% tuition coverage (A Level)** and **75% (B Level)**, with vocational programs like **Gold Seal CAPE/GSV**. Eligibility requires **16 college-prep courses, GPA recognition, ACT/SAT/CLT scores, and service hours**, with recent reforms (2021) raising thresholds and expanding to homeschooled students by **2025**. Awards include **FAS (100% tuition), FMS (75% tuition), GSV/GSC ($39–$49/credit hour), and ATS (FAS + extra funding)**, with renewal demands **3.00+ GPA (FAS) or 2.75+ (others)**.

Norway’s scholarships evolved from the **Oxford Fund (1919)**, originally supporting **Norwegian students at Wadham College, Oxford**, to a broader fellowship program across disciplines like **Law, Economics, English Literature, History, Medicine, and specialized fields** (e.g., **maritime law, quantum chemistry**). Modern awards cover **global fields** like **biochemistry, neuroscience, and oncology**, with gaps in funding during **1926–1927, 1961–1963, 1973–1975, 2001–2005, 2017–2018, and 2021–2022**. Recent recipients pursued **DPhil/PhD degrees**, reflecting Norway’s academic priorities.

**Cambridge and Eton’s King’s Scholarships** (since **1317**) award **70 scholars annually** at Eton (with **privileges like black gowns and College House status**), including **Boris Johnson, Aldous Huxley, and George Orwell**. Ely, Canterbury, and Worcester scholars enjoy unique benefits like **marriage/buryal rights and academic/musical scholarships**, with Worcester’s scholars coordinating events via Latin petitions. The scholarships emphasize **long-term academic and leadership development**, with a legacy spanning centuries.
