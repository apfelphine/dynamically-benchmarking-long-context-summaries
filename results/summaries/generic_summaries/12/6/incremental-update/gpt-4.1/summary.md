## Overview

**Daughters of Africa** (1992), edited by Margaret Busby, and its sequel, **New Daughters of Africa** (2019), are landmark anthologies celebrating the literary, cultural, and historical achievements of over 200 women of African descent. These collections have significantly expanded the Black feminist literary canon and advanced recognition, educational, and professional opportunities for Black women writers.

## Reception and Impact

Both anthologies have fostered global solidarity and empowerment, transforming the literary landscape and amplifying appreciation for Black women’s contributions.

## Scholarships, Programs, and Educational Advancement

### Prestigious International and U.S. Scholarships

Prominent scholarships—including the **Marshall**, **Harkness**, **Rhodes**, **Kennedy**, **Shuping**, **Fulbright**, and Kazakhstan's **Bolashak**—promote academic exchange and leadership development. The Kennedy Scholarship, launched in the UK after President John F. Kennedy’s assassination, enables study at Harvard or MIT, managed by the Kennedy Memorial Trust and supported by the Kennedy family. Nearly 600 Kennedy Scholarships have been awarded based on academic excellence, leadership, and public service, resulting in distinguished alumni: Bank of England governors, foundation directors, policy advisors, leading journalists and editors (The Economist, BBC), eminent poets and authors, renowned scientists and academics across major universities, and senior legal professionals, including UK Supreme Court Justices. The Marshall Scholarship strengthens U.S.–UK academic ties, while Fulbright is globally respected, with prominent alumni such as Nelson Mandela and Angela Merkel.

### Historic UK Scholarships: King's Scholars

King's Scholars at institutions like Eton College, Westminster School, and King’s College Cambridge receive lifelong privileges, merit awards, special attire, and ceremonial roles, with many prominent former scholars.

### Norway Scholarship

Established in 1919, the Norway Scholarship has enabled Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford in diverse fields, fostering robust alumni networks and annual traditions attended by dignitaries, including King Harald V.

### Support for Historically Black Colleges and Institutions

- **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF):** Raised over $500 million for public HBCUs/PBIs.
- **United Negro College Fund (UNCF):** Provides scholarships for HBCUs, including redirecting National Achievement Scholarship funding.

### National Merit Scholarship Program

This program recognizes U.S. academic excellence via standardized tests, conferring college funding and admissions advantages. Its alumni include Bill Gates and Elena Kagan. The related National Achievement Scholarship for Black students ended in 2015, with funding transferred to UNCF.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)

Florida’s Bright Futures program awards merit scholarships for undergraduate and some graduate studies, with multiple categories (FAS, FMS, GSV/GSC, ATS) and renewable options for eligible recipients.

### Muskie Internship Program

Promotes career advancement for Eurasian scholars, building on Edmund S. Muskie’s dedication to public service.

## International Cultural Promotion

The **British Council** fosters UK educational and cultural exchange worldwide.

## Miss America Pageant: History, Reform, and Inclusion

Since 1921, the Miss America Pageant has championed diversity, scholarship, and inclusion, promoting social values and expanding opportunities for women.

## Profiles in Leadership and Public Service

### Edmund S. Muskie

A transformative leader in government, civil rights, and environmental law, Muskie’s legacy is honored through various scholarships and institutional programs promoting public service.
