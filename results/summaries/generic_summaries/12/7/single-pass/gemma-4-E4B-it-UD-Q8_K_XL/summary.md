# Prestigious International Scholarship and Philanthropic Programs

## Harkness Fellowship
Established by Anna Harkness's family, the Harkness Fellowships originally aimed to facilitate British graduates studying in the U.S. in reciprocity to the Rhodes Scholarships. The program expanded to include Dominion Fellowships for graduates from Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and South Africa. Since 1997, the focus has been exclusively on health care policy, making it a prestigious health policy award. Fellows from various international nations spend one year conducting research at major American institutions, enhancing their skills and fostering international collaboration.

The Fellowship involves a rigorous seminar program, including orientations, an International Symposium on Healthcare Policy, and a Washington Policy Briefing on Capitol Hill. The program is administered and funded by the Commonwealth Fund, with support also coming from various external bodies in Germany, Canada, and the UK.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by Koo to support poor, academically strong students in Shanghai, the scholarship was inspired by his father's motto: "Return to the Society what you have gained from the society." Initially, the scholarship provided full tuition and comprehensive support, including a study center with labs, and volunteer opportunities in charity work, which helped shape the scholars into future contributors to China's rebuilding.

Following the Cultural Revolution, the scholarships were revived in 1986 with government support. Today, the program awards over 700 scholarships yearly to secondary and tertiary institutions, maintaining a focus on service. Koo's late-life donation of HK$10 million in 1995 ensured long-term funding via the Shu Ping Scholarship Foundation.

## Rhodes Scholarship
The Rhodes Scholarships originated in 1902 from Cecil John Rhodes's will to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. Today, the scholarship is open to postgraduate students globally, adhering to criteria emphasizing intellectual and scholastic attainments alongside moral character, courage, and leadership potential.

Despite historical controversies regarding Rhodes's colonialist views and early exclusions of women and Black Africans, the scholarship has evolved. It is highly selective, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%. Recent developments include the introduction of the Global Rhodes Scholarships, extending opportunities worldwide, and the increasing public criticism regarding its founder's legacy.

## Marshall Scholarship
The Marshall Scholarships were established in 1953 by the UK Parliament as a memorial to George C. Marshall, aiming to foster understanding between the U.S. and the UK. The primary objectives are to enable intellectually distinguished American young leaders to study in the UK, help them appreciate contemporary Britain, and motivate them to act as cultural ambassadors.

Selection requires university endorsement, and recipients can study at any UK university. The scholarship is comparable in selectivity to the Rhodes and Mitchell Scholarships, with a strong emphasis on academic distinction and potential for significant societal contribution. The Association of Marshall Scholars hosts the Marshall Forum to continue these transatlantic dialogues.

## Kennedy Scholarship
The Kennedy Scholarship was established in the UK in 1964 as a living memorial to John F. Kennedy. It supports British graduates in studying at either Harvard or MIT.

Selection criteria include intellectual attainment, ability to express oneself, and the suitability of the proposed study course. The program aims for standards comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships, maintaining a commitment to excellence and public service potential.

## Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program, founded in 1945 following WWII, aims to promote international peace and mutual understanding through educational exchange between the U.S. and other countries. It operates on a bi-national basis, offering various grants, including for international students to study in the U.S. (Foreign Student Program) and for U.S. scholars to study abroad (U.S. Scholar Program).

The program is administered by the U.S. Department of State and utilizes bilateral commissions worldwide. While the program has faced recent political scrutiny regarding ideological screening, it remains a vast network of educational and cultural exchange.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF advocates for higher education at public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). It has raised over $500 million to provide scholarships and support for programmatic growth at its 55 member institutions.

TMCF acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) in 2013 to enhance talent identification and entrepreneurship development within the HBCU community.

# Other Notable Programs

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida-based program, designed to emulate the HOPE Scholarship, funds four levels of awards: Florida Academic Scholars (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS), Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV), and Gold Seal CAPE Scholars (GSC). The requirements mandate specific GPA, standardized test scores, and completion of volunteer or paid work hours. The program has undergone several overhauls to adapt to budgetary and demographic changes.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This program uses the PSAT/NMSQT as its initial screening tool, recognizing top students as "Commended" and further into Semifinalists and Finalists. Finalists are considered for one of the 2,500 National Merit $2,500 Scholarships. Critics have noted concerns over relying solely on the PSAT/NMSQT for such a prestigious award.

## National Achievement Scholarship Program (NASAP)
Established in 1964 to encourage Black American youth to pursue higher education, NASAP originally allowed Black students to compete in both the National Merit and NASAP. Following the 2015 program conclusion, its functions transitioned to the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) through the Achievement Capstone Program for college graduates.

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944, UNCF was created by activists to support Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), aiming to make them "strong citadels of learning." The fund has secured major donations, including a $70 million gift from MacKenzie Scott in 2025. The UNCF motto is "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

## Bolashak
Instituted in 1993 by Kazakhstan's Nursultan Nazarbayev, Bolashak (meaning "Future") provides scholarships to Kazakh youth for study overseas, billed as a method to instill Western, democratic values. Over time, the program has expanded significantly, with thousands of scholarships granted to leading universities across Australia, the UK, Germany, and Canada.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
This U.S. Department of State program provides summer internships, career training, and financial support to Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the U.S. The program was established in 1992 and was later relaunched by Cultural Vistas in 2015.

# Literary Anthologies and Culture

## Daughters of Africa
Margaret Busby's *Daughters of Africa* (1992) is a landmark anthology featuring over 200 women writers of African descent, from the ancient Egyptian period to the present. It was lauded for shattering the misconception that there was little literary output by black women before the 1980s and for tracing continuities across continents and centuries.

A companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), expanded the scope to include writers who came of age after the first anthology. The work has significant global influence, inspiring further educational initiatives and receiving critical acclaim for its depth and vibrancy.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award, partnered with SOAS University of London, supports African women students. Its funding is sustained by donations from Busby and her publisher, as well as contributions from all contributors to the anthology, who waived their fees.

## British Council
As a charity sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, the British Council promotes British culture and English language learning globally in over 100 countries. Activities range from offering English examinations and MOOCs to running mobility programs like Education UK and Erasmus+.

The organization faces scrutiny regarding its operational strategies, including outsourcing functions to India and controversies surrounding its operations in politically sensitive regions like Russia, Afghanistan, and Syria.

## King's Scholars and Queen's Scholars
This tradition dates back to 1317 when King Edward II established the first scholars at Cambridge. At Eton College, King's Scholars are admitted via a competitive examination and wear a special gown. The term is also used at King's College Cambridge for undergraduates achieving first-class degrees.

The King's Schools at Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester award these scholarships in recognition of academic or musical attainment, with certain schools granting Queen's Scholarships in honor of the reigning monarch.

# Miscellaneous Educational/Competitive Awards

## Miss America Pageant
Originating from a 1920 Atlantic City event, the pageant has evolved significantly. Early in its history, it was racially segregated by rule seven until 1950. Major shifts occurred during the Civil Rights and feminist movements. The format has undergone radical changes, including the removal of the swimsuit competition in 2018 ("Miss America 2.0"), though some of these changes were later reversed in 2023.

## National Merit Scholarship Program Criticism
Admissions officers from NACAC have criticized the National Merit Scholarship Corporation for relying solely on the PSAT/NMSQT as the initial screening tool, arguing it deviates from best practices in admissions testing.

## Norway Scholarship
Conceived in 1919 by an alumnus to promote closer association between Norwegian and British youth, this scholarship allows Norwegian students to study in Oxford. It has faced funding challenges over the decades but maintains an active tradition of annual dinners and a Norwegian chapter of the Oxford Society.

## Studienstiftung
Founded in Dresden in 1925, this German scholarship offers general financial and academic support to exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates. Selection is multi-channel, including nominations from schools, professors, or examination boards, alongside an assessment test. Support is provided for study in Germany, EU, or Switzerland, based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility."

## Comprehensive Comparisons
Several programs, including the Rhodes, Marshall, and Fulbright Scholarships, share similar structural similarities—such as the focus on post-graduate study, international exchange, and elite selection—though they differ in their geographic focus (UK vs. US), applicant nationality requirements, and specific programmatic missions (e.g., Rhodes focusing on broader global citizenship vs. Marshall focusing on UK-US relations).
