## Harkness Fellowship

**History & Evolution:**  
The Harkness Fellowships, initiated in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness under the Commonwealth Fund, were designed to allow British graduates to study in the U.S., later expanding to include students from other Commonwealth nations. Renamed in 1961, the fellowships broadened fields beyond civil service and since 1997 have focused exclusively on health care policy. Today, they are regarded as prestigious health policy awards, tenable for twelve months for scholars from multiple countries.

**Current Program:**  
Harkness Fellows spend a year at top U.S. institutions conducting research in health care policy, supplemented by seminars and policy briefings with international leaders. The program emphasizes understanding the U.S. health system, research skill development, and fostering international collaboration.

**Administration & Funding:**  
Administered by the Commonwealth Fund in NYC, additional funding comes from organizations in Germany, Canada, Switzerland, UK, and the Netherlands.

**Notable Alumni:**  
Alumni include prominent figures in academia, government, medicine, and the arts, such as historians, Nobel laureates, university chancellors, medical leaders, writers, composers, and journalists.

## Shuping Scholarship

**Founding & Scope:**  
Established in Shanghai in 1939 by philanthropist and businessman Koo Soh Bing, the Shuping Scholarships support academically strong but financially disadvantaged students, offering tuition, health care, libraries, and extracurricular activities. The foundation also developed a strong tradition of alumni-led charitable activities, including refugee support and emergency medical aid.

**Development & Legacy:**  
Between 1939–1949, over 1,100 students benefitted. Post-Cultural Revolution, the program resumed in the mid-1980s, expanding to over 6,000 recipients by 2000. The scholarship continues to focus on academic support, leadership, and service, managed by alumni associations. Koo’s philanthropic work and personal story underpin the scholarship’s ethos of “returning to society what you have gained.”

**Funding & Structure:**  
The program is supported by the Shanghai Education Development Foundation and the Shuping Scholarship Foundation, with a significant personal endowment by Koo. Activities are administered by alumni committees in Shanghai and Beijing.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

**Anthology & Reception:**  
Margaret Busby's "Daughters of Africa" (1992) and its successor "New Daughters of Africa" (2019) are landmark anthologies compiling work by over 400 women of African descent. They have been lauded for their literary significance, representation, and influence in foregrounding Black women's voices globally.

**Influence & Legacy:**  
The anthologies have inspired similar international collections, supported the black feminist literary canon, and motivated readers and creators worldwide. They remain essential educational and reference works, appearing on “must-read” lists and in major literary discussions.

**Award & Outreach:**  
In partnership with Myriad Editions and SOAS, the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students studying in the UK, funded partly by waived contributor fees. The project has also donated thousands of copies to educational groups and was recognized for its impact with an NAACP Image Award nomination.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes

**History & Structure:**  
Founded in 1925, dissolved under National Socialism, and re-established in 1948, Studienstiftung is Germany’s largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation for talented students and doctoral candidates.

**Admission & Scholarships:**  
Candidates are selected through school, university, competitions, assessment tests, and nomination. Scholarships offer need-based financial support and extensive academic development opportunities, including conferences, seminars, mentoring, and summer academies.

**Internal & Open Programmes:**  
Additional programmes exist through partnerships, offering international opportunities in STEM and other fields.

**Statistics & Notables:**  
In 2023, nearly 15,000 scholars were funded. Alumni include Nobel laureates, leading academics, business leaders, politicians, and artists.

## Rhodes Scholarship

**Origins & Purpose:**  
Created in 1902 under the will of Cecil Rhodes to strengthen unity among English-speaking nations; open to all races and religions. Administered by the Rhodes Trust in Oxford and expanded over time to include students globally.

**Selection & Structure:**  
The selection, modeled on academic and personal excellence and leadership, is highly competitive—with acceptance rates less than 1%. Scholars study postgraduate courses at Oxford with full tuition and stipend.

**Controversies & Reforms:**  
Historically, women and non-white scholars were excluded, with admissions reforms in the late 20th century. Ongoing criticism centers on Rhodes’s legacy, colonialism, and alumni’s career choices away from public service.

**Alumni Impact:**  
Rhodes Scholars have excelled in academia, government, science, advocacy, arts, and more, including heads of state, Nobel winners, and influential thinkers.

**Comparison:**  
The Rhodes model has influenced several major global scholarship programs, such as the Marshall, Gates Cambridge, and Knight-Hennessy scholarships.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)

**Mission & Reach:**  
Established in 1987, TMCF supports students at public HBCUs through scholarships, programmatic, and capacity-building support—raising over $500 million to date. It is distinct from UNCF, focusing on 55 member schools including HBCUs and PBIs.

**Partnerships & Leadership:**  
TMCF has received major grants from corporations like Apple and Boeing. It merged with the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to enhance entrepreneur identification and support within HBCUs.

## British Council

**Mission & Activities:**  
The British Council, established in 1934, promotes UK culture, education, and English language worldwide, operating in over 100 countries. It offers teaching, exams (notably IELTS), educational programs, arts and cultural initiatives, and digital educational resources.

**Funding & Governance:**  
Chartered and publicly funded; in 2014–2015 received nearly £1 billion income. It operates under UK government sponsorship as a non-departmental public body.

**Major Programs:**  
Mobility schemes include Education UK, Erasmus+, and Connecting Classrooms. The British Council also runs arts initiatives, radio, online festivals, and educational exchanges, including work in challenging political contexts such as North Korea.

**Controversies:**  
Issues have arisen over outsourcing, library closures, perceived politicization, staff safety failures, diplomatic tensions (especially Russia), and transparency in management and expenses.

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)

**Purpose & History:**  
Founded in 1944 to support black students and HBCUs, UNCF provides scholarships open to all but primarily supports African American students. It is recognized for its fundraising, including high-profile donations from prominent figures and corporations, and its iconic motto “A mind is a terrible thing to waste.”

**Member Institutions:**  
UNCF funds 37 member HBCUs, with alumni notable in politics, arts, health, and business.

## Fulbright Program

**Origins & Purpose:**  
Founded in 1946, Fulbright aims to foster international understanding through educational exchange. Managed by the U.S. State Department, the program offers grants to U.S. and international students, scholars, teachers, and professionals in over 160 countries.

**Program Structure:**  
Grants cover study, research, teaching, and professional development across disciplines, managed through bi-national commissions.

**Controversies & Recent Issues:**  
Fulbright has faced program suspensions due to political interference (notably in Russia and by the Trump administration in 2025) and board resignations over politicization.

**Notable Alumni:**  
Fulbright alumni include heads of state, Nobel laureates, and leading figures in arts, sciences, and academia.

## Bolashak Scholarship

**Description & Impact:**  
Established in Kazakhstan in 1993 for advanced study abroad to foster Western values and skills among Kazakh youth. Over 12,800 scholarships had been granted by 2018, with a majority given to ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements. Bolashak alumni hold key positions across Kazakh society.

**Universities:**  
Bolashak recipients study at leading universities globally in diverse fields.

## Miss America

**History & Development:**  
Originating in Atlantic City in 1921, Miss America grew from a local event into a national scholarship-based competition. Over time, it faced and responded to controversies, including issues of racial segregation, inclusion, and sexism, adapting competition formats and eligibility.

**Recent Changes:**  
The "Miss America 2.0" era eliminated swimsuit judging and focused on talent and advocacy, but some changes were reversed in 2023 with new leadership.

**Cultural Impact:**  
Miss America remains a significant cultural event, with notable titleholders and hosts, and has prompted books and documentaries exploring its legacy.

## National Merit Scholarship Program

**Structure:**  
The U.S.-based National Merit Scholarship is a competitive, standardized-test-driven program for high-achieving high school students. Eligibility is based on PSAT/NMSQT scores and ongoing academic achievement.

**Competition Stages:**  
Students progress from Commended, to Semifinalist, to Finalist status, with about 8,000 receiving scholarships annually. Scholarships are sponsored by NMSC, corporations, and colleges.

**National Achievement Program:**  
The related National Achievement Program, focusing on Black students, ended in 2015 with funds redirected to UNCF’s Achievement Capstone Program.

**Criticism:**  
Use of standardized testing as the sole criterion has been debated for its fairness and transparency.

## Marshall Scholarship

**Origins & Objectives:**  
Established in 1953 as a memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship enables outstanding young Americans to study at any UK university, supporting leadership and UK–US understanding.

**Selection & Structure:**  
Awarded via a rigorous, competitive process, scholars are chosen for intellect, character, and leadership promise. Around 1000 are endorsed annually; recent cohorts number 36–51. The scholarship typically covers two years of postgraduate study.

**Comparison:**  
Marshall is often compared to Rhodes and Fulbright, with flexibility in university choice and a slight emphasis on academic achievement.

**Association & Forums:**  
AMS sustains alumni connections and hosts international forums on transatlantic issues. Notable alumni span government, academia, journalism, law, and the arts.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program

**History & Structure:**  
Florida’s Bright Futures, started in the late 1990s, is a merit-based scholarship providing significant tuition coverage for in-state college students, with multiple award levels based on GPA, standardized test scores, and volunteer/work hours.

**Changes & Eligibility:**  
After years of expansion, recent years saw higher test score requirements and more complex eligibility. The program now offers four scholarship levels and additional awards for top-ranked students in each county.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

**Background:**  
Named after Senator Edmund S. Muskie, a major figure in U.S. environmental legislation and diplomacy, the Muskie Internship Program supports Eurasian graduate students in the U.S., offering placements and career training.

**Muskie’s Legacy:**  
He is considered a father of the 1960s environmental movement (Clean Air and Water Acts) and the federal budget process, and was influential in diplomacy and civil rights.

## Kennedy Scholarship

**History & Selection:**  
Founded in 1963 as a UK memorial to President Kennedy, providing scholarships for British students at Harvard or MIT. Selection is national and highly competitive, seeking intellectual ability, communication, originality, public service commitment, and resilience.

**Trustees & Notables:**  
Managed by a high-profile board, Kennedy Scholars have become leaders in politics, academia, finance, and the arts.

## King's Scholar

**Historical Origins & Modern Role:**  
King's Scholars are students selected for academic merit at elite UK institutions (notably Eton College and King's College Cambridge), receiving scholarships, honors, and unique privileges. The title extends to various King's Schools established or re-endowed by royalty, and carries historical prestige.

## Norway Scholarship

**History & Impact:**  
Established in 1919, the Norway Scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Funded and managed by the Norway Scholarship Committee, it has supported studies in diverse fields. Funding expanded in the late 20th century, and annual gatherings continue as key networking events.
