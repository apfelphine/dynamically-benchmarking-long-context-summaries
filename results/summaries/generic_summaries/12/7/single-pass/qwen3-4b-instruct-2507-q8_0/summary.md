### **Harkness Fellowship**
Established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness, the Harkness Fellowship began as a reciprocal program to the Rhodes Scholarships, enabling British graduates to study in the U.S. It expanded to include Dominion students from Australia, Canada, South Africa, and New Zealand. In 1961, it was renamed and evolved into a premier health policy fellowship, now open to international health professionals. Fellows spend 12 months conducting research at U.S. institutions, gaining deep insight into health systems, policy, and global health challenges. The program includes seminars with government officials, health ministers, and academic leaders, fostering international collaboration.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, the Shuping Scholarship supports poor students from China with strong academic records. Initially, it provided full tuition and ongoing support, including a library, free medical care, and extracurricular classes. During the 1940s, it expanded into social service, including emergency medical aid and a home for refugee children. After the Cultural Revolution, Koo revived the program in 1986 with government and private support, now awarding scholarships to over 6,000 students annually. The program emphasizes service, self-reliance, and community engagement, with scholars continuing to serve as pillars in Chinese society.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
This award honors women of African descent through a powerful anthology, *Daughters of Africa*, which celebrates the rich literary traditions of Black women globally. Widely praised for its scope and impact, the anthology is a foundational text in African feminist literature. In 2019, a companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, was published, featuring 200 contemporary voices. The award includes a scholarship for African women students, funded by Margaret Busby and supported by major institutions. It promotes representation, equity, and the global recognition of Black women’s contributions to literature and society.

### **Studienstiftung**
A German foundation established in 1925, the Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes supports undergraduate and graduate students through financial and academic aid. It operates under the principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Students are selected through school or university nominations, and the program offers summer academies, language courses, and mentoring. The foundation supports over 2,000 scholars annually, with a strong focus on STEM, social sciences, and the arts. It has a long-standing tradition of student-led activities and has produced numerous Nobel laureates and leading scholars.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the Rhodes Scholarship enables postgraduate students from around the world to study at Oxford University. It emphasizes leadership, intellectual excellence, and global citizenship. The scholarship is open to men and women from over 20 countries, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%, making it one of the most competitive. The program fosters international understanding and has produced influential leaders in politics, science, and human rights. In recent years, it has expanded to include women and scholars from non-Western nations, though controversies persist over its historical ties to colonialism and gender exclusivity.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
Established in 1987, the Thurgood Marshall College Fund supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) by providing scholarships, capacity-building, and programmatic support. It has raised over $500 million and supports 55 HBCUs. The fund promotes educational equity and leadership development, with a mission to strengthen HBCUs and empower students from underrepresented communities. The organization has received major donations from Apple, Koch Industries, and Netflix, and its work is aligned with broader efforts to advance racial equity in higher education.

### **British Council**
A global cultural and educational organization, the British Council promotes mutual understanding through education, arts, and language. It operates in over 100 countries, offering English language teaching, cultural exchange, and academic programs. It is known for its IELTS exams, teacher training, and international partnerships. Despite facing criticism over outsourcing and political controversies, the organization remains a key player in global education and cultural diplomacy.

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
Founded in 1944, UNCF supports African American students through scholarships and educational programs at historically Black colleges and universities. It has awarded over 4 million scholarships and supported the education of prominent leaders in business, politics, and the arts. The organization emphasizes academic excellence and leadership, with a strong legacy of promoting Black excellence and equity in education.

### **Fulbright Program**
Established in 1946, the Fulbright Program fosters international educational exchange between the U.S. and over 160 countries. It offers scholarships for students, scholars, and teachers to study, teach, and conduct research abroad. The program promotes mutual understanding and cultural exchange, with over 325,000 alumni. It has faced criticism over political interference and racial bias, but remains a cornerstone of U.S. international education policy.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
Florida’s Bright Futures program provides merit-based scholarships to high school graduates, with levels based on academic performance. Initially a major state investment, it has undergone significant changes due to political and policy shifts. The program now includes four levels and has faced criticism for racial disparities and increasing SAT score requirements. It remains a significant source of financial support for Florida students, though its future is subject to ongoing reform.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
Established in 1992, the Muskie Internship Program supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students through summer internships and career training in the U.S. It is administered by the U.S. Department of State and promotes international understanding. Named after Senator Edmund Muskie, a prominent advocate for environmentalism and civil rights, the program reflects his legacy of diplomacy, public service, and global cooperation.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
Created in 1964 in memory of President John F. Kennedy, the Kennedy Scholarship provides postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. It emphasizes leadership, public service, and intellectual excellence. The program has produced prominent leaders in politics, economics, and public service. Selection is rigorous and comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships, with a strong focus on service to the public good.

### **King's Scholar**
A historic designation with roots in 1317, the King's Scholar title is awarded at Eton College, Westminster School, and King's College Cambridge. At Eton, it is a prestigious scholarship for students admitted to the central College, with privileges including special gowns and access to traditions. At King's College Cambridge, it is awarded to undergraduates with first-class degrees. The title also exists at seven King's Schools in England, where it recognizes academic or musical excellence and often includes fee reductions.

### **Norway Scholarship**
Established in 1919, the Norway Scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Founded by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, it promotes educational and cultural ties between Norway and the U.K. The program has awarded over 100 scholars since its inception, with a focus on diverse fields such as law, medicine, and the humanities. Despite funding challenges, the scholarship has been revitalized through private and institutional support, continuing to serve as a bridge between Norway and British academia.
