# Harkness Fellowship
The Harkness Fellowship, originally established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarship, is now exclusively focused on health care policy and practice. Administered by the Commonwealth Fund of New York, the program offers 12-month fellowships to individuals from over a dozen countries, including Australia, Canada, France, Germany, and the UK. Fellows conduct research at prestigious U.S. institutions such as Harvard, Stanford, and Kaiser Permanente, while also attending seminars on healthcare policy, international symposiums, and congressional briefings. The program is funded primarily by the Commonwealth Fund, with additional support from entities in Germany, Switzerland, the UK, and the Netherlands. Since its inception, it has produced notable alumni in fields ranging from medicine and public health to law, arts, and academia, cementing its status as a premier award in global health policy.

The fellowship's history reflects a significant evolution from its original intent to broaden academic exchange. Initially open to British graduates and later expanded to include Canadian, Australian, New Zealand, and South African students, the program introduced categories for business, politics, and the arts in 1961. However, a strategic shift occurred in 1997, limiting the scope to health care to enhance the program's impact and prestige within that specific sector. Current fellows engage with diverse U.S. healthcare systems and develop international collaborations, supported by a rigorous selection process and a curriculum designed to deepen their understanding of American health policy challenges.

# Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by businessman Koo in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship was established to support poor but academically excellent students, embodying the motto "Return to the Society what you have gained from the society." Originating as a personal initiative to help apprentices during the Japanese invasion, the scholarship grew to support over 1,100 students by 1949, providing full tuition, library access, science laboratories, and various extracurricular activities. Koo personally treated scholars as family, encouraging them to engage in charitable work, such as building a home for refugee children and establishing an emergency medical fund to assist the impoverished during wartime inflation.

After a brief suspension, the scholarships were revived in 1986 with government and alumni support, expanding to over 6,000 recipients by 2000 across secondary and tertiary institutions in Shanghai, Beijing, and Huzhou. In 1995, Koo donated HK$10 million to the Shanghai Education Development Foundation and established the Shuping Scholarship Foundation to ensure long-term sustainability. Today, the program continues its tradition of self-reliance, where students manage their own study centers and receive supplementary financial aid for those in financial hardship, fostering a legacy of service and national rebuilding.

# Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award is a prestigious honor established to benefit African women students, named after the acclaimed editor of the landmark anthology *Daughters of Africa*. The award, launched in partnership with the University of London's SOAS, features accommodation at International Students House in London. It is supported by significant donations from the anthology's publisher, Myriad Editions, and contributions from the book's contributors, who waived their fees to fund the initiative. The first recipient, Idza Luhumyo from Kenya, was announced in 2020, followed by Stella Gichohi in 2023.

The award stems from the immense success and influence of Busby's 1992 anthology, which gathered over 200 women of African descent and is widely regarded as a monumental achievement in documenting the literary canon of black women globally. The anthology has been praised for its breadth, connecting writers across continents and centuries, and has inspired subsequent works like the 2019 *New Daughters of Africa*. Beyond the scholarship, Busby and her publisher have donated books to schools and libraries worldwide, aiming to ensure that the voices of African women are heard and celebrated in educational settings.

# Studienstiftung
The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is Germany's largest and oldest student scholarship foundation, founded in 1925 as a department of the Deutsche Studentenschaft. Although dissolved during the Nazi era, it was re-established in 1948 as a registered association in Cologne, dedicated to supporting academically and socially committed students. The foundation offers comprehensive scholarships covering both financial needs (based on the BAFöG system) and academic excellence, providing monthly allowances, health insurance, and access to summer schools, research groups, and language courses. Admission is competitive, with candidates selected through various channels including nominations by schools, professors, or examination boards, and the foundation emphasizes principles of excellence, initiative, and responsibility.

As of 2023, the Studienstiftung approved 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships, serving approximately 4.8% of the student population in Germany. The organization provides substantial support for students studying in Germany, the EU, and Switzerland, regardless of citizenship or age. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize laureates in physics, chemistry, and medicine, as well as prominent figures in politics, business, and the arts. The foundation also runs internal programs for selected scholars and alumni and collaborates on open scholarship programs with other organizations to broaden its reach.

# Rhodes Scholarship
The Rhodes Scholarship, established in 1902 under the will of Cecil John Rhodes, was originally designed to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the United States. Initially limited to single men under 25, the scholarship evolved over time to include women (1977), married men (1959), and students from diverse global backgrounds. Today, it is one of the world's most competitive postgraduate scholarships, with an overall acceptance rate of 0.7%, offering two-year funding for study at Oxford University. Recipients receive full tuition coverage, a maintenance stipend, and access to Rhodes House, a historic mansion serving as a hub for scholars.

Despite its prestigious history, the scholarship faces ongoing controversies regarding its founder's colonial legacy, the exclusion of women and Black Africans in its early years, and the trend of recipients entering private business rather than public service. In response, the Rhodes Trust has introduced the Global Rhodes Scholarships to expand access and partnerships with organizations like the Atlantic Philanthropies. Notable alumni include heads of state such as Bill Clinton and Bob Hawke, as well as distinguished scientists, authors, and human rights advocates who have utilized the fellowship to advance their fields and influence global discourse.

# Thurgood Marshall College Fund
The Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF) was established in 1987 to support Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), evolving from the Thurgood Marshall Scholarship Fund in 2006. TMCF is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization that has raised over $500 million in donations from major philanthropists and corporations, including Apple, Charles Koch Foundation, and Boeing. Unlike the United Negro College Fund, which supports approximately 900 colleges, TMCF focuses specifically on 55 member schools, providing scholarships, capacity building, and institutional support to enhance the quality of education at these institutions.

The fund has played a crucial role in the development of HBCUs, offering financial aid to students and funding academic programs, including law and medical schools. TMCF's leadership has included figures like Harry L. Williams, who served as president and CEO from 2018, and it has expanded its mission to include entrepreneurship education through the acquisition of the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013. The organization's efforts have been instrumental in preserving the legacy of Thurgood Marshall and ensuring the continued success of HBCUs in the higher education landscape.

# British Council
The British Council is a unique organization that operates simultaneously as a charity, a public corporation, and an executive non-departmental public body, sponsored by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. Founded in 1934 to promote English education and British culture abroad, it has grown into a global network with offices in over 100 countries. Its activities include promoting the English language through face-to-face teaching and standardized exams like IELTS, facilitating cultural exchange through arts and educational programs, and supporting UK universities internationally. In 2014–2015, the Council reported an income of £973 million, with significant portions allocated to language development and educational cooperation.

The British Council has faced various controversies, including accusations of outsourcing to Indian entities, allegations of bias in IELTS testing, and closures of offices in regions like Syria and Russia due to geopolitical tensions. Despite these challenges, it remains a key player in international relations, hosting major cultural events and educational initiatives. The organization has also adapted to changing global dynamics, such as suspending operations in Russia in 2025 following its designation as an "undesirable organization" by the Kremlin, which restricted access to English language exams and academic resources.

# UNCF
The United Negro College Fund (UNCF) was founded in 1944 by William J. Trent, Frederick D. Patterson, and Mary McLeod Bethune to unite college presidents and raise funds for Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). Originally named the Negro College Fund, it shifted to its current name in 2008 to reflect evolving social attitudes. The UNCF has raised hundreds of millions of dollars through major donations from philanthropists like John F. Kennedy, Walter Annenberg, and Reed Hastings, who contributed $120 million in 2020. The organization administers scholarships for students attending HBCUs and has a rich history of fundraising events, most notably the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars" telethon, which has raised over $200 million.

In addition to scholarships, the UNCF advocates for the success of HBCUs and has recently focused on capacity building and long-term sustainability, with a $70 million donation from billionaire MacKenzie Scott in 2025. The organization's motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste," has become iconic in advertising. The UNCF continues to play a vital role in supporting the educational and professional advancement of African Americans, with notable alumni including Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson.

# Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program, established in 1945, is the world's largest educational exchange program, dedicated to promoting peace and understanding through the exchange of scholars and students between the United States and other countries. Administered by the Institute of International Education and coordinated by the U.S. Department of State, the program operates through bi-national agreements with over 160 countries. It offers various grants, including the Fulbright U.S. Student Program, Foreign Student Program, and Scholar grants, supporting fields ranging from the humanities to the sciences, with specific awards for teachers and professionals.

The program's administration has faced recent challenges, including a 2024 designation of the Institute of International Education as "undesirable" by the Russian government, which effectively ended the program in the USSR, and a 2025 funding freeze by the Trump administration that disrupted support for thousands of scholars. Despite these disruptions, the Fulbright Association and alumni networks continue to foster international cooperation. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners, heads of state, and influential figures in academia and public service, cementing the program's legacy in global diplomacy and education.

# Bolashak
The Bolashak Scholarship Program, launched in 1993 by the first President of Kazakhstan, Nursultan Nazarbayev, aims to send Kazakh youth overseas to study at top universities to inculcate Western democratic values. Instituted to broaden the horizons of Kazakh students, the program has granted over 12,000 scholarships by 2018, with applicants selected through rigorous testing in language, psychology, and academic performance. The scholarships cover tuition and living expenses, allowing students to study in more than 30 countries, including major universities in the UK, US, Canada, and Australia.

While the program has seen significant growth, with the number of applicants exceeding the intake capacity of major universities in Kazakhstan, it has faced criticism for not always granting recipients the authority to implement positive changes upon their return. Nevertheless, many Bolashak graduates hold key positions in state and private organizations within Kazakhstan. The program continues to expand, offering students access to leading global institutions, reflecting Kazakhstan's commitment to international education and development.

# Miss America
The Miss America Pageant originated in 1921 as a competition to boost tourism in Atlantic City, evolving from a "bather's revue" into a formal beauty contest. Initially segregated and restricted to white contestants, the pageant faced significant criticism and protests in the 1960s and 1970s for promoting sexism, racism, and militarism, leading to the crowning of a sheep as a symbolic protest. The pageant underwent major reforms in 2018 under the "Miss America 2.0" format, eliminating the swimsuit competition and focusing on talent and scholarship, although these changes were later reversed in 2023.

The pageant has a rich history of inclusivity, with the first African-American contestant in 1971 and the first African-American winner, Vanessa Williams, in 1984. Williams resigned under pressure following the unauthorized publication of nude photographs. The pageant continues to face scrutiny regarding its scholarship programs, which have been criticized for not matching their claimed financial reach, and its ongoing efforts to balance tradition with modern inclusivity and representation.

# National Merit Scholarship Program
The National Merit Scholarship Program is a prestigious academic competition for U.S. high school students that recognizes outstanding achievement through standardized testing and academic evaluation. The process begins with the PSAT/NMSQT, where top scorers (approximately the top 50,000 nationally) are recognized as "Commended" students. About 16,000 of these advance as Semifinalists, and roughly 15,000 of those qualify as Finalists, who are considered for merit scholarships. The program includes various scholarship levels, from $2,500 National Merit Scholarships to college-sponsored awards, with winners selected by committees of college admissions officers and counselors.

Established in 1964, the program also included the National Achievement Scholarship Program for Black American youth, which was discontinued in 2015 and its funds redirected to the United Negro College Fund. The National Merit Scholarship Corporation has faced criticism from college admissions counselors for relying heavily on standardized test scores as the primary screening tool. Despite this, the program continues to be a significant achievement marker for high school students, with notable alumni including Bill Gates, Steve Ballmer, and numerous other distinguished individuals.

# Marshall Scholarship
The Marshall Scholarship, established in 1953, is a prestigious program that enables intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the United Kingdom. Unlike the Rhodes Scholarship, which is limited to single men under 25, the Marshall Scholarship was designed to be inclusive of women and married men under 28, reflecting a commitment to broader participation. The scholarship covers university and college fees and provides a quarterly maintenance stipend, allowing scholars to study at any British university. The program has seen significant growth, with the number of scholarships increasing to 50 per year in 2024, the largest cohort in its history.

The Marshall Scholarship has produced many influential figures, including Supreme Court Justices, Nobel Prize winners, and prominent politicians. The Association of Marshall Scholars (AMS) actively promotes the scholarship's legacy through the annual Marshall Forum, engaging leaders in discussions on transatlantic issues. The program remains highly selective, with an acceptance rate around 3.4% to 3.7%, emphasizing academic excellence and potential to contribute to British-American understanding.

# Bright Futures Scholarship Program
The Bright Futures Scholarship Program is a state-funded initiative in Florida designed to provide tuition assistance to students based on academic merit rather than financial need. Originally modeled after Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, the program offered four levels of scholarships (A, B, Vocational, and later CAPE) covering up to 100% of tuition at public institutions. However, the program faced criticism for disproportionately benefiting wealthy families and impacting minority students, leading to significant cuts in 2011 that raised SAT score requirements and reduced award amounts.

In 2018, the program underwent a major overhaul, renaming the levels to Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Florida Gold Seal Vocational (GSV), and Florida Gold Seal CAPE (GSC). Despite these changes, controversies continue, with debates over SAT score thresholds and the impact on low-income and minority students. The program includes specific requirements for volunteer service hours and maintains a tiered structure that rewards high academic achievement, with recent updates in 2025 introducing the Classic Learning Test as an alternative to SAT and ACT scores.

# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program is a professional exchange program established by the United States Congress in 1992, named after the influential Maine Senator and Secretary of State, Edmund S. Muskie. The program provides summer internship placements, career training, and financial support to Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the United States. It was originally administered by various boards but was relaunched by Cultural Vistas in 2015 with a renewed format and scope. The program aims to foster international understanding and professional development by connecting students with U.S. institutions and professionals.

Edmund Muskie's legacy is marked by his significant contributions to environmental policy, civil rights, and diplomacy, including the passage of the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts. His political career spanned the governorship of Maine, the U.S. Senate, and the Secretary of State under President Jimmy Carter. The Muskie Internship Program honors his commitment to international cooperation and education, continuing his work in bridging cultural and educational gaps between the U.S. and Eurasian nations.

# Kennedy Scholarship
The Kennedy Scholarship, created in 1964, is a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy, offering scholarships to British students to study at Harvard University or the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. Established following the assassination of JFK, the program was designed to honor his ideals of freedom and unity, with trustees appointed by the UK Prime Minister. The selection process is rigorous, comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships, evaluating candidates on intellectual attainment, readiness, and commitment to public service.

The scholarship has produced notable alumni in politics, economics, journalism, and academia, including figures like David Miliband, Mervyn King, and David Held. The Kennedy Memorial Trust, which manages the scholarship, includes prominent individuals such as former scholars and academic leaders. The program continues to uphold the legacy of JFK, fostering international understanding and academic excellence among British students.

# King's Scholar
The term "King's Scholar" has historical significance in British education, originating from King Edward II in 1317 who sent boys to study at Cambridge. At Eton College, King's Scholars (Collegers) are the oldest students, selected through competitive examination and admitted into the school's original central college buildings. They wear a distinctive black academic gown over their uniform and enjoy traditional privileges, such as eating in College Hall and participating in ancient formal traditions. Since the 1960s, the scholarship has been subject to means testing, though it still provides financial support for fee waivers based on circumstances.

The title "King's Scholar" is also used at King's College Cambridge for undergraduates who obtain first-class degrees, and at the seven King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) for high-achieving students. These scholarships often come with fee discounts and are awarded based on academic or musical attainment. The tradition of King's Scholars reflects a long history of academic excellence and royal patronage in British education.

# Norway Scholarship
The Norway Scholarship was established in 1920 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford University, specifically at Wadham College. Conceived by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a former British vice-consul in Norway, the fund was created to foster cultural and educational ties between Norway and Britain. Initially funded by a donation of NOK 60,000, the scholarship faced financial challenges, leading to periods where no scholars were appointed due to inadequate funding. In the late 1970s, a new committee raised significant funds to ensure the program's continuity.

Over the decades, the Norway Scholarship has supported students in diverse fields, including law, English literature, economics, and medicine. The program has maintained a strong connection with Oxford University, with the committee organizing annual dinners featuring guests of honor from Oxford. Notable scholars include King Harald V of Norway, who attended Balliol College, and the program continues to facilitate academic exchanges between Norway and the UK, despite occasional gaps in funding or awards.
