### **Harkness Fellowship**

#### **History**
The **Harkness Fellowship**, established by philanthropist Anna Harkness in 1918, was initially created to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships by supporting British graduates studying in the U.S. In 1927, it expanded to include Dominion Fellowships for students from Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and South Africa. By 1929, it introduced Dominion Civil Service Fellowships. The fellowship was renamed the **Harkness Fellowships** in 1961, broadening its scope to include General Fellowships in business, banking, politics, creative arts, and journalism. Since 1997, it has focused exclusively on health care, accepting Fellows from Australia, Canada, France, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland, and the UK.

#### **Current Program**
Harkness Fellows spend a year conducting research at U.S. institutions like Harvard, Brigham and Women’s Hospital, Columbia, Stanford, Johns Hopkins, Kaiser Permanente, or the Veterans Health Administration. They engage in seminars, including an international healthcare policy symposium, a Washington policy briefing, and a Canadian policy briefing. Fellows also participate in a final reporting seminar and the AcademyHealth Annual Research Meeting.

#### **Administration and Funding**
The program is funded by the **Commonwealth Fund** in New York City, with additional support from external organizations like the B. Braun Stiftung, Robert Bosch Stiftung, Canadian Health Services Research Foundation, and others.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Prominent alumni include historians, scientists, politicians, artists, and activists, such as Professor David Armitage, Sir Jonathan Bate, and Nobel Prize-winning chemist Manfred Eigen.

### **Shuping Scholarship**

#### **History**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, a philanthropist, the **Shuping Scholarship** aimed to support financially disadvantaged students with strong academic records. During the first decade, 1,100 students received scholarships, and the program expanded to include libraries, science labs, free medical care, and extracurricular activities. Koo revived the scholarship in 1986, supporting students in Shanghai, Beijing, and Huzhou. The program now awards scholarships to 37 secondary schools and 24 tertiary institutions, with an emphasis on fostering service to society.

#### **Key Initiatives**
Koo established the **Emergency Medical Fund** in 1945 to provide financial aid to those in need of urgent medical treatment, and the **Home for Refugee Children** in 1947 to offer education and vocational training to homeless children. The scholarship continues to emphasize self-reliance, with students managing the scholarship’s administration and activities.

#### **Philanthropist: Koo Soh Bing**
Koo’s life motto was **"Filial Piety, Honesty, Credibility, and Tolerance."** He donated HK$10 million to the **Shuping Scholarship Foundation** in 1995, ensuring its long-term funding. His legacy includes numerous schools and institutions in Huzhou, Zhejiang Province, named after his father, Shuping.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**

#### **Reception and Legacy**
The **Daughters of Africa** anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, was widely praised for its comprehensive collection of works by women of African descent. It was included in prestigious lists, such as *Sacred Fire: QBR*’s "100 Essential Black Books" and was recognized for its impact on fostering global understanding and literary diversity. The anthology inspired similar projects, including *Töchter Afrikas* (German edition) and was celebrated for its role in promoting African women’s writing.

#### **Contributors**
The anthology features over 200 contributors, including literary giants like Toni Morrison, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, and Nobel laureates like Wole Soyinka. Notable entries span poetry, fiction, memoir, and essays, reflecting the rich cultural and literary heritage of African women.

#### **New Daughters of Africa (2019)**
The follow-up anthology, *New Daughters of Africa*, expanded the collection to include 200 new writers, further cementing Busby’s legacy as a pioneer in African women’s literature. It was supported by the **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**, which funds an African woman student annually.

### **Studienstiftung**

#### **History and Administration**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** was founded in 1925 in Dresden and later relocated to Cologne. It offers scholarships for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students based on academic merit and social commitment. The organization is governed by a president and secretary-general, with a focus on fostering excellence, initiative, and responsibility.

#### **Admission Criteria**
Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence and social engagement. Selection is done through nomination by schools, professors, or examination boards, or through an assessment test. Scholarships are renewable based on academic performance and social contributions.

#### **Scholarships**
The Studienstiftung provides financial support (€300 monthly) and academic resources, including summer schools, research groups, and mentoring programs. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners, politicians, and leaders in science, business, and arts.

#### **Statistics**
As of 2023, the Studienstiftung approved 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships, with 71.5% of scholars receiving a study expense allowance. The organization has a strong presence in universities across Germany, with notable contributions to education and research.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History and Motivation**
The **Rhodes Scholarship**, established in 1902 under Cecil Rhodes’ will, aims to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties with the U.S. It was initially open only to male students but became coeducational in 1977. The scholarship is administered by the **Rhodes Trust** and is one of the most prestigious postgraduate scholarships globally.

#### **Selection Criteria**
Applicants must demonstrate literary and scholastic attainments, energy, truth, courage, and leadership potential. The selection process is highly competitive, with an overall global acceptance rate of 0.7%.

#### **Scholarship Terms**
Rhodes Scholarships cover university and college fees and provide a monthly maintenance stipend. Scholars may study any full-time postgraduate course at Oxford University. The scholarship is tenable for one, two, or three years.

#### **Controversies**
Criticisms include concerns about racial exclusion, colonial legacies, and the tendency of recipients to pursue careers in business rather than public service. Despite these controversies, the scholarship remains highly regarded for its impact on global leadership and diplomacy.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**

#### **History**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund** was established in 1987 to support public **Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)**. It provides scholarships, capacity-building support, and programmatic funding. TMCF is a 501(c)(3) organization dedicated to advancing education and economic opportunities for students at HBCUs.

#### **Member Schools**
TMCF supports 55 member schools, including 42 HBCUs, 3 Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), and 7 Historically Black Community Colleges. Notable schools include Howard University, Tuskegee University, and Morehouse College.

#### **Leadership and Funding**
The organization is led by figures like Johnny C. Taylor Jr. and Harry L. Williams. It has received significant funding from Apple, the Charles Koch Foundation, and The Boeing Company, totaling over $500 million to date.

### **British Council**

#### **History and Activities**
The **British Council** was established in 1934 to promote British culture, education, and language globally. It operates in over 100 countries, offering English language teaching, cultural exchange programs, and educational initiatives. The British Council has faced controversies, including accusations of political influence and human rights concerns.

#### **Key Programs**
The British Council provides English language courses, standardized tests (e.g., IELTS), and educational exchanges. It also supports initiatives like the **Connecting Classrooms** program, which connects schools globally, and the **Peacekeeping English Project** to improve military personnel’s English skills.

#### **Controversies**
Criticisms include allegations of outsourcing key functions to Indian partners, political influence, and controversies related to human rights and cultural diplomacy.

### **Fulbright Program**

#### **History and Mission**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1945, aims to promote international understanding and cultural exchange through educational opportunities. It provides grants for U.S. students, scholars, and professionals to study, research, or teach abroad, and for foreign nationals to visit the U.S.

#### **Program Structure**
The Fulbright Program includes various grants such as the **U.S. Student Program**, **Foreign Student Program**, and **Scholar Program**. It is administered by the **Institute of International Education (IIE)** and supported by the U.S. Department of State.

#### **Selection and Impact**
Selection is based on academic achievement, leadership potential, and a compelling project proposal. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners, Pulitzer Prize recipients, and leaders in government, academia, and industry.

### **Miss America**

#### **Overview**
The **Miss America Pageant** began in 1919 and has evolved significantly over the years. Originally a beauty contest, it became a platform for promoting cultural and social values. The pageant has faced controversies related to racism, sexism, and its impact on participants.

#### **History and Changes**
The pageant was racially segregated until 1950 and faced protests in the 1960s and 1970s. In 2018, it adopted a new format, **Miss America 2.0**, which removed physical appearance judging. Recent changes, including the return of swimsuit judging, have sparked further debate.

#### **Controversies**
Criticisms include allegations of elitism, lack of transparency in scholarship distribution, and controversies surrounding past winners, such as Vanessa Williams’ resignation due to nude photos.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**

#### **Program Entry Requirements**
To enter the competition, students must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents, take the **Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT)**, and meet academic and citizenship criteria.

#### **Selection Process**
The program uses the PSAT/NMSQT scores to determine Semifinalists and Finalists. Semifinalists must submit SAT scores, academic records, and essays to advance to the Finalist stage. Approximately 8,000 Finalists receive National Merit Scholarship awards.

#### **Criticism**
Critics argue that the program’s reliance on a single standardized test does not adequately measure academic potential and that transparency in selection processes is lacking.

### **Marshall Scholarship**

#### **History and Objectives**
The **Marshall Scholarship**, established in 1953, is a living memorial to George C. Marshall. It supports outstanding U.S. graduates to study in the UK, fostering understanding and cooperation between the two nations.

#### **Selection Criteria**
Applicants must demonstrate academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. Selection is based on university endorsements and interviews by regional committees.

#### **Scholarship Terms**
Marshall Scholarships cover university and college fees and provide a quarterly maintenance stipend. Scholars may study any full-time postgraduate course at a UK university.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable alumni include former U.S. Secretaries of State, Nobel Prize winners, and leaders in academia and business.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**

#### **History**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**, established in Florida, aims to provide academic merit-based scholarships to high school graduates. It was initially based on the HOPE Scholarship model but underwent significant changes in 2011 and 2018.

#### **Scholarship Levels**
The program offers four levels: **Florida Academic Scholar (FAS)**, **Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS)**, **Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholar (GSV)**, and **Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholar (GSC)**. Each level has specific academic and service requirements.

#### **Award Amounts**
FAS covers 100% of tuition and fees, while FMS covers 75%. Vocational scholarships provide funding for career and technical education programs.

### **King’s Scholar**

#### **Historical Origins**
The **King’s Scholar** tradition dates back to 1317 when King Edward II established the first King’s Scholars at the University of Cambridge. The scholarships are awarded to students who excel academically and are admitted to King’s College.

#### **Eton College**
At Eton College, King’s Scholars are selected through competitive examinations and wear academic gowns. They are housed in the central College building and participate in traditional Etonian activities.

#### **King’s Schools**
The King’s Schools at Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester award King’s Scholarships based on academic or musical attainment. Scholars are admitted to the foundation of the respective cathedral and receive special privileges.

### **Norway Scholarship**

#### **History**
The **Norway Scholarship** was established in 1919 to support Norwegian students studying at Wadham College in Oxford. It was conceived by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a former British vice-consul in Norway, who raised funds to enable Norwegian students to participate in English university life.

#### **Past Scholars**
Notable past scholars include researchers, academics, and professionals who have contributed to various fields such as law, economics, and literature. The scholarship has faced financial challenges but continues to support students annually.
