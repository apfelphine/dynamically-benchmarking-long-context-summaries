## Prestigious and International Scholarships

Elite scholarships at UK institutions, such as Eton College, Westminster School, and King's College Cambridge, have shaped global leaders, including George Orwell, Aldous Huxley, Boris Johnson, and John Maynard Keynes. The Rhodes Scholarship at Oxford is renowned for fostering international leadership and now emphasizes diversity and inclusion, though it faces scrutiny over its colonial legacy.

## Major Transatlantic and Inspired Scholarships

The Marshall and Kennedy Scholarships reinforce US-UK academic ties, inspired by the Rhodes model. The Harkness Fellowships (est. 1925) support international study in the US, currently focusing on health care policy and boasting distinguished alumni.

## Other International Scholarships

The Norway Scholarship (since 1919) supports Norwegian study at Oxford to promote bilateral relations, while Kazakhstan’s Bolashak Scholarship (since 1993) funds overseas education for national advancement, though its impact is debated.

## European Scholarship Foundations

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes

Germany's Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, founded in 1925 and re-established in 1948, awards scholarships based on academic merit and social responsibility, open to all backgrounds and ages. Scholars receive financial and mentoring support, with programs for personal development. In 2023, over 2,179 new students and 301 doctorates were supported, with alumni including Nobel and Fields Medal laureates and notable leaders.

## American Scholarship Programs

Programs such as the Miss America Pageant and Florida Bright Futures offer educational support, while National Merit Scholarships reward high PSAT scores, increasingly recognizing HBCUs.

## Fulbright Program

Since 1946, the Fulbright Program has enabled international academic exchanges, operating via bilateral commissions despite political and financial challenges.

## Scholarships Supporting African American Education

### United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944, UNCF sustains 37 HBCUs and provides scholarships for all backgrounds, propelled by fundraising and a prominent alumni network, including Martin Luther King Jr., Alexis Herman, and Spike Lee.

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports 55 public HBCUs and PBIs, distributing over $500 million in scholarships and championing entrepreneurship.

## British Council: Activities and Controversies

Founded in 1934, the British Council promotes UK culture and education globally through initiatives like Connecting Classrooms and Erasmus+. It faces scrutiny over transparency and finances and is headed by Paul Thompson.

## Daughters of Africa and Black Feminist Literature

Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* anthologies showcase women writers of African descent, inspiring awards and influencing Black feminist literature internationally.

## Edmund S. Muskie: Career and Legacy

Muskie’s public service as Maine governor, US senator, and Secretary of State featured achievements in civil rights, environmental policy, and diplomacy, inspiring fellowships and internships.

## Shuping Scholarship

Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, guided by filial piety, honesty, and integrity, the Shuping Scholarship supports academically talented but financially disadvantaged students in China. Koo personally funded the scholarships until 1995, then endowed HK$10 million and established the Shuping Scholarship Foundation in Hong Kong to ensure continuity. The program expanded across decades, supporting hundreds annually at secondary and tertiary levels, and offering additional educational and welfare initiatives. Milestones include commemorative events, addition of new schools, and alumni associations that foster ongoing engagement and societal service. Despite declining health, Koo remained actively involved until his death in 1998, after which his legacy was continued by his son, George Koo, helping thousands across China and Hong Kong.
