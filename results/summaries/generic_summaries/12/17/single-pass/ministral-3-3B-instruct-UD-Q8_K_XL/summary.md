### **King’s Scholars and Related Scholarships**

#### **Historical Origins and King’s Scholars at Cambridge**
The **King’s Scholars** program was established in **17th-century England** under King Edward II, initially as a merit-based scholarship at the University of Cambridge. The scholarships were later expanded by King Henry VI to Eton College, where King’s Scholars (now called "Collegers") are selected through competitive exams, awarded a Foundation Scholarship, and live in the oldest house, "College," distinguished by a black academic gown. Scholars receive a **KS** designation in their surname and are eligible for scholarships, though financial support has increasingly been means-tested since the 1960s.

#### **King’s Scholars at Eton College**
At Eton College, King’s Scholars are students who pass the College Election exam and are awarded a Foundation Scholarship. They live in the central College building, share academic life with Oppidans (non-scholars), and participate in formal traditions. Collegers are distinguished by a black gown over their school uniform and are eligible for scholarships, though funding is now often tied to financial need. Notable alumni include politicians, writers, and scholars such as **James Arbuthnot**, **Eric Blair (George Orwell)**, and **Boris Johnson**.

#### **King’s Scholars at Westminster School**
Westminster School awards **Queen’s Scholars** when there is a reigning Queen and **King’s Scholars** when there is a reigning King. These scholars are selected by competitive exams, board at the oldest house, and wear gowns during school services in Westminster Abbey.

#### **King’s Scholars at King’s College, Cambridge**
King’s College Cambridge offers scholarships to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees. Winners receive a **£350 prize**, attend a ceremony in King’s College Chapel, and dine for free with their Director of Studies. This scholarship is a historical legacy of King Henry VI’s founding of both King’s College and Eton College.

#### **King’s Scholars at the King’s Schools**
The King’s Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester) were re-endowed by King Henry VIII after the Dissolution of the Monasteries. Scholarships are awarded based on academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts. Notably, **King’s Ely** awards scholarships to high-achieving students, including those from the lower sixth, and grants special privileges such as the right to marry in Ely Cathedral and be buried within its grounds.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
#### **Early Life and Education**
Edmund Muskie (1914–1996) was born to Polish immigrants in Maine and attended Bates College, where he excelled in debate and sports. After graduating, he studied law at Cornell University, where he secured funding from a wealthy benefactor. Muskie later worked as a lawyer and served in the **U.S. Navy Reserve (1942–1945)**, where he trained as a diesel engineer and later as a lieutenant.

#### **Political Career**
Muskie’s political career began in Maine’s House of Representatives (1946–1959), where he advocated for bipartisanship and environmental policies. He became **Governor of Maine (1955–1959)**, where he expanded infrastructure, environmental protections, and public education. His tenure marked a shift in Maine’s political landscape, increasing Democratic influence.

#### **U.S. Senate (1959–1980)**
Muskie served in the U.S. Senate for **21 years**, becoming a key figure in environmental legislation, including the **Clean Air Act (1970)** and **Clean Water Act (1972)**. He was a vocal critic of the Vietnam War and later served as **U.S. Secretary of State (1980–1981)** under President Jimmy Carter. His leadership in diplomacy and environmental policy cemented his legacy as a champion of public service and social justice.

#### **Legacy and Honors**
Muskie’s contributions include founding the **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**, which supports international scholars and graduate students in the U.S. He was awarded the **Presidential Medal of Freedom** and the **Laetare Medal** by the University of Notre Dame. His work in environmentalism, civil rights, and diplomacy remains influential.

### **Norway Scholarship**
#### **History**
The **Norway Scholarship** was established in **1919** by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a British vice-consul in Norway, to enable Norwegian students to study at **Wadham College, Oxford**. The scholarship was initially funded by donations, including a significant contribution from the famous polar explorer **Fridtjof Nansen**. Over time, the scholarship expanded to support students from various fields, though funding became insufficient in later years.

#### **Past Recipients**
The scholarship has supported students from diverse academic backgrounds, including law, economics, history, and medicine. Notable recipients include scholars from fields such as **philology, social sciences, and the humanities**.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
#### **History and Objectives**
The **Marshall Scholarship**, established in **1953**, honors the contributions of **George Marshall**, the U.S. Secretary of State who spearheaded the **Marshall Plan** to rebuild post-war Europe. The scholarship enables **American students** to study in the UK, fostering cultural and academic exchange. It is open to students of all disciplines, excluding pre-professional programs, and provides full tuition, living expenses, and a maintenance stipend.

#### **Selection Criteria**
Applicants must demonstrate **academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service**. Selection is based on university-endorsed applications, interviews, and essays. The scholarship is highly competitive, with an acceptance rate of approximately **3.4%**.

#### **Notable Recipients**
Marshall Scholars have included figures such as **Stephen Breyer** (U.S. Supreme Court Justice), **Ray Dolby** (inventor), **Thomas Friedman** (journalist and Pulitzer Prize winner), and **Nannerl Keohane** (former president of Duke University). The scholarship has also produced leaders in academia, business, and public policy.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
#### **Creation and Purpose**
The **Kennedy Scholarship**, established in **1964**, honors the legacy of **President John F. Kennedy** and his commitment to education and democracy. It allows **British nationals** to study at **Harvard University** or the **Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)**. The scholarship is funded by the **John F. Kennedy Memorial Trust** and selected through a rigorous competition.

#### **Selection Criteria**
Candidates must demonstrate **exceptional academic achievement, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service**. The selection process includes interviews and evaluations by a panel of trustees.

#### **Notable Recipients**
Prominent alumni include **Ed Balls** (politician), **Dame Kate Bingham** (former head of the UK Government’s COVID-19 Vaccine Task Force), and **David Miliband** (former Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs). The scholarship has supported many leaders in politics, academia, and public service.

### **Miss America Pageant**
#### **History and Controversies**
The **Miss America Pageant** began in **1919** as a beauty contest, but it has evolved into a cultural and political symbol. The pageant faced criticism for its **racial and gender biases**, particularly in the early 20th century, when it was racially segregated. The first Black contestant, **Cheryl Browne**, competed in **1970**, and the first Black winner, **Vanessa Williams**, won in **1984**. The pageant has since undergone reforms, including the introduction of **Miss America 2.0** in **2018**, which aimed to reduce physical appearance judging. However, recent controversies, including **sexual harassment allegations** and **financial mismanagement**, have led to further scrutiny.

#### **Recent Changes**
In **2023**, the pageant reversed some of its **Miss America 2.0** changes, including the return of swimsuit judging and the reintroduction of physical appearance as a criterion. The pageant continues to face criticism for its **commercialization, lack of diversity, and ethical concerns**.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
#### **Overview**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida provides **merit-based scholarships** to high school graduates. It was established to emulate the **HOPE Scholarship** in Georgia and offers four levels of funding: **Florida Academic Scholar (FAS)**, **Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS)**, **Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholar (GSV)**, and **Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholar (GSC)**. Scholarships cover tuition, fees, and living expenses, though eligibility requirements have been tightened over time.

#### **Eligibility and Renewal**
To qualify, students must meet **academic standards**, including SAT/ACT scores and high school GPA. Scholarships are renewable for up to five years, provided students maintain academic standards. The program has faced criticism for **disproportionately affecting minority students** and for its **increasingly restrictive eligibility criteria**.

### **Fulbright Program**
#### **History and Administration**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in **1946**, is the largest international educational exchange program in the world. It was created to promote **peace and understanding** through educational exchange. The program is administered by the **U.S. Department of State** and offers fellowships for **students, scholars, and professionals** to study, research, or teach in the U.S. or abroad.

#### **Program Types**
The Fulbright Program includes:
- **U.S. Student Program**: Supports U.S. students to study abroad.
- **U.S. Scholar Program**: Supports U.S. faculty and professionals to teach or research abroad.
- **Foreign Student Program**: Supports international students to study in the U.S.
- **Special Programs**: Includes the **Fulbright-Clinton Fellowship**, **Fulbright-Schuman Program**, and **Fulbright-Hays Program**.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Fulbright alumni include **Nobel Prize winners**, **Pulitzer Prize recipients**, and **heads of state**, such as **Jimmy Carter**, **Bill Clinton**, and **Angela Merkel**. The program has also produced leaders in **academia, business, and public policy**.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
#### **Overview**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is a merit-based scholarship program for high school students in the U.S. It is administered by the **National Merit Scholarship Corporation (NMSC)** and is open to all students who take the **Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT)** in their junior year.

#### **Selection Process**
The program has four rounds:
1. **PSAT/NMSQT**: Students are recognized as **Commended Merit** if they score in the top 5% of their state.
2. **Semifinalist**: Approximately **16,000** students are selected as semifinalists based on PSAT scores and academic records.
3. **Finalist**: Semifinalists must submit SAT scores, academic records, and essays to be considered for **National Merit Scholarships**.
4. **Scholarships**: About **8,000** finalists receive **$2,500 National Merit Scholarships**, with additional scholarships provided by corporate sponsors.

#### **Criticism**
Critics argue that the program **overemphasizes standardized test scores** and **undermines other forms of academic achievement**. The NMSC has faced scrutiny for its **lack of transparency** in the selection process.

### **British Council**
#### **History and Activities**
The **British Council** is a British international organization that promotes **cultural, educational, and scientific exchange** between the UK and other countries. Founded in **1934**, it has played a key role in promoting the English language and British culture globally. The British Council operates in over **100 countries**, offering programs such as **English language courses, scholarships, and cultural exchanges**.

#### **Controversies**
The British Council has faced criticism for **outsourcing key functions**, including teacher recruitment, to **India**. Critics argue that this has led to **political blind spots** and **lack of transparency** in policy-making. Additionally, the British Council has been accused of **prioritizing cost-cutting over human rights** and **British cultural diplomacy**.

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
#### **Overview**
The **UNCF** is a **501(c)(3) nonprofit organization** founded in **1944** to support **historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs)**. It provides scholarships, grants, and other forms of financial aid to students attending its member institutions and other colleges. The UNCF has supported many prominent alumni, including **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.**, **Spike Lee**, and **Samuel L. Jackson**.

#### **Scholarships and Funding**
The UNCF administers a variety of scholarships, including the **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars** telethon, which has raised over **$200 million** for scholarships. The organization has also received significant donations, such as **$70 million from MacKenzie Scott** in **2025** and **$120 million from Netflix founder Reed Hastings** in **2020**.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
#### **Overview**
The **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award** is an annual literary award established to honor **women of African descent** for their contributions to literature. The award is named after **Margaret Busby**, a renowned British author and editor, who edited the groundbreaking anthology **Daughters of Africa** in **1992**. The award supports an African woman student and is funded by the publisher **Myriad Editions**.

#### **Significance**
The award recognizes the **diverse and influential voices** of women of African descent in literature. It aims to **amplify underrepresented stories** and support emerging writers. The award has been praised for its **inclusivity and impact** on the literary community.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
#### **Overview**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** is a **501(c)(3) nonprofit organization** established in **1987** to support **public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)**. It provides **scholarships, grants, and capacity-building support** to its member schools and students. ### **Harkness Fellowship**
#### **Overview**
The **Harkness Fellowship** is a prestigious fellowship program established in **1925** by the **Commonwealth Fund** to support **healthcare policy and practice**. The fellowship enables **international scholars** to study and research in the U.S. for **12 months**, focusing on healthcare systems and policy challenges.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Harkness Fellows have included **Nobel Prize winners**, **academic leaders**, and **public health experts**, such as **Sir Ronald Bottrall**, **Sir Graeme Catto**, and **Dame Rosalyn Higgins**. The program has played a key role in shaping **global healthcare policy**.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**

The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is a **German merit scholarship program** established in **1925**. It supports **exceptionally talented students** from all backgrounds, including those from **non-German EU countries and Switzerland**. The program has supported many **notable alumni**, including **scientists, politicians, and artists**.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
#### **Overview**
The **Shuping Scholarship** was established in **1939** by **Koo Soh Bing**, a Chinese businessman and philanthropist, to support **academically excellent students from poor families**. The scholarship was initially awarded in **Shanghai** and later expanded to other regions in China. It emphasizes **service to society** and provides **tuition, living expenses, and extracurricular activities**.

#### **Legacy**
The Shuping Scholarship has supported over **6,000 students** and has played a significant role in **educating and empowering future leaders** in China. The scholarship continues to promote **merit-based education and social responsibility**.
