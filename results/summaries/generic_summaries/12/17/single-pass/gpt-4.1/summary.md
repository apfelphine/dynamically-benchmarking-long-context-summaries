## Major International Scholarships and Fellowships

### Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 via Cecil Rhodes's will, the Rhodes is one of the world’s oldest and most prestigious postgraduate scholarships, enabling students from over 60 countries to study at the University of Oxford. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, integrity, leadership, and commitment to service. The program expanded in the 21st century to include women, global candidates, and underrepresented groups; as of 2018, selections are from over 20 constituencies. Its structure inspired other major scholarships (Marshall, Kennedy, Schwarzman, etc.), and today, its selectivity is among the most rigorous globally (acceptance rate ~0.7%). Scholars have made notable contributions in government, civil rights, academia, law, medicine, and the arts. The scholarship has attracted criticism over Rhodes's colonialist legacy, historic exclusions, and a tendency for graduates to enter private professions rather than public service.

### Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953 as a "living memorial" to US Secretary of State George C. Marshall and as a gesture of postwar gratitude, Marshall Scholarships fund outstanding American students for graduate study at any UK university. The program emphasizes intellectual distinction, academic ambition, leadership, and fostering US-UK relations. It currently awards up to 50 scholarships annually, with an approximate 3–4% selectivity for endorsed applicants. Marshall Scholars have excelled in academia, law, government, and research. The scholarships feature an association of alumni and host annual forums on transatlantic issues.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Founded in 1964 as a UK memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this program enables British postgraduates to study at Harvard or MIT. Selection focuses on intellectual achievement, communication skills, suitability of study program, originality, and public service potential. Nearly 600 scholars have been chosen, who have often gone on to distinguished careers in government, economics, journalism, academia, law, and the arts.


## Other Prestigious Scholarships

### Fulbright Program
Started in 1946, the Fulbright Program is among the largest and most renowned international exchange initiatives, fostering mutual understanding and peace through academic, research, and cultural exchanges between the US and over 160 countries. Programs include funding for students, researchers, teachers, and professionals. It is overseen by the US State Department and bi-national commissions. The program has produced over 325,000 alumni, including Nobel laureates, heads of state, and Pulitzer Prize winners. Recent years have seen political challenges, including withdrawals from Russia and political interference in US program administration.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Germany’s largest and oldest scholarship foundation, established in 1925, funds high-achieving students at German/EU universities based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Admission is highly competitive via nominations or assessment, and scholars receive substantial financial and academic support plus access to seminars and mentoring. The foundation runs additional specialized and joint scholarships and partners with major organizations. Alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, leading scientists, artists, politicians, and business figures.

### Harkness Fellowships (Commonwealth Fund Fellowships)
Created in 1925 to reciprocate Rhodes Scholarships, Harkness Fellowships have supported study in the US for graduates from UK and Commonwealth nations. Since 1997, they focus on health policy, accepting fellows from multiple countries for research placements at leading US institutions and seminars with policymakers.

### Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship supports academically gifted but financially disadvantaged secondary and tertiary students. The program has been sustained for decades with comprehensive support (tuition, extracurriculars, medical care), and engagement in community service; alumni have played key roles in China’s development in government, academia, health, and engineering.


## British and International Scholarships

### King's Scholar (UK)
"King’s Scholar" denotes students awarded elite scholarships at historic institutions:
- **Eton College:** Since 1440, King’s Scholars (around 70 at any time) are selected by competitive exams, live in College, and participate in school traditions including the Wall Game. Notable alumni include John Maynard Keynes, Aldous Huxley, George Orwell, and UK prime ministers.
- **Westminster School:** Scholars are selected via exams and called King’s or Queen’s Scholars depending on the monarch.
- **King’s College, Cambridge:** Awarded to undergraduates who achieve first-class degrees.
- **Historic King's Schools:** Re-endowed by Henry VIII, King's Scholarships are awarded for academic or musical achievement and may include fee discounts.
- **Norway Scholarship:** Established in 1919, funds study for Norwegian students at Wadham College, Oxford, sustaining cultural ties.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)
A merit-based undergraduate scholarship for Florida residents, founded in 1997. Program tiers (Florida Academic Scholars, Medallion Scholars, Gold Seal Vocational, and Gold Seal CAPE) offer tuition coverage tied to academic, test score, and service hour requirements. At its peak, covered close to 40% of high school graduates, but was later restricted, affecting minority students. The program underwent reform in 2018 (restoring value and adding vocational tiers).

## US Merit-Based and Minority-Focused Scholarships

### National Merit Scholarship Program
An academic competition for US high school students, using PSAT scores to identify Commended Students, Semifinalists, and Finalists, with scholarships offered to about 8,000 top Finalists. Additional awards are funded by colleges and corporations, with many universities offering special scholarships to National Merit Scholars. The former National Achievement Scholarship Program focused on Black American students. The process has been criticized for reliance on standardized testing. Notable alumni include Bill Gates, Jeff Bezos, Susan Rice, and Elena Kagan.

### UNCF and Thurgood Marshall College Fund
- **UNCF:** Since 1944, supports students at historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs), providing scholarships and institutional support (open to all, but primarily benefits African-American students). High-profile fundraising and donations have expanded its reach.
- **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF):** Founded in 1987 to support public HBCUs and PBIs, TMCF funds scholarships, programmatic support, and capacity building.

## British Council

Founded in 1934, the British Council is the UK’s international cultural and educational organization. It promotes English language and UK culture worldwide through teaching, exams (notably IELTS), education mobility programmes (Erasmus+), school and university partnerships, arts initiatives, and other exchange programs. The Council has faced controversies over closures in certain countries, outsourcing practices, and political sensitivities, but is recognized for its role in cultural diplomacy and excellence in global English teaching.

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Established in 1993, Bolashak funds Kazakh students to study at top international universities. Over 12,800 scholarships have been granted, producing graduates who often occupy key roles in Kazakhstan's public and private sectors. Applicants must meet rigorous criteria; most scholars are ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements.

## Anthologies and Honours

### Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Inspired by the influential anthologies *Daughters of Africa* (1992) and *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), edited by Margaret Busby, these works celebrate the literary output of hundreds of Black women from ancient to contemporary times. The New Daughters of Africa Award provides scholarships for African women studying at SOAS, University of London, funded through book proceeds and community support. The anthologies are widely lauded as essential references in African and feminist literature.
