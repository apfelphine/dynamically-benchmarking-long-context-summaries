# Global Scholarships and Educational Foundations

## King's Scholarships
The term "King's Scholar" has evolved across several institutions. Originally established by King Edward II in 1317 at the University of Cambridge, it later expanded to Eton College, where King's Scholars (Collegers) are the oldest students, admitted via competitive examination and distinguished by wearing a black gown. At Westminster School, the title reflects the reigning monarch, and at King's College Cambridge, it denotes undergraduates achieving first-class degrees. Historically tied to endowments by Henry VI, these scholarships now vary significantly; for instance, the seven King's Schools founded by Henry VIII in 1541 award scholarships for academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts. Notable alumni include prominent figures such as Boris Johnson, Aldous Huxley, and George Orwell.

## International Exchange Programs
International scholarship programs continue to foster global understanding. The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1945, promotes peace through educational exchange between the U.S. and other nations, though it faced significant disruption in 2025 due to political interference and a funding freeze. The **Marshall Scholarship** serves as a coeducational alternative to the Rhodes Scholarship, funded by the U.S. government to enable American students to study in the UK. It is highly selective, focusing on intellectual distinction and the potential to strengthen U.S.-UK relations. The **Rhodes Scholarship**, founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, aims to promote unity within the British Empire, though it has faced criticism regarding its founder's colonial legacy and its historical exclusion of women and Black Africans until recent decades.

## Domestic and Regional Scholarships
Domestic and regional initiatives also support educational advancement. The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students, while the **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida provides merit-based aid to high school graduates, though it faced criticism and legislative changes regarding SAT score requirements and racial impact. In Germany, the **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** offers scholarships based on academic excellence and social commitment, with a significant portion of funding derived from private donations. The **Bolashak Scholarship**, instituted by Kazakhstan's first president, sends students to study abroad to inculcate Western democratic values, with graduates often returning to hold key positions in state organizations.

## Specific Foundations and Anthologies
The **Shuping Scholarship**, founded in Shanghai in 1939 by businessman Koo Soh Bing, honors his father's motto of "returning to society what one gains." It supports poor students from academic families and includes extensive charitable activities like a medical fund and a library. In literature, the **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award** and the anthologies *Daughters of Africa* and *New Daughters of Africa* celebrate the work of women of African descent, serving as a vital archive of their contributions to global literature. The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund** focuses on supporting Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), providing scholarships and capacity building to ensure educational equity.
