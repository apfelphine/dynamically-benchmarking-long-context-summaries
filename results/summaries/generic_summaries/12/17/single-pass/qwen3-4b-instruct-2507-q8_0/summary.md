### **King's Scholar**  
Historically, King's Scholars originated in 1317 with King Edward II establishing 12 boys at Cambridge. In 1440, King Henry VI founded Eton College, creating King's Scholars as free-educational students. At Eton, scholars (known as "Collegers" or "tugs") are selected via competitive exams, live in the central College buildings, and wear black gowns. They participate in formal traditions, eat in College Hall, and play Wall Game until age 18. Scholarships are now means-tested, with post-nominals "KS" or "OS" awarded. Notable scholars include George Orwell, John Maynard Keynes, and Boris Johnson.  

At King’s College Cambridge, King’s Scholars are undergraduates with first-class degrees who receive £350 and attend a ceremony in the college chapel. At the seven King’s Schools (Canterbury, Ely, etc.), King’s Scholarships are awarded for academic or musical excellence, often with fee discounts. At King’s Ely, scholars participate in traditions like the "Hoop Trundle" and wear red gowns; they enjoy privileges like burial in Ely Cathedral.  

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**  
A professional exchange program established in 1992 by the U.S. Department of State, supporting Eurasian scholars and graduate students in the U.S. Administered by Cultural Vistas since 2015, it offers summer internships, career training, and financial support.  

### **Norway Scholarship**  
Founded in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, this scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Oxford. Initially funded by Jayne’s personal wealth, it evolved with support from alumni and later raised funds through a committee. The program has awarded students since 1920, with notable scholars including G. Astrup-Hoel and Just Faaland.  

### **Marshall Scholarship**  
Established in 1953 by George C. Marshall, the scholarship enables intellectually distinguished American students to study in the UK. It emphasizes mutual understanding and peace, with a strong focus on leadership, academic excellence, and cultural exchange. The program has expanded from 12 to 50 scholars annually, with a rigorous selection process involving academic achievement, leadership, and personal motivation. Notable scholars include Stephen Breyer, Nannerl Keohane, and Ray Dolby.  

### **Kennedy Scholarship**  
Created in 1964 in memory of John F. Kennedy, it supports British students to study at Harvard or MIT. It promotes international understanding and peace. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and public service. Notable scholars include Ed Balls, David Curry, and Nicholas Boles.  

### **Miss America**  
Founded in 1921, the Miss America Pageant began as a beauty contest to boost tourism in Atlantic City. It evolved into a platform for cultural exchange and advocacy, though it faced criticism for promoting sexism and racism. In 2018, it adopted "Miss America 2.0," removing physical appearance as a judging criterion. Notable figures include Bess Myerson, Vanessa Williams, and Nina Davuluri.  

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**  
Established in 1993, it provides academic merit-based scholarships to Florida high school graduates. It includes A-level and B-level awards, with vocational and Gold Seal programs. The program faced criticism for racial bias and funding cuts. In 2018, it restructured with new award levels and expanded eligibility.  

### **Fulbright Program**  
Established in 1946, the Fulbright Program fosters international educational exchange, promoting mutual understanding. It supports students, scholars, and teachers in over 160 countries. The program has faced controversies over political interference and criticism of its selection process. Notable scholars include John F. Kennedy, Margaret Thatcher, and Nelson Mandela.  

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**  
Founded in 1955, it identifies high-achieving U.S. high school students through the PSAT/NMSQT. Students are evaluated on academic performance, leadership, and extracurricular involvement. Awards range from $2,500 to $10,000, with over 8,000 awarded annually. The program has been criticized for over-reliance on standardized tests and lack of transparency.  

### **British Council**  
Founded in 1934, the British Council promotes English language and cultural exchange globally. It operates in over 100 countries, offering teaching, examinations (IELTS), and cultural programs. It has faced criticism over political interference, attacks, and outsourcing. Notable activities include the "Peacekeeping English" program and the "UK-India Year of Culture."  

### **Bolashak Scholarship**  
Established in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s President Nazarbayev, it supports young Kazakhs to study abroad and develop democratic values. It has awarded over 12,800 scholarships, with a focus on leadership and public service.  

### **Rhodes Scholarship**  
Founded in 1902, it supports postgraduate students from around the world to study at Oxford. It emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and service. It has been criticized for its colonial origins and gender exclusivity until 1977. Notable scholars include Nelson Mandela, Barack Obama, and Margaret Thatcher.  

### **UNCF**  
Founded in 1944, the United Negro College Fund supports African American students at HBCUs. It provides scholarships, promotes educational equity, and funds community programs. Notable alumni include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Dr. David Satcher.  

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**  
Established in 2019, this award supports African women students. It stems from the anthology *New Daughters of Africa*, which celebrates Black women writers. The award promotes representation and equity in education.  

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**  
Founded in 1987, it supports public HBCUs with scholarships and capacity-building. It has raised over $500 million and supports 55 institutions. Notable members include Howard University and Florida A&M University.  

### **Harkness Fellowship**  
Established in 1925, it enables British graduates to study in the U.S. with a focus on health policy. It has evolved into a prestigious health policy fellowship, with notable alumni including Sir Paul Nurse and Professor David Armitage.  

### **Studienstiftung**  
A German scholarship organization founded in 1925, it supports students in Germany and abroad. It offers financial aid, academic mentorship, and summer programs. Notable scholars include Nobel laureates and political leaders.  

### **Shuping Scholarship**  
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai, it supports poor, academically strong students. It includes tuition, medical care, and volunteer work. Over 6,000 students have been awarded, with a focus on service and community contribution.
