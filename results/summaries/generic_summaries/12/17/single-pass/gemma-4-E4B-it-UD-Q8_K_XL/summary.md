# Scholarships and Academic Programs

## Collegiate and Institutional Scholarships
A variety of specialized scholarships exist across several institutions. **King's Scholars** originated with King Edward II at Cambridge in 1317 and are also awarded by King Henry VI at Eton College, where a "Colleger" has passed the College Election examination. At Eton, these students reside in the College house and wear a black academic gown, with about 70 students admitted annually, though financial aid has become subject to means testing since the 1960s.

At **Westminster School**, foundation scholars are called Queen's or King's Scholars depending on the reigning monarch, and they board at the College house. **King's College Cambridge** uses the term "King's Scholar" for undergraduates achieving first-class degrees, which historically involved a £350 prize. Furthermore, specific schools—**King's Ely, Canterbury, and Worcester**—award scholarships in the king's name, often recognizing academic or musical attainment and granting traditional privileges.

## International Exchange Programs
Several globally recognized scholarship programs foster international understanding:

*   **Marshall Scholarship:** Established in 1952, this program aims to enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK, contributing to British-American understanding. Selection criteria prioritize intellect and character, and the program has seen expansions in funding and participant numbers.
*   **Fulbright Program:** Founded in 1945, this program promotes peace and mutual understanding through educational exchange between the U.S. and other nations. It operates through various programs, including the U.S. Student Program and the Fulbright Foreign Student Program.
*   **Rhodes Scholarship:** Established in 1902, this scholarship aims to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. It is highly selective and has faced historical criticisms regarding its founder's supremacist views.
*   **Kennedy Scholarship:** Established as a memorial to John F. Kennedy, this scholarship allows UK nationals to study at Harvard or MIT, with selection criteria focusing on intellectual attainment, expressiveness, and commitment to public service.
*   **Harkness Fellowship:** Now focused on health care policy, this fellowship allows international scholars to conduct research at American institutions, providing insight into the U.S. health care system.
*   **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes:** This German scholarship supports talented students pursuing studies in Germany or the EU, based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility."

## Specialized and Domestic Awards
Domestic and specialized awards also exist, including the **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida, which provides scholarships based on academic merit to eligible students, though its requirements have changed over time. In contrast, the **National Merit Scholarship Program** uses the PSAT/NMSQT as its initial screening mechanism for college-bound students. The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund** supports higher education at public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs).

## Literary and Social Anthologies
*   ***Daughters of Africa***: This anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, gathers writing by women of African descent globally, serving as a literary testament to the diverse and resilient voices of Black women. Its successor, *New Daughters of Africa*, expands this collection further.

# Notable Figures and Organizations
Edmund S. Muskie was a highly influential political figure in Maine, serving as a U.S. Senator (1959–1980) and Governor (1955–1959). His legislative legacy is marked by significant contributions to the environmental movement, including the passage of the Clean Air Act and the Clean Water Act, earning him the nickname "Mr. Clean." He also played a role in establishing the U.S. federal budget process.

The **British Council**, a royal charter charity, promotes British culture and English language knowledge globally across over 100 countries. However, it has faced controversies related to political interference, its handling of crises (such as in Afghanistan), and the concentration of its operations.

The **UNCF** focuses on supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), maintaining the motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

# Miss America Pageant
The **Miss America Pageant** began in Atlantic City in 1921, evolving from an event intended to drive business to a televised pageant. Historically, it was known for its physical presentation, but it has undergone significant cultural shifts, including the abolition of racial segregation rules in 1950. Major changes have involved attempts to evolve the event to be more inclusive, leading to the "Miss America 2.0" format, which eventually saw the reversal of some of those changes in the 2023-present era.
