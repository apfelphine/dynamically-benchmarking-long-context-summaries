### **Fulbright Program**
- **Mission**: Promotes peace, understanding, and global cooperation through educational exchange.
- **History**: Founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to foster international understanding post-WWII. Expanded by the Fulbright–Hays Act (1961) to include cultural, scientific, and language programs.
- **Structure**: Operates via bilateral agreements with over 160 countries, managed by U.S. Department of State and 50 bi-national commissions. Administered by the Institute of International Education (IIE) since 1996.
- **Programs**: Includes student, scholar, teacher, and professional exchanges across disciplines. Grants are awarded in nearly all academic fields, excluding clinical medical research.
- **Controversies**: 
  - In 2024, Russia declared IIE and Cultural Vistas "undesirable," ending the Fulbright program in the USSR.
  - In 2025, the Trump administration froze funding, impacting over 19,000 participants.
  - The Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board resigned en masse, citing political interference in 2025, including unauthorized ideological screening of applicants.
- **Legacy**: Over 325,000 alumni, including 89 Pulitzer Prize winners, 78 MacArthur Fellows, and 62 Nobel laureates.

### **British Council**
- **Mission**: Promotes British culture, language, and education globally.
- **History**: Established in 1934 to support English education and fight extremism. Expanded post-WWII, with key cultural programs in the 1940s. Closed in Russia in 2007 and designated as "undesirable" in 2025 due to alleged tax violations and support for LGBT rights.
- **Structure**: A public corporation under the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. Operates in over 100 countries with teacher training, exams (IELTS), and cultural programs.
- **Key Activities**: 
  - Runs IELTS exams and teaching programs.
  - Offers "Peacekeeping English" for military personnel.
  - Hosts global cultural events like "UK-India Year of Culture" and "fiveFilms4freedom."
- **Controversies**: 
  - Criticized for outsourcing teacher recruitment to India, raising concerns about political influence and human rights.
  - Accused of leaving behind Afghan teachers during the Taliban takeover in 2021.
  - Alleged involvement in political bias and cultural bias in programming.

### **Miss America Pageant**
- **History**: Originated in 1921 as a beauty pageant in Atlantic City. Evolved into a platform for social activism and public service.
- **Structure**: A national competition for young women aged 18–28, judged on appearance, talent, and leadership. 
- **Evolution**: 
  - In 2018, introduced "Miss America 2.0" format, eliminating swimsuit and appearance-based judging, emphasizing talent and personal development.
  - Reversed in 2023, returning to appearance-based competitions.
- **Notable Figures**: 
  - Vanessa Williams (1984) was the first Black woman to win, facing backlash and resignation due to nude photo scandal.
  - Nina Davuluri (2014) became the first Indian-American winner, facing xenophobic attacks.
- **Criticism**: 
  - Accused of promoting sexism and racism.
  - Facing scrutiny over financial practices and scholarship claims.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To foster international understanding through postgraduate education in the UK.
- **History**: Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes to promote unity among English-speaking nations. Opened to women in 1977.
- **Structure**: Selects scholars from over 20 countries based on academic excellence, leadership, and character. Scholarships are for two-year postgraduate studies.
- **Controversies**: 
  - Criticized for its founder's colonialist views and exclusion of Black Africans.
  - Aligned with Rhodes's legacy of white supremacy, prompting campaigns like "Rhodes must fall."
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates, civil rights leaders, and influential figures in science, law, and public service.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To strengthen U.S.-UK relations through postgraduate study in the UK.
- **History**: Created in 1953 in honor of George C. Marshall. Initially limited to single men under 25; expanded to include women and married applicants.
- **Structure**: Awards two-year postgraduate scholarships to American citizens. Selection emphasizes academic achievement, leadership, and potential for public service.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent figures in politics, law, and diplomacy (e.g., Stephen Breyer, John Kerry, and Margaret Thatcher).
- **Legacy**: Recognized as one of the most prestigious U.S.-UK academic programs.

---

### **Harkness Fellowship**
- **Mission**: To promote health policy and research through international exchange.
- **History**: Established in 1925 to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarship. Now focused exclusively on health care.
- **Structure**: Awards 12-month fellowships to professionals in health policy, research, and practice. Fellows conduct research at top U.S. institutions.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent figures in medicine, public health, and policy (e.g., David Armitage, Sir Harold Evans).

### **King’s Scholar**
- **Mission**: To identify and support high-achieving students through academic and leadership development.
- **History**: Originated in 1317 at Cambridge; expanded to Eton College in 1440. Awarded to students who excel academically and demonstrate leadership.
- **Structure**: At Eton, scholars live in the historic "College" and wear black gowns. At King’s College, Cambridge, scholars receive a prize upon earning a first-class degree.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent writers, scientists, and politicians (e.g., John Maynard Keynes, George Orwell, and Stephen Wolfram).

---

### **Shuping Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To support students from poor families in China through education and service.
- **History**: Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing to honor his father. Expanded post-1986 with government and alumni support.
- **Structure**: Offers full tuition and living support. Scholars engage in community service and volunteer work.
- **Legacy**: Over 6,000 scholars by 2000; many have become leaders in government, education, and civil society.

---

### **Norway Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To promote international academic exchange between Norway and Oxford.
- **History**: Established in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne. Awarded to Norwegian students for one year at Wadham College, Oxford.
- **Structure**: Supports study in the UK, with a focus on cultural and academic exchange.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes scholars in law, literature, and the arts.

---

### **Studienstiftung**
- **Mission**: To support excellence in German higher education through scholarships.
- **History**: Founded in 1925, re-established in 1948. Supports students in Germany and abroad.
- **Structure**: Offers scholarships for undergraduate and doctoral students based on academic merit and social responsibility.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates, economists, and leaders in science and public service.

---

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To honor John F. Kennedy’s legacy through academic excellence in the U.S.
- **History**: Established in 1964. Awards scholarships to study at Harvard or MIT.
- **Structure**: Selects students based on academic excellence, leadership, and commitment to public service.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent politicians, economists, and public servants (e.g., Ed Balls, David Miliband).

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
- **Mission**: To support public historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs).
- **History**: Founded in 1987, renamed in 2006. Raises funds for scholarships, capacity building, and programmatic support.
- **Structure**: Supports 55 HBCUs and PBIs; funds scholarships and capacity-building initiatives.
- **Impact**: Raised over $500 million to support HBCUs and student success.

---

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
- **Mission**: To provide academic scholarships to Florida high school graduates based on merit.
- **History**: Launched in 1993, modeled after Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship. Criticized for racial bias and financial inequities.
- **Structure**: Offers A-level (100% tuition), B-level (75% tuition), and vocational scholarships.
- **Controversies**: 
  - Criticized for favoring wealthier students due to high SAT score requirements.
  - Reforms in 2018 and 2025 expanded access and added new award categories.

---

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
- **Mission**: To support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and promote educational equity.
- **History**: Founded in 1944. Shifted from using "Negro" to "Black" in its name in 2008.
- **Structure**: Offers scholarships to students at member institutions; funds capacity building.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Dr. David Satcher.
- **Funding**: Supported by major donors including MacKenzie Scott ($70M in 2025).

---

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
- **Mission**: To identify and reward high-achieving U.S. high school students.
- **Structure**: 
  - PSAT/NMSQT screening → Semifinalists → Finalists → Merit Scholarship awards.
  - Awards range from $2,500 to $10,000.
- **Criticism**: 
  - Criticized for over-reliance on standardized test scores and lack of transparency.
  - NACAC and FairTest have raised concerns about fairness and bias.

---

### **Bolashak Scholarship**
- **Mission**: To develop future leaders in Kazakhstan through international education.
- **History**: Established in 1993 by President Nazarbayev. Over 12,800 scholarships awarded by 2018.
- **Structure**: Students study at 630 leading universities in 32 countries.
- **Ethnicity**: Predominantly Kazakh (93.6%), with smaller shares of Russian, Korean, and Tatar.
- **Impact**: Many graduates hold key positions in government and private sectors.
