### **Fulbright Program**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1945, aims to foster international understanding and peace through educational exchange. Founded by Senator J. William Fulbright, it initially supported international exchange programs post-World War II, emphasizing mutual understanding and cooperation. The program expanded in 1961 with the Fulbright-Hays Act, enabling exchanges in cultural, scientific, and educational fields.

#### **History and Evolution**
- **Origins (1945):** Created to promote peace and cultural exchange post-WWII, funded by surplus U.S. war property.
- **21st Century Challenges:** Disruptions in 2024 (Russian government designation of IIE and Cultural Vistas as "undesirable") and 2025 (Trump administration funding freeze affecting 19,000 participants).
- **Board Resignations (2025):** Eleven members resigned due to allegations of political interference, citing ideological screening of applicants.

#### **Program Structure**
The Fulbright Program offers multiple grants, including:
- **Student Grants:** Fulbright U.S. Student Program (research/study abroad), Fulbright Foreign Student Program (study in the U.S.), and English Teaching Assistant Programs.
- **Scholar Grants:** Fulbright Distinguished Chair Awards, Fulbright U.S. Scholar Program (faculty research abroad), Fulbright Specialist Program (expert consultations).
- **Teacher Grants:** Fulbright Teacher Exchange Program and Distinguished Fulbright Awards in Teaching.

**Eligibility:** High academic achievement, compelling project proposals, leadership potential, and adaptability.

#### **Administration**
Administered by the **Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs (ECA)** of the U.S. Department of State, with oversight by the **Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board**. Bilateral commissions in 49 countries manage local exchanges.

### **British Council**
The **British Council**, established in 1934, promotes British culture, education, and English language globally. Originally focused on English education abroad, it expanded post-WWII to support cultural exchanges and scientific cooperation.

#### **History and Controversies**
- **Origins (1930s–40s):** Created to counter fascism and communism, later expanded post-war.
- **Controversies:** Closures in Russia (2007–2025), attacks on its offices (e.g., Kabul, Tripoli), and accusations of political influence (e.g., ties to Indian diaspora).
- **Recent Challenges:** Suspension in Russia (2025) due to sanctions, and controversies over funding and transparency.

#### **Activities**
- **English and Examinations:** Offers IELTS, face-to-face English teaching, and MOOCs.
- **Mobility Programs:** Education UK (international student portal), Erasmus+ (EU-funded exchanges).
- **Arts and Culture:** Shakespeare Lives, fiveFilms4freedom (LGBTQ+ film festival), and cultural exchanges.

### **Miss America Pageant**
The **Miss America Pageant**, founded in 1921, is a beauty pageant with evolving formats. Originally a commercial event, it faced protests in the 1960s for sexism and racism, leading to reforms.

#### **History and Milestones**
- **Origins (1921):** Began as a summer event in Atlantic City, later becoming a national competition.
- **Key Changes:** Adoption of "Miss America 2.0" (2018) to eliminate swimsuit competition, reversed in 2023.
- **Notable Events:** First Black contestant (Cheryl Browne, 1971), first deaf winner (Heather Whitestone, 1995), and first transgender titleholder (Brían Nguyen, 2022).

#### **Controversies**
- **Protests (1968):** Feminist demonstrations against sexism, militarism, and racism.
- **Scandals:** Vanessa Williams’ resignation (1984) due to nude photos, and election eve speech (1970) criticized for divisive rhetoric.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
The **Rhodes Scholarship**, founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, is one of the most prestigious international scholarships. Originally limited to men, it opened to women in 1977.

#### **History and Controversies**
- **Origins (1902):** Established to promote unity among British Empire students and foster diplomatic ties.
- **Controversies:** Criticized for colonialist ties to Cecil Rhodes, exclusion of women and Black Africans, and allegations of hypocrisy in accepting scholarships while criticizing its founder.

#### **Selection and Criteria**
- **Eligibility:** Academic excellence, leadership potential, and commitment to public service.
- **Allocation:** Scholarships awarded to students from over 60 countries, with a focus on the U.S., UK, and Commonwealth nations.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
The **Marshall Scholarship**, established in 1953, supports U.S. students to study in the UK. Named after George Marshall, it aims to strengthen transatlantic relations.

#### **History and Evolution**
- **Origins (1953):** Created to aid post-war European recovery and promote cultural exchange.
- **Recent Changes:** Expanded funding in 2023 (50 scholarships/year), with increased emphasis on leadership and academic excellence.

#### **Selection and Criteria**
- **Eligibility:** High academic achievement, demonstrated leadership, and commitment to public service.
- **Allocation:** Scholarships awarded to students from the U.S., with a focus on STEM, humanities, and social sciences.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
The **New Daughters of Africa** anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, celebrates the literary contributions of African women. The award honors women writers of African descent.

#### **Influence and Legacy**
- **Anthology (1992):** A groundbreaking collection of poetry, fiction, and essays, featuring 200+ contributors.
- **Impact:** Inspired global literary movements, including the **Daughters of Africa** (1992) and **New Daughters of Africa** (2019), both of which highlight the rich literary traditions of African women.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
The **Muskie Internship Program**, established in 1992, supports international students and scholars in the U.S. government. Named after former U.S. Senator Edmund Muskie, it emphasizes professional development and cultural exchange.

#### **History and Administration**
- **Origins (1992):** Created to foster international cooperation and support graduate students.
- **Administration:** Administered by the U.S. Department of State, offering internships in various government sectors.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
The **Harkness Fellowship**, founded in 1925, supports health policy research in the U.S. Originally for British graduates, it expanded to include students from Australia, Canada, and other countries.

#### **Current Program**
- **Focus:** Health care policy and practice, with fellows studying at top U.S. institutions.
- **Administration:** Funded by the **Commonwealth Fund**, offering research grants and networking opportunities.

### **King’s Scholar**
The **King’s Scholar** program at King’s College Cambridge and Eton College recognizes academic excellence. At Eton, it includes King’s Scholars (collegers) and Queen’s Scholars (for female students).

#### **Notable Scholars**
- **Eton:** Includes figures like Boris Johnson, Aldous Huxley, and James Arbuthnot.
- **Cambridge:** Recognizes first-class degree holders with a prize and formal dinner.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
The **Shuping Scholarship**, founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, supports students from poor families in Shanghai. It emphasizes education, charity, and community service.

#### **History and Legacy**
- **Origins (1939):** Established to support academically gifted students during WWII.
- **Modern Expansion:** Continues to provide scholarships and mentorship, with a focus on moral and academic development.

---

### **Norway Scholarship**
The **Norway Scholarship**, established in 1919, enables Norwegian students to study in the UK. It aims to foster cultural and academic exchange.

#### **History and Alumni**
- **Origins (1919):** Created to promote understanding between Norway and the UK.
- **Notable Alumni:** Includes scholars from various fields, such as history, literature, and economics.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
The **Studienstiftung**, founded in 1925, is Germany’s premier merit-based scholarship program. It supports students from all backgrounds, emphasizing academic excellence and social responsibility.

#### **Admission and Scholarships**
- **Eligibility:** High academic achievement, leadership potential, and commitment to public service.
- **Scholarships:** Includes general scholarships for undergraduate and graduate students, as well as doctoral fellowships.

---

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
The **Kennedy Scholarship**, established in 1964, honors the legacy of President John F. Kennedy. It supports British students to study at Harvard or MIT.

#### **History and Selection**
- **Origins (1964):** Created to commemorate Kennedy’s contributions to education and diplomacy.
- **Selection:** Based on intellectual attainment, leadership potential, and readiness for public service.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**, founded in 1987, supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It provides scholarships, capacity-building, and financial aid.

#### **History and Mission**
- **Origins (1987):** Established to address funding inequities in HBCUs.
- **Mission:** Advocates for higher education access and success for Black students.

---

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship**, established in Florida in 1997, provides merit-based scholarships to high school graduates. It has faced controversies over racial bias and funding cuts.

#### **History and Controversies**
- **Origins (1997):** Designed to emulate Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship.
- **Controversies:** Criticized for racial disparities and funding reductions in 2011.

---

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
The **UNCF**, founded in 1912, supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships, research, and advocacy.

#### **History and Impact**
- **Origins (1912):** Created to address educational inequities for Black Americans.
- **Fundraising:** Includes major donations from figures like John F. Kennedy and Lou Rawls.

---
### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program**, established in 1955, recognizes high-achieving high school students. It offers scholarships based on PSAT/NMSQT scores and academic excellence.

#### **History and Controversies**
- **Origins (1955):** Created to encourage academic achievement among high school students.
- **Criticisms:** Accusations of bias and lack of transparency in selection processes.

---
### **Bolashak Scholarship**
The **Bolashak Scholarship**, established in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s President Nursultan Nazarbayev, supports students to study abroad. It aims to promote Western democratic values.

#### **History and Ethnic Distribution**
- **Origins (1993):** Created to foster global education and cultural exchange.
- **Ethnic Composition:** Primarily supports Kazakh students, with a focus on language proficiency.

---
### **Key Themes Across Programs**
- **Education and Cultural Exchange:** All programs emphasize fostering international understanding and cooperation.
- **Merit-Based Selection:** Most scholarships prioritize academic excellence, leadership, and public service.
- **Controversies and Reforms:** Many programs have faced criticism over racial bias, political interference, and funding issues, leading to reforms and expansions.
