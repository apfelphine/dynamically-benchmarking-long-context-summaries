# Global Educational and Cultural Initiatives

Numerous international and national programs support higher education and cultural exchange. Prestigious global fellowships include the Fulbright Program (spanning 49 nations), the Rhodes Scholarship, and the Marshall Scholarship, while specific focus areas are addressed by the Harkness and Kennedy Scholarships. Nationally, merit-based supports include the National Merit Scholarship Program and Florida Bright Futures, with specialized funding provided by the Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF) and UNCF for Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs).

Globally, academic study destinations are diverse, featuring top institutions in the US (e.g., Harvard, MIT), the UK (Oxford, Cambridge), Australia, Canada, and numerous countries in Europe and Asia. Other international funding examples include the Kazakh Bolashak Scholarship.

# Literary, Historical, and Cultural Notes

In literature, the *Daughters of Africa* anthologies, curated by Margaret Busby, document the Black feminist literary canon through works by over 200 global Black women writers. Historically, Edmund S. Muskie was a prominent public servant, serving as Maine's first Catholic Governor, a U.S. Senator, and as U.S. Secretary of State, while also being a staunch environmental advocate involved in passing the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts.

# Pageantry and Social Change

The Miss America pageant, established in the 1920s, reflects evolving social dynamics, having historically faced critiques regarding racism and sexism while achieving milestones like ending racial segregation in 1950. The organization recently underwent structural changes, including modifications to the swimsuit competition between 2017 and 2022, and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2024 amid financial and security challenges.
