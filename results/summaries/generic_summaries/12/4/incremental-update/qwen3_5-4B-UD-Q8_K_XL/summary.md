The **National Merit Scholarship Program** and the **National Achievement Scholarship Program** represent key pillars of U.S. financial aid, though they differ in scope and historical focus. The Merit Program awards full-time undergraduate funding to students who enroll in accredited institutions, while the National Achievement Program, established in 1964, specifically supported Black American youth until 2016, after which its funds were redirected to the United Negro College Fund (UNCF). Both programs have produced notable honorees such as Bill Gates, Ben Bernanke, and Mae Jemison, with top universities like the University of Florida and USC enrolling hundreds of scholars annually.

Despite their impact, both programs face significant criticism regarding their reliance on a single standardized test (PSAT/NMSQT) for selection and concerns over the lack of transparency in state-level score requirements. Organizations such as NACAC and FairTest have highlighted these issues, calling for more equitable and transparent evaluation methods.
