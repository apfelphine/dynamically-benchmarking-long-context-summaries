### **British Council**

#### **History**
The British Council was established in the 1930s as the **British Committee for Relations with Other Countries**, focusing on promoting English education, British culture, and countering fascism/communism. Officially renamed the **British Council** in 1936, it opened its first offices in 1938 and received a Royal Charter in 1940 to foster cultural and educational ties. During WWII, it supported cultural initiatives, including music recordings and theater tours. Post-war, it expanded its global reach, offering scholarships, language programs, and cultural exchanges.

Key milestones include:
- **1940s–1950s**: Promoted British culture through exhibitions, theater, and music.
- **1960s–1970s**: Expanded educational programs, including English language teaching and scholarships.
- **2000s–Present**: Faced controversies (e.g., closures in Russia, accusations of bias) while maintaining its role in promoting UK values and international cooperation.

#### **Organization**
The British Council is a **public corporation** under the Foreign, Commonwealth, and Development Office, governed by a royal charter. Its headquarters are in London, with operations in over **100 countries**. Its income in 2014–2015 was £973 million, primarily from grants, teaching services, and examinations. Key activities include:
- **English Language & Examinations**: Offers face-to-face teaching in 80+ centers and administers standardized tests like **IELTS** (jointly with Cambridge and IDP).
- **Cultural & Educational Exchange**: Supports programs like **Peacekeeping English** (training military personnel) and **MOOCs** (e.g., *Exploring English*).
- **Arts & Culture**: Initiatives like **Shakespeare Lives**, **fiveFilms4freedom** (LGBTQ+ film festival), and **Selector Radio** (global music platform).
- **Scholarships & Mobility**: Programs like **Education UK** (global student portal) and **Erasmus+** (EU mobility).

#### **Notable Activities**
- **English & Examinations**: Over **3 million candidates** took UK exams in 2014–2015, with **IELTS** being a cornerstone.
- **Cultural Exchange**: Collaborations with institutions like the **Palace of Westminster** (e.g., peacock projection) and **BBC**.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for outsourcing roles to India, closures in Russia (2007, 2018), and allegations of bias (e.g., Indian diaspora influence).

### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History**
Founded in 1902 under **Cecil Rhodes’** will, the scholarship aimed to foster unity among British Empire members and strengthen ties with the U.S. Originally open only to men, it became coeducational in 1977. The scholarship is administered by the **Rhodes Trust** and expanded globally in 2018 to include students from anywhere.

Key milestones:
- **1900s**: Opened to women (1977) and later to non-white scholars (1991).
- **2000s–Present**: Globalized to 64 countries, with **Global Rhodes Scholarships** for international students.

#### **Selection & Criteria**
- **Intellectual attainment, leadership potential, and moral character** (e.g., truth, courage, devotion to duty).
- **Academic excellence** (e.g., high GPAs, research potential).
- **Selectivity**: ~0.7% acceptance rate globally, with top institutions like **Harvard, Oxford, and Cambridge** being preferred.

#### **Notable Scholars**
- **Politics**: Bill Clinton, Tony Abbott, and Wasim Sajjad (Pakistan President).
- **Science**: Howard Florey (penicillin discovery), Brian Greene (string theory).
- **Controversies**: Criticized for **colonialist legacies** (e.g., Cecil Rhodes’ racist views) and **exclusion of women/Black Africans** until the 1970s.

### **Fulbright Program**

#### **History**
Established in 1945 by **Senator J. William Fulbright**, the program promotes mutual understanding between the U.S. and other nations through educational exchanges. It was expanded in 1961 under the **Fulbright-Hays Act** and remains one of the largest international education exchange programs.

Key milestones:
- **1940s–1950s**: Focused on post-WWII recovery and cultural diplomacy.
- **2000s–Present**: Expanded to **49 countries**, with programs like **Fulbright–Hays** (language training) and **J. William Fulbright Prize** (international understanding).

#### **Program Structure**
- **Student Grants**: Fulbright U.S. Student Program (study/teach abroad) and Fulbright Foreign Student Program (study in the U.S.).
- **Scholar Grants**: Fulbright U.S. Scholar Program (research/teaching abroad) and Fulbright Specialist Program (consultancy).
- **Teacher Grants**: Supports K–12 and post-secondary educators.

#### **Notable Alumni**
- **Politics**: Hillary Clinton, Barack Obama, and Rishi Sunak.
- **Science**: Kofi Annan (Nobel Peace Prize), Richard Feynman.
- **Controversies**: Political interference (e.g., 2025 board resignations) and funding freezes (e.g., 2024–2025).

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
#### **History**

Key milestones:
- **2019**: *New Daughters of Africa* expanded the anthology with 200+ new contributors.

#### **Contributors & Impact**
- **Notable Authors**: Toni Morrison, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, Zadie Smith, and **Sylvia Wynter**.
- **Influence**: Recognized for breaking barriers in **African diaspora literature**, with editions published in **UK, US, and South Africa**.

### **Miss America**
#### **History**
Inaugurated in 1919, the pageant evolved from a **beauty contest** to a **cultural and political symbol**. Key changes:
- **1970**: Cheryl Browne became the first **Black contestant**.
- **1984**: Vanessa Williams won as the first **Black Miss America**, but faced backlash.
- **2018**: Adopted **"Miss America 2.0"** (non-physical appearance judging), later reversed.

#### **Controversies**
- **Racism**: Protests in the 1960s (e.g., "No More Miss America!").
- **Political Criticism**: Allegations of **militarism** (e.g., tours in Vietnam) and **body-shaming**.
- **Recent Changes**: Reversals of 2018 reforms (e.g., swimsuit competition reinstated).

### **Marshall Scholarship**
#### **History**
Established in 1953 as a **living memorial to George Marshall**, it funds **postgraduate studies in the UK** for U.S. students. Originally open only to men, it became coeducational in 1977.

Key milestones:
- **1960s–1970s**: Expanded to **44 scholars/year**.
- **2023**: Increased to **50 scholars/year** (largest cohort in history).

#### **Selection & Criteria**
- **Academic excellence** (e.g., high GPAs, research potential).
- **Leadership potential** and **commitment to public service**.
- **Selectivity**: ~3.6% acceptance rate (similar to Rhodes).

#### **Notable Recipients**
- **Politics**: Bill Clinton, George H.W. Bush.
- **Science**: Stephen Hawking (posthumous), **Roger Penrose** (Nobel Prize).
- **Controversies**: Criticized for **selectivity** and **limited diversity**.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
#### **History**
Launched in **1997** in Florida, this scholarship supports **academic merit** in higher education. It was **overhauled in 2018** to include **four levels** (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC).

- **FMS (Florida Medallion Scholar)**: 75% tuition coverage.
- **GSV (Gold Seal Vocational)**: Vocational training programs.
- **GSC (Gold Seal CAPE)**: Career-focused certifications.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
#### **History**
Established in **1955**, this program recognizes **top PSAT/NMSQT scorers** for merit-based scholarships. It has awarded **over 8,000 scholarships annually**.

Key milestones:
- **2016**: Transitioned to **new SAT/PSAT scoring system**.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for **selectivity** and **lack of transparency**.

#### **Selection Process**
1. **PSAT/NMSQT**: Top 50,000 scorers advance to **Commended**.
2. **Semifinalist**: ~16,000 advance based on **state quotas**.
3. **Finalist**: ~15,000 submit **SAT scores, transcripts, and essays**.
4. **Scholarships**: ~8,000 awarded (including **$2,500 Merit Scholarships**).
