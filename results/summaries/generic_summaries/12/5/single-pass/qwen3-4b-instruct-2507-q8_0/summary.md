### **Rhodes Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes' will to promote unity in the British Empire and strengthen ties between Britain and the U.S. Initially open only to men, it expanded to include women in 1977. The scholarship is administered by the Rhodes Trust and awarded to postgraduate students worldwide.
- **Selection & Selectivity**: Highly selective, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%. Criteria include academic excellence, leadership, moral character, and a commitment to public service. The U.S. program is among the most competitive, with a 1.4% award rate.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for its colonialist roots, including Rhodes’ white supremacist views. Scholars have challenged the legacy, with campaigns like "Rhodes must fall" and calls for a critical reevaluation of its history. The scholarship has also faced criticism for a lack of diversity among recipients, especially in public service.
- **Notable Alumni**: Many have become leaders in government, law, and human rights. Notable figures include Bill Clinton, Tony Abbott, and activists like Bram Fischer and Zambian activist Lucy Banda.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
- **Purpose**: A literary award to celebrate and promote the work of women of African descent. It aims to highlight their contributions and challenge historical erasure.
- **Impact**: The anthology *Daughters of Africa* (1992) was widely praised for its comprehensive collection of writings. Its sequel, *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), expanded the scope to include contemporary voices and is a key resource in African feminist literature.
- **Legacy**: The award supports African women in education and promotes their voices through scholarships and funding. It has become a significant platform for recognizing and amplifying African women’s literary achievements.

### **British Council**
- **History & Mission**: Founded in 1934 to promote British culture and education abroad, it operates in over 100 countries. It promotes cultural exchange, language learning, and educational cooperation.
- **Activities**: Offers English language exams (IELTS), teacher training, arts programs, and cultural exchanges. It runs initiatives like "Peacekeeping English" and the "UK-India Year of Culture."
- **Controversies**: Criticized for outsourcing key functions to India, raising concerns about political influence and human rights. It has also faced attacks, including a 2011 bombing in Kabul and closures in Russia due to political tensions.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1925 to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarship, it enables British graduates to study in the U.S. The program is now focused on health care policy and practice.
- **Structure**: Offers fellowships for health professionals and researchers. Fellows conduct research and gain experience in U.S. health systems.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes prominent figures such as Professor David Armitage, Professor Eric Ashby, and Professor Sir Jonathan Bate.

### **Fulbright Program**
- **History & Mission**: Established in 1946 to promote mutual understanding through educational exchange. It operates in over 160 countries and is a cornerstone of U.S. international education policy.
- **Structure**: Offers scholarships for students, scholars, and teachers to study or conduct research in the U.S. or abroad. The program emphasizes academic excellence and cross-cultural understanding.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for political interference, including allegations of bias in 2025. The program has also faced scrutiny over its scholarship distribution and transparency.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing to support poor students from financially disadvantaged families. The scholarship emphasizes service to society and personal growth.
- **Structure**: Offers full tuition and support for students to complete their education. Recipients are encouraged to engage in community service and charitable work.
- **Legacy**: The scholarship has produced many influential leaders in education, public service, and social development, with over 6,000 scholars by 2000.

### **Miss America**
- **History & Evolution**: Originated in 1920 as a beauty pageant, it evolved into a platform for advocacy and social change. The 2018 "Miss America 2.0" reform eliminated the swimsuit competition and emphasized personal development and leadership.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for promoting beauty standards and gender norms. The pageant has faced protests over its portrayal of women and its treatment of African American and LGBTQ+ contestants.
- **Impact**: Has played a role in advancing social issues, including civil rights and gender equality.

### **Studienstiftung**
- **History & Purpose**: Founded in 1925 as a German student support organization, it now provides scholarships for undergraduate and graduate students in Germany and abroad.
- **Structure**: Offers financial and academic support, including summer academies and research opportunities. It supports students in STEM, social sciences, and the arts.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates like Manfred Eigen and Reinhard Genzel, as well as prominent figures in law, politics, and business.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
- **History & Purpose**: Established in 1953 to honor George C. Marshall and promote U.S.-UK relations. It supports American students to study in the UK.
- **Structure**: Offers two-year scholarships for postgraduate study. Recipients are selected based on academic excellence and leadership potential.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for its limited scope and selectivity. However, it has produced influential leaders in government, academia, and public service.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
- **Purpose**: Supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and promotes access to higher education for underrepresented students.
- **Structure**: Provides scholarships, capacity-building support, and funding for member institutions. It has raised over $500 million since its founding.
- **Impact**: Has helped increase access to higher education for African American students and strengthened HBCUs.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
- **Purpose**: A U.S. Department of State program that provides internships and training to Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the U.S.
- **Structure**: Offers summer internships, career training, and financial support. The program aims to foster international understanding and professional development.
- **Legacy**: Has helped build bridges between the U.S. and Eurasian countries through professional exchange.

### **UNCF**
- **Purpose**: Founded in 1944 to support African American students through scholarships and educational opportunities. It now serves a broader demographic.
- **Structure**: Offers scholarships to students at HBCUs and other institutions. The program has received significant donations from individuals and corporations.
- **Impact**: Has produced many prominent leaders in business, politics, and the arts, including Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Dr. David Satcher.

### **King's Scholar**
- **History & Purpose**: A prestigious scholarship program at Eton College and other institutions in the UK, awarded to students who demonstrate academic excellence and leadership.
- **Structure**: Students are selected through competitive exams and awarded a black gown and privileges. The program emphasizes academic achievement and service to the community.
- **Legacy**: Has produced many notable figures in politics, science, and the arts, including Harold Macmillan, John Maynard Keynes, and Boris Johnson.

### **Bolashak**
- **Purpose**: A Kazakh scholarship program established in 1993 to promote Western democratic values and support youth development.
- **Structure**: Offers students the opportunity to study in 32 countries at leading universities. The program emphasizes leadership and cross-cultural understanding.
- **Impact**: Has produced many prominent leaders in Kazakhstan’s government and private sector.

### **Norway Scholarship**
- **Purpose**: A scholarship program enabling Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. It promotes cultural exchange between Norway and the UK.
- **History**: Founded in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne. The program has been restructured over time to meet rising costs and maintain its mission.
- **Legacy**: Has supported Norwegian students in academic and professional development, with notable alumni in law, politics, and the arts.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
- **Purpose**: A U.S. program that identifies high-achieving high school students and offers scholarships for college.
- **Structure**: Students take the PSAT/NMSQT, with top scorers advancing through semifinalist and finalist stages. Awards are given based on academic performance, extracurriculars, and recommendations.
- **Criticism**: Criticized for relying solely on standardized test scores and for potential racial and socioeconomic bias in selection.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
- **Purpose**: A U.S.-UK scholarship established in 1964 to honor President John F. Kennedy. It enables British students to study at Harvard or MIT.
- **Structure**: Highly selective, with a focus on leadership, public service, and intellectual achievement. Recipients are evaluated on academic merit and potential for public impact.
- **Impact**: Has produced numerous leaders in government, business, and academia, including Ed Balls and David Miliband.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
- **Purpose**: A Florida-based scholarship program to support high-achieving students in post-secondary education.
- **Structure**: Offers four levels based on academic performance and volunteer service. The program has undergone significant changes in recent years, including increased SAT score requirements and reduced funding.
- **Controversies**: Criticized for racial bias and inequities in access, particularly for Black and Latino students. Despite reforms, it continues to face scrutiny over fairness and effectiveness.
