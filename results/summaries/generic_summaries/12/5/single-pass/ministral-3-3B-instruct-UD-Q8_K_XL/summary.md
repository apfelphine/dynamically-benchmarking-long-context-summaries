### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History and Foundations**
The Rhodes Scholarship, established in 1902 under the terms of Cecil Rhodes’ will, aims to foster international unity and diplomatic ties. Originally limited to single male students under 25, it opened to women in 1977 after the Sex Discrimination Act of 1975. The scholarship is administered by the Rhodes Trust, with modifications by Parliament over the years. Controversies persist regarding Rhodes’ colonialist views, though the scholarship itself excludes race and religious discrimination.

#### **Selection and Terms**
Selection criteria emphasize literary and scholastic attainment, leadership, and moral character. Scholarships are awarded annually to students from over 64 countries, with eligibility based on academic excellence and potential. Scholarships cover tuition, living expenses, and maintenance, typically for two years, though extensions are possible. The scholarship is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of approximately 0.7%.

#### **Controversies and Legacy**
Criticism surrounds Rhodes’ legacy, including his white supremacist views and the scholarship’s historical exclusion of women and Black Africans. Recent efforts include renaming initiatives and addressing systemic inequities. Notable alumni include leaders in medicine, politics, and human rights.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
#### **Literary Anthology**
The *Daughters of Africa* anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, celebrates the literary contributions of African women across the diaspora. First published in 1992, it includes works by over 200 writers, spanning fiction, poetry, and essays. The anthology has been widely praised for its historical and literary significance, influencing generations of writers.

#### **Influence and Legacy**
The anthology inspired similar projects, such as *Töchter Afrikas* (German edition) and *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), which expanded its scope. It has been featured in major literary circles, including the *Guardian*, *The Washington Post*, and *Elle Magazine*. The award, established in partnership with the UNCF, supports African women students, highlighting the ongoing legacy of Busby’s work.

### **British Council**
#### **History and Mission**
The British Council, founded in 1934, promotes British culture, education, and diplomacy globally. It supports English language learning, cultural exchanges, and academic collaborations. Notable initiatives include the IELTS test, MOOCs, and programs like *Connecting Classrooms* for international school partnerships.

#### **Controversies and Challenges**
Criticisms include allegations of political influence, particularly in India, and controversies over teacher strikes and educational policies. The British Council has faced scrutiny for its handling of conflicts, such as closures in Russia and its role in cultural exchanges.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
#### **History and Purpose**
The Harkness Fellowship, established in 1925, supports health care policy research in the U.S. It is funded by the Commonwealth Fund and offers fellowships to scholars from Australia, Canada, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Switzerland, and the UK. Fellows spend a year conducting research and attending seminars.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable alumni include scientists, politicians, and academics like Stephen Breyer (U.S. Supreme Court Justice), Sir Roy Calne (British surgeon), and Nobel laureates. The fellowship emphasizes interdisciplinary collaboration and policy impact.

### **Fulbright Program**
#### **History and Global Reach**
The Fulbright Program, established in 1946, is the largest international educational exchange program, offering grants for students, scholars, and professionals. It operates under the U.S. Department of State and supports bilateral partnerships with over 160 countries. Recent controversies include political interference and funding freezes.

#### **Selection and Impact**
Selection is based on academic achievement, leadership, and cultural exchange potential. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, Pulitzer winners, and government leaders. The program supports research, teaching, and cultural diplomacy.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
#### **History and Philanthropy**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, the Shuping Scholarship supports underprivileged students in China. It provides financial aid, mentorship, and extracurricular activities. The scholarship has expanded to 37 secondary schools and 24 tertiary institutions, with ongoing support from the Shuping Scholarship Foundation.

#### **Legacy and Impact**
The program emphasizes service and community engagement, with many alumni contributing to China’s social and economic development. Koo’s legacy continues through the foundation’s efforts to sustain the scholarship.

### **Miss America Pageant**
#### **History and Evolution**
The Miss America pageant began in 1921, evolving from a beauty contest to a cultural and political symbol. It faced protests in the 1960s for its sexist and racist practices but has since embraced inclusivity. Recent changes, such as the "Miss America 2.0" initiative, aim to modernize the competition.

#### **Controversies and Legacy**
Criticisms include allegations of elitism, lack of diversity, and controversies over scholarship funding. Despite these issues, the pageant remains a significant cultural event, with notable alumni influencing politics, entertainment, and activism.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
#### **History and Scholarship**
The Studienstiftung, founded in 1925, is Germany’s premier merit-based scholarship program. It supports students from all ethnicities and backgrounds, offering financial and academic support. The program is highly competitive, with over 2,000 scholarships awarded annually.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Alumni include Nobel laureates, politicians, and leaders in academia, business, and the arts. The scholarship emphasizes excellence, initiative, and social responsibility.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
#### **History and Diplomacy**
The Marshall Scholarship, established in 1953, honors the contributions of George Marshall and supports U.S. students studying in the UK. It emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and cultural exchange. The scholarship is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of approximately 3.5%.

#### **Selection and Impact**
Selection is based on academic achievement, potential, and commitment to public service. Notable alumni include U.S. senators, diplomats, and academics. The program strengthens transatlantic relations and fosters global understanding.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
#### **History and Mission**
TMCF, established in 1987, supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships, capacity-building, and programmatic support. It aims to enhance educational opportunities for students from underrepresented communities.

#### **Member Schools and Impact**
TMCF supports 55 member schools, including HBCUs and predominantly Black institutions. It has raised over $500 million for scholarships and institutional development, contributing to the success of its students.

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
#### **History and Scholarships**
UNCF, founded in 1944, is a nonprofit organization supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It provides scholarships, grants, and advocacy for students from underrepresented communities. The organization has raised over $700 million for its programs.

#### **Legacy and Impact**
UNCF’s scholarships and initiatives have empowered thousands of students, many of whom go on to achieve success in business, politics, and the arts. The organization continues to advocate for equitable access to higher education.

### **King’s Scholar**
#### **History and Tradition**
The King’s Scholar program at Eton College and King’s College Cambridge offers academic scholarships to outstanding students. At Eton, King’s Scholars are selected by competitive examination and live in the College, distinguished by academic gowns. The program emphasizes tradition, excellence, and community.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable alumni include politicians, scientists, and leaders such as Boris Johnson, Winston Churchill, and Nelson Mandela. The program continues to uphold its legacy of fostering future leaders.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
#### **History and Selection**
The National Merit Scholarship Program, established in 1955, recognizes high-achieving high school students. It uses the PSAT/NMSQT to screen candidates, with about 50,000 students advancing to finalist status. The program offers scholarships and recognition to top performers.

#### **Criticisms and Impact**
Criticisms include concerns about transparency and fairness in the selection process. Despite these issues, the program remains a significant recognition of academic excellence, with many alumni achieving success in higher education and beyond.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
#### **History and Evolution**
The Bright Futures Scholarship Program, established in 1997, provides merit-based scholarships to Florida high school graduates. It was initially based on academic merit and has undergone significant changes, including adjustments to SAT scores and scholarship levels. The program aims to support students in achieving higher education.

#### **Scholarship Levels and Impact**
The program offers four levels of scholarships, including Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), and vocational scholarships. It has faced criticism for its impact on students from lower-income backgrounds but remains a significant resource for Florida students.
