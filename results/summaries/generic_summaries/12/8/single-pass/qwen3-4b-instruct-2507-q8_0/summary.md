### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai during the Japanese invasion, the Shuping Scholarship aimed to support academically excellent students from poor families. It offered full tuition for up to graduation, with continued support if academic records remained strong. The program included a library with science labs, extracurriculars (e.g., typing, nursing), free medical care, and vacation trips. Over 1,100 students were awarded between 1939 and 1949. The scholarship evolved, resuming in 1986 with support from the government and past scholars. Today, it awards over 700 scholarships annually across 37 secondary and 24 tertiary institutions, with a focus on service to community and moral education. A foundation established in 1995 ensures long-term funding.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
Established in 1925, the Studienstiftung supports German students through a general scholarship combining financial aid and academic support. Students must meet BAFöG criteria and study full-time. Scholarships are awarded via nomination by schools, professors, or competitions. Additional support includes summer academies, language courses, and mentoring. As of 2023, 2,179 scholarships and 301 doctoral scholarships were awarded. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates in chemistry and physics, and prominent figures in social sciences and business.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
Established in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowship enables British graduates to study in the U.S. Initially focused on civil service, it expanded to include business, arts, and politics. Since 1961, the fellowship has been limited to health care policy. Fellows spend 12 months conducting research at U.S. institutions, attending seminars, and engaging with policymakers. The program is administered by the Commonwealth Fund and supported by international partners.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
Founded in 1987, TMCF supports public historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It provides scholarships, capacity-building, and programmatic support. With over $500 million raised, it serves 55 HBCUs. The organization is a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt entity, and its mission emphasizes equity in education. Acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013, expanding its focus on entrepreneurship and talent development.

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
Founded in 1944, UNCF promotes education for African Americans. It administers scholarships to students at HBCUs and provides financial support. The organization has raised over $113 million. Notable alumni include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Dr. David Satcher, and Spike Lee. In 2025, MacKenzie Scott donated $70 million to support HBCUs. The UNCF also hosts fundraising events like the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars."

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
Established in 1902, the Rhodes Scholarship enables postgraduate students from over 20 countries to study at Oxford. It emphasizes leadership, intellectual excellence, and service. The program is administered by the Rhodes Trust and has a global acceptance rate of 0.7%. Women were admitted in 1977. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, U.S. Presidents, and heads of state. The scholarship has faced criticism over racial exclusion and the perceived lack of public service among recipients.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
Created in 1993, the Bright Futures Scholarship in Florida supports high-achieving students based on academic merit. It offers four levels: Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Gold Seal Vocational, and Gold Seal CAPE. The program has undergone reforms, including changes to SAT/ACT score requirements and increased funding. It has faced criticism for racial bias and financial disparities.

### **Miss America**
Originating in 1920, the Miss America pageant began as a beauty contest to promote tourism in Atlantic City. Over time, it evolved into a platform for advocacy, with notable figures like Bess Myerson and Vanessa Williams. In 2018, the pageant adopted a "Miss America 2.0" format, eliminating the swimsuit competition and focusing on personal stories and leadership. The pageant has faced criticism for sexism, racism, and controversies over scholarships and media representation.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
Established in 1964, the Kennedy Scholarship enables U.S. students to study at Oxford or MIT. It emphasizes leadership, public service, and international understanding. Selection is competitive and based on academic excellence and potential. Notable alumni include U.S. politicians, diplomats, and scholars such as Stephen Breyer and Thomas Friedman.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
Founded in 1953, the Marshall Scholarship supports U.S. students to study in the UK. It promotes mutual understanding between the U.S. and UK through academic excellence and leadership. The program has a rigorous selection process, with a 3.4% acceptance rate. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, U.S. government officials, and leaders in academia and policy.

### **Norway Scholarship**
Established in 1920, the Norway Scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Funded by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, it has supported students in diverse fields. The scholarship has faced funding challenges and periods of inactivity, but remains active with a focus on international academic exchange.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
Administered by the U.S. Department of State, this program supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students through summer internships and career training. Named after Senator Edmund Muskie, it promotes international understanding and professional development. The program was established in 1992 and has been administered by Cultural Vistas since 2015.

### **King's Scholar**
The term "King's Scholar" refers to several educational programs in the UK. At Eton College, it denotes students selected through competitive exams and awarded a Foundation Scholarship. At Westminster School, it is awarded based on academic excellence. At King's College Cambridge, it honors undergraduates with first-class degrees. The King's Schools (Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, Worcester) award scholarships for academic or musical achievement, with privileges such as cathedral services and burial rights.
