## Shuping Scholarship

Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by industrialist and philanthropist Koo in memory of his father, the Shuping Scholarship supports academically outstanding students from impoverished backgrounds. Initially awarding 300 scholarships per year, the program also provided a self-managed library, extracurricular activities, medical care, and charitable engagements. Over its first decade, 1,100 scholars benefited, many later contributing significantly to China's modernization. Revived in 1986 after reforms in China, it expanded to nearly 6,000 recipients by 2000, with ongoing support through the Shu Ping Scholarship Foundation, funded by Koo’s endowments and managed by alumni associations. The program continues to foster both financial support and community service among scholars.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes

The Studienstiftung, Germany’s largest scholarship foundation for exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates, was established in 1925 and reconstituted in 1948. Admission is highly competitive, with scholars selected via nominations, competitions, or assessment tests. The scholarship offers financial support (up to €855 for students and €1,550 for PhDs, tax free) and robust academic programming, including mentoring and summer academies. Internal and open scholarship programs facilitate international research and discipline-specific opportunities. In 2023, the Studienstiftung supported 14,888 scholars, who have distinguished themselves across science, politics, business, arts, and journalism.

## Harkness Fellowships

Established in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowships initially supported graduates from the UK and Dominions to study in the US and expanded over time to include professionals from various fields. Since 1997, the program focuses strictly on health care policy, offering a one-year research placement at major American institutions for fellows from several countries. It is recognized as a prestigious award in health policy and includes seminars, government briefings, and international symposia. The program is funded primarily by the Commonwealth Fund with contributions from international partners. Notable alumni include influential scholars, policymakers, scientists, and cultural figures.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)

Founded in 1987, TMCF advances opportunities for students at public HBCUs (Historically Black Colleges and Universities) and PBIs (Predominantly Black Institutions), supporting 55 member schools through advocacy, scholarships, and institutional grants exceeding $500 million. Major donors include Apple, Koch Foundation, and Boeing. TMCF also provides leadership development and entrepreneurship programs and absorbed the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to broaden its reach in talent identification and support.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

Margaret Busby's landmark anthologies, "Daughters of Africa" and "New Daughters of Africa," compile writings from over 400 women of African descent, receiving universal acclaim for their literary breadth and historical significance. The "New Daughters of Africa" anthology inspired a scholarship for African women at SOAS, University of London, funded collaboratively by contributors and publishers. The award supports tuition and living expenses and reflects the anthologies’ ethos of fostering literary recognition, education, and global awareness of Black women’s voices.

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)

Established in 1944, UNCF provides scholarships and institutional support to Black students at its member HBCUs, helping hundreds of thousands of individuals and driving social progress. Its famous motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste," has raised broad public awareness. UNCF garners significant contributions—from individuals, corporations, and foundations, including Lilly Endowment and Netflix founder Reed Hastings. Notable alumni include Martin Luther King Jr., Samuel L. Jackson, and Alexis Herman. Its annual fundraising events, such as the "An Evening of Stars," have raised millions for scholarships and continue to support educational equity.

## Rhodes Scholarship

Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the Rhodes Scholarship is a globally renowned postgraduate program at Oxford University promoting international leadership and academic excellence. While initially restricted to white men, the program now admits candidates of all genders and races from over 60 countries. Scholars are chosen for academic merit, leadership, moral character, and commitment to service; the selection process is highly competitive (global award rate ~0.7%). The scholarship covers university fees and a living stipend. Notable alumni include political leaders (Bill Clinton, Bob Hawke), academics, scientists, and human rights advocates. The Rhodes model has inspired numerous similar programs worldwide.

## Bolashak Scholarship

Instituted by Kazakhstan’s President in 1993, the Bolashak ("Future") Scholarship funds study at leading global universities for Kazakh citizens, aiming to foster Western values and expertise. By 2018, over 12,800 scholarships had been awarded, with recipients holding key roles across sectors in Kazakhstan. The ethnic composition is predominantly Kazakh, reflecting the language requirement. The program partners with a wide selection of universities worldwide.

## British Council

Founded in 1934 under Royal Charter, the British Council promotes British culture, education, and English language teaching globally and operates in over 100 countries. Activities include administering the IELTS exam, launching educational initiatives like MOOCs, supporting arts (e.g., Selector Radio, fiveFilms4freedom), and mobility programs (e.g., Erasmus+, Connecting Classrooms). The Council faces ongoing controversies regarding financial management, outsourcing and cultural diplomacy, and has experienced enforced closures in several countries, most recently Russia in 2025. Its work persists as a key vehicle for UK soft power, cultural exchange, and international collaboration.

## National Merit Scholarship Program

Launched to recognize and support outstanding U.S. high school students, the National Merit Scholarship Program relies on the PSAT/NMSQT as its qualifying exam, advancing top performers through Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist stages. About 8,000 finalists receive scholarships annually, funded by NMSC, corporations, and colleges. Criticisms include reliance on standardized testing and limited transparency. The former National Achievement Program for Black students now operates via the UNCF. Prominent alumni include Bill Gates, President John Roberts, and Mae Jemison.

## Fulbright Program

Established in 1946 to promote international academic exchange and mutual understanding, the Fulbright Program funds U.S. and foreign students, scholars, and professionals for research, study, and teaching worldwide. Managed by the U.S. Department of State and supported by bi-national commissions, Fulbright is the largest global education exchange program. Disrupted recently by political interference and program cessation in Russia, it remains vital, with broad alumni impact including heads of state and Nobel laureates. Specialized grants (e.g., Fulbright–Hays) support area studies, languages, and teacher exchanges.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)

Florida’s Bright Futures Program, launched in the late 1990s, funds college tuition for high-achieving state residents based on GPA, test scores, and service hours, irrespective of financial need. After initial generosity, scholarship values and eligibility tightened in response to fiscal pressure and criticism of inequity. The program now supports four levels of awards, renewable under strict academic conditions, and has added alternative test options (CLT). Legislative attempts to alter the coverage and eligibility continue to be debated.

## Miss America Pageant

Originating in 1921, Miss America evolved from a Boardwalk beauty contest into a scholarship-based national event promoting talent and social engagement, and embracing--with significant controversy and setbacks--racial and diversity inclusion. The pageant shifted its format over time, notably dropping the swimsuit competition in 2018 before partly reversing changes in 2023. It remains the largest scholarship provider for women in the U.S., though claims about funding amounts have been heavily scrutinized.

## Kennedy Scholarship

Created in 1964 as Britain’s memorial to President Kennedy, the Kennedy Scholarship enables UK graduates to study at Harvard or MIT, fostering U.S.-UK understanding and excellence. Selection is based on intellectual achievement and leadership, and the scholarship has produced leaders in politics (Yvette Cooper, Ed Balls), economics (Mervyn King), journalism, arts, and academia.

## Marshall Scholarship

Established in 1953 as a “living memorial” to the Marshall Plan, the Marshall Scholarship funds exceptional Americans for graduate study at any UK university. Selection values academic distinction, leadership, and ambassadorial potential, with a competitive process comparable to Rhodes. Scholars study diverse fields at top institutions and have become leaders across sectors, with the Association of Marshall Scholars fostering transatlantic dialogue.

## Norway Scholarship

Started in 1920 to deepen cultural and academic ties between Norway and Oxford’s Wadham College, the Norway Scholarship supports Norwegian students for varied study. While historically underfunded, it has seen periodic revitalization and engagement from the Norwegian academic and diplomatic communities. Past scholars occupy notable positions in Norway and UK academia.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

Named for influential politician Edmund Muskie (1914–1996)—noted for his environmental legislation, public service, and diplomacy—the Muskie Internship Program is a U.S. Department of State-supported exchange for Eurasian graduate students in American universities, offering career training and internship placements. Muskie's legacy includes major contributions to civil rights, environmental policy, and public administration, with extensive memorials and honors. 

## King's Scholar

King’s Scholars are distinguished students at historic British schools and colleges—most famously at Eton and Westminster—who have earned scholarships through academic excellence or competitive exams, often residing in dedicated houses and participating in special traditions. The title dates to the 14th century and has evolved across institutions, with modern scholars also recognized at King’s College Cambridge and several King’s Schools re-endowed by Henry VIII. Notable alumni include leaders in politics, literature, science, and the arts.
