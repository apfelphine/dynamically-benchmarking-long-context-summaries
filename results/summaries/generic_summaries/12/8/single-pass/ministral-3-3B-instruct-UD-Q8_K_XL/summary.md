### **Shuping Scholarship**

#### **History and Founding**
The **Shuping Scholarship**, established in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, was founded in memory of his father, who emphasized the principle of "returning to society what one has gained from it." Koo aimed to support academically gifted students from poor families, offering full tuition and additional support, including a library, science labs, free medical care, and vocational training. Initially, 600 students were awarded scholarships, with 300 renewed annually until graduation. The scholarship expanded to 1,100 students by 1949, with Koo personally financing awards until 1995, when he donated HK$10 million to the **Shuping Scholarship Foundation** for long-term funding.

#### **Expansion and Legacy**
During its first decade, the scholarship supported 1,100 students, many of whom became leaders in China. Koo revived the scholarship in 1986, expanding it to 37 secondary schools and 24 tertiary institutions across China. The foundation, led by Koo’s son George Koo, continues to administer the scholarship, emphasizing service to society. Notable alumni include government officials, party secretaries, naval officers, and educators, embodying the scholarship’s legacy of fostering social responsibility.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)**

#### **Overview**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (SdDV) is Germany’s premier merit-based scholarship program, founded in 1925 as a department of the *Deutsche Studentenschaft*. It was dissolved under Nazi rule in 1934 but revived in 1948 as a registered association in Cologne. The scholarship supports undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students based on academic excellence, leadership, and social commitment, with eligibility determined by the **BAföG** system.

#### **Selection and Support**
Applicants are nominated by schools, professors, or through self-nomination via an assessment test. Scholars receive financial support (€300/month for study expenses, need-based scholarships up to €855/month, and health insurance subsidies). Academic support includes summer schools, research groups, language courses, and mentorship. The program also offers specialized scholarships, such as the **KAUST-Studienstiftung** for STEM students and **Beethoven House** residencies for composers.

#### **Impact and Recognition**
The SdDV has supported over 70,000 scholars since its revival, with 71.5% receiving study expense allowances in 2023. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners (e.g., Manfred Eigen, Gerd Faltings), political leaders (e.g., Fritz Kuhn, Gesine Schwan), and artists (e.g., Hans Magnus Enzensberger, Zadie Smith). The scholarship’s emphasis on excellence and social responsibility has cemented its reputation as a cornerstone of German higher education.

### **Harkness Fellowship in Health Care Policy**
#### **History and Purpose**
The **Harkness Fellowship** was established in 1925 by Anna Harkness and later renamed the **Harkness Fellowships in Health Care** in 1961. It supports international scholars to study health care policy and practice in the U.S., fostering cross-cultural understanding. Fellows spend a year conducting research at U.S. institutions like Harvard, Johns Hopkins, or Stanford, with support for travel, seminars, and policy briefings.

#### **Administration and Funding**
The program is funded by the **Commonwealth Fund** and additional grants from organizations like the **B. Braun Stiftung** and **Robert Bosch Stiftung**. Fellows are selected from Australia, Canada, France, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland, and the UK.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Alumni include scholars like **Professor David Armitage** (historian), **Sir Jonathan Bate** (Shakespeare scholar), and **Sir Ronald Bottrall** (poet). The fellowship’s prestige lies in its focus on health policy, with alumni contributing to global health debates and advancements.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
#### **Mission and Growth**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** was founded in 1987 to support **public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)**. It evolved from the **Thurgood Marshall Scholarship Fund** and now provides scholarships, capacity-building programs, and advocacy for HBCUs. TMCF supports 55 member schools, including law, medical, and technical institutions, and has raised over $500 million for scholarships and institutional support.

#### **Leadership and Impact**
TMCF’s leadership includes figures like **Johnny C. Taylor Jr.** (former president and CEO) and **Harry L. Williams** (current president). The organization has secured major donations, such as $50 million from Apple and $25.6 million from the **Charles Koch Foundation**, to fund scholarships and institutional development. Notable alumni include **Spike Lee** (director), **Samuel L. Jackson** (actor), and **General Chappie James** (U.S. Air Force’s first Black four-star general).

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
#### **Legacy and Influence**
The **New Daughters of Africa** anthology, edited by Margaret Busby, is a landmark collection of writing by women of African descent, expanding on the 1992 anthology *Daughters of Africa*. The award honors African women students, funded by Myriad Editions in partnership with **SOAS, University of London**. The first recipient, **Idza Luhumyo** (Kenya), was chosen in 2020, with the award supporting students’ academic pursuits.

#### **Contributions and Recognition**
The anthology features works by over 200 writers, including **Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie**, **Zadie Smith**, and **Nicola Griffith**, and has inspired global literary movements. Busby’s work has been celebrated for exposing the rich literary contributions of African women, with the anthology included in lists like *Sacred Fire: QBR 100 Essential Black Books* and *The Guardian’s Top 10 Dissenting Life Stories*.

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
#### **History and Scholarships**
The **UNCF**, founded in 1944 by **William J. Trent**, **Frederick D. Patterson**, and **Mary McLeod Bethune**, is a nonprofit supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It administers scholarships for students at member institutions and beyond, with over 1.5 million recipients. UNCF alumni include **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.** (Nobel Peace Prize laureate), **Spike Lee**, and **Samuel L. Jackson**.

#### **Fundraising and Impact**
UNCF has secured major donations, such as $70 million from **MacKenzie Scott** (2025) and $120 million from **Netflix founder Reed Hastings** (2020). The organization’s **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars** telethon has raised over $200 million since 1980. Its motto, *"A mind is a terrible thing to waste,"* reflects its commitment to education equity.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
#### **Origins and Selection**
The **Rhodes Scholarship**, founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, supports postgraduate studies at Oxford University. Selection is based on academic merit, leadership, and service to society, with applicants from over 60 countries. The scholarship is highly competitive, with an acceptance rate of 0.7%.

#### **Controversies and Legacy**
Criticisms include **racial exclusion** (until 1977) and **colonialist ties** to Rhodes. Despite controversies, the scholarship has produced leaders like **Bill Clinton** (U.S. President), **Wasim Sajjad** (Pakistan President), and **Bob Hawke** (Australia Prime Minister). Notable scholars include **Howard Florey** (penicillin discoverer) and **Lucy Banda** (activist).

### **Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)**
#### **Description and Expansion**
The **Bolashak** program, established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev, supports Kazakh students studying abroad. Since 1994, over 12,000 students have received scholarships, with 2,000 returning to Kazakhstan annually. The program is administered by the **Ministry of Education and Science of Kazakhstan**, offering funding for studies in Australia, Canada, China, and the U.S.

#### **Ethnic Distribution and Universities**
Over 93% of applicants are Kazakh, with support for students in fields like engineering, medicine, and business. Notable universities include **Australian National University**, **Harvard University**, and **Tsinghua University**.

### **British Council**
#### **History and Activities**
The **British Council**, founded in 1934, promotes British culture and education globally. It offers English language courses, exams (e.g., IELTS), and cultural exchange programs. The council has faced controversies, including closures in Russia (2007) and criticism over its partnerships with Indian institutions.

#### **Notable Programs**
Key initiatives include **Connecting Classrooms** (international school partnerships), **Shakespeare Lives** (global Shakespeare celebrations), and **fiveFilms4freedom** (LGBTQ+ film festival). The council’s work in education and cultural diplomacy has been both praised and scrutinized for its alignment with British interests.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
#### **Program Overview**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** selects top high school students based on PSAT/NMSQT scores. Semifinalists advance to a finalist round, with 8,000 receiving $2,500 scholarships. The program has faced criticism for its reliance on standardized tests and lack of transparency in selection criteria.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Notable recipients include **Bill Gates** (Microsoft co-founder), **Mae Jemison** (NASA astronaut), and **Jeff Bezos** (Amazon founder). The program’s impact on college admissions and scholarship distribution remains a subject of debate.

### **Fulbright Program**
#### **History and Global Reach**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1946, is the largest international educational exchange program, funded by the U.S. government. It supports students, scholars, and teachers in the U.S. and abroad, with over 325,000 alumni. The program has faced political interference, such as the resignation of the Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board in 2025.

#### **Key Initiatives**
The Fulbright Program includes the **Fulbright U.S. Student Program** (for U.S. students) and the **Fulbright Foreign Student Program** (for international students). Notable alumni include **Nobel Prize winners** (e.g., **Kofi Annan**, **Kary Mullis**), **politicians** (e.g., **Sonia Sotomayor**, **Kamala Harris**), and **scientists** (e.g., **Freeman Dyson**, **James Watson**).

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)**
#### **History and Changes**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship**, established in 1997, provides merit-based scholarships to Florida high school graduates. Initially covering 39% of graduates, it faced cuts in 2011 due to budget constraints. Recent reforms (2018) expanded eligibility and funding, with the program now supporting students at public and private institutions.

#### **Eligibility and Renewal**
Scholarships are awarded based on SAT/ACT scores, GPA, and volunteer work. Renewals require maintaining academic standards, with varying support levels (e.g., 100% tuition for "Florida Academic Scholars"). The program’s impact on Florida’s education system remains a topic of debate.

### **Miss America Pageant**
#### **History and Evolution**
The **Miss America Pageant**, founded in 1919, has evolved from a beauty contest to a platform for advocacy. It has faced protests for its sexist and racist origins but has since embraced inclusivity, with the first Black, Asian, and transgender winners. Recent changes (e.g., "Miss America 2.0") aimed to modernize the pageant, though controversies persist.

#### **Controversies and Legacy**
Criticisms include the pageant’s focus on physical appearance and its ties to militarism. Despite changes, the pageant remains a cultural icon, with winners like **Vanessa Williams** (first Black Miss America) and **Nina Davuluri** (first Indian-American winner) shaping its narrative.
