# Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 in Shanghai by businessman and philanthropist Koo Soh Bing to support poor students with good academic records during the Japanese invasion, the Shuping Scholarship has awarded over 6,000 scholarships. It offers full tuition, extracurricular activities, medical care, and fosters social responsibility through charitable projects such as homes for refugee children and emergency medical funds. After suspension during political upheaval, it was revived in 1986 with government and alumni support, now providing over 700 awards annually across numerous schools and institutions, governed by alumni associations and a foundation established by Koo’s family.

# Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Originally founded in 1925, dissolved during Nazi rule, and reformed in 1948, the Studienstiftung is Germany’s largest scholarship foundation supporting highly talented students and doctoral candidates across all disciplines. Admission is competitive via nominations (schools, professors, competitions) or a standardized assessment, focusing on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. It provides financial support based on need and academic enrichment through seminars, mentoring, and research opportunities. With around 15,000 scholars as of 2023, notable alumni include multiple Nobel laureates and leaders across sciences, arts, politics, and business.

# Harkness Fellowship
Established by the Commonwealth Fund in 1925 to reciprocate Rhodes Scholarships, the Harkness Fellowship offers prestigious 12-month fellowships focused on healthcare policy and practice. It enables professionals from nations including Australia, Canada, Germany, the UK, and others to conduct research at leading U.S. institutions and participate in seminars addressing global health policy. Administered by the Commonwealth Fund with support from various foundations, the fellowship has produced notable alumni in diverse fields of science, medicine, arts, and public service.

# Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Founded in 1987, TMCF is a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization advocating for public historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), offering scholarships and programmatic support to 55 member institutions including law and medical schools. It has raised over $500 million, with major donations from corporations and philanthropists. TMCF also acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation to enhance entrepreneurship education. Leadership includes President Harry L. Williams, and member schools span across the U.S., serving tens of thousands of students.

# Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* Anthology and Award
First published in 1992, *Daughters of Africa* is a landmark anthology collecting writings from over 200 women of African descent worldwide, spanning centuries and genres. Praised for its breadth and impact, it serves as a key reference in African and black women’s literature. Its 2019 companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, updated and expanded the collection. The associated Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students, funded by contributor donations, and extends the anthology’s legacy by promoting black women’s literary and academic achievements.

# United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944 to support Black students and HBCUs, the UNCF provides scholarships and advocacy, raising major funds from figures like John F. Kennedy, Walter Annenberg, Charles Koch, and Reed Hastings. It is distinct from but complementary to the United Negro College Fund’s focus on funding schools and students. UNCF organizes high-profile fundraising events and supports a broad network of member institutions across the U.S., aiding tens of thousands of students in higher education.

# Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes to promote unity and diplomatic ties in the English-speaking world, Rhodes Scholarships are among the most competitive global postgraduate awards, supporting study primarily at Oxford University. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, character, leadership, and commitment to service. The scholarship was originally male-only and white-exclusive in some regions but now admits applicants worldwide, including women and diverse ethnicities. Rhodes Scholars have significantly contributed to politics, sciences, and social justice, though controversies persist over Rhodes’s colonial legacy and scholarship demographic shifts.

# Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Created in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s first president, the Bolashak Scholarship funds study abroad at leading global universities to promote Western values and expertise among Kazakh youth. Since inception, it has awarded over 12,800 scholarships, primarily to ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements. Recipients often hold key positions in Kazakhstan’s public and private sectors. The program competitively selects candidates via examinations and interviews, with universities in Australia, Europe, North America, and Asia among approved destinations.

# British Council
Founded in 1934 to promote British culture, education, and English language worldwide, the British Council operates in over 100 countries offering teaching, examinations (including IELTS), cultural exchange, and educational mobility programs like Education UK and Erasmus+. It is a UK government-sponsored charity with a history of adapting to geopolitical changes, including closures in some countries amid diplomatic tensions. It is also known for arts and cultural initiatives, with notable controversies including staff disputes, outsourcing, and political conflicts.

# National Merit Scholarship Program
A U.S.-based academic competition that recognizes high school students based on PSAT/NMSQT scores, progressing through stages from Commended status to Semifinalist, Finalist, and Scholarship winner. The program provides scholarships ranging from corporate-sponsored awards to college-funded grants. It includes the now-transitioned National Achievement Scholarship Program for Black American students. Despite its prestige, the program has faced criticism for reliance on standardized testing and lack of transparency.

# Fulbright Program
Established in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international peace and understanding through educational exchange, the Fulbright Program funds research, study, and teaching across over 160 countries. It offers diverse grants for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals in multiple disciplines. Operated by the U.S. Department of State with bi-national commissions worldwide, the program is highly prestigious with alumni achieving leadership, Nobel Prizes, and notable contributions across fields. Recent challenges include political interference and restriction in countries like Russia.

# Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)
Launched to reward academic merit among Florida high school graduates, this state-funded program offers four levels of scholarships covering varying tuition amounts for study at Florida colleges and universities or vocational schools. Over time, funding cuts and increased eligibility standards reduced participation, disproportionately affecting minority students. The program requires academic achievement, standardized test scores, volunteer/work hours, and renews annually based on GPA. The Bright Futures program has undergone reforms and controversies related to fairness and funding.

# Miss America Pageant
Founded in 1921 as a beauty and talent contest to support Atlantic City tourism, Miss America evolved into a scholarship pageant offering educational awards. It has a complex history marked by racial segregation, civil rights milestones including the first African-American winner in 1984, and recent format changes focusing on inclusivity and talent over physical appearance. The pageant has faced scandals, leadership controversies, and shifting cultural roles but remains a significant American institution reflecting social change.

# Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 as a British memorial to U.S. President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship funds British postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT for selected British students. Administered by trustees appointed by the UK prime minister, it emphasizes intellectual ability, communication skills, and leadership potential. Notable alumni span politics, law, economics, journalism, and academia, contributing significantly to UK public life.

# Marshall Scholarship
Created in 1953 as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall to strengthen U.S.-UK relations, Marshall Scholarships fund American graduate study in the UK. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and engagement with British society. It offers flexibility in university choice across the UK and includes an expanding network of alumni active in policy, academia, and innovation. The Scholarship hosts forums promoting transatlantic dialogue and honors distinguished leaders advancing bilateral understanding.

# Norway Scholarship
Founded in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford’s Wadham College, this scholarship promotes Anglo-Norwegian cultural exchange. Despite funding challenges, it has facilitated study in diverse disciplines for Norwegian scholars, with notable support from Oxford alumni and Norwegian dignitaries. The scholarship sustains traditions such as annual dinners and lectures fostering UK-Norway academic relations.

# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program and Legacy
Named after Edmund Muskie (1914–1996), a prominent American politician known for environmental advocacy, civil rights, and diplomacy, the internship program offers Eurasian scholars summer placements and training in the U.S. Muskie’s career spanned Maine governorship, U.S. Senate service championing environmental laws (Clean Air and Water Acts), the 1968 vice-presidential nomination, and U.S. Secretary of State during a critical Cold War period. He is remembered for his legislative accomplishments, political integrity, and impact on U.S. environmental policy.

# King’s Scholar Traditions
Dating back to 1317, King’s Scholar designations exist at prestigious UK institutions such as Eton College, Westminster School, and King’s College Cambridge. Awarded through competitive examinations or academic achievement, King’s Scholars enjoy historic privileges including residence in specific college houses, distinctive gowns, and lifelong association with cathedral foundations. Notables with King’s Scholar status span politics, literature, science, and the arts, including Prime Ministers Boris Johnson and Harold Macmillan, economist John Maynard Keynes, and writer George Orwell.
