# Scholarship and Education Initiatives Summary

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by Koo in Shanghai to support poor, academically strong students, the Shuping Scholarships initially provided full tuition and extensive ancillary support, including a library, science labs, and free lessons in various skills. Over its first ten years (1939-1949), 1,100 students were supported, and the founders also established charitable works, such as a Home for Refugee Children and an Emergency Medical Fund. The scholarships were revived in 1986 and, as of 2004, provide over 700 annual awards across secondary and tertiary institutions, alongside extracurricular activities to foster good citizenship. In 1995, Koo donated HK$10 million to the Shanghai Education Development Foundation, ensuring the long-term continuation of the scheme.

## Studienstiftung
Established in Cologne in 1948, the Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates based on the principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility." Admission is achieved through multiple channels, including nominations by schools, professors, examination boards, competition winners, or via an assessment test since 2010. The general scholarship provides financial aid (€300 monthly, or up to €855 based on BAföG) and academic support, such as summer schools and mentoring. The foundation also offers internal and open scholarship programs, such as the KAUST-Studienstiftung program.

## Harkness Fellowship
Originally established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships for British graduates, the Harkness Fellowships broadened its scope. Since June 1997, the program has focused exclusively on healthcare policy and practice. Fellows spend a year conducting research at major American institutions (like Harvard and Johns Hopkins) to understand U.S. health care policy challenges. The program is administered by the Commonwealth Fund and includes a structured seminar schedule, featuring international policy briefings.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Founded in 1987, the TMCF advocates for higher education at public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). It supports 55 member schools, which include both HBCUs and Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs). TMCF has raised over $500 million and acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) to enhance talent identification and entrepreneurship within the HBCU community.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 under the will of Cecil John Rhodes, the scholarship aims to foster unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. Today, the global acceptance rate is 0.7%, making it highly competitive. Selection criteria emphasize scholastic attainments alongside moral force of character, courage, and unselfishness. While historically criticized for its colonialist views, the scholarship has expanded globally, with recent selections including transgender and non-binary scholars.

## Kennedy Scholarship
The John F. Kennedy Scholarship was created in 1964 as a living memorial to John F. Kennedy, offering scholarships for postgraduate study at either Harvard or MIT. The selection process emphasizes intellectual attainment, the suitability of the proposed course of study, and a commitment to public service. The program is comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships in its high selectivity.

## Marshall Scholarship
Founded in 1953, the Marshall Scholarship aims to enable intellectually distinguished American young leaders to study in the UK, thereby strengthening British-American understanding. Scholars receive a two-year award covering UK university fees and a quarterly maintenance stipend. The selection process requires university endorsement and emphasizes intellectual distinction, character, and potential to contribute to one's society.

## Fulbright Program
Established in 1945 following WWII, the Fulbright Program promotes international peace and understanding through educational exchange. It operates on a bi-national basis, funding programs for both U.S. persons studying abroad and foreign nationals visiting the U.S. Key programs include the Fulbright Degree Program for international graduate study and the Fulbright U.S. Student Program for U.S. seniors to study abroad.

## Bolashak
"Bolashak," meaning "Future," was instituted by Kazakhstan's President Nursultan Nazarbayev in 1993 to inculcate youth with Western, democratic values. The program has seen massive growth, granting scholarships to thousands of students to study at leading international universities across various countries.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida-based program emulates the HOPE Scholarship. It funds four levels of scholarship—Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Gold Seal Vocational (GSV), and Gold Seal CAPE (GSC)—based on academic merit and service hours. Requirements include specific GPA and standardized test scores. The program has undergone several revisions, including adjustments to funding amounts and score requirements.

## Miss America
Originating from a 1920 Atlantic City event, the pageant evolved to incorporate college scholarships in 1944. The competition has faced continuous scrutiny regarding racism, sexism, and commercialism throughout its history. Significant changes occurred in 2018 with the "Miss America 2.0" format, removing swimsuit judging, though some changes have since been reversed. The organization has recently faced financial issues, filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in late 2024.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award is connected to Margaret Busby's anthology, *Daughters of Africa*. The anthology is lauded as a "literary first" and a "landmark anthology" that gathers world literature by women of African descent. The accompanying award provides accommodation for an African woman student, supported by donations from the anthology's earnings.

## UNCF
Founded in 1944, the UNCF supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). While its scholarships are open to all ethnicities, the majority of recipients are African-American. It has raised substantial funds through high-profile donations, including a $70 million gift from MacKenzie Scott in 2025, and uses the motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This program screens over 1.5 million high school entrants using the PSAT/NMSQT. Students recognized as National Merit Semifinalists advance by submitting SAT scores, academic records, and essays. The ultimate goal is to award scholarships to finalists based on a combination of accomplishments and potential. Admissions officers have criticized the program for relying too heavily on a single standardized test.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
The program is a professional exchange supported by the U.S. Department of State, offering summer internships and career training to Eurasian scholars and graduate students in the U.S. The program was established in 1992 and has been renewed and updated, with recent formats administered by Cultural Vistas.

## King's Scholar
The term "King's Scholar" applies to several distinct institutions. At Eton College, a King's Scholar is one admitted to the original central house, distinguishing them with a black academic gown. At King's College Cambridge, it refers to undergraduates achieving first-class degrees. Other schools (like King's Ely and Canterbury) use the designation to recognize high academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by fee discounts.

## Comparison of Post-Graduate Scholarships (General Trends)
Various scholarships—including Rhodes, Marshall, and Fulbright—share a focus on promoting global understanding, academic excellence, and leadership. While Marshall Scholars are often noted for higher GPAs and careers in academia, Rhodes Scholars are more broadly distributed across professional fields. Fulbright emphasizes direct educational exchange, while the Marshall and Rhodes programs focus on studying within the UK.
