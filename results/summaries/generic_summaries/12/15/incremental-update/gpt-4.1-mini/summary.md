**Kennedy Scholarship Overview**  
Established in 1964 to honor President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship supports UK students pursuing graduate studies at Harvard or MIT. Administered by trustees appointed by the UK Prime Minister and supported by the Kennedy family and UK dignitaries, it awards nearly 600 competitive scholarships based on intellect, communication, originality, public service, and resilience. Alumni include prominent UK ministers, Bank of England leaders, journalists, poets, academics, and Supreme Court Justices.

**Norway Scholarship**  
Founded in 1919 to promote Norwegian studies at Oxford’s Wadham College and strengthen UK-Norway relations, it covers humanities, sciences, and medicine, though interrupted in the 1920s and during WWII.

**Florida Bright Futures Scholarship Program**  
A merit-based aid program for Florida high school graduates covering tuition at Florida institutions, contingent on maintaining academic standards and FAFSA completion. It has experienced funding cuts and restructurings since 2011.

**King’s Scholar Traditions and Scholarships**  
Historic scholarships at Eton College, Westminster School, King’s College Cambridge, and others honoring scholastic excellence with distinctive gowns and privileges rooted in long-standing traditions.

**National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)**  
Annually, about 1.5 million U.S. high school juniors compete via PSAT/NMSQT for ~16,000 Semifinalist and ~8,000 Finalist scholarships from state, corporate, and college sources. Alumni include Bill Gates and Jeff Bezos. The program faces criticism over exclusivity and transparency.

**Edmund S. Muskie: Life, Career, and Legacy**  
Maine politician and Secretary of State known for bipartisan leadership, civil rights advocacy, and Cold War diplomacy. The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program, established in 1992, offers Eurasian scholars U.S. internships fostering public service.

**Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)**  
Since 1993, funds Kazakh youth studying abroad to promote Western democratic values, with over 12,800 scholars educated in 32 countries and alumni in key national roles.

**Marshall Scholarship**  
Founded in 1953 honoring George C. Marshall, this inclusive program funds top American graduate studies in the UK across diverse fields, favoring leading universities. Alumni include notable judiciary members and Nobel laureates.

**United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**  
Since 1944, supports African American education principally at Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), sustained by donors and producing prominent alumni like Martin Luther King Jr. and Spike Lee. Member schools are historically significant, diverse institutions across many states.

**Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**  
Established in 1987 to assist public HBCUs, Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), and Black community colleges, offering scholarships, entrepreneurship programs, and institutional support with corporate partnerships across multiple states.

**Miss America Pageant: Overview and History**  
Founded in 1921, it evolved from a beauty contest to a scholarship competition emphasizing talent and inclusivity. It overcame racial exclusions and now embraces diversity across race, disability, gender identity, and sexual orientation. After 2017 scandals, it reformed but faced leadership crises and bankruptcy in 2024.

**Fulbright Program**  
Since 1946, promotes international peace via educational and cultural exchanges with global grants overseen by the U.S. State Department and partners. It involves over 19,000 annual participants and notable alumni including global leaders and Nobel laureates. Geopolitical tensions have led to bans and funding freezes in some regions.

**Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**  
Germany’s largest scholarship foundation since 1925, supporting top students (~0.48% annually) with financial aid and enrichment programs. Alumni include Nobel laureates and Fields Medalists.

**British Council**  
Founded in 1934 to promote UK culture and English language globally through teaching, exchanges, arts, and education programs. It exerts wide influence but faces controversies related to accountability, security, funding, and geopolitical issues.

**Shuping Scholarship (Shanghai)**  
Established in 1939 by Koo for financially disadvantaged gifted students emphasizing comprehensive aid and community service. Interrupted during the Cultural Revolution, it was revived in 1986 with government and alumni support. The foundation in Hong Kong (1995) ensures sustainability and promotes values like filial piety and scholar governance.

**Rhodes Scholarship**  
Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes’s will to unite the British Empire and strengthen US ties, administered by the Rhodes Trust with UK parliamentary oversight. It inspired global scholarships like the Kennedy and Schwarzman, expanding inclusivity over time to include women and gender-diverse candidates, now funding 1–3 years postgraduate study at Oxford with 101 awards in 2022. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, character, leadership, and public service. Despite early racial and gender exclusions and ongoing debates over Rhodes’s legacy, it has produced influential alumni across academia, judiciary, business, medicine, government (including eight heads of state), and civil rights movements.

**Harkness Fellowship**  
Founded in 1918 by the Commonwealth Fund for Commonwealth scholars to study in the U.S., since 1997 focused on healthcare research with 12-month fellowships at premier U.S. institutions, supported by multiple international foundations and ministries. Alumni include distinguished academics, scientists, philosophers, musicians, and public figures driving global leadership.

**Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* Anthologies and Legacy**  
The 1992 *Daughters of Africa*, edited by Margaret Busby, is a comprehensive, internationally acclaimed anthology celebrating writings by women of African descent across history. It serves as a vital resource for reclaiming African women’s narratives and fostering black feminist literary traditions, featuring renowned contributors such as Maya Angelou, Toni Morrison, and Audre Lorde.

In 2019, Busby published *New Daughters of Africa*, a sequel with over 200 writers arranged by birth decade, continuing themes of sisterhood, identity, and African diasporic narratives. Published across multiple countries and languages, the anthology has received critical acclaim, including 5-star reviews and a 2020 NAACP Image Award nomination. It powerfully explores themes of freedom, race, and social change, highlighting underrepresented voices globally.

Connected to the anthology, the **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award** provides financial support to African women students, funded by Busby, contributors, and Myriad Editions, with recipients announced since 2020. Initiatives include donating hundreds of copies to UK schools and African libraries, further promoting access and literary empowerment.
