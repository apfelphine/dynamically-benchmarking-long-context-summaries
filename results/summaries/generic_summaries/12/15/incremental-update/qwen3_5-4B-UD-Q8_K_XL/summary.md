### Literary Anthology: *Daughters of Africa*

**_Daughters of Africa_**, edited by Margaret Busby, stands as a monumental achievement tracing the history of Black women's writing from ancient Egypt to the present. Originally published in 1992 with 1,089 pages, the landmark volume restored marginalized voices to the literary center and challenged the misconception that Black women's literature began in the 1980s. Recognized as one of the 25 most influential books of the last quarter-century, it features iconic figures like Maya Angelou, Toni Morrison, and Zora Neale Hurston alongside historical icons such as Hatshepsut and Phillis Wheatley. Its extensive bibliographies and headnotes serve as a definitive reference guide, inspiring both a German-language equivalent and generations of writers, particularly during and after apartheid.

In 2019, Busby released **_New Daughters of Africa_**, a companion volume featuring over 200 writers who came of age after the first edition. Organized by decade of birth to highlight generational links, this international anthology celebrates sisterhood, tradition, and identity across the Diaspora. Launched at the South Bank Centre, the book has received high praise for its chronological organization and cultural significance, appearing on essential reading lists from institutions like the Royal African Society. It has been widely translated and published in multiple regions, including the US, South Africa, and Germany. Furthermore, the project launched the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award to support African female students, with proceeds funded by the publisher, author, and contributors who waived their fees. Both volumes have garnered critical acclaim as vital texts for understanding the global tradition of Black women's writing.
