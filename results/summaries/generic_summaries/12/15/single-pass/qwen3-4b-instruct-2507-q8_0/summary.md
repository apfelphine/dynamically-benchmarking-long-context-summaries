### **Kennedy Scholarship**
Established in 1964 by Sir Alec Douglas-Home, the Kennedy Scholarship honors John F. Kennedy’s legacy through a dual structure: a living memorial scholarship to attend Harvard or MIT, and a permanent memorial site at Runnymede, England. The Kennedy family, including Senator Edward Kennedy, strongly supported the initiative. Over 600 scholars have been awarded, selected through a national competition emphasizing intellectual attainment, public service potential, and academic excellence, with criteria comparable to Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships. Notable recipients include politicians, economists, journalists, and academics.

### **Norway Scholarship**
Founded in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, this scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Initially funded with NOK 60,000, the program has evolved, with funding later supplemented by private contributions. Since 1920, over 100 scholars have been awarded across varied disciplines, with the program experiencing interruptions due to financial constraints. The scholarship is administered through a committee and has been a significant educational exchange between Norway and the UK.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
Established in Florida, this program provides academic merit-based scholarships to high school graduates, with awards covering tuition and fees. It operates on a tiered structure (A-level and B-level) and includes a vocational track. The program faced criticism for favoring wealthier students and increasing financial burdens on state budgets. In 2018, it underwent a major overhaul, introducing new levels and criteria, including a focus on academic rank and expanded eligibility.

### **King's Scholar**
A historical designation at Eton College and King's College, Cambridge, the King's Scholar is a prestigious academic position for students who pass rigorous examinations. At Eton, scholars live in the central College building, participate in formal traditions, and are recognized for their intellectual and personal growth. At King's College, scholars receive a prize and attend formal dinners. The title is also used for undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
Launched in 1955, the National Merit Scholarship Program is a prestigious U.S. academic competition that identifies high-achieving high school students through the PSAT/NMSQT. Students are recognized as Semifinalists or Finalists based on scores and academic records. The program offers monetary awards, with recipients often going on to successful careers in academia, politics, and business. The program has faced criticism for its reliance on standardized testing and concerns about equity.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
Established by the U.S. Department of State in 1992, the Muskie Internship Program supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students through summer internships, career training, and financial support. Administered by Cultural Vistas since 2015, the program fosters international exchange and professional development in the fields of public service and policy.

### **Bolashak**
Instituted in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s President Nursultan Nazarbayev, the Bolashak scholarship aims to promote Western democratic values among young Kazakh youth. The program has awarded over 12,800 scholarships since its inception, with recipients holding key positions in state and private organizations. The program emphasizes academic excellence and leadership, with a focus on international engagement and national development.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
Founded in 1953, the Marshall Scholarship enables American students to study in the UK, promoting mutual understanding between the U.S. and UK. Selected through a competitive process, scholars receive full tuition coverage and a maintenance stipend. The program has a long-standing legacy, with notable alumni in politics, science, and diplomacy. It has evolved to include more diverse candidates and is now more inclusive of women and minorities.

### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
Established in 1944, UNCF supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships and funding. It has raised over $500 million to support HBCU institutions and students. The organization has played a critical role in advancing educational equity for African Americans, with notable alumni in business, politics, and the arts.

### **Miss America**
Originating in 1920, the Miss America Pageant began as a beauty pageant to promote tourism in Atlantic City. Over time, it evolved into a platform for cultural and social engagement, with a focus on leadership, service, and public service. The pageant has faced criticism for promoting gender stereotypes and has undergone significant changes, including the elimination of the swimsuit competition and the adoption of a more inclusive, values-based format.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
Founded in 1987, TMCF supports public HBCUs through scholarships, capacity-building, and institutional development. It has grown to support 55 member institutions and has raised over $500 million. The fund emphasizes the importance of public HBCUs in promoting educational equity and leadership development among African American students.

### **Fulbright Program**
Established in 1946, the Fulbright Program promotes international educational exchange through scholarships for students, scholars, and professionals. It operates in over 160 countries and has a long-standing legacy of fostering cross-cultural understanding. The program has faced challenges, including political interference and funding cuts, but continues to be a cornerstone of international academic cooperation.

### **Studienstiftung**
Founded in 1925, the Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes supports German and international students through scholarships, academic development, and extracurricular activities. It emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility in scholarship recipients. The organization offers both general and specialized scholarships, with a strong focus on academic and personal growth.

### **British Council**
A UK-based cultural and educational organization, the British Council promotes English language learning, cultural exchange, and educational cooperation worldwide. It operates in over 100 countries and offers a range of programs, including English language teaching, youth programs, and academic exchanges. The organization has faced criticism over political interference and controversies related to its operations in certain countries.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship supports students from poor families with excellent academic records. The program emphasizes service to society and includes extracurricular activities and community engagement. It has supported over 6,000 students and continues to promote social responsibility and educational equity.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
Established in 1902, the Rhodes Scholarship provides postgraduate education to students from around the world, with a focus on leadership and public service. The scholarship is administered by the Rhodes Trust and is one of the most selective and prestigious academic awards globally. It has a long history of producing influential leaders in politics, science, and human rights, though it has also faced criticism for its colonial legacy and gender exclusivity.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
Initiated in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowship enables British graduates to study in the United States. It has evolved into a premier health policy fellowship, with a focus on health care research and policy development. The program supports fellows from Australia, Canada, the UK, and other nations, fostering international collaboration in health policy.
