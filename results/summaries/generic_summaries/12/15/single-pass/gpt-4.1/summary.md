# International and National Scholarship Programs

## Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 as a British memorial for U.S. President John F. Kennedy, the Kennedy Scholarship provides British students with scholarships to Harvard or MIT, alongside a physical memorial at Runnymede. Supported by the Kennedy family, its trustees are appointed by the UK Prime Minister and include prominent former scholars and public figures. Nearly 600 scholarships have been granted via a national competition, with selection criteria akin to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships, emphasizing academic excellence, originality, public service, and resilience.

## Norway Scholarship
Founded in 1919, the Norway Scholarship enables Norwegian students from the University of Oslo to study for a year at Wadham College, Oxford. Despite funding challenges, alumni and organizational efforts have ensured its continuation, with the scholarship now awarded annually to students pursuing varied subjects.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
The Florida Bright Futures program, inspired by Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, provides merit-based scholarships at four levels to Florida students attending state colleges and universities. The program covers up to 100% of tuition depending on academic and service requirements, having undergone significant changes over time regarding award amounts and eligibility criteria. Notably, federal investigations in 2014 addressed concerns over racial disparities, and recent years have brought increased SAT/ACT/CLT score requirements and changes in benefits.

## King's Scholar
Dating to the 14th century, King's Scholars are distinguished students at prestigious UK schools (Eton, Westminster) and King's College Cambridge, selected for academic excellence and tradition. Similar awards exist at various King's Schools and have evolved to include both genders and privileges such as participation in historic ceremonies, marriage rights, and academic accolades.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
In the U.S., the National Merit program selects high-achieving high school seniors via PSAT scores for scholarships, proceeding through multiple recognition rounds (Commended, Semifinalist, Finalist) and awarding over $2,500, corporate, and college-sponsored scholarships. The program has faced criticism for relying on standardized testing, which may disadvantage certain groups. The separate National Achievement program, aimed at Black American students, was merged into the UNCF after 2015.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
Named after Edmund Muskie, a key U.S. politician and diplomat, the Muskie Program supports Eurasian scholars studying or interning in the U.S. and is funded by the Department of State. Muskie's legacy includes significant contributions to environmental and civil rights legislation.

## Bolashak Scholarship
Kazakhstan's Bolashak Program, launched in 1993, funds study at top international universities for nationals, with over 12,000 scholarships awarded by 2018. The majority of recipients are ethnic Kazaks due to language criteria. Graduates often fill roles in state and private institutions, with the program emphasizing cultural and professional exchange.

## Marshall Scholarship
The UK-founded Marshall Scholarship (1953) supports accomplished American graduates for postgraduate study at any UK university, aiming to foster US-UK relations and future leadership. Highly competitive and prestigious, selection favors those with academic distinction and leadership potential, with alumni pursuing careers predominantly in academia, research, and public service.

## UNCF and Thurgood Marshall College Fund
The United Negro College Fund (UNCF) and the Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF) are the major U.S. organizations supporting scholarships for students at historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs). UNCF, founded in 1944, is renowned for fundraising initiatives and a large scholarship footprint, while the TMCF, founded in 1987, focuses on public HBCUs and capacity building.

## Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program, initiated post-WWII by Senator J. William Fulbright, is the largest international exchange program, funding U.S. citizens and foreigners for study, research, and teaching worldwide. It operates via bilateral commissions, selecting candidates based on academic excellence and leadership. Recent political developments have disrupted its operation in Russia and raised concerns over program politicization in the U.S.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Germany's Studienstiftung is the country's largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation, supporting outstanding undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students with financial and academic resources. Admittance is through rigorous national and institutional nomination processes. It offers internal and open scholarships and maintains a tradition of promoting excellence, initiative, and responsibility.

# British Council

## Overview and Activities
The British Council, a UK charity and public corporation, promotes British culture, education, and English language abroad through teaching centers, IELTS testing, arts initiatives, and partnership programs (e.g., Erasmus+). It works in over 100 countries, with its history marked by the Royal Charter and a strong focus on international cultural diplomacy.

## Controversies and Challenges
The council has faced challenges, including closures in Russia due to diplomatic strain and accusations of political interference, outsourcing concerns, pay disputes, security risks, and allegations of inadequate responses to the needs of international teachers/students.

# Other Major Scholarship and Anthology Initiatives

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, the Shuping Scholarship aids academically talented yet financially disadvantaged school and university students in China, coupled with extracurricular and social support. Its alumni have significantly contributed to China's development, and governance is overseen by alumni associations and an endowed foundation.

## Rhodes and Harkness Fellowships
The Rhodes Scholarship, established in 1902 for study at Oxford, selects global future leaders based on academic achievement and character, serving as a model for similar programs like the Harkness Fellowships (1925), which enable international study in the U.S. and now mainly focus on health policy research.

# Anthologies and Literary Scholarships

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Connected to the acclaimed anthologies "Daughters of Africa" and "New Daughters of Africa," edited by Margaret Busby, the scholarship supports African women students at SOAS, University of London. These anthologies assemble hundreds of works by black women writers, celebrating their global impact and fostering opportunities for future authors.
