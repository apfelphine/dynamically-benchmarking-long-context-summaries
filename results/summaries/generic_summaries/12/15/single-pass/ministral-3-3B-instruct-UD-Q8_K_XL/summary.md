### **Kennedy Scholarship**
The **Kennedy Scholarship** was established in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, funded by Prime Minister Sir Alec Douglas-Home and supported by the Kennedy family. It comprises two memorials: a **living scholarship** for postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT, and a **permanent memorial site** at Runnymede, England, symbolizing the birthplace of freedoms championed by Kennedy.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Selection Process:** A national competition selects around 25 finalists, who are interviewed by trustees. Criteria include intellectual attainment, leadership potential, commitment to public service, and ability to overcome adversity.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Scholars receive full tuition and living expenses for two years, with the option to extend for up to five years. The program is comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships.
- **Notable Alumni:** Prominent figures include politicians (e.g., Kwasi Kwarteng, Yvette Cooper), economists (e.g., Mervyn King), and academics (e.g., Tim Sims, Emma Rothschild).

### **Norway Scholarship**
Instituted in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, the **Norway Scholarship** enables students from the University of Oslo to study in Oxford, Wadham College. Initially funded by Jayne’s donations, it expanded significantly in the late 20th century, with over 12,000 scholarships awarded by 2018.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Eligibility:** Open to students from Norway, with preference given to those with strong academic records and leadership potential.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Scholarships cover tuition and living expenses for one year, with some renewable. The program emphasizes cultural exchange and academic excellence.
- **Notable Recipients:** Notable alumni include scholars in fields like medicine, law, and the humanities.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida, originally modeled after Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship, provides merit-based funding for high school graduates. It offers four levels of scholarships (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) based on academic merit, SAT/ACT scores, and volunteer work.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Eligibility:** Open to Florida residents with high academic achievement, including minimum SAT/ACT scores and volunteer hours. Requirements vary by scholarship level.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Covers tuition and fees at eligible institutions, with renewal conditions based on GPA and course completion. The program has faced controversies over racial disparities and funding cuts.
- **Notable Alumni:** Notable alumni include politicians (e.g., Ed Balls, Kwasi Kwarteng) and business leaders.

### **King’s Scholar**
The **King’s Scholar** program is rooted in British tradition, with origins dating back to 1317 when King Edward II established scholarships at Cambridge. It includes:
- **King’s Scholars at Eton College:** Awarded to students excelling in exams, with privileges like access to historic facilities and academic gowns.
- **King’s Scholars at King’s College Cambridge:** Recognizes first-class degrees with a £350 prize and a formal dinner.
- **King’s Scholars at King’s Schools:** Scholarships awarded for academic or musical excellence, often accompanied by fee reductions.

#### **Notable Alumni:** Includes politicians (e.g., Boris Johnson, Tony Blair), scientists (e.g., Stephen Hawking), and artists (e.g., Sir Paul McCartney).

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is a U.S. initiative that selects top-performing high school students based on PSAT/NMSQT scores. It offers:
- **Selection Rounds:** Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist stages, with Finalists receiving merit scholarships.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Awards include $2,500 scholarships and additional funding from corporate sponsors. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates (e.g., Joseph Stiglitz, Ben Bernanke) and political figures (e.g., Ted Cruz).

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** supports graduate students and professionals from Eurasian countries, offering summer internships, career training, and financial support. Named after former U.S. Secretary of State Edmund Muskie, it emphasizes international exchange and professional development.

### **Bolashak Scholarship**
The **Bolashak Scholarship** in Kazakhstan, established in 1993, enables students to study abroad, particularly in the UK, Australia, and the US. It supports academic excellence and cultural exchange, with over 12,000 scholarships awarded since its inception.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
The **Marshall Scholarship** was created in 1953 to honor U.S. Secretary of State George Marshall’s post-WWII European recovery efforts. It funds postgraduate studies in the UK for American students, emphasizing leadership, academic excellence, and cultural understanding.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Eligibility:** Open to U.S. citizens with outstanding academic records and leadership potential.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Covers tuition, living expenses, and a maintenance stipend for two years. Scholarships are highly competitive, with around 40 awarded annually.
- **Notable Alumni:** Includes Nobel laureates (e.g., Stephen Breyer, Roger Tsien) and political leaders (e.g., Bill Clinton).

### **UNCF Scholarships**
The **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)** provides scholarships and grants to students at historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). Founded in 1944, UNCF supports over 65,000 students annually, with notable alumni including civil rights leaders (e.g., Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.) and business figures (e.g., Spike Lee, Samuel L. Jackson).

### **Miss America**
The **Miss America Pageant** began in 1919 as a beauty contest, evolving into a platform for cultural and social commentary. It includes:
- **Selection Process:** State-level pageants followed by a national competition, with criteria shifting over time to emphasize talent, scholarship, and activism.
- **Controversies:** Criticized for promoting traditional beauty standards, with recent reforms focusing on inclusivity and diversity.
- **Notable Alumni:** Includes activists (e.g., Vanessa Williams, Nina Davuluri) and public figures (e.g., Brooke Burke).

---

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** supports public HBCUs, offering scholarships, capacity-building initiatives, and professional development. Founded in 1987, it has grown to support 55 schools and over 500,000 students.

---

### **Fulbright Program**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1945, is the largest international educational exchange program, offering grants for U.S. students and scholars to study abroad, and for foreign students to study in the U.S. It emphasizes cultural exchange and academic collaboration.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Eligibility:** Open to students and scholars in various disciplines, with selection based on academic merit, leadership, and cultural fit.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Covers tuition, living expenses, and travel for one academic year. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates (e.g., Joseph Stiglitz, Kofi Annan) and political leaders (e.g., Bill Clinton).

---

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is Germany’s premier merit scholarship program, supporting students from all backgrounds based on academic excellence, initiative, and social commitment. It offers financial and academic support, including summer academies and mentorship programs.

---

### **British Council**
The **British Council** promotes the English language and cultural exchange globally. It administers exams, supports education programs, and fosters partnerships between the UK and international institutions. Notable activities include English-language testing (e.g., IELTS) and cultural initiatives like the Shakespeare Lives program.

---

### **Shuping Scholarship**
The **Shuping Scholarship** in Shanghai, founded in 1939, supports students from poor families with academic excellence. It emphasizes character-building through voluntary work, library access, and extracurricular activities, with over 6,000 scholarships awarded since its revival in 1986.

---

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
The **Rhodes Scholarship**, established in 1902, is one of the most prestigious international scholarships, funding postgraduate studies at Oxford University. It emphasizes leadership, intellectual excellence, and service to society.

#### **Selection and Structure**
- **Eligibility:** Open to students from 64 countries, with selection based on academic merit, character, and potential for leadership.
- **Scholarship Terms:** Covers tuition, living expenses, and travel for two years. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates (e.g., David Thorburn Woods) and political leaders (e.g., Bill Clinton, Tony Blair).

---

### **Harkness Fellowship**
The **Harkness Fellowship** supports health policy and practice research in the U.S. and Europe. Founded in 1925, it provides funding for one-year fellowships, emphasizing collaboration and policy innovation.

---

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
The **New Daughters of Africa Award** celebrates and supports African women writers. Established in 2019, it honors contributions to literature and cultural exchange, with a focus on fostering new voices in African writing.
