# Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 as a British memorial to President John F. Kennedy, the Kennedy Scholarship provides postgraduate opportunities at Harvard or MIT for British students. The scholarship was part of a twofold memorial including a permanent site at Runnymede, England, symbolizing the freedoms Kennedy championed. Strongly supported by the Kennedy family, the scholarship program emphasizes intellectual attainment, expression ability, suitability of study, originality, public service commitment, and overcoming adversity. Trustees, appointed by the UK Prime Minister, include distinguished former scholars and public figures. Notable alumni have significantly impacted politics, economics, journalism, arts, academia, and law.

# Norway Scholarship
Conceived in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, the Norway Scholarship enabled Norwegian students to study at Oxford’s Wadham College. Despite funding challenges causing temporary suspensions, renewed efforts in the late 20th century revitalized the program. The scholarship has supported diverse fields from law and languages to science and arts, with long-standing organizational support via alumni and government involvement. Numerous scholars have contributed notably to Norwegian academia, government, and culture.

# Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)
Launched as an academic merit-based scholarship in Florida, the Bright Futures program has awarded support to a large portion of high school graduates, emphasizing GPA, standardized test scores (SAT, ACT, CLT), and community service or work. It features several award levels (Florida Academic Scholar, Florida Medallion Scholar, Gold Seal Vocational and CAPE Scholars). The program experienced funding cuts and changes impacting minority and low-income students, sparking a federal investigation that found disparate impacts without discriminatory intent. Recent reforms reinstated funding percentages and adjusted eligibility criteria, with renewal requirements tied to academic performance.

# King's Scholar (UK)
Originating in 1317, King's Scholars at institutions like Eton College are students awarded foundation scholarships and housed within exclusive college residences, participating in unique traditions. Selection is through competitive examination. Similar scholarships exist at Westminster School and King's College Cambridge, often tied to academic excellence. King's Schools across England award these scholarships recognizing academic or musical talents, affording privileges including ecclesiastical benefits. Notable King's Scholars include prime ministers, academics, writers, and public figures.

# National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)
The National Merit Scholarship Program identifies high-achieving U.S. high school students via the PSAT/NMSQT. Competition advances through Commended, Semifinalist, Finalist, and Scholarship winner stages, with awards based on shared academic excellence, achievements, and potential. Approximately 8,000 scholarships are awarded annually, supported by NMSC funds and corporate sponsors. Criticisms include reliance on standardized testing and lack of transparency in selection processes. Top enrolling universities often attract substantial numbers of National Merit Scholars.

# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program & Biography
Edmund Muskie (1914–1996) was an influential American politician, serving as Maine’s governor, U.S. Senator, Vice Presidential candidate (1968), U.S. Secretary of State (1980–81), and environmental pioneer. Of Polish immigrant descent, Muskie’s career was marked by bipartisan leadership, environmental legislation (Clean Air and Water Acts), civil rights advocacy, and pivotal roles in foreign policy including the Iran hostage crisis. The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program supports Eurasian scholars with U.S. internships, honoring his legacy in public service, environmentalism, and diplomacy.

# Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev to promote Western education and values among Kazakhstani youth, the Bolashak Scholarship has awarded over 12,000 scholarships, enabling study at leading global universities across 32 countries. While predominantly awarded to ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements, recipients hold significant roles in Kazakh state and private sectors. The program continues to be a major driver of international educational exchange for Kazakhstan.

# Marshall Scholarship
Created in 1953 as a living memorial to General George C. Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship enables American students to pursue postgraduate study at any UK university. It emphasizes academic excellence, leadership potential, and ambassadorial qualities to strengthen UK-US relations. The scholarship is highly selective, has expanded over decades, and supports a wide range of disciplines. The Association of Marshall Scholars organizes forums and events fostering transatlantic dialogue. Distinguished alumni include Supreme Court justices, academics, and policymakers.

# UNCF (United Negro College Fund)
Founded in 1944, the UNCF is a nonprofit supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships, advocacy, and fundraising. It has aided thousands of African American students and notable alumni across diverse fields. Major fundraising efforts include televised telethons initiated by Lou Rawls and significant donations from philanthropists like MacKenzie Scott and Netflix founder Reed Hastings. Member institutions span across the U.S., providing educational opportunities and resources primarily for Black students.

# Fulbright Program
Established in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote peace and mutual understanding through educational exchange, the Fulbright Program facilitates scholar and student exchanges globally. It offers multiple grant types for graduate study, research, teaching, and professional development, administered in partnership with over 50 bi-national commissions. The program has supported prominent alumni across disciplines. Recent challenges include political interference allegations and program suspension in Russia and funding freezes in the U.S. The Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board oversees policy and selections.

# Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (German Academic Scholarship Foundation)
Founded in 1925 and reestablished in 1948, the Studienstiftung is Germany’s largest and most prestigious scholarship foundation, supporting outstanding students and doctoral candidates regardless of nationality. It selects scholars via school, university, competitions, testing, and nominations emphasizing excellence, initiative, and responsibility. The foundation offers financial aid, academic enrichment, and various internal and external scholarship opportunities. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, political leaders, scientists, and artists.

# British Council
Established in 1934 to promote British culture, education, and the English language internationally, the British Council operates in over 100 countries. It offers English teaching, examinations (including IELTS), cultural exchange programs, and educational cooperation. The Council has faced challenges, including closures in Russia and Syria, security incidents, and controversies over funding and operations. It supports arts, education partnerships (e.g., Connecting Classrooms), and digital initiatives. Governed as a public charity, it works closely with the UK government’s Foreign, Commonwealth, and Development Office.

# Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 by industrialist Koo Soh Bing during wartime Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship supports academically talented but financially disadvantaged Chinese students with continuous financial aid and extracurricular opportunities. It emphasized self-reliance and community service, establishing institutes like a refugee children's home and a medical aid fund staffed with scholar volunteers. The program was revived in the 1980s post-Cultural Revolution, expanding to multiple cities and schools. It remains a significant philanthropic educational legacy fostering leadership and social responsibility.

# Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 through Cecil Rhodes's will, the Rhodes Scholarship offers prestigious postgraduate study at Oxford University to outstanding international students, originally to strengthen British Empire unity and Anglo-American ties. It is highly selective based on academic excellence, character, leadership, and commitment to service. Though historically restricted by gender and race, it has progressively become inclusive. The scholarship has generated numerous influential leaders in politics, academia, and other fields. It has inspired various global scholarship programs and faced critiques over legacy and selection trends.

# Harkness Fellowship
Created in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund's Edward Harkness, the Harkness Fellowship initially fostered reciprocal scholarly exchange between the UK and the US and Commonwealth countries, now focusing exclusively on health care policy research. It offers prestigious year-long research fellowships at leading U.S. institutions with seminars and policy briefings. Funded by various foundations and governments, the program boasts notable alumni in diverse fields including science, arts, academia, and public service.

# Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Inspired by the landmark anthology *Daughters of Africa* (1992) edited by Margaret Busby, which compiles literary works by women of African descent worldwide, the New Daughters of Africa anthology (2019) extends this legacy, showcasing over 200 writers across generations. Celebrated for its cultural and literary impact, the collection has been praised for restoring marginalized voices to central literary and historical narratives. The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students, reinforcing the anthology’s commitment to education and representation.
