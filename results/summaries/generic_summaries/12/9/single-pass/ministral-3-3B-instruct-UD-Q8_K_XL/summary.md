### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (founded in 1925, revived in 1948) is a prestigious German scholarship program supporting high-achieving students and doctoral candidates in undergraduate, graduate, and postgraduate studies. It operates on merit-based criteria aligned with the **BAföG** system, offering financial and academic support, including stipends, health insurance subsidies, and mentorship. Admission is open to full-time students from Germany, EU, Switzerland, or abroad, with no citizenship or age restrictions. Scholarships are awarded through nomination by schools, professors, or competitions, with selection involving interviews and seminars. The program also supports doctoral candidates and offers specialized scholarships, such as the **KAUST-Studienstiftung** for STEM students and **Beethoven House** residencies for musicians.

**Key Features:**
- **Financial Support:** Monthly stipends (€300–€1,650) and need-based scholarships (up to €855).
- **Academic Support:** Summer schools, workshops, language courses, and mentoring.
- **Notable Alumni:** Includes Nobel laureates, scientists, politicians, and artists, such as **Manfred Eigen** (Nobel Prize in Chemistry) and **Reinhard Zimmermann** (legal historian and former president).

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
Established in 1987, the **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** supports **public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)** and their students. Originally founded to aid HBCUs, it now advocates for higher education in public HBCUs, raising over **$500 million** for scholarships, capacity-building, and institutional support. The fund operates as a **501(c)(3)** nonprofit, focusing on 55 member schools across the U.S., including **Howard University, Tuskegee University, and Spelman College**.

**Key Features:**
- **Member Schools:** 55 HBCUs (42 HBCUs, 3 Predominantly Black Institutions, 7 Historically Black Community Colleges).
- **Funding:** Scholarships, grants, and programs for students, faculty, and institutional development.
- **Leadership:** Former president **Harry L. Williams** and **Johnny C. Taylor Jr.** (current president).

### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded in **1939** by Koo, a philanthropist, the **Shuping Scholarship** provides financial and social support to underprivileged students in Shanghai and beyond. Initially established to help academically gifted students overcome poverty, the scholarship includes a library, science labs, vocational training, and healthcare. It has evolved into a comprehensive program supporting **37 secondary schools and 24 tertiary institutions** in China, with an emphasis on service to society. The scholarship’s legacy includes contributions to education, healthcare, and social welfare in China.

**Key Features:**
- **Origins:** Founded by Koo Soh Bing in 1939 to support poor but academically talented students.
- **Activities:** Library, science labs, vocational training, and healthcare services.
- **Legacy:** Over **6,000 scholars** have been awarded, many contributing to China’s development.

### **Bolashak Scholarship**

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is a **U.S. competition** for high school students based on **PSAT/NMSQT scores** and academic achievement. Established in **1955**, it awards **2,500 National Merit $2,500 Scholarships** annually, with additional scholarships from corporate sponsors. The program has **over 1.5 million entrants** each year, with **15,000 finalists** advancing to scholarship consideration. Notable alumni include **Bill Gates, Bill Clinton, and Ted Cruz**.

**Key Features:**
- **Selection:** Based on PSAT scores and academic records.
- **Awards:** $2,500 scholarships and additional corporate-funded awards.
- **Criticism:** Criticized for its reliance on standardized testing and lack of transparency in selection processes.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
The **Rhodes Scholarship** is one of the most prestigious international scholarships, established in **1902** by **Cecil Rhodes** to promote **British-American relations**. It supports **postgraduate studies** at **Oxford University** for students from over **64 countries**, with a focus on leadership, academic excellence, and public service. The scholarship includes **tuition, living expenses, and travel costs**. Notable alumni include **Bill Clinton, Nelson Mandela, and Barack Obama**.

**Key Features:**
- **Selection:** Highly competitive, with a **0.7% acceptance rate**.
- **Destinations:** Oxford University and other top institutions.
- **Controversies:** Criticized for its historical ties to colonialism and exclusion of women until **1977**.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** is a **Florida state-funded scholarship** for high school graduates, offering **tuition coverage** at public universities. Established in **1997**, it replaced the **HOPE Scholarship** and has faced criticism for **racial disparities** and **financial mismanagement**. The program has been restructured multiple times, with recent changes increasing scholarship requirements and reducing eligibility.

**Key Features:**
- **Levels:** Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Gold Seal Vocational, and Academic Top Scholar (ATS).
- **Eligibility:** Requires high SAT/ACT scores, GPA, and volunteer work.
- **Controversies:** Accused of **racial bias** and **financial mismanagement**.
