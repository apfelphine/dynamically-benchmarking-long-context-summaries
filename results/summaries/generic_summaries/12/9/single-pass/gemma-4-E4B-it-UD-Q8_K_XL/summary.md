# Summary of Scholarship and Educational Programs

## Studienstiftung
The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, originally founded in 1925 in Dresden, was dissolved in 1934 under National Socialism and reformed as a registered association in Cologne in 1948. It offers general scholarships for bachelor's and master's studies, providing both financial and academic support based on BAFöG criteria. Admission is highly competitive and granted through several channels: nomination by schools (requiring academic excellence and social commitment), nomination by professors, nomination by examination boards, winning national/federal competitions, self-nomination via an assessment test, nomination by alumni, or support for musicians/artists. Scholarship holders receive financial aid (€300 monthly, or up to €855 based on need) and academic support like summer schools and mentoring. It also provides doctoral scholarships and offers internal/open scholarship programs in cooperation with other organizations.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF advocates for public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). It has raised over $500 million for programmatic support and scholarships across its 55 member schools, including 42 HBCUs, 3 Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), and various community colleges. In 2013, TMCF acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC), maintaining a mission focused on service to the HBCU community, especially in talent identification and entrepreneurship.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, the scholarship was established to aid poor but academically strong students, embodying the motto "Return to the Society what you have gained from the society." Initially, it provided full tuition and extensive support through a scholars' library and activity center. After a hiatus, it was revived in 1986, and in 1995, Koo donated HK$10 million to the Shanghai Education Development Foundation to ensure its long-term continuation.

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)
Founded in 1944 by William J. Trent, UNCF supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), though its scholarships are open to all ethnicities. It has received significant donations, including $70 million from MacKenzie Scott in 2025. The organization operates through member colleges across the US, providing scholarships and promoting financial stability within the HBCU community.

## Harkness Fellowship
Initially established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships, the Harkness Fellowships were renamed in 1961. Since 1997, the focus has been limited to health care. Fellows spend a year researching at top American institutions like Harvard and Johns Hopkins, enhancing their understanding of the U.S. health care system while participating in policy briefings and symposia.

## Bolashak
Instituted in Kazakhstan in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev, the "Bolashak" scholarship aims to instill Western, democratic values in Kazakhstan's youth by sending them overseas for education. The program has grown significantly, granting thousands of scholarships, and students are encouraged to hold key positions in state and private organizations upon return.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award is connected to the anthology *Daughters of Africa*, which features over 200 women writers of African descent. The award, supported by the anthology’s earnings and contributions from contributors, provides accommodation for an African woman student to pursue her studies in London.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This US program uses the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT) as an initial screening tool. Students move through stages: Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist. Finalists are evaluated on academics, extracurriculars, and essays for scholarships. Critics have argued that relying solely on the PSAT/NMSQT is inadequate for measuring real-world achievement.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes’s will, the scholarships were initially intended to promote British imperial unity and diplomatic ties. The global acceptance rate is exceptionally low (0.7%). Selection criteria emphasize scholastic attainments, leadership potential, character, and courage. Historically, the scholarship has faced criticism regarding its colonialist origins, its exclusion of Black Africans, and the tendency of recipients to pursue careers in business rather than public service, though women were admitted in 1977.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Created in 1964 as a memorial to John F. Kennedy, this scholarship allows British post-graduate students to study at either Harvard or MIT. Selection criteria include intellectual attainment, readiness to express themselves, and suitability of the proposed study course. The program emphasizes potential for public service and is comparable in selection standards to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships.

## Fulbright Program
Founded in 1945 by Senator J. William Fulbright, its mission is to advance global understanding through educational exchange, promoting peace and friendship. It operates on a bi-national basis, offering various grants for U.S. persons to study abroad or foreign nationals to visit the U.S. The program's administration is managed by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida program, originally emulating Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, is based purely on academic merit, not financial need. It has undergone significant overhauls, with current levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) requiring specific GPA and standardized test scores (ACT/SAT/CLT) alongside volunteer or paid work hours.

## British Council
As a charity governed by a royal charter and sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, the British Council promotes English language knowledge and British culture globally. It runs major activities including joint operation of the IELTS test and various mobility programs like Education UK and Erasmus+. The organization has faced controversies regarding its closures in Russia and Syria, as well as concerns over outsourcing functions to the Indian diaspora.

## King's Scholar
This tradition originates from King Edward II establishing scholars at Cambridge in 1317. At Eton College, a King's Scholar passes an election and is admitted to the exclusive "College" house. The scholarship has evolved, moving from unconditional waivers to means-tested support, while the term is also used for high-achieving undergraduates at King's College Cambridge.

## Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953 to honor George C. Marshall, this program sends intellectually distinguished American future leaders to study in the UK. The objectives include fostering understanding between the UK and the US. Selection prioritizes intellectual distinction and character, with candidates needing university endorsement. While similar to the Rhodes and Fulbright programs, the Marshall scholarship is more flexible in its academic destinations within the UK.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
This program, supported by the U.S. Department of State and launched in 1992, provides summer internships and financial support to Eurasian scholars studying in the U.S. It was renamed and relaunched by Cultural Vistas in 2015, continuing its legacy of fostering international diplomatic and educational exchange.
