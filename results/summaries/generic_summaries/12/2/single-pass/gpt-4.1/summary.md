## Major International Scholarships Overview

### Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the Rhodes Scholarship is one of the oldest and most prestigious postgraduate awards, enabling students from over sixty countries to study at Oxford University. Recipients are selected for academic excellence, leadership, and commitment to service. Although historically restricted to men and to white British Empire citizens, the program gradually opened to women (after 1977) and expanded globally. The highly competitive process uses national quotas and rigorous interviews. Notable alumni include heads of state (Bill Clinton; three Australian and Canadian Prime Ministers), activists, scientists, and influential academics. The scholarship has faced criticism regarding its founder's colonial views, the historic exclusion of women and non-whites, and trends toward private sector careers over public service.

### Marshall Scholarship
Founded in 1953 as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the British government awards Marshall Scholarships to distinguished American graduates for postgraduate study at any UK university. Modeled as a sister program to Rhodes (but open from the beginning to women and married men), selection emphasizes intellectual distinction, leadership, and ambassadorial potential. About 40 awards are made annually. Scholars have gone on to prominent careers in politics, academia, law, business, and journalism. The program is seen as a symbol of U.S.–UK diplomacy and partnership.

### Fulbright Program
Launched in 1946, the U.S. government’s Fulbright Program provides mutual educational exchange and professional development between the United States and more than 160 countries. It includes student, scholar, teacher, professional, and doctoral grants, bilaterally managed via commissions or U.S. embassies. Awardees are chosen for academic or professional excellence, leadership, and cultural adaptability. The program has over 325,000 alumni, including Nobel laureates, heads of state, and other distinguished individuals. Recent years saw political interference and board resignations regarding ideological screening under the Trump administration.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964, the Kennedy Memorial Trust honors President John F. Kennedy by supporting British graduates pursuing postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. Recipients are chosen for intellect, leadership, and an ability to foster UK–US understanding. Kennedy Scholars have excelled in government, academia, business, journalism, and law.

### Harkness Fellowship
Beginning in 1925, Harkness Fellowships enable graduates from Commonwealth countries and Europe to pursue research or professional development in the United States, especially in health policy today. Fellows are selected for academic distinction and leadership. Alumni include scientists, academics, policy leaders, and creatives.

### Norway Scholarship
Initiated in 1919 to foster cultural and academic bridges, the Norway Scholarship allows students from the University of Oslo to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Despite funding difficulties over time, it remains an emblem of Anglo-Norwegian educational exchange and royal patronage.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes
Founded in 1925 (and reestablished post-WWII), Studienstiftung is Germany’s largest merit-based scholarship foundation for undergraduates, graduates, and doctoral students. Admission occurs via nomination, national competition, or direct application and targets exceptional academic and civic achievement. Recipients receive financial and academic support, and the alumni network includes numerous Nobel laureates and leading figures in German society.

### National Merit Scholarship Program
An American merit-based program using PSAT scores to select scholars, the National Merit program identifies top high school students and offers scholarships via competitive rounds, culminating in about 8,000 awards annually. Criticized for reliance on standardized tests, it nonetheless remains highly prestigious and can lead to significant university funding.

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund and UNCF
Both organizations support scholarships and capacity building at Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) in the U.S. TMCF, founded in 1987, advocates for public HBCUs and receives large-scale corporate and philanthropic support. UNCF, since 1944, has provided hundreds of millions in scholarships and institutional aid, now supporting its member colleges and broader equity in education.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program
Florida’s state merit program offers significant tuition support to high-achieving state residents. Over time, it changed award levels and qualification criteria, introducing higher test score cutoffs and restoring full tuition for top levels. Criticized for access inequities, it remains a key funding source for college-bound Floridians.

### Bolashak
Kazakhstan’s state-sponsored Bolashak scholarship, founded in 1993, sends high-achieving students abroad for postgraduate study at top universities, with the aim of advancing domestic expertise and Western democratic values. Most recipients are ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements.

### Shuping Scholarship
The Shuping Scholarship, founded in Shanghai (1939) by Koo Soh Bing, supports financially disadvantaged but academically strong students through secondary and postsecondary education. The program emphasizes community service and self-governance among scholars, contributing thousands of alumni to Chinese society.

## Prestigious National and Regional Scholarships

### King’s Scholars (UK)
Dating to 1317 at Cambridge and 1440 at Eton College, King’s (and Queen’s) Scholarships recognize the most academically gifted students, often awarded through competitive exams and carrying ceremonial privileges and, historically, financial support. Similar traditions exist at Westminster School and at King’s Schools in England.

## Notable Anthologies and Awards

### Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Linked to the acclaimed anthologies "Daughters of Africa" (1992) and "New Daughters of Africa" (2019), edited by Margaret Busby, this benefit scholarship, in partnership with SOAS, University of London, supports African women studying in the UK. The anthologies have been broadly influential in amplifying Black women’s literary voices from the ancient world to the present.
