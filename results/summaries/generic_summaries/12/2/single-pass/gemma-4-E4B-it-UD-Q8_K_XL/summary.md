## Miss America Pageant
The pageant originated from a 1919 event in New York City, but the modern "Miss America Pageant" began in Atlantic City, New Jersey, at "The Fall Frolic" in 1920, designed to boost local business. The term "Miss America" was coined by a "newspaperman." The competition faced a temporary shutdown during the Great Depression (1928–1932) and was later refined; in 1935, Lenora Slaughter was hired to direct it for 32 years. In 1944, compensation shifted from "furs and movie contracts" to college scholarships, largely credited to Jean Bartel.

The pageant has evolved significantly regarding representation and format. Racial segregation was enforced via Rule Seven until it was abolished in 1950. Key milestones include Bess Myerson becoming the first Jewish-American winner in 1945, and Cheryl Browne becoming the first African-American contestant in 1971. Vanessa Williams was the first Black woman to win in 1984, though her reign ended due to a scandal involving nude photos. In 2018, "Miss America 2.0" dropped physical appearance judging and the swimsuit competition. Subsequently, in 2023, many of those "Miss America 2.0" changes were reversed by new leadership.

## Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program's goal, articulated by Senator J. William Fulbright, is to foster international peace and friendship through educational exchange. It was established in 1945, initially with the aim of promoting understanding between the US and other nations following World War II. The program operates on a bi-national basis through agreements with partner countries.

The program offers various grants, including the Fulbright U.S. Student Program for Americans studying abroad, and the Fulbright Foreign Student Program for international scholars studying in the U.S. While the program has faced disruptions, such as the Russian government designating it "undesirable" in 2024, it remains a major global educational exchange vehicle.

## Marshall Scholarship
Founded in 1953 by the UK Parliament as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, the scholarship aims to promote close accord between the UK and the U.S. by enabling intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK. Its objectives include advancing knowledge in various fields, strengthening British-American understanding, and ensuring the personal fulfillment of each scholar.

The selection criteria emphasize intellectual distinction and character, though the program has evolved. It is noted for its prestige, with scholars having careers across academia and public service. The Association of Marshall Scholars hosts the Marshall Forum annually to discuss key transatlantic issues.

## British Council
Governed by royal charter, the British Council promotes British culture, the English language, and educational cooperation across over 100 countries. Its activities include offering face-to-face English teaching and running the global IELTS test jointly with Cambridge University Press.

The Council has faced numerous controversies, including closures in Russia due to tax regulations and diplomatic disputes, and criticism regarding its outsourcing of functions to India. It also has a history of dealing with security threats in locations like Kabul and Tripoli.

## Other Educational and Philanthropic Programs
Several other programs were detailed:
*   **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program:** A professional exchange program supported by the US Department of State for Eurasian scholars.
*   **Rhodes Scholarship:** Founded in 1902, this highly selective scholarship promotes unity within the British Empire and strengthened diplomatic ties with the US. It has faced ongoing criticism regarding Cecil Rhodes's colonialist views and the career paths of its alumni.
*   **Harkness Fellowship:** Established in 1925 to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships, this program is now specialized in health care policy and practice, hosting fellows at major American institutions.
*   **Kennedy Scholarship:** Established in memory of John F. Kennedy after his assassination, this scholarship supports postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT, emphasizing intellectual attainment and commitment to public service.
*   **Shuping Scholarship:** Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo to aid poor, academically capable students, its motto stresses "Return to the Society what you have gained from it."
*   **Bright Futures Scholarship Program:** A Florida-based merit scholarship program that has undergone several overhauls and controversies regarding its reliance on standardized testing and racial bias.
*   **Studienstiftung:** A German scholarship offering both financial and academic support for Bachelor's and Master's studies, based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility."
*   **UNCF:** Founded in 1944, this fund supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), operating under the motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

## Summary of Selection Programs
In summary, major international scholarship programs—including the **Marshall**, **Rhodes**, **Fulbright**, and **Kennedy** scholarships—share similarities in seeking high academic achievement, leadership potential, and global impact. However, they differ in their specific focus, such as the Marshall's focus on the UK/US relationship, the Rhodes's focus on the British Empire's legacy, and the Harkness's modern focus on healthcare policy. Programs like **Bright Futures** and the **National Merit Scholarship Program** are domestically focused, emphasizing standardized testing, while the **Studienstiftung** prioritizes initiative and responsibility within German educational structures.
