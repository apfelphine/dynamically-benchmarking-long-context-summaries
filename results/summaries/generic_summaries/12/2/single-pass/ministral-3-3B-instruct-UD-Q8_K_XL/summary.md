### **Miss America**

#### **Origins and Evolution**
The **Miss America Pageant** began in 1919 as a beauty contest in New York, later evolving into a formal pageant in Atlantic City, New Jersey. Initially, it aimed to promote tourism and business, but it soon became a cultural and political symbol. The pageant faced financial struggles during the Great Depression (1928–1932) and was temporarily halted. In 1933, it resumed with a focus on scholarships, shifting from beauty contests to academic and talent competitions.

#### **Key Milestones and Controversies**
- **Racial Segregation (1940s):** The pageant was racially segregated, with Rule 7 banning contestants from non-white backgrounds until 1950.
- **Civil Rights Movement (1960s):** Protests emerged during the 1960s, criticizing the pageant’s sexism, racism, and militaristic undertones. The first Black contestant, Cheryl Browne, competed in 1971.
- **Notable Winners:** Vanessa Williams (1984) became the first Black Miss America, while Nina Davuluri (2014) was the first Indian-American winner. The pageant faced backlash for social media harassment against Davuluri.
- **Reforms:** In 2018, the pageant adopted **"Miss America 2.0"**, eliminating the swimsuit competition and focusing on talent, scholarships, and inclusivity. These changes were later reversed in 2023.

#### **Legacy and Modern Challenges**
The pageant remains a cultural icon, though it has faced criticism for perpetuating outdated norms. Recent efforts emphasize diversity, scholarships, and professional development, reflecting broader societal shifts.

### **Fulbright Program**

#### **History and Purpose**
Founded in 1945 by Senator J. William Fulbright, the program promotes international exchange through scholarships, fostering peace and mutual understanding. It supports students, scholars, and professionals in studying or researching abroad, with bilateral partnerships managed by commissions in each country.

#### **Program Structure**
- **Student Grants:** Fulbright U.S. Student Program offers fellowships for U.S. students to study abroad, while the Fulbright Foreign Student Program funds international students in the U.S.
- **Scholar Grants:** Supports U.S. faculty and professionals abroad, as well as visiting scholars from other nations.
- **Teacher Grants:** Supports one-to-one exchanges of teachers between K–12 schools.
- **Grants for Professionals:** Includes programs like the Hubert H. Humphrey Program for mid-career professionals.

#### **Recent Challenges**
- **Political Interference:** In 2025, the Trump administration froze Fulbright funding, impacting thousands of participants.
- **Russian Restrictions:** Russia designated the Institute of International Education (IIE) as "undesirable," halting Fulbright activities in the country.

#### **Notable Alumni**
Fulbright alumni include Nobel laureates, U.S. senators, and leaders in academia, industry, and diplomacy.

### **Marshall Scholarship**

#### **Founding and Objectives**
Established in 1953 as a memorial to George Marshall, the scholarship supports U.S. students to study in the UK. Its goals include fostering understanding between the U.S. and UK, advancing knowledge in science, humanities, and arts, and promoting public service.

#### **Selection Criteria**
- **Academic Excellence:** High GPAs and strong recommendations.
- **Leadership Potential:** Demonstrated commitment to public service and intellectual growth.
- **Motivation:** Clear academic and career plans.

#### **Scholarship Terms**
- **Duration:** Two years, with optional one-year extensions.
- **Funding:** Covers tuition, living expenses, and maintenance stipends.

#### **Comparison to Other Scholarships**
Similar to the Rhodes and Fulbright programs, the Marshall Scholarship is highly selective, with an acceptance rate of ~3.4%. It is open to U.S. citizens only, unlike the Rhodes Scholarship.

### **British Council**

#### **History and Mission**
The British Council, established in 1934, promotes British culture, education, and language globally. It operates through offices in over 100 countries, offering English classes, exams (e.g., IELTS), and cultural exchange programs.

#### **Key Activities**
- **English and Examinations:** Offers IELTS and other English-language tests.
- **Cultural Exchange:** Programs like the Peacekeeping English Project train military personnel.
- **Education Initiatives:** Supports international student mobility through platforms like Education UK.

#### **Controversies**
- **Criticism of Outsourcing:** Concerns over reliance on Indian diaspora influence in policy-making.
- **Protests and Closures:** Attacks on its offices in Afghanistan, Libya, and Russia, as well as controversies over human rights and transparency.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
A professional exchange program supported by the U.S. Department of State, offering internships, career training, and financial support to Eurasian scholars and graduate students. Named after former U.S. Senator Edmund Muskie, it emphasizes global cooperation and professional development.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
#### **Founding and Legacy**
Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the scholarship supports postgraduate study in the UK. It is one of the most prestigious international scholarships, known for its rigorous selection process.

#### **Selection Criteria**
- **Academic Excellence:** High literary and scholastic attainments.
- **Character Traits:** Truth, courage, and devotion to duty.
- **Leadership:** Potential to contribute to society.

#### **Controversies**
- **Exclusion of Women:** Initially open only to men; women admitted in 1977.
- **Criticism of Colonialism:** Debates over Rhodes’s racist legacy and the scholarship’s impact on global education.

### **King’s Scholar**
A prestigious scholarship at **King’s College Cambridge** and **Eton College**, awarded to undergraduates with exceptional academic records. King’s Scholars at Eton are known for their academic gowns and leadership roles.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
An anthology and award celebrating the work of women of African descent. The award honors contributions to literature, arts, and social justice, emphasizing diversity and representation in African-American writing.

### **Norway Scholarship**
A scholarship program enabling Norwegian students to study in the UK, established in 1919. It supports academic and cultural exchange, with a focus on fostering international understanding.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
A prestigious fellowship for health care policy and practice, supporting researchers and professionals in the U.S. and abroad. It promotes collaboration and policy innovation in healthcare.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
Created in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship allows British students to study at Harvard or MIT. It emphasizes leadership, public service, and academic excellence.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded by Koo Soh Bing in 1939, this scholarship supports students from poor families in China, promoting education and philanthropy. It has expanded to include scholarships for tertiary institutions and extracurricular activities.

---

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
A Florida state-funded scholarship program offering merit-based aid to high school graduates. It includes four levels of funding (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) and has faced criticism for racial disparities and funding cuts.

---

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
A German scholarship program supporting outstanding students and doctoral candidates. It emphasizes academic excellence, social engagement, and leadership, with a focus on fostering future leaders in Germany.

---

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
A U.S. program recognizing high-achieving high school students. It offers scholarships based on PSAT/NMSQT scores, academic records, and extracurricular activities. Controversies include criticism of its reliance on standardized tests.

---

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
A nonprofit supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships, capacity-building, and entrepreneurship programs. It advocates for equity in higher education.

### **Bolashak**
A Kazakhstani scholarship program enabling students to study abroad. It promotes Western democratic values and fosters international exchange, though it has faced criticism for limited implementation of reforms.

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
A nonprofit supporting historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) through scholarships, fundraising, and advocacy. It has received significant donations, including from MacKenzie Scott and Netflix founder Reed Hastings.
