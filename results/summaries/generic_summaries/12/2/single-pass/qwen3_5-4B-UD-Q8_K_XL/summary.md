# **Concise Summary**

## **Miss America**
The "Miss America" title originated from a 1921 pageant in Atlantic City, evolving from a "bather's revue" into a formal beauty competition. Under the long-term directorship of Lenora Slaughter, the pageant became racially segregated until the rule was abolished in 1950. It faced significant protests in 1968 regarding its perceived support of militarism and sexism, leading to the first major demonstrations. The pageant later faced internal scandals involving CEO Sam Haskell, which prompted a 2018 format overhaul known as "Miss America 2.0." This version eliminated the swimsuit competition and physical appearance judging, though these changes were reversed in 2023 under new leadership. The pageant has seen diverse representation, including the first African-American winner in 1984 (Vanessa Williams) and the first Indian-American winner in 2014 (Nina Davuluri).

## **International Education Scholarships**
The **Fulbright Program**, established in 1945, promotes peace through educational exchange between the U.S. and other nations. It is administered by the Institute of International Education and operates bi-nationally. In 2025, the program faced disruptions due to a funding freeze by the Trump administration and the Russian government designating the administering bodies as "undesirable," effectively ending the program in Russia.

The **Marshall Scholarship** (1953) and **Rhodes Scholarship** (1902) are prestigious postgraduate fellowships for the U.S. and global students, respectively, aimed at fostering international understanding. The Marshall Scholarship, available to American citizens, is coeducational and focuses on British universities, while the Rhodes Scholarship is highly selective and open to students worldwide. Both programs have faced modern controversies regarding colonial legacies and the exclusion of women in their early histories. The **Kennedy Scholarship** honors U.S. and British leaders by funding British students at Harvard or MIT, and the **Harkness Fellowship** supports international health policy research in the U.S.

## **Domestic and Regional Scholarships**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida provides merit-based funding, though it faced criticism for racial disparities and was significantly cut in 2011. The **National Merit Scholarship Program** selects high-achieving U.S. students via the PSAT/NMSQT, offering substantial awards to finalists. The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund** supports Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) through partnerships with major corporations.

Other notable programs include the **Shuping Scholarship**, founded in 1939 in Shanghai by Koo Soh Bing to support students from poor families, which emphasizes service to society. The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is Germany's largest scholarship program, providing financial and academic support to outstanding students. The **Bolashak** program, initiated by Kazakhstan in 1993, sends Kazakh youth to study abroad to inculcate Western democratic values, with a focus on returning graduates to national service. The **UNCF** (United Negro College Fund) continues to fund African American students at member institutions, relying heavily on major philanthropic donations.
