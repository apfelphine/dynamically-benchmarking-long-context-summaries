# Scholarships and Awards: Global Overview

---

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)

Founded in 1944 to address educational funding inequities for African Americans, the UNCF provides scholarships open to all ethnicities, though most recipients remain African American. Notable alumni include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson. The organization has received significant donations, such as MacKenzie Scott's $70 million gift in 2025, and runs high-profile fundraisers like the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars telethon, which has raised over $200 million. Its motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste," is one of the most recognized advertising slogans. UNCF's member institutions are primarily historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) across the U.S., supporting thousands of students each year.

---

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)

Established in 1993, the Bolashak ("Future") scholarship enables Kazakh students to study at leading global universities with aims to expose them to Western values and expertise. Despite its ostensibly open criteria, over 90% of recipients are ethnic Kazakh due to language requirements. Since its inception, over 12,800 scholarships have been awarded. The program partners with a wide array of universities across multiple countries covering all disciplines.

---

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)

Founded in 1987, TMCF provides institutional support and scholarships primarily for public HBCUs. The organization has raised over $500 million and supports 55 member schools, including several law and medical schools. Notable funding includes major grants from Apple and the Koch Foundation. TMCF merged with the Opportunity Funding Corporation to enhance entrepreneurship education and talent identification.

---

## National Merit Scholarship Program (U.S.)

This highly selective program recognizes top high school students via the PSAT/NMSQT, progressing them through Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist stages. About 8,000 students annually receive scholarships, funded mainly by NMSC and corporate sponsors, with many universities offering additional merit aid to recipients. A separate National Achievement Scholarship for Black students ran until 2015, after which funds were redirected to UNCF for college graduate awards. Critics have noted the sole use of standardized testing and transparency issues.

---

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)

Established in 1925 (re-founded in 1948), Studienstiftung is Germany’s largest scholarship foundation, supporting outstanding undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students irrespective of nationality or age. Admission is highly competitive, through nominations, competitions, and direct applications. Scholars receive substantial financial and academic support as well as opportunities for further research and networking. The foundation supports about 14,800 scholars annually, including notable figures across various disciplines.

---

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, U.S.)

This merit-based state program funds postsecondary education for Florida high school graduates at varying award levels, based on academic achievement and standardized test scores. Established in the late 1990s, it has undergone changes in eligibility criteria and award amounts, with a return to higher funding in recent years. The program remains pivotal in supporting Florida students, though has faced criticism over equity and funding sources.

---

## Shuping Scholarship (China)

Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing to support academically excellent but financially needy students during wartime, the Shuping Scholarship provides comprehensive support, including tuition, extracurricular activities, and medical care. It resumed in 1986 after a hiatus, expanding its reach to thousands across secondary and tertiary institutions. The foundation is governed by a committee of alumni, maintaining its tradition of student-run administration.

---

## Kennedy Scholarship (UK/USA)

Created as part of Britain's memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship annually selects outstanding UK students for postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. Selection is highly competitive and focuses on intellectual achievement and public service potential. Notable alumni have achieved prominence in government, economics, academia, and journalism.

---

## Harkness Fellowship

Originated in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, Harkness Fellowships facilitate research and professional exchange, now focused on healthcare policy with fellows from several countries spending a year at top U.S. institutions. Notable alumni include leading academics, scientists, and public figures.

---

## Norway Scholarship

Since 1920, the Norway Scholarship has enabled students from the University of Oslo to study at Oxford’s Wadham College. The fund has supported a diverse range of studies and is supported by Norwegian and British institutional partnerships.

---

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

Inspired by Busby’s anthologies celebrating the literary work of women of African descent, the scholarship benefits African women students at SOAS, University of London. The award and the anthologies have been widely influential, promoting visibility and recognition for hundreds of writers.

---

## King's Scholar (UK)

Originating in the 14th century, King's Scholarships are historic awards for academic excellence, granted by institutions such as Eton College and King's College Cambridge. Scholars receive prestige, privileges, and in some cases financial benefits, with many going on to distinguished careers.

---

## Rhodes Scholarship

Founded in 1902 for study at Oxford, the Rhodes Scholarship fosters cross-cultural academic exchange and leadership. It remains one of the most competitive and prestigious awards globally, with alumni including heads of state, activists, scientists, and writers. The program has evolved to address past exclusions and now selects scholars from over 60 countries.

---

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

Named after the prominent U.S. politician and Secretary of State, the Muskie Program provides internships and professional development for Eurasian graduate students in the United States, focused on fostering cross-cultural exchange and leadership.

---

## British Council

Established in 1934, the British Council promotes UK education, English language, and cultural exchange internationally, often through partnerships, mobility programs (e.g., Erasmus+), and arts initiatives. It operates in over 100 countries and administers key exams such as IELTS. The Council has faced closures and controversies but remains a major vehicle for UK international engagement.

---

## Marshall Scholarship

Created in 1953 as a living memorial to Secretary of State George C. Marshall, this scholarship enables top U.S. graduates to study at any UK university. It emphasizes academic achievement, leadership, and transatlantic understanding. The program is highly selective and has produced leaders in academia, government, science, and business.

---

## Fulbright Program

Established in 1946, Fulbright is the world’s largest international educational exchange program, fostering global understanding through scholarships for research, study, and teaching in over 160 countries. It offers grants for students, scholars, professionals, and teachers. Fulbright alumni include Nobel laureates and heads of state. The program operates through bilateral commissions and has faced recent political and funding challenges.

---

## Miss America

Since 1921, the Miss America pageant has undergone significant changes, evolving from a bathing beauty revue into a scholarship competition emphasizing talent, intelligence, and social impact. It became a platform for American women's issues, diversity, and activism, with recent shifts in format and leadership. The organization offers substantial scholarships and remains a significant cultural fixture in the United States.
