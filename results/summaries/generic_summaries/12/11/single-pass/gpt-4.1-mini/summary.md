# Scholarships and Educational Programs

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)
Founded in 1944 to address educational funding inequities for African Americans, UNCF provides scholarships to students regardless of ethnicity, with most recipients being African American. It supports students attending member colleges and beyond. Notable alumni include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson. The organization was established by educational leaders to collectively raise funds for historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). Significant donations include $70 million from MacKenzie Scott in 2025 to sustain HBCUs. UNCF's notable fundraising events include the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars telethon, which raised over $200 million by 2006. The motto “A mind is a terrible thing to waste,” coined in 1972, remains widely recognized. The organization supports numerous member institutions across multiple states, all of which are primarily HBCUs.

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Instituted in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s first president, the Bolashak program sends students abroad to study Western values and ideas, primarily funding Kazakh ethnicity students due to language requirements. By 2018, over 12,800 scholarships awarded. Recipients often occupy leadership roles in Kazakhstan. The program offers study opportunities at leading universities worldwide across 32 countries.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs, TMCF has raised over $500 million for scholarships and institutional support for 55 member schools. It differs from UNCF by focusing on public institutions. The fund has received major grants from Apple, Charles Koch Foundation, and Boeing. In 2013, it merged with Opportunity Funding Corporation to enhance entrepreneurship education among HBCUs.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
A U.S. competition primarily based on PSAT/NMSQT scores to recognize academic excellence of high school juniors. About 1.5 million enter annually; recognitions include Commended, Semifinalist, Finalist, and Scholarship winner levels. The National Achievement Scholarship Program, originally for Black American students, was merged into UNCF's Achievement Capstone Program in 2016. The program faces criticism for relying solely on standardized test scores and lacking transparency.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Founded in 1925 and re-established in 1948, it offers scholarship support (financial and academic) for German and international students based on excellence and social responsibility. Admission is via nominations by schools, professors, competitions, tests, and alumni. Scholarships include allowances and extensive academic programs. As of 2023, nearly 15,000 scholars participate, representing about 0.48% of German students. Notable alumni include multiple Nobel laureates, scholars, politicians, and artists.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)
Established to reward academic merit among Florida high school graduates, it originally covered 100% of tuition but underwent cuts from 2011 due to budget constraints, disproportionately affecting minority students. The program funds four scholarship levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC), each with specific GPA, test score, course, and service/work requirements. The program has faced investigations for racial bias and periodically revised eligibility and award amounts.

## Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 by businessman Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai to support academically excellent but financially disadvantaged students, the scholarship included additional support such as free medical care and extracurricular activities. It fostered community service among scholars. After a hiatus during political turmoil, the program relaunched in 1986 and now awards over 700 scholarships annually across numerous secondary schools and tertiary institutions in China. The foundation manages funds and alumni associations supporting the program.

## Kennedy Scholarship (UK)
Established in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, it offers scholarships to British graduate students to study at Harvard or MIT. Trustees include prominent academics and former scholars; selection emphasizes academic excellence, public service potential, and leadership. The Kennedy Scholarship forms part of a broader memorial including a permanent site at Runnymede, England. Notable scholars span government, academia, journalism, arts, and law.

## Harkness Fellowship
Founded in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowships were designed to reciprocate Rhodes Scholarships by enabling British and Commonwealth graduates to study in the U.S. Since 1997, the focus is on health care policy research. Fellows conduct year-long research at leading U.S. institutions, supported by seminars and international briefings. Notable alumni include leading academics, artists, and policymakers.

# Scholarship Foundations and Programs

## Norway Scholarship
Established in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford’s Wadham College, funded initially by Arthur Jayne. The program faced funding challenges but was revitalized in the late 20th century. Scholars have studied diverse subjects. The scholarship has seen intermittent awarding and includes notable alumni contributing to Norway’s academic and cultural sectors.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Originating from the 1992 anthology *Daughters of Africa* edited by Margaret Busby, the award supports African women writers and students. The anthology, and its 2019 companion, showcase diverse black women writers globally and have received critical acclaim for their literary and cultural impact. The award honors emerging African women talents, with partnerships supporting scholarships and literacy initiatives.

# Historic and Prestigious Scholarships

## King’s Scholars
Dating back to 1317 at Cambridge University and formalized with the founding of Eton College in 1440, King’s Scholars are students awarded scholarships based on academic examination and merit. At Eton, they reside in the historic “College” house, distinguished by black academic gowns, and have unique traditions and privileges. Other King's and Queen's Scholarships exist at Westminster, Cambridge, and several historic King's Schools, with ceremonial roles and lifetime affiliations.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 via Cecil Rhodes’s will to promote unity across the British Empire and ties between the UK, USA, and Germany, it is one of the world’s most prestigious postgraduate awards. While originally for male students only, women were admitted starting 1977. Scholarships support postgraduate study at Oxford across many constituencies worldwide. The program has faced criticism for colonialist legacy and demographic exclusions but remains highly selective. Alumni include heads of state, Nobel laureates, and leaders in various fields.

## Marshall Scholarship
Created in 1953 to commemorate U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall and strengthen UK–US ties. It provides scholarships to American students for graduate study in any UK university. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and cultural engagement. The scholarship is flexible in duration and university choice, with about 50 awards annually. The Association of Marshall Scholars hosts forums to discuss transatlantic relations. Alumni have contributed notably in academics, politics, and the arts.

## Fulbright Program
Established in 1946 by U.S. Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international peace and understanding through educational exchange. It funds U.S. students and scholars to study abroad and foreign candidates to study in the U.S. The program includes diverse grants for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals across many disciplines and countries. Fulbright commissions in participating countries oversee local administration. It is the largest and one of the most prestigious exchange programs globally, although it has recently faced political interference and restrictions in some countries.

# Other Notable Programs and Foundations

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
Named after Edmund Muskie, a prominent American politician and environmental advocate, the program offers summer internships and career training for Eurasian scholars studying in the U.S. Established by U.S. Congress in 1992, it fosters professional exchange and leadership development.

## British Council
Founded in 1934 as a cultural promotion and educational exchange organization of the UK government. It operates globally in over 100 countries, promoting English language, arts, education, and cultural understanding. The British Council administers English tests (including IELTS), educational programs like Erasmus+, and cultural initiatives. It has faced political challenges, controversies over outsourcing and operations in certain countries, and security incidents. Its activities include teacher training, international student recruitment, arts and cultural exchanges, and leadership programs.
