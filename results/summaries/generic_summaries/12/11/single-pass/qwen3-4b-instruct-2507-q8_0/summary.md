### **UNCF (United Negro College Fund)**
- Founded in 1944 to address educational inequities for African Americans, UNCF administers scholarships open to all ethnicities, with the majority of recipients being African American.
- Supports students at member historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), and has produced notable alumni in business, politics, health, and the arts (e.g., Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, Dr. David Satcher).
- In 2008, shifted from using its full name to initials (UNCF) to reflect changing attitudes toward the term "Negro."
- In 2025, received a $70 million donation from MacKenzie Scott to promote financial stability and long-term sustainability for HBCUs.
- Hosts fundraising events like the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars," which has raised over $200 million since 1980.

### **Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)**
- Established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev to promote Western democratic values among Kazakh youth.
- Initially offered to students in Kazakhstan, with 12,831 awards by 2018.
- Emphasis on academic excellence and leadership, with students often not given authority to implement change.
- 93.6% of applicants were Kazakh, with a strong requirement for proficiency in the Kazakh language.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
- Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs, with a mission to promote access to higher education for Black students.
- Raised over $500 million for scholarships, capacity building, and programmatic support.
- Member schools include 55 public HBCUs and PBI institutions.
- Received major donations from Apple, the Charles Koch Foundation, and Boeing.
- Advocates for public HBCUs and supports entrepreneurship and leadership development.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
- A prestigious academic competition for high school seniors based on PSAT/NMSQT scores.
- Students are recognized as "Commended," "Semifinalists," or "Finalists," with Finalists eligible for merit-based scholarships.
- The program includes a National Achievement Scholarship Program (ended in 2015) that supported Black American youth.
- Over 4 million African Americans participated, with over 34,000 receiving awards.
- Criticized for relying on a single standardized test and lack of transparency in selection processes.

### **Studienstiftung (Germany)**
- A German scholarship foundation established in 1948 to support students in higher education.
- Offers both general and doctoral scholarships with financial and academic support.
- Applicants must study full-time and demonstrate excellence, initiative, and responsibility.
- Scholarships include allowances, subsidies, and academic support such as summer schools and mentoring.
- Notable alumni include Nobel laureates and prominent figures in science, politics, and business.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)**
- Created to emulate Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship, providing academic merit-based funding to Florida students.
- Awarded in "A" and "B" levels, with vocational scholarships for trade schools.
- Criticized for subsidizing wealthier students and for racial disparities in access.
- In 2018, restructured with new levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) and added the Academic Top Scholar award.
- Adjusted requirements and funding levels due to criticism and political pressure.

### **Shuping Scholarship (China)**
- Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing to support poor students from excellent academic backgrounds.
- Aims to foster service to society and community, with scholars engaging in charitable work.
- Recipients are encouraged to participate in volunteer work, community projects, and social service.
- The scholarship is now administered by the Shuping Scholarship Foundation, with a focus on helping the poor and disadvantaged.
- Over 6,000 scholars have been awarded, with many becoming pillars in China’s modernization.

### **Kennedy Scholarship (UK)**
- Established in 1964 to honor John F. Kennedy, enabling British students to study at Harvard or MIT.
- Selection process is comparable to Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships, emphasizing intellectual attainment and leadership.
- Over 600 scholars have been awarded; notable recipients include Ed Balls, Dame Kate Bingham, and David Miliband.
- The program promotes public service and global leadership.

### **Harkness Fellowship (Health Care Policy)**
- Founded in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund to support British graduates studying in the U.S.
- Now focused exclusively on health care policy and practice, with a 12-month tenure.
- Fellows conduct research at U.S. institutions and attend seminars on healthcare policy.
- Notable alumni include David Armitage, Sir Ronald Bottrall, and Professor Sir David Wallace.
- Funded and administered by the Commonwealth Fund with support from international partners.

### **Norway Scholarship**
- Established in 1919 to allow Norwegian students to study at Oxford.
- Aims to foster cultural and educational exchange between Norway and the UK.
- Scholarships are awarded based on academic performance and commitment to service.
- Past scholars include prominent figures in law, medicine, and politics.
- The program has been restructured and expanded, with increased funding and support.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
- A scholarship established in 2019 to support African women in higher education.
- Named after author Margaret Busby, the anthology "Daughters of Africa" was foundational to the initiative.
- Awarded to African women students, with a focus on leadership, scholarship, and community service.
- First recipients were Idza Luhumyo and Stella Gichohi from Kenya.
- The award is part of a broader effort to promote African women’s voices and representation in education.

### **King's Scholar (UK)**
- A prestigious scholarship at Eton College and King’s College Cambridge.
- At Eton, King’s Scholars are selected through competitive exams and receive a foundation scholarship.
- At King’s College Cambridge, scholars receive a £350 prize and attend a formal ceremony.
- Notable scholars include John Maynard Keynes, Harold Macmillan, and Boris Johnson.
- The program emphasizes leadership, academic excellence, and service.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
- Founded in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes to promote unity within the British Empire.
- Open to students from over 20 countries, with a focus on leadership and public service.
- Highly selective, with an overall acceptance rate of 0.7%.
- Notable recipients include Nelson Mandela, Bill Clinton, and Barack Obama.
- Controversies include exclusion of women and Black Africans, and criticism of Rhodes’s colonialist views.
- The program continues to promote global leadership and cross-cultural understanding.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
- A U.S. Department of State program supporting Eurasian scholars and graduate students.
- Provides summer internships, career training, and financial support.
- Established in 1992 and restructured in 2015 by Cultural Vistas.
- Focuses on promoting international understanding and cultural exchange.

### **British Council**
- A global cultural and educational organization promoting English language and cultural exchange.
- Operates in over 100 countries, offering teaching, examinations (e.g., IELTS), and cultural programs.
- Faces controversies over outsourcing, political interference, and attacks in conflict zones (e.g., Kabul, Tripoli).
- Criticized for its role in cultural diplomacy and educational access, particularly in India and Russia.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
- Established in 1953 to promote U.S.-UK relations and support future leaders.
- Open to American citizens and emphasizes leadership, academic excellence, and public service.
- Selection process is rigorous, with a 3.4% acceptance rate.
- Notable recipients include Stephen Breyer, Thomas Friedman, and Nannerl Keohane.
- The program fosters transatlantic understanding and leadership in science, policy, and the arts.

### **Fulbright Program**
- A U.S. international exchange program promoting mutual understanding through education.
- Offers scholarships for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals in over 160 countries.
- Highly prestigious, with over 325,000 alumni, including Nobel laureates and Pulitzer Prize winners.
- Criticized for political interference and lack of transparency in recent years.
- The program was suspended in Russia in 2024 due to political tensions.

### **Miss America**
- A beauty pageant founded in 1920, evolving from a "bather's revue" to a national competition.
- Historically segregated by race, with African-American contestants not appearing until 1970.
- In 2018, adopted "Miss America 2.0," eliminating swimsuit and appearance-based judging.
- Controversies include racism, xenophobia, and criticism of scholarship claims.
- Recent winners include Nina Davuluri (2014), the first Indian-American, and Brían Nguyen (2024), the first transgender titleholder.
