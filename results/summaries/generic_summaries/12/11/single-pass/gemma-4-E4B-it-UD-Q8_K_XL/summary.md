# Educational and Philanthropic Organizations

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
The UNCF was founded in 1944 by William J. Trent, Frederick D. Patterson, and Mary McLeod Bethune to address funding inequities for African Americans in education. While its scholarships are open to all ethnicities, the majority of recipients are African-American. The UNCF has a distinguished alumni base, including figures such as Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and Spike Lee. Major financial support has included a \$70 million donation from MacKenzie Scott in 2025, and the organization uses the motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste." UNCF maintains a list of member Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) across various states, including Spelman College and Howard University.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF advocates for higher education at public HBCUs. It has raised over \$500 million for programmatic support, capacity building, and scholarships. TMCF acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) in 2013, enhancing its focus on talent identification and entrepreneurship within the HBCU community.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This program uses the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT) as an initial screen for over 1.5 million entrants. The process moves through Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist levels. Of the 15,000 Finalists, about 8,000 receive Merit Scholarship awards. Historically, the program also included the National Achievement Scholarship Program, which transitioned its funds to the UNCF.

## Harkness Fellowship
Initiated in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness, this fellowship aimed to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships by enabling British graduates to study in the U.S. It was renamed in 1961. Since 1997, the Harkness Fellowships have been specialized in health care policy and practice, with Fellows conducting research at major American institutions.

## Fulbright Program
Established in 1945 following WWII, the Fulbright Program seeks to foster peace and understanding through international educational exchange. It offers various grants, including the Fulbright Degree Program (for international graduate students in the U.S.) and the Fulbright U.S. Student Program (for U.S. students studying abroad). The program is managed by the Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs (ECA) of the U.S. Department of State.

## Marshall Scholarship
Created as a memorial to George C. Marshall, this program enables intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK. Its objectives include strengthening British-American understanding and advancing knowledge in various fields. Selection favors candidates with high academic achievement and leadership potential, and it is notable for its flexibility, allowing scholars to study at any British university.

## Rhodes Scholarship
The Rhodes Trust established the scholarships in 1902 to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. Today, it is open globally, focusing on postgraduate studies. Selection criteria include literary and scholastic attainments, coupled with traits like truth, courage, and a devotion to duty. The scholarship faces ongoing criticism regarding Cecil Rhodes's colonialist views.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 following JFK's assassination, this memorial scholarship funds studies at either Harvard or MIT. The selection process involves a national competition, and trustees evaluate candidates based on intellectual attainment, readiness to express themselves, and the suitability of their proposed course of study.

# International Cultural and Literary Works

## Daughters of Africa
Margaret Busby's anthology, *Daughters of Africa*, published in 1992, is a landmark collection featuring over 200 women writers of African descent from across the diaspora. It is praised for demonstrating the breadth and richness of black women's writing across centuries. A companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa* (2019), expanded this canon, and a related award benefits African women students.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, this scholarship adheres to the motto, "Return to the Society what you have gained from it." It supports students from poor families with good academic standing. The scholarship, revived in 1986, emphasizes not just financial support but also character building through voluntary charitable works.

## British Council
Governed by a royal charter and sponsored by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, the British Council promotes UK culture and the English language globally in over 100 countries. Activities include running IELTS exams and mobility programs like Erasmus+. The organization faces controversies regarding outsourcing functions to the Indian diaspora and the closure of operations in Russia and Syria.

# Specific Programs and Awards

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida program awards scholarships based on academic merit, currently featuring four levels: Florida Academic Scholars (FAS) and Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS). The program has seen several overhauls and controversies regarding racial bias. Renewal requirements mandate maintaining minimum GPA standards for each level.

## King's Scholars
The tradition of King's Scholars dates back to 1317 at Cambridge. At Eton College, a King's Scholar (Colleger) is a distinguished student in the central College, receiving foundation scholarship benefits. The term is also used for undergraduates at King's College Cambridge who attain first-class degrees, receiving a stipend and a special dinner.

## Norway Scholarship
Conceived in 1919 by Arthur Jayne, this scholarship facilitates one-year study for Norwegian students at Wadham College in Oxford. After periods of funding inadequacy, the scholarship has been supported by efforts to raise supplementary grants, and it maintains a history of supporting varied academic disciplines.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
This program, supported by the U.S. Department of State, offers Eurasian scholars summer internships, career training, and financial support for graduate students studying in the United States. The program was established in 1992 and has been renewed and expanded in subsequent years.

# Pageant and Academic Life

## Miss America Pageant
Originating from an event in Atlantic City in 1920, the Miss America Pageant has undergone numerous changes, including the removal of racial segregation rules in 1950. The pageant has faced scrutiny over sexism and militarism during the 1960s protests. Recent iterations have introduced major format shifts, such as replacing the swimsuit competition with fitness modeling in 2018, and the event has faced financial instability, filing for bankruptcy protection in 2024.

## Studienstiftung
This German foundation awards general scholarships for bachelor's and master's studies based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility." Admission occurs through multiple channels, including high school and university nominations, and an assessment test. Support includes a monthly allowance and academic assistance, with doctoral scholarships also available.

# Comparative Scholarship Overview
Various international scholarships—including the Rhodes, Marshall, Kennedy, and Fulbright—share goals of promoting global understanding and leadership development. While the Rhodes Scholarship is noted for its focus on leadership and its global scope, the Marshall Scholarship emphasizes US-UK ties and academic depth. The Fulbright Program focuses on bi-national educational exchange, while the Kennedy Scholarship is a memorial focused on Harvard/MIT. The Marshall and Rhodes scholarships are often compared, with the Marshall historically perceived as more academically focused compared to the Rhodes.
