**UNCF Overview**

- Founded in 1944, the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) supports African-American education primarily through scholarships to Black students attending member colleges and other institutions. It includes notable alumni such as Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson.

- Established by William J. Trent, Frederick D. Patterson, and Mary McLeod Bethune, UNCF has raised substantial funds for historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) via major donors and events. The 1972 slogan, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste," remains central to its mission.

- The fund encompasses over 40 HBCUs, primarily in the Southern U.S., varying widely in age, size, and endowments.

**Global Scholarship Programs**

- **Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan):** Launched in 1993 to foster Kazakhstan’s youth through international education emphasizing Western democratic values; nearly 13,000 scholarships awarded by 2018, recipients study worldwide.

- **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF):** Founded in 1987 to support public HBCUs, providing scholarships, capacity building, and entrepreneurship, backed by substantial donations and led by CEO Harry L. Williams.

- **National Merit and National Achievement Scholarships (USA):** National Merit recognizes academic excellence via PSAT-based selection; National Achievement, for Black students, ran from 1964 to 2015 before merging into UNCF programs.

- **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany):** Founded in 1925, supports gifted students with allowances and programs, serving about 0.48% of German university students, boasting notable alumni.

- **Bright Futures Scholarship (Florida, USA):** Merit-based aid covering tuition with tiered awards, adjusted testing requirements; criticized for favoring wealthier students and racial disparities.

- **Shuping Scholarship (China):** Established in 1939 to aid poor but promising students, emphasizing self-reliance, providing financial and medical support.

- **Kennedy Scholarship (UK/USA):** Postgraduate scholarship from 1964 for Harvard or MIT study, emphasizing intellect and leadership, with prominent UK recipients.

- **Harkness Fellowship:** Since 1925, a 12-month US research fellowship for professionals from select countries, focusing currently on health policy.

- **Norway Scholarship:** Established in 1919 to strengthen Norwegian-UK academic ties, supporting advanced Oxford study; notable alumni include King Harald V.

- **Marshall Scholarship:** Founded post-WWII as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, promoting US-UK academic exchange and producing diverse scholars of high academic achievement, leadership, and motivation.

  - Expanded from 30 scholars in 1973 to 50 annually by 2024, supporting two years of postgraduate study at any UK university except pre-professional programs.

  - Selection is highly competitive (~3–4% acceptance of endorsed applicants), favoring intellectual distinction, character, leadership, and clear academic plans, typically requiring GPA ≥ 3.7 and university endorsement.

  - Scholars commonly attend top UK institutions like Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, UCL, Edinburgh, King’s College London, and Imperial College.

  - Academically regarded as rigorous and often more research-focused than the Rhodes Scholarship, with greater flexibility in university choice and study duration.

  - The Association of Marshall Scholars (AMS) organizes the annual Marshall Forum, fostering transatlantic dialogue among leaders and awarding the Marshall Medal to promote UK-US understanding.

  - **Notable Scholars:** Include U.S. Supreme Court Justices Stephen Breyer and Neil Gorsuch; Dolby Laboratories founder Ray Dolby; Nobel laureate Roger Tsien; journalists Anne Applebaum and Thomas Friedman; CIA Director William J. Burns; among others in law, science, politics, literature, and arts.

**Literary and Educational Legacies**

- **Daughters of Africa Anthologies:** Landmark collections edited by Margaret Busby showcasing writings by women of African descent, influential in black feminist literature and supporting emerging African women writers via awards funded by proceeds.

- **King's Scholars (UK):** Prestigious scholarships offered by historic UK institutions emphasizing academic excellence, cathedral affiliations, and longstanding traditions.

**Rhodes Scholarship**

- Established in 1902 to promote international understanding via Oxford postgraduate study, progressively opening to global, gender, and racial inclusion.

- Known for rigorous selection criteria emphasizing intellect, character, and leadership, with an acceptance rate under 1% globally.

- Alumni have significantly influenced civil rights, sciences, literature, and public service, though the scholarship’s colonialist origins provoke controversy.

- The Rhodes model inspired numerous global scholarships like Schwarzman and Knight-Hennessy.

**Edmund S. Muskie: Life and Legacy**

- A Polish-American from Maine, Muskie excelled academically, served in WWII, and held political offices including Maine governor, U.S. senator, 1968 vice-presidential candidate, and Secretary of State (1980–81).

- Known for environmental legislation, civil rights support, and Cold War diplomacy.

- The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program (established 1992) offers summer internships and career training to Eurasian scholars, honoring his public service legacy.

**British Council**

- Founded in 1934 as a UK cultural diplomacy organization promoting English teaching, education, cultural exchange, and arts worldwide.

- Conducts global programs including English language centers, exam administration (IELTS), MOOCs, educational mobility initiatives (Education UK, Erasmus+), and cultural festivals.

- Financed by UK government grants and fees, with significant presence in over 100 countries, managed via seven global regions.

- Faces operational challenges including office closures, budget cuts, accusations of elitism, intelligence ties allegations (denied), data security issues, and scrutiny over parliamentary accountability exemptions.

- Controversially engaged in geopolitical cultural diplomacy balancing acts, such as support for Palestinian literature while opposing cultural boycotts of Israel.

- Historically linked to events in Myanmar (1947) and Libya (2011), depicted in WWII literature and espionage novels, and affected by scandals such as IELTS cheating rings and visa scams.

- Governed by a board with notable chairs since inception; current chair is Paul Thompson (2024–present); staff represented by UK and Japanese unions.

- Awarded the Princess of Asturias Award in 2005 alongside European cultural bodies, marking its cultural influence.

**Fulbright Program**

- A U.S. government-sponsored international educational exchange program founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote peace and mutual understanding through global academic, cultural, and professional exchanges.

- Expands via the Fulbright–Hays Program (U.S. Department of Education-funded) focusing on non-Western languages and area studies, offering training and research for American educators.

- Provides diverse grant programs:

  - **Hubert H. Humphrey Program:** One-year U.S. study and professional development for mid-career professionals from developing countries.

  - **Fulbright U.S. Scholar Program:** American scholars conduct limited-term research or lectures abroad.

  - **Fulbright Specialist Program:** Short-term (2–6 weeks) consultancy assignments abroad.

  - **Fulbright U.S. Student Program:** Fellowships for U.S. students and young professionals to study or teach English abroad.

  - **Fulbright Foreign Student Program:** Scholarships for international graduate students and professionals to study or research in the U.S.

- Administered by the U.S. Department of State’s Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs and the Department of Education, with policies set by the Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board.

- Operates globally in over 160 countries with 49 bi-national commissions funded jointly by the U.S. and partner governments, managing recruitment, administration, and alumni engagement.

- Implemented through partner organizations such as the Institute of International Education (IIE), AMIDEAST, LASPAU, and World Learning.

- Supported by alumni bodies like the Fulbright Association (U.S.-based) and the Fulbright Academy (global network for science and technology collaboration).

- Recognized as the largest and one of the most prestigious international educational exchange programs, fostering academic excellence, leadership, and cross-cultural understanding despite operational challenges and recent political tensions affecting certain country operations.

- **J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding:** Established in 1993 by the Fulbright Association to honor outstanding contributions toward global understanding. Notable recipients include Nelson Mandela, Jimmy Carter, Angela Merkel, Bono, and Matt Damon & Gary White.

- **Notable Alumni:** Over 325,000 globally, including 89 Pulitzer Prize winners, 78 MacArthur Fellows, 62 Nobel laureates, 40 heads of state or government, 10 U.S. Congress members, and 1 U.N. Secretary-General; distinguished figures include Linus Pauling, Maria Ressa, Joseph Stiglitz, Rishi Sunak, Sylvia Plath, Desmond Tutu, and Muhammad Yunus.

**Miss America Pageant: History and Evolution**

- Originated in 1919 from Atlantic City beauty contests, formalized in 1921, introducing scholarships and talent competitions over time.

- Faced racial barriers until the 1970s, with milestones including Cheryl Browne (1970), first Black contestant; Vanessa Williams (1983), first Black Miss America amid controversy; Heather Whitestone (1995), first deaf winner; Nina Davuluri (2014), first Indian-American winner amid racist backlash; and Erin O’Flaherty (2016), first openly lesbian contestant.

- Experienced feminist and civil rights protests, legal disputes on eligibility, critiques on scholarship fairness, leadership changes, and shifts toward talent focus and reduced emphasis on physical appearance (Miss America 2.0).

---

**Miss America: 2017 to Present Developments**

- Leadership scandals in 2017 led to resignations and reform under Gretchen Carlson (Miss America 1989) chair, removing swimsuit and evening gown rounds, raising participant age to 25, and renaming contestants as “candidates.”

- Subsequent internal conflicts and public criticism culminated in legal disputes and leadership changes; the pageant relocated from Atlantic City to Connecticut in 2019, changed broadcasters, and paused due to COVID-19 pandemic.

- In 2022, Brían Nguyen became the first transgender Miss America.

- CEO/owner Robin Fleming later reversed some reforms, reinstating the term "contestants," adding physical appearance components via activewear fitness modeling, raising age eligibility to 28, and introducing the “HER Story” storytelling category.

- A 2023 A&E documentary exposed organizational issues of body-shaming, racism, and psychological abuse.

- The organization filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in late 2024 but sought dismissal shortly after.

- Recent titleholders include Emma Broyles (2022), Grace Stanke (2023), Madison Marsh (2024), Abbie Stockard (2025), and Cassie Donegan (2026).

- Event hosts have included Mario Lopez, Chris Harrison, Regis Philbin, Nikki Novak, Billy Gilman, and Laura Rutledge.

**Miss America Further Reading and Viewing**

- Archives and collections such as the Beauty Pageant Collection at Bowling Green State University and historic photographs document pageant history and societal protests.

- Notable books by former Miss Americas and scholars explore cultural impact, personal narratives, and feminist critiques.

- Documentaries include PBS’s *The American Experience: Miss America* (2001), A&E’s *Secrets of Miss America* (2023), and the 2023 docuseries *American Royalty: The Miss America Pageant – Its Legacy and Its Future.*
