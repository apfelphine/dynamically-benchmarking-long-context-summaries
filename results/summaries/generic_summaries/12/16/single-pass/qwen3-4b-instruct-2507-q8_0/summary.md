### **Norway Scholarship**
Established in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a former British vice-consul in Norway, the Norway Scholarship enables students from the University of Oslo to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Inspired by the need for cultural exchange during WWI, the scholarship initially faced funding challenges but was revitalized in the 1970s through a major fundraising effort. It has awarded students since 1920, with a long history of diverse academic fields, though funding has fluctuated over time. The program includes an annual dinner with a guest of honor from Oxford, often attended by King Harald V of Norway.

### **King's Scholar**
The term originates from the 1317 establishment of King's Scholars at Cambridge, later expanded to Eton College. At Eton, scholars are selected through competitive exams and reside in the "College" houses, distinguished by wearing a black gown. The tradition includes formal traditions and a strong emphasis on academic and personal development. Notable scholars include prominent figures in politics, science, and the arts. The concept has been adopted at other institutions, such as King's College Cambridge and the King's Schools, where scholars receive financial support and academic privileges.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
Created in 1964 in memory of President John F. Kennedy, the scholarship provides one-year study at Harvard or MIT. Funded by the UK government and supported by the Kennedy family, it emphasizes leadership, public service, and global understanding. Selection is highly competitive, with candidates evaluated on intellectual attainment, leadership potential, and commitment to public service. Notable scholars include prominent politicians, economists, and journalists.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
Established by the U.S. Department of State, this program supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students through summer internships, career training, and financial aid. It aims to foster international understanding and professional development. The program has been administered by various organizations, including Cultural Vistas and the International Research & Exchanges Board.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
A Florida-based scholarship program established to emulate Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, it provides merit-based funding to high school graduates. Initially offering full tuition coverage, the program has faced criticism for subsidizing wealthy students and for racial bias. Reforms in 2018 introduced new levels and requirements, including a focus on academic performance and community service. The program remains a significant source of financial aid for Florida students.

### **Marshall Scholarship**
Founded in 1953 in memory of U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the scholarship enables intellectually distinguished American students to study in the UK. It emphasizes understanding of British society and promotes international cooperation. Selection is highly competitive, with candidates evaluated on academic achievement, leadership potential, and commitment to public service. The program has a strong legacy of producing leaders in various fields, including politics, science, and diplomacy.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
A U.S. program that identifies high-achieving high school students through the PSAT/NMSQT. Students who score in the top 1% are recognized as "National Merit Semifinalists," and those who meet additional criteria become "Finalists" and are awarded scholarships. The program is known for its rigorous selection process and has produced numerous notable alumni in politics, science, and the arts.

### **Miss America**
A pageant founded in 1920 in Atlantic City, initially focused on promoting tourism and business. Over time, it evolved into a platform for social and political commentary, facing criticism for its sexist and racist practices. In 2018, the pageant adopted a new format, "Miss America 2.0," removing physical appearance as a judging criterion. The organization has faced controversies over its scholarship programs and leadership, including allegations of racial bias and financial mismanagement.

### **Bolashak**
A Kazakh scholarship program established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev to promote Western democratic values. It has awarded over 12,000 scholarships, primarily to students from poor families with strong academic records. The program emphasizes leadership and civic responsibility, and many recipients have gone on to hold key positions in government and private organizations.

### **Fulbright Program**
A U.S.-led international exchange program founded in 1946 to promote mutual understanding through education. It enables students, scholars, and professionals to study or conduct research in the U.S. or abroad. The program is administered through bilateral commissions and has a strong legacy of producing global leaders. It has faced recent challenges due to political interference and funding issues, particularly in Russia.

### **UNCF**
A nonprofit organization founded in 1944 to support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It provides scholarships and funding to students and institutions, with a mission to promote educational equity. The organization has received significant donations from notable figures, including John F. Kennedy and Reed Hastings. It has been criticized for its reliance on Indian corporate partnerships and for financial mismanagement.

### **British Council**
A UK-based international organization promoting cultural and educational exchange. It offers English language teaching, examinations, and cultural programs in over 100 countries. The organization has faced criticism for political bias, outsourcing, and controversies over its operations in countries like Russia and Syria.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs, the fund provides scholarships, capacity-building support, and programmatic funding. It has grown to support 55 member institutions, including HBCUs and PBIs. The organization has received major donations from Apple, the Koch Foundation, and Boeing, and is committed to advancing educational equity for underrepresented students.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
Founded in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes, the scholarship enables outstanding students from around the world to study at the University of Oxford. It emphasizes leadership, intellectual excellence, and public service. The program has faced criticism for its historical ties to colonialism and for gender exclusivity, which were addressed in 1977. Notable alumni include leaders in politics, science, and civil rights.

### **Studienstiftung**
A German scholarship program founded in 1925 to support students in higher education. It offers financial and academic support to students from diverse backgrounds, with a focus on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. The program has a long history of supporting students in Germany and abroad, with notable alumni in science, business, and public service.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
A literary and educational initiative launched in 2019 to honor and support African women writers. The award benefits African women students through scholarships and educational outreach. The program is part of a larger anthology, "New Daughters of Africa," which celebrates the literary contributions of women of African descent.

### **Shuping Scholarship**
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship supports students from poor families with strong academic records. It includes financial aid, extracurricular activities, and a strong emphasis on community service. The scholarship has grown significantly since its revival in 1986, with over 6,000 scholars to date. The program continues to promote social responsibility and service to the community.

### **Harkness Fellowship**
Established in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, the Harkness Fellowship enables British and other international graduates to study in the United States, focusing on healthcare policy and practice. The fellowship provides a year-long research opportunity at leading U.S. institutions and includes seminars on healthcare policy. It has produced notable alumni in healthcare, politics, and the arts.
