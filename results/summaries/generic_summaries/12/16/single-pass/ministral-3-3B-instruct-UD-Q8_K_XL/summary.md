### **Scholarship Programs Overview**

#### **1. Norway Scholarship**
The **Norway Scholarship** was established in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, a former British vice-consul in Norway, to enable Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. Initially, it was funded by £60,000 (equivalent to ~£27,800 in 2024), but due to insufficient funding, the scholarship became dependent on supplementary grants from other university funds. The program faced financial challenges, with the basic foundation capital shrinking to just NOK 130,000 (~£6,500) by the late 1970s. In the late 1970s, the scholarship was revived with significant new funding, including contributions from Wadham College, the British Ambassador to Norway, and other prominent figures. The scholarship has since supported numerous Norwegian students in pursuing studies in the UK, with notable alumni including politicians, academics, and public servants.

---

#### **2. King’s Scholarship**
The **King’s Scholarship** originates from the establishment of King’s Scholars at the University of Cambridge in 1712, with the first scholarships awarded in 1317 by King Edward II. These scholarships were initially for 12 boys from the king’s household. Over time, the scholarship evolved into a prestigious program for undergraduates at King’s College Cambridge who achieve first-class degrees, receiving a £350 prize and attending a ceremony in the college chapel. The scholarship also extends to King’s Scholars at Eton College, Westminster School, and King’s Schools in Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester.

- **Eton College**: King’s Scholars are distinguished by their academic excellence and wear a black academic gown. They are selected through competitive examinations and admitted into a house known as "College."
- **Westminster School**: Scholars are selected by competitive examination ("The Challenge"), board at the oldest house in the school, and wear gowns during school Abbey services in Westminster Abbey.
- **King’s Schools**: Scholarships are awarded based on academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by a fee discount. Queen’s Scholarships are also granted for female scholars.

#### **3. Kennedy Scholarship**
The **Kennedy Scholarship** was established in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy. It was created by Sir Alec Douglas-Home, the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, in collaboration with Harold Wilson, Sir David Ormsby-Gore, Dean Rusk, and the Kennedy family. The scholarship aims to promote international understanding and leadership, offering funding for students to study at either Harvard University or the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). The program has been administered by the Kennedy Memorial Trust, with notable trustees including former U.S. Secretaries of State and prominent figures in politics and academia.

- **Selection Criteria**: Applicants must demonstrate intellectual attainment, readiness to express themselves, and a commitment to public service. The selection process includes interviews and evaluations based on academic records, leadership potential, and personal qualities.
- **Notable Alumni**: The scholarship has produced prominent figures in politics, economics, journalism, and academia, including former U.S. Secretaries of State, economists, journalists, and public servants.

#### **4. Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** is a professional exchange program supported by the U.S. Department of State. It provides summer internships, career training, and financial support to scholars and graduate students from Eurasian countries studying in the United States. The program was established in 1992 and is named in honor of Edmund Muskie, a former U.S. Secretary of State and Governor of Maine. Muskie’s legacy includes significant contributions to environmentalism, civil rights, and diplomacy, and the internship program continues his efforts to foster international exchange and professional development.

---

#### **5. Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** is a merit-based scholarship program in Florida, designed to emulate the HOPE Scholarship from Georgia. Initially, it provided scholarships to over 42,000 students, covering up to 100% of tuition and fees for high-achieving students. However, due to budget cuts and increased competition, the program underwent significant changes in 2011, including increased minimum SAT scores and reduced award amounts. In 2018, the scholarship was overhauled to include four levels: Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV), and Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholars (GSC). The program remains open to students from all ethnicities, though historically, it has disproportionately impacted students from wealthy families.

---

#### **6. Marshall Scholarship**
The **Marshall Scholarship** was established in 1953 as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, the U.S. Secretary of State who played a pivotal role in the Marshall Plan. The scholarship enables intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the United Kingdom, fostering mutual understanding and cooperation between the two countries. The program is highly selective, with applicants required to demonstrate academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. Marshall Scholars are awarded full funding for tuition, living expenses, and travel, and they are expected to engage in cultural exchange and contribute to the UK-US relationship.

- **Selection Criteria**: Applicants must have a minimum GPA of 3.7 and demonstrate leadership potential, intellectual distinction, and a commitment to public service.
- **Notable Alumni**: The scholarship has produced many influential figures in academia, politics, and business, including Nobel Prize winners, MacArthur Fellows, and prominent leaders in various fields.

---

#### **7. National Merit Scholarship Program**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is a merit-based scholarship program in the United States, administered by the National Merit Scholarship Corporation (NMSC). It is open to high school students who take the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT) in their junior year. Students who score in the top 1% nationally are recognized as National Merit Semifinalists and can apply for scholarships. The program has faced criticism for its reliance on a single standardized test score to determine merit, and it has been accused of not adequately considering other factors such as socioeconomic status and extracurricular activities.

---

#### **8. Miss America**
The **Miss America Pageant** is one of the oldest and most iconic beauty pageants in the world, with its origins dating back to 1919. Initially held to promote tourism and cultural exchange, the pageant has evolved into a symbol of American beauty and tradition. Over the years, the pageant has faced criticism for its sexist and racist practices, leading to significant changes in its format and values. In 2018, the pageant introduced a new format, referred to as "Miss America 2.0," which aimed to shift its focus from physical appearance to talent, scholarship, and leadership. Despite these changes, the pageant continues to be a subject of controversy and debate.

---

#### **9. Bolashak Scholarship**
The **Bolashak Scholarship** is a scholarship program established by the first President of Kazakhstan, Nursultan Nazarbayev, in 1993. The program aims to promote Western democratic values among Kazakh youth by sending them to study abroad. Bolashak graduates have gone on to hold significant positions in state and private organizations within Kazakhstan. The scholarship has been expanded to include students from various countries, with a focus on fostering international understanding and collaboration.

---

#### **10. Fulbright Program**
The **Fulbright Program** is the largest international educational exchange program in the world, established in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright. The program facilitates cultural and academic exchange between the United States and other countries, providing funding for students, scholars, and professionals to study, teach, or conduct research abroad. The Fulbright Program is highly selective and emphasizes intellectual excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to mutual understanding. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners, MacArthur Fellows, and prominent figures in politics, academia, and the arts.

---

#### **11. United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
The **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)** is a nonprofit organization founded in 1944 to address funding inequities in education for African Americans. UNCF administers scholarships and provides financial support to students attending its member colleges and universities, as well as other institutions. The organization has played a significant role in promoting education and social advancement for African Americans, with notable alumni including civil rights leaders, politicians, and prominent figures in business, health care, and the arts.

---

#### **12. British Council**
The **British Council** is an international organization that promotes the UK’s global cultural, educational, and scientific exchange programs. Established in 1934, the British Council supports English language teaching, cultural exchange, and educational cooperation between the UK and other countries. The organization has faced criticism for its financial management and transparency, particularly in relation to its operations in Russia and other regions. Despite these challenges, the British Council continues to play a vital role in fostering international understanding and collaboration.

---

#### **13. Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** is a nonprofit organization established in 1987 to support public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs). TMCF provides scholarships, capacity-building support, and professional development opportunities for students and faculty at its member schools. The organization is named in honor of Thurgood Marshall, the first African American Supreme Court justice, and aims to advance educational opportunities and leadership development for students of color.

---

#### **14. Rhodes Scholarship**
The **Rhodes Scholarship** is one of the most prestigious international scholarships in the world, established in 1902 under the terms of Cecil John Rhodes’ will. The scholarship enables intellectually distinguished young people from around the world to study at the University of Oxford. Rhodes Scholars are selected based on their academic excellence, leadership potential, and commitment to public service. The program has faced criticism for its historical association with colonialism and its exclusion of women and people of color until recent reforms. Despite these controversies, the Rhodes Scholarship remains a symbol of academic excellence and global leadership.

---

#### **15. Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** is a German scholarship program that supports exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates in their academic pursuits. Established in 1925, the program offers both financial and academic support, including summer schools, research grants, and mentoring opportunities. The Studienstiftung is known for its rigorous selection process and its commitment to fostering excellence and social responsibility among its scholars.

---

#### **16. Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
The **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award** is an award established to honor and promote the work of African women writers. Named after Margaret Busby, a renowned literary critic and editor, the award celebrates the contributions of women of African descent to literature and culture. The anthology *New Daughters of Africa* features a diverse range of writers, including established names and emerging talents, and is considered a landmark in African literary history.

---

#### **17. Shuping Scholarship**
The **Shuping Scholarship** was founded in 1939 by Koo, a business man and philanthropist, in memory of his father. The scholarship aims to support academically excellent students from poor families, providing them with full tuition and additional support for extracurricular activities and voluntary work. The program has been revived in China and continues to provide opportunities for students to pursue higher education and contribute to society.

---

#### **18. Harkness Fellowship**
The **Harkness Fellowship** is a prestigious fellowship program established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness, a philanthropist, to enable British graduates to study in the United States. The fellowship was initially limited to health care policy and practice but has since expanded to include other fields. Harkness Fellows spend a year conducting research and gaining an in-depth understanding of the U.S. health care system. The program is highly selective and has produced many influential figures in academia, politics, and health care.

### **Key Themes Across Programs**
- **International Exchange**: Many programs, such as the Fulbright Program, Marshall Scholarship, and Rhodes Scholarship, emphasize fostering international understanding and collaboration.
- **Academic Excellence**: Scholarships like the Rhodes, Marshall, and Fulbright focus on selecting students with outstanding academic records and leadership potential.
- **Social Impact**: Programs such as the Kennedy Scholarship and Thurgood Marshall College Fund aim to support students who will make a positive impact on society.
- **Cultural and Educational Exchange**: Initiatives like the British Council and Bolashak Scholarship promote cultural exchange and educational opportunities abroad.
- **Controversies and Reforms**: Several programs, including the Rhodes Scholarship and Miss America, have faced criticism and undergone reforms to address historical injustices and modern challenges.
