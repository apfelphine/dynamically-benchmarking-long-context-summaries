# Scholarship Programs Summary

## Norway Scholarship
The Norway Scholarship, initially conceived in 1919 by alumnus Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, aimed to facilitate one-year studies for students from Royal Frederik University (now the University of Oslo) at Wadham College in Oxford. While awarded regularly from 1920 (with interruptions during 1926–27 and 1940–44), the fund faced severe financial inadequacy, shrinking to NOK 130,000 by the late 1970s. A significant fundraising effort in the late 1970s, led by Alf Bøe, substantially boosted the capital. The scholarship has supported scholars across diverse fields, from Law to Medicine, and continues to have an organized committee and an annual dinner, with King Harald V of Norway frequently attending the Oslo event.

## King's Scholarship
The King's Scholarship has varied manifestations across several institutions. At Eton College, a King's Scholar ("Colleger") is admitted after passing an election, resides in the College house, and is distinguished by a black academic gown, traditionally receiving a fee waiver though this has become means-tested since the 1960s. At Westminster School, the term is used for foundation scholars, depending on the reigning monarch. Furthermore, King's College Cambridge uses the term for undergraduates achieving first-class degrees, granting a prize and chapel ceremony attendance. At the King's Schools network, the scholarship recognizes academic or musical merit, with specific traditions at King's Ely and King's School, Canterbury, including access to cathedral grounds.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964 following the assassination of President John F. Kennedy, this memorial program comprises two parts: a scholarship to study at Harvard or MIT, and a permanent memorial site in Runnymede, England. Trustees, appointed by the UK Prime Minister, evaluate candidates based on intellectual attainment, communication skills, and the suitability of their proposed course of study. The selection process is comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarship programs. Notable scholars have gone on to significant careers in politics, economics, journalism, and academia.

## Marshall Scholarship
The Marshall Scholarship, established in 1953 as a "living gift" to the US, aims to enable intellectually distinguished American youth to study in the UK to foster mutual understanding between Britain and the US. Selection criteria emphasize intellectual and personal character, scholastic attainment, and potential for significant contribution to society. The scholarship's tenure is typically two years, with main destinations including Oxford and Cambridge, though scholars can study at any UK university. The program is comparable in selectivity to the Rhodes and Mitchell Scholarships.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 by Cecil John Rhodes's will, the Rhodes Scholarships were intended to promote British imperial unity and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the US. Originally restricted to single men under 25, the scholarship was later opened to women in 1977, and now Global Rhodes Scholarships are available worldwide. Selection is highly competitive, prioritizing scholastic attainment alongside character traits like courage and devotion to duty. While often criticized for its colonialist ties and the tendency of scholars to enter business rather than public service, many alumni have made notable impacts in fields like medicine, science, and human rights.

## Harkness Fellowship
The Commonwealth Fund established the Harkness Fellowships in 1925 to reciprocate the Rhodes Scholarships, originally for British graduates to study in the US. Since 1997, the fellowships have been heavily limited to health care policy and practice, with Fellows conducting research at top American institutions. The program is funded by the Commonwealth Fund and various international bodies, and Fellows participate in specialized seminars on healthcare policy.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida scholarship program, initially designed to emulate Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, is based solely on academic merit, not financial need. It has evolved significantly since 2008, experiencing funding cuts in 2011 which disproportionately affected minority and low-income students. The program was overhauled in 2018 with renamed levels (FAS, FMS, etc.) and subsequent changes in 2021 and 2025 regarding testing requirements and stipends.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
This program uses the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT) as its initial screening tool for over 1.5 million entrants. Students recognized as Semifinalists must submit SAT scores and an essay to advance to the Finalist level. Of the Finalists, approximately 8,000 receive a \$2,500 scholarship. Critics, including NACAC, have challenged the reliance on the PSAT/NMSQT as the sole pre-screening measure.

## National Achievement Scholarship Program
Established in 1964 to encourage African American youth to pursue education following the Civil Rights Act of 1964, this program transitioned to the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) in 2015. While students could previously qualify for both National Achievement and National Merit scholarships, they could only receive one monetary award.

## UNCF
Founded in 1944 by William J. Trent and leaders like Mary McLeod Bethune, the UNCF was designed to support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) to become strong institutions. While scholarships are open to all ethnicities, the majority of recipients remain African-American. The organization has benefited from massive donations, including \$120 million from Netflix founder Reed Hastings in 2020.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports higher education at public HBCUs, focusing on programmatic support, capacity building, and scholarships, with a distinct mission from the UNCF. The organization acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to enhance talent identification and entrepreneurship development within public and private HBCUs.

## Bolashak
The "Bolashak" program, meaning "Future" in Kazakh, was instituted by Nursultan Nazarbayev in 1993 to inculcate Kazakh youth with Western democratic values through overseas study. The program has significantly expanded its scope and intake over the years, with thousands of scholarships granted, and the list of partner universities is extensive, spanning multiple continents and regions.

## Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program seeks to promote international understanding through educational exchange, aiming to foster peace. Established in 1945, it operates on a bi-national basis through various commissions. It offers multiple grants, such as the U.S. Student Program for U.S. citizens studying abroad, and the Foreign Student Program for international students in the U.S. The program has faced recent political disruptions, including a funding freeze in 2025 and a 2025 board resignation citing political interference.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award, partnered with SOAS, University of London, supports an African woman student. The companion anthology, *Daughters of Africa*, is a celebrated literary collection featuring over 200 women writers of African descent from ancient times to the present. The anthology has been praised globally as a landmark text for revealing the vast and varied body of black women's writing across the diaspora.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, this scholarship was motivated by the motto "Return to the Society what you have gained from it." It provided full tuition and comprehensive support, including a library, medical care, and vocational training, to poor, academically strong students during wartime. The scheme was revived in 1986 and continues to emphasize service to the community, with administration managed by student and alumnus associations.

## General and Specialized Scholarship Programs
Other notable scholarships include:
*   **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program:** A professional exchange program supported by the US Department of State, providing placements and training to Eurasian scholars.
*   **Pomona/Oxford Scholarship:** The Norway Scholarship historically focused on Oxford, while the Marshall Scholarship provides flexible study options across the UK.
*   **Studienstiftung:** A German scholarship based on principles of "excellence, initiative and responsibility," offering financial and academic support for study in Germany or the EU/Switzerland through various nomination channels.
*   **Kennedy Scholarship:** Offers scholarships at Harvard or MIT to promote liberty.
*   **James Marshall Scholarship:** Focuses on high-level postgraduate study in the UK.

# Miscellaneous
## Miss America Pageant
The Miss America pageant, originating from a 1920 Atlantic City event, has evolved significantly. Initially featuring a "Golden Mermaid," it temporarily halted during the Great Depression. While early rules enforced racial segregation, the pageant has seen a gradual progression towards diversity, with the first African-American contestant appearing in 1971 and the first Black winner in 1984. The format has undergone major changes, including the introduction of "Miss America 2.0" in 2018, which removed swimsuit judging, before some of those changes were reversed in 2023.

## Cultural and Political Movements
Several texts highlight broader cultural and political themes:
*   **Muskie's Legacy:** Edmund S. Muskie is regarded as the "father of the 1960s environmental movement in America" due to his legislative successes with the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts. His career also included significant roles in civil rights advocacy and the establishment of the federal budget process.
*   **Human Rights:** The J. William Fulbright Prize recognizes contributions to international understanding, honoring figures like Nelson Mandela and Jimmy Carter.
*   **Social Justice:** The *Daughters of Africa* anthology is lauded for showcasing the diverse literary contributions of women of African descent across centuries.
