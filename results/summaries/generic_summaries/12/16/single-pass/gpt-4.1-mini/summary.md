# Scholarships and Educational Programs Overview

## Norway Scholarship
Established in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford, the Norway Scholarship has supported varied academic pursuits despite funding challenges. After near discontinuation in the 1970s, revitalization efforts raised substantial funds, leading to continued awards and annual events involving scholars and prominent figures, including King Harald V. The scholarship has a long history of laureates studying diverse subjects across decades.

## King's Scholar Programs
Originating in 1317 with King Edward II’s initiative, King's Scholars have a storied presence at institutions such as Eton College, Westminster School, and King's College Cambridge. At Eton, King's Scholars, known as "Collegers," enjoy unique traditions and historical privileges, including wearing gowns and exclusive dining. Similar programs exist at Westminster and King's Schools, which award scholarships based on academic or musical attainment, often accompanied by distinct honors and rights. Notable King's Scholars include politicians, academics, writers, and leaders such as Boris Johnson, John Maynard Keynes, and Harold Macmillan.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Created in memory of President John F. Kennedy in 1964, the Kennedy Scholarship funds British postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. Supported by familial and political ties, it fosters UK-US understanding through rigorous selection emphasizing intellect, public service potential, and originality. Trustees have included prominent academics and statespersons. Notable scholars span politics, economics, journalism, arts, academia, and law, including Ed Balls, Stephanie Flanders, and Mary Arden.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
Named after Edmund Muskie, a distinguished American politician and statesman known for environmental leadership and civil rights advocacy, the program supports Eurasian scholars with internships and career training in the U.S. Muskie’s legacy includes significant environmental legislation (notably Clean Air and Water Acts), Democratic Party revitalization in Maine, Senate leadership, and service as U.S. Secretary of State. The program continues his dedication to public service and international cooperation.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)
Started to mirror Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, Florida’s Bright Futures awards merit-based scholarships at several levels based primarily on academic achievement and standardized test scores. It has undergone funding cuts and eligibility tightening, notably raising SAT/ACT thresholds and adjusting coverage percentages. It offers scholarships covering tuition for university and vocational programs, along with renewal requirements tied to GPA and enrollment. The program has faced critiques over equity and racial disparities.

## Marshall Scholarship
Founded in 1953 as a tribute to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, this scholarship enables American students to pursue postgraduate studies at any UK university. Designed as a coeducational alternative to the Rhodes Scholarship, it emphasizes academic excellence and leadership potential. The scholarship has expanded steadily, with increased annual awards and prestigious recipients. The Association of Marshall Scholars facilitates alumni engagement and hosts forums on transatlantic issues.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
A U.S. academic competition based primarily on PSAT/NMSQT scores, it recognizes high-achieving students through stages: Commended, Semifinalist, Finalist, and Scholarship winner. Around 8,000 awards are granted annually for college tuition assistance. The program also featured a National Achievement Scholarship for Black American students, which transitioned to supporting graduates post-2015. While broadly respected, it has been critiqued for reliance on standardized testing as a sole screening tool.

## Miss America Pageant
Originating in the early 1920s to boost Atlantic City tourism, the Miss America pageant evolved from a beauty contest to a scholarship provider emphasizing talent and education. The pageant's history includes significant milestones such as racial integration, controversies, and role shifts aligning with feminist movements. Recent reforms aimed to discontinue judging physical appearance (swimsuit competition removed), though some changes were later partly reversed. The organization has faced scandals but continues as a cultural institution providing scholarships to women.

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Initiated in 1993 by Kazakhstan's first president to expose youth to Western education and values, the Bolashak Scholarship sends students abroad for studies at leading global universities. It has grown significantly, with over 12,800 scholarships awarded by 2018. The program focuses on Kazakh ethnicity but includes other groups aware of language requirements. It lists an extensive roster of partner universities worldwide.

## Fulbright Program
Established in 1946 after WWII by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international understanding through educational exchange, the program supports study, research, and teaching worldwide. It includes student, scholar, teacher, and professional grants across diverse disciplines. Administered by the U.S. State Department and international commissions, the program has faced recent political challenges affecting funding and operations. Alumni include Nobel laureates, political leaders, and prominent academics.

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)
Founded in 1944 to address educational funding inequities for African Americans, UNCF supports scholarships for students attending member institutions and beyond. Its alumni include civil rights leaders, politicians, and cultural figures such as Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson. UNCF conducts high-profile fundraising efforts, including the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars, and has received significant philanthropic donations from major individuals and foundations.

## British Council
Established in 1934 to promote British culture and English language education overseas, the British Council operates globally, offering English teaching, cultural programs, and educational exchanges. It cooperates with numerous countries via bilateral agreements and manages major exams like IELTS. Despite achievements, it has faced political controversies, closures (notably in Russia and Syria), accusations of espionage links, and internal scandals. The British Council also leads global educational initiatives and arts programs.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Founded in 1987 to support public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs), TMCF provides scholarships, programmatic funding, and capacity building. It differs from the UNCF by focusing on public HBCUs and has partnerships with major corporations. The organization has expanded its reach via acquisitions and offers leadership development alongside financial aid.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 through Cecil Rhodes's will, this prestigious postgraduate scholarship promotes academic excellence, leadership, and international understanding. Initially male-only and limited geographically, it has evolved to include global candidates and women since 1977. Rhodes Scholars have significantly impacted politics, academia, civil rights, science, and arts globally. The program faces ongoing debates regarding Rhodes's colonialist legacy and scholarship career paths. It inspired sister scholarships like the Kennedy and Marshall Scholarships.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (German Academic Scholarship Foundation)
Founded in 1925, re-established in 1948, it is Germany's largest scholarship organization supporting undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students based on academic and social criteria. Scholarship recipients receive financial aid, mentoring, and academic enrichment activities. Admission channels include school and university nominations, competitions, and testing. It supports diversity and excellence without age or citizenship restrictions. Notable alumni span sciences, politics, arts, and journalism.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Building on the influential anthology *Daughters of Africa* (1992), edited by Margaret Busby, the 2019 *New Daughters of Africa* compiles writings by over 200 women of African descent worldwide. The collection has been widely praised for showcasing diversity and literary excellence. Linked to the anthology, the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students, promoting literary heritage and scholarship.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 in Shanghai by businessman Koo Soh Bing to aid financially disadvantaged but academically talented students, the Shuping Scholarship has provided continuous support including tuition, healthcare, and extracurricular opportunities. It maintained operations during wartime and was revived in the 1980s with government and alumni support. The scholarship emphasizes social responsibility, with alumni contributing significantly to China’s modernization.

## Harkness Fellowship
Originating from the Commonwealth Fund in 1925 as a reciprocal program to Rhodes Scholarships, the Harkness Fellowships focus today on health care policy and practice research. Candidates from several countries spend a year studying and collaborating at prestigious American institutions. The program includes seminars and briefings that promote international health care understanding. Notable alumni include influential academics, policymakers, and cultural figures.
