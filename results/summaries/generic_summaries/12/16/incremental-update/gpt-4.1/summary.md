## International Scholarship Traditions and Major Academic Programs – Concise Summary

### Historic Scholarship Exchanges and Academic Ties
Longstanding international programs, such as the Norway Scholarship (1919), reinforce historic academic connections between Norway and the UK. Prestigious UK institutions—including Oxford, Cambridge, Eton, Westminster, and King’s College Cambridge—offer academic and musical merit-based scholarships, with programs like Worcester’s Queen’s Scholarships showing growing inclusivity.

### UK and US Exchange Scholarships
Scholarships such as the Kennedy, Marshall, and Rhodes foster international study and strengthen transatlantic ties, focusing on diversity, leadership, and reform in response to historical challenges.

### Major U.S. Scholarship Programs
U.S. programs like Edmund S. Muskie, Bright Futures, and National Merit/Achievement reward academic excellence, though inclusivity issues persist. The UNCF supports students affected by changes to the National Achievement program.

### Support for HBCUs: UNCF and Thurgood Marshall College Fund
UNCF (since 1944) and the Thurgood Marshall College Fund (est. 1987) provide vital scholarships and fundraising for 55 HBCUs and technical colleges, with engagement from alumni and donors.

### Fulbright Program
Since 1946, the Fulbright Program has promoted U.S. global educational exchanges, cultivating a diverse alumni network amidst financial and operational challenges.

### Miss America Pageant: Evolution
Established in 1921, Miss America has evolved in response to shifting social norms, embracing greater inclusivity while retaining cultural influence.

### Bolashak Program (Kazakhstan)
Bolashak (est. 1993) enables Kazakh students to study abroad, contributing to the nation's development.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Germany’s Studienstiftung provides merit-based financial aid, mentoring, and international opportunities to outstanding students.

### British Council: Global Reach and Criticism
The British Council promotes UK education and culture globally but faces criticism for elitism and budget constraints.

### Margaret Busby, Daughters of Africa, and the New Daughters of Africa Award
Margaret Busby’s anthologies, **Daughters of Africa** and **New Daughters of Africa** (2019), spotlight literary achievement among women of African descent. The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women’s higher education.

### Shuping Scholarship (Shanghai)
Established in 1939 by Koo in Shanghai, the Shuping Scholarship provides financial aid and educational initiatives, later expanded by the Shuping Scholarship Foundation. The program champions service, self-reliance, filial piety, honesty, philanthropy, and has grown to reach numerous schools, supported by engaged alumni and an executive committee. Koo’s humanitarian efforts, including medical aid and support for refugees, shaped the scholarship’s enduring legacy.

### Harkness Fellowship
Founded in 1925 by Edward Harkness, the Harkness Fellowships enable Commonwealth scholars to study in the US, focusing on health policy since 1997. Fellows spend a year at top American institutions and collaborate on research, supported by the Commonwealth Fund. The fellowship’s international reach and impact are documented via extensive references, with numerous distinguished alumni across academic, professional, and cultural fields.
