# Summary

## Bolashak

Bolashak is a Kazakh scholarship program founded in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev to foster future leaders by sending students abroad for higher education and exposure to Western democratic values. Over 12,800 scholarships had been granted by 2018, resulting in many graduates occupying key roles in Kazakhstan's public and private sectors, though there are criticisms about limits on graduates’ ability to effect change. Selection is competitive, involving language and psychological testing. As of 2008, the majority of recipients were ethnically Kazakh due to the language requirement. Bolashak scholars study at leading universities worldwide.

## National Merit Scholarship Program

This U.S. program identifies high school students via the PSAT/NMSQT for academic scholarships using a multi-stage process: Commended, Semifinalist, then Finalist. Of the ~1.5 million entrants annually, around 8,000 Finalists receive scholarships. Sponsor-specific, college-sponsored, and special scholarships also exist. The program is highly selective and influential, but faces criticism for relying on a single standardized test for screening and for limited transparency about score cutoffs. The program previously included a separate National Achievement Scholarship for Black students, now administered by UNCF. Many top universities compete to enroll National Merit Scholars with additional funding.

## UNCF

Founded in 1944, the United Negro College Fund provides scholarships—primarily to Black students— and supports 37 historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) via fundraising and advocacy efforts. Through high-profile campaigns and celebrity-driven events such as the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars, UNCF has raised hundreds of millions for scholarships. Major recent donations include gifts from MacKenzie Scott, Reed Hastings, Koch Industries, and Lilly Endowment. The organization is known for the motto "A mind is a terrible thing to waste.” UNCF member institutions are listed extensively, emphasizing their role in education and social advancement.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program

Established to mirror Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, Florida's Bright Futures program funds postsecondary education for state students based on academic merit, not financial need. It offers four scholarship levels, each with defined GPA, test score, and service hour requirements, subject to periodic changes in response to budget pressures and criticism about racial disparities. Recent years saw increased SAT/ACT/CLT requirements and restoration of full-tuition awards for top levels. Scholarships are renewable, and the program has significantly supported degree attainment among Florida residents.

---

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund

Founded in 1987, TMCF supports public HBCUs with scholarships, capacity-building, and advocacy, raising over $500 million to date. The fund has received substantial support from corporate donors (Apple, Koch Foundation, Boeing) and expanded its reach through acquisitions like the Opportunity Funding Corporation. It serves 55 member schools, encompassing HBCUs, PBIs, community colleges, and a technical college.

---

## Kennedy Scholarship

Created in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this British program supports top UK students to study at Harvard or MIT. Recipients are selected for intellectual distinction, public service potential, and resilience, paralleling Rhodes and Marshall selection standards. Trustees include both eminent academic figures and Kennedy family representatives. Notable scholars have impacted government, economics, journalism, academia, law, and the arts.

---

## Studienstiftung

Germany’s Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is the nation’s largest scholarship provider for exceptional students and doctoral candidates, supporting around 15,000 scholars annually. Admission is highly competitive via school, university, competitions, or assessment tests, with selection based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility. Financial and academic support includes generous stipends, allowances, seminars, summer schools, and special programs. Alumni include many leading figures in science, politics, business, arts, and journalism.

---

## Norway Scholarship

Since 1920, the Norway Scholarship has enabled students from the University of Oslo to study at Oxford (Wadham College) as a means of promoting Anglo-Norwegian ties. Funding has seen fluctuations, but the program continues through fundraising and alumni support. Annual dinners and events foster a network among past scholars, with King Harald V regularly attending.

---

## Shuping Scholarship

Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by philanthropist Koo Soh Bing, the Shuping Scholarship supports academically talented, financially needy students, encouraging a spirit of service and voluntary work. It survived wartime disruptions, expanded post-1986, and now awards hundreds annually in secondary and tertiary institutions. The scheme is run largely by alumni associations and funded by Koo and later family donations, promoting self-reliance, character development, and community leadership among scholars.

---

## King's Scholar

King's Scholars are select students supported by scholarships at prestigious British institutions such as Eton College, Westminster School, King's College Cambridge, and King's Schools across England. These scholarships originated in medieval times and are awarded for academic or musical excellence, often accompanied by traditions and privileges. The scholarships, sometimes means-tested, have produced notable alumni in politics, arts, sciences, and law.

---

## Harkness Fellowship

Established in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, Harkness Fellowships allow distinguished scholars and professionals (now exclusively in health policy) from various countries to conduct research in the U.S. and participate in seminars with leading experts. The Fellowships have contributed significantly to transatlantic cooperation and produced leading figures in academia, literature, journalism, science, law, and the arts.

---

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program

Named after U.S. Senator and Secretary of State Edmund Muskie, the program provides professional internships, training, and support to Eurasian graduate students studying in the U.S., aiming to develop international understanding and advanced skills. Muskie's own legacy includes significant contributions to environmental legislation, civil rights, diplomacy, and governmental innovation.

---

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award

Inspired by Margaret Busby’s landmark anthologies, "Daughters of Africa" and "New Daughters of Africa," these awards support African women’s educational opportunities. The anthologies have been influential in literary scholarship and global feminist discourse, celebrating the diversity and achievement of women of African descent. The awards at SOAS enable access for African women to study in the UK, reflecting the values of representation and empowerment noted in the anthologies.

---

## Marshall Scholarship

Founded in 1953, the Marshall Scholarship enables top American graduates to study at any UK university, building transatlantic understanding and fostering future leaders. Selection (1,000+ applicants annually) is based on academic excellence, leadership, and ambassadorial potential, with recipients enjoying flexible study options and substantial financial support. The program maintains a vigorous alumni network, and its rivalry with the Rhodes and Fulbright scholarships is longstanding. Notable Marshall Scholars have excelled in government, academia, law, science, journalism, and business.

---

## Rhodes Scholarship

Established in 1902, the Rhodes is the oldest international postgraduate award, enabling global students to study at Oxford. Selection is based on academic merit, leadership, moral force, and commitment to public service, with a 0.7% global acceptance rate. Its history includes expanding access to women and various world regions, though past exclusion and colonial legacy have prompted critique. Noteworthy alumni include multiple heads of state, Nobel laureates, civil rights leaders, and innovators in sciences, law, arts, and academia.

---

## Miss America

Originating in 1921, Miss America is a scholarship-focused pageant promoting higher education, talent, service, and empowerment among young women in the U.S. Over its history, the event evolved to address issues of inclusion and diversity, removed swimsuit competitions, and shifted to evaluate candidates on merit and personal narratives. The organization provides millions in scholarships annually, though scandals and controversy regarding governance and transparency have periodically emerged.

---

## British Council

Founded in 1934, the British Council promotes British culture, English language, and education worldwide. Operating in over 100 countries, it offers teaching, exams (notably IELTS), cultural exchange, and supports arts, science, and educational initiatives. The Council is funded by UK government grants and commercial activities, though has faced financial challenges, political controversies, and closures, especially in Russia and Afghanistan. Criticisms include outsourcing, management of staff abroad, and handling of sensitive geopolitical situations.

---

## Fulbright Program

The U.S. Fulbright Program, established in 1946, is a flagship international exchange facilitating academic, teaching, and professional opportunities across 160+ countries. Funded by the U.S. government and run with bi-national partnerships, it aims to foster world peace through educational exchange. Grants support students, scholars, teachers, and professionals, with variations like the Fulbright-Hays program focusing on non-Western languages and area studies. The program is globally prestigious, producing Nobel laureates, heads of state, and leaders in all major fields. Recently, it has seen disruptions due to political interference and diplomatic disputes, notably with Russia and changing U.S. administrations.
