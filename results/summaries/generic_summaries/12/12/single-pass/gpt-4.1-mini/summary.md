# Scholarship and Fellowship Programs Overview

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev, the Bolashak ("Future") Scholarship sends Kazakh youth abroad to study at top universities, aiming to imbue them with Western democratic values. Initially, limited authority upon return restricted graduates from enacting reforms. By 2018, over 12,800 scholarships had been awarded, with many recipients holding key roles in Kazakhstan’s public and private sectors. The program primarily serves ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements and includes study opportunities in 32 countries across numerous prestigious universities worldwide.

## National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)
This highly selective program honors U.S. high school students based primarily on their PSAT/NMSQT scores, progressing through Commended, Semifinalist, and Finalist stages to award about 8,000 scholarships annually. It includes corporate, college-sponsored, and special scholarships. Critics highlight the reliance on a single standardized test and lack of transparency in selection criteria. The National Achievement Scholarship Program, focused on Black American youth, transitioned in 2016 to honor underrepresented college graduates instead, ceasing high school student awards.

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944 to support Black students and historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), UNCF offers scholarships open to all ethnicities, though primarily benefiting African Americans. Supported by major fundraising events like the Lou Rawls Parade of Stars and significant donations (e.g., $120 million from Netflix founder Reed Hastings), UNCF plays a central role in funding HBCUs and students. The fund’s celebrated motto is "A mind is a terrible thing to waste." Its member institutions include a wide array of HBCUs across the U.S.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, USA)
Initiated to emulate Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, Bright Futures awards merit-based scholarships not dependent on financial need, supporting Florida high school graduates attending in-state post-secondary institutions. The program has multiple levels—Florida Academic Scholars, Medallion Scholars, Gold Seal Vocational, and Gold Seal CAPE—with varying GPA, standardized test scores, community service, and course requirements. Funding and eligibility have fluctuated over time, with recent reforms restoring some benefits but also raising standardized test score thresholds.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports approximately 55 public HBCUs and related institutions with scholarships, programmatic funding, and capacity building. It has raised over $500 million, distinct from UNCF. The fund has received major grants from corporations like Apple and the Charles Koch Foundation and merged with the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to enhance entrepreneurial support within HBCUs.

## Kennedy Scholarship (UK to US)
Created as a British memorial to John F. Kennedy, this scholarship enables British students to pursue postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT. Selection is highly competitive and seeks candidates demonstrating intellectual excellence, leadership potential, and commitment to public service. The Kennedy family and UK government actively support the program, which has honored nearly 600 scholars and counts many prominent politicians, academics, and public figures among its alumni.

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Germany's most prestigious scholarship foundation, the Studienstiftung supports academically outstanding students at bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels, regardless of nationality. Candidates are nominated by schools, professors, or through competitions and rigorous assessments. Scholarship benefits include financial aid, health insurance subsidies, academic mentoring, and funded research opportunities. The foundation also runs specialized programs and partners with international institutions.

## Norway Scholarship
Established in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford’s Wadham College, the Norway Scholarship promotes cultural and academic ties between Norway and Britain. Funded initially by Arthur Jayne and others, it has experienced intermittent operation due to financial constraints and wartime interruptions but continues to offer annual awards to Norwegian scholars.

## Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 in Shanghai by philanthropist Koo Soh Bing to support poor but talented secondary students, the Shuping Scholarship has a rich history including social welfare initiatives like medical emergency funds and refugee children homes. After interruptions during political turmoil, the scholarship was revived in the 1980s and continues to award scholarships to thousands of students, promoting community service alongside academic achievement.

## King's Scholar (UK)
Originating at Cambridge University in 1317 and expanded to Eton College by King Henry VI in 1440, King's Scholars are students awarded foundation scholarships based on academic merit. At Eton, King's Scholars ("Collegers") live centrally, wear distinctive gowns, and are entitled to special privileges. Similar scholarships exist at Westminster School, King’s College Cambridge, and various historic King’s Schools in England, serving as prestigious recognition of academic or musical excellence.

## Harkness Fellowship (Commonwealth Fund)
Founded in 1925 to reciprocate Rhodes Scholarships by bringing British graduates to study in the U.S., the Harkness Fellowship now focuses exclusively on health care policy. It offers prestigious year-long placements at leading American institutions combined with seminars and policy briefings. Funded partly by foundations in participating countries, alumni include distinguished academics and public figures.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program (USA)
Named after Edmund Muskie, a prominent American politician and former Secretary of State, this U.S. State Department program provides summer internships, training, and financial support to Eurasian graduate students studying in the U.S. Established by Congress in 1992, it aims to promote professional development and foster ties between the U.S. and Eurasia.

## Daughters of Africa Anthology and Award
Edited by Margaret Busby, *Daughters of Africa* (1992) is a comprehensive international anthology showcasing writings by women of African descent across centuries and genres. Celebrated for its scope and influence, it has inspired a 2019 companion volume, *New Daughters of Africa*, featuring new and emerging writers. Connected to the anthology, the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students, with the inaugural recipients enrolled in 2020 and 2023.

## Marshall Scholarship (UK to US)
Established in 1953 as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, this scholarship funds American students to pursue postgraduate study anywhere in the UK. It stands as a coeducational alternative to the Rhodes Scholarship, with rigorous selection emphasizing academic excellence and leadership. Annual awards typically number around 40-50, with scholars attending prestigious UK institutions. The associated Association of Marshall Scholars fosters transatlantic dialogue and hosts the annual Marshall Forum.

## Rhodes Scholarship (International)
Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the Rhodes Scholarship enables outstanding students worldwide to study at Oxford University. Originally male-only and limited in scope, the scholarship now admits women and global applicants. Selection criteria blend intellectual ability with moral character and leadership potential. Though prestigious, it faces criticism for its colonial legacy and shifting career outcomes of scholars, with many alumni impacting academia, politics, arts, and sciences.

## Miss America Pageant (USA)
Beginning unofficially in 1919 and officially established in 1921, the Miss America pageant evolved from a beauty contest to a scholarship competition emphasizing talent and education. Historically criticized for racial and gender exclusion, the pageant broke racial barriers in the 1970s and removed the swimsuit competition in 2018 as part of "Miss America 2.0". Amid organizational scandals and leadership changes, some earlier reforms have been reversed, and the pageant continues to adapt culturally and socially.

## British Council
Founded in the 1930s by the British government to promote UK culture and education globally, the British Council operates in over 100 countries. It administers English language teaching, cultural exchange programs, and various international initiatives. Its history includes closures and political disputes, with recent controversies over outsourcing, censorship, and funding transparency. It remains a key player in UK international soft power and education diplomacy.

## Fulbright Program (USA)
Created in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international peace and understanding through educational exchange, the Fulbright Program offers scholarships for study, research, and teaching worldwide. The program includes student, scholar, teacher, and professional exchanges across nearly all academic disciplines. Administered by the U.S. State Department and bi-national commissions, it has produced distinguished alumni including Nobel laureates, leaders, and innovators. The program has faced recent challenges from political interference and funding freezes but remains a cornerstone of U.S. cultural diplomacy.
