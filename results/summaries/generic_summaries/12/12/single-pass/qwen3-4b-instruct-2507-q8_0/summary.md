### **Bolashak Scholarship**
- Founded in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s first president, Nursultan Nazarbayev, with the goal of promoting Western democratic values among youth through international study.
- Initially, 785 scholarships were awarded from 1994 to 2004; by 2017, the total reached 12,831.
- In 2005, 1,796 were awarded, with 537 applicants rejected due to non-conformity to selection rules.
- Many graduates hold key positions in state and private organizations in Kazakhstan.
- Ethnic distribution (2008): 93.6% Kazakh, 3% Russian, 0.9% Korean, 0.5% Tatar.
- First recipient: Kuanysh Satybaldievich Sazanov, who presented the first diploma to President Nazarbayev in 1996.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program (USA)**
- A prestigious U.S. program for high-achieving high school seniors.
- Entry requires taking the PSAT/NMSQT, with top scorers qualifying for "Commended" status.
- Selection process includes semifinalist and finalist rounds, with final awards based on academic performance, leadership, and extracurriculars.
- Offers scholarships (up to $2,500) and recognizes top scholars with awards.
- Criticized for over-reliance on a single test (PSAT) and lack of transparency in selection criteria.

### **National Achievement Scholarship Program (USA)**
- Established in 1964 to encourage African American youth in education.
- Over four million African Americans participated; over 34,000 received awards.
- Students could receive one award from NMSC, with recognition in both programs.
- Transitioned in 2015 to the United Negro College Fund (UNCF), which now supports underrepresented college graduates.

---

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**
- Founded in 1944 to support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs).
- Provides scholarships and funding to HBCUs to promote educational equity.
- Notable alumni include Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Dr. David Satcher.
- Fundraising includes major events like the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars" and donations from figures like John F. Kennedy and Walter Annenberg.
- Slogan: "A mind is a terrible thing to waste" and "Not asking for a handout, just a hand."

---

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program (USA)**
- A merit-based program for Florida high school graduates, offering full or partial tuition coverage.
- Based on academic merit, with "A" and "B" levels, and vocational tracks.
- Criticized for subsidizing wealthier students and racial bias against minorities.
- In 2018, the program was restructured with new levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC) and added an Academic Top Scholar award.
- Adjusted SAT score requirements in 2014 and later accepted scores from the Classic Learning Test (CLT).

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**
- Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs and promote access to higher education.
- Supports 55 HBCUs and funds scholarships, research, and capacity-building.
- Acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013.
- Notable for its focus on entrepreneurship and talent identification.

### **Kennedy Scholarship (UK)**
- Established in 1964 in memory of President John F. Kennedy.
- Awards scholarships to students to study at Harvard or MIT.
- Selection emphasizes intellectual attainment, public service, and leadership.
- Notable scholars include Ed Balls, David Miliband, and others in politics, economics, and academia.

---

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)**
- A German scholarship for university students offering both financial and academic support.
- Students are selected through nomination by schools, professors, or competitions.
- Offers financial aid, summer academies, and academic mentoring.
- Scholarships are renewable and support students in Germany and abroad.
- Over 2,179 scholarships were awarded in 2023.

---

### **Norway Scholarship**
- Founded in 1919 by Arthur Jayne to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford.
- Originally funded by personal donations; later supported by public funds.
- Students study at Wadham College, Oxford, with a focus on academic excellence and cultural exchange.
- Historical list includes scholars from the 1920s to 2025.

---

### **Shuping Scholarship (China)**
- Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing to support poor, academically strong students.
- Students receive full tuition, a library, and access to extracurricular activities.
- Focus on service to society and volunteerism.
- Revived in 1986 with support from the government and past scholars.
- Named after Koo’s father, with a mission to "return to society what you have gained."

### **King's Scholar (UK)**
- A prestigious position at Eton College and King's College, Cambridge.
- At Eton, scholars are selected through competitive exams and wear distinctive gowns.
- At King's College, scholars receive a prize and attend a formal ceremony.
- Notable scholars include John Maynard Keynes, Harold Macmillan, and Boris Johnson.

---

### **Harkness Fellowship (USA)**
- Established in 1925 to promote international understanding through health policy research.
- Fellows spend a year studying U.S. healthcare systems and policy.
- Focus on health policy, with participation from scholars in Australia, Canada, Germany, and the UK.
- Notable alumni include David Armitage, Peter Atkins, and Sir Ronald Bottrall.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program (USA)**
- A U.S. Department of State internship program for Eurasian scholars and graduate students.
- Provides summer internships, career training, and financial support.
- Established in 1992 and expanded in 2015 by Cultural Vistas.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
- An award established in 2019 to support African women students.
- Features 200+ contributors, including Maya Angelou, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, and others.
- Includes a scholarship for African women students; first recipients were from Kenya.
- Celebrates the literary contributions of Black women across the diaspora.

### **Marshall Scholarship (UK)**
- Established in 1953 to promote U.S.-UK understanding through postgraduate study.
- Open to American citizens, with a focus on intellectual distinction and leadership.
- Scholars study in the UK, receive tuition coverage, and are expected to act as ambassadors.
- Notable scholars include Stephen Breyer, Nannerl Keohane, and Thomas Friedman.

---

### **Rhodes Scholarship (UK)**
- Founded in 1902 to promote international understanding through education.
- Open to students from over 20 countries, with a strong emphasis on leadership and service.
- Highly selective, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%.
- Controversies include racial exclusion, colonialist legacy, and criticism over scholars entering business rather than public service.
- Notable alumni include Nelson Mandela, Bill Clinton, and Desmond Tutu.

---

### **Miss America Pageant**
- A U.S. pageant established in 1920 in Atlantic City, initially focused on beauty and cultural promotion.
- Evolved into a platform for social advocacy, with notable moments including the 1968 protest and the 2018 "Miss America 2.0" reform.
- Reforms included eliminating the swimsuit competition and focusing on personal narrative and scholarship.
- Has faced criticism over elitism, racial exclusion, and political interference.
- Recent winners include Nina Davuluri (2014) and Brían Nguyen (2024).

---

### **British Council**
- A UK-based charity promoting English language and cultural exchange globally.
- Operates in over 100 countries, offering teaching, exams (e.g., IELTS), and educational programs.
- Has faced controversies over political interference, outsourcing, and attacks in regions like Afghanistan and Syria.
- Criticized for alleged ties to the Indian diaspora and issues in teacher pay and duty of care.

---

### **Fulbright Program**
- A U.S. international exchange program founded in 1946 to promote mutual understanding.
- Offers grants for students, scholars, and professionals to study or work abroad.
- Administered by the U.S. Department of State and IIE.
- Notable for its impact on global leaders, with over 325,000 alumni.
- In 2025, faced political interference and funding suspensions due to U.S. administration actions.
- The J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding honors individuals promoting global understanding.
