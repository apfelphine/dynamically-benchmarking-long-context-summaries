## Scholarship and Achievement Programs

### Major Scholarship Programs

- **Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan):** Since 1993, empowers Kazakh students to study abroad, fostering language skills and democratic values, with 12,800+ alumni in key roles.
- **U.S. Scholarship Initiatives:**  
  - *National Merit Scholarship Program:* Grants 8,000 merit awards annually; alumni include Bill Gates.
  - *UNCF (United Negro College Fund):* More than $200M awarded to Black students at HBCUs since 1944.
  - *Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF):* Over $500M funded for 50+ public HBCUs.
  - *Bright Futures Scholarship (Florida):* Accepts CLT exam scores; supports graduate studies.
- **International Scholarships:**  
  - *Kennedy Scholarship (UK):* Since 1963, supports UK students at Harvard/MIT, emphasizing public service leadership.
  - *Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany):* Leading merit scholarship since 1925.
  - *Norway Scholarship:* Established in 1919 for Oslo students to study at Oxford.
  - *Shuping Scholarship (China):* Assists talented, financially needy students since 1939.
  - *King’s Scholar (UK):* Prestigious, long-standing awards.
  - *Harkness Fellowship:* Supports international health policy research in the U.S.
  - *Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program:* Since 1992, offers Eurasian graduate internships in the U.S.

### Prestigious Leader-Focused Scholarships

- **Marshall Scholarship (UK):** Enables outstanding Americans to pursue postgraduate study in the UK.
- **Rhodes Scholarship:** Established in 1902, brings international students to Oxford; renowned alumni contribute diverse accomplishments.

### Fulbright Program and Honors

- The Fulbright Program, managed by the U.S. Department of State, is a leading international educational exchange initiative that provides grants for study, research, lecturing, and cultural exchange worldwide. It operates through bilateral commissions in 49 countries, U.S. embassies, and partner organizations. Specialized subprograms, such as Fulbright–Hays, support language and area studies. The program is further supported by active alumni associations fostering global cooperation.
- Originated in 1946 and expanded by the Fulbright–Hays Act in 1961, Fulbright is administered by the Institute of International Education but has faced recent challenges such as Russia's 2024 termination, U.S. funding freezes in 2025, and controversy over political interference.
- Over 325,000 alumni include 40 heads of state or government, 62 Nobel Prize winners, 89 Pulitzer Prize winners, and 78 MacArthur Fellows, among them Boutros Boutros-Ghali, Linus Pauling, Sylvia Plath, Joseph Stiglitz, Maria Ressa, Juan Manuel Santos, Rishi Sunak, and Muhammad Yunus.
- The **J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding**, created in 1993, recognizes individuals or organizations for major contributions to international understanding, with recipients such as Nelson Mandela, Kofi Annan, Angela Merkel, and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.

### Influential Figures and Honors

## Miss America Pageant

Founded in 1921, Miss America shifted from a beauty contest to focus on talent and scholarship, addressing issues of inclusion and representation. Historic firsts include winners from diverse racial, ethnic, and LGBTQ+ backgrounds. The pageant has wrestled with controversies around discrimination, credibility, and commercialization, and recent reforms include “Miss America 2.0,” virtual events during COVID-19, expanded eligibility, a fitness component, and declared bankruptcy in 2024. Recent titleholders are Cassie Donegan (2026), Abbie Stockard (2025), Madison Marsh (2024), Grace Stanke (2023), and Emma Broyles (2022).

## British Council

Formed in the 1930s, the British Council fosters global cultural and educational exchange, with programs in over 100 countries including Education UK, Erasmus+, Connecting Classrooms, teacher training, cultural activities, and entrepreneurial awards. It produces educational resources and is influential in arts, education, and international relations, despite criticism regarding transparency, priorities, exam fraud, and historic controversies. The organization is union-represented and remains a subject of strategic debate.
