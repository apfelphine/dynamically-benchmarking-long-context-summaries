# **Working Summary: The Fulbright Program – Mission, Structure, Administration, and Legacy**

Established in 1946 to foster mutual understanding through academic and cultural exchange, the Fulbright Program remains the United States' leading international educational initiative. Originally funded by war surplus sales, it operates bi-nationally (excluding clinical medical research) and is structured around four key pillars: **Scholar Grants** (for faculty and researchers), **Teacher Grants** (K–12 and post-secondary), **Professional Grants** (mid-career experts and artists), and the **Fulbright–Hays Program** (language and area studies).

**Governance and Administration**
The program is coordinated by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs (Fulbright) and the Department of Education (Fulbright–Hays). Policy and candidate approval are managed by the **Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board**, with implementation delegated to **Bi-national Fulbright Commissions** in 49 countries or U.S. Embassies. Support is further decentralized to entities like the Institute of International Education (IIE) and World Learning, while the **Fulbright Association** and **Fulbright Academy** focus on alumni support and professional advancement in science and technology, respectively.

**Contemporary Challenges and Geopolitical Context**
Despite its robust framework, the program faces significant geopolitical headwinds. In 2024, Russia declared its administering body "undesirable," and a 2025 funding freeze under the Trump administration, alongside board resignations, highlighted concerns over political interference. These issues contrast with the British Council's operational struggles, which include office closures in Russia and Syria, financial cuts in the Middle East and India, and controversies ranging from governance ethics to visa scams.

**Legacy and Global Impact**
The program boasts a distinguished track record, anchored by the **J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding**, established in 1993. Nelson Mandela was the first recipient, followed by global icons such as Angela Merkel (2018), Kizzmekia Corbett and Anthony Fauci (2022), and Rishi Sunak (2024). Over 325,000 alumni have achieved remarkable success, including 62 Nobel laureates, 78 MacArthur Fellows, 89 Pulitzer Prize winners, and 40 heads of state. Notable achievements include alumni serving as UN Secretaries-General, U.S. Senators, and Presidents of major nations like Brazil, Ghana, and Colombia, demonstrating the program's enduring influence in government, academia, industry, and the arts.
