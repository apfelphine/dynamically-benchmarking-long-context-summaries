**Scholarship and Memorial Programs:**
Global and domestic students benefit from numerous scholarships, including international options like the Bolashak, John F. Kennedy Memorial, Harkness Fellowship, Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes, Norway, and the Rhodes Scholarship (for Oxford postgraduate study). Domestic support encompasses the National Merit Scholarship Program, UNCF (for HBCUs since 1944), Shuping, Florida's Bright Futures, and TMCF (for HBCU entrepreneurship since 1987), alongside awards like the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award. The Fulbright Program (since 1946) promotes peace through education bi-nationally, offering diverse grants (Scholar, Teacher, professional) and specialized programs (Fulbright Distinguished Chair Awards, U.S. Scholar Program, Humphrey Program, Fulbright–Hays Program). This program is managed by the U.S. Department of State's ECA, utilizing bilateral commissions, but has faced geopolitical disruptions. Additionally, the J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding (est. 1993) honors those advancing global understanding, with distinguished recipients like Nelson Mandela and Jimmy Carter.

**Political Career and Legacy of Edmund S. Muskie:**
Edmund S. Muskie, a WWII veteran and Cornell law graduate, held prominent public service roles, including U.S. Senator (1958) and serving as the "father of the federal budget process" (Senate Budget Committee Chair, 1973–1980). His legislative impact includes supporting the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and passing the Clean Air Act (1963) and Clean Water Acts (1965 and 1972). Later, as U.S. Secretary of State under Jimmy Carter (1980–1981), he handled Soviet negotiations and secured the release of American hostages from Iran. He also established the Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program for Eurasian scholars posthumously.

**Miss America Pageant History and Evolution:**
Established in 1920/1921, the Miss America pageant evolved from awarding furs to providing college scholarships in 1944. The pageant has marked significant social progress, featuring the first African-American contestant in 1971, the first Black winner in 1984, and subsequent recognition for foreign-born/bilingual, deaf, Asian-American, and openly lesbian contestants. Format changes include the removal of the swimsuit competition in 2018. Following instability (2017–2022) under CEO Sam Haskell, Gretchen Carlson introduced "Miss America 2.0" in 2018, prioritizing talent and scholarship. In 2023, Robin Fleming took leadership, partially restoring the term "contestant" while retaining physical fitness judging, even as the operator faced bankruptcy in late 2024.

**The British Council: Mission, Scope, and Controversies:**
Sponsored by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, the British Council promotes UK culture and English language globally across over 100 countries since 1934. Its functions include English language teaching, administering IELTS, and running educational platforms like Education UK. Cultural outreach involves initiatives such as fiveFilms4freedom. While structured into seven regions, the Council has encountered operational issues, including closures in Russia and Syria due to geopolitics, and past concerns regarding outsourcing and teacher safety in Afghanistan post-2021. More recent criticisms involve reduced European focus, accountability issues in China's higher education marketing, findings of elitism by the National Audit Office, and problems related to its testing and visa programs.
