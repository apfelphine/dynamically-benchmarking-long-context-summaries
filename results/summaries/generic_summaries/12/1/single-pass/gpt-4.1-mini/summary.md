# Scholarship and Fellowship Programs Overview

## Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953 by the UK Parliament as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship supports intellectually distinguished young Americans to pursue postgraduate study in the UK. Unlike the Rhodes Scholarship, it was designed from inception to include women and married men under 28. The scholarship funds university fees and living expenses, traditionally enabling study at any UK university with a focus on fostering UK-US understanding. Selection emphasizes academic excellence, leadership potential, and character, with about 3-4% of endorsed applicants elected annually. The scholarship has grown in size, recently expanding to 50 places per year, and maintains an active alumni association that engages in transatlantic dialogues, including the annual Marshall Forum. Notable scholars include U.S. Supreme Court Justices Stephen Breyer and Neil Gorsuch.

## Miss America Pageant
Originating in 1921 in Atlantic City as a business promotion event, the Miss America Pageant evolved into a national competition emphasizing scholarships over time. Historically, it faced issues of racial and religious exclusion, integrating African-American contestants only from 1970 and officially rescinding racial segregation in 1950. The pageant has endured controversies related to sexism, racism, and organizational scandals, leading to leadership and format changes such as the removal and later partial restoration of judging on physical appearance. The pageant awards scholarships and includes talent and interview components. It has been a platform for social commentary and cultural change, with notable winners including Vanessa Williams and Nina Davuluri. Recent years have seen governance challenges and shifts in broadcast and venue.

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program and Legacy
Named after Edmund S. Muskie (1914–1996), a prominent U.S. politician, environmentalist, and diplomat, the Muskie Internship Program promotes professional exchange and career development for Eurasian scholars in the United States. Muskie’s career included serving as Governor of Maine, U.S. Senator, Democratic vice-presidential candidate, and Secretary of State. His legislative legacy notably includes significant contributions to environmental protection laws such as the Clean Air and Water Acts. The program fosters international understanding in line with his public service ethos.

## Fulbright Program
Founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international educational exchange as a path to peace, the Fulbright Program is the largest of its kind, operating bi-nationally with over 160 countries. It offers grants for students, scholars, teachers, and professionals to study, teach, or conduct research abroad across almost all academic disciplines. The program is managed by the U.S. Department of State and various bi-national commissions. Recent challenges include political interference allegations and suspension of activities in Russia. Fulbright alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, and heads of state globally.

## King’s Scholars
King’s Scholars originated in the 14th century as royal-funded scholarships for academically distinguished boys, notably at Eton College and Westminster School in the UK. At Eton, King’s Scholars (Collegers) live in the historical College, wear distinctive gowns, and partake in unique traditions. The title also applies to academic prize recipients at King’s College Cambridge and selected students in historic King’s Schools across England, which have evolved scholarship systems linked to academic or musical excellence.

## British Council
Founded in 1934 to promote British culture and education abroad, the British Council is a UK-chartered public organization operating in over 100 countries. It offers English language teaching and exams (including IELTS), cultural programming, and international education services. It manages substantial English proficiency testing, online courses, and mobility programs like Education UK and Erasmus+. The Council faces geopolitical challenges, notably in Russia where its operations were curtailed, and has been criticized for outsourcing functions and certain policy decisions. It also supports arts initiatives and cultural diplomacy, despite controversies around political neutrality and resource allocation.

## Norway Scholarship
Established in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to experience collegiate life at Oxford, the Norway Scholarship has supported studies at Wadham College and other Oxford venues. Though funding challenges occurred mid-century, renewals and expansions have sustained the program. Annual events and lectures with distinguished Oxford guests help maintain scholarly ties.

## Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 through Cecil Rhodes’s will to foster unity within the British Empire and strengthen UK-US ties, the Rhodes Scholarship enables students worldwide to pursue postgraduate degrees at the University of Oxford. It historically excluded women and faced criticism over colonialist origins and representation issues, including racial and gender exclusions resolved mainly by the late 20th century. Selection is based on intellect, character, leadership, and commitment to service. Rhodes Scholars have become influential in government, academia, human rights, science, and arts. The scholarship remains highly competitive and influential, inspiring other global awards.

## Kennedy Scholarship
Created in 1964 as a British memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this scholarship funds British nationals to study at Harvard or MIT. Trustees, appointed by the UK Prime Minister, select scholars based on intellectual achievement and suitability for study in the US. The scholarship complements other transatlantic awards like the Rhodes and Marshall.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
A Florida state merit-based scholarship program established to fund high school graduates attending postsecondary institutions in Florida. It provides several award levels based on GPA, standardized test scores (including SAT, ACT, and CLT), and community service or work hours. The program experienced funding cuts and policy changes affecting eligibility and benefits, with controversies around racial disparities and administrative reforms since inception.

## Harkness Fellowship
Originating in 1925 as Commonwealth Fund Fellowships to reciprocate Rhodes Scholarships, the Harkness Fellowships provide a prestigious one-year research opportunity in health policy and practice in the United States, targeting fellows from multiple countries. Fellows conduct research at leading US institutions and participate in seminars and briefings.

## National Merit Scholarship Program
A US academic merit scholarship program based on PSAT/NMSQT testing for high school juniors. Students progress through stages from Commended to Semifinalist, Finalist, and Scholar with scholarships awarded based on test scores, academic records, and extracurricular achievements. The program includes corporate-sponsored scholarships and has been criticized for overreliance on standardized testing.

## Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by philanthropist Koo with the mission to support academically excellent but financially disadvantaged students. The program offers continuous funding through secondary and tertiary education and includes a scholar-run library and extracurricular activities. After a wartime hiatus, the scholarships resumed in the 1980s and now support hundreds of students annually across multiple schools.

## Bolashak Scholarship
Established by Kazakhstan’s government in 1993 to send the country’s youth abroad for education and inculcate western democratic values. Thousands have been awarded scholarships to study internationally, with a large number returning to Kazakhstan to occupy leadership roles. The program involves rigorous selection including language and psychological testing.

## Studienstiftung (German Academic Scholarship Foundation)
Founded in 1925 and reestablished in 1948, it is Germany’s largest and oldest scholarship foundation, awarding financial support and academic development to outstanding students and doctoral candidates without citizenship or age restrictions. Admission is through multiple channels including school and university nominations, competitions, and assessment tests. It offers additional internal and open scholarship programs, summer academies, and supports over tens of thousands of scholars annually.

## UNCF (United Negro College Fund)
Founded in 1944 to support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and their students, UNCF administers scholarships open to all but primarily benefits African Americans. It has raised significant funds through high-profile donors and events, supporting thousands of students pursuing higher education and careers across various fields.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund
Formed in 1987 to support public HBCUs, TMCF focuses on scholarships, programmatic capacity building, and entrepreneurship among member schools. It differs from UNCF by its institutional focus and represents 55 member institutions, mostly HBCUs. It has received major donations from corporations and philanthropists and advocates for educational advancement within its network.
