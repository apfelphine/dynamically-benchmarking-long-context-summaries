# Marshall Scholarship
The Marshall Scholarship was established in 1953 as a living memorial to George C. Marshall. Its primary objectives are to enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK, foster an appreciation of contemporary Britain, advance knowledge in various fields, and motivate scholars to act as ambassadors between the US and UK. Initially limited to single men under 25, the scholarship expanded to include women and married men under 28. The selection process requires university endorsement, followed by interviews coordinated through regional British embassy/consulate regions.

The scholarship has seen significant evolution, with cohorts increasing over time, culminating in a 25% funding expansion announced in 2023 to increase places to 50 annually. Scholars typically pursue postgraduate courses at prestigious UK universities like Oxford and Cambridge. The Association of Marshall Scholars (AMS) hosts the Marshall Forum annually, fostering dialogue on transatlantic issues.

# Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 under Cecil John Rhodes' will, the Rhodes Scholarship aimed to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the United States. While founded on Rhodes's Anglo-supremacist beliefs, the Trust has been modified, and the scholarship is now globally open, though historically focused on constituencies.

Selection criteria emphasize literary and scholastic attainments, courage, duty, and leadership potential. The scholarship has faced historical controversies regarding the exclusion of women and Black Africans. Despite this, it is recognized as one of the world's most competitive scholarships, and its alumni have achieved prominence across academia, science, and public service, though criticism persists over the trend of recipients entering private business rather than public service.

# Fulbright Program
The Fulbright Program, established in 1946 following World War II, seeks to promote peace and understanding through educational exchange between the U.S. and numerous countries. It operates bi-nationally, with bilateral agreements managed by commissions for each country.

The program offers various grants, including the U.S. Student Program for American students studying abroad, and the Foreign Student Program for international scholars studying in the U.S. Candidates must demonstrate high academic achievement, leadership potential, and adaptability. Recently, the program has faced disruption due to Russian government actions and allegations of political interference from the Trump administration.

# Kennedy Scholarship
The Kennedy Scholarship was created in 1964 to serve as a living memorial to John F. Kennedy. It provides scholarships for individuals to attend either Harvard or MIT. Selection criteria weigh intellectual attainment, communication ability, suitability of the proposed study, and potential for public service.

Notable alumni have made significant impacts in politics, government, economics, and academia. The trustees evaluate candidates based on these criteria, aiming for a program comparable to the Rhodes and Marshall Scholarships.

# Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program is a professional exchange program supported by the U.S. Department of State, offering summer internships and training to Eurasian scholars studying in the United States. It was formally established in 1992 and later relaunched by Cultural Vistas in 2015.

# Bright Futures Scholarship Program
The Bright Futures Scholarship Program in Florida, originally emulating the HOPE Scholarship, was based solely on academic merit, not financial need. Over time, the program underwent several overhauls, including increased SAT/ACT score requirements and changes to award levels (FAS, FMS, GSV, GSC).

The program faced scrutiny for potential racial bias, though investigations found no evidence of discriminatory intent. The scholarship requires meeting specific GPA and service hour requirements, and awards are renewable for up to five years.

# Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) through scholarships and programmatic support. Its mission is distinct from the UNCF, focusing on 55 specific schools.

TMCF has received substantial donations, including from Apple and the Charles Koch Foundation. The organization also acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) to enhance talent identification and entrepreneurship within HBCUs.

# UNCF
Founded in 1944 by activists like William J. Trent, the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) was established to address educational funding inequities for African Americans. While historically serving African Americans, the scholarships are open to all ethnicities.

The UNCF has been supported by major philanthropic donations, such as those from MacKenzie Scott. The organization operates a telethon, the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars," to fund its mission, summarized by its motto, "A mind is a terrible thing to waste."

# Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award benefits an African woman student and is part of the "New Daughters of Africa" anthology, which collects writing by women of African descent. The launch of the award was supported by donations from Margaret Busby and the anthology's earnings, with the first recipient being announced in 2020.

# Shuping Scholarship
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by Koo, the Shuping Scholarships were established to support poor, academically gifted students, embodying the motto "Return to the Society what you have gained from it." The initial focus included providing educational, medical, and vocational assistance.

The scholarships were revived in 1986, and today they support students in 37 secondary schools and 24 tertiary institutions. The administration is managed by two Alumni Associations.

# Bolashak
The Bolashak scholarship, meaning "Future," was initiated by Kazakhstan's Nursultan Nazarbayev in 1993 to instill Western, democratic values in the youth. The program has expanded significantly, with over 12,800 scholarships granted by 2018.

Selection requires adherence to competition rules, and applicants undergo language and psychological testing. The scholarship provides opportunities for students to study in various international universities across multiple continents.

# King's Scholar Programs
The term "King's Scholar" is used across several prestigious UK institutions with different meanings. At Eton College, a King's Scholar (Colleger) is a high-achieving student in the oldest house, receiving a foundation scholarship.

At King's College Cambridge, it denotes undergraduates who attain first-class degrees and receive a prize. The term is also used for scholars at various other King's Schools across England, where the scholarship recognizes academic or musical attainment, sometimes accompanied by fee discounts.

# British Council
Governed by a Royal Charter, the British Council promotes knowledge of the UK and the English language globally, encouraging cultural and educational understanding. It operates in over 100 countries and engages in various activities, including English teaching, running global IELTS tests, and cultural programs.

The Council has faced controversies, including allegations of politically motivated closures in Russia and concerns regarding outsourcing key functions to Indian partners, despite its efforts to promote UK culture.

# Comparative Scholarship Overview
The Marshall, Rhodes, and Kennedy Scholarships are highly selective programs with similar structures. The Marshall Scholarship is distinguished by its openness to women and married men, while the Rhodes Scholarship is rooted in its founder's legacy and has seen debates over its colonialist history. The Fulbright Program emphasizes educational exchange on a bilateral basis, while the Kennedy Scholarship is specifically tied to a memorial to JFK and focuses on studies at Harvard or MIT.

# Miscellaneous Scholarships
*   **Norwegian Oxford Scholarship:** Conceived in 1919, this scholarship facilitates Norwegian students studying at Wadham College in Oxford, rooted in fostering close cultural association between Norway and Britain.
*   **Studienstiftung:** This German-based scholarship offers both financial and academic support for exceptional talent in Germany or the EU/Switzerland. Admission is based on excellence, initiative, and responsibility, through nomination or assessment testing.
*   **National Merit Scholarship Program:** This US program uses the PSAT/NMSQT as its initial screen to identify top academic talent, leading to various levels of recognition and financial awards, though it faces criticism over relying on a single standardized test.
