### Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953 to honor George C. Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship enables intellectually distinguished American students to study in the UK. It aims to foster mutual understanding between the U.S. and UK through academic exchange, with scholars gaining exposure to British society and contributing to long-term bilateral relations. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership potential, and personal growth. Over time, it has evolved in scope and size, with the number of scholars increasing from 12 to 50 annually. Notable alumni include Stephen Breyer, Ray Dolby, and Thomas Friedman. The Association of Marshall Scholars organizes events like the Marshall Forum to promote transatlantic dialogue and awards the Marshall Medal to distinguished Americans.

### Miss America
Originating in 1919, the Miss America pageant began as a beauty contest to boost tourism in Atlantic City. Over time, it evolved into a platform for promoting women’s roles and societal values. The pageant faced criticism for sexism and racism, particularly during the 1960s and 1970s, leading to protests and a shift in focus. In 2018, it adopted a new format ("Miss America 2.0") to reduce emphasis on physical appearance and promote personal development. The pageant has seen milestones such as the first African-American contestant in 1970 and the first Indian-American winner in 2014. It has also faced controversies over scholarship claims and financial practices.

### Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
Established in 1992, the Muskie Internship Program supports Eurasian scholars and graduate students studying in the U.S. It fosters international academic exchange and cultural understanding. Administered by the U.S. Department of State, the program provides funding, mentorship, and career development opportunities. The program has been expanded and restructured, including a 2015 partnership with Cultural Vistas. It reflects a commitment to promoting diplomacy and mutual understanding through education.

### Fulbright Program
Founded in 1946, the Fulbright Program promotes international educational exchange through scholarships and cultural programs. It operates on a bi-national basis with over 160 countries and supports students, scholars, and professionals in various fields. The program emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and cross-cultural understanding. In recent years, it has faced challenges due to political interference, including a 2025 funding freeze and controversy over political screening. Despite these issues, it remains one of the most prestigious international education programs, with notable alumni in politics, science, and the arts.

### King's Scholar
The King's Scholar designation originated in 1317 at Cambridge and was later established at Eton College in 1440. At Eton, scholars are selected through competitive examinations and receive a Foundation Scholarship, allowing them to live in College and participate in academic and social traditions. The title is also used at King's College Cambridge for undergraduates with first-class degrees. The King's Schools in Canterbury, Chester, Ely, Gloucester, Peterborough, Rochester, and Worcester award scholarships based on academic or musical excellence. These programs emphasize character development, service, and leadership.

### British Council
Founded in 1934, the British Council promotes knowledge of the UK and English language globally. It operates in over 100 countries, offering language teaching, cultural programs, and educational exchanges. The organization supports initiatives like the "English for Peace" project and the "Shakespeare Lives" global celebration. It has faced criticism over outsourcing and political influence, particularly in India and Russia. Despite controversies, it remains a key player in international education and cultural diplomacy.

### Norway Scholarship
Established in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, the Norway Scholarship enables Norwegian students to study at Wadham College, Oxford. It began as a way to strengthen ties between Norway and Britain during wartime. Over time, the program has adapted to rising costs and has seen a revival in the 1980s. The scholarship emphasizes academic excellence and personal development, with scholars engaging in community service and cultural activities. The program continues to support Norwegian students in pursuing advanced degrees in various disciplines.

### Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902, the Rhodes Scholarship provides postgraduate students from around the world with the opportunity to study at the University of Oxford. It promotes global understanding and leadership through academic excellence and service to society. The program is selective, with a global acceptance rate of 0.7%, and has produced notable leaders in politics, science, and human rights. Women were admitted in 1977, and the program has expanded to include global participants. The scholarship is often compared to the Marshall and Fulbright programs, with a strong emphasis on leadership, integrity, and public service.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Established in 1964, the Kennedy Scholarship enables British nationals to study at Harvard or MIT. It was created as a reciprocal to the Rhodes Scholarship and aims to strengthen U.S.-UK relations. The program emphasizes leadership, public service, and academic excellence. Notable recipients include politicians, economists, and public servants. The scholarship has been instrumental in shaping leaders in various fields and continues to support future leaders in government and civil service.

### Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
Launched in 2019, the Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award supports African women students through financial aid and mentorship. It is part of a broader initiative to highlight and celebrate the contributions of Black women in literature and culture. The award, established in partnership with SOAS and Myriad Editions, aims to promote equity and representation in education. It has been instrumental in supporting emerging African female voices in literature and has helped foster a sense of community and empowerment among young women.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program
Created in 2008, the Bright Futures Scholarship in Florida provides financial support to high school graduates based on academic merit. The program offers scholarships at various levels, including A-level, B-level, and vocational tracks. It has faced criticism for its impact on low-income students and for increasing SAT score requirements over time. Despite controversy, it remains a major source of college funding in Florida, with significant influence on student enrollment and academic achievement.

### Harkness Fellowship
Established in 1925, the Harkness Fellowship enables British graduates to study in the U.S., promoting cultural and academic exchange. The program, now focused on health care policy, supports fellows in conducting research and attending seminars in U.S. institutions. It has produced notable alumni in health, science, and public service. The fellowship emphasizes leadership, research, and policy development, contributing to international collaboration in health care.

### National Merit Scholarship Program
Established in 1955, the National Merit Scholarship Program identifies high-achieving U.S. high school seniors through the PSAT/NMSQT exam. It offers scholarships to top students based on academic performance, extracurricular involvement, and recommendations. The program has a strong focus on academic excellence and has produced numerous prominent leaders in business, science, and public service. It has also faced criticism for relying on standardized testing and for its limited diversity.

### Shuping Scholarship
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing, the Shuping Scholarship supports poor, academically excellent students in China. It provides full tuition coverage and encourages service to the community. Over the decades, it has helped thousands of students become leaders in education, healthcare, and public service. The program emphasizes character development, volunteerism, and service to society. It has been revived in the 1980s and continues to support students in China.

### Bolashak
Established in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s President Nazarbayev, the Bolashak Scholarship supports young Kazakhs studying abroad to gain Western values and skills. It has grown significantly, with over 12,800 scholarships awarded by 2018. The program emphasizes leadership, initiative, and responsibility. Most recipients go on to hold key positions in state and private organizations. The scholarship has been criticized for its ethnic bias, with Kazakh applicants dominating the pool.

### Studienstiftung
Founded in 1925, the Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes supports German and international students through scholarships. It emphasizes excellence, initiative, and responsibility in academic and personal development. The organization offers financial and academic support, including summer academies and mentoring. It has a strong focus on research and leadership, with notable alumni in science, politics, and the arts.

### UNCF
Established in 1944, the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) supports African-American students through scholarships and institutional funding. It promotes access to higher education and has produced many leaders in business, politics, and the arts. The organization has received major donations, including from John F. Kennedy and Reed Hastings, and continues to advocate for educational equity. It supports historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and promotes financial stability and long-term sustainability.

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund
Established in 1987, the Thurgood Marshall College Fund supports public historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It provides scholarships, programmatic support, and capacity-building initiatives. The fund has raised over $500 million and supports 55 member institutions. It advocates for equity in higher education and has received major donations from Apple, Koch Foundation, and Boeing. The fund emphasizes leadership development and entrepreneurship in HBCU communities.
