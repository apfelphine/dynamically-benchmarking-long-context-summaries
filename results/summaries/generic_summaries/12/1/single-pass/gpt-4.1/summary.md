# Summary of Major Scholarship, Exchange, and Fellowship Programs

---

## American and British Prestigious Scholarships

### Marshall Scholarship
Established in 1953 as a UK's living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the Marshall Scholarship enables outstanding young Americans to study at any UK university. It focuses on fostering transatlantic understanding, leadership, and academic excellence. Selection is highly competitive, favoring intellectual distinction and leadership potential. Scholars receive two years of funding, covering tuition and living expenses, with eligibility based primarily on academic achievement and university endorsement. Harvard, Princeton, Yale, and Stanford are top alma maters. Marshall alumni include Supreme Court Justices, Nobel laureates, and influential figures in government, academia, and industry.

### Rhodes Scholarship
Founded in 1902 from Cecil Rhodes’s will, the Rhodes Scholarship selects exceptional students globally for postgraduate study at Oxford University. Its aim is to cultivate leaders and promote unity among English-speaking nations. Selection is based on intellectual distinction, moral character, leadership, and physical vigor; initially only men, it became coeducational in 1977. Rhodes Scholars have gone on to become heads of state, Nobel laureates, and major contributors in law, science, and social justice. Controversies have centered on its colonial past and recipient diversity.

### Kennedy Scholarship
Created in 1964 as a UK memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this program annually funds up to 10 British graduates for advanced study at Harvard or MIT. Selection emphasizes intellectual attainment, leadership, and public service. Kennedy Scholars have distinguished careers in politics, academia, law, and journalism.

### Fulbright Program
Founded in 1946, the Fulbright Program advances international educational and cultural exchange, supporting U.S. and foreign students, scholars, and professionals for study and research. It is managed through the U.S. Department of State and bi-national commissions worldwide. Recent years have seen political interference and funding challenges, with board resignations citing ideological screening. Fulbright alumni include Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, and heads of state, reflecting its global prestige and impact.

### Harkness Fellowship
Originated in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, Harkness Fellowships were the American reciprocal to Rhodes, enabling British and later Commonwealth professionals to study in the U.S. Now focused on health care policy, fellows conduct research at leading U.S. institutions and participate in policy briefings. Notable alumni have shaped science, academia, journalism, and public policy.

### National Merit Scholarship Program
This U.S. academic scholarship program uses PSAT/NMSQT scores to initially screen over 1.5 million students yearly, with finalists and scholars selected through further achievements, recommendations, and essays. About 8,000 scholarships are awarded annually, including additional corporate and college-sponsored options. The program faces criticism over reliance on standardized tests and transparency. Notable scholars include leading public figures in academia, business, and the arts.

## Global and Regional Scholarship Programs

### King's Scholar (UK)
Dating to 1317, King's Scholarships are prestigious academic awards at institutions such as Eton College, Westminster School, and King's College Cambridge, based on competitive examination and academic excellence. Named scholars enjoy significant historical privileges and include many notable alumni in government, literature, and science.

### Norway Scholarship
Launched in 1920, it funds Norwegian university students for one year at Oxford (Wadham College). Despite fluctuations in funding, a dedicated committee sustains annual awards.

### Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida, U.S.)
Funded by state lottery revenues since the late 1990s, Bright Futures supports Florida students at in-state colleges based chiefly on academic merit. The program has undergone significant changes to eligibility and funding, particularly impacting underrepresented minority students. It currently provides four scholarship levels and recognizes top scholars in each county.

### Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in Shanghai in 1939 by philanthropist Koo Soh Bing, Shuping Scholarships support academically gifted, financially needy students, emphasizing ongoing support and character-building service. The program has awarded thousands of scholarships and fostered lifelong alumni engagement.

### Bolashak (Kazakhstan)
Established in 1993 by President Nazarbayev, Bolashak allows Kazakhstani students to study at leading global universities, aiming to cultivate Western-oriented professionals. By 2018, nearly 13,000 recipients were trained; the program shapes national leadership, though recipients sometimes face barriers implementing reforms.

### Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Germany’s largest and most prestigious merit-based scholarship, founded in 1925, provides financial and academic support to high-achieving students and doctoral candidates, regardless of age or citizenship. Admission relies largely on nomination or competitive assessment. The foundation supports about 15,000 scholars annually, with numerous distinguished alumni in sciences, arts, business, and public service.

## Historically Black College and University (HBCU) Scholarships

### United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944, the UNCF raises and distributes scholarships for students at member HBCUs and beyond, focusing on closing educational funding gaps for African Americans. Its fundraising legacy includes telethons and major donations, supporting institutions that have produced notable Black leaders. Recent major gifts aim to address long-term financial stability and sustainability for HBCUs.

### Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987, TMCF supports students at public HBCUs, PBIs, and community colleges, offering scholarships, career support, and advocacy. It has raised substantial funding for member institutions and expanded its reach through partnerships and acquisitions. TMCF member schools range from major universities to law and medical schools.

## Major Fellowships and Awards for Black Women Writers

### Margaret Busby (New) Daughters of Africa Award
Inspired by Busby's ground-breaking anthologies "Daughters of Africa" and "New Daughters of Africa" celebrating women writers of African descent, the Award supports African women students at SOAS University of London. The anthologies have garnered wide praise for their diversity and representation, becoming essential literary references and influencing generations of writers.

## British Council
Founded in 1934, the British Council is the UK’s official cultural, educational, and language diplomacy organization. It offers English teaching, standardized exams, international school partnerships, university networks, cultural and arts programs, and mobility schemes globally. Notable for running IELTS with partners, and for its cultural exchanges, it also faces recent closures in Russia, funding challenges, and controversies over outsourcing, staff welfare, and cultural policy. The Council works in over 100 countries despite periodic restructuring and criticisms regarding priorities and accountability.
