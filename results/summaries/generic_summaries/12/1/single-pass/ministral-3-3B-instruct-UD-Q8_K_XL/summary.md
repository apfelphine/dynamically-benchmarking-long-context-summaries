### **Marshall Scholarship**

#### **History**
The Marshall Scholarship was established in 1953 as a living memorial to U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall. Initially conceived to partner with the Rhodes Scholarship, it was instead designed to be open to women and married men under 28, a departure from the Rhodes Scholarship’s restrictions. The scholarship was created to foster understanding and cooperation between the U.S. and the UK, with its founding driven by the need to counter isolationism and promote international unity. The first awards were made in 1954, with notable early recipients including Hugh Gallagher, the first wheelchair user to be selected, and John Willis, the first African American to receive a scholarship. Over the years, the program expanded its selection criteria and geographic reach, including the introduction of one-year awards in 2010 and a significant increase in scholarships in 2023, reaching 51 students in 2024.

#### **Objectives**
The Marshall Scholarship aims to:
1. Enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK.
2. Foster an understanding and appreciation of contemporary British society.
3. Contribute to advancements in science, technology, humanities, social sciences, and the creative arts.
4. Motivate scholars to act as ambassadors between the U.S. and the UK.
5. Promote personal and academic fulfillment for each scholar.

#### **Selection**
The selection process involves university endorsements, regional interviews, and final committee decisions. Criteria include academic achievement, leadership potential, and motivation for study in the UK. The program emphasizes intellectual distinction and character, with a minimum GPA of 3.7. Notable institutions producing Marshall Scholars include Harvard, Princeton, Yale, Stanford, and MIT. Scholarships cover tuition and living expenses for two years, with options for one-year awards.

#### **Academic Destinations**
Traditionally, Marshall Scholars study at prestigious UK institutions such as Oxford, Cambridge, London School of Economics, University College London, and King’s College London. Scholars are also encouraged to engage with broader UK academic and cultural life.

#### **Comparison to Other Scholarships**
The Marshall Scholarship shares similarities with the Rhodes Scholarship and Fulbright Program but offers greater flexibility in terms of study duration and university choice. Unlike the Rhodes Scholarship, which is limited to single men under 25, the Marshall Scholarship is open to a broader range of applicants. It is also more selective than the Fulbright Program, with a similar acceptance rate to the Marshall and Gates Cambridge Scholarships.

### **Miss America Pageant**

#### **Overview and History**
The Miss America pageant originated in 1919, though its modern form began in Atlantic City in 1921. Initially, the pageant was criticized for promoting "loose morals" and was temporarily shut down during the Great Depression. The pageant evolved significantly over the decades, including the introduction of talent competitions in 1938, the addition of a chaperone requirement, and the shift from furs and movie contracts to college scholarships in 1944. The pageant faced protests during the 1960s and 1970s due to its perceived sexism, racism, and militarism, leading to significant changes, including the adoption of a new format in 2018, referred to as "Miss America 2.0," which eliminated the swimsuit competition and emphasized talent and scholarship over physical appearance.

#### **Notable Events and Changes**
Key milestones include Cheryl Browne becoming the first African American contestant in 1971, Vanessa Williams winning in 1984 and resigning due to nude photos, and Nina Davuluri becoming the first Indian-American winner in 2014. The pageant has also faced controversies, such as the 2018 scandal involving derogatory emails from the organization’s leadership and the 2023 reversal of some of the "Miss America 2.0" changes. Recent years have seen efforts to modernize the pageant, including the introduction of a transgender titleholder in 2022.

#### **Recent Developments**
In 2023, the pageant reversed some of its earlier changes, including the return to judging contestants based on physical appearance and the reinstatement of the swimsuit competition. The pageant continues to evolve, reflecting broader cultural shifts and addressing ongoing criticisms of its practices.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**

#### **Overview**
The Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program is a professional exchange program supported by the U.S. Department of State. It provides summer internships, career training, and financial support to scholars and graduate students from Eurasian countries studying in the United States. The program was established in 1992 and was named in honor of Edmund S. Muskie, a prominent U.S. politician known for his contributions to diplomacy, civil rights, and environmental policy.

#### **Legacy and Honors**
Edmund S. Muskie was a key figure in U.S. history, serving as Governor of Maine, U.S. Senator, Secretary of State, and a prominent advocate for environmental protection and civil rights. His legacy includes significant achievements in environmental legislation, such as the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts, and his role in shaping U.S. foreign policy. Muskie was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom and has been honored with numerous awards and memorials, including honorary degrees from several universities and the establishment of the Edmund S. Muskie School of Public Service at the University of Southern Maine.

### **Fulbright Program**

#### **History**
The Fulbright Program was established in 1945 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international exchange and understanding through educational opportunities. Initially focused on graduate education, the program expanded in 1961 with the Fulbright-Hays Act, which included funding for international fairs, translations, and modern foreign language training. The program operates on a bi-national basis, with each country partnering with the U.S. government to administer scholarships.

#### **Program Structure**
The Fulbright Program offers various grants for students, scholars, and professionals, including the Fulbright U.S. Student Program, Fulbright Foreign Student Program, and Fulbright Scholar Program. It provides funding for research, study, teaching, and professional development in numerous countries. The program emphasizes cultural exchange and the development of international relationships.

#### **Controversies and Challenges**
The Fulbright Program has faced challenges, including political interference and funding freezes. In 2025, eleven members of the Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board resigned in protest over alleged political interference by the Trump administration. The program continues to be a cornerstone of U.S. international education efforts, though it has faced scrutiny over its administration and the influence of political agendas.

### **Rhodes Scholarship**

#### **History and Founding**
The Rhodes Scholarship was established in 1902 under the terms of Cecil Rhodes' will, aiming to promote unity within the British Empire and strengthen diplomatic ties between Britain and the United States. Initially open only to men, the scholarship was coeducationalized in 1977. The Rhodes Trust administers the scholarships, which are awarded to students from over 64 countries worldwide.

#### **Selection and Criteria**
The Rhodes Scholarship is highly selective, with applicants required to demonstrate academic excellence, leadership potential, and a commitment to public service. Selection criteria include literary and scholastic attainments, energy, truth, courage, devotion to duty, and a broad outlook. The scholarship covers tuition and living expenses for two years, with options for one-year or three-year awards.

#### **Notable Scholars and Impact**
The Rhodes Scholarship has produced many notable figures, including leaders in politics, academia, medicine, and the arts. The scholarship has been criticized for its historical association with colonialism and its exclusion of women and non-white applicants until recent reforms. Despite these controversies, the Rhodes Scholarship remains one of the most prestigious international scholarships.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**

#### **Creation and Purpose**
The Kennedy Scholarship was established in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy. It allows British nationals to study at either Harvard University or the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) for a year. The scholarship aims to honor Kennedy’s legacy of promoting peace, freedom, and democratic values.

#### **Selection Process**
Selection for the Kennedy Scholarship is based on academic merit, readiness to express themselves, and the suitability of their proposed course of study. The program is comparable to the Rhodes Scholarship and Marshall Scholarship in terms of selection criteria and standards.

#### **Notable Scholars**
Prominent former Kennedy Scholars include politicians, economists, journalists, and academics. The scholarship has supported individuals who have made significant contributions to public service and academic excellence.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**

#### **Overview**
The Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award is an annual literary award celebrating the work of women of African descent. The award honors the contributions of women writers from Africa and the diaspora, emphasizing their diverse voices and cultural heritage.

#### **Influence and Legacy**
The award has significantly influenced the literary landscape, providing a platform for underrepresented voices and fostering a sense of community among women writers of African descent. It has inspired numerous anthologies and literary initiatives, contributing to the broader recognition of African women’s writing.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**

#### **History and Structure**
The Bright Futures Scholarship Program is a merit-based scholarship program in Florida, established to emulate the HOPE Scholarship from Georgia. It provides financial support to Florida high school graduates based on academic achievement. The program has undergone significant changes, including increased minimum SAT scores and adjustments to the award amounts, reflecting shifts in educational priorities and funding.

#### **Scholarship Levels**
The program offers four levels of scholarships: Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholar (GSV), and Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholar (GSC). Each level has specific academic and extracurricular requirements and provides varying levels of financial support.

### **Harkness Fellowship**

#### **History**
The Harkness Fellowship was established in 1925 by Edward Stephen Harkness as a way to enable British graduates to study in the United States. It was later expanded to include students from other countries and is now focused on health care policy and practice.

#### **Current Program**
The Harkness Fellowships provide a year of research and study in the U.S., offering a deep understanding of the U.S. health care system and policy challenges. Fellows attend seminars and engage in policy discussions, contributing to international collaboration in health care.

#### **Notable Alumni**
The fellowship has produced many notable figures, including scientists, economists, politicians, and academics, who have made significant contributions to their respective fields.

### **National Merit Scholarship Program**

#### **Overview**
The National Merit Scholarship Program is an annual competition for high school seniors based on their performance on the Preliminary SAT/National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test (PSAT/NMSQT). The program recognizes students for their academic achievement and offers scholarships to finalists and semifinalists.

#### **Selection Process**
Selection is based on a national Selection Index score, which combines scores from the PSAT/NMSQT and standardized tests. Semifinalists must meet additional criteria, including academic records and recommendations, to advance to the finalist level.

#### **Scholarships**
The program offers various scholarships, including National Merit $2,500 Scholarships and college-sponsored Merit Scholarships. The National Merit Scholarship Corporation supports these awards, with corporate sponsors contributing additional funds.

### **Shuping Scholarship**

#### **History**
The Shuping Scholarship was founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in memory of his father, in recognition of the many academically excellent students who had to give up schooling due to financial hardship. The scholarship provides financial support and extracurricular activities to students from poor families, encouraging them to pursue higher education.

#### **Activities and Support**
The scholarship includes a library, science laboratories, and free lessons in various subjects. It also supports voluntary charitable works and provides financial aid to students in need. The Shuping Scholarship Foundation continues to support the program, ensuring its sustainability and growth.

### **Bolashak Scholarship Program**

#### **Overview**
The Bolashak Scholarship Program was established in 1993 by the first President of Kazakhstan, Nursultan Nazarbayev, to promote international exchange and Western democratic values among Kazakh youth. The program provides funding for students to study abroad, with a focus on developing their skills and understanding of global perspectives.

#### **Ethnic Distribution and Universities**
The scholarship is predominantly awarded to Kazakh students, though it also supports students from other ethnic groups. It offers opportunities to study at leading universities in Australia, Austria, Canada, China, Germany, Israel, Japan, and the United States, among others.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**

#### **History and Structure**
The Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes is a prestigious scholarship program in Germany, offering financial and academic support to exceptionally talented students and doctoral candidates. It operates under the principles of "excellence, initiative, and responsibility" and is open to students from Germany, the EU, and Switzerland.

#### **Admission Process**
Admission is based on academic merit, extracurricular activities, and social commitment. Students can be nominated by schools, professors, or through an assessment test. Scholars receive financial support, including a monthly allowance and subsidies for health insurance, as well as academic opportunities such as summer schools and workshops.

#### **Notable Alumni**
The Studienstiftung has produced many notable figures, including scientists, economists, politicians, and artists, who have made significant contributions to their fields.

### **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)**

#### **Overview**
The United Negro College Fund (UNCF) is a nonprofit organization dedicated to advancing education for African American students. It provides scholarships, grants, and other forms of support to students attending its member colleges and universities, as well as to those studying elsewhere.

#### **Scholarships and History**
UNCF-administered scholarships are open to all ethnicities but have historically supported African American students. The organization has received significant donations, including contributions from John F. Kennedy and Walter Annenberg, and has organized events like the "Lou Rawls Parade of Stars" telethon to raise funds for scholarships.

#### **Member Institutions**
UNCF supports over 900 colleges and universities, including historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It has played a crucial role in advancing education and social mobility for African American students.

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)**

#### **History and Mission**
The Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF) was established in 1987 to support public historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It has grown into a significant organization providing scholarships, capacity-building support, and programmatic support to its member schools and students.

#### **Acquisitions and Leadership**
TMCF has acquired organizations like the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) to enhance its efforts in supporting HBCUs. It has received substantial donations, including $50 million from Apple, $25.6 million from the Charles Koch Foundation, and $6 million from The Boeing Company.

#### **Member Schools**
TMCF supports 55 schools, including 42 HBCUs, 3 Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), 7 Historically Black Community Colleges, and 1 technical college. These institutions are committed to providing high-quality education and fostering academic excellence.
