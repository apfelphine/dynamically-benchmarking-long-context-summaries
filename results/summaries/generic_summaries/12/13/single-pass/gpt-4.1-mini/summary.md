# Summary of Scholarship Programs, Cultural Organizations, and Notable Figures

---

## National Merit Scholarship Program
A U.S. academic competition requiring students to be full-time high schoolers and U.S. citizens or lawful residents, qualifying via the PSAT/NMSQT. About 1.5 million students take the PSAT, with approximately 50,000 earning Commended status, 16,000 becoming Semifinalists (state-based quotas apply), and 15,000 advancing to Finalists by submitting SAT scores and essays. Around 8,000 Finalists receive scholarships funded mainly by NMSC and corporate sponsors. The National Achievement Scholarship Program (1964–2015) focused on African-American students but transferred to the United Negro College Fund (UNCF) post-2015, now supporting underrepresented college graduates. Select notable Scholars include Bill Gates, Jeff Bezos, and Elena Kagan. Major universities enroll large numbers of National Merit Scholars, often using them to attract high-achieving students. The program faces criticism for reliance on standardized testing and transparency issues.

---

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Florida)
Inspired by Georgia's HOPE Scholarship, Bright Futures provides merit-based scholarships for Florida high school graduates meeting academic and volunteer/work criteria. It has four levels: Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Gold Seal Vocational (GSV), and Gold Seal CAPE (GSC), supporting both college-preparatory and vocational students. Funding has fluctuated—initially covering full tuition but cut back in 2011, with later partial restoration and ongoing adjustments. The program has faced scrutiny for racial disparities and policy changes affecting eligibility and award amounts. Scholarships require renewal based on GPA and credit completion. Recently, the program began accepting Classic Learning Test scores and modified some award criteria.

---

## Bolashak Scholarship (Kazakhstan)
Established in 1993 by President Nursultan Nazarbayev, Bolashak funds study abroad for Kazakh youth, emphasizing Western values. From 1994 to 2018, over 12,800 scholarships were awarded primarily to ethnic Kazakhs due to language requirements. The program supports study in over 30 countries at leading universities across the globe, including institutions in Australia, the UK, Germany, Russia, the US, and more. Many Bolashak alumni hold key positions in Kazakhstan’s public and private sectors.

---

## Kennedy Scholarship (UK)
Created as a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy, this British scholarship funds postgraduate study at Harvard or MIT for UK nationals. Selection involves a competitive process emphasizing intellectual ability, articulation, leadership, public service commitment, and originality. The program is governed by trustees appointed by the UK Prime Minister and supported closely by the Kennedy family. Notable alumni span politics, finance, journalism, academia, arts, and law, including Ed Balls, Yvette Cooper, Mervyn King, and Elena Kagan.

---

## United Negro College Fund (UNCF)
Founded in 1944 to support historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and students, UNCF administers scholarships open to all ethnicities, predominantly benefiting African Americans. It has raised hundreds of millions through events like Lou Rawls’s telethon and recent major donations by MacKenzie Scott and Netflix’s Reed Hastings. Its member institutions cover numerous HBCUs across the U.S. and contribute significantly to Black higher education and leadership.

---

## Norway Scholarship
Initiated in 1919 to enable Norwegian students to study at Oxford, this scholarship was founded by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne inspired by cultural exchange needs. After financial challenges mid-20th century, renewed efforts in the late 20th century revitalized the program. It has supported diverse academic disciplines with recipients attending Oxford colleges and contributing prominently across fields.

---

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
Established in 1987 to support public HBCUs, the TMCF has raised over $500 million for scholarships and programmatic support. It acquired Opportunity Funding Corporation in 2013 to bolster entrepreneurship at HBCUs. The organization supports 55 member institutions, including law and medical schools, and has received significant donations from Apple, the Charles Koch Foundation, and Boeing.

---

## King’s Scholars
Dating back to 1317, King’s Scholars at institutions like Eton College, Westminster School, and King’s College Cambridge are students awarded scholarships based on academic merit. At Eton, scholars live in “College” and have distinct traditions and privileges. Similar scholarship traditions exist at King’s Schools in England, with historical ties to monarchs Henry VI and VIII.

---

## Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes (Germany)
Founded in 1925 (re-established 1948), Germany’s largest scholarship foundation offers financial and academic support, selecting students by nomination and testing based on academic excellence, initiative, and social commitment. It provides scholarships for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral studies with additional internal and external programs. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates in sciences and prominent social scientists and politicians.

---

## Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program
Named for Maine politician Edmund Muskie, the program provides internships and career support for Eurasian scholars studying in the U.S. It promotes professional exchange and was relaunched with expanded scope in 2015.

---

## Shuping Scholarship (China)
Founded in 1939 by Koo Soh Bing in Shanghai to support poor but academically talented students, the scholarship also fostered community service and extracurricular education. Despite wartime challenges, the program endured and resumed officially in 1986, now supporting thousands of scholars across multiple Chinese cities. It includes alumni associations and a foundation ensuring long-term funding.

---

## Marshall Scholarship (UK-based for Americans)
Established in 1953 as a living memorial to George C. Marshall, it supports American postgraduates studying in the UK at any university (e.g., Oxford, Cambridge, LSE). It emphasizes academic excellence, leadership, and fostering UK-US relations. Approximately 50 scholarships are awarded annually with a two-year tenure. It is among the most prestigious and selective scholarships for U.S. students abroad.

---

## Harkness Fellowship
Initiated in 1925 by the Commonwealth Fund, this fellowship provides funding for scholars, mainly in healthcare policy, from several countries including the UK, Canada, and Australia, to conduct research in the U.S. The fellowship lasts about 12 months and includes seminars and professional networking.

---

## Miss America Pageant
Originating in the early 1920s to boost Atlantic City tourism, it evolved into a national beauty and scholarship contest. Early years featured racial and religious exclusions, with barriers lifted in the mid-20th century. The pageant has undergone format changes, including removing the swimsuit competition in 2018 and recent reversals in 2023. It has faced controversies around racial inclusivity, management scandals, and scholarship distribution transparency. Notable firsts include the first African-American contestant (1970) and winner (Vanessa Williams, 1983).

---

## Margaret Busby’s *Daughters of Africa* Anthology
A groundbreaking anthology (1992, updated 2019) compiling writings by over 200 women of African descent worldwide, celebrated for its literary breadth and importance in highlighting black women's voices across centuries. The anthology has received wide critical acclaim and inspired related awards supporting African women scholars.

---

## Fulbright Program
Founded in 1946 by Senator J. William Fulbright to promote international educational exchange and mutual understanding. It includes various grants: student scholarships, scholar awards, teacher exchanges, and professional fellowships across numerous countries. Administered by the U.S. Department of State, overseen by the Fulbright Foreign Scholarship Board, it operates through bilateral commissions worldwide. Recent challenges include political interference and program suspension in Russia and funding freezes. The program has produced numerous notable alumni across diverse fields and promotes academic and cultural diplomacy globally.

---

## Rhodes Scholarship
Established in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes to promote unity among English-speaking nations and global leadership studies at Oxford University. Initially male-only, women were admitted from 1977. The scholarship is highly selective, offering postgraduate funding including tuition and living expenses. It is accompanied by significant traditions and alumni networking. The scholarship has faced criticism due to Rhodes's colonialist legacy and debates about diversity and career paths of scholars. Many Rhodes Scholars have had prominent careers in government, academia, arts, sciences, and human rights activism.

---

## British Council
Founded in 1934 to promote British culture and English language education internationally. It operates in over 100 countries, manages English-language exams including IELTS, supports education and cultural exchanges, and runs initiatives like MOOCs and international arts programs. Facing geopolitical challenges, including closures in Russia and Syria, and controversies such as outsourcing, union disputes, and administrative issues, the Council remains a key UK soft power agency. It is sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and continues to adapt its global presence through programs in education, arts, and diplomacy.
