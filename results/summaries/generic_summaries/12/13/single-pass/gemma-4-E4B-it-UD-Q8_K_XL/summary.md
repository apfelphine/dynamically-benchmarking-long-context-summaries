# Academic and Scholarship Programs

## National Merit Scholarship Program
Entry requires full-time high school enrollment, U.S. citizenship/lawful permanent residency, and taking the PSAT/NMSQT, usually in junior year. The program involves multiple rounds: initial screening by the PSAT/NMSQT for "Commended" recognition; subsequent determination of "National Merit Semifinalists"; and advancement to "Finalist" standing by submitting SAT scores, an outstanding academic record, and an endorsed application. Ultimately, approximately 8,000 of the 15,000 Finalists receive one of the 2,500 National Merit \$2,500 Scholarships, awarded based on a combination of accomplishments, skills, and potential, with corporate sponsors contributing additional funds.

## National Achievement Scholarship Program
Established in 1964 to support Black American youth, the program previously allowed Black students who met entry requirements to compete concurrently with the National Merit Program. While Black students could qualify for both, they could only receive one monetary award. Following the conclusion of the 2015 program, it transitioned to the United Negro College Fund, meaning Black American high school students no longer compete for this specific award, though they may still enter the National Merit competition.

## Bright Futures Scholarship Program
This Florida-based program, initially merit-based, underwent funding cuts in 2011, disproportionately affecting Black and Latino students. In 2018, it was overhauled into four levels: Florida Academic Scholar (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS), Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholar (GSV), and Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholar (GSC). Qualification for FAS requires a 3.50 weighted GPA or being a National Merit Finalist/Scholar, alongside specific standardized test scores and service hours.

## Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)
TMCF focuses on supporting public Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) with over \$500 million raised to date. It supports 55 member schools, which include HBCUs, Predominantly Black Institutions (PBIs), and community colleges. The fund has acquired the Opportunity Funding Corporation (OFC) to enhance entrepreneurship curricula within the HBCU community.

## UNCF
Founded in 1944 by William J. Trent, the UNCF originally supported historically Black colleges to become "citadels of learning." While scholarships are open to all ethnicities, the majority of recipients are African-American. In 2025, MacKenzie Scott donated \$70 million to support the financial stability and long-term sustainability of Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs).

## Other Philanthropic & Educational Awards
Several other scholarship programs exist:
*   **Marshall Scholarship:** A UK initiative to enable intellectually distinguished young Americans to study in the UK, fostering understanding between the UK and the U.S. It emphasizes intellectual and character distinction, and has expanded to offer 50 places annually.
*   **Rhodes Scholarship:** Established in 1902, this scholarship promotes unity within the British Empire and strengthens diplomatic ties between Britain and the U.S. It is highly selective, though it faces ongoing criticism regarding its founder's colonialist views and the tendency of scholars to pursue private careers over public service.
*   **Fulbright Program:** A U.S. government-funded international exchange program promoting peace and understanding, operating on a bi-national basis. It supports various programs, including graduate studies for international students in the U.S. and U.S. students studying abroad.
*   **Harkness Fellowships:** Since 1997, these fellowships are restricted to the field of health care policy and practice, funding one-year research at American institutions to study the U.S. healthcare system.
*   **Kennedy Scholarship:** This British memorial scholarship supports post-graduate students at Harvard or MIT, following a model similar to the Rhodes Scholarship.
*   **Studienstiftung:** A German foundation offering general scholarships for bachelor's and master's studies based on "excellence, initiative and responsibility," admitting scholars through nomination or an assessment test.
*   **Shuping Scholarship:** Founded in 1939 in Shanghai by Koo, this scholarship supported poor, high-achieving students, emphasizing service to society. It was revived in 1986 and now supports secondary and tertiary institutions in China.

# Historical and Political Figures

## Edmund S. Muskie
Edmund Muskie had a significant political career spanning from his election to the Maine House of Representatives in 1946 to his tenure as U.S. Secretary of State (1980–1981). As Governor of Maine (1955–1959), he championed environmentalism and public investment, successfully pushing for the creation of the Maine Department of Conservation. In the Senate (1959–1980), he was a major driver of environmental legislation, including the Clean Air Act of 1963 and the Clean Water Act of 1965, earning him the nickname "Mr. Clean." His time as Secretary of State involved engaging in Cold War diplomacy, confronting the Soviet Union, and working on the Iran Hostage Crisis.

## Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award
This award is connected to the anthology *Daughters of Africa*, a landmark collection featuring over 200 women writers of African descent. The award, launched in 2020, supports African women students and is intended to benefit from the anthology's spirit of celebrating the diverse literary contributions of women across the African diaspora.

## King's Scholars and Eton Scholars
The King's Scholars at Eton College are highly distinguished students who pass a competitive exam and are admitted into the oldest house, "College." These scholars receive foundation scholarships, which, while historically unconditional, are now subject to means testing. The title is associated with academic prestige and ancient school traditions.
