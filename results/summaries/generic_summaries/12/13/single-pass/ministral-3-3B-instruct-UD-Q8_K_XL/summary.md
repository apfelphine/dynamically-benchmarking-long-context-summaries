### **National Merit Scholarship Program**
The **National Merit Scholarship Program** is a prestigious U.S. competition for high school juniors based on PSAT/NMSQT scores. Entry requires U.S. citizenship, lawful residency, or citizenship intent, and participation in the PSAT/NMSQT in junior year. The program has three rounds:

1. **PSAT/NMSQT Screening**: Over 1.5 million students qualify, with the top 50,000 receiving "Commended" recognition. Scores vary by state, with higher thresholds in competitive states.
2. **Semifinalists**: About 16,000 of the 50,000 advance based on state quotas, with additional criteria like academic records and recommendations.
3. **Finalists**: Approximately 15,000 semifinalists proceed to final rounds, where they compete for scholarships. Around 8,000 receive Merit Scholarship awards, with additional corporate-sponsored scholarships available. Finalists must meet rigorous academic, extracurricular, and personal standards.

The program also includes the **National Achievement Scholarship Program**, originally established in 1964 to encourage Black American youth, though it transitioned to the **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)** in 2015.

### **Bright Futures Scholarship Program**
The **Bright Futures Scholarship Program** in Florida provides merit-based funding for high school graduates. Originally established to emulate Georgia’s HOPE Scholarship, it expanded to cover 39% of Florida high school graduates at its peak. The program includes four levels:
- **Florida Academic Scholar (FAS)**: Covers 100% of tuition and fees at public institutions, with additional stipends.
- **Florida Medallion Scholar (FMS)**: Covers 75% of tuition and fees.
- **Florida Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV)**: For vocational programs, covering partial tuition.
- **Florida Gold Seal CAPE Scholars (GSC)**: For career and professional education, with partial tuition coverage.

Eligibility requires a minimum SAT/ACT score, GPA, and volunteer work hours. Scholarships are renewable for up to five years, contingent on maintaining academic performance. The program faced controversies, including allegations of racial bias and funding cuts in 2011, leading to reforms in 2018.

### **Bolashak Scholarship**
The **Bolashak Scholarship**, established in 1993 by Kazakhstan’s first president, Nursultan Nazarbayev, aims to foster Western democratic values among Kazakh youth. It funds students to study abroad, with a focus on science, technology, and humanities. The scholarship prioritizes Kazakh ethnicity due to language requirements, though it has expanded to include students from other ethnic groups. Notable alumni include political economists, scientists, and government officials.

### **Kennedy Scholarship**
The **Kennedy Scholarship**, established in 1964 as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy, offers scholarships to attend either **Harvard University** or the **Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)**. Selection is based on intellectual attainment, leadership potential, and commitment to public service. Notable alumni include politicians, economists, journalists, and academics, such as **Ed Balls, Bill Gates, and Stephen Breyer**. The scholarship is administered by the **John F. Kennedy Memorial Trust**, with selection conducted through a national competition.

### **UNCF Scholarships**
The **United Negro College Fund (UNCF)** provides scholarships and support to students at historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and other institutions. Founded in 1944, UNCF supports over 65,000 students annually through grants and scholarships. It is renowned for fostering Black excellence in education, with alumni including **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Spike Lee, and Samuel L. Jackson**. UNCF’s fundraising efforts, such as the **Lou Rawls Parade of Stars**, have raised millions for scholarships and programs.

### **Norway Scholarship**
The **Norway Scholarship** enables Norwegian students to study at **Wadham College, Oxford**, for a year. Founded in 1919 by Arthur Ivor Garland Jayne, the scholarship aims to foster cultural exchange between Norway and Britain. Due to funding constraints, it was suspended during World War II and later revived in the 1970s. Notable recipients include scholars in law, medicine, and social sciences.

---

### **Thurgood Marshall College Fund**
The **Thurgood Marshall College Fund (TMCF)** supports public and private **HBCUs** through scholarships, capacity-building initiatives, and advocacy. Established in 1987, TMCF has grown to support 55 schools and over 500 million in funding. Its mission differs from UNCF by focusing on **public HBCUs** and providing targeted support for students and institutions. Notable alumni include **Dr. N. Joyce Payne**, the organization’s founder, and leaders in education, business, and public service.

### **King’s Scholars**
**King’s Scholars** are undergraduates at **King’s College, Cambridge**, who achieve first-class degrees. They receive a £350 prize and attend a formal dinner in the King’s College Chapel. The scholarship is historically linked to King Henry VI’s founding of King’s College, Cambridge, and Eton College. Notable alumni include **Sir Stephen Breyer, Nobel Prize-winning scientists, and political leaders**.

### **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes**
The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** (German Excellence Scholarship) is a merit-based scholarship program for undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral students in Germany. Established in 1948, it supports students with exceptional academic potential and social commitment. Scholarships include financial aid, mentorship, and access to summer academies. The program is highly selective, with over 14,000 scholars annually. Notable alumni include **Nobel Prize winners, politicians, and scientists**.

### **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program**
The **Edmund S. Muskie Internship Program** is a professional exchange program administered by the U.S. Department of State. It provides summer internships, career training, and financial support to scholars and graduate students from Eurasian countries studying in the U.S. Named after former U.S. Senator Edmund Muskie, the program aims to promote international understanding and collaboration. It was established in 1992 and is managed by **Cultural Vistas**.

### **Miss America Pageant**
The **Miss America Pageant** is an annual beauty competition that selects the "Miss America" titleholder. Founded in 1919, the pageant has evolved to include cultural and academic components, though it remains controversial for its historical ties to militarism and gender stereotypes. Recent changes, such as the removal of the swimsuit competition in 2018, reflect efforts to modernize the event. Notable winners include **Vanessa Williams (first Black Miss America)** and **Nina Davuluri (first Indian-American winner)**.

### **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award**
The **Margaret Busby New Daughters of Africa Award** honors emerging writers of African descent. Named after the editor of the groundbreaking anthology *Daughters of Africa* (1992), the award supports African women scholars and writers. The anthology, which includes works by **Maya Angelou, Toni Morrison, and Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie**, is celebrated for its contributions to global literature. The award aims to amplify the voices of underrepresented women in African literature.

### **Fulbright Program**
The **Fulbright Program** is the largest international educational exchange program in the world, established in 1946. It funds students, scholars, and professionals to study, teach, or conduct research abroad. The program operates in over 160 countries and is administered by the U.S. Department of State. Notable Fulbright alumni include **Nobel Prize winners, politicians, and academics**. The program has faced political interference, including a 2025 freeze on funding due to U.S. administration policies.

---

### **Rhodes Scholarship**
The **Rhodes Scholarship** is one of the most prestigious postgraduate scholarships globally, enabling students from around the world to study at **Oxford University**. Founded in 1902 by Cecil Rhodes, the scholarship is based on academic excellence, leadership, and character. It has been criticized for its colonial legacy, but it remains highly selective and influential. Notable alumni include **Nelson Mandela, Bill Clinton, and Barack Obama**. The scholarship has expanded to include scholars from all nations, with a focus on public service and global leadership.

### **British Council**
The **British Council** promotes the UK’s cultural, educational, and linguistic influence worldwide. Established in 1934, it supports English language teaching, academic exchanges, and cultural programs. The British Council operates in over 100 countries, offering exams like **IELTS**, scholarships, and educational partnerships. It has faced controversies, including accusations of political influence and closures in Russia and Libya due to geopolitical tensions. The British Council remains a key player in global education and cultural diplomacy.
