The University of Oxford, founded in the 12th century, is one of the world’s oldest and most prestigious universities, with a rich history of academic and social influence. It evolved from a medieval clerical institution into a modern research university, with significant developments during the Renaissance, Enlightenment, and 19th century reforms. The university is a "city university" with no central campus, instead housing colleges and departments across central Oxford. Its academic structure is centered on tutorials, with a strong emphasis on classical languages and later expansion into science and modern studies. Women gained admission in the 19th century, and full equality was achieved in 1920. The university is renowned for its academic excellence, with over 70 Nobel laureates, and is consistently ranked among the world’s top universities. It has a diverse student body, with notable alumni including British prime ministers, monarchs, and influential scientists. The university maintains a strong tradition of student life, including academic dress, student unions, and traditions like "trashing" and "blue" awards. It operates with a decentralized governance model, including a chancellor, vice-chancellor, and a network of colleges, each with its own autonomy. Financially, Oxford is one of the wealthiest universities in the UK, with a massive endowment, and has faced criticism over historical ties to slavery and fossil fuel investments.
