# Final Summary

The University of New South Wales (UNSW), established in 1958 from the Sydney Mechanics' School of Arts, is a globally ranked institution (top 80) that evolved to meet post-WWII engineering demands through a blend of practical training and compulsory humanities. Key milestones include its expansion to a major university by 1968, campus independences in 1965 and 1975, and the 2019 introduction of a trimester system. Despite facing significant financial hurdles, such as a $370 million shortfall and 493 job cuts in 2020, UNSW maintains strong outcomes, including an 81.3% undergraduate and 91.7% postgraduate employment rate, with starting salaries of $75k and $120k respectively. Currently led by President Attila Brungs, the university operates across Kensington, Paddington, Randwick, a CBD pilot campus, and the Canberra ADFA campus, offering six faculties with specialized defense programs.

Academically, the university is managed by Educational Assessment Australia (EAA) for large-scale programs and admissions rely on ATAR, UMAT, and LAW tests, supplemented by an "HSC Plus" bonus scheme (excluding medicine and law). Student support includes the Co-op program, Scientia Scholarships, and a $50,000/year PhD package. The student community is anchored by the Arc @ UNSW union and diverse media outlets like *Blitz* and *Noise@UNSW*, with extensive international exchange opportunities (250+). Students engage in innovative projects ranging from fusion research (*AtomCraft*) to electric vehicle racing (*Sunswift*).

UNSW boasts a distinguished alumni network, producing more millionaires than any other Australian university and including figures like Scott Morrison and Tim Flannery. However, the institution has faced significant controversies, including legal issues (copyright infringement in 1975, privacy violations in 2018, and safety fines), serious incidents (a fatal 2000 truck accident, a 2012 assault, and Singapore campus closures), and security/political controversies (a 2015 security scare and a 2020 tweet deletion regarding Hong Kong). Recent challenges also involve investigations into research misconduct (duplicated photos in 2021) and legal action over underpayments to casual academic staff in 2023. Operationally, the university includes the UNSW College, a not-for-profit wholly owned enterprise providing global education and exam services.
