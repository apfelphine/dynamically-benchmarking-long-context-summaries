## Updated Working Summary:

This summary provides a comparative overview of major global universities, focusing on institutions like Oxford, and detailing the profiles of Cornell, U of T, NUS, UNAM, Melbourne, UNSW, UBC, HKU, and UFRJ.

**Institutional Profiles:**
*   **University of Oxford:** Dating back to the 12th century, this decentralized "city university" operates through 39 colleges, supported by an endowment exceeding £8.7 billion. It utilizes the tutorial system for undergraduate teaching and has demonstrated commitment to progressive values, including ensuring women's full membership by 1920 and divesting from fossil fuels since 2020.
*   **Cornell University:** Founded in 1865, this highly research-intensive ("R1") institution boasts a \$10.7 billion endowment and decentralized campuses (including Ithaca, Cornell Tech in NYC, and Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar). It is noted for its need-blind admissions, international focus, and strengths in architecture and business, aiming for carbon neutrality by 2035.
*   **University of Toronto (U of T):** Established in 1827, U of T is a large, decentralized collegiate university spanning three campuses, housing North America's third-largest library system and serving a highly diverse student body. Academic strengths include computer science, global health, and communication theory.
*   **National University of Singapore (NUS):** Founded in 1980, NUS is an international institution ranked #8 in 2026 QS, focusing on English and entrepreneurship across its three campuses. Key areas of expertise include Geography and Nanoscience & Nanotechnology, alongside biomedical and engineering research.
*   **Other Key Institutions:**
    *   **UNAM:** A secular, premier Latin American research center since 1910, recognized by UNESCO for its work in AI and robotics.
    *   **University of Melbourne:** Established in 1855, it is noted for introducing the "Melbourne Model," which emphasizes generalized three-year degrees and entrepreneurship, and for integrating women in 1883.
    *   **UNSW:** Founded in 1833, UNSW transitioned to a generalist focus in 1958 and employs a trimester system to bolster advanced research.
    *   **UBC:** Founded in 1908, UBC maintains a strong sustainability focus while operating across various campuses and relying significantly on external funding.
    *   **HKU:** Established in 1911, this global research university is recognized for its established formal legislative and academic structures.

**Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ) Focus:**
UFRJ, established in 1920, is a large public university structured around six main University Centers (covering areas like Health Sciences, Technology, Law, and Arts) and manages thousands of students. Governance involves a rector and six pro-rectors nominated by the Ministry of Education (MEC). While historically using self-organized exams, admissions now primarily utilize ENEM via SiSU, while maintaining 30% affirmative action.
*   **Student Life and History:** Student governance is managed by the DCE, and the university's history includes the "Massacre da Praia Vermelha" in 1968.
*   **Innovation and Research Hub:** UFRJ is a center for technological innovation, anchored by the **Technological Park of Rio**, which functions as a Brazilian "Silicon Valley" through collaborations with Petrobras and hosting key centers like CENPES, CEPEL, and COPPE.
*   **Advanced Projects:** The university is involved in significant endeavors, including the **UFRJ Newspaper**, **UFRJ Sea** coastal studies, wave energy extraction (**Plant Waves**), low-cost levitation train research (**MagLev Cobra**), and the utilization of **LabOceano**, a large ocean tank for deep-sea simulation crucial for offshore energy. Major campus features include the Law School and the National Museum, with ongoing efforts to centralize activities at the College City on Ilha do Fundão.
