The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) is a prominent public university in Mexico, founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, aiming to create a secular, national, and modern institution to reform higher education. It began as a consolidation of Mexico City’s decentralized colleges, including the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico, and established its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, in the 1950s. UNAM has a long history of student activism, including major strikes over examination methods, grading systems, and political issues such as the 1968 Mexico City Olympics protests, culminating in the Tlatelolco massacre. The university has consistently maintained autonomy, with key milestones including the creation of its coat-of-arms by José Vasconcelos and the adoption of the motto "For my people the spirit shall speak," reflecting its humanistic mission.

UNAM operates multiple campuses across Mexico, including the main Ciudad Universitaria, satellite campuses in the metropolitan area, and specialized institutions such as the National Preparatory School and the School of Music. It is organized into schools and colleges offering both undergraduate and graduate degrees, with a strong emphasis on research and academic excellence. UNAM is consistently ranked as Mexico’s top university and one of the top 10 in Latin America. Its research output is globally significant, particularly in artificial intelligence, robotics, and various scientific fields, with numerous research institutes and international collaborations. The university actively promotes diversity and inclusion, especially in technology, and has produced many Nobel laureates, including Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, and Mario Molina.

UNAM’s culture is deeply rooted in academic and political engagement, with student-led movements, notable alumni in politics, business, and the arts, and a strong tradition of social activism. The university has a vibrant student life, including cultural events, student organizations, and a rich tradition of student-led protests and political movements. It is also recognized for its contributions to minority inclusion, especially women in STEM fields, and for its role in fostering a diverse and inclusive academic environment.
