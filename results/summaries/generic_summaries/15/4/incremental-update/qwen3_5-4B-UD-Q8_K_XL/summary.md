### UNAM: Origins, Scale, Structure, and Cultural Identity
Founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra as a secular reformer, the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) is one of the world's largest institutions, hosting over 324,000 students across 19 full-degree schools and extensive open-learning options. Shaped by political challenges during the Mexican Revolution, UNAM achieved full autonomy in the mid-1920s and cemented its role in Mexican liberalism. It relocated to its iconic *Ciudad Universitaria* campus in 1952, a UNESCO World Heritage Site designed by Mario Pani, and remains culturally defined by the seal and motto of José Vasconcelos, which symbolize unity and humanistic values.

### History, Activism, Research, and Global Presence
UNAM's history is marked by student activism, including the 1966 and 1999–2000 strikes and the tragic Tlatelolco massacre of 1968. Despite this turbulent past, it remains a central force in Mexican thought, managing significant cultural landmarks like the Palacio de Minería and the birthplace of Mexican muralism. Academically, it is a top Latin American research institution, contributing over 50% of Mexico's scientific production, with strong outputs in artificial intelligence and robotics. Beyond academics, the university is culturally vibrant, renowned for its annual Day of the Dead celebrations, and maintains strong athletic programs, including Liga MX football and Olympic-caliber volleyball. It has expanded globally through satellite campuses and international extensions.

### Leadership, Notable Alumni, and Advocacy
UNAM has produced a distinguished roster of figures who have shaped national and global discourse. Its faculty and alumni list includes all three of Mexico's Nobel laureates (García Robles, Paz, and Molina) and numerous Peace Prize contributors. Prominent individuals have served as heads of state, such as Andrés Manuel López Obrador and Claudia Sheinbaum, alongside high-ranking politicians, diplomats, and world-renowned artists and writers like Carlos Fuentes, Alfonso Cuarón, and Juan Rulfo. The university is also recognized for its advocacy for minorities, particularly its strong support for women in technology. Its graduates further include renowned scientists like Nobel laureate Mario Molina and astronomer Carlos Frenk; influential business leaders such as Carlos Slim and Alfredo Harp Helú; and celebrated athletes including footballer Hugo Sánchez and Olympic sprinter René Ahumada, alongside prominent physicians like Ignacio Chávez and Nora Volkow.
