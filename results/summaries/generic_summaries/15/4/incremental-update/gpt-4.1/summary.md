## Summary

### Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)
Founded in 1920, UFRJ is Brazil’s oldest and largest federal university, with 52 academic units, a distinguished faculty, robust research, 48,000 undergraduates, and 11,000 postgraduates. Admissions rely on ENEM/SiSU, and UFRJ implements strong affirmative action policies.

### Cornell University
Established in 1865, Cornell is a land-, sea-, and space-grant institution featuring 16 colleges, a $10.7 billion endowment, academic excellence, sustainability initiatives, vibrant student life, diverse housing, competitive athletics, and influential alumni.

### University of Toronto (U of T)
Founded in 1827, U of T enrolls over 100,000 students across several campuses, excelling in medicine and computer science. It offers extensive student services, boasts decentralized governance, and counts notable alumni among its ranks.

### National University of Singapore (NUS)
Established in 1905, NUS comprises 17 faculties and research institutes, fostering innovation, entrepreneurship, high global rankings, and a diverse international community.

### University of Oxford
Dating to 1096, Oxford consists of 39 colleges and halls, known for rigorous admissions, tutorials, vast libraries, historic traditions, and influential alumni—including Nobel laureates and global leaders.

### University of Hong Kong (HKU)
Founded in 1911, HKU features 10 faculties taught in English, a highly international student body (47.2% in 2023–24), and a governance framework focused on academic freedom and tradition.

### University of British Columbia (UBC)
Founded in 1908, UBC’s Vancouver, Okanagan, and upcoming Kelowna campuses collaborate with Indigenous nations. The university excels in research, sustainability, and diversity (72,000+ students; 28% international, 3.2% Indigenous), with alumni achievements, rich traditions, and a $3.8 billion budget.

### University of New South Wales (UNSW)
Founded in 1949, UNSW leads in research and innovation, offers multidisciplinary programs, achieves high graduate outcomes, boasts significant alumni, and has faced controversies relating to copyright, ethics, curriculum, wages, and free speech.

### University of Melbourne
Founded in 1853, Melbourne comprises nine faculties, a high proportion of international students (44%), the nation’s largest research student cohort, strong entrepreneurial programs, vibrant campus life, and a tradition of academic excellence. Recent major reforms include the “Melbourne Model.” Leadership includes Chancellor Jane Hansen AO and incoming Vice-Chancellor Emma Johnston (2025), with alumni including Nobel laureates and Prime Ministers.

### National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)
Founded in 1910, UNAM is one of the world’s largest universities, enrolling over 324,000 students across diverse schools and colleges. It boasts leading research centers and is responsible for half of Mexico’s scientific output, especially in AI and robotics. UNAM is highly ranked, known for political engagement, cultural influence (notable Day of the Dead celebration), extensive distance education, and support for minority advocacy. Universidad’s alumni include all Mexican Nobel laureates, leading academics, prominent artists, scientists, influential politicians (including heads of state), business leaders like Carlos Slim and Alfredo Harp Helú, and athletes such as Hugo Sánchez and Daniel Vargas. Notable alumni also include Nobel laureate Mario Molina, physicians Ignacio Chávez and Nora Volkow, scientist Luis E. Miramontes, and astronaut Rodolfo Neri Vela. UNAM’s history features activism, campus expansion, and the cultural legacy of Ciudad Universitaria, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
