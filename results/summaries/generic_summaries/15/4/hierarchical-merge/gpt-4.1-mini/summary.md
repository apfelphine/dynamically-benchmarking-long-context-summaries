# Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)

Founded in 1920 with roots from the 18th century, UFRJ is Brazil’s largest and most prestigious public university, educating over 59,000 students with nearly 3,800 faculty members. It features six university centers across diverse fields including health sciences, technology, law, and arts, plus the internationally recognized COPPEAD Graduate School of Business. Admissions include affirmative action policies reserving 30% of places for low-income public school students. The main campus, Cidade Universitária on Ilha do Fundão, spans over 5 million m² and hosts landmark institutions such as the National Museum and Valongo Observatory. UFRJ integrates a comprehensive health complex and operates a Technological Park fostering innovation in energy and oil sectors, housing Petrobras’ CENPES and numerous SMEs.

# Cornell University

Established in 1865 in Ithaca, New York, Cornell University is a private land-grant research institution governed by a 64-member Board of Trustees. It comprises 16 semi-autonomous colleges covering undergraduate, graduate, and professional studies, with notable strengths in architecture, business, international relations, and philosophy. Classified as an “R1” research university, Cornell invests over $1 billion annually in fields such as space exploration, nuclear physics, computing, and automotive safety. The 745-acre campus offers unique residential communities and the expansive Cornell Botanic Gardens spanning 2,800 acres. Cornell is committed to sustainability with a target for net carbon neutrality by 2035 and supports over 1,000 student organizations and 35 Ivy League NCAA Division I teams.

# University of Toronto (U of T)

Founded in 1827, the University of Toronto is Canada’s largest research university with three campuses. It operates a collegiate system with seven undergraduate colleges at the historic St. George campus and houses 14 professional faculties including the Temerty Faculty of Medicine and Rotman School of Management. Supported by a $1.2 billion research budget, U of T has led medical innovations such as insulin and stem cell research. Its vibrant campus culture includes a wide array of student organizations and traditions. Its library system holds over 12 million volumes, and the university was named North America’s most sustainable university in 2024 by QS.

# University of British Columbia (UBC)

Founded in 1908, UBC is a foundational Canadian university with campuses on the Musqueam and Syilx Okanagan Nation territories. Its Vancouver campus covers 420 acres featuring modern facilities including the Brock Commons Tallwood House—the world’s tallest mass timber building—and the Okanagan campus opened in 2005. Enrolling over 72,000 students, UBC maintains a strong international and Indigenous presence across 12 faculties in Vancouver and 7 in Okanagan. It is Canada’s second-largest research institution by income, specializing in marine sciences, particle physics, and quantum matter. UBC is internationally recognized for its engineering student traditions and notable alumni including four Canadian Prime Ministers, multiple Nobel Laureates, and leaders across literature, environmentalism, judiciary, and technology sectors.

# National University of Singapore (NUS)

Tracing origins to a 1905 medical school and established formally through mergers by 1980, NUS is Singapore’s leading comprehensive university with 17 faculties covering fields such as business, law, medicine, public policy, and music. It delivers an interdisciplinary semester-based modular curriculum blending British and American models, with programs emphasizing regional engagement. Ranked #8 globally in QS World Rankings 2026, NUS excels in Architecture, Computer Science, Chemical Engineering, and Business Management. Its Kent Ridge campus spans 170 hectares, hosting extensive libraries with over three million titles and digital archives. NUS boasts a highly international community—with 65% faculty and 36% students from abroad—and notable alumni including two Singaporean Prime Ministers, four Presidents, two Malaysian Prime Ministers, and WHO Director-General Margaret Chan.

# University of Oxford

Dating from circa 1096, Oxford is a globally preeminent institution known for its decentralized collegiate system of 39 autonomous colleges and 4 private halls. Teaching centers on small-group tutorials supported by world-class resources including the Bodleian Libraries and Ashmolean Museum. Oxford University Press publishes the authoritative Oxford English Dictionary. With an annual intake of about 23,000 applicants and a competitive 16% offer rate, Oxford balances tradition, diversity, and vibrant student life. It claims over 70 Nobel laureates and numerous world leaders among alumni, maintains the UK’s largest university endowment (~£8.7 billion), and funds prestigious scholarships such as Rhodes and Clarendon.

# University of Hong Kong (HKU)

Founded in 1911 as the Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese, HKU is a prestigious research-intensive university blending historic British Colonial architecture with modern facilities in fields like medicine and marine biology. It has expanded faculties to include Social Sciences, Dentistry, Law, Architecture, Education, and Business. HKU is known for milestones such as renaming its medical faculty after Li Ka Shing and joining the UN Women’s HeForShe campaign. Despite political challenges, it remains a research leader with significant funding and a diverse student body, nearly half international. Governance includes the Hong Kong Chief Executive as chancellor, and its alumni include revolutionary leader Sun Yat-sen.

# University of New South Wales (UNSW)

Established in 1949, UNSW evolved from a technical institute into a comprehensive university with faculties in Arts, Business, Engineering, Law, Medicine, and Science, including a Canberra campus focused on defense education. Ranked 20th globally in QS 2026, UNSW is recognized for graduate employability and innovation in renewable energy and robotics. Its vibrant student life is supported by the not-for-profit Arc union. Notable alumni include Australian Prime Minister Scott Morrison, NSW Premier Gladys Berejiklian, and Atlassian CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes. The university has faced controversies related to copyright issues, safety, research integrity, political disputes, and legal challenges.

# University of Melbourne

Founded in 1853 as Australia’s second oldest university, Melbourne pioneered law, engineering, medicine, and agriculture faculties and implemented the “Melbourne Model” degree structure. Its campuses include the historic Parkville and the developing Fishermans Bend campus. As Australia’s top university (QS #19 globally, 2024), it invests over $800 million annually in research with the nation’s largest research student body. With competitive admissions (median ATAR 94.05) and substantial international enrollment, it supports disadvantaged students through Access Melbourne. Student life thrives under the University of Melbourne Student Union and Graduate Student Association managing over 300 clubs and sporting traditions. Alumni include Nobel laureates Elizabeth Blackburn and Howard Florey, and Australian Prime Ministers Alfred Deakin, Julia Gillard, and Robert Menzies.

# National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

Founded in 1910, UNAM is Mexico’s leading university and a Latin American research powerhouse enrolling over 324,000 students across undergraduate, graduate, and distance education. Its UNESCO World Heritage Ciudad Universitaria campus is noted for iconic architecture and comprehensive facilities. UNAM produces over half of Mexico’s scientific publications in AI, robotics, astronomy, and biotechnology. The university supports rich cultural traditions and minority initiatives, is symbolized by its emblematic seal and puma mascot, and owns the prominent professional football team Club Universidad Nacional (Pumas). Distinguished alumni include three Nobel laureates, Mexican presidents Andrés Manuel López Obrador and Claudia Sheinbaum, billionaire Carlos Slim, and acclaimed filmmakers Alfonso Cuarón and Guillermo del Toro, reflecting broad influence across politics, science, culture, and sports in Latin America.
