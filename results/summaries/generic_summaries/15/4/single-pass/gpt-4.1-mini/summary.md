# Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)

Founded in 1920, UFRJ is Brazil’s oldest university, tracing its origins to the first higher education courses established in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. It evolved through significant reforms, adopting the French model of university organization in 1937 and gaining full autonomy in 1965. Presently, it embraces international collaboration, extensive research, and public health contributions, notably through its nine teaching hospitals. The university is symbolized by Minerva, the patroness of arts and knowledge, and maintains an open-door policy for foreign academics.

Administratively, UFRJ operates as a federal autarchy under the Ministry of Education, with governance by multiple councils overseeing academic and financial affairs. Its sprawling main campus, Cidade Universitária on Ilha do Fundão, houses six academic centers ranging from Health Sciences to Literature and Arts. Extensions include satellite campuses in Macaé and Duque de Caxias, plus e-learning poles. UFRJ is celebrated for its robust student activism history, distinguished alumni across numerous fields, and a technological park focused on energy and petroleum research.  

# Cornell University

Founded in 1865 in Ithaca, New York, by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, Cornell is a land-grant institution renowned for innovation and research excellence. Early on, it pioneered electrification and integrated state-funded colleges, evolving into a top-ranked research university with notable PhD programs. It has global outreach through campuses in New York City and Qatar and international partnerships.  

Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca features diverse architectural styles, extensive natural surroundings, and sustainability initiatives, including solar farms and geothermal projects. It follows a 4–1–4 academic calendar and offers interdisciplinary research opportunities. The governance includes a 64-member board of trustees and decentralized colleges, spanning fields from arts to engineering. Cornell has a strong philanthropic base with a $10.7 billion endowment (2024).  

Student life is vibrant with over 1,000 organizations, extensive Greek life, and numerous media outlets. Cornell athletics compete in NCAA Division I. The university is ranked consistently among the top global universities, noted for strengths in architecture, business, international relations, and philosophy.  

# University of Toronto

Established as King’s College in 1827 and secularized in 1849, the University of Toronto (U of T) is Canada’s largest university, with three campuses—St. George (main), Mississauga, and Scarborough. Its collegiate system resembles UK models, with significant college autonomy at St. George. U of T is a leading research university known for contributions to communication theory, biomedical breakthroughs (insulin discovery), computing technology, and global studies.

The St. George campus blends Gothic Revival, Romanesque, and modern architecture amid gorges and botanic gardens. U of T operates 40 libraries, including the extensive Robarts Library, and numerous cultural and research institutes. It boasts top global rankings, particularly in research output and sustainability, and a diverse, high-achieving student body with strong international representation.

Student life includes over 1000 clubs, active Greek systems, multiple performing arts venues, and significant student media. Varsity Blues represents the university in intercollegiate sports.  

# National University of Singapore (NUS)

Tracing its origin to the 1905 Straits Settlements medical school, NUS was formed in 1980 by merging the University of Singapore and Nanyang University. It operates across three campuses in Singapore, with extensive global collaborations. NUS focuses on entrepreneurship, research, and internationalization, with 65% foreign faculty and over one-third foreign student body.

NUS comprises 17 faculties and schools, including Business, Computing, Dentistry, Engineering, Law, Medicine, Music, and Public Policy. Recent curricular innovation includes the University Scholars Programme merging into NUS College.  

NUS is internationally ranked among the world’s top universities (#8 QS 2026), with strong performances across 19 subjects in arts, sciences, engineering, and social sciences. It has robust research centers of excellence and entrepreneurial initiatives like the NUS Entrepreneurship Centre and Overseas Colleges program.  

Student accommodations include halls, residences, and residential colleges modeled after traditional college systems, with unique academic and community programs.  

# University of Oxford

Oxford is the oldest English-speaking university, dating back to the 12th century, evolving through medieval ecclesiastical origins to a modern research university with 39 colleges and four permanent private halls. It pioneered collegiate education and the tutorial teaching system. The university has a decentralized structure with a chancellor (ceremonial) and vice-chancellor (executive) governance.

The university is renowned for its historical architecture scattered throughout Oxford city, with landmark sites like the Bodleian Library, Radcliffe Camera, and Ashmolean Museum. Oxford ranks consistently among the world’s top universities, with strengths in humanities, sciences, and professional disciplines.

Student life is rich with traditions, numerous clubs, debating societies, media outlets, and extensive collegiate communities. Oxford has produced numerous notable alumni, including Nobel laureates, royalty, British prime ministers, and global leaders. It features prominently in literature and popular media.  

# University of Hong Kong (HKU)

Founded in 1911 with roots in the 1887 Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese, HKU is Hong Kong’s oldest tertiary institution. It grew from colonial beginnings into a leading comprehensive university with a strong emphasis on research, particularly in medicine, law, engineering, and social sciences.

HKU’s main campus combines British Colonial architecture, including the 1912 Main Building, and modern facilities on its Centennial Campus. The university has multiple residential halls and extensive library collections. It has faced political challenges related to academic freedom but remains Hong Kong’s most international university with close to half its student body from abroad.

Research excellence spans biomedical sciences, public health, and emerging fields, with high faculty compensation and strong global scientific recognition. HKU maintains various international academic partnerships and a vibrant student life aided by numerous student bodies and cultural organizations.

# University of British Columbia (UBC)

UBC, established in 1908 and commencing classes in 1915, is a major Canadian public research university with campuses in Vancouver and the Okanagan. Its main Vancouver campus is located on traditional Musqueam land, blending extensive green spaces, modern architecture, and research facilities. The Okanagan campus, founded in 2005, offers diverse academic programs and is expanding into downtown Kelowna.

UBC hosts Canada’s second-largest academic library and houses notable museums and art galleries. The university is committed to sustainability, demonstrated by its pioneering CIRS building and water conservation efforts. It is governed by a bicameral system with strong student representation via the Alma Mater Society and Graduate Student Society.

UBC supports a large, diverse student body including significant Indigenous and international populations. Its varsity teams, the Thunderbirds, are successful nationally. UBC is globally ranked among top universities for research output with strengths in multiple disciplines and notable alumni including Canadian prime ministers and Nobel laureates.

# University of New South Wales (UNSW)

Originating from technical colleges in the 19th century, UNSW was established in 1949 as the New South Wales University of Technology and later expanded as a comprehensive research university. It is recognized for engineering, business, law, medicine, and arts.

The main campus is in Kensington, Sydney, with additional campuses in Paddington, Randwick, Canberra, and others. UNSW operates a student organisation, Arc@UNSW, which supports clubs, media, and student welfare. Its academic structure comprises six faculties, including Arts, Business, Engineering, Law, Medicine & Health, and Science, plus UNSW Canberra.

UNSW is globally ranked within the top 80 universities and hosts several research centers. It offers numerous outreach and school engagement programs. The university has a competitive admissions process and notable alumni across politics, business, arts, and sciences. It has faced student satisfaction challenges, several controversies, and large-scale student projects in engineering and robotics.

# University of Melbourne

Founded in 1853, Melbourne is Australia’s second oldest university, widely regarded for research excellence and broad disciplinary coverage. Located primarily at the Parkville campus, it has multiple suburban and regional campuses. It is known for pioneering educational initiatives like the Melbourne Model—an undergraduate-generalist/master’s-specialist degree structure.

Melbourne houses nine faculties and offers diverse programs spanning arts, sciences, engineering, law, medicine, education, architecture, and fine arts. It holds substantial endowments and a leading research profile, including connections to Nobel laureates.

Student life features many clubs, sporting teams, and residential colleges. The university has faced workforce casualization and associated controversies, recent faculty strikes, and political activism notably around global issues. A new engineering campus at Fishermans Bend is planned for 2026.

# National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

Founded in 1910 as a secular national university replacing colonial antecedents, UNAM is Mexico’s largest and most prestigious public research institution. The sprawling main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, built in the 1950s, is a UNESCO World Heritage site featuring murals by renowned artists and notable architecture.

UNAM encompasses numerous schools offering undergraduate and graduate programs and runs extensive satellite campuses. It has a strong tradition of political activism and cultural influence in Mexico and Latin America.

The university is a global leader in scientific research, especially in artificial intelligence and robotics, hosting many Mexican Nobel laureates among alumni and faculty. UNAM’s athletic programs include successful football and volleyball teams.

It is symbolized by the motto “For my people the spirit shall speak,” embracing a pan-American identity and social commitment. Alumni include heads of state, prominent intellectuals, scientists, and artists.
