### **Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)**

#### **History**
UFRJ, established in 1920, traces its origins to Brazil’s first higher education courses. Initially named the University of Rio de Janeiro, it evolved from three foundational institutions: the *Escola Politécnica* (founded 1792), the *Faculdade Nacional de Medicina* (1808), and the *Faculdade Nacional de Direito* (1891). The university underwent significant restructuring, including a name change to the *University of Brazil* in 1937 under Getúlio Vargas’s educational reforms, influenced by French university models. In 1965, it gained full autonomy, adopting its current name, *Federal University of Rio de Janeiro*.

UFRJ played a pivotal role in Brazil’s higher education system, fostering innovation and research. Its history reflects Brazil’s cultural, economic, and social development, with notable milestones including the establishment of research institutes, partnerships with international agencies, and a shift toward a more research-focused university.

#### **Present Day**
UFRJ maintains an "open-doors" policy, welcoming international students and fostering global exchange. Its mascot, Minerva, symbolizes knowledge and intellectuality. The university emphasizes research, with a motto: *"In a university, one teaches because one researches."* It offers extensive extension programs, particularly in public health, and is renowned for its nine college hospitals, contributing significantly to Rio de Janeiro’s healthcare system.

The university operates on a Roman Catholic-inspired model, with Minerva as its mascot, and has been involved in groundbreaking projects like the Technological Park of Rio, which focuses on energy, oil, and gas research. UFRJ’s commitment to sustainability and innovation is evident in its initiatives, such as the *MagLev Cobra*, a levitation train project, and *Plant Waves*, a wave energy research project.

#### **Organization**
UFRJ is an autonomous public institution under the Ministry of Education (MEC). Its governance includes a University Council, Curators Council, Undergraduate Council, and Graduate Council, with the rector as the highest authority. The university comprises six academic centers, including the Center of Health Sciences (CCS), Center of Technology (CT), and Center of Mathematical and Natural Sciences (CCMN). Notable alumni include scientists, politicians, and cultural figures, reflecting its diverse impact on Brazilian society.

#### **Campuses**
UFRJ’s main campus, *Cidade Universitária* (University City), is located on Ilha do Fundão, a former artificial island. The campus includes the *Clementino Fraga Filho University Hospital*, a major healthcare facility. Other notable buildings include the *School of Music*, *National Museum*, and *Technological Park of Rio*. The university also operates campuses in Macaé, Duque de Caxias, and other regions, catering to specialized programs in engineering, medicine, and environmental sciences.

### **Cornell University**

#### **History**
Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, initially as a land-grant institution. It quickly became a leader in technological innovation, pioneering electrification and research collaborations. During the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Cornell expanded its academic offerings and became a hub for cultural and social activism, including opposition to the Vietnam War.

The university’s history is marked by significant milestones, such as its role in the development of nuclear and high-energy physics, computing technology, and advancements in automotive safety. Cornell’s academic programs have consistently ranked among the best globally, and its research contributions have been substantial.

#### **Campuses**
Cornell’s main campus in Ithaca, New York, is renowned for its picturesque setting and diverse architectural styles. The campus includes historic buildings like the Andrew Dickson White House and Morrill Hall, as well as modern facilities like the Atkinson Center for Sustainability. Cornell also operates campuses in New York City, including the Weill Cornell Medical College and Cornell Tech, and has international partnerships with institutions worldwide.

#### **Organization**
Cornell’s governance is structured around a board of trustees, a decentralized academic structure, and 16 colleges, including 12 privately endowed and four publicly supported statutory colleges. The university’s research activities are extensive, with a focus on science, technology, engineering, and medicine. Cornell’s commitment to sustainability is evident in its green initiatives, such as solar farms and an enhanced geothermal system.

#### **Academics**
Cornell offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, with a strong emphasis on research and interdisciplinary studies. The university is known for its rigorous admissions process and commitment to need-blind admission for U.S. applicants. Cornell’s reputation for excellence in business, engineering, and the arts is well-established, with notable rankings in global university lists.

#### **Student Life**
Cornell’s student life is vibrant, with over 1,000 registered student organizations, including clubs for sports, arts, and cultural activities. The university’s Greek life system is extensive, with many chapters among the oldest in the nation. Student media, such as *The Cornell Daily Sun* and *The Cornell Review*, provide a platform for diverse perspectives. Cornell’s commitment to sustainability and community engagement is reflected in its initiatives, including the Cornell Outdoor Education program.

### **University of Toronto**

#### **History**
The University of Toronto traces its origins to the colonial college established in 1827, initially named King’s College. The university underwent significant changes in the 19th century, including the secularization of its institutions and the establishment of the Faculty of Medicine, Law, and Dentistry. The university’s growth was accelerated by the establishment of colleges like Trinity and University College, which contributed to its collegiate system.

In the 20th century, the university expanded its academic offerings and research capabilities, becoming a leading institution in Canada. Notable events include the establishment of the Faculty of Education, the opening of the Scarborough and Mississauga campuses, and the university’s role in the development of key scientific advancements, such as insulin and the discovery of stem cells.

#### **Campuses**
The University of Toronto operates three campuses: the St. George campus in downtown Toronto, the Scarborough campus, and the Mississauga campus. The St. George campus is the largest and most historic, featuring Gothic Revival and neoclassical architecture. The university’s research facilities, such as the David Dunlap Observatory and the Hart House, are integral to its academic and cultural life.

#### **Organization**
The university’s governance is structured around a Governing Council, Senate, and faculties. The university’s collegiate system, which includes seven constituent colleges and several federated colleges, provides a unique academic environment. The university’s research output is substantial, with a significant focus on biomedical science, engineering, and social sciences.

#### **Academics**
The University of Toronto offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs across 14 professional faculties and schools. The university is renowned for its research contributions, including the discovery of insulin, the development of the glycaemic index, and advancements in stem cell research. The university’s commitment to interdisciplinary studies and public engagement is evident in its programs and initiatives.

#### **Student Life**
The University of Toronto’s student life is rich and diverse, with numerous student organizations, clubs, and cultural events. The university’s student unions, such as the University of Toronto Students’ Union, play a crucial role in representing student interests. The university’s commitment to sustainability and community engagement is reflected in its initiatives, including the Hart House, a student activity center that promotes communal living and cultural events.

### **National University of Singapore (NUS)**

#### **History**
The National University of Singapore (NUS) was founded in 1911 as the University of Malaya, initially offering medical and arts programs. The university underwent significant changes in the mid-20th century, including the establishment of the Faculty of Law, the Faculty of Science, and the Faculty of Engineering. In 1980, NUS was formed by the merger of the University of Singapore and Nanyang University, reflecting the government’s desire to promote English as the primary language of education.

NUS has since become a leading research university in Asia, known for its strong emphasis on innovation, entrepreneurship, and interdisciplinary studies. The university has celebrated its 120th anniversary and has been recognized for its global rankings and research contributions.

#### **Campuses**
NUS operates three main campuses: the Kent Ridge campus, the Bukit Timah campus, and the Outram campus. The Kent Ridge campus is the largest and most central, housing most of the university’s faculties and research institutes. The university also operates the NUS High School of Mathematics and Science, a specialized secondary school.

#### **Organization**
NUS is governed by a university council, vice-chancellor, and various faculties and schools. The university’s research output is substantial, with a focus on biomedical science, engineering, and social sciences. NUS is known for its strong partnerships with international universities and its commitment to sustainability and innovation.

#### **Academics**
NUS offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs across 17 faculties and schools. The university is renowned for its research contributions, including advancements in biomedical engineering, nanoscience, and artificial intelligence. The university’s commitment to interdisciplinary studies and global engagement is reflected in its programs and initiatives.

#### **Student Life**
NUS’s student life is vibrant, with numerous student organizations, clubs, and cultural events. The university’s residential colleges, such as Cinnamon College and Tembusu College, provide a unique living and learning environment. The university’s commitment to sustainability and community engagement is evident in its initiatives, including the NUS University Town and the NUS Sea project.
