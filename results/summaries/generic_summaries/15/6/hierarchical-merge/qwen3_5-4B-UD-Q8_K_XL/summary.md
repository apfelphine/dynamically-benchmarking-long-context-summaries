# Master Summary

## Asian and North American Institutions

The National University of Singapore (NUS) marks its 120th anniversary in 2025 following a 1980 merger that integrated British and American models, evolving from Singapore's sole law school into an interdisciplinary powerhouse through its 2021 partnership with Yale. The University of Hong Kong (HKU), founded in 1911, maintains a unique governance structure where the Chief Executive serves as Chancellor, upholding a legacy of research excellence highlighted by its role in identifying the SARS virus.

Cornell University, established in 1865 as New York's land-grant institution, operates as a decentralized research hub with campuses in New York, Qatar, and Roosevelt Island, boasting 62 Nobel laureates and a robust STEM startup ecosystem. Despite facing challenges including historical fires, antisemitism investigations, and DEI scrutiny, Cornell aims for net carbon neutrality by 2035. Meanwhile, the University of Toronto functions as a decentralized federation of autonomous colleges, securing a $1 billion endowment and a $4 billion fundraising campaign to address funding pressures and enhance mental health support.

## Australian and North American Institutions

The University of New South Wales (UNSW) has transitioned from a technology-focused institute to a generalist university with multiple Australian campuses. After overcoming a 2020 deficit that required job cuts and a shift to a trimester system, UNSW expanded its research portfolio, including the Lowy Cancer Research Centre, and remains the record holder for producing the most millionaires among Australian universities.

The University of Melbourne distinguishes itself through the "Melbourne Model" of generalized undergraduate degrees and its history as the first institution to admit women. While renowned for high retention rates and vibrant campus life, recent years have seen significant governance restructuring, including workforce casualization and 450 academic layoffs due to cost-cutting measures.

The University of Oxford, with 12th-century roots, maintains a unique collegiate structure comprising 39 colleges that manage admissions and social life alongside departmental oversight. It has successfully transitioned from a rigid theological establishment into a modern research powerhouse, achieving full coeducation in 1920 and cultivating a diverse community of 47% female undergraduates.

The University of British Columbia (UBC), established in 1908 on the territories of the Musqueam and Syilx Nations, operates under a bicameral governance structure and maintains a balanced budget exceeding $3.8 billion. As a top-ranked research-intensive institution, UBC has produced four Canadian Prime Ministers and eight Nobel Prize winners, fostering a diverse student body that includes over 28% international and 3.2% Indigenous students.
