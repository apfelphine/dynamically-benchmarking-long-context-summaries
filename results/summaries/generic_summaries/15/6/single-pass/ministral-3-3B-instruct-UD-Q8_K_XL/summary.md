### **National University of Singapore (NUS)**

#### **History**
The NUS traces its origins to 1904, when Tan Jiak Kim and others petitioned for a medical school in Singapore. Founded in 1905 as the Straits Settlements and Federated Malay States Government Medical School, it evolved into the King Edward VII Medical School (1912) and King Edward VII College of Medicine (1921). The university expanded with the merger of Raffles College (1928) and the University of Malaya (1949). The NUS was officially established in 1980 by merging the University of Singapore and Nanyang University, aiming to centralize resources and promote English as the primary language of instruction.

#### **Campuses and Structure**
NUS operates across three campuses: **Kent Ridge** (main), **Bukit Timah**, and **Outram**. The university comprises **17 faculties and schools**, including the **Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine**, **Duke-NUS Medical School**, and **NUS College**, which offers honors programs. The university also hosts the **NUS Entrepreneurship Centre** and the **NUS High School of Mathematics and Science**.

#### **Academics and Reputation**
NUS is renowned for its **strong research output** and **international reputation**. It consistently ranks among the top universities globally, with a focus on **STEM fields, business, and medicine**. Notable achievements include the **QS World University Rankings (2026)**, where NUS secured **#8 globally** and **#17 in Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings (2025)**. The university is also recognized for its **diverse student body** (36% international students) and **high graduate employability**.

#### **Research and Innovation**
NUS is a leader in **biomedical engineering, nanoscience, and artificial intelligence**. Key research centers include the **Cancer Science Institute of Singapore**, **Centre for Quantum Technologies**, and **Mechanobiology Institute**. The university collaborates with global institutions, fostering innovation and entrepreneurship through initiatives like the **NUS Entrepreneurship Centre**.

#### **Campus Facilities**
NUS boasts state-of-the-art facilities, including **NUSNET (intranet)**, **NUS Libraries** (with over 3 million volumes), and **Digital Gems**, a digital newspaper archive. The university also operates **University Town (UTown)**, a residential hub with dining, lecture theaters, and recreational spaces.

#### **Student Life**
NUS offers a vibrant student experience with **over 1,000 student organizations**, including **sports clubs, cultural societies, and academic societies**. The university emphasizes **diversity and inclusion**, with initiatives supporting underrepresented groups. Notable traditions include **Slope Day** and **Dragon Day**, celebrating architectural and cultural heritage.

### **University of Hong Kong (HKU)**

#### **History**
Founded in 1911, HKU emerged from the **Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese** (1887) and the **Hong Kong College of Medicine** (1907). The university was established to promote British culture and technological advancement in the region. Key milestones include the **opening of the Main Building (1912)**, the **Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine (2001)**, and the **Centennial Campus (2012)**. HKU has faced political challenges, including the **2011 protests** and **2015 political interference allegations**.

#### **Campuses and Structure**
HKU operates across **three campuses**: the **main campus (Pok Fu Lam)**, the **Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine (Sandy Bay)**, and the **Kadoorie Agricultural Research Centre (New Territories)**. The university comprises **ten faculties**, including the **Faculty of Medicine, Law, Business & Economics, and Science**, alongside **graduate schools and non-faculty academic units**.

It secured **#11 globally in QS World University Rankings (2026)** and **#35 in THE World University Rankings (2025)**. The university is known for its **strong research output**, particularly in **biomedical sciences, engineering, and social sciences**.

#### **Research and Innovation**
HKU is a leader in **research funding and international collaborations**. Key research centers include the **Centre for Development of Teaching and Learning (CDTL)**, **Institute of Systems Science (ISS)**, and the **Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine**. The university operates the **HKU Scholars Hub**, a platform for research output and citations.

#### **Campus Facilities**
HKU’s main campus features **the Main Building, Swire Building, and Tang Chi Ngong Building**, along with the **Centennial Campus**. The university also operates the **Fung Ping Shan Library**, **University Museum and Art Gallery**, and **Kadoorie Agricultural Research Centre**.
