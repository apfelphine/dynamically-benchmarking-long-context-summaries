Cornell University, founded in 1865, is a leading U.S. research institution with a strong focus on engineering, computer science, and physics, and a global presence through its medical and international campuses. It maintains a $10.7 billion endowment, a decentralized structure, and a commitment to diversity and sustainability, with notable research achievements and a vibrant student life. The National University of Singapore (NUS) is a globally top-tier institution, renowned for entrepreneurial education and innovation, with strong international ties and top global rankings. The Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ) is a major public research university in Brazil, known for its research in health, technology, and environmental sciences, and a historically active student body. The University of Hong Kong (HKU) is ranked #1 in Asia and recognized as the most international university globally, with a strong emphasis on scientific impact and student well-being. The University of Toronto is Canada’s oldest and most prominent university, known for its research excellence, public health, and global leadership in education and innovation. The University of New South Wales (UNSW) is a leading Australian university with strong research outcomes and a vibrant student life, though it has faced controversies over governance and ethics. The University of Oxford, one of the world’s oldest and most prestigious institutions, is renowned for its academic rigor, extensive library system, and global influence in humanities, law, and science. The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) is one of the largest and most prestigious universities in the world, with over 324,000 students, a major research focus in AI and robotics, and a rich cultural and historical legacy, including its UNESCO-recognized Ciudad Universitaria campus and prominent student-led movements. UNAM is politically active, with students and faculty deeply engaged in left-wing movements, yet it has also produced influential right-wing leaders and business figures like Carlos Slim. The university hosts vibrant cultural traditions, such as an annual Day of the Dead display in its main square, and supports diversity through student associations and initiatives like large-scale Latina hackathons. UNAM has produced all three of Mexico’s Nobel laureates—Alfonso García Robles (Peace), Octavio Paz (Literature), and Mario Molina (Chemistry)—and boasts a distinguished faculty and alumni list, including former Mexican presidents, prominent intellectuals, artists, scientists, and global figures in film, literature, and science. Notable UNAM alumni include physicians Nora Volkow and Ignacio Chávez, scientists Mario Molina and Guillermo Haro, business leaders Carlos Slim and Alfredo Harp Helú, and sports figures Hugo Sánchez and Daniel Vargas. The University of British Columbia (UBC), established in 1908 through the *University Act* and modeled after the University of Toronto, began operations at the Fairview site in 1915, absorbed McGill University College, and awarded its first degrees in 1916. It later moved to Point Grey in 1925 and features a bicameral governance structure, including the appointment of Evlyn Farris as the first woman on its board and senate. UBC began with a focus on professional education and graduate training, faced financial hardship during the Great Depression and WWII, including the 1935 Grand Campus Washout, and relied on federal research funding. Post-war growth was driven by returning veterans, leading to expanded enrollment and the construction of facilities such as the War Memorial Gym. UBC expanded its academic offerings, launched key initiatives like the Museum of Anthropology (1976) and programs for educators of students with disabilities, and undertook major capital campaigns including "World of Opportunity" (1993) and "Start an Evolution" (2015). The main Point Grey campus is located on Musqueam land and includes diverse facilities, gardens, and cultural venues, while UBC also operates a satellite campus in Vancouver and an Okanagan campus established in 2005 on Syilx Okanagan ancestral land. UBC operates two main campuses: Vancouver and Okanagan. UBC Okanagan, with 10,708 students in 2019, offers 62 undergraduate and 19 graduate programs across diverse disciplines, including Medicine through the Southern Medical Program. It underwent a $450 million expansion from 2005–2012 and is expanding into downtown Kelowna with a 43-storey sustainable building set to open in 2027. UBC’s library, Canada’s second-largest academic library, holds 7.8 million volumes and over 370,000 e-journals, with notable collections including the Kelmscott Chaucer and the Uno Langmann Family Collection of B.C. photographs. The Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery manages over 5,000 art objects and hosts annual exhibitions. UBC emphasizes sustainability through initiatives like ECOTrek and UBC Renew, which reduced water and energy use, and the net-positive CIRS building, which uses renewable energy, rainwater, and composting. Governance includes a chancellor, board of governors, senate, and convocation, with elected faculty and student representation. UBC has 12 faculties in Vancouver and 7 in Okanagan, including the Faculty of Arts (largest), Faculty of Science, Sauder School of Business, and the newly established School of Biomedical Engineering. UBC is a globally ranked research-intensive institution, consistently ranked among the top universities in Canada and the world in academic excellence, research output, and employability. It offers a dual Bachelor of Arts degree with Sciences Po in France, with students spending two years in France and two at UBC, and guaranteed admission to a Sciences Po graduate program. UBC has strong international partnerships, with over 200 study-abroad options, and a diverse student body of 72,585, including 28% international and 3.2% Indigenous students. The university is a leader in research, with the second-highest external research funding in Canada (C$577 million in 2017), and operates key research centers such as TRIUMF, the Peter Wall Institute, and the Stewart Blusson Quantum Matter Institute. UBC also has a dedicated Indigenous program through the Longhouse and supports Indigenous education, entrepreneurship, and community initiatives. Financially, UBC’s 2024–25 budget exceeded $3.8 billion, with government grants and student tuition accounting for 40% and 41% of revenue, respectively. Tuition has seen significant increases over the years, including a 30% rise in 2003–04 and a 46.8% increase for international students in 2016–19, totaling over 60% in four years. Graduate tuition varies by program, with PhD students receiving a guaranteed $24,000 annual funding package. Student representation is provided by the Alma Mater Society (AMS) and Graduate Student Society (GSS), with elected student representatives on the board of governors and academic senate. The AMS manages the Student Union Building ("The Nest"), a major hub with diverse amenities, while the Okanagan Campus is represented by the Students' Union Okanagan (UBCSUO), which also launched an emergency bursary program during the pandemic. UBC has a diverse student body, with 57% females at Vancouver and 53% at Okanagan. Key student facilities include the Ladha Science Centre, Arts Student Center, and bookstores. UBC’s 19 Greek organizations are among Canada’s largest, and the campus hosts a vibrant athletic culture through the Thunderbirds, who compete in U Sports and the NAIA, with a strong history in rowing and swimming. The Thunderbird Marching Band, student-run and unique in Western Canada, performs at major events. Traditional events like Storm the Wall and Day of the Longboat foster campus engagement. UBC offers a vibrant student life with faculty-led events such as faculty weeks, the former Arts County Fair (now replaced by the AMS Block Party), and the annual Undie Run charity event. The university hosts major academic events like the Harvard World Model United Nations Conference in 2012, which was the largest student conference in Canada. Engineering students are known for pranks, though UBC does not publicize them. UBC has produced numerous notable alumni and faculty, including Canadian Prime Ministers Justin Trudeau and Kim Campbell, Nobel laureates like Robert Mundell and Bertram Brockhouse, and influential figures in science, arts, and law. The University of Melbourne, founded in 1853 and headquartered at the Parkville campus, has multiple campuses across Melbourne and regional Victoria, including Southbank, Burnley, Werribee, Creswick, Dookie, and Shepparton, each serving specialized academic and research functions. Under Vice-Chancellor David Maskell, the university faced criticism for disciplinary actions, surveillance practices, and the use of student internet location data, leading to a privacy violation investigation by the Victorian Information Commissioner. In 2024, Maskell stepped down, succeeded by Emma Johnston, the first woman to hold the role. Student protests over Gaza-related issues led to a breach notice, ongoing encampment disputes, and continued tensions over university investments linked to Israel and the Gaza conflict. The university is governed under the University of Melbourne Act 2009, with the Council overseeing strategic direction, budget, and policies, while the Academic Board manages teaching and research. A new engineering and architecture campus in Fishermans Bend is planned for construction starting in 2024, with operations beginning in 2026. The University of Melbourne is a leading Australian research university with a strong academic and research profile. Governed by the University Council and led by the chancellor, vice-chancellor, and senior executives, it operates through nine faculties covering teaching and research. It has a significant endowment of $1.335 billion, recovering from financial downturns through restructuring and fundraising (e.g., "Believe in 2013" campaign). The university is ranked highly globally and domestically for research, with the second-largest research expenditure in Australia. It follows a unique "Melbourne Model" undergraduate structure with generalized three-year degrees. It supports entrepreneurship via the Melbourne Entrepreneurial Centre and Wade Institute, and maintains extensive library, museum, and gallery resources across campuses, including the Grainger Museum, Ian Potter Museum of Art, and Fiona & Sidney Myer Gallery. The university is recognized for high graduate employment rates (88.3% for postgraduates), strong employer satisfaction (85.4%), and exceptional student experience (73.8–73.9%). It has one of the highest admission requirements in Australia, with a median ATAR of 94.05. The student life is vibrant, supported by over 200 clubs and societies, the UMSU and GSA, and a wide range of sports and recreational facilities. The university has nine residential colleges, including notable historical and ongoing institutions like Melbourne University Football Club and Boat Club. The University of Melbourne recognizes outstanding contributions through awards such as the University of Melbourne Award (honoring enduring service) and the Patricia Grimshaw Awards for Mentor Excellence (recognizing mentoring excellence). The Dublin Prize, established by Trinity College Dublin, honors original contributions to literature, science, or art by former students. The university’s campus features notable buildings including the Clocktower, Old Arts Building, Conservatorium, Trinity College Chapel, and the Glyn Davis Building, reflecting its historical and architectural significance.
