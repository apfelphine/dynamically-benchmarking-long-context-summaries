**Institutional Profiles: Global University Overview**

This summary profiles several prominent global universities, detailing their characteristics, history, and operational focuses.

**University of Melbourne:** A highly ranked and multifaceted institution founded in 1857, it has a rich history, notably admitting its first female graduate in 1883. It operates across multiple campuses, overseen by a University Council, and boasts a strong academic profile (e.g., #19 in QS 2026) with high student satisfaction. The university features nine faculties covering diverse areas, strong entrepreneurial support, and maintains extensive resources. Key features include several owned halls of residence, the Clocktower, and the Glyn Davis Building. It also honors achievements through awards like the University of Melbourne Award and the Dublin Prize. International students account for 44% of the body. Leadership transitioned with Emma Johnston succeeding Maskell in September 2024.

**University of British Columbia (UBC):** A top-ranked institution with over 72,585 students (28% international) and a budget exceeding $3.8 billion (2024–25), UBC emphasizes sustainability and supports Indigenous students across strong academic disciplines.

**National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM):** Established in 1910, this large, secular national institution champions academic freedom, gender inclusion, and supports STEM minorities while researching AI and robotics.

**University of Oxford:** An elite, 12th-century Russell Group university with a vast endowment (£8.708 billion), it maintains a rigorous tutorial system across its 39 colleges and supports fossil fuel divestment.

**Cornell University:** A selective, non-profit research institution founded in 1865, known for its need-blind U.S. admissions policy and strengths in fields like nuclear physics.

**University of Toronto (U of T):** Founded in 1827/1878, this large, philanthropic institution serves over 100,000 students, contributing to research in computing and insulin while prioritizing mental health.

**National University of Singapore (NUS):** Founded in 1980, this top-ranked, comprehensive university excels in Computer Science and Engineering, supported by robust industry collaboration.

**University of Hong Kong (HKU):** Established in 1887, HKU is a globally recognized international research institution with ten faculties, notable for a large proportion of non-local students.

**University of New South Wales (UNSW):** This multi-campus, generalist institution with six faculties maintains high global rankings despite facing concerns related to campus safety and academic integrity.

**Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ):** Since 1920, this public research institution focuses heavily on public health through nine college hospitals and implemented affirmative action in 2011.
