# Cornell University

Founded in 1865 as New York's land-grant university, Cornell was established by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, focusing on innovative research and inclusive governance. Its Ithaca campus encompasses diverse architectural styles and natural surroundings, including gorge areas and extensive botanic gardens, and emphasizes sustainability through initiatives like solar farms and geothermal heating. Cornell also operates urban campuses in New York City (Weill Cornell Medicine and tech-focused Cornell Tech) and Qatar’s Education City medical college.

Cornell's decentralized structure includes 16 autonomous colleges, mixing private and state-supported entities, serving over 15,000 undergraduates with competitive admissions emphasizing diversity. The university has a strong philanthropic endowment ($10.7 billion in 2024) supporting premier research, sustainability, and professional schools. Academically, Cornell maintains top-ranked Ph.D. programs and is active in international education and partnerships. Its research spans nuclear physics, computing, and philosophy. Student life features vibrant activities, Greek life, and traditions, with a notable focus on health and campus safety. Cornell alumni and faculty include numerous Nobel laureates and influential figures across government, science, and arts.

# National University of Singapore (NUS)

NUS originated in 1905 with a medical school established by community petitioners and evolved through various mergers, notably the 1980 fusion of the University of Singapore and Nanyang University to form NUS. It now comprises 17 faculties across three campuses in Singapore, with strong international collaborations and a diverse body comprising a large proportion of foreign faculty and students.

The university maintains a modern, semester-based modular academic system featuring a recently formed honors college, NUS College, emphasizing global citizenship. It houses faculties spanning business, computing, dentistry, design and engineering, humanities and sciences, law, medicine, public policy, and music, alongside graduate schools and joint programs with Duke University and Yale (the latter now merged). NUS is prominent in research across biomedical sciences, nanoscience, and technology, hosting numerous government-recognized centers of excellence.

NUS consistently ranks among the top globally and nationally, with specialized subject excellence in engineering, medicine, arts, and social sciences. Its extensive library system holds over three million titles supplemented by digital archives. Student life includes diverse residential colleges supported by competitive admissions, vibrant student organizations, and strong emphasis on entrepreneurship and global experience through programs like NUS Overseas Colleges.

# Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)

UFRJ, established in 1920 and rooted in Brazil’s earliest higher education institutions, plays a foundational role in national education. It underwent reforms reflecting French university models and expanded research autonomy post-1958. The university operates across multiple campuses in Rio de Janeiro and other cities, with a main campus on Ilha do Fundão (College City) housing significant faculties, research centers, and historical museums including the National Museum and House of Science.

Structurally, UFRJ comprises six major academic centers covering health sciences, technology, natural sciences, law and economics, philosophy and humanities, and literature and arts. The university also relies heavily on its network of affiliated hospitals and research facilities, integrating public health services. Admissions are highly competitive with affirmative action policies, and the university is active in extension education.

UFRJ's research reputation spans biophysics, oceanography, and engineering, with notable projects like the MagLev Cobra train and oceanic tanks. Its Technological Park hosts partnerships with Petrobras and other major industry players. Alumni include leading scientists, artists, politicians, and Nobel laureates, reflecting the institution’s broad societal impact.

# University of Hong Kong (HKU)

Founded in 1911, HKU is Hong Kong’s oldest university with origins dating to an 1887 medical college. Established to promote British influence via education, it has evolved to embrace both Western and Eastern cultural education. Post-WWII, the university expanded faculties to social sciences, law, and dentistry, and suffered setbacks during Japanese occupation.

HKU includes a main campus with iconic Edwardian architecture, a medical campus, and satellite research facilities. It operates a broad academic portfolio across ten faculties and associate colleges, with teaching mainly in English.

The university is governed by a chancellor (Hong Kong’s chief executive) and bodies including the Court, Senate, and Council, with a focus on academic excellence and freedom. HKU ranks among the world's top universities, especially noted for internationalization and strong subject-specific global rankings in law, medicine, engineering, and business.

Student life comprises numerous residential halls, a rich tradition of student activism, diverse organizations, and notable alumni like Sun Yat-sen and government leaders. The university maintains robust research output and a significant library system critical to Hong Kong’s academic heritage.

# University of Toronto (U of T)

Chartered in 1827 as King's College and secularized in 1849, the University of Toronto grew into Canada’s largest research university with three campuses: St. George (downtown Toronto), Mississauga, and Scarborough. It has a collegiate system with federated religious colleges alongside constituent colleges.

U of T is renowned for its research productivity, medical discoveries (including insulin), and contributions to computing and science. It houses 14 professional faculties, including the prestigious Rotman School of Management and Temerty Faculty of Medicine. Its libraries form the third-largest academic system in North America.

The university has a rich culture, including Hart House, a hub for the arts, debating, and athletics. It offers diverse student media, Greek life, and a range of residences. U of T is highly ranked globally, with strong graduate employability. Alumni include multiple Canadian prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and leading professionals across various fields.

# University of New South Wales (UNSW)

Founded in 1949 as the New South Wales University of Technology to address post-WWII industrial needs, UNSW evolved into a broad-based university renamed in 1958. It is located primarily in Sydney with multiple campuses, including the Kensington main campus.

UNSW emphasizes engineering, business, arts, medicine, and law, with notable facilities like the Lowy Cancer Research Centre. The university has a governance structure headed by a chancellor and an academic board and comprises six faculties. It has a history of entrepreneurial and social impact initiatives, including international partnerships and academic competitions.

Student life is coordinated through Arc @ UNSW, combining previous student organizations, offering extensive services, media, and event venues. UNSW also fosters student projects and boasts extensive exchange programs.

The university has faced controversies including campus incidents, labor disputes over academic staff pay, and political challenges linked to free speech. UNSW regularly ranks among Australia’s top universities in research and graduate outcomes.

# University of Oxford

Established in the 12th century, Oxford is the oldest university in the English-speaking world, with roots in ecclesiastical scholarship. It developed a collegiate system with 39 colleges and several permanent private halls, forming a world-renowned center for humanities, sciences, and professional studies.

Oxford’s governance includes the Chancellor (chief executive of Hong Kong), Vice-Chancellor (principal academic officer), the Court, Senate, and Council. Admissions are highly competitive, involving interviews and college affiliations. It offers tutorial-based teaching with strong scholarship and financial aid programs.

Its Bodleian Libraries system is among the largest globally, alongside a network of museums including the Ashmolean and Pitt Rivers. Oxford University Press is the world’s second-oldest and largest university press.

Consistently ranked among the world’s top universities, Oxford boasts a rich history of notable alumni including British prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and monarchs. Student life revolves around collegiate traditions, societies like the Oxford Union, and a vibrant intellectual and cultural milieu.

# National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)

Founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra to replace colonial-era universities with a secular, national institution, UNAM has grown into Latin America’s largest and most prestigious university. It played a key role in Mexican cultural and political life, enduring student strikes and political challenges, notably during the 1968 Tlatelolco massacre.

UNAM’s main campus, Ciudad Universitaria, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site hosting numerous faculties, cultural centers, museums, and libraries, with satellite campuses nationwide. Academically, it is organized into multiple schools offering undergraduate and graduate programs, including an extensive high school system.

UNAM excels in research, especially in AI and robotics, and operates many research institutes addressing regional and global issues. Its athletic programs are significant nationally, including professional and college football teams.

The university’s cultural legacy includes a distinctive seal and mascot (the puma), a commitment to minority inclusion and political activism. Its alumni include Mexican presidents, Nobel laureates, and influential artists, scientists, and politicians.

# University of British Columbia (UBC)

Established by provincial legislation in 1908 and opened in 1915, UBC is a leading Canadian research university with two main campuses: Vancouver (Point Grey) and Okanagan. The Vancouver campus is near downtown Vancouver and features extensive gardens, cultural venues, and sustainable architecture.

UBC’s governance features a board of governors and senate, with a diverse student body including many international and Indigenous students. Academically, it has twelve faculties in Vancouver and seven at Okanagan, offering a wide range of programs and strongly emphasizing research.

Known for sustainability efforts and Indigenous engagement, UBC operates Canada’s second-largest academic library system and multiple research centers. Student life includes a large student union (AMS), active Greek organizations, residences, and a vibrant athletics program featuring the Thunderbirds teams.

UBC alumni and faculty include multiple Canadian prime ministers, Nobel laureates, astronauts, artists, and academics, reflecting its broad influence.

# University of Melbourne

Founded in 1853 in Victoria, Australia, the University of Melbourne is the country’s second-oldest university, established during the gold rush era with strong Irish influences. It expanded through the 20th century, incorporating several colleges, adopting a generalist "Melbourne Model" undergraduate structure in 2008, and developing multiple campuses.

The university has diverse faculties spanning arts, sciences, business, engineering, medicine, law, education, and fine arts, with significant research output and over three million library items. It maintains notable museums, galleries, and cultural collections across its campuses in Parkville, Southbank, Burnley, and regional sites.

Governed by a council and academic board, Melbourne boasts numerous alumni distinguished in politics, science, arts, and business, including multiple prime ministers and Nobel laureates. Student life is rich with unions, sports, residential colleges, and traditions, though recent years have seen financial and workforce challenges, along with student activism related to global issues.
