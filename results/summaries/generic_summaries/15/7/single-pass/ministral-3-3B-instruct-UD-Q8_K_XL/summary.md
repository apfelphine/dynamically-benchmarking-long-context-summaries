### **University of Hong Kong (HKU)**

#### **History**
The University of Hong Kong (HKU) traces its origins to the **Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese (1887)**, later renamed the **Hong Kong College of Medicine (1907)** and incorporated as HKU’s medical school in **1911**. Founded in **1911**, HKU was established to promote British culture and education in China, competing with European universities. The university’s founding was supported by Sir Frederick Lugard, Sir Hormusjee Naorojee Mody, and the Hong Kong government. Initially an all-male institution, women were admitted in **1921**. The university expanded its faculties to **Arts, Engineering, and Medicine** and later included **Law, Social Sciences, Dentistry, and Business & Economics**.

During the **1920s–1930s**, HKU shifted toward Chinese cultural education and established the **Queen Mary Hospital (1937)**. Post-WWII, the university expanded its **Law and Social Sciences faculties**, growing its student population significantly. In **1982**, the **Faculty of Dentistry** was established, and in **2001**, the **Faculty of Business & Economics** became the university’s newest faculty.

In **2006**, the **Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine** was renamed to honor Li Ka Shing’s contributions. HKU has celebrated its **100th anniversary (2010–2012)** and continues to expand with new initiatives, including the **Centennial Campus (2012)** and partnerships with global institutions.

#### **Campuses**
- **Main Campus**: Located in **Pokfulam**, covering **177,000 sq m**, featuring historic buildings like the **Main Building** (1910–1912) and the **Swire Building** (1980). The campus includes the **Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine** (near Sandy Bay) and the **Kadoorie Agricultural Research Centre** (New Territories).
- **Queen Mary Hospital**: A teaching hospital for the medical faculty.
- **Other Facilities**: Includes the **Swire Institute of Marine Science** (Hong Kong Island) and the **University of Hong Kong Library** (over **2.3 million holdings**).

#### **Academics**
HKU is highly competitive, with **over 40,000 applications** for undergraduate programs in **2016**. Admissions are based on **HKDSE** scores, with a **high enrollment rate for Mainland China applicants**. The university offers **4-year undergraduate degrees** (except for Medicine and Nursing) and emphasizes **English as the medium of instruction**. Research is strong, with **100+ faculty members ranked in the top 1% globally** by Thomson Reuters’ Essential Science Indicators.

The university is a member of **Universitas 21** and has a **large research budget** from government funding. Notable research areas include **biomedical sciences, engineering, and social sciences**.

#### **Reputation and Rankings**
HKU consistently ranks among the top universities in **Asia and globally**, including:
- **QS World University Rankings 2026**: #11 globally, #1 in Asia.
- **THE World University Rankings 2025**: #35 globally.
- **Academic Ranking of World Universities 2024**: #69 globally.
- **U.S. News 2025–2026**: #44 globally.
- **QS Graduate Employability Ranking 2022**: #47 globally.

HKU is also recognized for its **internationalization** and **graduate employability**.

#### **Student Life**
The university has a **diverse student population**, with **19,145 non-local students (47.2%)** in **2023**. There are **20 residential halls and colleges**, including **Swire Hall, Simon K. Y. Lee Hall, and the Jockey Club Student Village**.

Student organizations include the **Hong Kong University Students’ Union (HKUSU)**, which offers **over 100 student societies**. The university provides **student welfare services**, including career counseling, health care, and financial support.

#### **Notable Alumni**
HKU has produced many notable figures, including:
- **Sun Yat-sen** (founder of the Republic of China).
- **Guan Yi** (researcher in SARS).
- **Over 40 principal officials and permanent secretaries of the Hong Kong SAR Government**.

### **University of New South Wales (UNSW)**

#### **History**
UNSW was founded in **1949** as the **New South Wales University of Technology** and became a generalist university in **1958**. The university was established to meet the growing demand for higher education in engineering and technology post-WWII. Key figures included **Arthur Denning (first director)** and **Philip Baxter (first vice-chancellor)**.

In the **1960s–1970s**, UNSW expanded its faculties to include **Arts, Medicine, and Law**. The university faced challenges, including **student protests, funding cuts, and the closure of its Singapore campus (2007)** due to financial losses.

In **2012**, UNSW celebrated its **50th anniversary** and launched the **UNSW Bragg Prize for Science Writing**. The university has since faced controversies, including **job cuts (2020)**, **research misconduct allegations (2021)**, and **political controversies (2020–2021)**.

- **Art & Design Campus (Paddington)**: Offers undergraduate and Honours degrees in **Visual Arts, Design, Animation & Moving Image, and Art Theory**.
- **Sydney CBD Campus**: A pilot project in **2025**, located at **Poly Centre**.
- **Other Campuses**: Includes **Randwick, Manly Vale, Canberra, and regional campuses**.

Admissions are competitive, with **high ATAR scores** required for domestic students. The university provides **scholarships and financial aid**, including the **Scientia Scholarships** and **Co-op program**.
