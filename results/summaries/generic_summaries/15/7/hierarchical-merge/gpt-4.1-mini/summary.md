# University of British Columbia (UBC)  
Founded in the early 20th century, UBC is a leading Canadian research university with campuses in Vancouver and Kelowna, serving over 70,000 students including substantial Indigenous and international communities. It offers diverse programs, including selective international dual degrees, and sustains extensive global partnerships. UBC is notable for its strong emphasis on sustainability, pioneering green buildings like Brock Commons, and conservation initiatives. Its research enterprise, supported by a multibillion-dollar budget, includes major facilities such as TRIUMF and the Peter Wall Institute, with innovative projects in big data and renewable energy. The vibrant campus life features Canada’s largest Greek system and a strong athletic tradition. Alumni and faculty have achieved global influence in politics, sciences, and arts, including multiple Canadian Prime Ministers and Nobel laureates.  

# University of Toronto (U of T)  
Established in 1827, U of T is Canada’s largest research university with over 100,000 students across three campuses. It offers a wide array of academic programs through 14 professional faculties and is renowned for pioneering scientific breakthroughs like the discovery of insulin and stem cell research. Supported by a substantial budget, U of T fosters a vibrant student life with strong traditions in activism, arts, and athletics, centered around hubs such as Hart House. Its alumni profoundly impact the economy and culture, having founded companies responsible for roughly one-quarter of Canada’s GDP. Notable alumni include multiple Canadian Prime Ministers, Nobel laureates, and celebrated authors and scientists.  

# University of Oxford  
Dating back to 1096, Oxford is one of the world’s oldest and most prestigious universities, comprising 39 colleges responsible for undergraduate admissions and a wide range of graduate-focused academic departments. Its distinctive tutorial system emphasizes intensive small-group learning across humanities, sciences, and medicine. Annually admitting around 16% of applicants, Oxford actively pursues diversity and offers prestigious scholarships like Rhodes and Clarendon. The historic and architecturally rich campus is spread throughout Oxford city and anchored by the extensive Bodleian Libraries and renowned museums such as the Ashmolean. Oxford boasts the largest UK university endowment and a strong publishing arm through Oxford University Press. Its traditions include formal academic dress, debating societies, and sporting culture. Oxford’s alumni network is globally distinguished, featuring over 70 Nobel laureates, numerous British prime ministers, monarchs, and leaders in law, politics, and the arts, with a profound cultural impact reflected in literature and media.
