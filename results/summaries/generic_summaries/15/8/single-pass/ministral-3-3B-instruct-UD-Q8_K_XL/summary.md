### **University of New South Wales (UNSW)**

#### **History**
UNSW traces its origins to the **Sydney Mechanics' School of Arts (1833)** and **Sydney Technical College (1878)**, established to meet the growing demand for technological education in New South Wales. The university was formally established in 1949 as the **New South Wales University of Technology** following World War II, driven by the need for specialized engineering and technology training. In 1958, it was renamed **University of New South Wales (UNSW)** to reflect its broader academic scope.

#### **Expansion and Recent Developments**
UNSW expanded significantly in the mid-20th century, establishing faculties in arts, medicine, and law. In the 1960s, it transitioned from a technology-focused institution to a generalist university. Key milestones include:
- **1960**: Establishment of faculties of arts and medicine.
- **1980s**: Introduction of fine arts and expansion of research facilities.
- **2000s–Present**: Increased internationalization, collaborations, and a shift to a trimester-based academic calendar.

#### **Campuses and Buildings**
- **Main Campus**: Located in Kensington, Sydney, spanning 38 hectares. Features two main areas: upper and lower campuses.
- **Art & Design Campus**: Paddington, Sydney.
- **Sydney CBD Campus**: Pilot project in 2025.
- **Additional Campuses**: Randwick, Coogee, Botany, Dee Why, Cowan, Manly Vale, Fowlers Gap, Port Macquarie, Wagga Wagga, Albury, Coffs Harbour, Griffith, and Bankstown Airport.

#### **Academics and Research**
UNSW is organized into six faculties: Arts, Design & Architecture; Business; Engineering; Law and Justice; Medicine & Health; Science; and UNSW Canberra. It is recognized for its research in areas such as cancer research (Lowy Cancer Research Centre), cybersecurity (UNSW Canberra Cyber), and sustainability. The university is ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Union**: Arc @ UNSW, providing services, clubs, and advocacy.
- **Student Media**: Publications like *Blitz* and *Gamamari*.
- **Student Accommodation**: Residential halls like Philip Baxter College and Basser College.
- **Exchange Programs**: Over 250 international partner institutions.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Scott Morrison (Prime Minister of Australia), Gladys Berejiklian (Premier of New South Wales), and many other prominent figures in politics, business, and academia.
- **Controversies**: Past incidents include allegations of copyright infringement, student deaths, and controversies related to international campuses.

### **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)**

#### **History**
Founded in 1910 by Justo Sierra, UNAM was established to replace the colonial-era University of Mexico, which had been closed in 1865. It was designed to be a secular, national institution promoting positivism and Latin American unity. Key historical events include:
- **1920**: Creation of UNAM’s coat of arms and motto.
- **1943**: Construction of Ciudad Universitaria (University City).
- **1968**: Student protests during the Mexico City Olympics, leading to the Tlatelolco Massacre.
- **2009**: Awarded the Prince of Asturias Award for Communication and Humanities.

#### **Campuses and Facilities**
- **Ciudad Universitaria**: Main campus in Coyoacán, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Satellite Campuses**: Across Mexico, including in Santiago de Querétaro, Morelia, Mérida, and Ensenada.
- **Museums and Buildings**: Notable sites include Palacio de Minería, Casa del Lago, and the National Biodiversity Pavilion.

#### **Academics and Research**
UNAM is organized into schools and colleges, offering undergraduate and graduate studies. It is renowned for its research in artificial intelligence, robotics, and multidisciplinary studies. UNAM is consistently ranked among the top universities in Latin America and globally.

#### **Notable People**
- **Nobel Laureates**: Alfonso García Robles, Octavio Paz, Mario Molina.
- **Notable Alumni**: Former presidents of Mexico and Costa Rica, politicians, scientists, and artists.

### **University of Hong Kong (HKU)**

#### **History**
Founded in 1911, HKU began as the Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese, later becoming the University of Hong Kong. Key milestones include:
- **1927**: Introduction of Chinese studies.
- **1941**: Closure due to WWII; medical school relocated to Chengdu.
- **1982**: Establishment of the Faculty of Dentistry.
- **2006**: Renaming of the Faculty of Medicine to the Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine.
- **2011**: Opening of the Centennial Campus and University of Hong Kong–Shenzhen Hospital.

#### **Campuses and Buildings**
- **Main Campus**: Pok Fu Lam Road and Bonham Road, with the Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine located nearby.
- **Medical Campus**: Nearby Queen Mary Hospital.
- **Dentistry Campus**: Prince Philip Dental Hospital.
- **Centennial Campus**: Newly built for additional student space.

#### **Academics and Research**
HKU is a member of Universitas 21 and offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs. It is known for its research in medicine, engineering, and social sciences. The university is ranked among the top universities in Asia and globally.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Welfare**: Services provided by the Centre of Development and Resources for Students (CEDARS) and University Health Service.
- **Student Organizations**: Recognized student bodies, including the Postgraduate Student Association (PGSA) and HKU Business School Postgraduate Student Association (HKUBSPA).
- **Campus Facilities**: Residential halls, libraries, and various student services.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Sun Yat-sen, Guan Yi (researcher on SARS), and many prominent figures in politics, business, and academia.

### **University of Melbourne**

#### **History**
Founded in 1853, the University of Melbourne was established to provide higher education in Victoria, Australia. Key historical events include:
- **1855**: First classes held, with 16 students.
- **1881**: Admission of women.
- **1900s–1970s**: Expansion and introduction of new faculties and programs.
- **1980s–2000s**: Major restructuring, including the Melbourne Model for undergraduate degrees.
- **2000s–Present**: Continued expansion, focus on research, and internationalization.

#### **Campuses and Buildings**
- **Parkville Campus**: Main campus in Melbourne, featuring historic buildings like the Old Quad and Baldwin Spencer buildings.
- **Southbank Campus**: Home to the Victorian College of the Arts and Melbourne Conservatorium of Music.
- **Burnley Campus**: Dedicated to horticultural courses.
- **Creswick and Dookie Campuses**: For forestry and agriculture.
- **Shepparton Campus**: For rural health and medical education.
- **Werribee Campus**: For veterinary science.
- **Fishermans Bend**: New engineering campus under planning.

#### **Academics and Research**
The University of Melbourne is organized into nine faculties, including Architecture, Arts, Business and Economics, Education, Engineering and Information Technology, Fine Arts and Music, Law, Medicine, Dentistry and Health Sciences, and Science. It is recognized for its research in various fields, including medicine, engineering, and humanities.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Unions**: University of Melbourne Student Union (UMSU) and Graduate Student Association.
- **Sports and Athletics**: Over 200 clubs and sports teams, including the Melbourne University Football Club.
- **Residential Colleges**: Nine independent residential colleges, including Ormond College and Ridley College.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Julian Assange, Elizabeth Blackburn (Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine), and many other prominent figures in politics, science, and arts.

### **National University of Singapore (NUS)**

#### **History**
Founded in 1962 as the University of Singapore, NUS was formed by merging the University of Singapore and Nanyang University. Key milestones include:
- **1980**: Renamed National University of Singapore.
- **1988**: Establishment of the Centre for Management of Innovation and Technopreneurship.
- **2001**: Launch of the NUS Entrepreneurship Centre.
- **2010s–Present**: Continued expansion, internationalization, and focus on research and innovation.

#### **Campuses and Facilities**
- **Kent Ridge Campus**: Main campus, home to various faculties and research centers.
- **Bukit Timah Campus**: Home to the Faculty of Law and Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy.
- **Outram Campus**: Home to the Duke-NUS Medical School.
- **University Town**: Features residential colleges, lecture theaters, and research institutes.

#### **Academics and Research**
NUS is organized into 17 faculties and schools, including Business, Computing, Dentistry, Design and Engineering, Humanities and Sciences, Medicine, and Public Health. It is renowned for its research in biomedical science, engineering, and social sciences. NUS is consistently ranked among the top universities globally.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Accommodation**: Halls of residence, student residences, and residential colleges.
- **Student Organizations**: Various clubs and societies, including the NUS High School of Mathematics and Science.
- **Research Initiatives**: Major research focuses on biomedical science, physical science, and engineering.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Lee Kuan Yew, Goh Chok Tong, Margaret Chan, and many prominent figures in politics, business, and academia.

#### **Notable People**

### **Cornell University**

#### **History**
Founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, Cornell University was established as New York's land-grant institution. Key milestones include:
- **1868**: Formal inauguration of the university.
- **1883**: First electrification of the campus.
- **1967**: Student protests during the Vietnam War.
- **2000s–Present**: Expansion of international programs and research initiatives.

#### **Campuses and Facilities**
- **Ithaca Campus**: Main campus featuring historic and modern buildings.
- **New York City Campuses**: Weill Cornell Medical College and Cornell Tech.
- **Research Facilities**: Shoals Marine Laboratory, various research institutes, and study abroad programs.

#### **Academics and Research**
Cornell is organized into 16 colleges and schools, including the College of Arts and Sciences, College of Engineering, and Weill Cornell Medicine. It is known for its research in science, technology, engineering, and medicine. Cornell is ranked among the top universities globally.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Organizations**: Over 1,000 registered student organizations.
- **Greek Life**: Active fraternity and sorority system.
- **Campus Traditions**: Slope Day, Dragon Day, and various cultural events.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Bill Nye, Christopher Reeve, and many other prominent figures in science, business, and arts.

### **University of Oxford**

#### **History**
Founded in the 12th century, Oxford University is one of the oldest universities in the world. Key milestones include:
- **1248**: Royal charter granted.
- **1333–1334**: Attempt to found a new university at Stamford, Lincolnshire.
- **1827**: Major review of university statutes.
- **1920**: Admission of women to full membership.

#### **Campuses and Buildings**
- **Main Sites**: Central governance buildings, colleges, and parks.
- **Science Area**: Home to many science departments.
- **Parks**: University Parks and various college-owned open spaces.

#### **Academics and Research**
Oxford is organized into 39 colleges and four permanent private halls. It is renowned for its research in various fields, including humanities, sciences, and medicine. Oxford is consistently ranked among the top universities globally.

#### **Student Life**
- **Traditions**: Academic dress, trashing, and various college-specific customs.
- **Clubs and Societies**: Oxford Union, student newspapers, and various cultural groups.
- **Student Union**: Oxford University Student Union (OUSU).

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Over 70 Nobel laureates, including three Fields Medallists, and many prominent figures in politics, science, and arts.

### **University of Toronto**

#### **History**
Founded in 1827 as King's College, the University of Toronto was secularized in 1849 and renamed the University of Toronto. Key milestones include:
- **1878**: Establishment of the School of Practical Science.
- **1964**: Opening of Scarborough College.
- **2000s–Present**: Expansion, focus on research, and internationalization.

#### **Campuses and Buildings**
- **St. George Campus**: Historic downtown campus.
- **Mississauga Campus**: Suburban campus.
- **Scarborough Campus**: East Toronto campus.
- **Other Locations**: UTIAS, Hart House Farm, and various research facilities.

#### **Academics and Research**
The University of Toronto is organized into several divisions, including faculties and schools such as Arts and Science, Engineering, Medicine, and Law. It is recognized for its research in various fields, including medicine, engineering, and social sciences.

#### **Student Life**
- **Colleges**: Seven constituent colleges and various federated colleges.
- **Student Unions**: University of Toronto Students' Union (UTSU), Mississauga Students' Union, and Scarborough Campus Students' Union.
- **Student Organizations**: Over 200 clubs and societies.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: William Lyon Mackenzie King, Lester B. Pearson, and many other prominent figures in politics, science, and arts.
