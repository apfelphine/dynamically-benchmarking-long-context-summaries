### Cornell University: Comprehensive Overview

**Founding, Governance, and Structure**
Established in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, Cornell is a large, decentralized "R1" research university governed by a 64-member Board of Trustees that elects the President and maintains a unique "life trustee" role within the Cornell family. As of July 2024, Michael Kotlikoff serves as interim President. The academic structure includes 15 colleges: four state-funded institutions offering discounted tuition to New York residents and nine privately endowed colleges with greater autonomy.

**Campuses, Global Reach, and Sustainability**
The university's footprint is anchored by its historic Ithaca campus, recognized for its natural surroundings and 2023 Kyoto Protocol commitment. Key extensions include Weill Cornell Medicine in NYC, Cornell Tech on Roosevelt Island, and facilities in Qatar, alongside extensive off-campus research sites and study abroad programs across six continents. Cornell is classified as a land-, space-, and sea-grant university with deep international collaborations. Historically notable for innovations like 1883 campus electrification, the university has committed to net carbon neutrality by 2035 through solar farms and geothermal systems.

**Academics, Research, and Admissions**
Cornell excels in architecture, design, business, international relations, and STEM, contributing significantly to fields like space exploration, automotive safety, and high-energy physics. It hosts extensive resources, including the 13th-largest academic library in the U.S. and Cornell University Press, the nation's first university publishing enterprise. Scholarly output is robust, featuring titles like *Physical Review*. Admissions are highly competitive (6.9% acceptance rate for Class of 2026), practice need-blind admissions for U.S. applicants, and prioritize scholarships over loans for lower-income students.

**Student Life, Finance, and Community**
Student life is vibrant, characterized by over 1,000 registered organizations, diverse housing across North, West, and Collegetown campuses, and a prominent Greek life system (9% participation). The university boasts 35 varsity teams competing primarily in the Ivy League without athletic scholarships, complemented by nationally ranked dining facilities and a renowned outdoor education program with traditions like Slope Day. Health services include counseling and specialized care, supported by state-certified Cornell University Police following past safety incidents.

**Financial Strength and Legacy**
Cornell holds a $10.7 billion endowment as of 2024. Its legacy is defined by over 250,000 alumni, including four female Nobel laureates, and a faculty comprising 62 Nobel laureates. In November 2025, the university agreed to pay $30 million and invest in agricultural research to settle investigations into campus antisemitism and DEI practices.
