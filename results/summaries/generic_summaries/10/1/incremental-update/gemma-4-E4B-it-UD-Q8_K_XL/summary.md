**Comparative Education Systems: Global Overview**

**System Specific Highlights:**

*   **Republic of Ireland:** The compulsory education system runs from ages six to sixteen, with primary education beginning around four or five. The system is diversifying from its historical Catholic Church influence. Primary education (eight years) includes various types, with Catholic schools being dominant (88.3% in 2021). Secondary education, which is state-funded since 1967, progresses through the Junior Cycle, Transition Year (TY), and Senior Cycle, culminating in the Leaving Certificate (ages 16–19). Schools are categorized as Voluntary, Community, or Comprehensive. Post-primary schooling features Catholic, multi-denominational, and inter-denominational options. Academically, the system shows high performance relative to international averages, with high rankings in third-level education. Initiatives like the DEIS program address disadvantage by providing extra resources, including smaller classes and financial aid for low-income families, and the system supports special needs students via established legal frameworks and SNAs. The school calendar consists of three terms.
*   **Israel:** Features a segmented system (state-secular, state-religious, Haredi, Arab) linked to the high-tech economy via *Bagrut* exams, but faces challenges like class size and resource disparities.
*   **Nigeria:** Operates under the UBE framework but struggles with poor infrastructure, staff shortages, insecurity, a low 2022 literacy rate (69%), and gender bias.
*   **Sri Lanka:** Utilizes a multi-system structure incorporating public, Catholic, Muslim/madrasas, and secular institutions.
*   **Taiwan:** Maintains a comprehensive 12-year compulsory public education system focusing on core subjects, leading to high university attendance (>95%).
*   **Egypt:** Has a centralized, French-influenced model but suffers from quality issues, high student-teacher ratios, and reliance on private tutoring.
*   **Iran:** Is shaped by post-1979 Islamization, mandates Arabic, and enforces gender segregation, while reporting strong female university participation alongside reforms.
*   **France:** Implements a centralized, secular system (*Laïcité*), guiding students through *collège* to *lycée* toward the *baccalauréat* or vocational training, integrating the Bologna process.
*   **England and UK:** Operates a state-funded system spanning 3–18 years, adhering to the National Curriculum and monitored by Ofsted, with compulsory schooling ending at age 16.
*   **Sweden:** Provides continuous education from preschool to doctorate; foundational schooling (*Grundskola*) includes Individual Development Plans (IUPs), and higher education is tuition-free for EU/EEA citizens.
*   **Norway:** Uses a phased compulsory structure (6–19 years) that integrates apprenticeships through a "2+2 model."
*   **Armenia:** Achieved universal literacy by 1960 through free, compulsory K-12 (97% state-funded) while integrating the Bologna Process.
*   **Turkey:** Implements a unified, secular "4+4+4" structure, allowing 11th graders to choose academic, vocational/technical, or IB tracks.
*   **Indonesia:** Operates a twelve-year system influenced by Islamic traditions (*Pesantren* and *Madrassa*) under the KKNI framework, challenged by decentralization and rote learning.
*   **Hong Kong:** Features a modern, bilingual/bicultural, multi-tiered system integrating AI, where university access depends on exams (HKDSE or IBDP) under national security guidelines.
*   **Ghana:** Runs an English-medium system aiming for 12-year universal access via WASSCE but faces equity challenges in tertiary education and TVET.

**General Trends:**
Globally, educational systems are undergoing modernization in curricula and infrastructure while continuously grappling with persistent challenges related to equity, demographic shifts, and the impact of diverse ideological frameworks.
