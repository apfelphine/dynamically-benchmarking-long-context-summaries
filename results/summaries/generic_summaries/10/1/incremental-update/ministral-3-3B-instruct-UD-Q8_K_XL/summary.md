### **Updated Summary of Hong Kong’s, Ghana’s, Sweden’s, England’s, Norway’s, France’s, Armenia’s, Turkey’s, Indonesia’s, Iran’s, Egypt’s, Taiwan’s, Sri Lanka’s, Nigeria’s, Israel’s, Ireland’s, and Ghana’s Education Systems**

#### **Historical Context & Evolution**
Hong Kong’s system evolved from **missionary schools** into a **bilingual (Chinese/English) curriculum**, with **2019 protests** prompting **patriotism reforms**—textbooks revised, teachers mandated to take **allegiance oaths**, and **AI integration** prioritized. The **2021 transition to a 3-year secondary system** reshaped tertiary pathways, while **exam pressure** and **AI adoption** remain central.

Taiwan’s system reflects **colonial influences** (Dutch, Ming, Qing, Japanese) and **post-1949 assimilation policies**, blending **public schools for national unity** with **private revival**. Strict **discipline (uniforms, exams)** and **mandatory military education** persist, though **12-year compulsory education (2014)** expanded access. **Engineering diplomas (2025)** modernize vocational pathways, and **STEM focus** dominates, with **IB programs** and **digital literacy** globalizing curricula.

Ghana’s system emphasizes **free primary and secondary education** for all, with **UBE (Universal Basic Education)** ensuring **compulsory free primary education** (6 years) and **free secondary education for poor students** (3 years). **Denominational schools** (Catholic, Islamic) coexist with **public/private institutions**, though **teacher shortages** and **rural/urban disparities** persist. **NGO programs** (e.g., **Ghana Education Trust**) bridge gaps, while **digital literacy initiatives** aim for **95% coverage by 2030**.

#### **Structural & Reforms**
**Taiwan:**
- **12-year compulsory education** (Elementary: Grades 1–6; Junior High: 7–9; Senior High: 10–12).
  - **Senior High Tracks**: Regular (liberal arts/science) or **Skill-Based** (vocational training).
  - **Higher Education**: **95%+ university entry**; **universities** dominate STEM, while **vocational schools** offer **associate/doctoral degrees**.
  - **Post-Secondary**: **Bagrut exams** determine eligibility; **Mechina (IDF deferral)** or **tuition subsidies** support access.

**Sri Lanka:**
- **Primary (Grades 1–5)**, **Junior Secondary (6–9)**, **Senior Secondary (10–11)**, and **GCE O/L/A/L exams**.
  - **School Types**: Government (national/provincial/pirivenas), private (66 pre-1960, 33 fee-free), madrasas.
  - **Tertiary**: **State universities** (free but competitive) and **vocational education (NVQSL)** via **Open University/technical colleges**.

**Nigeria:**
- **Primary (6 years, UBE Act)**: Free and compulsory; **automatic progression**.
- **Secondary (9 years)**: **WAEC/NECO SSCE exams** determine progression; **C4-C6 grades** required for university.
  - **Types**: Federal (merit-based), state (free but underfunded), private (expensive).
- **Alternative Pathways**: **IJMB/JUPEB** (9-month/8-month programs for DE entry), **informal education** (e.g., Premier Reading Clubs).

**Israel:**
- **Compulsory education (ages 5–18)**, with **12 tiers**: Primary (6–12), Middle (12–15), High (15–18).
  - **Tracks**: State-secular, state-religious, Haredi, Arab, and private (e.g., international curricula).
  - **Integration**: **5 integrated schools** (e.g., Neve Shalom) for Jewish/Arab coexistence.
  - **Higher Education**: **46% of 25–64-year-olds** hold degrees; **Bagrut exams** (core + units) determine eligibility. **2011–2022 Plan** supports Arab/Druze students via scholarships, guidance, and institutional inclusion. **Gender disparities** (higher female matriculation rates but underrepresentation in STEM) and **teacher shortages** persist.

**Ireland:**
- **Compulsory education ages 5–16** (or 3 years of second-level).
  - **Primary**: **88.3% Catholic**, **5.7% Church of Ireland**, **4.9% multi-denominational**; **99.5% mixed-sex**, **0.3% single-sex girls**, **0.4% single-sex boys**.
  - **Secondary**:
    - **Junior Cycle (12–15)**: Junior Certificate (NFQ Level 4).
    - **Transition Year (optional, 15–16)**: Enrichment-focused.
    - **Senior Cycle (16–19)**: Leaving Certificate (NFQ Levels 5/6), with **core subjects (English, Irish, Maths)** + **3–4 optional subjects**.
    - **School Types**: **Voluntary (57%)**, **Community Colleges (28%)**, **Comprehensive (15%)**, **Grind Schools (fee-paying private)**, **Gaelcholáistí (Irish-medium, 3%)**.
  - **Performance**: **Pisa 2022**: 2nd in reading, 11th in maths (age 15).
  - **Special Needs**: **Act 2004** ensures **disability support**; **NCSE/SNAs** assist students with physical/intellectual disabilities.
  - **Disadvantage Support**: **DEIS Program** reduces class sizes in **1,203 schools** (2022), with **€200M EIB funding** for infrastructure upgrades.

**Sweden:**
- **9-year compulsory school (grundskola)**, followed by **3-year upper secondary (Gymnasieskola)**.
  - **Preparatory (67%)** → university; **Vocational (33%)** → industry training.
  - **Non-qualifiers (15.6%)** access **Introductory Programs**.

**Norway:**
- **Elementary (6–12 years)**: No grades; **Lower Secondary (13–16 years)**: Graded electives.
  - **Upper Secondary (16–19 years)**: **93% public**; **Reform 94’s unified programs** and **Kunnskapsløftet’s IT focus**.

**England:**
- **State-funded schools (93%)**: **Key Stages (KS1–KS5)**; **GCSEs at 16**, **A-levels/BTECs/T Levels at 18**.
  - **Grammar schools** (academic), **apprenticeships** (vocational).

**France:**
- **Mandatory (6–18 years)**: **Primary (École élémentaire)**, **Middle School (collège)**, **High School (lycée)** → **baccalauréat**.
  - **Vocational Paths**: *CAP* via *CFA* centers.

**Armenia:**
- **12-year compulsory education**; **Taiwan-inspired engineering diplomas** modernize vocational pathways.

**Turkey:**
- **12-year compulsory education (4+4+4)**: **General/vocational high schools**; **Imam Hatip** (religious vocational schools).
  - **Secondary Tracks**: Academic/vocational; **YÖS exams** determine university admissions.

**Indonesia:**
- **12 years compulsory**: **Public schools (90%)**, **religious schools (15%)**.
  - **Structure**: Primary (SD), Junior High (SMP), High School (SMA/SMK).
  - **Vocational Paths**: **D3/D4** (technical/health/tourism).

**Iran:**
- **12-year compulsory**: **Gender-segregated schools**; **vocational programs** expanded in the **1990s**.
  - **Higher Education**: **State-funded universities** (e.g., Islamic Azad) and **distance learning (Payame Noor)**.

**Egypt:**
- **Ancient**: **Bar Ankh/Alexandria’s university**; modern: **Taiwan-inspired engineering diplomas (2025)**.

#### **Equity & Access**
**Taiwan:**
- **Equity**: **Rural funding gaps** and **STEM focus** may exclude non-technical fields; **vocational schools** offer alternatives.
- **Access**: **Residency-based school assignments**; **private cram schools** bypass national exams.

**Sri Lanka:**
- **Government Schools**: **National/provincial/pirivenas** face **funding shortages**; **national schools** have better facilities.
- **Private Schools**: **104 institutions** (66 pre-1960, 33 fee-free); **international schools** cater to expats.
- **Madrasas**: **205 Islamic schools** + **749 Muslim schools** offer **separate curricula**.
- **Tertiary**: **State universities** are free but **highly competitive**; **gender disparities** in **STEM**.

**Nigeria:**
- **Primary**: **UBE Act (1999)** ensures **free, compulsory education**; **state schools underfunded**.
- **Secondary**: **Federal schools** (merit-based) dominate elite access; **private schools** cater to wealthier students.
- **Higher Education**: **Teacher qualifications vary**; **NGO programs** (e.g., Premier Reading Clubs) target **rural/urban gaps**.
- **Equity Gaps**:
  - **Rural/Urban**: **Informal education** and **NGO programs** (e.g., CELLE) address gaps.
  - **Gender**: **UNICEF’s GEP3-CTP** improved girls’ enrollment in **Niger/Sokoto States**, but **militancy** persists.

**Israel:**
- **Equity**: **5 integrated schools** (e.g., Neve Shalom) for **Jewish/Arab coexistence**; **Israeli Pupils’ Rights Law (2000)** prohibits sectarian discrimination.
- **Access**: **Education as a tool for mobility**; **Arab sector funding gaps** (~$192/student vs. $1,100 for Jews), higher **dropout rates (12% vs. 6%)**, and **limited resources**.
- **Affirmative Action**: **25% budget allocation for Arabs**; **Christian Arab success rates** higher than Arab sector averages.

**Ireland:**
- **Equity**: **NFQ levels** standardize **tertiary admissions**; **Gaelcholáistí** ensure **Irish-medium access**.
- **Challenges**: **Dublin’s admission disparities**; **88.4% of schools** remain Catholic-run, with **non-Catholic reforms** ongoing.
- **Support**: **DEIS Program** reduces class sizes in **1,203 schools** (2022), with **€200M EIB funding** for infrastructure.

**Sweden:**
- **Upper Secondary**: **99% enrollment**; **preparatory programs (67%)** dominate university entry.

**Norway:**
- **Compulsory until age 16**; **Reform 94’s flexibility** reduces rigid pathways.

**England:**
- **State schools** (free at point of entry) vs. **private schools** (tuition-based); **grammar schools** (selective).

**France:**
- **Free and mandatory schooling** eliminates tuition fees, but **private schools** (especially *grandes écoles*) remain elite.

**Armenia:**
- **100% public schools** ensure **universal access**, though **tuition-based private schools** limit low-income families.

**Turkey:**
- **12-year compulsory education** ensures **universal access**, but **private school disparities** persist.

**Indonesia:**
- **Colonial-era disparities** and **Japanese occupation’s disruption** left lasting gaps; **post-independence**:
  - **Pondok Pesantren** and **Sekolah Dasar** expanded access, but **gender gaps** and **rural literacy gaps** persist.

**Iran:**
- **Free public schools** ensure **universal access**, but **gender-segregation** and **privatization pressures** complicate equity.

**Egypt:**
- **Free compulsory education** ensures **access**, but **wealth gaps** persist in **higher education**.

**Ghana:**
- **Compulsory education ages 6–16** (6–12 years).
  - **Primary**: Free; **Secondary**: Free for poor students.
  - **Equity**: **NGO programs** (e.g., **Ghana Education Trust**) target **rural/urban gaps**; **free secondary education** for poor students.
  - **Challenges**: **Out-of-school rates (~15%)**, **teacher shortages**, and **corruption**.
