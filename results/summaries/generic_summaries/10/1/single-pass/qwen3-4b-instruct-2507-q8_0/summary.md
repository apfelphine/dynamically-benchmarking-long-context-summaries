### **Education in Hong Kong**

**History & Early Development:**  
Education in Hong Kong began with British missionary efforts in the 1840s, leading to the establishment of early schools. The British government introduced a bilingual curriculum in the 1860s, combining Chinese and English studies. The Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese (1887) and Ying Wa Girls' School (1900) were pivotal in expanding medical and girls' education. The 1997 handover introduced a shift toward national curriculum and political sensitivity, including a push for patriotic education.

**Curriculum & Structure:**  
- **Primary & Secondary:** Compulsory from age 6 to 18, with free public education. The curriculum is divided into stages (e.g., Primary 1–6, Secondary 1–6).  
- **Medium of Instruction:** Shifted from English to Chinese (Cantonese) post-1990s. A minority of schools (EMI schools) maintain English as the medium.  
- **Secondary Education:** Divided into junior (broad curriculum) and senior (selective, with choices). Students take the HKDSE exams (ages 15–16).  

**Higher Education & Trends:**  
- **Universities:** HKU, CUHK, and HKUST are globally recognized.  
- **Recent Developments:**  
  - **Post-2020:** AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) are permitted in coursework.  
  - **National Security Law (2020):** Led to a decline in enrollment in prestigious schools, with over 1,400 students leaving.  
  - **Curriculum Shifts:** Emphasis on patriotism, with new subjects like Citizenship and Social Development (replacing Liberal Studies).  
  - **Textbooks:** Revisions removed references to Hong Kong as a former colony and downplayed foreign involvement in protests.  

**Challenges & Criticisms:**  
- **Overemphasis on Exams:** Students face high academic pressure, with excessive homework and rote learning.  
- **Class Size & Discipline:** Class sizes have decreased due to falling birth rates, but discipline remains strict.  
- **Cultural Issues:** Education is often described as "spoon-fed," with a focus on exam performance over critical thinking.  

**International & Special Education:**  
- **International Schools:** Offer English-medium curricula (e.g., IB, IGCSE).  
- **Special Education:** Integrated education is promoted, with special schools transitioning to mainstream settings.  
- **Immigrant Students:** Services exist for non-Cantonese-speaking children, including free induction programs.

### **Education in Ghana**

**History & Structure:**  
- Early education was informal, with oral transmission and apprenticeships.  
- Mission schools (e.g., Wesleyan, Basel) were established in the 19th century, providing book-based education to elites.  
- Post-independence (1957), education was made free and compulsory, with a focus on universal access.  

**Basic Education:**  
- 12-year compulsory system (ages 4–15), divided into kindergarten, primary (ages 6–11), and junior secondary (ages 12–15).  
- Curriculum includes core subjects (English, Math, Science), religious education, and vocational skills.  

**Secondary Education:**  
- Students can pursue academic or vocational tracks.  
- Final exams: WASSCE (West African Senior School Certificate Examination).  

**Tertiary Education:**  
- 6 public universities and 49 private institutions.  
- Tertiary education has grown significantly, with 261,962 students in 2011/12.  

**Challenges & Reforms:**  
- **Vocational Education:** Historically underdeveloped and seen as informal.  
- **Gender & Equity:** Women and rural students face underrepresentation.  
- **Private Education:** Private schools outnumber public ones, with high fees and limited access.  
- **Adult Education:** Offers vocational training and evening courses.  

**Key Policies:**  
- **FCUBE (Free, Compulsory, Universal Basic Education):** Introduced in 1996, aimed at improving access and equity.  
- **Vocational Education:** Gained recognition, though low completion rates persist.

### **Education in Sweden**

**Structure & History:**  
- Compulsory education spans 9 years (ages 6–15), with a strong focus on equality and inclusion.  
- The system is based on a comprehensive school (grundskola), which is mandatory and free.  

**Primary & Secondary:**  
- **Subjects:** All students take 16 core subjects (e.g., Swedish, Math, English, History).  
- **Grading:** Uses a letter-based system (A–F), with a student’s total score based on subject grades.  
- **National Tests:** Administered in grades 3, 6, and 9.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Tertiary education is structured into three cycles (Bachelor, Master, PhD).  
- Universities offer degrees in both academic and professional fields.  

**Private & Independent Schools:**  
- "Friskolor" (independent schools) are publicly funded and offer a range of pedagogical approaches.  
- Criticized for potential inequities in grading and quality.  

**Reforms & Criticisms:**  
- **Inclusion:** Emphasis on integration, with special education schools transitioning to mainstream settings.  
- **Discipline:** School discipline is strict, with guidelines to promote positive behavior.  
- **Public Opinion:** Mixed views on friskolor, with concerns about quality and equity.

### **Education in England**

**Structure & History:**  
- Education is compulsory from age 5 to 18.  
- The system is divided into early years, primary, secondary, and post-16 education.  

**Primary & Secondary:**  
- **Curriculum:** National curriculum with core subjects (English, Math, Science).  
- **Key Stages:** KS1–KS4 (ages 5–16), leading to GCSEs.  
- **School Types:** Academies, community, foundation, and voluntary controlled schools.  

**Post-16 Education:**  
- Students can pursue A-levels, BTECs, or T-levels.  
- Apprenticeships and traineeships are available for those seeking vocational training.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Tertiary education is largely state-funded, with tuition fees introduced in 1998.  
- The Russell Group includes top universities.  

**Challenges & Reforms:**  
- **Funding:** Increased funding since 2018, with a focus on teacher salaries and school infrastructure.  
- **Equity:** Efforts to reduce the gap between rich and poor students.  
- **Private Schools:** Offer alternative curricula and are often seen as elite.

### **Education in Norway**

**Structure & History:**  
- Education is divided into elementary (barneskole), lower secondary (ungdomsskole), and upper secondary (videregående skole).  
- Mandatory until age 16, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity.  

**Primary & Secondary:**  
- Students take exams at age 16 (final exams).  
- Electives and career-focused tracks are available.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Universities and university colleges offer degrees in both academic and vocational fields.  
- Students must pass general university admission exams.  

**Special Education:**  
- Students with special needs are educated in mainstream schools, with support from educational psychologists.  

**Grading & Examinations:**  
- Grading is based on a 1–6 scale, with 6 being the highest.  
- Exams are administered at the end of secondary education.

### **Education in France**

**Structure & History:**  
- Education began with the French Revolution, with Jules Ferry introducing secular, mandatory schooling.  
- The modern system emphasizes a national curriculum and secularism.  

**Primary & Secondary:**  
- Compulsory from age 6 to 16.  
- Curriculum includes French, history, geography, math, and foreign languages.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Divided into "grandes écoles" (elite institutions) and public universities.  
- "Grandes écoles" are highly selective and prestigious.  

**Private & International Schools:**  
- Private schools follow the national curriculum or offer alternative curricula.  
- International schools offer curricula in English or other languages.  

**Challenges & Criticisms:**  
- **Discrimination:** Religious instruction is limited, with some schools offering religious education.  
- **Quality:** Concerns about quality and equity in education.
