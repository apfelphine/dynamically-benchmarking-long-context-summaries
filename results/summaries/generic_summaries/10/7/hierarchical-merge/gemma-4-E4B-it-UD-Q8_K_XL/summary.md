# Global Education System Comparison

Global education systems vary widely in structure, governance, and delivery. Models range from France's secular, mandatory education and England's National Curriculum, to Taiwan's comprehensive coverage. Challenges are pervasive, including resource disparities (Egypt, Israel), infrastructure deficits (Nigeria), and socioeconomic barriers affecting access across regions.

## National Models and System Challenges

Specific national models present unique features and obstacles. Some systems integrate religious values (Indonesia, Turkey), while others focus on rigorous performance and technology integration (Hong Kong). Conversely, nations like Ghana face difficulties integrating required ICT despite limited resources.

## Higher Education and Regional Trends

In higher education, Nordic countries like Sweden and Norway offer structured K-12 pathways leading to internationally aligned tertiary education, with Sweden providing tuition-free access. Meanwhile, other nations are modernizing their tertiary sectors, such as Armenia, though it struggles with student persistence and quality issues despite pursuing global standards.
