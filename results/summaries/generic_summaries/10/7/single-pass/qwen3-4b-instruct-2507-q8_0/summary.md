### **Education in Taiwan**
- **History**: Influenced by Dutch, Ming, Qing, and Japanese rule; early academies existed. Post-1949, the Nationalist government suppressed private schools, later revitalizing them under state control.
- **Public Education**: 12-year compulsory education (replaces 9-year system). Students take national exams for high school and university entry. Discipline is strict, with uniforms and daily cleaning; corporal punishment is banned but reportedly still practiced.
- **School Structure**: Grades 1–6 (elementary), 7–9 (junior high), 10–12 (senior high). Subjects include Mandarin, math, science, English, and social studies.
- **Higher Education**: University entrance via national exams; 95% of high school graduates enter university. Engineering is popular. Universities include NTU, NTHU, and others. Private institutions offer degrees and support from religious or commercial bodies.
- **Private Education**: Cram schools are widespread; English immersion preschools are growing. The "3+3" system allows skipping the CAP test for senior high school entry.

### **Education in Nigeria**
- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 5; students take a Common Entrance Examination for secondary school. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) system (6 years primary + 3 years junior secondary) ensures free, compulsory education.
- **Secondary Education**: 6 years (JSS + SSS) with a focus on preparing for the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE). Federal schools are elite; state schools face budget issues. Private schools are expensive and have smaller class sizes.
- **Tertiary Education**: Government controls most institutions. 153 universities exist (40 federal, 45 state, 68 private). Private universities are expanding. The government promotes vocational education.
- **Vocational Education**: Offered at junior colleges, institutes, and universities. 87 institutions exist; apprenticeships are encouraged.
- **Female Education**: Women's participation is rising, though challenges remain (e.g., teen pregnancy, religious beliefs). The Girls' Education Project supports enrollment and retention.
- **Challenges**: Rural schools suffer from poor infrastructure, teacher shortages, and insecurity. Literacy remains low (62.02% in 2018), with regional disparities.

### **Education in Iran**
- **History**: Ancient Persian schools; Safavid era introduced scientific and religious education. Post-1979 Islamic Revolution emphasized Islamic values; textbooks were Islamized.
- **Structure**: 12-year education (pre-primary to secondary). Students take exams to enter university; the "Konkur" exam is key.
- **Curriculum**: Compulsory subjects include science, math, and foreign languages. Students are segregated by gender in schools.
- **Higher Education**: Top universities include Tehran University, Sharif University, and Amirkabir. Engineering is popular. The "4+4+4" system (4 years primary, 4 years middle, 4 years secondary) is in place.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages (60,000 needed), rising private school costs, and textbook bias toward Islamic values. Mandatory Islamic prayer and "jihad" content in curricula are controversial.

### **Education in the Republic of Ireland**
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 4 to 16. Junior Cycle (3 years), Transition Year (optional), Senior Cycle (2 years). Students take the Leaving Certificate.
- **School Types**: National, Gaelscoileanna (Irish-medium), multi-denominational, and independent schools. Religious education is mandatory in some schools.
- **Secondary Education**: Focus on breadth over depth; students take exams in English, history, math, and science.
- **Higher Education**: Strong emphasis on higher education; 90% of school-leavers attend university. The University of Dublin, Trinity College, and University College Dublin are top institutions.
- **Challenges**: Religious bias in curricula; gender disparities in STEM fields; teacher shortages and low wages.

### **Education in Turkey**
- **History**: Reformed under Atatürk; secular education introduced. The 1924 Law of Integration of Education unified schools. Religious education was phased out.
- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education (primary + secondary). Students take national exams for university entry.
- **Secondary Education**: Three-year high school with four tracks (Turkish secular, religious, Haredi, Arab). Students take exams to enter university.
- **Higher Education**: Public universities offer free tuition. Private universities are expensive. Engineering and computer science are popular.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor quality of textbooks, and religious indoctrination in curricula. The government has faced criticism for promoting Islamic values.

### **Education in Israel**
- **Structure**: Compulsory from kindergarten to 12th grade. Three tiers: primary (1–6), middle (7–9), and high school (10–12).
- **School Tracks**: State-secular, state-religious, Haredi (religious), and Arab schools. Haredi schools lack mainstream education and have low workforce participation.
- **Higher Education**: Students take the Bagrut exam. Admission to universities is competitive. The military conscription system requires service after university.
- **Challenges**: Haredi students face low workforce participation; Arab students face underfunding and disparities. The government has invested in Haredi education.

### **Education in France**
- **History**: Jules Ferry established secular, free public education in the 1880s. The system is centralized under the Ministry of National Education.
- **Structure**: 6 years primary, 4 years middle, 3 years high school. Students take the baccalauréat exam.
- **Private Schools**: "Under contract" schools follow the national curriculum; private schools offer religious instruction.
- **Higher Education**: Public universities offer free tuition. The Bologna Process standardizes degrees. Grandes écoles are elite institutions focused on specific fields.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, low wages, and a rigid curriculum. The government has faced criticism for promoting a "common culture" and limiting student diversity.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**
- **History**: Education based on Buddhist temples and colonial missionary schools. Post-independence, a national education system was established.
- **Structure**: 12 years of basic education (ages 4–14); 3 years of secondary education (ages 15–18). Students take the GCE O/L and A/L exams.
- **Higher Education**: State universities are free but highly competitive. Only 16% of qualified students gain admission. Private universities offer degrees.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, high dropout rates, and gender disparities. The government has introduced compulsory military training for undergraduates.

### **Education in England**
- **History**: Education was initially tied to religious institutions. The 1870 Education Act made primary education free and compulsory.
- **Structure**: 5 years of early years (ages 3–5), 6 years of primary (ages 5–11), 4 years of secondary (ages 11–16). Students take GCSEs and A-levels.
- **School Types**: Academies, community, free, and voluntary schools. Private schools are selective and often expensive.
- **Higher Education**: Free tuition for UK students; international students pay higher fees. The university system is highly ranked globally.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, high student-to-teacher ratios, and pressure from private tutoring.

### **Education in Egypt**
- **History**: Ancient schools (Bar Ankh) and Islamic institutions (al-Azhar, Dar al-Ilm). Post-colonial reforms introduced free education.
- **Structure**: 12 years of basic education (ages 4–14), followed by 3 years of secondary education. Students take national exams.
- **Higher Education**: Public and private institutions offer degrees. The government has introduced reforms to improve quality.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low quality of education. Private tutoring is widespread and costly.

### **Education in Ghana**
- **History**: Early village schools; colonial-era mission schools. Post-independence, free and compulsory primary education was introduced.
- **Structure**: 12 years of basic education (ages 4–15), 3 years of secondary (ages 15–18). Students take national exams.
- **Higher Education**: Public and private universities offer degrees. Admission is competitive.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, gender disparities, and low enrollment in vocational education. Private schools are growing.

### **Education in Indonesia**
- **History**: Dutch colonial education system emphasized European superiority. Post-independence, education became more inclusive.
- **Structure**: 12 years of compulsory education. Students take national exams.
- **Islamic Schools**: Pesantren, madrasah, and sekolah islam offer religious education. Students can enter and leave at any time.
- **Higher Education**: Public and private institutions offer degrees. The National Qualification Framework (KKNI) standardizes qualifications.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low access to quality education in rural areas.

### **Education in Hong Kong**
- **History**: Missionary schools introduced in the 1800s. Post-1997, the education system shifted to emphasize patriotism and national security.
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 6 to 18. Students take the HKDSE exam. EMI (English-medium instruction) schools are prestigious.
- **Higher Education**: Eight UGC-funded universities (e.g., HKU, CUHK, HKUST). Private international schools offer alternative curricula.
- **Challenges**: Declining enrollment in prestigious schools due to national security law. Curriculum changes have led to criticism over political indoctrination.

### **Education in Armenia**
- **Literacy**: 100% literacy rate since 1960. Education is free and compulsory up to age 18.
- **Structure**: 12 years of education (primary, middle, high school). Students take national exams for university entry.
- **Higher Education**: 26 state universities and 33 private institutions. Strong in STEM and medical fields.
- **Challenges**: Student absenteeism and dropout rates are rising. Limited access to higher education for rural students.

### **Education in Sweden**
- **Structure**: 9 years of compulsory primary school (ages 6–15), 3 years of upper secondary (ages 16–19). Students take national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9.
- **Grading**: A–F scale; students receive a total score based on subject grades.
- **Higher Education**: Free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens. Degrees follow the Bologna Process.
- **Challenges**: High levels of student absenteeism and violence in schools. Private schools (friskolor) have faced criticism for profit-making and grade inflation.

### **Education in Norway**
- **Structure**: 9 years of compulsory education (ages 6–16), 3 years of upper secondary (ages 16–19). Students take national exams.
- **Grading**: 1–6 scale; 6 is best, 2 is passing.
- **Higher Education**: State-run universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Private schools are rare.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, especially in rural areas. Special education is integrated into mainstream schools. High dropout rates in poorer regions.
