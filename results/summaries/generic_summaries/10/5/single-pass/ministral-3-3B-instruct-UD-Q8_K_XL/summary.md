### **Education in Turkey**

#### **Historical Overview**
Turkey’s education system evolved significantly since the establishment of the Turkish Republic in 1923. The Ministry of Education underwent multiple reorganizations, including its name changes (e.g., Ministry of National Education). The **Law of Integration of Education (1924)** unified three separate educational channels (madrasas, reform schools, and minority schools) into a single system, emphasizing secularism. The **Law for Education Organization (1926)** standardized curricula and vocational-technical education under the Ministry’s control.

Key milestones include:
- **1928**: Adoption of a Latin-based alphabet.
- **1930s**: Establishment of the Turkish Association of History and Language to promote Turkish identity.
- **1938**: Introduction of mobile courses for women in rural areas.
- **1997**: Compulsory education extended to eight years.
- **2012**: Implementation of the **4+4+4** system (4 years primary, 4 years junior high, 4 years senior high).
- **2017**: New curriculum removed evolution theory and included Islamic concepts like *jihad*.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Pre-primary Education**
- Optional for children aged 36–72 months (under compulsory primary education).
- Institutions include nurseries, kindergartens, and practical classes.

**Primary Education (İlköğretim)**
- **8 years** (compulsory, free in public schools).
- Core subjects: Turkish, mathematics, life knowledge (*Hayat Bilgisi*), and foreign language (English most common).
- Electives: Science, social studies, and religious education.
- **Key Features**: Standardized curriculum, free in public schools, and mandatory attendance.

**Secondary Education (Lise)**
- **3 years** (post-primary education).
- **General Secondary Education**: High schools (e.g., Anatolian High Schools) for academic preparation.
- **Vocational-Technical Secondary Education**: Schools for technical and vocational training (e.g., trade schools, health education schools).
- **Tracks**: Students choose between Turkish-Mathematics, science, social sciences, or foreign languages.
- **Exams**: High school diploma required for university entrance.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: 76 public and 23 private universities (e.g., University of Istanbul, Middle East Technical University).
- **Admission**: National university entrance exam (*YKS*).
- **Tuition**: Low for public universities; private universities charge higher fees.
- **International Programs**: Erasmus+ and Turkish Scholarships (e.g., *Türkiye Scholarships*) for foreign students.

**Vocational Education**
- Offered at junior colleges, institutes of technology, and universities.
- **National Vocational Education Law (3308)**: Apprenticeship training with government insurance coverage.
- **Apprenticeship**: 2–4 years, depending on the profession.

**International Education**
- **International Baccalaureate (IB)**: Available in 53 schools.
- **Private Schools**: Categorized into Turkish, minority, foreign, and international schools.

**Religious Education**
- **De-establishment (1927)**: Religion excluded from public school curricula.
- **Re-establishment (1956)**: Religion reintroduced in secondary schools; parents can opt out.
- **Current Practice**: Mandatory in 4th–8th grades, with content on Islam, secularism, and ethics.

**Foreign Languages**
- **English**: Mandatory from 2nd grade; additional foreign language (e.g., German, French) introduced in high school.
- **Kurdish**: Added as an optional subject in 2012.
- **Arabic**: Introduced as an elective in 2015.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Frequent curriculum changes, textbook quality, and bias in curricula (e.g., Armenian genocide denial).
- **Reforms**: 2012’s **4+4+4** system, 2017’s new curriculum, and efforts to improve vocational education.

### **Education in Iran**

#### **Historical Overview**
Iran’s education system traces back to ancient Persia, with the **Academy of Gondishapur** (3rd century CE) as an early higher education institution. The **Safavid era** (16th–18th centuries) marked the birth of modern education, blending Islamic values with scientific advancements. Post-revolution reforms (1979) emphasized Islamic values, leading to segregated schools and mandatory religious instruction.

Key milestones include:
- **1969–1979**: Literacy Corps program to improve literacy rates.
- **1979**: Islamization of textbooks and segregation by gender.
- **1990s**: Shift to credit-based systems and vocational programs.
- **2012**: New education reform plan to integrate research and family involvement.
- **2023**: Mandatory Islamic prayer time for schoolchildren.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Pre-primary Education**
- **Kindergarten**: Optional, 1 year (ages 5–6).
- **Enrollment**: 50% of children at this age.

**Primary Education (Dabestan)**
- **6 years** (grades 1–6), compulsory.
- **Enrollment**: Free and compulsory; full enrollment achieved in primary schools.

**Secondary Education**
- **Lower-secondary (Motovasseteh Avval)**: 3 years (grades 7–9), mandatory.
- **Upper-secondary (Motovasseteh Dovom)**: 3 years (grades 10–12), optional.
  - **Theoretical Branch**: Academic studies (e.g., literature, sciences).
  - **Technical-Vocational/Professional Branch**: Practical training (e.g., agriculture, trades).
  - **Manual Skills Branch**: Hands-on skills (e.g., carpentry, cooking).
- **Exams**: *Konkur* (national entrance exam) for university admission.

**Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: 16 public universities (e.g., Tehran University, Sharif University).
- **Admission**: *Konkour* exam; some universities offer scholarships.
- **Tuition**: Free for public universities; private universities charge higher fees.
- **International Students**: 795,962 foreign students in 2016; government offers scholarships.

**Vocational Education**
- **Technical-Vocational Schools**: Offer associate degrees (e.g., *Fogh-e-Diplom*).
- **Apprenticeships**: 2–4 years, with government insurance coverage.

**Adult Education**
- **Literacy Corps**: 200,000 participants taught reading/writing (1969–1979).
- **Non-formal Education**: Life-skills training, occupational skills, and adult literacy programs.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Low literacy rates (31% illiteracy in 2022), gender disparities, and political influence in education.
- **Reforms**: 2012 education reform plan, focus on digital literacy, and efforts to reduce out-of-school children.

### **Education in France**

#### **Historical Overview**
France’s education system was shaped by **Napoleon’s reforms** (1800s) and **Jules Ferry’s laws (1881–1882)**, which mandated free, secular, and compulsory education for all children aged 6–12. The **Third Republic** expanded state-funded schools, and the **Bologna Process** (2000s) aligned higher education with European standards.

Key milestones include:
- **1881**: *Loi Ferry* established free, secular primary education.
- **1968**: Student revolts led to reforms in higher education.
- **2000**: *Laboulaye Law* on higher education freedom.
- **2021**: Mandatory schooling until age 16.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Pre-school (Maternelle)**
- **Ages 3–6**: Introduces community living and early literacy/math skills.
- **Enrollment**: 50% of children at this age.

**Primary School (École Élémentaire)**
- **Ages 6–11**: 5 years (grades CP–CM2).
- **Core Subjects**: French, mathematics, science, arts.
- **Exams**: None at this level.

**Secondary School (Collège/Lycé)**
- **Middle School (Collège)**: Ages 11–15 (grades 6–9).
  - **Core Subjects**: French, mathematics, history, geography, foreign languages, PE.
- **High School (Lycée)**: Ages 15–18 (grades 10–12).
  - **Baccalauréat (Bac)**: National exam for university admission.
  - **Tracks**: General (*baccalauréat général*), technological (*baccalauréat technologique*), or professional (*baccalauréat professionnel*).

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: Public and private; tuition fees range from €170–€380/year for EU students.
- **Grades**: Licence (3 years), Master (2 years), Doctorat (3–5 years).
- **Grades**: A–E (160 ECTS for Licence, 120 for Master).
- **International Schools**: 105 schools (ISC definition); regulated by AEFE.

**Private Schools**
- **Under Contract**: Follow national curriculum; state-funded.
- **Without Contract**: Independent curriculum; less state oversight.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: High student-teacher ratios, pressure on students, and debates over secularism.
- **Reforms**: 2021 mandate for schooling until age 16, focus on digital literacy, and efforts to reduce inequality.

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **Historical Overview**
Taiwan’s education system evolved under **Dutch, Ming, Qing, and Japanese rule**, with formal education introduced by missionaries. After the **1949 retreat of the ROC**, the government sought to assimilate Taiwanese into the Chinese cultural and political system, leading to the suppression of private schools. Reforms in the 1950s–1960s expanded public education, and the **12-year compulsory education policy** was implemented in 2014.

Key milestones include:
- **1944–1945**: ROC took control of Taiwan; private schools suppressed.
- **1968**: Compulsory education extended to junior high school.
- **2011**: 12-year compulsory education confirmed.
- **2016**: "Vocational High Schools" renamed "Skill-Based Senior High Schools."

#### **Structure of Education**
**Pre-primary Education**
- **Kindergarten**: Optional, regulated by the Early Childhood Education and Care Act.

**Primary Education (Elementary School)**
- **6 years** (grades 1–6), compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: Mandarin, mathematics, science, English, native languages (Taiwanese/Hakka).
- **Exams**: None at this level.

**Junior High School (Junior High)**
- **3 years** (grades 7–9), compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: Literature, mathematics, English, science, social studies.
- **Exams**: National senior high school entrance exams.

**Senior High School (Senior High)**
- **3 years** (grades 10–12), compulsory.
  - **Regular Senior High**: Academic track; national university entrance exams.
  - **Skill-Based Senior High**: Vocational track; prepares for workforce entry.
- **Exams**: National university entrance exams.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: 105 public and private universities (e.g., National Taiwan University, National Chengchi University).
- **Admission**: National university entrance exams.
- **Tuition**: Lower in public universities; private universities charge higher fees.
- **International Programs**: English-language programs for foreign students.

**Vocational Education**
- **Junior Colleges**: 5-year or 2-year programs leading to associate degrees.
- **Institutes of Technology**: Associate’s, bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.
- **Apprenticeships**: 2–4 years, with government support.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: High pressure on students, overemphasis on exams, and inequality in access to elite schools.
- **Reforms**: 12-year compulsory education, focus on vocational education, and efforts to reduce class sizes.

### **Education in England**

#### **Historical Overview**
England’s education system evolved from **medieval Latin schools** to **state-funded schools** in the 19th century. Key reforms include:
- **1870**: *Elementary Education Act* allowed local governments to create schools.
- **1902**: *Education Act* abolished fees for elementary schools.
- **1944**: *Education Act* established free, compulsory schooling until age 15.
- **1988**: *Education Reform Act* introduced academies and marketization of schools.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Early Years**
- **Nursery/Reception**: Ages 3–5; optional state-funded pre-school education.

**Primary School (Key Stages 1–2)**
- **Ages 5–11**: 5 years (Years 1–6).
- **Core Subjects**: English, mathematics, science, history, geography, PE.
- **Exams**: None at this level.

**Secondary School (Key Stages 3–5)**
- **Ages 11–18**: 7 years (Years 7–11 for Key Stage 3, Years 12–13 for Key Stage 5).
  - **Grammar Schools**: Selective schools for academic track.
  - **Comprehensive Schools**: Non-selective schools.
  - **Sixth Form**: Ages 16–18; prepares for A-levels or vocational training.
- **Exams**: GCSEs (Key Stage 4), A-levels (Key Stage 5).

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: Public and private; tuition fees up to £9,535/year for UK students.
- **Grades**: Bachelor’s (3 years), Master’s (1 year), PhD (3 years).
- **International Students**: Popular destinations due to high-quality education.

**Private Schools**
- **Prep Schools**: Ages 3–13; prepare for independent schools.
- **Public Schools**: Ages 13–18; historically elite schools.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Inequality in access, pressure on students, and debates over secularism.
- **Reforms**: 2018 funding formula, focus on digital literacy, and efforts to reduce inequality.

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **Historical Overview**
Nigeria’s education system was shaped by **British colonial policies** and **post-independence reforms**. Key milestones include:
- **1924**: Universal Primary Education (UPE) introduced.
- **1976**: UPE replaced by **Universal Basic Education (UBE)**.
- **1981**: Reforms to improve quality and accessibility.
- **2012**: New curriculum and focus on vocational education.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Primary Education**
- **6 years** (grades 1–6), compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: Mathematics, English, religious knowledge, indigenous languages, agriculture.
- **Exams**: Common Entrance Examination for secondary school admission.

**Secondary Education**
- **Junior Secondary School (JSS)**: 3 years (grades 7–9).
- **Senior Secondary School (SSS)**: 3 years (grades 10–12).
  - **Core Subjects**: English, mathematics, economics, civics, science (biology, chemistry, physics).
  - **Exams**: Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE) or General Certificate of Education (GCE).

**Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: 153 universities (public, private, and state-owned).
  - **First Generation**: 5 universities (e.g., University of Ibadan, University of Nigeria).
  - **Second Generation**: 12 universities (e.g., University of Calabar).
  - **Private Universities**: 75 universities (e.g., Babcock University).
- **Admission**: JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board) exams or direct entry.
- **Tuition**: Free for public universities; private universities charge higher fees.

**Vocational Education**
- **National Technical Certificate/Advanced National Technical Certificate**: Overseen by the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE).
- **Apprenticeships**: 2–4 years, with government insurance coverage.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Low literacy rates (31% illiteracy in 2022), inequality, and political influence in education.
- **Reforms**: 2012 curriculum, focus on vocational education, and efforts to improve teacher quality.

### **Education in Ghana**

- **1961**: Free and compulsory primary education introduced.
- **1987**: Education Act aimed to improve quality and accessibility.
- **2007**: Free, Compulsory, and Universal Basic Education (FCUBE) implemented.

#### **Structure of Education**

- **Kindergarten**: 2 years (ages 4–6).

**Primary Education**
- **6 years** (grades 1–6), compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: English, Ghanaian languages, mathematics, science, social studies.
- **Exams**: None at this level.

**Junior High School (JHS)**
- **3 years** (grades 7–9).
- **Exams**: Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE).

**Senior High School (SHS)**
- **3 years** (grades 10–12).
  - **Academic Track**: Prepares for university admission.
  - **Vocational Track**: Prepares for technical/vocational careers.
- **Exams**: West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE).

**Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: 6 public and 49 private universities (e.g., University of Ghana, Kwame Nkrumah University).
- **Polytechnics**: 10 public polytechnics offering vocational degrees.
- **Admission**: WASSCE results; minimum 24 points for bachelor’s degree.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Low literacy rates (69% in 2022), inequality, and political influence in education.
- **Reforms**: FCUBE, focus on vocational education, and efforts to improve teacher quality.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **Historical Overview**
Israel’s education system reflects its **Jewish and Arab communities**. Key milestones include:
- **1944**: First integrated schools (Neve Shalom).
- **2000**: Israeli Pupils’ Rights Law prohibited discrimination.
- **2012**: Council for Higher Education invested in Haredi education.

#### **Structure of Education**
**Primary Education**
- **6–12 years** (grades 1–6), compulsory.

**Middle School**
- **7–9 years** (grades 7–9), compulsory.

**High School**
- **10–12 years** (grades 10–12), compulsory.
  - **State-Secular**: Prepares for matriculation exams.
  - **State-Religious**: Emphasizes Jewish studies.
  - **Independent Religious**: Focuses on Torah study.
  - **Arab Sector**: Teaches in Arabic; emphasizes Arab history and culture.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: 9 public universities (e.g., Hebrew University, Technion).
- **Admission**: Psychometric Entrance Test (PET) and bagrut matriculation exams.
- **Tuition**: Free for public universities; private universities charge higher fees.

**Criticisms and Reforms**
- **Criticisms**: Inequality between Jewish and Arab sectors, low teacher wages, and political influence.
- **Reforms**: Focus on vocational education, efforts to improve teacher quality, and efforts to reduce inequality.
