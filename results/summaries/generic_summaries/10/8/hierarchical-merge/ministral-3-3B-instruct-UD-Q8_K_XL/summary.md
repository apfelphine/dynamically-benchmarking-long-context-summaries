### **Global Education Systems: Comparative Trends and Challenges**

#### **Foundational Education Systems**
Most countries adopt **12-year compulsory education**, though variations persist. **Nigeria** (9 years, 6 primary + 3 junior secondary) struggles with **funding gaps and informal education**, while **Ireland** and **Taiwan** enforce **12-year systems with rigorous academic standards**. **Israel** and **Sri Lanka** blend **secular/religious traditions** (e.g., Israel’s segregated schools, Sri Lanka’s free education with high female participation ~60%). **Iran** (12 years, gender-segregated) reflects **post-Revolution reforms**, and **Turkey** (12 years, secular-nationalist) prioritizes **standardized curricula**, though **France** and **England** maintain **12-year secular frameworks**.

Special education and vocational pathways vary significantly. **Taiwan** mandates **military training in junior high** and offers **special education since 1984**, while **Israel’s STEM focus** (46% tertiary-degree attainment) contrasts with **Iran’s gender-segregated schools** and **Sri Lanka’s vocational training** (e.g., *NVQSL*). **France’s preparatory tracks** (*CPGEs*) and **England’s T-levels** provide vocational alternatives, whereas **Nigeria’s vocational education** faces **teacher shortages and outdated curricula**. **Turkey’s Anatolian high schools** offer specialized tracks, and **Egypt’s TVET programs** address **youth unemployment (~36.1%)**.

---

#### **Higher Education and Equity Challenges**
Admission models differ: **Israel** and **Taiwan** rely on **highly competitive exams (Bagrut/PET)**, while **Ireland’s EHEA alignment** facilitates **EU mobility**. **Iran’s 4.5 million students** prioritize **STEM/social sciences**, with **mandatory service obligations**, whereas **Sri Lanka’s free education** and **vocational training** support **equity**. **France’s Bologna Process** and **England’s A-levels** dominate, but **teacher shortages** persist in **Nigeria, Iran (~950,000 teachers, 60,000 gaps)**, and **Turkey’s curriculum bias** (e.g., historical denialism).

Gender and socioeconomic disparities highlight systemic inequities. **Iran’s gender-segregated system** and **male-dominated academia (81% male professors)** reflect **Islamic integration challenges**, while **Israel’s Arab sector disparities** (lower certification rates) drive **scholarship programs (e.g., Ruad)**. **Sri Lanka’s high female participation (~60%)** contrasts with **Iran’s mandatory Islamic prayer and dropout rates**, though **France’s secularism (*laïcité*)** and **England’s SEND support** address inclusion. **Turkey’s religious governance** and **Egypt’s colonial legacy** shape **access inequalities**.

---

#### **Vocational Training Systems**
**Sri Lanka’s non-formal programs** serve **dropouts and adults** in trades (e.g., dressmaking, carpentry), regulated by the **Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission**, though **unregulated competition** limits quality. **Egypt’s TVET schools** and **Turkey’s vocational high schools** address **youth unemployment**, while **France’s T-levels** and **England’s vocational pathways** integrate **work-based learning**. **Taiwan’s private-driven STEM focus** and **Israel’s STEM scholarships** prioritize **high-performing outcomes**, whereas **Iran’s digital learning (LMS, Payame Noor University)** and **Armenia’s tech-based programs** reflect **equity-focused reforms**.

**Hong Kong** blends **Cantonese-medium and international schools** (IGCSE/A-Levels/IB), with **private schools catering to non-Cantonese students**, though **rote-learning debates** persist. **Sweden’s vocational paths** (e.g., healthcare, trades) and **apprenticeships (2+2 model)** support **free laptops and social inclusion**, while **Norway’s structured programs** ensure **equitable resource allocation**.

---
#### **Funding, Equity, and Systemic Challenges**
**Resource allocation** varies: **EU-funded Ireland** and **state-funded Norway** ease access, but **Nigeria/Iran** face **teacher shortages** (~60,000 gaps in Iran, severe in rural areas). **Sweden’s free tuition** (for EU/EEA students) and **France’s healthcare subsidies** mitigate costs, though **religious governance** (e.g., **Israel/Iran**) and **colonial legacies** (e.g., **Egypt**) persist. **England’s £7.1B funding increases** improved **PISA rankings (13th globally)**, but **teacher retention** remains critical.

**Reform priorities** emphasize **performance vs. equity**:
- **Taiwan/Israel**: **STEM focus, special education, and vocational innovation**.
- **Iran/Sri Lanka**: **Gender-inclusive, digital/vocational reforms**.
- **Turkey/France**: **Secular/nationalist models with curriculum biases**.
- **Global trends**: **Trade-offs between high performance (Israel/Taiwan)** and **social justice (Iran/Sri Lanka)**, with **Norway/Ghana** balancing **equity and tech integration**.

---
#### **Key Takeaways**
1. **Compulsory Education**: Most systems extend to **12 years**, but **Nigeria (9 years)** and **Sri Lanka (14 years)** vary. **Taiwan/Ireland** prioritize **rigor/equity**, while **Iran/Turkey** integrate **religious/secular values**.
2. **Higher Education**: **Israel/Taiwan** excel in **STEM**, but **Iran/Sri Lanka** emphasize **equity/vocational paths**. **France/England** dominate **academic pathways**, with **teacher shortages** as a persistent issue.
3. **Vocational Training**: **Sri Lanka/Egypt** address **youth unemployment** via **non-formal/TVET programs**, while **France/England** offer **work-based alternatives (T-levels)**. **Turkey/Iran** rely on **vocational schools**, and **Hong Kong/Sweden** blend **international/local models**.
4. **Funding and Equity**: **EU-funded systems (Ireland/Norway)** and **free tuition (Sweden)** ease access, but **Nigeria/Iran** struggle with **teacher shortages and rural disparities**. **Global reforms** balance **performance-driven models (Israel/Taiwan)** with **equity-focused ones (Iran/Sri Lanka)**.
