## Global Education System Overviews and Comparisons

Global education systems are highly diverse, featuring models ranging from centralized to multi-track. Examples include Taiwan’s intensely academic 12-year compulsory system, France’s standardized secular model (*Laïcité*), and England's National Curriculum with varied post-16 options. In contrast, Norway emphasizes foundational skills in its public system, while Hong Kong focuses on K-12 bilingualism and offers a tertiary sector primarily in English.

Many nations face significant systemic challenges concerning quality, equity, and resources. Difficulties noted worldwide include low literacy and instability (Nigeria), funding gaps and disparities (Indonesia, Israel), and deficits in vocational pathways. Modern reforms often involve integrating global standards, seen in Iran’s hybrid learning or Armenia's use of technology to build 21st-century skills.

### Regional and Specific Models

Several countries exhibit unique structural approaches. Africa and the Middle East feature varied landscapes; Nigeria strives for universal basic education but contends with infrastructure issues, while Israel maintains an integrated K-12 system accommodating diverse tracks. In Europe and Asia, Sweden operates a comprehensive system allowing both public and independent schools (*Friskolor*), aligning with the Bologna Process, whereas Sri Lanka utilizes a multi-ministry setup.

Specific regional examples showcase different priorities. Ghana offers a 12-year compulsory, free system but struggles with resource scarcity and low vocational completion. Meanwhile, established systems like Norway and Hong Kong, despite high structure, are continuously adapting—Hong Kong reforming rote learning while Norway maintains high performance metrics.
