**Global Education Systems Comparison:**

**Sweden:** The Swedish system is comprehensive, featuring free preschool from age three. Mandatory schooling covers nine years (*grundskola*, ages 6-16) across 16 core subjects, with upper secondary (*Gymnasieskola*) being elective (Preparatory or Vocational), requiring a diploma project. Higher education follows the Bologna Process, offering Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral degrees through public institutions. Since 2011, higher education is free for Swedish, EU/EEA, and Swiss citizens, with financial aid available via CSN. Sweden also features privately run *Friskolor* which receive public funding, sparking debates over standards and inequality. National assessments occur in grades 3, 6, and 9, though international performance (PISA/PIRLS) has fluctuated.

**Other National Systems Highlights:**
*   **Hong Kong:** Mandates education until age 18, offering free public primary/secondary education, but is criticized for its intense exam focus and homework load.
*   **Norway:** Features a public system allowing seamless credit transfer between general and vocational secondary/higher education via state institutions, using a 1-to-6 grading scale.
*   **Ghana:** Offers free basic education (ages 4-15) leading to BECE, with SHS offering academic/vocational tracks; however, TVET is hampered by informal training, and tertiary education is costly.
*   **Armenia:** Secondary education is free and compulsory, with 97% of public schools state-funded under a national curriculum, aligning higher education with European standards in STEM.
*   **England:** Mandatory schooling spans ages 5 to 18, regulated by Ofsted, with post-16 qualifications including GCSE/A-levels/T-levels.
*   **Indonesia:** Has a 12-year structure with secular and religious schools; primary to middle school is free, but high school fees apply, and tertiary quality is a concern.
*   **France:** Education is free, compulsory, and secular (*Laïcité*), progressing through *École Élémentaire* to *Lycée*, culminating in the *baccalauréat* or *CAP*.
*   **Egypt:** Despite free primary education, socioeconomic gaps and low teacher pay necessitate reliance on private tutoring.
*   **Turkey:** Utilizes a twelve-year "4+4+4" structure, integrating general and vocational secondary tracks alongside expanded religious education.
*   **Sri Lanka:** Offers free primary/secondary education up to Grade 9, but university admission is highly competitive, and the system is noted for rigidity and unregulated private schools.
*   **Taiwan:** Implements a 12-year Mandarin curriculum using a "3+3 High School System" to manage academic stress and offer specialized tracks.
*   **Nigeria:** Follows a 9-year UBE framework ("6-3-3-4") leading to the SSCE, but progress is limited by poor infrastructure, insecurity, and literacy gaps.
*   **Republic of Ireland:** Compulsory education runs from age six to sixteen, integrating English and Irish (*Gaelscoileanna*) before the Leaving Certificate, noted for high standards and EHEA compliance.
*   **Israel:** Features 12-year compulsory education across segregated tracks (state-secular, religious, Arab), with the *Bagrut* emphasizing STEM, though funding disparities exist.
*   **Iran:** Operates a centralized, Islam-influenced system with gender segregation, while focusing on digital integration.

**General Observations:** Across these varied global models, a common trend is the move toward mandated structures. Nevertheless, significant challenges persist concerning funding, infrastructure, and equitable access due to socioeconomic or regional disparities. Many nations actively strive to balance local cultural preservation with adopting modern, international educational benchmarks.
