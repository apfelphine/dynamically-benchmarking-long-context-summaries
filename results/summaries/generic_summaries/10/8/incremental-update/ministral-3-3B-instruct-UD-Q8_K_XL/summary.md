### **France vs. Indonesia vs. England vs. Armenia vs. Ghana vs. Norway vs. Hong Kong vs. Sweden: Comparative Education Systems**

Sweden’s education system stands out for its **universal, free, and inquiry-driven approach**, structured into three core stages:

1. **Early Childhood Education**
   - **Preschool (Förskola)** is **mandatory for 3+ hours/day starting at age 3**, free for all children (cost capped at **€135/month**), emphasizing socialization and work-life balance.

2. **Comprehensive School (Grundskola, ages 6–16)**
   - A **9-year compulsory program** with **16 core subjects**, including Swedish, math, English, and PE. Grading begins in **grade 6 (A–E, F failing)**, though **non-graded electives** (e.g., art) are optional. **National tests** assess core competencies in grades 3, 6, and 9, with **grading reforms (1994/2011)** introducing **Individual Development Plans (IUP)** before formal grading. Specialized programs include **Sámi schools, special needs education, and komvux (adult education)**.

3. **Upper Secondary (Gymnasieskola, ages 16–19)**
   - A **3-year program** with **99% enrollment**, offering **18 national tracks** (academic/vocational). Students choose between **preparatory programs** (e.g., Social Science, Business Management) or **vocational tracks** (e.g., Health & Social Care, Technology). Admission requires passing **8/12 subjects** (Swedish, English, math mandatory), with eligibility based on **elementary school grades**. **Introductory programs** assist non-qualified students, and all complete a **pass/fail diploma project**.

#### **Higher Education & Bologna Process**
Sweden’s higher education follows the **Bologna Process**, with degrees structured as:
- **Bachelor’s (180 credits)**, **Master’s (120 credits)**, and **Doctoral (240 credits)**.
- **Public universities** offer general degrees, while **vocational education** covers specialized fields (e.g., IT, hospitality).
- Admission requires **SweSAT** (a standardized test) and **ECTS-compatible grading scales** (e.g., A–F, VG/G/U).
- **Free tuition** for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens (since 2011), supported by **CSN student aid (loans/grants)**.
- **Higher vocational education** (1–2 years) is separate, workplace-focused, and non-Bologna-compatible.

#### **Independent Schools (Friskolor)**
- **~10% of pupils** attend **publicly funded but privately run independent schools**, offering alternatives like **Montessori or elite sports programs**.
- Recent debates highlight concerns over **standards, grading distortions, and profit motives**, with mixed public opinion.

#### **Education Outcomes & Challenges**
- **Declining PISA/PIRLS scores** (e.g., **19-point drop in PIRLS 2001–2011**) attributed to **immigration, testing flaws, and equity gaps**.
- **Political reforms** proposed to improve performance, balancing **equity and academic rigor**.

#### **Comparative Context**
While Sweden’s system emphasizes **equity, lifelong learning, and inquiry-based pedagogy**, contrasts with **Hong Kong’s exam-driven model** (strict discipline, cram schools) or **France’s vocational apprenticeships**. **Norway’s Grunnskole** and **Ghana’s FCUBE reforms** also highlight regional disparities, with **Armenia’s Bologna challenges** and **Hong Kong’s bicultural tensions** shaping broader trends in **digital integration, language reforms, and adult education**.
