### **Education in Nigeria**

- **Research & Evidence**: AI in open and distance learning shows mixed effects on performance, with methodological gaps noted.
- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 5, lasts 6 years, with core subjects including Mathematics, English, Religious Knowledge, and indigenous languages. Students take a Common Entrance Examination for secondary school admission. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) system (1999) mandates 9 years of schooling (6 primary + 3 junior secondary) with automatic progression and a focus on equity.
- **Secondary Education**: Follows a 6-3-3-4 system (6 primary, 3 junior secondary, 3 senior secondary). Core subjects include English, Mathematics, and Science, with electives in Arts, Business, and Technical fields. Students take the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by WAEC. The National Examination Council (NECO) also administers a parallel exam.
- **Federal Schools**: Government-funded, prestigious, and based on merit-based admissions via the National Common Entrance Examination. They offer good reputations and lower fees.
- **State Schools**: Funded by state governments, often with high fees for books and uniforms. Teacher qualifications vary, and many schools are understaffed or lack qualified instructors.
- **Private Schools**: Expensive, with fees rising rapidly (up to 30 million naira). Offer smaller classes, modern equipment, and attract qualified teachers. Religious schools (e.g., Catholic, Methodist) were historically cheaper.
- **Promotional Examinations**: SSCE and GCE (administered by WAEC and NECO) are key exams. Students take a maximum of 9 subjects, with grades ranging from A1 (Distinction) to F9 (Fail). Credit grades and above are required for university admission.
- **A-Levels**: IJMB and JUPEB are advanced-level programs for university direct entry, recognized by the government and international bodies.
- **Tertiary Education**: Government controls most institutions (40 federal, 45 state, 68 private universities). The system includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. Admission requires SSCE/GCE credits and a high JAMB score. Duration varies by program (e.g., 4 years for Social Sciences, 5 years for Engineering).
- **Vocational Education**: Administered by the National Board for Technical Education. Offers National Technical Certificates. Challenges include teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and poor labor market alignment.
- **Informal Education & Literacy**: Plays a foundational role. Challenges include funding and centralization. Programs like CELLE promote literacy. Literacy remains low (62.02% in 2018), with regional disparities in the North-East and North-West.
- **Female Education**: Women’s enrollment is increasing, though gender bias exists in specific disciplines. Barriers include teen pregnancy, poverty, and insecurity. UNICEF’s Girls' Education Project supports access and retention.
- **Rural Education**: Characterized by poor infrastructure, staff shortages, and insecurity. Government efforts are limited.
- **International Education**: 129 international schools (ISC-defined), offering curricula in English outside English-speaking countries.
- **Education in the Republic of Ireland**: Free upper second-level education (since 1967), with a strong emphasis on bilingualism and religious education. The Junior Cycle (3 years) leads to the Junior Certificate, followed by the Senior Cycle (2 years) culminating in the Leaving Certificate. Schools are categorized by type (national, gaelscoil, multi-denominational, preparatory). The system is known for high academic performance in reading and mathematics.
- **Education in Taiwan**: A 12-year compulsory education system (1983) replaced the old 9-year system. The curriculum is highly structured, with a strong focus on science and technology. The National Education policy emphasizes secularism and national identity. The system includes public, private, and international schools. Vocational education is growing. The government promotes digital literacy with a target of 95% by 2030.
- **Education in Israel**: A three-tier system (primary, middle, high school) with strong societal emphasis on higher education. The Israeli Pupils’ Rights Law protects against religious discrimination. The matriculation (bagrut) exam is critical for university admission. The system is highly competitive, with high dropout rates in Haredi schools. The Arab sector faces significant disparities in funding and access.
- **Education in Egypt**: A 12-year compulsory system (pre-primary to secondary). The national curriculum emphasizes secular values and the five principles of Pancasila. The education system is highly centralized, with a focus on national identity. Challenges include teacher shortages, low quality, and high student-to-teacher ratios. The government has implemented reforms to improve access and quality.
- **Education in France**: A secular, state-run system with a strong emphasis on national identity. The curriculum includes history and literature, with a focus on common culture. The system is highly structured, with a strong emphasis on discipline and national values. Private schools are allowed but must follow state curriculum guidelines.
- **Education in Indonesia**: A 12-year compulsory system (pre-primary to secondary). The curriculum emphasizes Pancasila, national identity, and religious values. The system is highly centralized, with a strong focus on national identity. The government has implemented reforms to improve access and quality.

The system includes a wide range of school types (grammar, comprehensive, private). The national curriculum is balanced and broadly based, with a focus on spiritual, moral, cultural, mental, and physical development. ### **Key Themes Across Systems**
- **Compulsory Education**: Most countries mandate education from early childhood through secondary school.
- **Curriculum and Values**: Emphasis on national identity, secularism, and cultural values is common.
- **Examinations**: Standardized exams (e.g., SSCE, WAEC, GCE, HKDSE) are central to progression and university admission.
- **Teacher Quality and Training**: A persistent challenge across systems, with shortages and inconsistent training noted.
- **Equity and Access**: Disparities in access, especially in rural and marginalized communities, remain significant.
- **Reforms and Innovation**: Ongoing reforms to improve quality, equity, and relevance, including digital literacy and vocational training.
