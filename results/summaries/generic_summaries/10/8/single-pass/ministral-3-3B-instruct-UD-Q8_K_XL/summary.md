### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **Research and Evidence**
A 2024 systematic review examined AI’s impact on open and distance learning (ODL), revealing mixed outcomes and identifying methodological gaps. A separate study proposed a multilayered framework for predicting academic performance in ODL contexts, emphasizing model evaluation approaches.

#### **Primary Education**
Primary education in Nigeria begins at age 5, spanning six years, culminating in a first school-leaving certificate. Core subjects include Mathematics, English, Religious Knowledge, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, and indigenous languages (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). Private schools offer additional subjects like Computer Science, French, and Fine Arts. Students must pass the Common Entrance Examination for admission to Federal/State Government Secondary Schools or private schools.

**Historical Context:**
Before 1976, education policy was shaped by colonial practices. The Universal Primary Education (UPE) program (1976) faced challenges and was revised in 1981 and 1990. The Universal Basic Education (UBE) Act (1999) replaced UPE, mandating 9 years of uninterrupted schooling (6 years primary + 3 years junior secondary), transitioning automatically between classes. UBE is funded and monitored by the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC), ensuring it is free, compulsory, and a right for all children.

#### **Secondary Education**
Secondary education serves as a bridge between primary and tertiary education. The 6-3-3-4 system (introduced in 1982) divides secondary education into Junior Secondary School (JSS) and Senior Secondary School (SSS). JSS spans 3 years, covering subjects like Mathematics, English, Social Studies, Cultural Arts, Basic Science, and Technology. SSS (3 years) includes core subjects (English, Mathematics, Economics, Civic Education) plus electives (e.g., Biology, Chemistry, Physics, History, Geography, Agricultural Science, or vocational subjects).

**Federal-Sponsored Secondary Schools:**
- Operated by the Federal Government through the Ministry of Education.
- Typically boarding schools with 2-3 federal colleges per state.
- Admission is merit-based, determined by the National Common Entrance Examination.
- Tuition is low (~₦25,000) due to partial federal funding.
- Reputed as elite due to merit-based admissions.

**State-Sponsored Secondary Schools:**
- Funded by respective state governments.
- Education is nominally free, but students pay for books, uniforms, and miscellaneous expenses (~₦50,000 annually).
- Historically elite due to high educational standards, but rankings have declined due to budget issues and competition from private schools.

**Private, Religious, and Independent Secondary Schools:**
- Private schools are expensive, with fees ranging from hundreds of thousands to millions of naira annually, often rising by 50-70%.
- Smaller class sizes, modern facilities, and better salaries attract qualified teachers.
- Religious schools (e.g., Catholic, Methodist, Baptist) historically offered lower fees but now vary in cost.

**Promotional Examinations:**
- The 6-3-3-4 system replaced the 6-3-3 system, merging primary and junior secondary years into 9 years of compulsory education.
- The Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) is conducted at the end of SSS.
- The West African Examination Council (WAEC) administers both SSCE and the General Certificate of Education (GCE).
- Students must register for 9-7 subjects, with Mathematics and English as compulsory.
- Grades range from A1 (highest) to F9 (fail), with Credit grades (C4-C6) considered adequate for university admission.

#### **International Education**
As of 2015, Nigeria had 129 international schools, offering curricula in English or other languages, often modeled after foreign systems.

#### **A-Levels in Nigeria**
- The Interim Joint Matriculation Board (IJMB) offers an advanced-level program for direct entry into universities.
- The Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board (JUPEB) provides direct entry for students without JAMB scores.
- Both programs are government-approved and recognized for admission to universities in Nigeria and abroad.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Tertiary education in Nigeria is majority-controlled by the government, comprising universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education. The country has 153 registered universities (40 federal, 45 state, and 68 private). Admission requirements include SSCE/GCE Ordinary Level credits, a minimum JAMB score of 180, and direct entry qualifications for those with NCE, ND, or Advanced Level Certificates.

**Tiered Universities:**
- **First Generation:** Established between 1948 and 1965 (e.g., University of Ibadan, University of Nigeria, Nsukka, Obafemi Awolowo University, Ahmadu Bello University).
- **Second Generation:** Established between 1970 and 1985 (e.g., University of Calabar, University of Ilorin, University of Jos, University of Maiduguri).
- **Third Generation:** Established in the 1980s to address technological and agricultural demands (e.g., Federal University of Technology, Owerri).

**Private Universities:**
- Established in 1993, private universities are regulated by the Federal Government.
- Tuition fees vary widely, with some private universities charging up to $30,000 annually.

#### **Vocational Education**
Vocational training and informal education dominate in Nigeria. The National Board for Technical Education oversees vocational education, with the Master Plan for 2001-2010 aimed at improving vocational programs. Challenges include a shortage of teachers, outdated curricula, and lack of labor market data. The National Business and Technical Examinations Board (NABTEB) administers vocational certificates, and apprenticeships are encouraged for skill development.

#### **Informal Education and Literacy Programs**
Informal education is decentralized and unique, often decentralized and mission-driven. Successes include communal literacy programs like the Premier Reading Club (PRC), but funding remains a challenge. The government has partnered with UNESCO to support mass literacy programs and review non-formal education policies.

#### **Female Education**
Education is recognized as a basic human right, with a positive correlation between girls' enrollment and national development metrics. Despite progress, challenges include gender bias, teen pregnancy, religious beliefs, poverty, and poor school facilities. Initiatives like the UNICEF Girls' Education Project aim to improve enrollment, retention, and completion rates.

#### **Secondary Education in Rural Areas**
Education in rural Nigeria faces significant challenges, including poor infrastructure, insufficient academic staff, insecurity, and non-payment of staff salaries. Rural schools are often in a "sorry state," with a majority of the population living in neglected areas.

#### **Literacy in Nigeria**
The literacy rate in Nigeria is 62.02% (2018), with significant regional disparities. The North-East and North-West regions have the highest out-of-school children due to poverty, insecurity, and cultural factors like child marriage and religious extremism. Efforts to improve literacy include multilingual education, access to resources, and initiatives like the Premier Reading Club (PRC). The government has prioritized literacy, with policies aimed at enhancing functional literacy and creating equitable access to education.

### **Education in Ireland**

#### **History**
- **1966:** The Fianna Fáil Education Minister, Donogh O’Malley, announced plans for free upper secondary education, which was eventually introduced in 1967.
- **1973:** The Irish language requirement for a second-level certificate was abandoned.

#### **Structure**
Education in Ireland is compulsory from ages 5 to 16 or until students complete three years of second-level education. Under the Constitution of Ireland, parents are not obligated to send their children to state schools but have legal rights regarding education.

**Types of Schools:**
- **National Schools:** Controlled by a board of management under diocesan patronage, often including a local clergyman.
- **Gaelscoileanna:** Irish-language schools, with Irish as the primary medium of instruction.
- **Multidenominational Schools:** Open to students from all religions and backgrounds.
- **Preparatory Schools:** Independent, fee-charging primary schools preparing students for secondary education.

#### **Framework**
Education is structured around the European Qualifications Framework (EQF) and the European Higher Education Area (EHEA) framework, with levels corresponding to the National Framework for Qualifications (NFQ).

#### **School Years**
- **Primary School:** Ages 4–12, divided into Junior Infants (age 4–5/5–6), Senior Infants (age 5–6/6–7), and grades 1–6.
- **Secondary School:** Ages 12–19, divided into Junior Cycle (ages 12–15), Transition Year (optional, ages 15–16), and Senior Cycle (ages 16–19).

#### **Primary Education**
The Primary School Curriculum (1999) focuses on nurturing children in all dimensions of life, including spiritual, moral, cognitive, emotional, imaginative, aesthetic, social, and physical development. The curriculum is prepared by the National Council for Curriculum and Assessment.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **Junior Cycle:** A three-year program culminating in the Junior Certificate Examination.
- **Transition Year:** Optional one-year program for students aged 15–16.
- **Senior Cycle:** A two-year program culminating in the Leaving Certificate Examination.

#### **Third-Level Education**
Ireland is recognized as one of the top twenty best countries for education globally.

#### **Special Needs Education**
The Education for Persons with Special Educational Needs Act (2004) established a framework for education of students with special needs. Special Needs Assistants (SNAs) provide additional learning support for students with disabilities.

#### **Areas of Disadvantage**
The Department of Education identifies disadvantaged schools and provides additional assistance through schemes like the Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools (DEIS) program, which includes reduced class sizes and other supports.

#### **International Students**
Ireland is a popular destination for international students, with many universities and colleges educating global leaders.

#### **Holidays**
The school year is divided into three terms, with major holiday breaks around Easter, Christmas, and mid-term.

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **History**
- **Dutch East India Company:** Implemented education systems in Taiwan.
- **Ming Chinese Loyalists:** Established academies like Daodong Academy.
- **Republic of China:** After 1949, public schools were established, and private schools were suppressed to ensure loyalty.
- **1954:** Educational reforms began, revitalizing and expanding private schooling.

#### **Public Education**
- **Compulsory:** Primary school through junior high school since 1968.
- **12-Year Compulsory Education:** Implemented by 2014.
- **Discipline:** Strict with school uniforms and morning reveille.
- **Semesters:** Two semesters per year, with winter and summer vacations.
- **Language:** Mandarin.

#### **School Grades**
- **Elementary School:** Grades 1–6.
- **Junior High School:** Grades 7–9.
- **Senior High School:** Grades 10–12.
- **Vocational Schools:** Three-year programs focusing on practical skills.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities:** Public and private, with public universities receiving government support.
- **Engineering:** Popular field, with engineering degrees accounting for over a quarter of bachelor's degrees.
- **Tuition:** Lower in public universities compared to private ones.

#### **Vocational Education**
- **Junior Colleges:** Five-year and two-year programs offering associate degrees.
- **Institutes of Technology:** Offer associate's, bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees.
- **Challenges:** Shortage of teachers, outdated curriculum, and lack of labor market data.

#### **Private Education**
- **Cram Schools:** Popular for after-school classes to supplement regular education.
- **Kindergartens and Preschools:** Increasingly popular, with some offering accelerated courses.

#### **Special Education Services**
- **Special Education Act:** Established in 1984, providing services for students with special needs.
- **Three Main Ways:** Centralized special education classes, decentralized resource rooms, and itinerant resource programs.

#### **Nursing Education**
- **Entry:** After completing education, students gain qualifications to participate in the National exam for professional nurse license.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **Schools: Tiers and Tracks**
- **Primary Education:** Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12).
- **Middle School:** Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15).
- **High School:** Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18).
- **Compulsory Education:** From kindergarten through 12th grade.

**Tracks:**
- **State-Secular (Mamlachti):** Secular curriculum.
- **State-Religious (Mamlachti Dati):** Intensive Jewish studies.
- **Independent Religious (Haredi):** Focuses on Torah study.
- **Arab Schools:** Teach in Arabic, emphasizing Arab history, religion, and culture.

#### **Societal Approach to Education**
- **Higher Education:** Seen as key to socioeconomic mobility.
- **Education Value:** Highly valued in Israeli society, with many parents prioritizing education.
- **Labor Market:** High demand for higher education, particularly in science and engineering.

#### **Matriculation (Bagrut)**
- **High Schools:** Prepare students for the Israeli matriculation exams (bagrut).
- **Exams:** Cover various academic disciplines, with passing marks required for matriculation.
- **Impact:** Matriculation certificates determine acceptance into elite military units, admission to academic institutions, and job prospects.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities:** Nine public universities, with some private institutions.
- **Admission:** Requires certain bagrut matriculation units and a good grade in the Psychometric Entrance Test.
- **Tuition:** Public universities subsidized by the government, with discounts for military service.
- **Rankings:** Israel's universities rank highly in science and engineering-related subjects.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Test Scores:** Scores from standardized tests exclude Haredi and special education students, not accurately reflecting overall education quality.
- **Teacher Quality:** Large classes, low wages, and low morale among teachers.
- **Textbooks:** Negative portrayal of Arabs in textbooks.

#### **Arab Sector**
- **Education System:** Operates separately, teaching in Arabic about Arab history and culture.
- **Funding Disparities:** The Jewish education system receives more resources.
- **Challenges:** High dropout rates, underfunding, and lack of resources in Arab schools.

### **Education in Iran**

#### **History**
- **Pre-Islamic Iran:** Emphasis on education to understand God and live a prosperous life.
- **Safavid Era:** Modern education system formed with a mixed emphasis on Islamic values and scientific advancements.
- **Qajar Era:** Educational Committee (Anjoman-e Ma'arf) promoted educational reform.
- **Literacy Corps (1969–1979):** Aimed to improve literacy rates through mass education.
- **Post-Islamic Revolution:** Islamization of textbooks, segregation by sex, and mandatory religious ceremonies.

#### **Grades and Structure**
- **Pre-Primary:** Kindergarten (1 year).
- **Elementary Education:** Grades 1–6 (6 years).
- **Lower Secondary:** Grades 7–9 (3 years).
- **Upper Secondary:** Grades 10–12 (3 years).
- **Vocational Schools:** 2 years.
- **University:** 4 years (undergraduate), 2 years (graduate).

#### **Budget and Challenges**
- **Education Budget:** 20% of government spending and 5% of GDP.
- **Teacher Shortage:** Around 60,000 teachers are needed but only 950,000 are available.
- **Privatization:** Rising costs of private schools, with public schools considered weak and expensive for the poor.

#### **Foreign Languages**
- **Persian:** Official language.
- **Arabic:** Taught in grades 7–12.
- **English:** Most desired language.
- **French:** Added to the curriculum in 2022.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities:** 16 universities, closed after the 1979 revolution, reopened gradually under Islamic supervision.
- **Admission:** Based on the nationwide Konkur exam.
- **Notable Universities:** Tehran University, Sharif University of Technology, Amirkabir University of Technology.

#### **Women in Education**
- **Enrollment:** Women comprised over 70% of university students in 2015.
- **Challenges:** Restrictions on women's education due to Islamic society norms.

#### **Schools for Gifted Children**
- **National Organization for Development of Exceptional Talents (NODET):** Maintains middle and high schools for gifted students.
- **Tuition:** Similar to private schools but may be partially or fully waived based on financial conditions.

#### **Education Reform**
- **Fourth Five-Year Development Plan:** Focus on upgrading educational quality and reforming curricula.
- **Hybrid Method Learning Program:** Mass hybrid teaching in universities with learning management systems.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Administration**
- **Ministries:** Ministry of Education, Ministry of Higher Education, Ministry of Education Services, Ministry of Skills Development and Vocational Training.
- **National Institute of Education (NIE):** Provides leadership for the development of general education.

#### **History**
- **Ancient Education:** Sanskrit introduced by Buddhism, with schools and libraries in ancient times.
- **Colonial Period:** British introduced standardized schools, leading to the establishment of the Royal College in Colombo.
- **Post-Independence:** Increased number of schools and literacy rates, with the introduction of free education in 1938.

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Structure:** Five stages: primary, junior secondary, senior secondary, collegiate, and tertiary.
- **Primary School:** Ages 4–11, with a Scholarship exam at the end of Grade 5.
- **Junior Secondary:** Ages 11–14, leading to the General Certificate of Education (GCE) Ordinary Level (O/Ls).
- **Senior Secondary:** Ages 14–16, leading to the GCE Advanced Level (A/Ls).

**Types of Schools:**
- **National Schools:** Established during colonial times, with a rich history.
- **Provincial Schools:** Majority of schools, with varying quality.
- **Pirivenas:** Monastic colleges for Buddhist priests.
- **Private Schools:** Increasing in number, offering alternative curricula.
- **International Schools:** Offering IGCSE, A-levels, and IB programs.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities:** 15 state universities, with high competition for admission.
- **Vocational Education:** Managed by the Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission.
- **Criticisms:** High competition, limited exposure, and brain drain.

#### **Vocational Education and Training**
- **National Vocational Qualifications Systems:** Seven levels of qualifications from Level 1 to Level 7.
- **Training:** Offered at technical colleges, the Open University, and the University of Vocational Technology.

### **Education in Turkey**

#### **History**
- **Republic of Turkey:** Education gradually developed and reorganized with the Law no 2287 in 1933.
- **Pre-Colonial Era:** Schools were organized into three separate channels: district schools, madrasas, and reform schools.
- **1924:** Law of Integration of Education (no 430) combined these channels, promoting secularism.
- **1928:** New Latin-based alphabet introduced.

#### **Pre-Primary Education**
- **Kindergarten:** Optional education for children aged 36–72 months.
- **Nurseries and Kindergartens:** Opened by the Ministry of National Education and other institutions.

#### **Primary Education**
- **Duration:** 8 years.
- **Core Subjects:** Turkish, Mathematics, Life Knowledge, and Foreign Language.
- **Curriculum:** Standardized across the country.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **General Secondary Education:** Covers children aged 15–17 for at least three years.
- **Vocational and Technical Secondary Education:** Prepares students for higher education and vocational careers.
- **Curriculum:** Includes Turkish language, mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, geography, religion, physical education, and foreign languages.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities:** 76 universities, with 53 state and 23 foundation universities.
- **Admission:** Based on the national university entrance examination.
- **Research:** Coordinated by the Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey (TÜBİTAK).

#### **Private Schools**
- **Types:** Private Turkish schools, minority schools, foreign schools, and international education institutions.
- **Religious Education:** Introduced in 1956, with a focus on secularism and ethical values.

#### **Foreign Languages**
- **English:** Most common foreign language, taught from grade 2.
- **Other Languages:** German, French, Spanish, Arabic, Russian, and Chinese.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Education System:** Constant alterations have caused controversy.
- **Textbooks:** Negative portrayal of Armenians in textbooks.
- **Bias:** Curricula are required to teach history based on state-approved textbooks.

- **Duration:** Three years, elective but highly enrolled.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Types:** Universities, university colleges, and higher vocational education.

- **Grading:** Uses a scale from A to F, with A being the highest.

#### **Independent Schools**
- **Types:** Free schools, religious schools, and for-profit schools.
- **Challenges:** Controversies over educational standards and inequality.

- **PIRLS:** Decline in reading scores from 2001 to 2011, recovery in 2016.
