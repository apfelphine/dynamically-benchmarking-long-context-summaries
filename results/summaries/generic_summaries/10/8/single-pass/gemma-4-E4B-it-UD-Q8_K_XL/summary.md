## Global Education Systems Overview

This document summarizes education systems across numerous countries, detailing structures from early childhood to tertiary levels. Key global themes covered include:

*   **Systemic Structures:** Systems range from highly centralized models (e.g., France, Iran, Egypt) to those with significant local/state autonomy (e.g., Ghana, Nigeria). Educational structures often define compulsory periods and transition points (e.g., Nigeria's 6-3-3-4 system; Sri Lanka's five-part structure).
*   **Funding and Access:** Funding models vary widely, including state-funded systems (e.g., Norway, England), heavily subsidized models, and systems reliant on private tuition (e.g., Egypt, some private schools in Nigeria). Disparities exist based on socioeconomic status, particularly in contexts like Ghana and Egypt.
*   **Curriculum and Pedagogy:** Approaches differ, ranging from rote memorization emphasized in some systems (e.g., Indonesia) to contemporary, activity-based learning promoted in others (e.g., Hong Kong's shift toward experiential learning).
*   **Higher Education:** Systems vary in their organization (universities vs. colleges vs. vocational institutes) and the degree pathways (e.g., Bologna Process in Sweden/France vs. distinct tracks in Iran).

## Country-Specific Highlights

### Nigeria
Nigerian education is structured into primary (age 5-10), secondary (6-3-3-4 system), and tertiary levels. The Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) monitors the mandatory, "free" nature of basic education. Secondary education is divided into Federal-sponsored, State-sponsored, and Private schools, each with different standards and funding levels. Promotional examinations like the SSCE are critical for progression.

### Ireland
Ireland features a compulsory education system from age six to sixteen, with a primary education system spanning eight years. The secondary structure involves Junior Cycle (three years) and Senior Cycle (two years), culminating in the Leaving Certificate. The system balances state funding with the presence of specialized schools, such as Gaelscoileanna (Irish-language schools).

### Turkey
Turkish education was secularized through the 1924 Law of Integration. The system is centralized under the Ministry of National Education. Compulsory education was extended to 12 years in 2012. The curriculum promotes Turkish nationalism rooted in Atatürk's thought, and while public education is largely free, private textbooks are highly sought after.

### Egypt
The Egyptian system is highly centralized, with compulsory basic education covering nine years (ages 4-14). It includes general and technical secondary tracks. Quality is a major challenge, leading to a massive reliance on private tutoring, despite efforts toward reform and expansion of access.

### England
England mandates education from age 5 to 18. State-funded schools operate under various models (Academy, Community, Free Schools), with the National Curriculum covering 13 core subjects. Post-16 education offers pathways like A-levels or BTECs, while state funding mechanisms are governed by the Office for Students (OfS).

### Sweden
Sweden utilizes a comprehensive, nine-year *grundskola* (compulsory school) system, which is largely state-funded. Upper secondary education (*gymnasieskola*) is elective, offering both preparatory and vocational paths. The system is aligned with the Bologna Process, and independent schools (*friskolor*) are publicly funded but face ongoing political debate regarding their profit models.

### Hong Kong
Education is legally compulsory until age 18, with public primary and secondary education being free. The system transitions from a broad junior curriculum to a selective senior curriculum, culminating in the HKDSE. Medium of instruction has shifted towards Chinese, though English-Medium International (EMI) schools remain prestigious alternatives.

### Armenia
Armenia maintains a free and compulsory education spanning 12 years, structured into primary, middle, and high schools. The public system is dominant, adhering to a national curriculum. The private sector operates under specific licensing guidelines, offering specialized programs.

### Other Notable Systems
*   **Sri Lanka:** Features five educational parts; compulsory education extends to Grade 9, with the GCE O/Ls serving as the university entrance exam.
*   **Norway:** Mandatory education covers ages 6-16, divided into elementary, lower secondary, and upper secondary levels.
*   **China/Taiwan:** Taiwan enforces a 12-year compulsory education, with high stakes placed on national entrance exams.
*   **Ghana:** Education is expanding rapidly, with a focus on free and compulsory basic education, though vocational education faces low prestige.
*   **Israel:** The system has four main tracks (state-secular, state-religious, Haredi, Arab), and higher education is highly valued for socioeconomic mobility.
