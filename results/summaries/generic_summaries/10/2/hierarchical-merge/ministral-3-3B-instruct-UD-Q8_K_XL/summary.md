### **Global Education Systems: Comparative Overview & Key Challenges**

#### **Foundational Structures and Compulsory Education**
Education systems vary significantly in compulsory duration and access. In **Africa**, **Ghana** enforces **12 years (KG–SHS)** with **90% primary enrollment** and **79% literacy (2018)**, though **informal vocational training dominates**. **Nigeria** follows a **9-3-4 structure** (primary, junior/senior secondary, tertiary) but struggles with **teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and conflict-related enrollment drops**, particularly in the North-East/North-West. **Egypt**, rooted in institutions like **Al-Azhar**, offers **free compulsory basic education** but ranks **49th in TIMSS reading (2022)**, with **50% graduation rates** in TVET programs.

In **Europe**, **Sweden** mandates **9 years of compulsory school**, integrating **vocational tracks (18 options)** and **Komvux (adult education)**, while **Ireland** enforces **16 years (ages 6–16)** with **free preschool**, ranking **2nd in EU reading literacy (PISA 2022)**. **Hong Kong** requires **6–18 years**, emphasizing **bilingualism (English/Cantonese)** and **patriotic curricula**, though **teacher shortages (3,500 resignations, 2022)** persist. **France** follows the **Bologna Process**, offering **3-year licences, 2-year masters, and 3-year PhDs**, with **Grandes Écoles** via **CPGEs (5% admit rate)**, while **Iran** centralizes **9 years of compulsory education** with **gender-segregated STEM tracks** (70% university students) but faces **teacher shortages (950,000+ missing)**.

---

#### **Higher Education and Funding**
Nordic models prioritize **free tuition for EU/EEA students**, exemplified by **Sweden’s 120–240 ECTS credits** and **Norway’s universal access**, though **teacher shortages and regional disparities** remain. **Taiwan** achieves **95% university enrollment** post-2008 via **Mandarin-only instruction**, while **Armenia** aligns with the **Bologna Process** but has **65.5% high school completion rates**. **England** charges **£9,535/year** (regulated by **OfS**), with **£7.1B funding** for **SEND support and teacher pensions**, ranking **13th globally (PISA 2019)**. **Hong Kong** offers **8 UGC-funded universities** via **JUPAS**, with **private/international schools costing HKD $360,000/year**, while **France** charges **€170–3,770/year**, with **Grandes Écoles** via **CPGEs (5% admit rate)**. **Sweden** provides **free higher education (120–240 ECTS)**, though **15.6% dropout rates (2018)** persist.

**Israel** has **46% post-secondary enrollment**, with **Haredi youth (8% matriculation pass rates)** and **Arab students** facing funding disparities. **Sri Lanka** offers **free education (since 1938)** but struggles with **vocational gaps** and **private tutoring culture**, particularly for **G.C.E exams**. **Nigeria** has **153 universities** but **unpaid salaries, policy gaps, and informal apprenticeships** hinder progress, while **Egypt** faces **funding shortages** in TVET programs despite **€200M EU funding (2023)** for infrastructure.

---

#### **Curriculum and Challenges**
Curricula reflect **cultural, colonial, and economic priorities**. **Hong Kong** excludes **colonial history** from its patriotic curriculum, while **France** enforces **Laïcité (secularism)** and **French-language dominance**. **Sweden** balances **equity and vocational flexibility**, though **friskolor (private schools)** face funding disparities. **Iran** centralizes **grading (A–D scale)** and **textbooks**, with **critical thinking reforms** under pressure. **Taiwan** mandates **Mandarin-only instruction** and debates **corporal punishment**, while **Sri Lanka** integrates **colonial-era biases** in governance and curriculum.

**Africa’s systems** grapple with **teacher shortages, conflict-driven gaps, and wealth-based inequities**. **Nigeria’s** **9-3-4 structure** prioritizes **formal education** but lacks **vocational alignment**, whereas **Egypt’s** **multilingual/digital literacy targets (95% by 2030)** reflect global trends. **Israel** addresses **demographic pressures** (e.g., **Haredi/Arab populations**) via **affirmative action (25% Arab budget share)** and **military-service subsidies**, though **strikes demand reforms in tuition and teacher pay**. **Sri Lanka’s** **Madhya Maha Vidyalayas (MMVs)** provide **Sinhala/Tamil-medium education**, but **colonial legacies** persist in curriculum and governance.

**Key challenges**:
- **Teacher shortages** (Hong Kong, Iran, Sweden, Sri Lanka).
- **Funding disparities** (Sweden’s free tuition vs. France’s fees; Egypt’s TVET gaps).
- **Vocational alignment** (Sweden’s 18 tracks vs. Africa’s informal apprenticeships).
- **Colonial legacies** (Taiwan’s Mandarin-only, Sri Lanka’s MMVs, Nigeria’s class-segregated systems).
- **Digital literacy and AI integration** (Hong Kong’s university tools, Egypt’s 2030 targets).

#### **Vocational and Adult Education**
**Sweden** offers **8-core-subject vocational programs** and **Komvux (adult education)**, while **Hong Kong** integrates **Vocational Training Council (VTC)** and **HKIVE**, with **evening courses/fee remission**. **France** provides **CAP (vocational certification)** and **private college partnerships**, whereas **Iran’s Islamic Azad University** offers **vocational alternatives** despite centralized control. **Taiwan** embeds **vocational education into high school tracks** (e.g., **3+3 model**) and **specialized nursing programs**, with a **booming supplementary education market**.

In **Africa**, **Nigeria** relies on **private technical schools** for vocational training, while **Egypt** faces **funding shortages** in TVET programs. **Sri Lanka** prioritizes **Madhya Maha Vidyalayas (MMVs)** but lacks **vocational gaps**, and **Israel** subsidizes **STEM and military training** for Arab/Haredi students. **Ireland** aligns vocational education with **EHEA (European Higher Education Area)**, though **dropout rates (12.3%)** remain below the EU average.

---
#### **Global Standing and Future Trends**
**Global rankings** highlight **Hong Kong (7th UN Education Index)** and **Sweden (top in PISA literacy, 4th in 2021)** as leaders in **primary education**, while **France (15th PISA, 2018)** and **England (13th)** excel in **higher education prestige**. **Iran (15th PISA, 2018)** ranks high in **STEM** but faces **gender segregation**. **Future trends** emphasize:
- **AI integration** (Hong Kong’s university tools, Sweden’s digital literacy reforms).
- **Vocational flexibility** (Sweden’s 18 tracks, France’s CAP, Taiwan’s 3+3 model).
- **Funding equity** (Nordic free tuition vs. France’s fees; Egypt’s EU funding).
- **Demographic adaptation** (Israel’s Haredi/Arab policies, Sri Lanka’s MMVs).
- **Colonial legacy reforms** (Taiwan’s Mandarin-only debates, Nigeria’s UBE mandate).

**Critical gaps**:
- **Teacher retention** (Hong Kong, Iran, Sweden).
- **Digital infrastructure** (Egypt, Sri Lanka).
- **Affirmative action** (Israel’s Arab/Haredi disparities, Sri Lanka’s national language mandates).
- **Global alignment** (Bologna Process in Armenia, Taiwan, Iran).
