### **Ghana’s, England’s, Hong Kong’s, France’s, Sweden’s, Turkey’s, Norway’s, Iran’s, Armenia’s, Taiwan’s, Indonesia’s, Nigeria’s, Egypt’s, Ireland’s, Sri Lanka’s, Israel’s Education Systems: Comparative Overview

Ghana, England, Hong Kong, France, Sweden, Turkey, Norway, Iran, Armenia, Taiwan, Indonesia, Nigeria, Egypt, Ireland, Sri Lanka, and Israel exhibit **diverse education models** shaped by **historical legacies, ideological influences, and global reforms**, addressing **funding gaps, equity disparities, and cross-border integration challenges**. Each system prioritizes distinct goals—from **universal access and vocational alignment** to **secularism, religious integration, and national identity**—through **grading reforms, inclusive education, and digital transformation**.

---

#### **Religious, National, and Secular Systems**
**Iran** emphasizes **Islamic-aligned curricula** and **merit-based NODET schools**, funded by **20% of its budget**, with **70% female enrollment** in higher education. **Armenia**, post-Soviet, achieved **100% literacy by 1960** but shifted to **Armenian-centric curricula**, expanding **English instruction** and establishing **Chinese-language schools**. Its **K-12 system** includes structured stages:
- **Preschool (2–6)**: Foundational skills.
- **Primary (1–4)**: Core literacy/math.
- **Middle (5–9)**: Comprehensive subjects.
- **High School (10–12)**: General or specialized tracks.
High school enrollment stands at **65.5%**, with **97% public schools** and **private schools** (tuition-dependent).

**Ireland’s** system is **compulsory from age 6 to 16**, with **Catholic (88.3%) and Church of Ireland schools** dominating primary education, followed by **voluntary, community, comprehensive, and fee-paying schools** in secondary. **Grind schools** (Senior Cycle-only) and **Gaelcholáistí** (Irish-medium) cater to diverse needs.

**Sri Lanka’s** system, rooted in **Buddhist pirivenas**, now emphasizes **Sinhala/Tamil-medium education**, with **pirivenas** (free government schools) and **104 private schools** (33 fee-free, 33 fee-paying). **Tertiary education** includes **39 state universities** (free but competitive) and **vocational technical colleges**, with **private universities restricted**.

**Israel’s** system is **compulsory from kindergarten to 12th grade**, divided into **state-secular, state-religious, Haredi, and Arab tracks**. By **2030**, **Haredi and Arab students will constitute 60% of elementary pupils**, though **Haredi education lags** (only **8% pass matriculation exams**). The **Pupils’ Rights Law (2000)** ensures **anti-discrimination protections**, and **Arab Christian and Russian-speaking sectors** excel academically, while **Haredi and Arab students face disparities** in funding, teacher quality, and matriculation rates. **Textbooks** sometimes promote **negative stereotypes of Arabs**, though reforms aim to address this.

#### **Vocational and Higher Education Systems**
**Taiwan’s** 12-year system integrates **vocational/higher education** via **skill-based senior high tracks** and **95%+ university enrollment**. **Indonesia’s** system reflects **Dutch colonial and Islamic traditions**, with **6 years of compulsory primary education**, followed by **3 years of junior high and high school**, including **Islamic schools (Madrasah)** and **vocational tracks (47 specializations)**.

**Israel’s Higher Education System** is **highly subsidized**, with **public universities (9 total)** offering **tuition-free education for military service exemptions**, supplemented by **loans, grants, and scholarships**. Despite **top global STEM rankings**, challenges include:
- **Funding disparities**: Arab students receive **$192/year** vs. **$1,100 for Jewish students**, leading to **high dropout rates (12% vs. 6%)**.
- **Teacher shortages and low morale**: **Classroom shortages** and **teacher shortages** (4,000+ by 2012), with **PISA 2002 ranking dropping to 33rd**.
- **Gender gaps**: **56.7% female university enrollment**, but **male dominance in STEM (81% of professors)**.
- **Strikes**: Frequent **faculty and student strikes** over **tuition fees, pay, and academic policies** (e.g., **2007 strike** over tuition hikes, **2008 teachers’ strike** for pay raises).

**Ireland’s** **Third-Level Education** remains globally elite, with **97.7% progression beyond Junior Certificate** and **top 20 rankings** in education (2022 *US News*). **Sri Lanka’s** higher education, overseen by the **Ministry of Higher Education**, includes **universities and vocational technical colleges**, with **private universities restricted**.

#### **Engineering, International Curricula, and Global Rankings**
**Egypt’s** **2025 engineering diploma** (inspired by Japan) and **184 international schools** (English, British, IB, French, German curricula) reflect **globalized reforms**. **Taiwan’s** engineering dominance stems from **government high-tech policies**, while **Sweden’s Higher Vocational Education (HVEs)** and **France’s CAP** align education with labor demands. **Turkey’s** mandatory English instruction and **Norway’s** multifaith inclusivity highlight **globalized vocational reforms**.

**Ireland’s** **National Framework of Qualifications (NFQ)** aligns higher education with the **European Higher Education Area (EHEA)**, with **PISA 2022 rankings** (2nd in reading, 11th in maths) underscoring **high academic performance**. **Sri Lanka’s** **National Institute of Education (NIE)** promotes **quality, equity, and relevance**, with **elite schools** retaining prestige due to **old-boy/girl networks**, while **international schools** offer **IGCSE/Cambridge curricula**. Israel’s **matriculation exams (Bagrut)** are critical for university admission, with **only ~46% of 12th graders earning full certificates**, and **urban disparities** (e.g., Ramat Gan at 65.3%, Jerusalem at 36%).

#### **Digital, Equity, and Holidays Reforms**
**Digital Literacy**:
- **Nigeria’s 2030 target**, **Indonesia’s multilingual education**, **Sweden’s digital teacher training**, **Ireland’s Early Childcare Scheme**, **Sri Lanka’s shift to national languages**, and **Israel’s tech-adapted Haredi education** (e.g., **PET integration**).

**Equity Challenges**:
- **Gender gaps** in **Iran/Ireland’s religious dominance** and **Nigeria’s teacher qualifications**.
- **Rural/urban divides** in **Nigeria’s UBE disparities**, **Indonesia’s underfunded schools**, **Sri Lanka’s language-based access barriers**, and **Israel’s Haredi segregation** (e.g., **matriculation pass rates vary by region**).
- **Nationalist curricula** (e.g., **Turkey’s English mandate**) and **secularism debates** (e.g., **France/Iran/Ireland**) drive policy tensions.
- **Israel’s disparities**: **Arab students face funding gaps, teacher shortages, and lower matriculation rates**, while **Haredi students lag in STEM and secular education access**.

**Vocational Integration**:
- Systems like **Sweden’s HVEs**, **France’s CAP**, **Taiwan’s skill-based tracks**, **Indonesia’s vocational schools**, **Nigeria’s NBTE programs**, **Ireland’s NFQ**, **Sri Lanka’s technical colleges**, and **Israel’s vocational high schools** (e.g., post-secondary military ties) align education with labor demands.

**Holidays**:
- **Ireland**: **182 days** (primary), **167 days** (post-primary).
- **Sri Lanka**: Holidays align with **Buddhist/Hindu festivals**.
- **Israel**: Standard school year (September–June), with **Haredi yeshivas** operating on separate schedules.

- **Vocational Modernization**:
  - **Taiwan’s skill-based senior high**, **Nigeria’s NBTE apprenticeships**, **Indonesia’s vocational schools**, **Ireland’s NFQ alignment with EHEA**, **Sri Lanka’s technical colleges**, and **Israel’s vocational high schools tied to military service**.

- **Gender Inclusivity**:
  - Balancing **Iran’s gender-inclusive reforms**, **Ireland’s secular governance reforms**, **Nigeria’s gender equity challenges**, **Sri Lanka’s religious inclusivity**, and **Israel’s gender-segregated tracks** (e.g., **Haredi education gaps**).

- **Curriculum Modernization**:
  - **Egypt’s distance learning**, **Turkey’s language shifts**, **Ireland’s NFQ alignment**, **Armenia’s after-school programs**, **Sri Lanka’s NIE-led reforms**, and **Israel’s secular/religious curriculum integration** (e.g., **Bagrut exams**).

- **Teacher Training**:

Municipalities also receive **annual education prizes** for **innovative programs** and **diverse student success**.
