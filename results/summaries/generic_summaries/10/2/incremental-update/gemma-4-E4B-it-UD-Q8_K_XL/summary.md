## Comparative Education Systems: Global Overview

**National Education Systems Overview & Regional Highlights:**
Global education systems show significant diversity, ranging from strictly secular models (e.g., France, Turkey) to centralized, Islamized systems (e.g., Iran, focusing on STEM via the Konkour). Regional variations include Armenia's 100% compulsory K-12 literacy since 1960, Sweden's ongoing debate over free preschool, and Ghana's 12 years of free basic education alongside quality concerns.

**Deeper Dives into Specific Systems:**
*   **Ireland:** Mandates education from ages six to sixteen (Primary, Junior, Senior Cycles culminating in the Leaving Certificate), integrating Catholic institutions while using the DEIS scheme to mitigate inequality.
*   **Taiwan:** Operates a complete public system (nursery to university) requiring 12 years of basic Mandarin. Public schools maintain discipline without corporal punishment, supported by 87 vocational institutions and private "Cram Schools."
*   **Indonesia:** Has a modern, non-discriminatory structure evolving from *Pondok Pesantren*, spanning 6 years of Primary, 3 years of Junior High, and 3 years of High School (offering vocational tracks, SMK). Primary and middle school are free, though high school involves fees, reflecting varied Islamic educational approaches.
*   **Egypt:** Features a duality between traditional religious (*mansouri*) and modern secular schooling. While public primary education is free, the centralized system is hampered by low quality, outdated instruction, and inequality, despite religious instruction through the Al-Azhar System.
*   **Nigeria:** Implements a 9-year free and compulsory Universal Basic Education (UBE) in a **9-3-4** structure, yet it grapples with low literacy rates (62.02% in 2018) and regional instability.
*   **Sri Lanka:** Its five-level system requires primary education through Grade 9. Higher education is highly selective; state universities offer free undergraduate programs based on GCE Advanced Levels. Vocational training is managed by the Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission (NVQSL), integrating government-private partnerships and Madrasas.
*   **Israel:** Education is compulsory from kindergarten through 12th grade, operating across four distinct tracks: state-secular, state-religious, independent religious (Haredi), and Arab. The system aims for socioeconomic mobility, with secondary completion marked by the *Bagrut* exams. State-religious schools emphasize Jewish studies, Haredi schools focus on Torah, and Arab schools use Arabic.

**Key Thematic Comparisons & Global Trends:**
Globally, there is a trend toward moving beyond rote learning. University entrance hinges on national exams or international benchmarks. Religious instruction varies significantly, from strict secularism to diverse Islamic models. Persistent inequality remains a major global challenge, seen in access gaps and specialized educational disparities.

**Focus on Israel and Socioeconomic Factors:**
Israel's higher education is strongly subsidized through loans, grants, and scholarships, contributing to high global rankings in science and engineering, although PISA scores reveal areas needing improvement. A significant issue is the Arab education sector, which faces allegations of underfunding and resource shortages relative to the Jewish system, leading to higher dropouts; initiatives are underway to boost access for Arab, Druze, and Circassian students. Gender parity is rising in higher education, though academia remains male-dominated, particularly in STEM. Teacher morale and wages are also concerns, prompting calls for reforms targeting teacher quality. Furthermore, Israeli education faces scrutiny over textbooks, with claims regarding negative portrayals and underrepresentation of Arab-Palestinians in Arabic materials. The system has seen disruptions, including strikes over tuition fee increases (2007) and teacher demands for higher pay and smaller classes. Conversely, municipalities are recognized annually by the Ministry of Education for exemplary initiatives.
