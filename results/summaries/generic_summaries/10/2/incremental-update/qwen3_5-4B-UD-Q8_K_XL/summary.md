# **Israel: Education System Overview (Updated)**

Israel's education system, driven by a societal emphasis on schooling as a primary pathway to socioeconomic mobility, operates across four main compulsory tracks (kindergarten through 12th grade): State-secular, State-religious, Independent religious (Haredi), and Arab. Significant disparities persist, particularly regarding systemic underfunding and resource shortages affecting Arab students, while the Haredi sector faces challenges with low exam participation and pass rates. Despite these issues, mainstream and Arab students generally participate fully in the *Bagrut* exams, though critics argue the curriculum increasingly prioritizes Zionist values.

**Performance, Access, and Demographics:**
*   **Higher Education:** The system is highly subsidized with strong international rankings in science and engineering, though per-student spending remains low. Admissions typically require the *Psychometric Entrance Test*, with the Open University offering alternative pathways.
*   **Demographics & Gender:** The system boasts a high tertiary attainment rate and a growing Arab student population. A distinct gender divide exists, with women outperforming men in high school exams and university enrollment, while men dominate professoriate roles and STEM fields.
*   **Textbooks:** Perspectives on educational materials are divided. While some argue state textbooks promote negative images of Arabs, others dispute this citing efforts to remove stereotypes; conversely, a 2011 Arab Cultural Association report criticized Arabic textbooks for containing errors, ignoring Arab-Palestinian presence, and minimizing Arab culture.

**Operational Challenges & Reforms:**
*   **Labor & Strikes:** The sector faces frequent strikes due to low teacher wages, large class sizes, and demands for extended school hours. Notable incidents include a 2007 student strike against tuition fees (resolved via the Shochat reforms) and a 1.5-month teacher strike demanding better conditions.
*   **Incentives:** Most students serve in the IDF before or after university; pre-service students often enter under the *Atuda* program, receiving tuition subsidies in exchange for extended military service.
*   **Recognition:** Municipalities are recognized annually for educational excellence, with six municipalities awarded in 2012 for initiatives in immigrant absorption, student empowerment, teaching quality, and innovation.

**Legal Framework & Rights:**
*   **Rights & Integration:** The **2000 Israeli Pupils' Rights Law** prohibits discrimination based on sect, supporting integrated schools in cooperative villages and Jerusalem/Galilee to reduce sectarian segregation.
*   **Challenges:** Ongoing issues such as low teacher wages, large class sizes, and union strikes continue to impact overall educational performance.
