## Education Systems Worldwide

Countries demonstrate diverse education systems shaped by historical, cultural, and political influences. England and France maintain long-established, compulsory education frameworks emphasizing support for disadvantaged groups, centralized curricula, selective higher education, and minimal university tuition for some students. Ghana and Nigeria offer free, compulsory schooling and academic/vocational tracks but grapple with funding, infrastructure, and regional inequalities. Turkey enforces twelve years of secular education, integrates apprenticeships, and debates curriculum content, while Hong Kong’s bilingual system has recently undergone patriotic reforms affecting traditional practices.

Others, like Iran, Sweden, Taiwan, and Norway, balance compulsory and inclusive schooling with academic rigor and ongoing reforms. Iran’s education is grounded in Islamic values and faces privatization and resource challenges. Sweden and Norway promote accessible, high-quality education with continuous discussion on standards and inclusivity. Taiwan adopts robust academic and vocational pathways with a disciplined, internationally oriented curriculum. Armenia upholds near-universal literacy and specialized tertiary education, focusing on practical rural skills and international cooperation through modernizing initiatives like the Bologna Process and Erasmus. Ireland has transitioned toward multi-denominational schools and high performance, while Indonesia’s compulsory system addresses literacy and indigenous access amidst teacher shortages and funding constraints.

## Regional Highlights and Challenges

Israel’s tracked system values education for socio-economic progress but faces disparities in resources, curriculum, and outcomes, notably in Arab schools and STEM representation among women. Egypt combines secular and religious elements, expanding vocational and distance learning amidst teacher shortages, corruption, and graduate unemployment. Sri Lanka offers universal free education with exam-driven advancement and diverse schooling options, although rural disparities and competition persist, with significant numbers seeking higher education abroad. Across these nations, ongoing reforms, international collaborations, and targeted investments address persistent gaps in equity, quality, and access, while vocational training uptake and educational regulation remain key areas for development.
