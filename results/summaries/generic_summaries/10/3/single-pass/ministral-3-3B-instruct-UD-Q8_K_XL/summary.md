### **Education in England**

#### **History**
Education in England was historically tied to religious institutions, with Latin-based education for aristocratic sons destined for clergy or governance. The University of Oxford and Cambridge emerged in affiliation with the Church. By the 19th century, charity schools and "free grammar schools" became more common, though education remained largely religiously influenced. Public schools and universities significantly shaped modern sports, contributing to British sports culture globally. The 19th-century reforms expanded state-funded schooling, culminating in the **Elementary Education Act (1870)**, which allowed local governments to establish schools. The **Education Act (1902)** introduced secondary schools, and **1918** abolished fees for elementary schools.

Women’s access to university education expanded in the 19th century, with institutions like Bedford College (1849), Girton College (1869), and Newnham College (1871) opening. The University of London introduced special examinations for women in 1868 and granted degrees to them in 1878. University College Bristol became the first mixed higher education institution in 1876.

#### **Legally Compulsory Education**
Education is compulsory for children aged **5 to 18**, with students required to attend traditional schools until age 16. Post-16 education or training is mandatory until age 18, through further education, apprenticeships, or part-time education. State-provided schooling and sixth-form education are tax-funded. Children aged 3–5 can access **600 hours/year** of optional state-funded pre-school education. Compulsory stages include:
- **Foundation Stage** (ages 4–5)
- **Key Stages 1–4** (ages 5–16)
- **Post-16 education** (ages 16–18)

#### **Types of Schools**
England’s education system includes diverse school types, categorized by funding and governance:

- **Grammar Schools**: Selective, admitting students from age 11 based on academic ability.
- **Primary Schools**: Offer nursery education (ages 3–5) and primary education (ages 5–11).
- **Comprehensive Schools**: Non-selective, accepting students from all backgrounds.
- **Academy Schools**: Established by the 1997–2010 Labour Government, replacing poorly-performing community schools; funded by private means and central government.
- **Community Schools**: Managed by local authorities, with primary responsibility for admissions.
- **Free Schools**: Newly established schools funded by taxpayers, academically non-selective, and free to attend.
- **Foundation Schools**: Governed by a charitable foundation, with a minority of governors appointed by the foundation.
- **Voluntary Aided Schools**: Affiliated with religious organizations (e.g., Church of England, Roman Catholic).
- **Voluntary Controlled Schools**: Primarily faith schools, with lands and buildings often owned by charitable foundations but staff employed by local authorities.
- **University Technical Colleges (UTCs)**: Sponsored by universities, offering technically oriented courses with vocational training.
- **Maths Schools**: Selective 16–19 schools specializing in mathematics, sponsored by universities.
- **Private Schools**: Fee-charging institutions, including "public schools" for 13–18-year-olds and "prep schools" for 8–13-year-olds.

#### **National Curriculum**
The national curriculum covers primary (ages 5–11) and secondary (ages 11–16) education, focusing on core subjects (English, Mathematics, Science) and broader areas (Art, History, Geography). Students at Key Stage 4 (ages 14–16) must study at least one subject from the arts, humanities, business, or modern languages. Post-16 education includes further education colleges, sixth-form colleges, and vocational training.

#### **Higher Education**
Higher education in England includes universities, higher education colleges, and private institutions. Undergraduate degrees range from certificates of higher education to bachelor’s degrees with honors. Tuition fees vary, with state-funded institutions offering free education for students from low-income backgrounds. The **Quality Assurance Agency for Higher Education (QAA)** oversees academic standards, while the **Office for Students (OfS)** manages access and fairness in higher education.

#### **Adult Education**
Adult education is offered through vocational qualifications, access courses, and distance learning programs. The **Open University** provides undergraduate and postgraduate distance learning. The **Workers’ Educational Association** offers semi-recreational courses in arts, crafts, life skills, and languages.

### **Education in France**

#### **History**
Napoleon established France’s university and secondary education systems. Jules Ferry introduced the **modern school (l’école républicaine)** in 1881, requiring children aged 6–12 to attend free, secular, and compulsory public schools. The **Lubbers Laws** (1881–1882) abolished many of the **Falloux Laws (1850–1851)**, which granted significant roles to the clergy. The curriculum emphasized French literature and history, aiming to foster a "common culture" among students, regardless of ethnic or cultural background.

#### **Governance**
All educational programs in France are regulated by the **Ministry of National Education and Youth**. The curriculum is standardized across public, semi-public, and subsidized institutions. The school year runs from early September to early July, with major holidays around **All Saints’ Day, Christmas, winter break, Easter, and summer**.

#### **School Structure**
Education is divided into three stages:
- **Preschool (maternelle)**: Ages 3–6, focusing on socialization and early learning.
- **Elementary School (école élémentaire)**: Ages 6–10, covering reading, writing, math, and basic sciences.
- **Middle and High School**:
  - **Collège (middle school)**: Ages 11–15, preparing students for the **baccalauréat**.
  - **Lycée (high school)**: Ages 15–18, offering three main types of baccalauréat: general, technological, and professional.

Private schools include:
- **Private schools under contract**: Follow the national curriculum, with teachers recruited by the state.
- **Private schools without contract**: Offer their own curriculum, monitored by the state.

#### **Higher Education**
France’s higher education system is divided into **grandes écoles** (elite institutions) and public universities. **Grades écoles** are highly selective, offering specialized programs in science, engineering, business, or humanities. Public universities follow the **Bologna Process**, with degrees divided into bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral levels. The **Licence** (bachelor’s) lasts 6 semesters, followed by a **Master’s** (4 semesters). Universities also offer **IUTs** (University Technical Institutes) for vocational education.

#### **Foreign Languages**
English is widely taught, but French is also emphasized. The government has introduced **Kurdish, Abkhaz, Adyghe, Standard Georgian, Laz, Albanian, and Bosnian** as optional languages in schools.

#### **Religious Education**
Religious instruction is not mandatory in public schools (except in Alsace and Moselle under the Concordat of 1801). The **laïcité** (secularism) principle is central to French education. In 2004, the French government banned conspicuous religious symbols in schools to prevent proselytization and promote tolerance.

### **Education in Ghana**

#### **History**
Education in Ghana traces back to the **1700s**, with the **University of Moliyili** established by the Dagbon king. Missionary schools, such as those by the **Wesleyan and Basel missions**, became prominent in the 19th century. The **1944 Education Act** introduced free and compulsory primary education, and the **Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology** was established in 1961. Post-independence reforms aimed to universalize education, but challenges like teacher shortages and curriculum quality persisted.

#### **Structure**
Ghana’s education system spans **kindergarten, primary, junior high school (JHS), senior high school (SHS), and tertiary education**. Compulsory education lasts **12 years** (ages 4–15), with free and compulsory primary education (ages 4–11) and junior high school (ages 12–15). The **Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE)** determines progression to senior high school.

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public Schools**: Free and compulsory, with a focus on English and mathematics.
- **Private Schools**: Offer additional subjects like French, computer science, and fine arts.
- **International Schools**: Follow curricula like the **International Baccalaureate (IB)** or **Advanced Level General Certificate of Education (A-Levels)**.
- **Vocational Education**: Technical and vocational institutes (TVI) offer specialized training in trades and professions.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Universities (public and private) offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. **Polytechnics** provide vocational training, while **colleges of education** train teachers. The **National Service** requires graduates to serve one year in various sectors.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Over 900,000 teachers, with a shortage of 60,000.
- **Vocational Education**: Low completion rates and outdated curricula.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women are underrepresented in tertiary education, facing barriers like early marriage and societal expectations.

### **Education in Turkey**

#### **History**
The **Republic of Turkey** established a centralized education system in 1923, with the **Law of Integration of Education (1924)** unifying religious, reform, and minority schools. The **1926 Education Organization Law** standardized curricula and school structures. The **1938 mobile courses for women** aimed to educate rural female populations. In 1997, compulsory education was extended to **8 years**, and in 2012, to **12 years**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Pre-primary Education**: Optional for children aged 36–72 months.
- **Primary Education**: 8 years, compulsory and free.
- **Secondary Education**: Includes general, vocational, and technical schools. The **4+4+4 system** (2012) restructured primary and secondary education into 4 years each.
- **Higher Education**: Universities, faculties, institutes, and vocational schools. The **Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey (TÜBİTAK)** coordinates research.

#### **Religious Education**
Religious education began in **4th grade (age 10)** and continues through secondary and high school. The curriculum includes secularism, ethics, and Islamic values, with textbooks approved by the Ministry of Education.

#### **Foreign Languages**
English is the most common foreign language, taught from **2nd grade**. Other languages like German, French, and Spanish are also offered.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Education System Changes**: Frequent reforms have led to controversy.
- **Textbook Quality**: State-approved textbooks are criticized for low quality, leading to private textbook purchases.
- **Curriculum Bias**: Textbooks promote **Atatürk’s thought** and deny the **Armenian genocide**, sparking criticism.

### **Education in Hong Kong**

#### **History**
Hong Kong’s education system evolved from **missionary schools** in the 19th century to a **bilingual and bicultural curriculum** (Chinese and English). The **1997 handover** led to reforms emphasizing **project-based learning**. The **Hong Kong national security law** (2020) impacted enrollment, with many families choosing to send children abroad.

#### **School Structure**
- **Pre-school**: Optional, with fees subsidized by the government.
- **Primary and Secondary Education**:
  - **Primary (1–6)**: Compulsory, with English and Chinese as primary languages.
  - **Secondary (7–12)**: Separated into junior and senior years. **English Medium Instruction (EMI)** schools use English as the primary language.
  - **Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE)**: Taken in **Form 6** (ages 17–18).
- **International Schools**: Offer curricula like the **International Baccalaureate (IB)** or **UK-based GCSE/A-Levels**.

#### **Higher Education**
Hong Kong’s universities are ranked among the top globally. **English is the primary medium of instruction**, with degrees awarded in English. The **Joint University Programmes Admissions System (JUPAS)** is the main route for university admission.

#### **Challenges**
- **Class Size Reduction**: Declining birth rates have led to debates on small class sizes.
- **Discipline**: Strict discipline is emphasized, with guidelines on student behavior.
- **Cultural Shift**: Increased focus on **group projects and experiential learning** over rote memorization.

### **Education in Iran**

#### **History**
Iran’s education system dates back to the **Sasanian era**, with the **Academy of Gondishapur** established around the 3rd century. The **Safavid era** introduced modern education, while the **Qajar era** saw the establishment of the **Educational Committee (Anjoman-e Ma’arif)**. The **Literacy Corps (1969–1979)** aimed to improve literacy rates during the **White Revolution**. Post-revolution, education was **Islamized**, with segregation by gender and mandatory religious instruction.

#### **School Structure**
- **Pre-primary (Kindergarten)**: Optional, ages 5–6.
- **Elementary School (Dabestan)**: Ages 6–12, free and compulsory.
- **Lower Secondary (Motovasseteh Avval)**: Ages 12–15, mandatory.
- **Upper Secondary (Motovasseteh Dovom)**: Ages 15–18, optional.
- **Vocational Schools**: Offer technical and vocational training.
- **Higher Education**: Universities, including **Tehran University, Sharif University of Technology, and Amirkabir University of Technology**.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Over **950,000 teachers**, with a shortage of 60,000.
- **Privatization**: Private schools are seen as more prestigious, leading to inequality.
- **Mandatory Islamic Prayer**: Introduced in 2023, requiring students to pray for 30 minutes daily.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **History**
Education in Norway dates back to **2000 BCE**, with cathedral schools established in the 12th century. The **folkeskole (people’s school)** was introduced in 1827, making education compulsory for seven years. The **grunnskole (foundation school)** was introduced in the 1970s, extending compulsory education to **nine years**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Primary School (barneskole)**: Ages 6–12, compulsory.
- **Lower Secondary School (ungdomsskole)**: Ages 13–16, compulsory.
- **Upper Secondary School (videregående skole)**: Ages 16–19, elective but highly attended.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and university colleges, with degrees following the **Bologna Process**.

#### **Grading System**
- **Compulsory School**: Grades A–F (E=pass, F=fail).
- **Upper Secondary School**: Grades A–E (E=pass, F=fail).

#### **Challenges**
- **Student Absenteeism**: Rising dropout rates, particularly among immigrant students.
- **Teacher Shortages**: High demand for educators, especially in rural areas.

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **History**
Education in Nigeria was influenced by colonial policies, with the **1943 Elliot Commission** leading to the establishment of the **University College, Ibadan (1948)**. The **Universal Primary Education (UPE) program (1976)** aimed to make primary education free and compulsory. The **Universal Basic Education (UBE) (1999)** expanded access to basic education.

#### **School Structure**
- **Primary Education**: Ages 5–11, free and compulsory.
- **Secondary Education**: Ages 11–16, divided into **Junior Secondary School (JSS)** and **Senior Secondary School (SSS)**.
- **Tertiary Education**: Universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education. **JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board)** administers university admissions.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and poor working conditions.
- **Vocational Education**: Limited access and outdated curricula.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women face barriers like early marriage and societal expectations.

### **Education in Armenia**

#### **History**
Armenian education dates back to the **Sasanian era**, with the **Yerevan State University** established in 1920. The **1990s** saw reforms to decentralize and modernize the education system, with increased emphasis on **Armenian history and culture**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Preschool**: Ages 2–6.
- **Primary School**: Ages 6–10.
- **Middle School**: Ages 10–15.
- **High School**: Ages 16–18, leading to the **Certificate of Full Secondary Education**.
- **Public vs. Private Schools**: Public schools follow the national curriculum, while private schools offer alternative programs.

#### **Challenges**
- **Student Absenteeism**: Rising dropout rates, particularly among rural students.
- **Teacher Shortages**: High demand for educators, especially in rural areas.

### **Education in the Republic of Ireland**

#### **History**
Education in Ireland was influenced by the **Fianna Fáil Education Minister Donogh O’Malley’s (1966) announcement of free upper second-level education**. The **1973 Irish language requirement** was abandoned, and **Gaelscoileanna (Irish-language schools)** were established.

#### **School Structure**
- **Primary School**: Ages 4–11, with **Junior Infants (age 4)** and **Senior Infants (age 5)**.
- **Secondary School**: Ages 12–18, divided into **Junior Cycle (Junior Certificate)**, **Transition Year**, and **Senior Cycle (Leaving Certificate)**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and colleges, with degrees following the **Bologna Process**.

#### **Challenges**
- **Disadvantaged Schools**: The **Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools (DEIS)** program aims to address educational disadvantage.
- **Teacher Shortages**: High demand for educators, especially in rural areas.

### **Education in Indonesia**

#### **History**
Education in Indonesia was introduced by the **Dutch** in the colonial era, with schools for Europeans, natives, and Chinese. The **Japanese occupation (1942–1945)** led to the consolidation of the Dutch education system. Post-independence, the **1945 Constitution** declared education a right for all citizens.

#### **School Structure**
- **Preschool (PAUD)**: Ages 2–6, including **Taman Bermain (playgroup)** and **Taman Kanak-Kanak (kindergarten)**.
- **Primary School (Sekolah Dasar)**: Ages 6–12, free and compulsory.
- **Junior High School (Sekolah Menengah Pertama)**: Ages 12–15.
- **Senior High School (Sekolah Menengah Atas)**: Ages 15–18, leading to the **SMA diploma**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and poor working conditions.
- **Vocational Education**: Limited access and outdated curricula.
- **Religious Schools**: Islamic schools (pesantren, madrasah) offer alternative curricula.

### **Education in Egypt**

The **French expedition (1798–1801)** led to the establishment of the **Institut d’Égypte**. The **Muhammad Ali reforms (1805–1848)** introduced modern education, with the **Al-Azhar system** offering Islamic education.

#### **School Structure**

#### **Challenges**

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **History**
Education in Sri Lanka dates back to the **Sanskrit tradition**, introduced by Buddhist monks. The **Colebrooke Commission (1836)** established the **Royal College, Colombo**, marking the beginning of the government’s schooling system. Post-independence, the **1938 Education Act** made education free and compulsory.

#### **School Structure**
- **Primary School**: Ages 4–11, leading to the **Scholarship Examination**.
- **Junior Secondary School**: Ages 11–14, leading to the **GCE O/L Examination**.
- **Senior Secondary School**: Ages 14–16, leading to the **GCE A/L Examination**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and poor working conditions.
- **Vocational Education**: Limited access and outdated curricula.
- **Competitive Exams**: High pressure and stress among students.
