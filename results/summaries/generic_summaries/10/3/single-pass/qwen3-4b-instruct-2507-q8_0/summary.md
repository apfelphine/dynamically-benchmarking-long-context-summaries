**Education in England**  
- **History**: Rooted in medieval religious institutions, evolving through the 19th century with public school development and sports. The 1944 Education Act established universal education.  
- **Legally Compulsory Education**: Children aged 5–18 must attend school; education continues until age 18 through school, apprenticeships, or training. Pre-school (3–5 years) is optional but state-funded.  
- **School Types**: 93% of students attend state-funded schools. Types include academies, community schools, free schools, foundation schools, voluntary aided/controlled schools, and UTCs (technical colleges).  
- **Curriculum**: National Curriculum covers core subjects (English, Maths, Science) and key stages. Students take exams at key stages; GCSEs are common at age 16.  
- **Post-16 Education**: Students pursue A-levels, BTECs, or apprenticeships. Sixth forms and further education colleges offer diverse qualifications.  
- **Higher Education**: State-funded universities (e.g., Oxford, Cambridge) offer degrees; students pay fees (up to £9,535/year post-2024), with state support for low-income students.  
- **Standards & Funding**: Schools are inspected by Ofsted; funding is national and includes support for SEND and special needs.  

**Education in France**  
- **History**: Began under Napoleon; Jules Ferry introduced secular, mandatory public education. Curriculum emphasizes French identity.  
- **Governance**: Ministry of National Education oversees curriculum; schools follow national standards.  
- **School Year**: Runs from September to June; includes standardized breaks.  
- **Schools**: Primary (elementary), middle (collège), and high school (lycée) stages. Private schools are "under contract" or offer religious instruction.  
- **Private & International**: Private schools follow state curriculum; international schools offer curricula in English.  
- **Higher Education**: Three levels (Licence, Master, Doctorat); universities are public or private. Tuition is low for citizens (€170–380/year).  
- **Key Features**: Strong emphasis on civic and national identity; curriculum promotes "common culture."  

**Education in Ghana**  
- **History**: Pre-colonial education was informal; colonial period introduced formal schools. Post-independence reforms led to free universal basic education.  
- **Structure**: 12-year basic education (ages 4–15); includes kindergarten, primary, and junior high school.  
- **Secondary Education**: Students enter senior high school (SHS) after passing BECE; curriculum includes core and elective subjects.  
- **Tertiary Education**: Universities and polytechnics offer degrees. Private institutions exist; access is limited by cost.  
- **Private Education**: Private schools outnumber public ones; parents pay fees.  
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortage, poor vocational training, and gender disparities.  

**Education in Turkey**  
- **History**: Post-Republic reforms emphasized secularism; introduced compulsory education (8 years) and new curriculum.  
- **School System**: Primary (8 years), secondary (3 years), and higher education.  
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on Turkish nationalism; religious education introduced in 1956.  
- **Private Schools**: Offer English and foreign language programs; some are elite.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities dominate; private institutions charge high fees.  
- **Key Features**: Religious education is mandatory; curriculum promotes national values.  

**Education in Hong Kong**  
- **History**: British-era education emphasized bilingualism and biculturalism. Post-1997 reforms introduced patriotic education.  
- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education (ages 6–18); includes kindergarten, primary, and secondary stages.  
- **Secondary Education**: Students take HKDSE; curriculum includes core and elective subjects.  
- **Higher Education**: University system with strong international links; degrees are globally recognized.  
- **Key Features**: Bilingual education (English and Chinese); curriculum now emphasizes patriotism and national security.  

**Education in Iran**  
- **History**: Ancient Persia had formal education; modern system introduced under the Islamic Revolution.  
- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education (ages 5–18); includes primary, secondary, and higher education.  
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on Islamic values; religious education is mandatory.  
- **Higher Education**: State-funded universities; private institutions offer specialized programs.  
- **Key Features**: High literacy rate; strong focus on Islamic and national identity.  

**Education in Sweden**  
- **History**: Developed from 19th-century reforms to modern comprehensive schools.  
- **Structure**: 9-year compulsory primary school (ages 6–16), followed by upper secondary (ages 16–19).  
- **Curriculum**: Balanced, with mandatory subjects in all stages.  
- **Grading**: A–F system; national tests administered at grades 3, 6, and 9.  
- **Higher Education**: Bologna-aligned system with bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.  
- **Key Features**: High-quality education; strong emphasis on equality and inclusion.  

**Education in Taiwan**  
- **History**: Colonial-era education introduced by Dutch, Chinese, and Japanese; post-1945 reforms standardized the system.  
- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education (ages 5–18); includes primary, junior high, and senior high school.  
- **Secondary Education**: Students take national exams; curriculum includes core and elective subjects.  
- **Higher Education**: Universities and colleges offer degrees; private institutions are common.  
- **Key Features**: High university enrollment; strong focus on STEM and vocational training.  

**Education in Norway**  
- **History**: Centralized education system evolved from 19th-century reforms.  
- **Structure**: 9-year compulsory education (ages 6–16); includes primary, lower secondary, and upper secondary.  
- **Curriculum**: Balanced and inclusive; emphasizes social and cultural development.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities offer degrees; private institutions are limited.  
- **Key Features**: High educational standards; strong focus on equity and inclusion.  

**Education in Nigeria**  
- **History**: Colonial-era education introduced; post-independence reforms led to universal basic education.  
- **Structure**: 9-year basic education (ages 5–14); includes primary, junior secondary, and senior secondary.  
- **Examinations**: Students take SSCE and NECO exams; results determine university admission.  
- **Higher Education**: Universities, polytechnics, and colleges offer degrees; private institutions are growing.  
- **Key Features**: High dropout rates; challenges in teacher quality and infrastructure.  

**Education in Armenia**

**Education in the Republic of Ireland**  
- **History**: Free upper secondary education introduced in 1967; religious education remains a key component.  
- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education (ages 5–16); includes primary, junior cycle, and senior cycle.  
- **Curriculum**: Balanced, with emphasis on Irish language and civic education.  
- **Higher Education**: Universities offer degrees; strong international reputation.  
- **Key Features**: High academic performance; emphasis on inclusivity and diversity.
