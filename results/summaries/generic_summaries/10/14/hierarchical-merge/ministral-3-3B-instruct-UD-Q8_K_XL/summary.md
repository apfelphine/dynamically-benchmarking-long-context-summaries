### **Global Education Systems: Comparative Insights and Challenges**

#### **Compulsory Education and Structural Variations**
Four countries—**Armenia, Norway, Sweden, and Indonesia**—require **7–12 years of compulsory education**, though Norway and Sweden emphasize **early learning and inclusivity** (ages 6–19). Norway’s system integrates **vocational pathways** (*skoleplikt*), while Sweden’s follows a **unified secondary structure** (*gymnasieskola*). Indonesia’s system blends **Islamic (pesantren/madrasah) and secular education**, with **vocational pathways deeply embedded**, though it remains **decentralized**. In contrast, **Hong Kong**’s **12-year system (2024)** shifts to a **2-year secondary phase**, incorporating **Putonghua medium-of-instruction** amid **patriotic education reforms**, while **Egypt** retains a **colonial-era curriculum** under **funding shortages and teacher shortages**.

#### **Higher Education and Funding Disparities**
**Sweden and Norway** offer **tuition-free higher education** (Sweden) and **state-funded vocational training**, though **private schools (*friskolor*)** face criticism for **grade inflation**. **Armenia** and **Indonesia** (36.3% tertiary enrollment) struggle with **fragmentation and funding gaps**, with Indonesia’s system dominated by **vocational schools (*SMK*)**. **Hong Kong**’s **top-tier universities** drive global rankings but face **patriotic education debates**, while **Egypt**’s **engineering diplomas** (Japan-inspired) coexist with **low graduate employment rates (36.1%)**. **Europe** (e.g., **France’s Grandes Écoles**, **England’s T Levels**) and **Asia-Pacific** (e.g., **Israel’s $1,100 vs. $192 student funding**) exhibit **funding inequities**, with **vocational reforms** prioritizing industry alignment.

---

### **Equity, Employment, and Future Reforms**
**Sweden and Norway** achieve **high youth employment** through vocational pathways but face **dropout/immigrant integration gaps**. **Indonesia’s** **low tertiary enrollment (36.3%)** and **rural-urban disparities** reflect **decentralized vocational challenges**, while **Egypt’s** **teacher shortages** and **underfunded schools** limit progress. **Hong Kong’s** **global rankings** contrast with **mainland China ties**, and **Israel’s** **ethnic funding gaps** persist despite **STEM excellence**. Future reforms include:
- **AI-driven education** (Sweden), **2-year secondary system** (Hong Kong, 2024), and **engineering diploma modernizations** (Egypt).
- **Vocational equity initiatives** (e.g., **France’s CAP**, **Sri Lanka’s TVET reforms**) and **gender/STEM diversification** (e.g., **Israel’s affirmative action debates**, **France’s Grandes Écoles reforms**).

### **Regional Challenges and Trends**
#### **Asia-Pacific**
- **Urban-rural divides** (e.g., **Ghana’s teacher shortages**, **Sri Lanka’s state university bias**) and **exam-driven rigidity** (Hong Kong) vs. **vocational underdevelopment** (Ghana, Sri Lanka).
- **Funding disparities** persist (e.g., **Israel’s Arab-Jewish gaps**, **Egypt’s $46 teacher salaries**), with **AI and digital education** emerging as priorities.

#### **Europe**
- **Secularism vs. religious exclusion** (e.g., **France’s laïcité**, **Israel’s Haredi/Arab divides**) and **vocational integration** (e.g., **England’s T Levels**, **Ireland’s Leaving Certificate alternatives**).
- **Equity in special education** (e.g., **England’s SEND funding**, **Ireland’s DEIS schools**) and **flexibility reforms** (e.g., **semester penalties in France**).

#### **France’s Higher Education**
France’s system combines **public universities** (€170–3,770/year) and **selective Grandes Écoles** (5% admit rate, oral exams required). Private programs cost **€7K–€40K+**, with **scholarships for low-income students**. Admissions to **Grandes Écoles** are **intensive**, while **Enseignants-chercheurs** require PhDs. The system reflects **19th-century reforms**, serving **1.65M students**, with **61% in Licence** and **35% in Master**.

### **Key Vocational and Minority Education**
- **Turkey**: **Vocational high schools** with **apprenticeships** (Law 3308), minority schools under **Lausanne Treaty**, and **private international schools**.
- **Nigeria**: **Vocational training (NTC, apprenticeships)** expanding but **underfunded**; **JAMB-based admissions** dominate tertiary education.

---
### **Cross-Country Higher Education Trends**
- **Turkey**: **Highly competitive admissions** (public/private universities), **Türkiye Scholarships** for international students, and **research-focused public universities** (e.g., TÜBİTAK).
- **Nigeria**: **Public-private university mix**, **vocational training gaps**, and **JAMB-based admissions** (scores ≥180 for first-entry).

---
### **Secularism and Discipline**
- **Turkey**: **Strict public school rules** (secularized since 1923) with **mandatory religious education** and **nationalist narratives**.
- **France**: **Laïcité** enforces **secularism**, prohibiting religious symbols (except in **Alsace & Moselle**).

### **Global Education Highlights**
- **AI and digital education** (Sweden’s pilots) and **bilingualism/patriotic reforms** (Hong Kong’s **Putonghua shift**).
- **Decentralization vs. standardization**: **Indonesia’s local autonomy** vs. **Sweden’s unified system**.
