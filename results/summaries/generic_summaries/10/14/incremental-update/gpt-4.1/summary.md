## Summary

Global education systems are shaped by local governance, mandates, and academic/vocational tracks, adapting to sociopolitical and demographic changes. Common global challenges include disparities in quality and equity, funding and teacher shortages, technology integration, vocational training gaps, and discipline concerns.

### Country Highlights

- **Armenia:** Free secondary education, diverse language offerings, innovative higher learning.
- **Norway:** Publicly funded compulsory schooling (ages 6–16), varied secondary tracks, high-performing universities.
- **Indonesia:** From colonial exclusion to decentralized compulsory schooling and diversified pathways.
- **Sweden:** Equitable, compulsory education with flexible upper levels and reforms for integration.
- **Egypt:** Technical/vocational routes, facing challenges in teacher quality, equity, and system efficiency.
- **Hong Kong:** Bilingual, multi-curriculum schooling, competitive exams, collaborative teaching, premier universities.
- **Sri Lanka:** Compulsory schooling to 14, competitive higher education entry, expanding vocational/international options.
- **Ghana:** Decentralized, focused on ICT/equity; TVET access issues for girls and rural youth.
- **Israel:** Distinct tracks for secular, religious, Haredi, and Arab students; resource and inclusion disparities, continual reform.
- **England:** State-funded compulsory schooling, diverse options, strong academic/vocational pathways, robust rankings.
- **Ireland:** Multi-cycle structure, Catholic legacy, support for disadvantaged/special needs, and recent COVID-19 impacts.
- **France:** Centralized, secular, compulsory system with strong vocational sector, regulated private schools, low violence.
- **Nigeria:** Split elementary/secondary system, national exams, diverse tertiary sector, rapid private growth, literacy gaps (rural/northern), growing AI-based distance learning amid challenges.
- **Turkey:** Unified secular “4+4+4”, academic/vocational tracks, standardized exams, accessible public universities, minority and religious curricula, ongoing identity debates.
- **Taiwan:** 12-year compulsory education, high transitions to senior/vocational schools, strong teacher training, rigorous special/Chinese language education.
- **Iran:** Ancient foundations modernized through post-1979 Islamization and reform, with a highly centralized system. Optional pre-primary, compulsory elementary, technical/vocational tracks, and research/inquiry-based curriculum. Education receives about 20% of government spending; private school sector is growing and costly. Entry to higher education is exam-based, with prominent universities. Recent reforms emphasize global knowledge, religious values, and social justice. The curriculum integrates Persian, mandatory Arabic, and other languages; hybrid/online learning has expanded post-pandemic. Teacher and counselor shortages and infrastructure gaps persist. Highly centralized teacher training and higher education enroll about 4.5 million students. Notably, the system excels in science and engineering, with some of the world's highest graduation rates in those fields. Women now comprise over 70% of university students, with Iran leading in female/male primary school enrollment ratios. Selective schools for gifted students and entrepreneurship education are prominent; Iran produces the third most engineers worldwide, with high female STEM participation. Literacy and enrollment have markedly increased over recent decades, supported by substantial government funding. International Baccalaureate schools operate in several cities, and education is guided by Islamic/national values under bodies like the OERP.

### French Higher Education

Composed of public universities, technical institutes, grandes écoles, and regulated private colleges. Students from EU/EEA/Switzerland/Quebec benefit from low tuition and scholarships. The system features tiered faculty, broad access, strong research networks, and an older student demographic.

### Global Trends and Challenges

Universal concerns include expanding access, fostering inclusive and flexible pathways, reforming vocational education, standardized assessments, and bridging divides in quality, teacher development, technology, vocational opportunities, and gender/rural equity. Reform and benchmarking efforts continue amid inequities and evolving modes such as hybrid and distance learning.
