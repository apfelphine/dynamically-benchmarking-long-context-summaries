### **Education in Armenia**

#### **Literacy and Education System**
Armenia boasts a **100% literacy rate** since 1960, though early education was limited by Ottoman and Persian policies. The Soviet era standardized education, emphasizing state control and political alignment. Post-Soviet reforms shifted curricula toward Armenian history and culture, with Armenian becoming the dominant language of instruction. Today, English is rapidly gaining traction, with programs like the Chinese-Armenian Friendship School expanding.

#### **K-12 Education**
- **Preschool**: Ages 2–6, with 808 community, 10 public, and 50 private facilities. Focuses on language development and foundational skills.
- **Secondary Education**: Compulsory and free, structured into primary (grades 1–4), middle (grades 5–9), and high school (grades 10–12). High school tracks include general and specialized education.
- **Exams**: State exams determine university eligibility. Entrance exams assess subject knowledge.
- **School Types**:
  - **Public Schools**: 97% of schools, funded by the state. Curriculum follows national standards.
  - **Private Schools**: Offer specialized programs but rely on tuition, limiting access for low-income families.

#### **Educational Institutions**
Armenia has **26 state universities** and **33 private higher education institutions**, with notable institutions like Yerevan State University and Yerevan State Medical University. Higher education includes programs in physics, mathematics, engineering, medicine, and IT. The country is part of the **European Higher Education Area** and adheres to the **Bologna Process**.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Agreements**: Signed the **Lisbon Recognition Convention** and **Bologna Process**, fostering comparability in higher education qualifications.
- **Student Absenteeism**: Rising absenteeism and dropout rates are linked to child labor and poor education quality, with dropout rates increasing by **250% annually** between 2002 and 2005.
- **After-School Education**: Programs like the **Tumo Center for Creative Technologies** and **Children of Armenia Fund SMART Center** provide 21st-century skills.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **History**
Norway’s education system dates back to **2000 BCE**, with organized schools established in the 11th century. The **folkeskole** (primary school) became mandatory in 1889, later expanded to **9 years** in 1969. The system is **compulsory** from ages 6–16, with **camp school** stays authorized for primary and secondary education.

#### **Education Today**
- **Primary School (barneskole)**: Ages 6–12, focusing on reading, writing, math, and English. No formal grades until Year 6.
- **Lower Secondary School (ungdomsskole)**: Ages 13–16, introducing grading and elective subjects like German, French, and Spanish.
- **Upper Secondary School (videregående skole)**: Ages 16–19, divided into preparatory and vocational tracks. Students take exams for graduation, with **73% graduation rate** as of 2017.
- **Educators**: Titles vary by degree, with preschool teachers requiring a bachelor’s degree, adjuncts needing a bachelor’s in a subject, and lecturers needing a master’s.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Structure**: Universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. **NOKUT** ensures quality.
- **Admission**: Requires a general university admissions certificate or a combination of education and work experience.
- **Language**: Proficiency in Swedish and English is mandatory for international applicants.
- **Student Aid**: Free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens; loans and grants available for others.

#### **Special Education**
- **Inclusive Approach**: Schools must create flexible learning environments for students with disabilities, supported by the **Educational/Psychological Service**.

#### **Grading and Examinations**
- **Grading**: Uses letters (A–F) for final grades, with numeric scales for intermediate assessments.
- **Exams**: Students take exams for graduation, with **trekkfag** (randomly selected subjects) for some students.

#### **Academic Year**
- **Semesters**: Two semesters per year, with breaks including **summer, Christmas, fall, and winter holidays**.

### **Education in Indonesia**

#### **History**
- **Islamic Kingdoms Period**: Islamic boarding schools (**Pondok Pesantren**) emerged, offering religious and secular education.
- **Dutch Colonial Period**: Formal education was introduced, segregated by social status. Schools included **Sekolah Rakjat** (folk schools) and **Desa Schools** for rural populations.
- **Post-Independence**: Education became free and compulsory, with a focus on nationalism and Pancasila principles. The **Universal Basic Education (UBE)** program aimed to improve access and quality.

#### **Early Education**
- **Pre-school (PAUD)**: Covers **Taman Bermain** (playgroup) and **Taman Kanak-Kanak** (kindergarten). Compulsory for ages 2–6, with **Class A** and **Class B** divisions.

#### **Public Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory**: 12 years of schooling, free for primary and middle school. High school fees are minimal.
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on secular knowledge, Pancasila principles, and vocational training. Rote learning and teacher authority are emphasized.
- **Types of Schools**:
  - **Public Schools**: Free, state-run.
  - **Private Schools**: Offer alternative curricula, including international diplomas (e.g., American High School Diploma).
  - **Islamic Schools**: Include **pesantren**, **madrasah**, and **sekolah islam**, with religious curricula.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Institutions**: Universities, institutes, academies, and conservatories. **KKNI (Indonesian National Qualification Framework)** organizes qualifications.
- **Vocational Education**: Includes **politeknik** (technical colleges) and **akademi** (vocational training).

#### **Challenges**
- **Quality and Access**: High dropout rates, especially in rural areas. **Private tutoring** is widespread due to low public education quality.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women’s education remains underrepresented, with cultural and economic barriers.
- **Digital Literacy**: Limited access to technology, hindering digital education initiatives.

### **Education in Hong Kong**

#### **History**
- **Early Schools**: Missionary-run schools in the 19th century, later expanded by the British government.
- **Post-Handover**: Shift to Chinese-medium instruction, with **EMI (English Medium Instruction)** schools remaining prestigious.
- **Recent Reforms**: Introduction of **Citizenship and Social Development** subject, field trips to mainland China, and AI integration in education.

#### **Pre-school Education**
- **Non-Free**: Parents pay fees, with subsidies for Hong Kong residents under the **Pre-primary Education Voucher Scheme**.

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory**: From age 6 to 18. **Primary 1–6** (ages 6–12), **Secondary 1–6** (ages 12–18).
- **School Types**:
  - **Government Schools**: Free, state-funded.
  - **Aided Schools**: Subsidized by the government.
  - **Private Schools**: Higher fees, specialized programs.
  - **International Schools**: Offer curricula from foreign countries (e.g., British, American, IB).

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **8 UGC-funded universities** (e.g., HKU, CUHK, HKUST), with **English as the medium of instruction**.
- **Admission**: **JUPAS (Joint University Programmes Admissions System)** for local students; international students apply directly.
- **Tuition**: Bachelor’s degrees awarded with honors (first, second upper, second lower, third class).

#### **Special Education**
- **Integrated Schools**: Schools for students with disabilities, though some face challenges due to discrimination.

#### **International Education**
- **175 International Schools**: Offer curricula from the UK, US, France, and Germany. Popular among expatriates and wealthy families.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Administration**
- **Ministries**: Primary education (Ministry of Education), higher education (Ministry of Higher Education), and vocational training (Ministry of Skills Development).
- **National Institute of Education (NIE)**: Provides leadership for general education.

#### **History**
- **Pre-Colonial**: Buddhist and monastic education in temples and **pirivenas**.
- **Colonial Era**: British introduced standardized schools, with **Colebrooke Commission (1836)** setting the foundation for the modern system.
- **Post-Independence**: Free and compulsory education introduced, with a focus on national languages (Sinhala and Tamil).

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Structure**: Five stages—primary, junior secondary, senior secondary, collegiate, and tertiary.
- **Compulsory**: Until Grade 9 (age 14). Students must pass the **GCE O/L** (Ordinary Level) exam.
- **School Types**:
  - **Government Schools**: Free, state-funded.
  - **Private Schools**: Offer international curricula (e.g., IGCSE, IB).
  - **Madrasas**: Islamic education, with **205 registered madrasas**.
  - **International Schools**: Prepare students for exams like IGCSE and Cambridge.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: **15 state universities** (e.g., University of Colombo, University of Peradeniya). Admission based on **GCE A/L** exam.
- **Vocational Education**: Managed by the **Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission**. Includes **National Vocational Qualifications Systems (NVQSL)**.

#### **Challenges**
- **Competition**: High pressure on exams (e.g., Grade 5, GCE O/L, GCE A/L).
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and lack of incentives attract unqualified teachers.
- **Brain Drain**: Many graduates leave for better opportunities abroad.

### **Education in Ghana**

#### **History**
- **Pre-Colonial**: Informal education through apprenticeships and oral traditions.
- **Colonial Era**: Missionary schools introduced, with **Colebrooke Commission (1836)** recommending standardized education.
- **Post-Independence**: Free and compulsory primary education (1961), followed by **Free, Compulsory and Universal Basic Education (FCUBE)**.

#### **Structure**
- **Basic Education**: 12 years (ages 4–15). Includes **kindergarten (2 years)**, **primary school (6 years)**, and **junior high school (3 years)**.
- **Secondary Education**: **Senior High School (SHS)** for academic or vocational programs. **WASSCE (West African Senior School Certificate Examination)** determines university eligibility.
- **Tertiary Education**: **Universities (6 public, 49 private)**, polytechnics, and colleges of education. Admission based on **WASSCE** scores.

#### **Private Education and Partnerships**
- **Private Schools**: Over **74% of primary and junior secondary schools** are private. Parents contribute to fees, textbooks, and uniforms.
- **Public-Private Partnerships**: Schools receive subsidies from the government, with parents covering additional costs.

#### **Challenges**
- **Quality and Access**: High dropout rates, especially in rural areas. **Vocational education** remains underdeveloped.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women underrepresented in higher education due to cultural and economic barriers.
- **Teacher Training**: Colleges of education (CoE) train teachers, but many lack qualifications or teaching experience.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **Schools: Tiers and Tracks**
- **Primary Education**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12).
- **Middle School**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15).
- **High School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18). Compulsory until age 18.
- **Tracks**:
  - **State-Secular**: General education.
  - **State-Religious**: Intensive Jewish studies.
  - **Independent Religious**: Focus on Torah study.
  - **Arab**: Teaches in Arabic, emphasizing Arab history and culture.

#### **Matriculation (Bagrut)**
- **Exams**: Cover Hebrew, English, mathematics, scripture, history, civics, and literature. Students must pass at least 21 units to receive a full matriculation certificate.
- **Impact**: Determines admission to universities, military units, and job prospects.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **9 public universities** (e.g., Tel Aviv University, Hebrew University). Admission based on **bagrut scores** and **Psychometric Entrance Test**.
- **Rankings**: Top-ranked in Asia, with strong performance in science and engineering.
- **Arab Sector**: Limited resources, with **33% of Jews** holding a college degree compared to **18% of Christians**.

#### **Challenges**
- **Discipline**: Large class sizes, low wages, and teacher unions hinder reforms.
- **Textbooks**: Criticized for promoting negative stereotypes of Arabs.
- **Strikes**: Frequent protests over tuition fees and teacher conditions.

### **Education in England**

#### **History**
- **Middle Ages**: Schools for Latin and religious education. Universities of Oxford and Cambridge established.
- **19th Century**: Reforms expanded education provision, introducing state-funded schools. **Education Act 1918** abolished fees for elementary schools.

#### **Legally Compulsory Education**
- **Compulsory**: Ages 5–18. Includes **Foundation Stage (ages 3–5)**, **Key Stages 1–4**, and **post-16 education**.
- **School Types**:
  - **Academies**: Free schools, non-local authority control.
  - **Community Schools**: State-funded, local authority control.
  - **Private Schools**: Fee-paying, regulated by **Independent Schools Inspectorate (ISI)**.

#### **National Curriculum**
- **Core Subjects**: English, mathematics, science, art, citizenship, computing, design and technology, languages, geography, history, music, physical education, religious education.
- **Post-16 Education**: **GCSEs** for ages 14–16, followed by **A-levels** or vocational qualifications.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Public universities** (e.g., Oxford, Cambridge) and private colleges. **Tuition fees**: Up to **£9,535/year** for undergraduates.
- **Grading**: Uses **Grade Point Average (GPA)** and **letter grades (A–F)**.

#### **Adult Education**
- **Continuing Education**: Offers vocational and academic qualifications, including **access courses** for adults without suitable qualifications.

### **Education in the Republic of Ireland**

#### **History**
- **1966**: Free upper second-level education introduced.
- **1973**: Irish language requirement for second-level certificates abandoned.

#### **Structure**
- **Compulsory**: Ages 5–16 or until completion of three years of second-level education.
- **School Types**:
  - **National Schools**: Under diocesan patronage.
  - **Gaelscoileanna**: Irish-language schools.
  - **Multidenominational Schools**: Open to all religions.
  - **Preparatory Schools**: Independent, fee-charging.

#### **Primary Education**
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on **spiritual, moral, cultural, mental, and physical development**.
- **Exams**: **Primary Certificate Examination** (1929–1967).

#### **Secondary Education**
- **Junior Cycle**: Junior Certificate Examination after three years.
- **Transition Year**: Optional one-year course for ages 15–16.
- **Senior Cycle**: Leaving Certificate Examination after two years.

#### **Third-Level Education**
- **Universities**: **Top 20 globally** (US News rankings). Most courses taught in English, with few Irish-language options.

#### **Special Needs Education**
- **Special Needs Assistants (SNAs)**: Support students with disabilities.

#### **Areas of Disadvantage**
- **Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools (DEIS)**: Provides additional resources for disadvantaged schools.

### **Education in France**

#### **History**
- **Napoleon and Jules Ferry**: Modernized education systems, making education **free, compulsory, and secular**.
- **19th Century**: Focus on French literature and history to promote national identity.

#### **Governance**
- **Ministry of National Education**: Regulates all educational programs.
- **School Types**:
  - **Public Schools**: Free, state-funded.
  - **Private Schools**: Under contract (same curriculum) or without contract (own curriculum).

#### **School Year**
- **September to July**: Standardized calendar across France.
- **Holidays**: Easter, Christmas, winter, spring, and summer breaks.

#### **Primary School**
- **Maternelle**: Ages 3–6, introduces basic arithmetic, reading, and social skills.
- **École Élémentaire**: Ages 6–10, covers French, mathematics, science, and arts.

#### **Middle and High School**
- **Collège**: Ages 11–15, prepares for **baccalauréat**.
- **Lycée**: Ages 15–18, offers **baccalauréat général, technologique, or professionnel**.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Licence (bachelor’s)**, **Master’s**, and **Doctorat**.
- **Grandes Écoles**: Elite institutions (e.g., HEC Paris, Sciences Po) with highly selective admissions.
- **Tuition**: Low for EU/EEA students (€170–380/year); higher for international students.

#### **Foreign Languages**
- **English**: Most common foreign language, taught from 2nd grade.
- **Other Languages**: German, French, Spanish, Arabic (since 2016).

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **Primary Education**
- **Compulsory**: Ages 5–12. **Universal Primary Education (UPE)** program (1976–1999) made education free and compulsory.
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on mathematics, English, religious knowledge, and indigenous languages.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **Federal-Sponsored**: **Unity Schools**, funded by the federal government.
- **State-Sponsored**: State-owned schools with limited resources.
- **Private Schools**: High fees, better facilities, and qualified teachers.
- **Promotional Exams**: **SSCE (Senior Secondary Certificate Examination)** determines university eligibility.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: **153 universities** (40 federal, 45 state, 68 private). Admission based on **JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board)** scores.
- **Polytechnics and Colleges**: Offer vocational and technical education.

#### **Vocational Education**
- **National Board for Technical Education**: Oversees vocational training.
- **Challenges**: Shortage of teachers, outdated curriculum, and lack of industry demand.

#### **Female Education**
- **Progress**: Increasing enrollment, but challenges remain due to poverty, cultural barriers, and militancy groups.

### **Education in Turkey**

#### **History**
- **Founding of the Republic**: **Law of Integration of Education (1924)** unified schools under state control.
- **Atatürk’s Reforms**: Secularism, Latin alphabet, and modern curricula.

#### **Pre-primary and Primary Education**
- **Pre-primary**: Ages 3–6, optional.
- **Primary School**: 8 years, compulsory. Core subjects: Turkish, mathematics, science, and foreign languages.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **General Secondary**: Ages 15–17, prepares for university.
- **Vocational Secondary**: Technical and professional training.
- **New System (4+4+4)**: 4 years primary, 4 years middle school, 4 years secondary.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **167 universities** (53 state, 23 private). Admission via **YÖS (University Entrance Exam)**.
- **Grandes Écoles**: Elite institutions with highly selective admissions.
- **International Students**: Popular due to affordable tuition and easier accessibility.

#### **Religious Education**
- **Islamic Studies**: Introduced in 4th grade, continues through high school.
- **Criticism**: Textbooks promote **Atatürk’s thought** and **Armenian genocide denial**.

#### **Foreign Languages**
- **English**: Most common, taught from 2nd grade.
- **Other Languages**: German, French, Arabic (since 2016).

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **History**
- **Colonial Era**: Dutch, Ming loyalists, and Qing China implemented education systems.
- **Post-1949**: Republic of China (ROC) government sought to assimilate Taiwanese population.

#### **Public Education**
- **Compulsory**: Ages 6–15 (12 years). **National Education Act** introduced in 2011.
- **School Structure**:
  - **Elementary School**: Grades 1–6.
  - **Junior High School**: Grades 7–9.
  - **Senior High School**: Grades 10–12.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Public (e.g., National Taiwan University)** and **Private (e.g., Fu Jen Catholic University)**.
- **Vocational Education**: Junior colleges, institutes of technology, and universities of science and technology.
- **International Programs**: English-language courses for international students.

#### **Challenges**
- **Cram Schools**: Popular for supplementary education, criticized for cultural overemphasis on academic achievement.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low wages and high workloads attract unqualified teachers.

### **Education in Iran**

#### **History**
- **Pre-Islamic**: Focus on religion, administration, and arts.
- **Safavid Era**: First modern education system, blending Islamic and scientific values.
- **Qajar Era**: **Educational Committee** promoted educational reform.

#### **Grades and Structure**
- **Pre-primary**: Ages 5–6, optional.
- **Elementary School**: Ages 6–12, compulsory.
- **Secondary School**: Ages 12–18, non-compulsory.
- **University**: 4 years for bachelor’s, 2 years for master’s, 3–4 years for PhD.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **16 universities** (e.g., University of Tehran, Sharif University). Admission via **Konkour exam**.
- **Islamic Azad University**: Private, tuition-based.
- **Starred Students**: Students charged with religious or political convictions.

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: 60,000 teachers needed; 950,000 teachers currently employed.
- **Privatization**: Rising costs of private schools, with public schools viewed as weak.
- **Mandatory Islamic Prayer**: Introduced in 2023 for schoolchildren.

#### **Women in Education**
- **Progress**: Women now outnumber men in universities. **UNESCO** ranks Iran as having the highest female-to-male ratio in primary education.
