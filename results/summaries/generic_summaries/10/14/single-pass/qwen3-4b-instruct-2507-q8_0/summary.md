### **Education in Armenia**
- **Literacy**: 100% literacy reported since 1960; historically low numeracy in Ottoman/Persian eras. Soviet system emphasized state control and integration with politics, economy, and culture. Free and compulsory primary and secondary education.
- **K–12 Education**: 
  - **Preschool**: 2–6 years; includes nursery (ages 2–3) and kindergarten (ages 3–6/7). Focuses on communication, language, and foundational math.
  - **Secondary**: 12-year structure (primary, middle, high school). High school offers general or specialized tracks (technical, artistic, vocational).
- **Exams & Admissions**: State exams determine university eligibility. Applications require transcripts, ID, and sometimes recommendation letters.
- **School Types**: 
  - **Public schools** (97% of schools): State-funded, follow national curriculum, require teacher qualifications.
  - **Private schools**: Offer specialized programs, more flexible curricula, but face equity issues due to tuition-based access.
- **Higher Education**: 
  - Armenia has 26 state universities (including 4 international) and 33 private institutions.
  - Key institutions: Yerevan State University, Yerevan State Medical University (YSMU), and others.
  - Joined the **Bologna Process** and **Lisbon Recognition Convention**; expanded international partnerships.
- **Student Absenteeism**: Rapidly growing due to child labor and poor education quality; dropout rates increased 250% from 2002–2005.
- **After-School Education**: Technology-based centers (e.g., Tumo Center) offer 21st-century skills and creativity.

### **Education in Norway**
- **History**: Evolved from cathedral schools (1153) to mandatory *folkeskole* (1827). Reformed in 1968 with *grunnskole* (foundation school).
- **Structure**: 
  - **Grundskole** (ages 6–16): Mandatory, includes primary and lower secondary.
  - **Videregående skole** (ages 16–19): Optional; includes general or vocational tracks.
- **Grading**: 1–6 scale (6 best, 2 lowest passing); final grades used for university admissions.
- **Education Today**:
  - **Primary/Secondary**: Emphasis on social studies, science, and foreign languages (e.g., French, German, Spanish).
  - **Grades**: Students take national exams in grades 3, 6, and 9.
- **Private Schools**: Limited; must follow national curriculum or offer religious/pedagogical alternatives. Heavily grant-aided.
- **Higher Education**:
  - Part of **Bologna Process** and **European Higher Education Area**.
  - **Quality Assurance**: Joined ENQA and EQAR; established national standards.
  - **Erasmus+**: Active participation in student and staff mobility programs.
- **Special Education**: Government policy emphasizes inclusion; special education services are available but face challenges in implementation.

### **Education in Indonesia**
- **History**: 
  - Islamic *Pesantren* schools emerged during colonial times; Dutch system introduced segregation.
  - Post-independence, the government promoted secular education and Pancasila (national values).
- **Structure**:
  - **Pre-school (PAUD)**: 2–6 years; private majority (99.35%).
  - **Primary (SD)**: 6 years (ages 6–12); free and compulsory.
  - **Middle School (SMP)**: 3 years (ages 12–15); includes accelerated tracks.
  - **High School (SMA)**: 3 years (ages 15–18); includes vocational (SMK) and general tracks.
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on Pancasila, rote learning, and teacher authority. Religious education in Islamic schools.
- **Schools**: 
  - Public schools dominate (90.29% of primary schools).
  - Private schools offer international curricula (e.g., British, American, IB).
- **Higher Education**:
  - 26 state universities and 33 private institutions.
  - Follows **Indonesian National Qualification Framework (KKNI)**.
  - Low tertiary participation (36.31%); challenges in quality and access.
- **Challenges**: 
  - Teacher shortages, high student-to-teacher ratios, low teacher status, and reliance on rote learning.
  - Significant regional disparities and gender gaps in access and outcomes.

### **Education in Sweden**
- **Structure**: 
  - **Grundskola** (ages 6–16): Mandatory, 9-year comprehensive school.
  - **Gymnasieskola** (ages 16–19): Elective, 3-year program with 18 national programs.
- **Curriculum**: 
  - Compulsory subjects: Swedish, math, English, history, geography, religion, physical education.
  - Students choose electives (e.g., arts, sciences, business).
- **Grading**: A–F scale; final scores used for university admissions.
- **Schools**: 
  - **Public schools**: State-funded, follow national curriculum.
  - **Private/Independent schools (friskolor)**: Privately run, public funding via tuition; growing influence.
- **Higher Education**:
  - Fully integrated into **Bologna Process**.
  - Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.
  - Strong emphasis on research and quality assurance.
- **Challenges**: 
  - Criticism of excessive homework and rote learning.
  - Debate over the role of private schools and their impact on equity.

### **Education in Egypt**
- **History**: 
  - Ancient Egypt: Schools linked to temples and religious institutions.
  - Colonial era: Introduction of Western-style schools; elite access only.
  - Post-independence: Free public education introduced; expansion of access.
- **Structure**:
  - **Basic Education**: 9 years (ages 4–14); free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary Education**: 3 years (ages 15–17); includes general and technical tracks.
  - **Higher Education**: 3–5 years; includes public and private institutions.
- **Curriculum**: 
  - Emphasis on national values, religion, and rote learning.
  - National exams (GCE O/L and A/L) determine university eligibility.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 30% of eligible youth attend university; only ~50% graduate.
  - Private institutions offer international curricula (e.g., American, British).
- **Challenges**:
  - Teacher shortages and low teacher salaries.
  - High dropout rates, especially among rural and poor students.
  - Poor quality of public education; widespread use of private tutoring.
  - Gender disparities in access and outcomes.

### **Education in Hong Kong**
- **Structure**:
  - **Pre-school**: 3–6 years; not free; government subsidies available.
  - **Primary (ages 6–12)**: 6 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 12–18)**: 6 years; includes junior and senior secondary.
- **Curriculum**: 
  - Medium of instruction: English (in most schools), Chinese (Cantonese or Putonghua), or bilingual.
  - Emphasis on academic performance and exam success (e.g., HKDSE).
- **School Types**:
  - **Government/aided schools**: Free, state-funded.
  - **Private/International schools**: Offer international curricula (e.g., IB, A-levels); high fees.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 8 UGC-funded universities (e.g., HKU, CUHK, HKUST).
  - Strong international ties and global rankings.
- **Challenges**:
  - High student workload and pressure.
  - Growing gap in access between public and private schools.
  - Post-2019 national security law led to declining enrollment in elite schools.
  - Criticism of curriculum shifts toward patriotism and national security.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**
- **History**: 
  - Pre-colonial: Buddhist *pirivenas* as centers of learning.
  - Colonial era: Mission schools introduced; British curriculum.
  - Post-independence: Nationalization of schools; emphasis on national languages.
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 4–14)**: 5 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 14–18)**: 4 years (middle school) + 2 years (senior secondary).
  - **Tertiary**: 3–5 years; includes public and private universities.
- **School Types**:
  - **Government schools**: Majority; under local authority.
  - **Private schools**: Offer English-medium education; high fees.
  - **Islamic schools (madrasas)**: Teach Islamic studies; 205 registered.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 15 state universities; limited access due to high competition.
  - Admission based on GCE A/L exam; only ~16% of applicants gain admission.
- **Challenges**:
  - Gender disparities in access and outcomes.
  - Rural-urban educational inequality.
  - Low teacher salaries and poor infrastructure.
  - High dropout rates in rural areas.

### **Education in Israel**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 6–12)**: 6 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Middle (ages 12–15)**: 3 years.
  - **High School (ages 15–18)**: 3 years; includes general, technical, and religious tracks.
- **School Types**:
  - **State-secular, state-religious, Haredi, Arab** schools.
  - Haredi schools offer minimal secular education; low workforce participation.
- **Higher Education**:
  - High university enrollment; strong focus on STEM.
  - Mandatory military service; some students defer to attend university.
- **Exams**: 
  - **Bagrut**: National exam determining university eligibility.
- **Challenges**:
  - Gender disparities in STEM fields.
  - Arab student underrepresentation and underfunding.
  - Criticism of religious bias in curriculum and textbooks.

### **Education in England**
- **Structure**:
  - **Early Years (3–5)**: Free preschool; 600 hours/year.
  - **Primary (ages 5–11)**: 6 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 11–18)**: 4–5 years; includes comprehensive, selective, and vocational tracks.
- **School Types**:
  - **State-funded**: 93% of students; include academies, community, foundation, and voluntary schools.
  - **Private schools**: 7% of students; offer non-national curricula.
- **Higher Education**:
  - University degrees (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD) available; tuition fees introduced in 1998.
  - Strong international student presence.
- **Challenges**:
  - High student workload and exam pressure.
  - Inequality in access due to socioeconomic status.
  - Teacher shortages and low salaries.

### **Education in the Republic of Ireland**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 4–12)**: 8 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 12–18)**: 6 years; includes Junior Cycle (3 years), Transition Year (1 year), and Senior Cycle (2 years).
- **School Types**:
  - **National, Gaelscoil (Irish-medium), multi-denominational, preparatory**.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 12-year system; strong international rankings.
  - Focus on holistic development and inclusion.
- **Challenges**:
  - Religious discrimination in school admissions.
  - Rural-urban disparities in access and quality.
  - Underfunding in rural areas.

### **Education in France**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 3–11)**: 8 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Middle/High School (ages 11–18)**: 6 years; includes general and vocational tracks.
- **School Types**:
  - **Public, private (under contract), private without contract**.
- **Higher Education**:
  - **Licence, Master, Doctorat** degrees; Bologna Process-aligned.
  - **Grandes écoles**: Elite institutions; highly selective.
  - **Private schools**: Offer international curricula; high fees.
- **Challenges**:
  - Religious instruction in some regions.
  - Curriculum debates on secularism and national identity.
  - Teacher shortages and low salaries.

### **Education in Nigeria**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 5–11)**: 6 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 11–18)**: 6 years; includes JSS and SSS.
- **Exams**: 
  - **SSCE (Senior Secondary Certificate Examination)**; administered by WAEC.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 153 universities (40 federal, 45 state, 68 private).
  - Admission via JAMB or IJMB/JUPEB.
- **Challenges**:
  - Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low funding.
  - High dropout rates in rural areas.
  - Gender disparities in access and outcomes.

### **Education in Turkey**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 6–14)**: 8 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 14–18)**: 4 years; includes general, vocational, and technical tracks.
- **School Types**:
  - Public, private, and religious schools.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 167 universities; public and private.
  - Strong focus on engineering and technology.
- **Challenges**:
  - Religious content in curriculum (e.g., evolution denial, jihad).
  - Textbook bias and political influence.
  - Teacher shortages and low pay.

### **Education in Taiwan**
- **Structure**:
  - **12-year compulsory education** (ages 3–18).
  - **Primary (ages 6–12)**: 6 years; includes English and native languages.
  - **Junior High (ages 12–14)**: 3 years; prepares for national exams.
  - **Senior High (ages 15–18)**: 3 years; includes science and liberal arts tracks.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 30+ public and private universities; strong in engineering and medicine.
  - High university entrance rate (>95%).
- **Challenges**:
  - Intense academic pressure and exam culture.
  - Cram schools and private tutoring dominate.

### **Education in Iran**
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary (ages 5–12)**: 7 years; free and compulsory.
  - **Secondary (ages 12–18)**: 3 years; includes theoretical, technical, and vocational tracks.
- **Higher Education**:
  - 92 universities; strong in engineering and science.
  - Admission via national *Konkur* exam.
- **Challenges**:
  - Teacher shortages (60,000 needed).
  - Religious influence in curriculum (e.g., mandatory prayer, anti-evolution).
  - Rising cost of private education; inequality.
  - Women's education has improved significantly, but gender gaps persist.
