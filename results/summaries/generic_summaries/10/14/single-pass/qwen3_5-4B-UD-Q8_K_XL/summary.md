# Global Education Systems: A Comparative Summary

The provided text details the educational landscapes of Armenia, Norway, Indonesia, Sweden, Egypt, Hong Kong, Sri Lanka, Ghana, Israel, England, Ireland, France, Nigeria, Turkey, Taiwan, and Iran. These systems vary significantly in governance, funding, cultural emphasis, and historical development, yet all share common goals of literacy, economic development, and social integration.

### **Governance, Structure, and Funding**
Most nations employ a mix of state and private institutions, though the balance differs greatly.
*   **Centralized vs. Decentralized:** Systems like **Armenia**, **Egypt**, **Turkey**, and **Iran** are highly centralized, with the Ministry of Education controlling curricula, admissions, and teacher training. In contrast, **Ghana** and **Nigeria** utilize decentralized models involving district assemblies or state governments, while **Sweden**, **England**, and **Ireland** feature robust local authority involvement.
*   **Compulsory Education:** Nearly all listed countries mandate primary and secondary education. **Armenia**, **Sweden**, **Norway**, and **Taiwan** have extended compulsory education to 12 years. **Egypt** and **Iran** also mandate 12 years. **Nigeria** follows a 9-year basic education model (6 primary + 3 junior secondary), while **Indonesia** and **Sri Lanka** require 12 years of schooling.
*   **Funding Models:** Public funding dominates in **Norway**, **Sweden**, and **France**, where tuition is often free or minimal for citizens. **Egypt** and **Nigeria** face challenges with funding gaps, leading to reliance on private tutoring and international aid. **Iran** and **Turkey** experience rising costs in private education, creating a "wealth gap" in access. **Hong Kong** and **Singapore** (implied via context) rely heavily on fees for private/international schools, contrasting with the low-cost public system.

### **Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Language**
Curricular approaches reflect local history, religion, and economic needs.
*   **Language of Instruction:** **Armenia** shifted from Russian to Armenian after the Soviet collapse. **Indonesia** and **Sri Lanka** use local languages (Indonesian/Sinhala/Tamil) as primary mediums, though English is growing. **Hong Kong** and **Taiwan** use Mandarin or English as primary mediums, with **France** enforcing strict secularism (*laïcité*) and **Turkey** promoting Turkish nationalism and Atatürk's values.
*   **Pedagogical Styles:** **Egypt** and **Indonesia** are criticized for rote learning and teacher-centered instruction. **Hong Kong** is known for high-pressure exam preparation ("cram schools") and intense competition. **Sweden** and **Norway** emphasize equity, play-based learning, and student choice. **Israel** values high academic achievement and scientific literacy, while **Turkey** has faced criticism for curriculum alterations regarding history and science.
*   **Religious Influence:** **Indonesia**, **Sri Lanka**, and **Turkey** integrate religion significantly into their school systems, with **Al-Azhar** in **Egypt** and **Madrasas** in **Sri Lanka** operating alongside secular schools. **France** and **Turkey** (post-1927) have historically banned religious instruction in public schools, though **Turkey** reintroduced it in 1956.

### **Higher Education and International Context**
*   **Access and Quality:** **Israel**, **Sweden**, and **Norway** boast high tertiary enrollment rates and strong research outputs. **Iran** and **Turkey** attract a large number of international students due to affordable costs. **Nigeria** and **Egypt** struggle with high dropout rates and unemployment among graduates.
*   **International Agreements:** **Armenia**, **Norway**, **Sweden**, and **Turkey** are active members of the Bologna Process and Erasmus programs, facilitating European mobility. **Hong Kong** and **Singapore** (contextual) serve as hubs for international curricula (IB, IGCSE).
*   **Challenges:** Common challenges include **teacher shortages** in **Iran**, **Nigeria**, and **Sri Lanka**; **inequality** based on gender and wealth in **Egypt**, **Ghana**, and **Nigeria**; and **political instability** affecting **Nigeria**, **Egypt**, and **Turkey**.

### **Key Regional Highlights**
*   **Europe:** Characterized by secularism, high spending, and a focus on equity (Sweden, Norway, France, UK, Ireland).
*   **Middle East & North Africa:** Blends of tradition and modernity, with varying degrees of religious integration and challenges in infrastructure (Egypt, Iran, Turkey, Israel).
*   **Asia:** Diverse systems ranging from highly competitive and exam-oriented (Hong Kong, Taiwan, Sri Lanka) to rapidly expanding and developing (Indonesia, Nigeria, Ghana).
*   **Africa:** Focus on universal access and basic education, with significant growth in enrollment but persistent issues with quality and funding (Ghana, Nigeria).
