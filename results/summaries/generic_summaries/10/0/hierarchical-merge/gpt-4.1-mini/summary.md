# Global Education Systems Overview

## Northern and Eastern Europe, Hong Kong, and Middle East

Northern and Eastern European countries like Sweden, Norway, and Armenia emphasize free, compulsory education with structured academic and vocational pathways. Sweden offers play-based preschool and near-universal upper secondary education aligned with the Bologna Process; Norway focuses on social development, apprenticeships, and digital integration; Armenia mandates 12 years of schooling with active participation in European frameworks but struggles with attendance due to economic issues. Hong Kong combines voluntary kindergarten with compulsory primary and secondary education, promotes bilingual instruction (Cantonese, English, Putonghua), and expands higher education with recent reforms fostering patriotism and interactive learning.

In the Middle East, Iran and Turkey present centrally managed systems blending religious and secular education. Iran combines Islamic curricula with modern STEM and vocational training, boasting high female university participation but facing regional disparities and rigid enforcement. Turkey delivers 12 years of compulsory education incorporating secular and religious streams, emphasizing nationalism and vocational apprenticeships amid curricular fluctuations. Egypt’s system confronts quality and infrastructure challenges despite high primary enrollment, while Indonesia mandates 12 years of schooling across secular and Islamic streams, addressing equity and teacher quality through reforms.

## Western Europe and East Asia

England requires education from ages 5 to 18, with diverse school types under a National Curriculum culminating in GCSEs, A-levels, and vocational qualifications such as T-levels. It supports special education and reforms via academies. France operates a centralized, secular system from preschool to lycée leading to the baccalauréat and offers accessible higher education through universities and selective Grandes Écoles, maintaining low school violence despite demographic shifts.

Taiwan mandates 12 years of schooling with academic and vocational tracks, integrating Mandarin, English, and native languages. It features strong vocational education and a robust private cram school sector. Government investments focus on early childhood education and special services, attracting many international students, especially from mainland China.

Ireland’s education system spans primary to secondary levels, traditionally dominated by Catholic schools but expanding multidenominational and Irish-language options. Its rigorous Leaving Certificate program achieves high completion rates and above-average OECD performance, complemented by support for disadvantaged and special education, though challenges persist in reducing religious influence and urban admissions equity.

## South Asia, West Africa, Israel, and Nigeria

Sri Lanka offers free education through five stages with national exams guiding university admission. Despite tuition-free state universities, private tuition is widespread to prepare for exams. Ghana delivers 12 years of basic education plus senior high and vocational schools, with ongoing teacher training and ICT integration to mitigate socioeconomic and access disparities accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Israel features compulsory education through grade 12 divided among secular, religious, Arab, and Haredi sectors. Despite high funding and tertiary attainment, disparities remain, particularly in minority communities. Women excel academically but remain underrepresented in STEM faculties. Challenges include teacher strikes and politically sensitive content.

Nigeria’s 9-3-4 system integrates free basic education, senior secondary, and tertiary levels across federal, state, and private schools. It incorporates academic, vocational, and technical pathways, overseen by national boards. Although literacy rates improve, systemic issues like underfunding, teacher shortages, security concerns, and cultural barriers—especially for female education—persist. Emerging efforts explore AI to enhance education quality and access.

---

Overall, global education systems exhibit common features of compulsory schooling, national curricula, and a mix of vocational and academic pathways. Challenges across regions include equity, quality assurance, modernization, and balancing cultural or religious influences within evolving political and socioeconomic contexts.
