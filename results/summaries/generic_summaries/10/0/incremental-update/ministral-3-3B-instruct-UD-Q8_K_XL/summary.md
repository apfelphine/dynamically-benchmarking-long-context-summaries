### **Comparative Education Systems: A Global Overview**

Sweden and Hong Kong prioritize **high-stakes exams and global competitiveness**, while Ghana’s system struggles with **budget constraints and fragmented vocational training**, and Norway’s model emphasizes **play-based, inclusive learning**. Historical influences shape diverse systems—Armenia’s **Soviet-era vocational diversification**, England’s **93% state-funded schools**, and Indonesia’s **12-year compulsory system** with **anti-discriminatory secular nationalism**, though it remains **fragmented** (including Islamic/Pesantren schools). France’s **Napoleonic-era reforms** established a **uniform secular curriculum** across **31 académies**, while Egypt blends **ancient Islamic traditions** (*al-Azhar*) with modern reforms. Turkey’s **12-year compulsory education** enforces **strict secularism**, though controversial narratives in textbooks require critical engagement. Sri Lanka’s system, rooted in **colonial-era English-medium schools**, evolved into a **multi-religious, multi-ministerial framework**, with **749 Muslim schools, 205 madrasas, and semi-government institutions**.

Iran’s system reflects **ancient Persian traditions**, with Safavid reforms blending **Islamic values and science**, and post-revolutionary expansions including **gender-segregated schools and mandatory Islamic observance**. Israel’s **tripartite system**—**state-secular, state-religious, and Haredi schools**—exhibits disparities, particularly for **Arab Israelis**, while Taiwan’s **12-year compulsory education** integrates **Mandarin, exam-driven junior high, and vocational pathways**. The **Republic of Ireland** stands out globally, ranking **2nd in reading and 11th in maths (Pisa 2022)**, with a **broad-based secondary system** (Junior/Senior Cycle) and high retention rates, supported by **special needs education** and the **DEIS scheme**.

### **Nigeria’s Education System: Challenges and Reforms**

Nigeria’s **6-3-3-4 system** (primary: 6 years, junior secondary: 3 years, senior secondary: 3 years, tertiary: 4 years) is under review for a proposed **9-3-4 transition**, with **primary education** covering core subjects like **Math, English, Religious Knowledge, and Science**, and admission to secondary school via the **Common Entrance Exam**. Tertiary education requires **SSCE/GCE credits, JAMB ≥180, or Merit Pass in NCE/ND**, with **153 universities** (43 federal, 47 state, 68 private) offering **4–6-year programs**. Vocational education, though **informal and underdeveloped**, offers **NTC/ANTC certificates** via **NABTEB**, but faces **teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and political resistance**.

**Vocational and Informal Education:**
- **Apprenticeships** (legal for children <15) foster skills but are hindered by **political instability**.
- **Informal programs** (e.g., **Premier Reading Club**) improve literacy and employment, though **funding gaps** persist.

**Literacy and Gender Disparities:**
- **2022 literacy rate: 69%** (31% illiteracy), with **North-East/North-West** facing higher out-of-school rates due to **poverty, insecurity, and child marriage**.
- **Female education progress** correlates with **GDP and life expectancy**, but **gender bias, teen pregnancy, and extremism** (e.g., **Boko Haram**) disrupt access.
- **UNICEF’s GEP3-CTP** (2014–2016) boosted girls’ enrollment in **Niger & Sokoto States** via cash transfers.

**Rural and Digital Literacy:**
- **Poor infrastructure** in rural schools limits **staff, funding, and security**, despite national policies.
- **Government efforts (2024)** prioritize **functional literacy** and **digital literacy** (targeting **95% by 2030**), though **high out-of-school rates** remain a concern.
