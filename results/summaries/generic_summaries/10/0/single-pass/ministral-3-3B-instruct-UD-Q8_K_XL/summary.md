### **Education in Sweden**

#### **Structure and Levels**
Sweden’s education system is structured into **primary, secondary, upper secondary, and higher education**, with a strong emphasis on **free and compulsory education** from ages 6–16. Key stages include:
- **Primary Education (Grundskola):** Ages 6–12 (9 years), covering core subjects like Swedish, math, science, and foreign languages.
- **Comprehensive School (Grundskola):** Ages 6–16 (9 years), mandatory and free, with a focus on foundational skills.
- **Upper Secondary Education (Gymnasieskola):** Ages 16–19 (3 years), elective but highly enrolled (99%). Divided into **preparatory** (e.g., business, arts) and **vocational** programs.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities (universitet) and university colleges (högskola), offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Admission is based on academic performance.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** All levels are tuition-free for Swedish citizens, with minimal fees for higher education (€1,500–€9,250/year for international students).
- **National Curriculum:** Standardized across schools, with core subjects taught uniformly.
- **Grading System:** Uses letters (A–E) for grades 6–9, with numeric scores (E=10, A=20) for university applications.
- **Preschool:** Mandatory for children aged 1–5, with free hours for children of working parents.
- **Special Education:** Includes **Sámi schools** for indigenous students and **special needs schools** for disabilities.

#### **History and Reforms**
- **Post-WWII Growth:** Rapid expansion in the 1950s–60s due to economic growth and social reforms.
- **1962 Curriculum:** Introduced a 9-year compulsory school, later expanded to 12 years.
- **2011 Reforms:** Grading system updated, introducing grades from year 6.
- **Challenges:** Declining PISA scores (2006–2012), rising absenteeism, and debates over independent schools (friskolor).

### **Education in Hong Kong**
#### **Structure and Levels**
Hong Kong’s education system is **highly centralized**, with **primary, secondary, and tertiary education** under the **Education Bureau**. Key features:
- **Primary Education (Primary 1–6):** Ages 6–12, free in public schools.
- **Secondary Education:** Divided into **junior (S1–S3)** and **senior (S4–S6)** years. Students take the **Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE)** for university admission.
- **Tertiary Education:** Includes **8 UGC-funded universities** (e.g., HKU, CUHK) and private institutions. Admission via **JUPAS** (Joint Universities Admissions System).
- **International Schools:** Over 175 schools offering curricula from the UK, US, and IB, catering to expatriates.

#### **Key Features**
- **Bilingual Education:** English and Chinese (Cantonese) are primary languages, with increasing use of **Putonghua** (Standard Mandarin).
- **Curriculum:** Focuses on **STEM, Chinese, and civic education**, with recent shifts toward **patriotism** post-2019 protests.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on **apprenticeships** and **Vocational Training Council (VTC)** programs.
- **Challenges:** High pressure in exams, **brain drain** due to national security law, and **increasing private tutoring**.

### **Education in Norway**
#### **Structure and Levels**
Norway’s education system is **compulsory from ages 6–16**, divided into:
- **Primary School (Barneskole):** Ages 6–12 (7 years), with a focus on **play-based learning** in early grades.
- **Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole):** Ages 13–16 (4 years), introduces grading and electives.
- **Upper Secondary School (Videregående skole):** Ages 16–19 (3 years), prepares students for university or vocational training.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (universiteter) and university colleges (högskoler), with **free tuition** for Norwegians.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** All levels are tuition-free for citizens.
- **Grading System:** Uses **1–6 scale** (6=highest), with **no letter grades**.
- **Vocational Pathways:** "2+2 model" (2 years of school + 2 years of apprenticeship).
- **History:** Post-WWII reforms emphasized **universal access** and **equality**.

### **Education in Ghana**
#### **Structure and Levels**
Ghana’s education system spans **basic, secondary, and tertiary education**, with **12 years of compulsory schooling** (ages 4–15). Key stages:
- **Basic Education:** Kindergarten (2 years) → Primary (6 years) → Junior High School (3 years).
- **Secondary Education:** Senior High School (3 years), leading to the **WASSCE** exam.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (public/private), polytechnics, and colleges of education. Admission via **WASSCE** scores.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Basic and secondary education are free, though fees exist for tertiary institutions.
- **Grading System:** Uses **letter grades (A–E)** for WASSCE, with numeric scores for university applications.
- **Vocational Training:** Limited but growing, with **TVET (Technical and Vocational Education and Training)** institutes.
- **Challenges:** High dropout rates, **brain drain**, and **funding gaps** in rural areas.

### **Education in Armenia**
#### **Structure and Levels**
Armenia’s education system is **compulsory from ages 2–18**, with:
- **Preschool:** Ages 2–6 (nursery/kindergarten).
- **Primary School:** Ages 6–10 (4 years).
- **Middle School:** Ages 10–15 (5 years).
- **High School:** Ages 16–18 (3 years), with **general or specialized tracks**.
- **Higher Education:** 26 state universities and 33 private institutions, with **Bologna Process-compliant degrees**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** All levels are tuition-free.
- **Grading System:** Uses **numerical grades (1–6)**.
- **Special Education:** Includes **SAMPAD schools** for gifted students.
- **Challenges:** High absenteeism, **brain drain**, and **teacher shortages**.

### **Education in England**
#### **Structure and Levels**
England’s education system is **compulsory from ages 5–18**, divided into:
- **Early Years (Ages 3–5):** Nursery/Reception (optional).
- **Primary School (Ages 5–11):** Key Stages 1–2.
- **Secondary School (Ages 11–16):** Key Stages 3–4, leading to **GCSEs**.
- **Post-16 Education (Ages 16–18):** Sixth form/college, preparing for **A-levels** or apprenticeships.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (e.g., Oxford, Cambridge) and colleges, with **tuition fees up to £9,535/year** for UK students.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free School Meals:** Entitled to pupils aged 7–16 from low-income families.
- **Grading System:** Uses **A–G scale** (A=highest) for GCSEs and A-levels.
- **Private Schools:** ~7% of students attend fee-paying schools.
- **Challenges:** **A-level reforms**, **teacher shortages**, and **inequality in funding**.

### **Education in Indonesia**
#### **Structure and Levels**
Indonesia’s education system is **compulsory from ages 6–15**, with:
- **Primary School (Sekolah Dasar):** Ages 6–12 (6 years).
- **Junior High School (SMP):** Ages 12–15 (3 years).
- **Senior High School (SMA):** Ages 15–18 (3 years), leading to **SMA diploma**.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (universitas) and polytechnics, with **tuition fees ranging from free to $30,000/year**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Primary/Junior High:** Government-funded.
- **Islamic Schools:** **Pesantren** (boarding schools), **Madrasah**, and **Sekolah Islam**.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on **apprenticeships** and **SMK (Senior High Technical)** schools.
- **Challenges:** **Low teacher salaries**, **rural school shortages**, and **exam pressure**.
