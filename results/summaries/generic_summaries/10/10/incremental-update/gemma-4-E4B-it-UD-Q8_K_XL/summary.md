## Global Education Systems Comparison

### **Regional System Overviews & Structural Features**

Global education systems exhibit high diversity. **France** features a secular system balancing public universities and elite *Grandes Écoles*. **Turkey** uses a centralized "4+4+4" model integrating nationalism and religious instruction, while **Iran** has a centralized, Islamization-integrated system determined by the Konkour. Other models include **Norway**'s tiered compulsory system, **Armenia**'s 12-year system emphasizing STEM, **Taiwan**'s 12-year mandate with a strong cram school culture, and **Indonesia**'s blend of state and religious education. Diverse K-12 structures exist in **Israel** (secular, religious, Arab, private) and **Ireland** (compulsory from age six to sixteen). Other nations noted are **Sri Lanka** (free education up to G.C.E. O/Ls), **Egypt** (nine years of free basic education facing mismanagement), **Nigeria** (mixed tertiary/vocational management), **Sweden** (extensive free education), **Hong Kong** (mandated until age 18 with specialized qualifications), and **England/UK** (mandated to 18; England is largely state-funded with Academies and Free schools).

### **Ghana Education System Details**

**Ghana** promotes universal basic education (ages 6-14, 12 years of basic education) progressing to WASSCE. Its system involves public funding for teachers/infrastructure alongside private contributions, with the Ministry of Education overseeing policy. While literacy campaigns have reduced historical inequalities, the system includes public provisions for adult/youth literacy, non-formal education, and TVET. However, TVET suffers from low completion rates, weak qualifications, and poor infrastructure. Tertiary access in Ghana is highly unequal, with socioeconomic status significantly impacting enrollment, disproportionately affecting women and rural residents.

### **England Education Specifics**

In **England**, compulsory education ends around age 16, followed by further education or apprenticeships until age 18. Higher education qualifications range from Level 4 to Level 8, with undergraduate degrees typically requiring three years; new entrant fees are often subsidized by loans.

### **Cross-Cutting Themes: Equity, Quality, and Challenges**

A universal challenge across these systems is balancing quality and equity, often leading to reliance on private tutoring due to socioeconomic disparities (seen in Egypt) or ideological constraints (Iran). Funding models vary widely (e.g., England's state funding vs. Sweden's comprehensive free access). Furthermore, while policy efforts are made in areas like ICT integration in Ghana, actual classroom implementation often lags.
