### **Education Systems in Israel, Sri Lanka, Ireland, Egypt, Nigeria, Indonesia, Taiwan, Armenia, Iran, Norway, Turkey, Sweden, France, Hong Kong, England, Ghana: Comparative Overview**

Israel’s **three-tiered system (ages 5–18)** operates under a **sectoral model** with **funding disparities** (Arab students: **$192/year** vs. Jewish students: **$1,100**), though **STEM gaps persist** (15% of Bagrut certificates lack full eligibility). Despite **PISA rankings (33rd in reading, 40th in math/science)**, **Arab dropout rates remain high (12%)**, and **gender disparities persist** (females drop out less but have lower matriculation pass rates). **STEM fields remain male-dominated (81% male professors)**, while **teacher shortages and low wages** undermine quality.

**Ghana’s education system**, shaped by **colonial legacy and post-independence reforms**, spans **12 years (kindergarten–junior high)**, with **free compulsory education** (primary: 6 years, junior high: 3 years, senior high: 3 years). **Gender parity exists in primary/secondary (GPI = 1 in 2013)**, but **literacy gaps persist** (79.04% overall, 74.47% female vs. 83.53% male). **Enrollment rates are high (90%)**, yet **vocational education (TVET) remains underdeveloped**, with **informal apprenticeships dominating**. **Tertiary education** relies on **WASSCE grades (A1–F9)**, with **6 public and 49 private universities**, and **private-public partnerships** fund infrastructure. **Teacher training** includes **46 public colleges (DBE curriculum)** and **universities (4-year degrees)**, with **distance learning for rural educators**. **Adult literacy programs** (e.g., FLSP/FNLP) targeted women/rural areas post-independence, though challenges persist in sustainability.

**Ghana’s literacy improvements** (adult literacy rose from **~50–81%** men/women by 2015) and **youth literacy (15–24) increased significantly**, though **non-formal education** focuses on life-skills and occupational training. **TVET reforms** (e.g., **STEP micro-credit, COTVET NAP**) aim to address informal apprenticeship gaps, but **poorly trained instructors and industry criticism** persist. **Equity in tertiary education** saw progress (girls’ share rose to **32%** by 2005), but **selectivity dropped (52% acceptance rate in 2011)**, favoring wealthy/male students. **Online scholarships (2020)** streamlined government support, while **ICT integration** (mandated but limited access) remains a priority, with ongoing efforts to improve **ICT literacy**.

**England** ranks **13th globally in PISA scores**, with a **unified compulsory system (5–18 years)** structured into **primary (5–11) and Key Stages 1–4 (7–16)**, culminating in **GCSEs at 16**. Post-compulsory pathways include **vocational training (T Levels, BTECs), apprenticeships, and part-time courses**, while **higher education** (ages 18+) offers **degree programs (up to £9,535/year for UK students)**. **Academies improved school ratings (88% "good" or "outstanding" by 2015)**, and **funding rose to £7.1B/year (2022–23)**, including **£780M for SEND support**. **Vocational expansion** (T Levels, apprenticeships) and **selective pathways** (grammar schools, UTCs) reflect recent reforms.

**Hong Kong’s 12-year compulsory education (6–18)** now emphasizes **vocational training** under **2024 reforms**, with **JUPAS governing tertiary access via HKDSE/HKALE**. **Secular Cantonese-medium instruction (84%)** coexists with **EMI and Putonghua programs**, while **immigrant children benefit from free induction programs**. **Tuition-free adult education** expands access, particularly for **low-income groups**, and **international schools** offer **IB/IGCSE curricula**.

**France’s sectoral higher education** (Bologna-aligned) features **intense preparatory tracks** (e.g., *Grandes Écoles* via CPGE, **5% entry rate**) and **standardized exams** (baccalauréat, CAPES/Agrégation). **Secularism (*laïcité*)** excludes religious instruction (except Alsace/Moselle), with **tuition varying**—low for EU students (~€170–380/year), high for non-EU (~€2,770–3,770). **Teacher recruitment demands PhD-level qualifications**, while **private elite schools** charge **€40,000+**.

**Sweden’s unified system** offers **free tuition for EU citizens**, **independent fee-free schools (Friskolor)**, and **vocational pathways** (e.g., *Grundlärarprogrammet*). **Admissions rely on SweSAT**, and **class sizes** remain debated amid low birth rates.

### **Common Challenges Across Systems**
- **Funding disparities**, **teacher shortages**, and **quality/equity gaps** (e.g., rote-learning, PISA disparities).
- **Vocational vs. academic divides**, **post-compulsory dropout rates**, and **special education access**.
- **Standardized testing pressures** (e.g., baccalauréat, GCSEs, HKDSE) and **equity in admissions**.
- **Secularism vs. religious influence** (e.g., France’s *laïcité*, England’s mixed history).
- **Emerging trends**:
  - **Ghana’s adult literacy gains**, **TVET reforms**, and **ICT integration challenges**.
  - **Hong Kong’s vocational reforms** and **adult education expansion**.
  - **Sweden’s fee-free policies** and **vocational pathways**.
  - **England’s selective pathways** (grammar schools, UTCs) and **vocational expansion**.
  - **France’s elite admissions** and **secular education policies**.
