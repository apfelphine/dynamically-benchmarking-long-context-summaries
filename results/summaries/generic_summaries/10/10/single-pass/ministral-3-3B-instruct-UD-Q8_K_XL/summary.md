### **Education in Israel**

#### **Structure and Tiers**
Israel’s education system spans three tiers:
- **Primary Education (Grades 1–6, ages 6–12):** Compulsory, covering core subjects like Hebrew, English, mathematics, and science.
- **Middle School (Grades 7–9, ages 12–15):** Prepares students for high school.
- **High School (Grades 10–12, ages 15–18):** Leads to matriculation exams (Bagrut), determining university admission and career prospects.

**Compulsory Education:** Mandatory from kindergarten through 12th grade. Haredi yeshivas follow an independent schedule.

**School Tracks:**
- **State-Secular (Mamlachti):** General curriculum.
- **State-Religious (Mamlachti Dati):** Intensive Jewish studies.
- **Independent Religious (Haredi/Chinuch Atzmai):** Focuses on Torah study.
- **Arab Sector:** Teaches in Arabic, emphasizing Arab history, religion, and culture.
- **Private Schools:** Offer alternative curricula (e.g., American, IB).

**Demographic Trends:** Haredi and Arab sectors are growing, with Haredi students facing low workforce participation and limited matriculation exam success.

---

#### **Pupils’ Rights and Societal Approach**
- **Pupils’ Rights Law (2000):** Prohibits discrimination based on sectarian reasons in admissions, curriculum, and expulsion.
- **Societal Value:** Education is highly valued, rooted in Jewish diaspora traditions and modern Zionism. Higher education is seen as key to socioeconomic mobility, especially in Israel’s high-tech economy.

**Economic Role:** High tertiary education rates (46% of 25–64-year-olds) and strong STEM focus drive economic growth. Israel ranks second in OECD for tertiary education attainment.

---

#### **Matriculation (Bagrut) and Higher Education**
- **Bagrut Exams:** Mandatory for matriculation, covering core subjects (Hebrew, English, mathematics, scripture, history, civics, literature). Students must pass at least 21 units and one 5-unit exam.
- **Admission Impact:** Determines university entry, military unit eligibility, and job prospects.
- **Higher Education:**
  - **Public Universities:** Subsidized by the government; students pay minimal tuition.
  - **Private Universities:** Offered by some institutions, with higher fees.
  - **Military Service:** Mandatory for graduates, with deferments for study.
  - **Psychometric Test (PET):** Standardized test for university admissions.
  - **International Rankings:** Six Israeli universities rank in the top 100 of Asia, and several in global rankings for STEM.

---

#### **Challenges and Reforms**
- **Haredi Education:** Low matriculation rates (8%) and limited workforce participation due to lack of secular education.
- **Arab Sector:** Underfunded schools, high dropout rates, and disparities in resources compared to Jewish schools.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and morale issues contribute to teacher turnover.
- **Textbook Bias:** Criticisms of negative portrayals of Arabs in textbooks.
- **Strikes:** Frequent faculty and student strikes over tuition fees, teacher salaries, and curriculum reforms.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Administration and History**
- **Government Structure:** Primary education funded by the Ministry of Education; higher education by the Ministry of Higher Education.
- **Historical Context:** Early education rooted in Buddhist temples and monastic colleges. Colonial reforms introduced English-medium schools, leading to a mix of vernacular and Western curricula.

**Key Institutions:**
- **Pirivenas:** Monastic schools for Buddhist priests.
- **Madrasas:** Islamic schools for Muslim students.
- **International Schools:** Privately run, often with foreign curricula (e.g., American, British, IB).

---

#### **Structure and Stages**
- **Primary to Higher Education:** Free and compulsory from kindergarten to university.
- **Five-Stage System:**
  - **Primary (Grades 1–5):** Scholarship exam after Grade 5.
  - **Junior Secondary (Grades 6–9):** Leads to GCE O/L exams.
  - **Senior Secondary (Grades 10–11):** Prepares for GCE A/L exams.
  - **Collegiate (Grades 12–13):** Prepares for university entrance.
  - **Tertiary Education:** GCE A/L exams required for university admission.

**School Types:**
- **National Schools:** State-funded, high-quality institutions.
- **Provincial Schools:** Majority of schools, often underfunded.
- **Pirivenas:** Monastic schools for Buddhist priests.
- **Private Schools:** Offer international curricula (e.g., IGCSE, IB).

---

#### **Vocational and Special Education**
- **Vocational Training:** Managed by the Ministry of Skills Development and Vocational Training. Includes apprenticeships and technical colleges.
- **Special Education:** Focuses on students with disabilities, with support from the National Institute of Education (NIE).

**Challenges:**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and inadequate training.
- **Funding Disparities:** National schools receive more resources than provincial schools.
- **Quality Issues:** Rote memorization and lack of critical thinking in some institutions.

### **Education in Ireland**

#### **Structure and Stages**
- **Compulsory Education:** Ages 6–16 (or 3 years of second-level education).
- **Three Stages:**
  - **Primary School (1–6):** Ages 6–12.
  - **Secondary School (7–12):** Ages 13–16.
  - **Third-Level Education:** Universities and colleges.

**Key Features:**
- **Irish Language:** Core subject in all schools; English is a second language.
- **Curriculum:** Primary school curriculum emphasizes holistic development (spiritual, moral, cognitive, social, physical).
- **Grades:** Junior Certificate (end of primary), Leaving Certificate (end of secondary).

**Types of Schools:**
- **National Schools:** Church-controlled, majority Catholic.
- **Gaelscoileanna:** Irish-language schools.
- **Multidenominational Schools:** Non-religious, open to all.
- **Private Schools:** Fee-charging, often religiously affiliated.

---

#### **Higher Education and Special Needs**
- **Higher Education:** Free upper second-level education since 1967. Universities offer English-language courses, with some Irish-language options.
- **Special Needs Education:** Act of 2004 established frameworks for students with disabilities. Special Needs Assistants (SNAs) provide support.

**Challenges:**
- **Disadvantage:** Schools in disadvantaged areas receive additional resources (e.g., Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools, DEIS).
- **International Rankings:** Ireland ranks 2nd in reading and 11th in mathematics in the 2022 PISA survey.

### **Education in Egypt**

#### **Structure and History**
- **Three-Tier System:**
  - **Basic Education (Ages 4–14):** Free and compulsory (kindergarten to preparatory school).
  - **Secondary Education (Ages 15–17):** Three-year general track or technical/vocational tracks.
  - **Tertiary Education:** Public and private universities, with public institutions offering free education.

**Key Institutions:**
- **Al-Azhar:** Islamic religious and educational institution.
- **International Schools:** Privately run, offering curricula from foreign countries (e.g., American, British, IB).

**History:**
- **Pre-Islamic:** Schools focused on religious and administrative training.
- **Safavid Era:** Introduction of modern education with a mix of Islamic and scientific studies.
- **Qajar Era:** Educational Committee promoted reforms, but limited success due to political conflicts.
- **Post-Revolution:** Islamization of textbooks and segregation by gender.

---

#### **Challenges and Reforms**
- **Quality Issues:** Low test scores in international assessments (e.g., TIMSS).
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low salaries and lack of training.
- **Funding:** 20% of government spending and 5% of GDP allocated to education.
- **Reforms:** 2012 education reform plan aimed at improving pedagogy, research, and teacher training.

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **Structure and Stages**
- **Primary Education (Ages 5–11):** Six years, leading to the Common Entrance Examination.
- **Secondary Education (Ages 12–18):** Six years (JSS 1–3, SSS 1–3), leading to the Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE).
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education.

**Key Features:**
- **Universal Basic Education (UBE):** Free, compulsory, and right to education for all children (9 years).
- **School Tracks:**
  - **Federal-Sponsored:** Elite schools with low fees.
  - **State-Sponsored:** Free but requires fees for books and uniforms.
  - **Private/Religious:** Expensive, with high-quality facilities.

**Vocational Education:** Managed by the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE). Includes apprenticeships and technical colleges.

---

#### **Challenges and Reforms**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and inadequate training.
- **Funding:** Limited resources, especially in rural areas.
- **Exams:** High-pressure standardized tests (e.g., SSCE, WAEC).
- **Reforms:** 2012 education reform plan aimed at improving infrastructure and teacher training.

**Challenges:**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and inadequate training.
