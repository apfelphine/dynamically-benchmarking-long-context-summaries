### **Education in Iran**

#### **History**
Iran’s education system traces back to ancient Persia, with early emphasis on religious and secular knowledge. The **Sasanian era** introduced the **Academy of Gondishapur**, one of the world’s oldest medical schools. The **Safavid era** marked the first modern education reforms, blending Islamic values with scientific advancements. The **Qajar era** saw the establishment of the **Educational Committee**, promoting educational reforms through private initiatives. Post-revolution reforms included **Islamization of textbooks**, gender segregation, and the introduction of a credit-based system.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education spans **pre-primary to university**, with **compulsory education** from ages 6–15. The system is highly centralized, with the **Ministry of Education** overseeing curriculum, textbooks, and teacher training. Grades range from **A (16–20)** to **F (0–9.99)**, with **English** and **Arabic** as core subjects. Vocational and technical programs are expanding, with **STEM education** being particularly strong.

#### **Key Reforms & Challenges**
- **Literacy Corps (1969–1979)**: A government-led initiative to improve literacy rates.
- **Post-Revolution Changes**: Islamization of textbooks, gender segregation, and a shift toward vocational training.
- **Budget Allocation**: 20% of government spending and 5% of GDP go to education.
- **Criticisms**: Teacher shortages, privatization pressures, and disparities in rural vs. urban education.

### **Education in Taiwan**
#### **History**
Taiwan’s education system evolved under **Dutch, Ming loyalists, Qing, and Japanese rule**, with early academies like **Daodong Academy**. Post-1945, under the **Republic of China**, public schools were expanded, and private schools were suppressed to enforce assimilation. The **1968 reform** introduced **12-year compulsory education**, and the **2011 reform** expanded preschool access.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 6–12**, followed by **12 years of compulsory education** (primary + junior high). The system is **bilingual (Mandarin)**, with **English** as a core subject. **Secondary education** includes **junior high (7–9)**, **senior high (10–12)**, and **vocational tracks**. **University entrance** is highly competitive, with **JUPAS** (Joint University Admissions System) managing admissions.

#### **Key Features**
- **12-year compulsory education** (previously 9 years).
- **Vocational education** is widely available, with **87 vocational training institutions**.
- **International schools** (e.g., **Mehr-e-Taban, Tehran International**) offer **IB and A-level programs**.
- **Cram schools** (buxiban) are common for supplementary learning.

### **Education in Turkey**
#### **History**
Turkey’s education system was **reorganized post-republic** (1923), with **secularism** as a core principle. The **1924 Education Law** integrated schools, and the **1926 Education Organization Law** standardized curricula. The **1997 reforms** extended compulsory education to **8 years**, and the **2012 "4+4+4" system** restructured primary and secondary education.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 6–14**, with **8-year primary school** followed by **4 years of middle school**. **Secondary education** includes **general, vocational, and religious tracks**. The system is **bilingual (Turkish + English)**, with **foreign languages** (English, German, French) as core subjects. **University entrance** is via the **YÖS exam** or equivalent credentials.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 8 years (primary) + 4 years (middle school).
- **Vocational education**: Highly developed, with **apprenticeship programs**.
- **International schools**: Offer **IB and British curricula**.
- **Criticisms**: Textbook bias, curriculum changes, and **mandatory religious education**.

### **Education in Nigeria**
#### **History**
Nigeria’s education system was influenced by **colonial policies**, with **mission schools** and **universities** established post-independence. The **Universal Primary Education (UPE)** program (1976) aimed for free, compulsory primary education, but faced challenges. The **6-3-3-4 system** (1982) restructured secondary education, and **UBE (Universal Basic Education)** was introduced in 1999.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education spans **primary (6 years) + junior secondary (3 years) + senior secondary (3 years)**, culminating in the **WAEC/NECO exams**. **Tertiary education** includes **universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education**. **Private schools** are expensive, while **state schools** face funding shortages. **Vocational education** is growing but lacks standardization.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 9 years (primary + junior secondary).
- **WAEC/NECO exams** determine secondary school progression.
- **Private schools** dominate, with fees ranging from **₦25,000–₦30,000,000/year**.
- **Vocational training**: Limited but expanding, with **National Board for Technical Education (NBTE)** oversight.

### **Education in France**
#### **History**
France’s modern education system was established by **Jules Ferry** in the late 19th century, introducing **free, secular, and compulsory** education for ages 6–12. The **Bologna Process** (2000s) aligned French higher education with European standards, introducing **bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees**.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 6–15**, with **primary (6 years) + middle/high school (9 years)**. **University education** follows the **Bologna Process**, with **licence (Bachelor’s), master’s, and doctorat (PhD)** degrees. **Private schools** (e.g., **grandes écoles**) offer specialized programs.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 12 years (primary + middle/high school).
- **Higher education**: **Bologna Process** compatibility; **grandes écoles** are elite institutions.
- **Tuition fees**: Low for EU students (~€170–380/year), higher for international students (~€2,770–3,770/year).
- **Criticisms**: Teacher shortages, **preparatory classes (CPGE)** for elite schools.

### **Education in Ireland**
#### **History**
Ireland’s education system was **reformed post-1960s**, introducing **free upper secondary education** (1967). The **1973 Irish language requirement** was abolished, and **Gaelscoileanna** (Irish-medium schools) expanded. The **2005 "Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools" (DEIS)** program addressed educational disadvantage.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 5–16**, with **primary (8 years) + secondary (6 years)**. **Leaving Certificate** exams determine university admission. **Gaelscoileanna** offer **Irish-medium education**, and **international schools** provide **IB and A-level programs**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 12 years (primary + secondary).
- **DEIS program**: Supports disadvantaged students.
- **Gaelscoileanna**: Rapidly growing Irish-medium schools.
- **Criticisms**: **Single-sex schools** remain rare; **multidenominational schools** are expanding.

### **Education in England**
#### **History**
England’s education system evolved from **charity schools** to **state-funded schools** post-1870. The **Education Act 1944** introduced **compulsory education** for ages 5–15, and **free school meals** for primary students. **Grammar schools** (selective) and **comprehensive schools** (non-selective) coexisted.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 5–18**, with **primary (5–11) + secondary (11–16)**. **GCSEs** (ages 14–16) and **A-levels** (ages 16–18) determine university admission. **Private schools** are expensive, while **state schools** face funding disparities.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 12 years (primary + secondary).
- **GCSEs/A-levels**: Standardized exams for university entry.
- **Private schools**: ~7% of students; fees range from **£5,000–£50,000/year**.
- **Criticisms**: **Teacher shortages**, **academy schools**, and **disparities in funding**.

### **Education in Israel**
#### **History**
Israel’s education system was **developed post-statehood (1948)**, with **state-secular, state-religious, and Arab schools**. The **1984 integrated schools** (e.g., **Neve Shalom**) and **2000 Pupils’ Rights Law** addressed sectarian discrimination. **Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) schools** face challenges in integration.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education is **compulsory from ages 5–18**, with **primary (6 years) + middle (3 years) + high school (3 years)**. **Matriculation exams (bagrut)** determine university admission. **Haredi students** have lower participation rates, and **Arab students** face disparities in funding.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 12 years (primary + middle + high school).
- **Bagrut exams**: Critical for university admission.
- **Haredi schools**: Low participation in mainstream education.
- **Criticisms**: **Teacher shortages**, **textbook bias**, and **vocational education gaps**.

### **Education in Ghana**

Ghana’s education system was **influenced by colonial missionaries**, with **mission schools** and **universities** established post-independence. #### **Structure & Grading**
Education spans **kindergarten (2 years) + primary (6 years) + junior high (3 years) + senior high (3 years)**. **BECE (Basic Education Certificate Examination)** determines progression. **Tertiary education** includes **universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 9 years (primary + junior high).
- **BECE exams**: Determine secondary school placement.
- **Vocational education**: Growing but lacks standardization.
- **Criticisms**: **Teacher shortages**, **rural-urban disparities**, and **informal education gaps**.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**
#### **History**
Sri Lanka’s education system was **influenced by British colonial rule**, with **mission schools** and **universities** established post-independence. The **1972 University of Sri Lanka Act** centralized university governance, and **1999 reforms** introduced **nationalized private universities**.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education spans **primary (5 years) + junior secondary (4 years) + senior secondary (2 years)**. **GCE O/Ls (Ordinary Level)** and **GCE A/Ls (Advanced Level)** determine university admission. **State universities** are highly competitive, with **Open University** offering flexible programs.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 9 years (primary + junior secondary).
- **GCE A/Ls**: University entrance exam.
- **State universities**: Highly competitive; **Open University** provides alternative access.
- **Criticisms**: **Teacher shortages**, **vocational education gaps**, and **brain drain**.

### **Education in Hong Kong**
#### **History**
Hong Kong’s education system was **developed under British rule**, with **mission schools** and **universities** established post-1997 handover. The **2012 curriculum reform** introduced **Project-Based Learning**, and **2023 AI integration** was introduced in universities.

#### **Structure & Grading**
Education spans **kindergarten (3 years) + primary (6 years) + secondary (6 years)**. **HKDSE (Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education)** exams determine university admission. **English-medium (EMI) schools** are prestigious, while **Chinese-medium (CMI) schools** are common.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education**: 12 years (primary + secondary).
- **HKDSE exams**: University entrance exam.
- **EMI/CMI schools**: EMI schools are highly competitive.
- **Criticisms**: **Teacher shortages**, **AI integration challenges**, and **rural-urban disparities**.
