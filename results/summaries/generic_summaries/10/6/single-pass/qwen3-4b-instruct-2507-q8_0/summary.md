### **Education in Iran**
- **History**: Ancient Persia emphasized knowledge for prosperity, with early institutions like the Academy of Gondishapur. The Safavid era introduced Islamic and scientific values. The Qajar era saw the first organized reform, and the White Revolution launched the Literacy Corps. Post-1979 Islamic Revolution emphasized Islamic values, leading to gender segregation, mandatory prayer, and curriculum Islamization. Reforms in the 1990s included a credit-based system, shortened high school, and vocational tracks.
- **Grades**: Structured from age 5–18, with compulsory elementary and secondary education. Upper secondary includes theoretical, technical, and manual skills tracks. A high school diploma is required for university entry.
- **Budget**: 20% of government spending and 5% of GDP go to education, with 50% allocated to secondary education.
- **Unconstitutional Change**: In 2024, a law was introduced making it illegal to protest educational reforms without a public referendum.
- **Mandatory Islamic Prayers**: Schools require half-hour daily Islamic prayers; students learn General Soleimani’s will.
- **Education Reform**: A 2012 plan added a year to elementary school, emphasized research, and promoted global knowledge, questioning, and family involvement.
- **Hybrid Learning**: Post-pandemic, universities and schools use hybrid models with learning management systems and Shad software, supported by government grants.
- **Privatization**: Private schools are rising in cost and prestige, with fees reaching 50 million toman annually. Public schools are seen as weak and for the poor.
- **Teacher Shortage**: Iran faces a shortage of about 60,000 teachers; 30,000 school counselors are needed. In Sistan and Baluchestan, schools are scarce, and 30% of students cannot attend.
- **Teacher Education**: Farhangian University trains teachers in primary, orientation, and gifted children’s schools. Admission requires a national exam.
- **Foreign Languages**: Persian is official; Arabic and one foreign language (e.g., English, French) are taught from grades 7–12. English is most desired; French was added in 2022.
- **Internet & Distance Education**: Full internet access is available; Payame Noor University offers distance education.
- **Higher Education**: Iran has over 4.5 million students. Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. The system is highly centralized. Students serve the government or pay a small fee.
- **Women in Education**: Women now outnumber men in higher education. Despite progress, access remains limited due to societal and policy barriers.
- **Schools for Gifted Children**: NODET and Nemoone Dolati schools offer competitive entrance exams and tuition. NODET schools are similar to private schools.
- **Organization**: OERP conducts research, develops curricula, and produces textbooks. The Ministry of Education oversees curriculum, exams, and teacher training.

### **Education in Taiwan**
- **History**: Dutch, Chinese, and Japanese systems implemented education. Post-1949, the Republic of China suppressed private schools to assimilate the population. The 12-year compulsory education policy began in 2011.
- **Public Education**: Compulsory from primary to senior high school. Discipline is strict, with uniforms and morning reveille. Students take national exams.
- **Grades**: 12-year system, with grades from 1 to 12. Senior high school includes science or liberal arts tracks.
- **National Education**: A 12-year system replaced the old 9-year model. Students take the national university entrance exam.
- **Higher Education**: Taiwan has many public and private universities. Engineering is popular. Students take national exams for university admission.
- **Private Education**: Private schools offer supplementary classes and are widespread. The "3+3" system allows students to skip the CAP test.
- **International Education**: Taiwan has three IB schools. International schools offer English-medium curricula.
- **Statistics**: Over 90% of students continue to university. The system emphasizes academic performance and national exams.

### **Education in Turkey**
- **History**: Post-Republic, education was secularized. The 1924 Law of Integration combined schools. The 1928 Latin alphabet law and the 1930s secular reforms promoted modern education. The 2017 curriculum removed evolution and added jihad.
- **Pre-primary to Secondary**: Compulsory from age 6–14. Pre-primary is optional. Primary school lasts 8 years. Secondary includes general, vocational, and technical tracks.
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on secular values, history, and language. The curriculum is standardized but allows for specialized tracks.
- **Private Schools**: Private schools are categorized as "under contract" (same curriculum as public schools) or without contract (can teach their own curriculum).
- **Religious Education**: Removed from public schools after 1927. Reintroduced in 1956 with optional religious instruction.
- **International Education**: Turkey has 53 IB schools. International schools offer English-medium curricula.
- **Higher Education**: Public universities are state-funded with low tuition. Private universities charge high fees. Engineering and sciences are highly developed.
- **Private Schools**: Private schools are fee-based and offer specialized curricula. The state monitors them.
- **Teacher Education**: Teachers are trained in state institutions and must pass competitive exams. Teacher training centers are centralized.

### **Education in Nigeria**
- **History**: Colonial education was limited to elites. Post-independence, the Universal Basic Education (UBE) policy aimed to expand access. The 1999 UBE policy improved access and quality.
- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 5, lasting 6 years. Students take a Common Entrance Examination (CEC) to enter secondary schools.
- **Secondary Education**: 6 years of secondary education (3 years junior secondary, 3 years senior secondary). Students take the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) or General Certificate of Education (GCE).
- **Higher Education**: The government controls university education. The system includes public and private universities. A large number of private institutions exist.
- **Vocational Education**: Vocational training is overseen by the National Board for Technical Education. Students can pursue a National Technical Certificate or Advanced National Technical Certificate.
- **Adult Literacy**: The government has introduced adult literacy programs, including the Functional Literacy Skills Project (FLSP) and the National Functional Literacy Programme (FNLP).
- **Gender Disparities**: Women are underrepresented in higher education. The government has launched initiatives to address gender gaps.
- **Private Education**: Private schools are expensive and have smaller class sizes. They offer modern curricula and better facilities.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low funding are significant challenges. The government has implemented reforms to improve quality and access.

### **Education in France**
- **History**: Napoleon established modern university and secondary systems. Jules Ferry created the secular, mandatory public school system in the 1880s.
- **Governance**: The Ministry of National Education and Youth regulates all education. The curriculum is standardized across the country.
- **School Year**: Runs from September to July. Holidays include winter, spring, and summer breaks.
- **Primary School**: Ages 3–11. Students learn French, math, science, and arts. Teachers are state civil servants.
- **Middle and High School**: Ages 11–18. Students take exams in French, history, geography, and foreign languages. The curriculum is standardized.
- **Private Schools**: Private schools are divided into "under contract" (same curriculum as public schools) and without contract (can teach their own curriculum).
- **International Education**: France has 105 international schools. The AEFE regulates international education.
- **Higher Education**: France has a system of public and private universities. The Bologna Process standardizes degrees. Public universities offer low tuition fees.
- **Tuition Costs**: Public universities have low fees. Private universities charge higher fees. Students from low-income families can apply for scholarships.
- **Religious Instruction**: Public schools do not teach religion. The principle of laïcité (secularism) is central.
- **Statistics**: France has a high literacy rate and strong performance in international assessments.

### **Education in the Republic of Ireland**
- **History**: Free upper secondary education was introduced in 1967. The Irish language requirement for a second-level certificate was abandoned in 1973.
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 5–16. Primary education (ages 4–12) and secondary education (ages 12–18) are structured into junior and senior cycles.
- **Primary School**: Begins at age 4–5. Students learn core subjects including English, math, science, and Irish.
- **Secondary School**: Junior Cycle (3 years) culminates in the Junior Certificate. Senior Cycle (2 years) culminates in the Leaving Certificate.
- **School Types**: National schools, Gaelscoileanna (Irish-language schools), multidenominational schools, and private schools.
- **Higher Education**: Students can attend universities or colleges. The system emphasizes academic excellence and national integration.
- **Adult Education**: Adult education is available through various programs and institutions.
- **Societal Approach**: Education is seen as a key to socioeconomic mobility. The government supports initiatives to improve access and quality.
- **Challenges**: The system faces challenges in equity, teacher shortages, and access to quality education.

### **Education in England**
- **History**: Medieval schools were religious institutions. The 19th century saw the expansion of public education. The 1944 Education Act established free, compulsory education.
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 5–18. The education system is divided into primary, secondary, and higher education.
- **Primary School**: Ages 5–11. Students learn core subjects including English, math, science, and history.
- **Secondary School**: Ages 11–16. Students take GCSEs at age 16. The curriculum includes core subjects and electives.
- **Higher Education**: Undergraduate degrees are typically three years. Postgraduate degrees are one to two years.
- **Tuition Fees**: Since 1998, tuition fees have been introduced, with a cap on fees. Students from low-income families can apply for loans.
- **Private Schools**: Approximately 7% of students attend private schools. These schools are not required to follow the national curriculum.
- **Adult Education**: Adult education is available through various institutions and programs.
- **Teacher Training**: Teachers are trained in universities and must have a degree and teaching qualification.
- **Challenges**: The system faces challenges in teacher shortages, class sizes, and access to quality education.

### **Education in Israel**
- **Schools**: Divided into state-secular, state-religious, independent religious (Haredi), and Arab schools. The majority of students attend state schools.
- **Curriculum**: The matriculation (Bagrut) exams are required for university admission. Students take exams in Hebrew, English, mathematics, history, and religion.
- **Higher Education**: Students are required to serve in the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) or study at a pre-service Mechina. Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees.
- **Arab Sector**: The Arab education system is underfunded and faces challenges in access and quality. The government has introduced affirmative action policies.
- **Gender Statistics**: Women are more likely to attend university. However, men dominate in engineering and science.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Teachers face low wages and high workloads. The government has introduced reforms to improve quality.
- **Textbooks**: Textbooks have been criticized for promoting negative images of Arabs.
- **Strikes**: Teachers have staged strikes over pay and working conditions.
- **International Recognition**: Israeli universities are ranked among the top in science and engineering.

### **Education in Ghana**
- **History**: Pre-colonial education was informal. The British introduced formal schools. Post-independence, education was expanded under Kwame Nkrumah.
- **Structure**: Basic education (ages 4–15) includes kindergarten, primary, and junior high school. Secondary education (ages 15–18) includes senior high school and vocational training.
- **Primary Education**: Begins at age 4. Students learn core subjects including English, Ghanaian languages, and mathematics.
- **Secondary Education**: Students take the West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE) at the end of senior high school.
- **Higher Education**: Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. The government provides financial aid to students.
- **Vocational Education**: Vocational training is limited and informal. The government has introduced reforms to improve access and quality.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low funding are major challenges. The government has implemented reforms to improve access and quality.

### **Education in Sweden**
- **History**: The 1962 curriculum introduced a nine-year comprehensive school. The 1994 grading system was changed to include grades from year 6.
- **Primary School**: Ages 6–16. Students take 16 compulsory subjects, including Swedish, mathematics, and English.
- **Secondary School**: Ages 16–19. Students choose from 18 national programs, including preparatory and vocational tracks.
- **Higher Education**: Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. The system is based on the Bologna Process.
- **Grading**: Uses a seven-grade system (A–F) or pass/fail system. Grades are converted to ECTS credits.
- **Private Schools**: Independent schools (friskolor) are funded by the government and offer alternative pedagogies. They are controversial due to profit-making models.
- **Education Quality**: Sweden performs well in international assessments, though recent declines have been reported.
- **Challenges**: Inequality in access and quality remains a concern. The government has introduced reforms to improve equity and access.

### **Education in Indonesia**
- **History**: Islamic kingdoms introduced pesantren schools. Dutch colonial education was segregated by race. Post-independence, the government created a national education system.
- **Primary and Secondary Education**: Compulsory from age 6–16. Students attend public or private schools. The curriculum emphasizes Pancasila (national values).
- **Islamic Schools**: Pesantren, madrasah, and sekolah islam offer religious education. Pesantren are led by hereditary kyais.
- **Higher Education**: Offers academic, vocational, and professional education. The system is organized under the Indonesian National Qualification Framework (KKNI).
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and low funding are significant issues. The government has introduced reforms to improve access and quality.

### **Education in Norway**
- **History**: Education dates back to 2000 B.C. The folkeskole system was introduced in the 19th century. The 1970s saw the introduction of the grunnskole system.
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 6–16. Primary (ages 6–12), lower secondary (ages 13–16), and upper secondary (ages 16–19).
- **Grading**: Uses a scale from 1 to 6, with 6 being the highest. Final grades are based on teacher assessments.
- **Higher Education**: Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. The system is highly centralized.
- **Special Education**: Students with disabilities are educated in local schools. The government provides support and resources.
- **Examinations**: Students take exams at the end of secondary education. A minority are chosen to sit a random exam (trekkfag).
- **Challenges**: Inequality in access and quality remains a concern. The government has introduced reforms to improve equity and access.

### **Education in Armenia**
- **Literacy Rate**: 100% since 1960. The numeracy level was low in the 18th–19th centuries.
- **K-12 Education**: Compulsory from age 6–18. Primary (ages 6–10), middle (ages 10–15), and high school (ages 15–18).
- **Exams**: Students take entrance exams for higher education. Results determine university admission.
- **School Types**: Public schools are dominant (97%). Private schools offer specialized programs and are funded by tuition.
- **Higher Education**: Armenia has 26 state universities and 33 private institutions. The country is a member of the Bologna Process and European Higher Education Area.
- **Challenges**: Student absenteeism and dropout rates are high, linked to child labor. The government has introduced reforms to improve access and quality.
