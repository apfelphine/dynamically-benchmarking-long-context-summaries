### **Comparative Education Systems: Global Trends and Challenges**

#### **Religious vs. Secular Models**
Iran’s education system blends **Islamic and scientific traditions**, with **compulsory 3-year high school** followed by **universities requiring service or tuition**, prioritizing **STEM fields (31% engineering, 10% science graduates)**. Women dominate higher education (**70% of students, 70% of engineering graduates**), though **teacher shortages (~60,000 missing)**, rural disparities, and **ideological constraints** (e.g., mandatory Islamic prayer sessions) persist. Recent **2024 protest bans** highlight tensions over curriculum integration.

Taiwan’s **12-year compulsory education** (expanded from 9 years) emphasizes **STEM and vocational tracks**, with **exam-driven admissions** and **mandatory military training**. Public universities lead in STEM, while private schools focus on **law, business, and languages**, ensuring **high academic rigor** without religious influence.

In contrast, **Turkey** integrates **secularism (Atatürk’s reforms)** with **Islamic elements**, including **creationism in biology** and **mandatory English**, though **censorship** and **funding disparities** (₺500 vs. ₺500,000 for elite schools) persist. **France** enforces **strict secularism (*laïcité*)**, banning religious symbols in schools (2004 law) while debates over **curriculum content** (e.g., ethnic diversity) continue. Public universities charge **€170–€3,770/year**, with **prestigious *grandes écoles*** requiring **preparatory classes (CPGEs)**.

**Ireland** reflects **Catholic influence** in primary education (e.g., religious studies) but ensures **Irish language instruction** nationwide. Compulsory education spans **6–16 years**, with **exams (Junior/Leaving Certificate)** determining progression. The system balances **multiculturalism** with **Irish heritage**, though **teacher shortages** and **curriculum reforms** remain challenges.

#### **Global Equity, Innovation, and Policy Trends**
**Israel’s 12-year system** is **sector-specific** (secular, religious, Haredi, Arab, international), with **Arab/Druze students facing funding disparities** and higher dropout rates. The **Bagrut exams** determine university eligibility and military service, with **STEM dominance** but **gender gaps**. **Affirmative action** (e.g., 25% budget for Arab students) aims to reduce inequality, though **sectarian divides** persist.

**Ghana’s system** combines **colonial-era missionary schools** with **African oral traditions**, offering **free basic education (6–14 years)** but facing **funding gaps** and **rural disparities**. **Decentralization reforms** improved gender parity but left **rural access gaps**, particularly in **vocational training**. **Sri Lanka’s system** is **religiously segmented**, with **Pirivenas (Buddhist monastic schools)** and **colonial-era institutions** shaping governance, while **vocational training gaps** persist despite **free education**.

**Hong Kong’s bilingual (Chinese/English) model** features **free primary education** and subsidized pre-schooling, with **HKDSE exams** determining progression. Recent reforms emphasize **patriotism**, altering curricula and introducing **Citizenship and Social Development**, though **teacher shortages** and **student exodus** remain challenges. **Vocational training exchanges** with Guangdong aim to align education with labor demand, but **patriotism curriculum reforms** spark debate.

**England** operates a **fee-regulated system** (£9,535/year), with **Russell Group universities** leading in prestige. The **national funding formula** and **academy reforms** improved outcomes in deprived areas, though **PISA rankings (13th place)** reflect modest performance. **Vocational training** (e.g., T Levels, apprenticeships) complements academic pathways, while **Open University/WEA** support adult education.

#### **Higher Education and Future Directions**
**Hong Kong’s JUPAS system** determines admissions, with **teacher resignations (~3,500 in 2022–2023)** and **student exodus** highlighting instability. **Ghana’s tertiary education** is highly competitive (≤24 WASSCE points), with **universities and polytechnics** competing for students. **Sweden’s free higher education** (for citizens) faces challenges like **school violence and declining PISA scores**, particularly among immigrant students, while **independent schools (*Friskolor*)** spark debates over **public funding**.

**Norway’s 9-year compulsory foundation phase** transitions to **folkeskole (grades 1–7)** and **upper secondary (grades 8–10)**, with **vocational and academic pathways** standardized by **KKNI (2012)**. Private schools account for **10% of students**, and **camp schools (*leirskole*)** offer immersive training. **Armenia’s K-12 system** integrates **English, Chinese, and Russian**, with **state exams** determining admissions, and **special education** founded in **1898** ensuring inclusive support.

**Key Challenges:**
- **Funding disparities** persist in **Israel (Arab students), Nigeria (state vs. private schools), and Hong Kong (teacher shortages)**.
- **Curriculum modernization** is critical, whether through **AI integration (Sri Lanka), vocational alignment (Hong Kong), or secularism (France)**.
- **Teacher shortages**, **rural-urban divides**, and **political influences** (e.g., patriotism curricula in Hong Kong) shape sustainability.
- **Global trends** emphasize **adaptive, inclusive models** balancing **academic rigor, vocational readiness, and cultural relevance**.

#### **Emerging Systems: Egypt, Sweden, Indonesia, and Armenia**
**Egypt’s system**, rooted in **colonial and Nasser-era reforms**, offers **free compulsory education (6–14 years)** but struggles with **weak academic quality (TIMSS: 49th in reading)** and **youth unemployment (36.1%)**. **COVID-19 digital shifts** and **Nasser reforms** introduced partial improvements, but **outdated curricula** and **low PISA scores** persist. Reforms like the **Higher Education Enhancement Project (HEEP)** aim to address gaps.

**Sweden’s 9-year *Grundskola*** is **grading-based (A–E)**, with **upper secondary education** offering **18 programs** (academic/vocational). Higher education is **free for citizens**, though **school violence, cheating scandals, and declining PISA scores** (especially among immigrant students) remain issues. **Independent schools (*Friskolor*)** (30% of upper-school pupils) spark debates over **public funding and religious autonomy**.

**Indonesia’s 12-year compulsory system** spans **primary (6 years), junior high (3 years), and high school (3 years)**, with **public/religious schools** dominating. **Teacher shortages, funding gaps, and quality in remote areas** persist, while **Islamic schools** blend secular and religious content. **Early childhood education (*PAUD*)** is **heavily privatized (99.35% private TKs)**.

**Armenia’s K-12 system** (ages 2–18) includes **mandatory subjects (English, Chinese, Russian)** and **state exams** for university admissions. **Special education**, founded in **1898**, integrates disabled students via **individualized support**. Higher education aligns with the **Bologna Process**, with **26 state universities** and **33 private institutions**, ensuring **quality assurance** through **EQAR frameworks**.
