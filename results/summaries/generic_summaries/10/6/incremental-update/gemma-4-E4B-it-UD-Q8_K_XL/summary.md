Global education systems exhibit significant diversity, ranging from centralized models (like France and Egypt) to varied national structures (such as Nigeria and Ghana). Indonesia utilizes a twelve-year system for over 50 million students, balancing a national curriculum based on Pancasila with decentralized and religious options like *pesantren*. Norway features a progressive, multi-stage system where mandatory schooling covers elementary (ages 6–12) and lower secondary (ages 13–16) with non-graded primary education, followed by public upper secondary (ages 16–19) offering general or vocational tracks via a "2+2 model." Norway's higher education is largely state-run, featuring theory-focused universities and vocational University Colleges, emphasizing inclusive education; its academic year runs for two semesters.

Armenia maintains a 100% literacy rate and offers free, compulsory K-12 education, culminating in a Certificate of Full Secondary Education. While public schooling adheres to a national curriculum, private options exist subject to national quality standards, which can lead to preparedness gaps. University admission relies heavily on state exams. Armenia's tertiary sector is strong, comprising 26 state and 33 private institutions, with particular strengths in Medicine, Engineering, and IT. Furthermore, Armenia is aligning its higher education with Europe through participation in the Bologna Process and the European Higher Education Area, supported by a quality assurance agency. Despite these advances, the sector faces challenges like student absenteeism and dropout rates linked to child labor and quality, leading to the emergence of technology-based after-school centers to impart 21st-century skills.
