### **Education in Egypt**

#### **Historical Overview**
Egypt’s education system has deep roots, dating back to ancient times, with the establishment of the first known school and library, the **Bar Ankh**, around 2000 BCE. The ancient Egyptians emphasized literacy and education in hieroglyphs. During the Coptic period, religious institutions like churches became centers of education. The **Alexandria University** (300 BCE) became a hub for scholars like Archimedes and Euclid.

The **Fatimid period** (970–972 CE) introduced key institutions like **Al-Azhar** and **Dar al-Ilm**, which shaped Islamic education. The **Ayyubid and Mamluk periods** (12th–16th centuries) saw decentralized education, with informal teaching methods and the **ijāza** certification system. Women’s education was limited but not absent, with some women contributing to Islamic scholarship and literacy.

The **French Expedition (1798)** introduced modern education, leading to the establishment of the **Institut d'Égypte**. **Muhammad Ali’s reforms** (1805–1848) modernized education, creating a dual system of traditional religious schools (**mansouri**) and secular institutions. The **British occupation (1882)** led to neglect, with budget cuts and reduced access to education.

Post-independence reforms introduced **free compulsory education**, though challenges like quality and equity persisted. Today, Egypt has a **centralized system** with three main levels: basic education (ages 4–14), secondary education (ages 15–17), and tertiary education (universities and polytechnics).

---

#### **Education System**
**Basic Education (Ages 4–14)**
- Compulsory and free, covering kindergarten, primary, and preparatory school.
- Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, and mathematics.
- Examinations determine promotion, except in grades 6 and 9.
- **National Strategic Plan for Pre-University Education Reform** emphasizes access, teacher development, and curriculum improvement.

**Secondary Education (Ages 15–17)**
- Divided into general and technical tracks.
- General track lasts three years, leading to national exams.
- Technical programs (e.g., industrial, agricultural) last three to five years.
- **Applied Technology Schools** and the **Industrial Training Council (ITC)** promote vocational education.

**Higher Education**
- **Public universities** (27) and **private universities** (27) offer diverse programs.
- **Public institutions** charge minimal fees (0–5,000 EGP/year).
- **Private institutions** charge higher fees (up to 30,000 EGP/year).
- **Ministry of Higher Education** oversees governance, with reforms focusing on quality and accreditation.
- **QS World University Rankings 2025** includes 15 Egyptian universities, with **Cairo University** ranked 350th.

**International Schools**
- **184 schools** offer curricula from foreign countries (e.g., British, American, IB).
- Mostly private, catering to expatriates and affluent locals.

**Al-Azhar System**
- A parallel system with a six-year primary stage, three-year preparatory stage, and three-year secondary stage.
- Separate schools for boys and girls, with religious studies as a core focus.
- **8,272 schools** in 2007, enrolling **1.9 million students**.

**Agricultural Education**
- **133 secondary agricultural schools** offer theoretical and practical training.
- Collaborate with the **Ministry of Agriculture** for hands-on experience.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Quality and Equity**: Low teacher salaries, high student-to-teacher ratios, and reliance on rote memorization hinder progress.
- **Private Tutoring**: Over **58% of families** spend **61% of educational budgets** on private tutoring, exacerbating inequality.
- **Funding Shortages**: Embezzlement and inadequate infrastructure limit access.
- **Corporal Punishment**: Persistent in some schools, despite legal restrictions.
- **Youth Unemployment**: High among graduates, particularly women (53.2% for females).

### **Education in Indonesia**

#### **Historical Overview**
Indonesia’s education system evolved under **Islamic kingdoms, Dutch colonialism, Japanese occupation, and post-independence governance**. The **Pondok Pesantren** (Islamic boarding schools) emerged during the Islamic state period. The **Dutch colonial era (1870)** introduced formal education, segregated by social status, with schools like **Sekolah Rakjat** (folk schools) for natives.

The **Japanese occupation (WWII)** consolidated education systems, emphasizing military and cultural indoctrination. Post-independence, the **1945 Constitution** guaranteed education as a right, leading to the establishment of the **Ministry of Education, Culture, and Research**. The **Guided Democracy (1967–1988)** and **New Order (1968–1998)** periods emphasized **Pancasila** (national principles) in curricula.

#### **Education System**
**Early Education**
- **PAUD (Pendidikan Anak Usia Dini)**: Covers playgroups (taman bermain) and kindergartens (taman kanak-kanak).
- **99.35% of kindergartens** are private, with **Class A and B** divisions.

**Public Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory for 12 years** (ages 6–18), with **5 days/week** attendance.
- **State-run schools** (supervised by the **Ministry of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology**).
- **Multilingual curriculum**: Rote learning dominates, with **Pancasila** (national principles) emphasized until the 1990s.
- **Key Stages**:
  - **Primary School (Sekolah Dasar)**: Ages 6–12 (6 years).
  - **Junior High School (SMP)**: Ages 12–16 (3 years).
  - **Senior High School (SMA)**: Ages 16–18 (3 years).

**Islamic Schools**
- **Pesantren**: Traditional Islamic boarding schools, led by **kyais** (religious scholars).
- **Madrasah**: Integrate religious and secular studies.
- **Sekolah Islam**: Follow the state curriculum with added Islamic content.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities, institutes, academies, and conservatories** offer degrees from bachelor’s to PhD.
- **Indonesian National Qualification Framework (KKNI)** standardizes qualifications.
- **Public universities** (e.g., **Universitas Indonesia, Universitas Gadjah Mada**) are prestigious.
- **Private universities** (e.g., **Bina Nusantara University**) cater to niche fields.

**International Schools**
- **184 schools** offer curricula from foreign countries (e.g., British, American, IB).
- Mostly private, catering to expatriates and affluent locals.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Inadequate salaries and qualifications in rural areas.
- **Large Class Sizes**: Affects learning outcomes.
- **Vocational Education**: Limited access and outdated curricula.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women’s education remains underrepresented in certain fields.
- **Quality and Equity**: Low PISA scores (ranked 118th in primary education quality).

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Historical Overview**
Sri Lanka’s education system dates back **2,300 years**, with Buddhist monks introducing Sanskrit education. The **British colonial period** established schools like **Royal College, Colombo**, and **Trinity College, Kandy**. Post-independence, the **1938 Education Act** made education free and universal. The **1942 Committee** recommended national languages over English, leading to the **Madhya Maha Vidyalayas (MMV)**.

**Post-colonial reforms** included the **National Council for Curriculum and Assessment**, **Madrasas**, and **International Schools**. The **1978 Universities Act** decentralized university governance. Today, Sri Lanka has a **multi-religious system** with **national, provincial, and private schools**.

#### **Education System**
**Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory until grade 9** (ages 6–14).
- **National Schools**: Directly funded by the **Ministry of Education**.
- **Provincial Schools**: Funded by local governments.
- **Pirivenas**: Buddhist schools for priests and lay students.
- **Private Schools**: Offer English-medium education (e.g., **St. Joseph’s College, St. Bridget’s Convent**).
- **International Schools**: Prepare students for **IGCSE, A-Levels, or IB**.

**University Education**
- **15 state universities** (e.g., **University of Colombo, University of Peradeniya**).
- **Open University of Sri Lanka**: Offers distance learning.
- **Private universities**: Limited due to political opposition.

**Vocational Education**
- **National Vocational Qualifications System (NVQSL)**: Seven levels of qualifications.
- **Technical colleges** and **Open University** provide vocational training.

**Special Needs Education**
- **Special Needs Assistants (SNAs)** support students with disabilities.
- **Sekolah Luar Biasa (SLB)**: Separate schools for students with special needs.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Competitive and Rigid System**: High pressure on exams (e.g., **GCE O/L, A/L**).
- **Private Universities**: Blocked due to protests over competition with state universities.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and poor working conditions.
- **Textbook Bias**: Negative portrayals of Arabs in textbooks.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women underrepresented in STEM fields.

### **Education in Armenia**

#### **Historical Overview**
Armenia’s education system traces back to **ancient Persia**, with the **Academy of Gondishapur** (3rd century CE). The **Safavid era** introduced modern education, while the **Qajar era** saw the **Educational Committee** promote reforms. Post-Soviet reforms (1990s) shifted curricula to emphasize **Armenian history and culture**.

The **1990–91 school year** saw **1,307 primary/secondary schools** and **10 post-secondary institutions**. Today, Armenia has **26 state universities** and **33 private higher education institutions**, with a focus on **STEM, medicine, and Armenian studies**.

#### **Education System**
**K-12 Education**
- **Preschool**: Ages 2–6 (nursery and kindergarten).
- **Primary School**: Ages 6–10 (4 years).
- **Middle School**: Ages 10–15 (5 years).
- **High School**: Ages 16–18 (3 years).
- **Exams**: State exams determine university admission.

**Public vs. Private Schools**
- **Public schools**: 97% of schools, funded by the state.
- **Private schools**: Follow national curriculum but offer specialized programs.
- **Teacher Qualifications**: Require a bachelor’s degree and certification.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Yerevan State University, Yerevan State Medical University**.
- **Private universities**: **American University of Armenia, Eurasia International University**.
- **Bologna Process**: Adopted to align with European higher education standards.
- **Student Absenteeism**: High dropout rates linked to child labor and poor education quality.

**International Programs**
- **Chinese-Armenian Friendship School**: First Chinese language school in Yerevan.
- **Erasmus+ and TEMPUS Programs**: Support academic exchanges.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Student Absenteeism**: Linked to child labor and education quality.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and lack of incentives.
- **Postgraduate Education**: Limited opportunities for research and innovation.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women underrepresented in STEM fields.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **Historical Overview**
Israel’s education system evolved under **Jewish diaspora traditions** and **Zionist movements**. The **1920s** saw the establishment of **integrated schools** like **Neve Shalom**. Post-independence, the **1949 Basic Law** guaranteed education as a right. The **1984 Neve Shalom-Wāḥat as-Salām** school integrated Jewish and Arab students.

#### **Education System**
**School Tiers and Tracks**
- **Primary (1–6)**: Ages 6–12.
- **Middle School (7–9)**: Ages 12–15.
- **High School (10–12)**: Ages 15–18.
- **Tracks**:
  - **State-Secular (Mamlachti)**: General curriculum.
  - **State-Religious (Mamlachti Dati)**: Intensive Jewish studies.
  - **Independent Religious (Haredi)**: Focus on Torah.
  - **Arab Schools**: Teach in Arabic, emphasizing Arab history and culture.

**Matriculation Exams (Bagrut)**
- Required for university admission and military service.
- **61.5% of 12th graders** earned a matriculation certificate in 2014.

**Higher Education**
- **Public universities**: **Hebrew University, Technion, Tel Aviv University**.
- **Open University of Israel**: Accepts all applicants.
- **Tuition**: Subsidized for military service graduates.
- **Student Aid**: Loans, grants, and scholarships from the **Ministry of Education**.

**International Rankings**
- **Top 200 in STEM fields** (e.g., **Technion, Hebrew University**).
- **OECD Ranking**: 2nd for tertiary education attainment (46% of 25–64-year-olds).

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Haredi Education**: Low participation in mainstream education.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low wages and high turnover.
- **Textbook Bias**: Negative portrayals of Arabs in textbooks.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women underrepresented in STEM fields.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **Historical Overview**
Norway’s education system dates back to **2000 BCE**, with cathedral schools in Trondheim and Oslo. The **Reformation (1537)** introduced Latin schools, and the **folkeskole (1889)** made primary education mandatory. The **1970s** saw the transition from **folkeskole** to **grunnskole** (foundation school).

#### **Education System**
**Primary School (Ages 6–12)**
- **Barneskole**: Compulsory for 7 years (1969).
- **Subjects**: Swedish, mathematics, English, science, religion, music, and arts.
- **Grading**: A–F scale (A=best).

**Lower Secondary School (Ages 13–16)**
- **Ungdomsskole**: Compulsory for 3 years.
- **Electives**: Languages, technology, and vocational tracks.

**Upper Secondary School (Ages 16–19)**
- **Videregående skole**: 3 years, 99% enrollment rate.
- **Programmes**: General (language, history) or vocational (e.g., carpentry, nursing).
- **Graduation**: Requires passing core subjects (Swedish, English, mathematics).

**Higher Education**
- **Universities and university colleges**: Offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees.
- **Tuition**: Free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens; higher fees for international students.
- **SweSAT**: Standardized test for university admission.

**Special Education**
- **Inclusive Schools**: All students receive support for disabilities.
- **Special Needs Assistants (SNAs)**: Provide tailored learning support.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **PISA Scores**: Declined since 2006, with concerns over cheating.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low wages and high student-to-teacher ratios.
- **Private Schools (Friskolor)**: Controversial due to profit motives and inequality.

### **Education in Ghana**

#### **Historical Overview**
Ghana’s education system began with **Portuguese and Dutch Castle Schools** in the 16th century. The **19th century** saw missionary schools (e.g., **Philip Quaque Boys School**). Post-independence, **Kwame Nkrumah** promoted universal education, leading to the **1961 Education Act** and the **Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology**.

#### **Education System**
**Basic Education (Ages 4–14)**
- **Kindergarten (2 years)**: Optional.
- **Primary School (6 years)**: Free and compulsory.
- **Junior High School (3 years)**: Leads to the **Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE)**.

**Secondary Education**
- **Senior High School (SHS)**: 3 years, leading to the **West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE)**.
- **Vocational Education**: Includes **TVET (Technical and Vocational Education)**.

**Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: 6 public (e.g., **University of Ghana**) and 49 private.
- **Polytechnics**: Offer **HND (Higher National Diploma)**.
- **Colleges of Education**: Train primary and secondary school teachers.

**International Schools**
- **129 schools** offer curricula from foreign countries (e.g., **IGCSE, A-Levels**).

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Funding Shortages**: Low government spending on education.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and poor working conditions.
- **Vocational Education**: Low prestige and outdated curricula.
- **Gender Disparities**: Women underrepresented in STEM fields.

### **Education in Iran**

#### **Historical Overview**
Iran’s education system dates back to **ancient Persia**, with the **Academy of Gondishapur** (3rd century CE). The **Safavid era** introduced modern education, while the **Qajar era** saw the **Educational Committee** promote reforms. Post-revolution (1979), the **Islamization of education** became a priority, with religious studies integrated into curricula.

#### **Education System**
**Primary School (Ages 6–12)**
- **Dabestan**: 6 years, free and compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: Turkish, mathematics, Arabic, and foreign languages.

**Secondary School (Ages 12–18)**
- **Lower Secondary (7–9)**: 3 years.
- **Upper Secondary (10–12)**: 3 years, leading to the **University Entrance Exam (Konkur)**.
- **Tracks**: Turkish-Mathematics, Science, Social Sciences, Foreign Languages.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Tehran University, Sharif University, Amirkabir University**.
- **Public vs. Private**: Public universities are tuition-free; private universities charge high fees.
- **Doctoral Programs**: 3–4 years, with research focus.

**Special Education**
- **SAMPAD**: Schools for gifted children.
- **NODET**: National Organization for Development of Exceptional Talents.

**Foreign Languages**
- **English**: Most common foreign language.
- **Arabic**: Required for Quran studies.
- **French**: Added in 2022.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and high turnover.
- **Privatization**: Rising costs in private schools.
- **Indoctrination**: Emphasis on Turkish nationalism and religious studies.
- **Textbook Bias**: Negative portrayals of Armenians and other minorities.

### **Education in England**

#### **Historical Overview**
England’s education system evolved from **medieval schools** for Latin and monastic work to **charity schools** and **grammar schools** in the 19th century. The **1870 Elementary Education Act** made primary education compulsory, and the **1902 Education Act** introduced secondary schools. Women’s access to university education began in the 19th century (e.g., **Bedford College, Cambridge**).

#### **Education System**
**Compulsory Education (Ages 5–18)**
- **Early Years (Ages 3–5)**: Nursery and Reception classes.
- **Key Stages**:
  - **KS1 (Ages 5–7)**: Primary school.
  - **KS2 (Ages 7–11)**: Primary school.
  - **KS3 (Ages 11–14)**: Comprehensive or secondary school.
  - **KS4 (Ages 14–16)**: GCSE exams.
  - **KS5 (Ages 16–18)**: Sixth form or further education.

**State-Funded Schools**
- **Academies**: Converted from failing schools.
- **Free Schools**: Newly established schools.
- **Voluntary Aided Schools**: Faith-based schools with state funding.

**Private Schools**
- **7% of pupils** attend privately run schools.
- **Prep Schools**: For 8–13-year-olds.
- **Public Schools**: Historically prestigious (e.g., **Eton, Harrow**).

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London**.
- **Tuition Fees**: £9,250/year (2024–25) for domestic students.
- **Student Aid**: Loans, grants, and scholarships.

**Adult Education**
- **Open University**: Offers distance learning.
- **Workers’ Educational Association**: Provides vocational courses.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low wages and high workloads.
- **Private Tutoring**: High demand due to competitive exams.
- **Inequality**: Disparities in access to top schools.
- **PISA Scores**: Declining performance in reading and mathematics.

### **Education in Turkey**

#### **Historical Overview**
Turkey’s education system evolved under **Ottoman, Republican, and modern governance**. The **1923 Law of Integration of Education** combined three separate channels (district schools, madrasas, reform schools). The **1928 Law** introduced the Latin alphabet, and the **1930s** saw the hiring of émigré professors.

Post-independence, the **1997 reforms** made education compulsory for 8 years, and the **2012 reforms** extended it to 12 years. The **2017 curriculum** removed evolution theory and added **jihad** as part of Islamic law.

#### **Education System**
**Pre-Primary Education**
- **Optional for ages 3–6**: Nurseries and kindergartens.

**Primary School (Ages 6–14)**
- **8 years**: Free and compulsory.
- **Core Subjects**: Turkish, mathematics, science, social studies.

**Secondary Education**
- **General Secondary**: 3 years, leading to the **University Entrance Exam**.
- **Vocational Secondary**: 3–4 years, preparing for work.
- **High School**: 3 years, leading to the **Baccalaureate**.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **167 public and private universities**.
- **Public vs. Private**: Public universities charge low fees; private universities charge high fees.
- **YÖS Exam**: University entrance exam for foreign students.

**International Schools**
- **53 schools** offer the **International Baccalaureate (IB)**.

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Curriculum Reforms**: Frequent changes lead to controversy.
- **Textbook Bias**: Negative portrayals of Armenians and minorities.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and high turnover.
- **Privatization**: Rising costs in private schools.

### **Education in France**

#### **Historical Overview**
France’s education system was shaped by **Napoleon’s reforms** and **Jules Ferry’s 1881 laws**, which made education free, secular, and compulsory for ages 6–12. The **1968 student revolts** led to reforms, including the **Bologna Process** to align with European higher education standards.

#### **Education System**
**Primary School (Ages 6–11)**
- **École élémentaire**: 5 years, covering French, mathematics, science, and arts.
- **Maternelle**: Preschool (ages 3–6).

**Middle and High School (Ages 11–18)**
- **Collège**: 4 years, leading to the **Baccalauréat**.
- **Lycée**: 3 years, offering **baccalauréat général, technologique, or professionnel**.
- **CAP (Certificat d’Aptitude Professionnelle)**: Vocational diploma.

**Higher Education**
- **Universities**: **4,000 Master programmes**, **17,000 undergraduate programmes**.
- **Grandes Écoles**: Elite institutions (e.g., **École polytechnique, HEC Paris**).
- **Tuition Fees**: Low for EU students; higher for international students.

**Private Schools**
- **Under Contract**: Follow the state curriculum.
- **Without Contract**: Offer alternative curricula.

**International Schools**
- **105 schools** offer curricula from foreign countries (e.g., **IB, A-Levels**).

---

#### **Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low salaries and high workloads.
- **Inequality**: Disparities in access to top schools.
- **PISA Scores**: Declining performance in reading and mathematics.
- **Privatization**: Rising costs in private schools.
