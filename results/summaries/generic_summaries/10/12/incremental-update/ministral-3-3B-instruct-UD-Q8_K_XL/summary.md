### **Sweden vs. Nigeria vs. Hong Kong vs. Taiwan vs. Ghana vs. Iran vs. England vs. Turkey vs. France: Comparative Education Systems**

Sweden’s **9-year *grundskola* (ages 6–16) and 3-year *gymnasieskola* (ages 16–19)** system achieves **near-universal enrollment (99%)**, though it grapples with **limited vocational integration (75%)**, **grading inconsistencies**, and a **129% rise in school violence (2012–2017)**. Declining **PISA/PIRLS scores** (e.g., a **19-point drop in PIRLS 2001–2011**) and **academic integrity issues** weaken its effectiveness.

Nigeria’s system, despite **99% university enrollment via JAMB/IJMB**, faces **teacher shortages, outdated curricula, and gender/poverty barriers**, despite reforms like the **2001–2010 Master Plan**. **Informal education initiatives** (e.g., *Premier Reading Club*) and **NGO programs** (e.g., *CELLE, UNICEF’s GEP3-CTP*) aim to boost **literacy (targeted at 95% by 2030)** and digital skills, though **rural infrastructure gaps** persist.

Hong Kong’s **4-year compulsory education** is expanding to a **6+3+3 structure by 2024**, with **HKDSE/IBDP** determining tertiary access. **Tertiary education** remains highly selective, with **strict discipline**—though **immigrant/non-Cantonese students** benefit from **free induction programs**. **Enrollment declines (2020–2023)** stem from the **impact of the national security law**.

Taiwan’s **12-year compulsory system** integrates **core subjects and vocational tracks**, with **competitive higher education** (public vs. private tuition differences). **Vocational education** includes **junior colleges, institutes of technology, and universities of science/technology**, while **cram schools (*buxiban*)** supplement high-pressure academic environments. **Teacher training** involves **university programs with internships**, though challenges include **high university competition, parental influence, and administrative reforms**.

Ghana’s **20-year education system** (kindergarten to undergraduate) allocates **25% of its budget**, achieving **gender parity (GPI = 1)** in primary/secondary (2013) and **79.04% literacy (2018)**. The **12-year free compulsory system** includes **kindergarten (2 years), primary (6 years), junior high (3 years)**, and **SHS/TVET programs**, with **WASSCE exams** determining tertiary admission. **Polytechnics (3-year HND programs)** and **Colleges of Education** dominate tertiary education, though **private sector dominance (74.7%)** and **teacher training gaps** persist. **Adult literacy improved from 54% (1997) to 71.5% (2010)**, with **non-formal education** focusing on life skills but facing **skill retention and certification gaps**. **TVET programs** struggle with **low completion rates (1.6%)** and **job placement challenges**, while **ICT policies** remain **underutilized** due to **access and staff theft issues**.

Iran’s system blends **Islamic and Western traditions**, with **6-year elementary (free)**, **3-year lower-secondary (mandatory)**, and **3-year upper-secondary (non-compulsory)** stages. **Konkur exams** determine university admission, while **vocational programs** (2-year associate degrees) and **universities** (public tuition-free except Islamic Azad University) dominate. **Reforms (2012)** shifted toward **facilitator-based teaching** and **vocational alignment**, though **dropout rates (~4M children by 2014)** persist. **Hybrid learning models** (LMS, Shad software) and **distance education** expand access, but **teacher shortages (~60,000 missing)** and **language restrictions** (e.g., English banned in primary schools) remain challenges. **Women’s education** has seen **70% university enrollment growth post-1993**, with Iran holding the **world’s highest female-to-male primary enrollment ratio (1.22:1)**. **STEM dominance** is notable, with Iran ranking **3rd globally in engineers (70% female)** and **5th in STEM graduates (335,000/year)**.

England’s education system ranks **13th globally in PISA scores (avg. 503.7)** but excels in **PIRLS (4th in primary reading literacy)** and ranks **7th in the UN Education Index**. **City academies** improved outcomes in deprived areas, with **88% of schools rated good/outstanding in 2015** (up from 68% in 2010). Post-16 education includes **A-levels, T-levels, and apprenticeships**, with **state-funded higher education** (£9,535/year) and **£780M allocated for SEND support (2020–21)**. **Funding** has increased significantly since 2018, with **£7.1B allocated in 2022–23** for teacher pensions and school budgets.

Turkey’s **12-year compulsory system** (4+4+4 structure) integrates **general and vocational tracks**, with **standardized diplomas** replacing track-specific credentials since **2010**. Higher education includes **76 universities** (53 state, 23 private/foundation), with **public universities** being cost-effective and **private tuition** reaching **$30K/year**. **TÜBİTAK** drives research in **agriculture, biotechnology, IT, and defense**, supported by **60 research institutes**, attracting **~500,000 foreign students**. Controversies include **genocide glorification in textbooks**, **language education disparities**, and **mandatory religious instruction**, while **private and international schools** cater to diverse communities.

France’s education system, rooted in **Napoleon and Jules Ferry’s secular reforms**, operates under a **three-tiered higher education structure** (Licence → Master → Doctorat), standardized via the **Bologna Process**. **Public universities** (€170–€3,770/year for non-EU students) and **grandes écoles** (elite, selective) dominate, alongside **private institutions** (e.g., engineering/business schools) charging **€7K–€40K+**. **Private higher education** includes **free private colleges** and **technical private colleges**, often partnering with public universities for **accredited diplomas**. **Preparatory classes (CPGEs)**—selective post-baccalaureate programs—prepare students for *Grandes Écoles*, with **5% acceptance rates** based on **Baccalauréat performance**. **Teacher recruitment** involves **Bac+5 degrees for primary/secondary teachers** and **CAPES/Agrégation exams**, while **teacher-researchers** (PhD holders) earn **€2,300–€8,800/month**, with **secondary teachers earning €1,400–€3,900/month**.

France’s **secularism (Laïcité)** excludes religious instruction in public schools (except in Alsace/Moselle under the 1801 Concordat). The **2004 law** banned conspicuous religious symbols in schools, sparking controversy. The system serves **~18 million students**, with **2.9 million in higher education**, reflecting a **declining youth population** and gradual aging. **Safety data** shows minimal school violence (**39/75,000 state schools** reported serious incidents in 2000). Historically, France expanded its **faculties to 350** by the 19th century, with **Camille Sée** establishing female secondary schools in **1880 (36 by 1896)**.
