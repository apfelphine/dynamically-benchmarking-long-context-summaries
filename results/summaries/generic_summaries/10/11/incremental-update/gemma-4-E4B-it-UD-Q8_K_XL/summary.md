**Global Education Systems Overview**

Global education systems are highly diverse, presenting a spectrum from free undergraduate models (like Sri Lanka) to structured K-12 mandates (like Israel). While some nations achieve high literacy (e.g., Armenia), many contend with issues such as funding deficiencies, inequity (e.g., Indonesia), or high dropout rates (e.g., Nigeria).

**Regional and National Models**

*   **Nordic/European Models:** Norway mandates Master's degrees for teachers and structures schooling with varying tracks, while Sweden offers a comprehensive path from preschool to tertiary level, featuring nine years of compulsory schooling and vocational options aligned with the Bologna Process.
*   **Taiwan:** Features a 12-year public system in Mandarin, complemented by specialized education (ages 2-22) and a strong private cram school culture that keeps public university costs low.
*   **Hong Kong:** Follows a structured progression leading to the HKDSE and JUPAS for university entry, integrating active learning and vocational training within its post-1997 framework.
*   **Iranian Education System:** This centralized, Islamized system mandates religious instruction and gender segregation. Despite high female university enrollment (over 70%) and STEM capacity, it faces challenges from inequality and dropouts linked to privatization.
*   **Turkish Education System:** Is undergoing reforms toward secularization and nationalization under a "4+4+4" model since 2012, emphasizing Turkish nationalism and *laïcité*.
*   **Ghana:** Provides education across basic, secondary (with academic/vocational tracks), and tertiary levels. Although adult literacy was high by 2010, tertiary access remains uneven, favoring wealthier males, prompting initiatives like online scholarships.
*   **France:** Implements free, secular, and compulsory public instruction (*Laïcité*) for its population. Compulsory schooling is structured through primary, middle, and high school, culminating in the *baccalauréat* or CAP. Higher education adheres to the Bologna process, involving public universities, IUTs, and competitive *Grandes Écoles*.
*   **England (UK):** Education operates with compulsory schooling from age 5 to 18, encompassing diverse schools (Academies, Community, etc.), with 93% in state schools. State schools adhere to the National Curriculum and are monitored by Ofsted. Primary schools are largely local, while secondary schools are mostly comprehensive, though selective grammar schools exist.
    *   **UK Performance & Policy:** UK 15-year-olds rank 13th globally in literacy, math, and science (PISA), though primary students ranked 4th in reading in 2021, and the UK ranks 7th in the UN Education Index. A significant policy shift since 1997 introduced city academies in deprived areas, which contributed to 88% of English schools being rated good or outstanding by 2015. Funding transitioned to a national formula in 2018, accompanied by budget increases for SEND pupils.
    *   **UK Structure:** Compulsory subjects include PSHE and RSE. Key Stage 4 allows elective study in various fields, concluding around age 16 with GCSEs. Post-16 education extends until age 18 through options like Sixth Forms, FE colleges, or apprenticeships, offering qualifications such as A-levels and T Levels. Higher Education (HE) involves universities and colleges; undergraduates start at 18, leading to Level 6 (bachelor's) or integrated Master's. Fees are largely income-contingent, overseen by the QAA and OfS, and all qualifications use shared European-linked frameworks.
