### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Administration**
Sri Lanka’s education system is administered by three main ministries: **Education**, **Higher Education**, **Education Services**, and **Skills Development**. The **Department of Examinations** oversees national examinations. Exceptions exist for tertiary education, where several public universities and institutes fall under different ministries, leading to inefficiencies. The **National Institute of Education (NIE)** aims to lead education with quality, equity, and relevance in a pluralistic society.

#### **History**
Education in Sri Lanka dates back over **2,300 years**, influenced by Buddhist monks and colonial missionaries. The **Colebrooke Commission (1836)** introduced a standardized system, leading to the establishment of **Royal College, Colombo**, and single-sex schools. Free education was introduced in **1938** under Dr. C.W.W. Kannangara, and the **National Education Act (1944)** formalized compulsory education. Post-independence, reforms included secular multi-religious schools, and the system expanded significantly.

#### **Structure of Education**
The education system is divided into **five levels**:
- **Primary (5 years)** → Scholarship exam at the end.
- **Junior Secondary (4 years)** → GCE Ordinary Level (O/L) exams.
- **Senior Secondary (2 years)** → GCE Advanced Level (A/L) exams.
- **Collegiate (2 years)** → Prepares for A/L exams.
- **Tertiary (4–6 years)** → University admission via A/L exams.

**Public schools** are the majority, with **10,155 public schools** (373 national, 9,782 provincial) and **104 private schools**, serving **4.2 million students**. **Pirivenas** (Buddhist monastic schools) cater to clergy and higher education. **Private schools** offer specialized curricula, while **international schools** cater to expatriates.

#### **Higher Education**
Sri Lanka has **15 state universities** and **private universities** offering degrees from bachelor’s to PhD levels. Admission is highly competitive, based on **GCE Advanced Level (A/L) exams**. The **Mahapola Fund** supports higher education. Private universities are regulated but face criticism for quality and competition with state universities.

#### **Vocational Education**
Managed by the **Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission**, vocational training includes apprenticeships and technical colleges. The **National Vocational Qualifications System (NVQSL)** offers structured qualifications from Level 1 to 7.

#### **Criticisms**
The education system is criticized for being **too competitive and rigid**, with inefficiencies in administration. Private universities face opposition due to perceived **lower standards** and competition for state university spots.

### **Education in Egypt**

#### **History**
Egypt’s education system has roots in **ancient times**, with the **Bar Ankh (House of Life)** being the first known school. The **Fatimid era (970–1171)** introduced **Al-Azhar University** and the **Dar al-Ilm**, which became centers of Islamic scholarship. The **French Expedition (1798)** established the **Institut d’Égypte**, leading to the **Description de l’Égypte**. Under **Muhammad Ali (1805–1848)**, modern education was introduced, blending traditional Islamic schools with secular institutions.

#### **Education System**
Egypt’s system is **highly centralized**, divided into **three main levels**:
- **Basic Education (6–14 years)** → Free, compulsory.
- **Secondary Education (15–17 years)** → Two tracks: general and technical.
- **Higher Education** → Extensive system with **27 public universities**, **51 public non-university institutions**, **27 private universities**, and **89 private higher institutions**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** covers nine years (ages 4–14).
- **National Strategic Plan for Pre-University Education Reform** focuses on access, teacher development, and curriculum improvements.
- **Secondary education** includes general and technical tracks, with **Applied Technology Schools** for vocational training.
- **Higher education** is highly centralized, with **limited autonomy** for institutions.

#### **Challenges**
Despite progress, Egypt faces **low-quality education**, high student-to-teacher ratios, and reliance on rote memorization. The **COVID-19 pandemic** accelerated the shift to **distance education**, though challenges remain in infrastructure and teacher training.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **Structure**
Israel’s education system consists of **three tiers**:
- **Primary (Grades 1–6, ages 6–12)**
- **Middle School (Grades 7–9, ages 12–15)**
- **High School (Grades 10–12, ages 15–18)**

Compulsory education spans **kindergarten through 12th grade**. Schools are divided into **four tracks**:
- **State-secular (Mamlachti)**
- **State-religious (Mamlachti dati)**
- **Independent religious (Haredi/Chinuch Atzmai)**
- **Arab**

#### **Key Features**
- **Haredi yeshivas** follow an independent schedule.
- **Integrated schools** (e.g., Neve Shalom) coexist with Jewish and Arab students.
- **Matriculation exams (Bagrut)** determine university admission and job prospects.
- **Higher education** includes mandatory military service for undergraduates.

#### **Higher Education**
Israel’s universities rank highly globally, with **six universities in the top 100 in Asia**. **Hebrew University, Technion, and Tel Aviv University** are among the top institutions. **Graduate studies** require the **Psychometric Entrance Test (PET)** and strong GCE Bagrut scores.

#### **Criticisms**
Critics argue that **test scores do not reflect overall education quality**, particularly for Haredi and special education students. **Large class sizes** and **low teacher wages** are also concerns.

### **Education in Indonesia**

#### **History**
Indonesia’s education system evolved under **Dutch colonial rule (1870–1942)**, with schools segregated by social status. **Pondok Pesantren** (Islamic boarding schools) and **Taman Siswa** (nationalist schools) emerged. Post-independence, the **1945 Constitution** declared education a right, leading to **free, secular, and anti-elitist** policies.

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory for 12 years** (ages 6–18). The system includes:
- **Preschool (PAUD, ages 3–6)**
- **Primary School (Sekolah Dasar, 6 years)**
- **Junior High School (SMP, 3 years)**
- **Senior High School (SMA, 3 years)**
- **Tertiary Education (Universities, Polytechnics, etc.)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public schools** (state-funded, free for primary and junior high).
- **Private schools** (offer specialized curricula, often bilingual).
- **International schools** (teach in English, cater to expatriates).
- **Islamic schools (Pesantren, Madrasah)** focus on religious and secular education.

#### **Challenges**
- **Rote learning** and **low teacher morale** persist.
- **Vocational education** is underfunded and lacks modern facilities.
- **Digital learning** is growing but faces infrastructure gaps.

### **Education in Ireland**

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory from ages 5–16** (or completion of three years of second-level education). The system includes:
- **Primary School (8 years, ages 4–12)**
- **Secondary School (6 years, ages 12–18)**
  - **Junior Cycle (Years 1–3)**
  - **Transition Year (Year 4, optional)**
  - **Senior Cycle (Years 5–6)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **National Schools** (state-funded, mostly Catholic).
- **Gaelscoileanna** (Irish-language schools).
- **Multidenominational schools** (non-religious).
- **Preparatory schools** (independent, fee-charging).

#### **Higher Education**
Ireland ranks **2nd in reading and 11th in mathematics** (PISA 2022). Universities offer **English-medium courses**, with some Irish-language options.

#### **Special Needs Education**
The **Education for Persons with Special Educational Needs Act (2004)** supports students with disabilities. **Special Needs Assistants (SNAs)** provide additional support.

### **Education in Armenia**

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory from ages 2–15**, divided into:
- **Preschool (2–6 years)**
- **Primary School (1–4 grades, ages 6–10)**
- **Middle School (5–9 grades, ages 10–15)**
- **High School (10–12 grades, ages 16–18)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public schools** (97% of schools, state-funded).
- **Private schools** (follow national curriculum, offer specialized programs).

#### **Higher Education**
Armenia has **26 state universities** and **33 private higher educational institutions**. The **Bologna Process** has reformed higher education, aligning with European standards. **English and Chinese language schools** are expanding.

#### **Challenges**
- **High student absenteeism** (linked to child labor and education quality).
- **Teacher training** is centralized but faces shortages in rural areas.

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **Structure**
Education is **free and compulsory for 9 years (ages 5–14)** under the **Universal Basic Education (UBE) Act (1999)**. The system includes:
- **Primary School (6 years)**
- **Junior Secondary School (3 years)**
- **Senior Secondary School (3 years)**
- **Tertiary Education (Universities, Polytechnics, etc.)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Federal-sponsored secondary schools** (highly competitive, low fees).
- **State-sponsored secondary schools** (free but require fees for books/uniforms).
- **Private, religious, and independent schools** (expensive, offer specialized curricula).
- **International schools** (teach in English, cater to expatriates).

#### **Higher Education**
Nigeria has **153 universities** (40 federal, 45 state, 68 private). **Admission** is based on **JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board) scores** or direct entry. **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in funding and curriculum relevance.

#### **Challenges**
- **High illiteracy rates** (31% as of 2022).
- **Gender disparities** and **militant groups** (e.g., Boko Haram) disrupt education.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory from ages 6–16**, divided into:
- **Elementary School (Grunnskole, ages 6–12)**
- **Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole, ages 13–16)**
- **Upper Secondary School (Videregående skole, ages 16–19)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public schools** (free, state-funded).
- **Independent schools (friskoler)** (privately run, state-funded).
- **Special education** for students with disabilities.

#### **Higher Education**
Norway has **11 universities** and **10 university colleges**, offering **bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees**. **Tuition is free for EU/EEA students**, with low fees for others.

#### **Challenges**
- **High student-to-teacher ratios**.
- **Criticism of rote learning** and **standardized testing**.

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory for 12 years (ages 6–18)**. The system includes:
- **Kindergarten (optional, ages 3–6)**
- **Elementary School (6 years, ages 6–11)**
- **Junior High School (3 years, ages 12–14)**
- **Senior High School (3 years, ages 15–17)**
- **Tertiary Education (Universities, Junior Colleges, etc.)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public schools** (state-funded).
- **Private schools** (offer specialized curricula).
- **International schools** (teach in English, cater to expatriates).
- **Cram schools (buxiban)** (supplementary classes for high-pressure exams).

#### **Higher Education**
Taiwan has **many universities**, including **National Taiwan University** and **National Chengchi University**. **Admission** is based on **national exams**. **English and Chinese language schools** are popular for international students.

#### **Challenges**
- **Intense pressure** for high exam scores.
- **Overemphasis on cram schools**.

### **Education in France**

#### **Structure**
Education is **compulsory from ages 3–16**, divided into:
- **Preschool (École Maternelle, ages 3–6)**
- **Elementary School (École Élémentaire, ages 6–11)**
- **Middle School (Collège, ages 11–15)**
- **High School (Lycée, ages 15–18)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **Public schools** (state-funded, free).
- **Private schools** (under contract or independent).
- **International schools** (teach in English, cater to expatriates).

#### **Higher Education**
France has **universities, grandes écoles, and private colleges**. **Tuition is low for EU students**, with higher fees for international students. **Bachelor’s (Licence) and Master’s degrees** are common, with **grandes écoles** offering specialized programs.

#### **Challenges**
- **High student diversity** requires culturally sensitive curricula.
- **Criticism of rote learning** and **standardized testing**.

### **Education in England**

#### **Structure**
Education is **legally compulsory from ages 5–18**, divided into:
- **Early Years (Reception, ages 4–5)**
- **Primary School (Years 1–6, ages 5–11)**
- **Secondary School (Years 7–11, ages 11–16)**
- **Post-16 Education (Years 12–13, ages 16–18)**

#### **Types of Schools**
- **State-funded schools** (community, foundation, academy, free schools).
- **Private schools** (fee-charging, offer specialized curricula).
- **Independent schools** (non-state, regulated by ISI).

#### **Higher Education**
England has **universities, polytechnics, and private colleges**. **Tuition fees** range from **£9,000–£35,000/year** for international students. **Russell Group universities** are seen as prestigious.

#### **Challenges**
- **High competition** for university admission.
- **Criticism of standardized testing** and **teacher shortages**.
