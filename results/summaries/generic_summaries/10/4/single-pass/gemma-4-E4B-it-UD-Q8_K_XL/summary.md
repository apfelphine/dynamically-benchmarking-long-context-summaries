# Summary of Global Education Systems

## France
**History and Curriculum:** Modern French education began in the late 19th century with Jules Ferry's laws, establishing free, mandatory, and secular public schooling for children aged 6 to 12. The curriculum heavily emphasizes French literature and history to transmit a "common culture," a mandate made more critical by increasing student diversity.
**Governance and Structure:** All educational programs are governed by the Ministry of National Education and Youth. The curriculum is standardized across public, semi-public, and subsidized institutions at primary and secondary levels. The system includes distinct stages: *école élémentaire* (primary), *collège* (middle school, ages 11-15), and *lycée* (high school, ages 15-18), leading to the *baccalauréat*.
**Higher Education:** Higher education is structured in Licence, Master's, and Doctorat degrees, utilizing the ECTS credit system. It is divided between highly selective *grandes écoles* and public universities. Tuition fees are very low for EU/EEA citizens but vary for international students.

## Turkey
**Historical Evolution and Modernization:** Following the establishment of the Republic, educational institutions were unified under the Ministry of National Education, aiming to implement secularism. Compulsory education was extended to eight, and later 12 years.
**Curriculum and Ideology:** The system promotes Turkish nationalism rooted in Atatürk's thought. Religious instruction, which was previously excluded, has been re-established, with recent curricula adding concepts like *jihad* alongside Islamic law.
**System Structure:** Pre-primary education is optional. Primary school is compulsory and free. Secondary education encompasses general and vocational tracks, aiming to prepare students for higher education or the workforce.
**Criticism:** The system faces controversy due to constant reforms, and state-approved textbooks are criticized for promoting Armenian genocide denial.

## England
**Structure and Compulsion:** Compulsory education is mandated for all children aged 5 to 18. The system is divided into Early Years, 4 Key Stages, and post-16 education. State-funded schools form the majority, though private schools exist.
**Curriculum and Assessment:** State schools adhere to a National Curriculum covering 13 subjects in primary/secondary school. Qualifications include GCSEs (at age 16) and A-levels/T-levels (post-16).
**School Types and Funding:** State-funded schools include Academies, Community schools, and Free schools. Private schools are fee-charging. Education is supported by a national formula funding system, though independent schools operate outside the National Curriculum.

## Iran
**Historical and Political Shifts:** Education's history spans from ancient Persian emphasis on knowledge to modern centralization. Post-1979 Islamic Revolution, the system underwent significant Islamization, including the segregation of students by sex.
**System and Focus:** Education is highly centralized under the Ministry of Education. The system features distinct tracks: theoretical, technical-vocational, and manual skills.
**Higher Education:** Public universities offer tuition-free education for students, but university students are generally required to serve the government for several years. Women have seen a massive increase in university enrollment, becoming the majority student demographic.

## Ghana
**System Overview and Governance:** Education is managed by the Ministry of Education, with implementation decentralized to local district assemblies. The system is divided into basic (12 years, compulsory), secondary, and tertiary levels.
**Curriculum and Access:** Basic education is free and compulsory. The system aims for universal access, though socioeconomic status heavily influences tertiary education participation.
**Challenges:** Despite high primary enrollment, vocational education suffers from a poor reputation, low completion rates, and challenges in formalization. There are ongoing issues with equity and the dominance of male and wealthy students in tertiary education.

## Taiwan
**System Structure and Compulsion:** The public education system is compulsory for 12 years (Primary through Junior High School). Instruction is predominantly in Mandarin.
**Education Tracks:** The system includes distinct pathways: general education, skill-based senior high schools, and tertiary options like junior colleges and universities.
**Academic Culture:** Discipline is typically strict, with daily rituals. High academic achievement is driven by intense pressure and the reliance on national exams for university entry.
**Higher Education:** Universities are diverse, with public and private options, and many students pursue graduate studies abroad.

## Hong Kong
**Structure and Dual System:** Education is legally compulsory from age 6 to 18. The system is structured into primary, junior, and senior secondary years, with the HKDSE being the key secondary qualification.
**Instruction and Types:** Medium of instruction shifted from English to Cantonese in secondary schools, though English Medium Instruction (EMI) schools remain prestigious. Schools fall into Government, Aided, DSS, and Private categories.
**Current Trends:** There is a noticeable shift towards active, experiential pedagogy, alongside increasing focus on patriotism and national security in the curriculum.

## Nigeria
**Structure and Levels:** The system operates on a 9-3-4 model (9 years primary/junior secondary, 3 years senior secondary, 4 years tertiary). Education is monitored by the UBEC to ensure it is "free" and "compulsory."
**Curriculum and Examination:** Subjects cover core areas like Mathematics and English, with electives varying by track. The SSCE, administered by WAEC, is the major exit examination.
**Challenges:** Vocational training faces challenges due to outdated curricula and poor implementation. Literacy rates remain low, especially in the North, and gender bias persists in some educational barriers.

## Sweden
**System Philosophy and Structure:** The system is built on a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*), followed by upper secondary school (*gymnasieskola*). Primary education is mandatory and free for all children aged 6-7.
**Curriculum and Assessment:** Students take 16 compulsory subjects, with grades assigned from year 6. Upper secondary education is elective, categorized as preparatory or vocational.
**Independent Schools:** A significant portion of students attend independent (*friskolor*) schools, which receive public funding but operate under different models, leading to debates over standards and profitability.
**Higher Education:** Higher education follows the Bologna Process structure (Bachelor's, Master's, PhD) and offers both university and university college paths.

## Israel
**Societal Value and Structure:** Education is viewed as crucial for socioeconomic mobility in Israeli culture. The system has three tiers: primary (1-6), middle school (7-9), and high school (10-12).
**Sectarian Tracks:** Schools operate under four tracks: state-secular, state-religious, independent religious (*Haredi*), and Arab.
**Challenges and Policy:** While the Pupils' Rights Law prohibits discrimination, disparities exist, with Haredi students often showing lower participation in matriculation exams. The system strongly emphasizes scientific and technological skills to support the high-tech economy.

## Armenia
**Structure and Language Shift:** Education is compulsory and free, structured into primary (4 years), middle (5 years), and high school (3 years). The system has transitioned from a Soviet-controlled model to one emphasizing Armenian history and culture.
**School Types:** Public schools dominate (97%) and adhere to national curriculum guidelines. Private schools operate under licensing but rely on tuition fees.
**Higher Education:** Armenia is integrating with the European Higher Education Area via the Bologna Process, supported by numerous EU mobility programs.

## Sri Lanka
**Historical Development and Control:** Education has ancient roots, evolving from Buddhist monastic colleges to a modern system managed by multiple governmental ministries. Education was made formally free in 1938.
**Structure and Assessment:** The system comprises primary (5 years), junior secondary (4 years), senior secondary (2 years), and collegiate/tertiary levels. The G.C.E O/Ls and A/Ls serve as major examination benchmarks.
**System Issues:** State schools are government-maintained, but the system is criticized for being overly competitive and rigid, leading many students to seek private tutoring.

## Indonesia
**Colonial Legacy and Modern System:** Early education was segregated during the Dutch colonial period based on social status. Post-independence, the goal shifted to a secular, anti-discriminatory national identity.
**Structure and Curriculum:** Students are required to attend 12 years of schooling. The curriculum historically emphasized Pancasila (national principles).
**School Types:** Education is offered through state-run nonsectarian schools and religious schools (Pesantren, Madrasah, Sekolah Islam).
**Higher Education:** Higher education is offered across various institutional types (Universitas, Institut, Politeknik), organized under the Indonesian National Qualification Framework (KKNI).

## Egypt
**Historical Trajectory and Centralization:** Education spans from ancient times, with centers like Alexandria, through medieval institutions like Al-Azhar. Modernization began significantly with Muhammad Ali's reforms, introducing a dual system of religious (*mansouri*) and modern secular schools.
**System and Quality:** The system is highly centralized, divided into basic education (ages 4-14) and secondary education (ages 15-17). While enrollment is high, quality is challenged by high student-to-teacher ratios, rote memorization, and reliance on private tutoring to offset public school inadequacies.

## United Kingdom (England)
**Compulsion and Structure:** Education is compulsory from age 5 to 18. The system utilizes Key Stages, progressing from Primary to Secondary (up to age 16).
**Curriculum and Assessment:** State schools teach a balanced National Curriculum. GCSEs are taken at age 16, and A-levels/T-levels at age 18.
**School Types and Funding:** Schools vary from Community schools to Academies and Free schools, with funding regulated nationally. Ofsted rigorously inspects all state schools to monitor quality.

## Comparative Overview
Systems worldwide vary widely in governance (centralized vs. decentralized), religious influence, and funding mechanisms (state-funded vs. high private involvement). While many countries emphasize national identity and standardized testing (France, Turkey, Taiwan), others exhibit strong private sector influence (England, Ghana, Nigeria). Most systems incorporate international frameworks (Bologna, ECTS) to facilitate global mobility, though implementation varies.
