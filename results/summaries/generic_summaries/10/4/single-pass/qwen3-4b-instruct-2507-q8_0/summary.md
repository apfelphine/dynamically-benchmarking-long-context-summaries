**Education in France**  
- **History**: Napoleon established the foundation of universities and secondary education. Jules Ferry introduced secular, free, and mandatory primary education. The curriculum emphasized French literature and history.  
- **Governance**: Regulated by the Ministry of National Education and Youth. Teachers are state civil servants. Curriculum is standardized via the *Bulletin officiel*.  
- **School Year**: Runs from September to July. Includes major breaks (e.g., winter, summer).  
- **Primary School**: Ages 3–11. Preschool (maternelle) introduces basic skills; primary school covers core subjects.  
- **Middle/High School**: Compulsory until age 18. Covers French, history, math, sciences, arts, and PE. Students prepare for the *baccalauréat* (general, technical, or professional).  
- **Private Schools**: "Under contract" schools follow the national curriculum; others teach independently with state oversight.  
- **International Education**: 105 schools; regulated by AEFE.  
- **Higher Education**: Three levels (Licence, Master, Doctorat). Public universities are large and highly selective. Private schools offer diplomas or are accredited.  
- **Tuition**: Very low for EU/EEA students; higher for international students.  
- **Religion**: *Laïcité* (secularism) is central. No religious instruction in public schools (except in Alsace and Moselle).  

**Education in Turkey**  
- **History**: Post-Republic, education was secularized. 1924 Law of Integration unified education. Vocational schools expanded.  
- **Pre-primary/Primary**: Free and compulsory until age 14. Includes English and foreign languages.  
- **Secondary**: General or vocational tracks. Students take exams to enter university or vocational programs.  
- **International Education**: 105 schools; regulated by AEFE.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities dominate. Private institutions offer diplomas.  
- **Religion**: Religious instruction is taught in schools, with a focus on Islamic values.  
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on national identity and Turkish nationalism.  
- **Challenges**: High dropout rates, poor textbook quality, and religious bias.  

**Education in England**  
- **History**: Medieval schools were church-affiliated. 19th-century reforms led to state-funded schools.  
- **Compulsory Education**: Ages 5–18. Includes free preschool and school fees.  
- **School Types**: Grammar, comprehensive, academy, free schools, and university technical colleges.  
- **Curriculum**: National curriculum with core subjects. GCSEs and A-levels are key exams.  
- **Higher Education**: University degrees (Bachelor's, Master's, PhD).  
- **Fees**: Tuition fees introduced in 1998; capped at £9,000 per year.  
- **Standards**: Ofsted inspects schools.  
- **Adult Education**: Available through colleges and open universities.  

**Education in Iran**  
- **History**: Pre-Islamic education emphasized knowledge. Post-Revolution, education became Islamicized.  
- **Grades**: 12 years of basic education (ages 5–18).  
- **Religion**: Mandatory Islamic prayer and religious education.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities dominate. Private institutions offer degrees.  
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortage, low quality in informal education, and political influence.  
- **Vocational Education**: Informal and underfunded.  

**Education in Ghana**

- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory system.  
- **Secondary Education**: Academic or vocational tracks.  
- **Tertiary Education**: Growing enrollment. Universities and polytechnics offer degrees.  
- **Challenges**: Gender inequality, teacher shortages, and low vocational quality.  

**Education in Taiwan**

- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education.  
- **Curriculum**: Emphasis on science, mathematics, and language.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities are highly ranked. Private institutions offer international programs.  
- **Private Schools**: Cram schools and international schools are common.  

**Education in Hong Kong**

- **Structure**: 12-year compulsory education.  
- **Curriculum**: English or Chinese as medium of instruction.  
- **Higher Education**: Public universities (e.g., HKU, CUHK) are top-ranked.  
- **Challenges**: Declining enrollment due to political tensions; curriculum changes.  

**Education in Nigeria**

- **Secondary Education**: Academic or vocational tracks.  
- **Higher Education**: Growing number of private universities.

- **Higher Education**: Public universities and university colleges offer degrees.

- **Higher Education**: Diverse institutions offer academic and vocational programs.
