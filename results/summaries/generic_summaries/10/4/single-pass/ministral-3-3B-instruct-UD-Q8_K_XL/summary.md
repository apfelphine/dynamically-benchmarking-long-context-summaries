### **Education Systems Overview**

#### **France**
**History & Foundations**
France’s modern education system was established in the late 19th century, primarily under **Jules Ferry’s** reforms (1881–1882), which mandated free, secular, and compulsory education for children aged 6–12. The system emphasized a "common culture" rooted in French literature and history, aiming to integrate immigrants into French society. The **Falloux Laws (1850–1851)** initially granted significant influence to the Catholic Church, but Ferry’s laws repealed them, promoting secularism.

**Governance & Structure**
Education in France is regulated by the **Ministry of National Education and Youth**, with state-funded teachers and a standardized curriculum across public, semi-public, and subsidized institutions. The system is divided into:
- **Primary School (École élémentaire)**: Grades CP to CM2 (ages 6–11), focusing on foundational literacy, numeracy, and core subjects.
- **Middle School (Collège)**: Grades 7–9 (ages 11–15), preparing students for high school.
- **High School (Lycée)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), leading to the **Baccalauréat** (general, technical, or professional tracks).
- **Vocational Training**: Includes **CAP (Certificat d'Aptitude Professionnelle)** and **BTS (Brevet de Technicien Supérieur)** programs.

**Higher Education**
France’s higher education system is divided into:
- **Universities**: Offer bachelor’s (Licence), master’s (Master), and doctoral (Doctorat) degrees, with a focus on research.
- **Grandes Écoles**: Elite institutions (e.g., École Polytechnique, HEC Paris) offering specialized, highly selective programs in engineering, business, and law.
- **Private Institutions**: Include religious and independent schools, though most follow the national curriculum.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory schooling** from ages 6–18 (since 2021).
- **Secularism (laïcité)** is a core principle, excluding religious instruction in public schools (except in Alsace-Moselle under the Concordat of 1801).
- **International schools** are regulated by the **Agence pour l'Enseignement Français à l'Étranger (AEFE)**.

#### **Turkey**
**History & Foundations**
Turkey’s education system evolved under **Atatürk’s** reforms post-republication (1923). The **Law of Integration of Education (1924)** unified schools, enforcing secularism and modernizing curricula. The system was later expanded under the **1987 reforms**, introducing compulsory education for 8 years (later extended to 12 years in 2012).

**Governance & Structure**
Education is overseen by the **Ministry of National Education**, with a mix of public and private institutions. The system includes:
- **Pre-primary Education**: Optional for ages 3–6.
- **Primary School (İlköğretim)**: 8 years (grades 1–8), compulsory and free.
- **Secondary Education**:
  - **Junior High School (Ortaokul)**: Grades 9–12 (ages 12–17), leading to the **WASSCE** exam.
  - **Vocational High Schools**: Offer specialized tracks (e.g., technical, religious).
- **Higher Education**: Includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools, with **YÖK (Council of Higher Education)** overseeing admissions.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 8 years (extended to 12 years in 2012).
- **Curriculum reforms** in 2017 introduced Islamic concepts (e.g., "jihad") and removed evolution theory.
- **International schools** follow curricula from countries like the UK, US, or IB, with **ISC** listing 53 authorized schools.

#### **England**
**History & Foundations**
England’s education system traces back to medieval universities (Oxford, Cambridge) and charity schools. The **1870 Elementary Education Act** introduced state-funded primary schools, while the **1902 Education Act** expanded secondary education. The **1944 Education Act** established the modern structure, including **compulsory schooling** until age 16.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Department for Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School (Foundation Stage)**: Ages 3–11, covering core subjects (English, Math, Science) and PSHE (Personal, Social, Health, and Economic education).
- **Secondary School (Key Stages 3–5)**:
  - **Key Stage 3 (Years 7–9)**: Ages 11–14, preparing for GCSEs.
  - **Key Stage 4 (Years 10–11)**: Ages 14–16, leading to GCSEs.
  - **Key Stage 5 (Years 12–13)**: Ages 16–18, preparing for A-Levels or vocational qualifications.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and colleges offer degrees (e.g., bachelor’s, master’s), with **tuition fees** capped at £9,535/year for UK students.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 5–18 (extended to 18 via further education).
- **National Curriculum** covers 13 core subjects, with **GCSEs** and **A-Levels** as key exams.
- **Private schools** (e.g., "public schools") offer elite education, while **academies** and **free schools** provide alternative models.

#### **Iran**
**History & Foundations**
Iran’s education system dates back to ancient Persia, with the **Academy of Gondishapur** (3rd century CE) as a precursor. Modern reforms began under **Mohammad Reza Pahlavi**, including the **Literacy Corps (1969–1979)** and the **Islamization of textbooks**. Post-revolution (1979), education became highly centralized, with **segregated schools** by gender and **mandatory Islamic prayers**.

**Governance & Structure**
Overseen by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Pre-primary Education**: Optional for ages 4–6.
- **Primary School (Dabestan)**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), free and compulsory.
- **Secondary School**:
  - **Lower Secondary (Motovasseteh Avval)**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **Konkur** exam.
  - **Upper Secondary (Motovasseteh Dovom)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **Diplom Motevaseth**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **Payame Noor University** (distance learning), with **tuition fees** ranging from free to high for private institutions.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 9 years (extended to 12 years in 2012).
- **Islamic curriculum** emphasizes Quranic studies and Islamic values.
- **Teacher training** is centralized, with **Farhangian University** overseeing programs.

#### **Ghana**
**History & Foundations**
Ghana’s education system evolved from **mission schools** (established by British and Christian missionaries) to a **free, compulsory primary education** system under **Kwame Nkrumah**. The **1987 Education Act** introduced reforms, including **vocational education** and **Free, Compulsory, and Universal Basic Education (FCUBE)**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School (Basic Education)**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–11), free and compulsory.
- **Junior High School (JHS)**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **BECE** exam.
- **Senior High School (SHS)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 16–18), culminating in the **WASSCE**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and polytechnics, with **WASSCE** as the entry requirement.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 4–14 (9 years).
- **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in quality and demand.
- **International schools** (e.g., Takoradi International School) offer curricula like IGCSE or IB.

#### **Taiwan**
**History & Foundations**
Taiwan’s education system was influenced by **Dutch, Qing, and Japanese** colonial rule. Post-1949, the **Republic of China (ROC)** government sought to assimilate Taiwanese into the national identity, leading to **12-year compulsory education** (introduced in 2014). The system emphasizes **Mandarin** and **national defense education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Overseen by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Elementary School**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), compulsory.
- **Junior High School**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **national entrance exam**.
- **Senior High School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), offering **general or vocational tracks**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **junior colleges**, with **SweSAT** as a key admission test.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 12 years.
- **Cram schools (buxiban)** are prevalent, offering after-school tutoring.
- **International programs** (e.g., IB) are available in select schools.

#### **Hong Kong**
**History & Foundations**
Hong Kong’s education system was shaped by **British colonial rule**, with schools initially offering **vernacular education** (Chinese) but later adopting **English** as the primary medium. Post-1997 handover, the system shifted to **Chinese-medium instruction**, though **English-medium international schools** remain popular.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Education Bureau**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–11), leading to the **Primary 6 Exit Test**.
- **Secondary School**:
  - **Junior Secondary**: Grades 1–3 (ages 12–14).
  - **Senior Secondary**: Grades 4–6 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **HKDSE**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **Vocational Training Council (VTC)** schools, with **JUPAS** as the admission system.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 6–18.
- **Medium of instruction**: Most schools use **Cantonese**, while **EMI schools** use English.
- **International schools** (e.g., Li Po Chun United World College) offer curricula like IB.

#### **Nigeria**
**History & Foundations**
Nigeria’s education system was influenced by **British colonial rule**, with **Universal Primary Education (UPE)** introduced in 1976. The **Universal Basic Education (UBE)** system (1999) expanded access but faced challenges like **teacher shortages** and **funding gaps**. Higher education includes **universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools**.

**Governance & Structure**
Overseen by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–11), free and compulsory.
- **Junior Secondary School (JSS)**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **WAEC/GCE**.
- **Senior Secondary School (SSS)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 16–18), culminating in the **SSCE**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **polytechnics**, with **JAMB** as the admission system.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 9 years (9–14 years old).
- **Private schools** are expensive, with fees ranging from **₦25,000–₦30,000,000/year**.
- **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in quality and industry alignment.

#### **Sweden**
**History & Foundations**
Sweden’s education system was shaped by **19th-century reforms**, introducing **compulsory primary education** (folkskola) and later the **9-year comprehensive school (grundskola)**. The system emphasizes **equality, secularism, and student-centered learning**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Swedish National Agency for Education**, the system includes:
- **Preschool**: Ages 1–5, free for children of working parents.
- **Comprehensive School (Grundskola)**: Grades 1–9 (ages 6–15), compulsory.
- **Upper Secondary School (Gymnasieskola)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 16–19), leading to the **bachelor’s degree**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **university colleges**, with **SweSAT** as a key admission test.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 6–15.
- **Independent schools (friskolor)** are funded by the government but operate independently.
- **Vocational education** is integrated into the system, offering practical training.

#### **Republic of Ireland**
**History & Foundations**
Ireland’s education system was influenced by **British colonial rule** and later **Gaelic revivalism**. The **1967 Free Upper Secondary Education Act** introduced free education for ages 16–18. The system emphasizes **multilingualism** (Irish, English) and **inclusive education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Department of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Ages 4–12, leading to the **Junior Certificate**.
- **Secondary School**:
  - **Junior Cycle**: Grades 1–3 (ages 12–15), leading to the **Junior Certificate**.
  - **Senior Cycle**: Grades 4–6 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **Leaving Certificate**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **colleges**, with **CAO** as the admission system.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 6–16.
- **Gaelscoileanna** offer education in Irish.
- **Vocational schools (ETBs)** provide technical and vocational training.

#### **Norway**
**History & Foundations**
Norway’s education system was shaped by **Reformation-era reforms** and later **19th-century secularization**. The system emphasizes **equality, secularism, and practical training**. The **1969 reform** introduced the **9-year comprehensive school (grunnskole)**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education and Research**, the system includes:
- **Primary School (Barneskole)**: Grades 1–7 (ages 6–12), compulsory.
- **Lower Secondary School (Ungdomsskole)**: Grades 8–10 (ages 13–16), leading to the **national exams**.
- **Upper Secondary School (Videregående Skole)**: Grades 11–13 (ages 16–19), leading to the **bachelor’s degree**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **university colleges**, with **SweSAT** as a key admission test.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 6–16.
- **Vocational education** is integrated into the system, offering **apprenticeships**.
- **Independent schools** are rare but exist for religious or pedagogical alternatives.

#### **Israel**
**History & Foundations**
Israel’s education system was shaped by **Jewish Zionist movements** and later **post-statehood reforms**. The system emphasizes **Jewish studies, Hebrew language, and national identity**. The **1984 Integrated Schools Law** allowed mixed Jewish-Arab schools.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), leading to the **bagrut** exam.
- **Middle School**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15).
- **High School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **bagrut**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **Mechina programs**, with **PET** as a key admission test.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 5–18.
- **Haredi and Arab sectors** have distinct curricula.
- **Vocational education** is limited but growing.

#### **Armenia**
**History & Foundations**
Armenia’s education system was shaped by **Soviet influence** and later **post-independence reforms**. The system emphasizes **Armenian language, history, and culture**. The **1991 reforms** introduced **12-year compulsory education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education and Science**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–4 (ages 6–10).
- **Middle School**: Grades 5–9 (ages 10–15), leading to the **basic education certificate**.
- **High School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **full secondary education certificate**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **private institutions**, with **state exams** as the entry requirement.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 12 years.
- **Public schools** are the majority, with **private schools** offering specialized curricula.
- **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in quality.

#### **Sri Lanka**
**History & Foundations**
Sri Lanka’s education system was shaped by **British colonial rule** and later **post-independence reforms**. The system emphasizes **Sinhala and Tamil languages** and **religious studies**. The **1948 Education Act** introduced **free primary education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–5 (ages 6–11), leading to the **Scholarship Exam**.
- **Junior Secondary School**: Grades 6–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **GCE O/L**.
- **Senior Secondary School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 16–18), culminating in the **GCE A/L**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **polytechnics**, with **GCE A/L** as the entry requirement.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** from ages 6–14.
- **Government schools** are the majority, with **private schools** offering specialized curricula.
- **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in quality and industry alignment.

#### **Indonesia**
**History & Foundations**
Indonesia’s education system was shaped by **Dutch colonial rule** and later **post-independence reforms**. The system emphasizes **Islamic studies, Pancasila (national ideology), and practical training**. The **1999 Education Law** introduced **12-year compulsory education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology**, the system includes:
- **Pre-primary Education**: Ages 3–6, optional.
- **Primary School (Sekolah Dasar)**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), compulsory.
- **Junior High School (SMP)**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **SMP exam**.
- **Senior High School (SMA)**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **SMA exam**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **polytechnics**, with **SNMPTN** as the admission system.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 12 years.
- **Islamic schools (pesantren, madrasah)** offer specialized curricula.
- **Vocational education** is integrated into the system, offering **apprenticeships**.

#### **Egypt**
**History & Foundations**
Egypt’s education system was shaped by **ancient Egyptian traditions**, **French colonial rule**, and **post-independence reforms**. The system emphasizes **Islamic studies, Arabic language, and modern sciences**. The **1981 Education Law** introduced **free compulsory education**.

**Governance & Structure**
Managed by the **Ministry of Education**, the system includes:
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), leading to the **primary exam**.
- **Preparatory School**: Grades 7–9 (ages 12–15), leading to the **preparatory exam**.
- **Secondary School**: Grades 10–12 (ages 15–18), culminating in the **secondary exam**.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **polytechnics**, with **national exams** as the entry requirement.

**Key Features**
- **Compulsory education** for 9 years (extended to 12 years in 2012).
- **Al-Azhar system** offers Islamic education alongside secular studies.
- **Vocational education** is growing but faces challenges in quality and industry alignment.

### **Key Themes Across Systems**
1. **Compulsory Education**: Most systems mandate education for 9–12 years, with variations in age ranges and duration.
2. **Secularism vs. Religious Influence**: Systems like France and Sweden emphasize secularism, while others (e.g., Iran, Egypt) integrate religious studies.
3. **Vocational Training**: Growing emphasis in countries like Nigeria, Indonesia, and Sri Lanka to address labor market needs.
4. **International Schools**: Common in urban centers (e.g., Hong Kong, Dubai) for expatriate communities.
5. **Teacher Quality and Funding**: Challenges persist in many systems, particularly in rural areas or lower-income regions.
6. **Standardized Exams**: Critical for university admissions (e.g., JAMB in Nigeria, SweSAT in Sweden, bagrut in Israel).
7. **Digital Learning**: Increasingly adopted post-pandemic, with platforms like **tuteclass.com** in Sri Lanka and **EdUHK** in Hong Kong.
