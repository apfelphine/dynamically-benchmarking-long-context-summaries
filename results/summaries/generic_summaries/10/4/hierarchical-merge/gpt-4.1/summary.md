## Global Education Systems Overview

### Europe
France, England, Ireland, and Sweden maintain centralized, government-regulated education systems emphasizing accessibility, low-cost or subsidized tuition, and rigorous academic standards. France and England offer diversified post-secondary tracks, including prestigious selective institutions and vocational pathways, supported by financial aid programs. Ireland and Sweden focus on inclusive education, with Ireland upholding Catholic and secular schooling options and Sweden providing free tuition for domestic and EU/EEA students along with robust support for immigrant and special needs learners. In Norway, education prioritizes inclusivity and practical skills, offering academic and vocational upper secondary paths, rising teacher qualification standards, and public higher education aligned with Bologna protocols.

### Middle East & Africa
Iran and Turkey have centralized, secular systems, with Iran emphasizing Islamic principles, gender segregation, and competitive university admissions, alongside reforms to boost research and critical thinking. Turkey’s "4+4+4" structure offers both academic and vocational tracks with mandatory religious studies, frequent reforms, and expanding international access despite ongoing curriculum and equity challenges. Israel divides schooling into secular, religious, Haredi, and Arab sectors, with persistent inequalities and sector-specific achievements; higher education is internationally recognized but access remains uneven. Egypt offers varied educational tracks—including secular, religious, and agricultural—yet struggles with quality, underfunding, disparities, and an oversupply of unemployable graduates due to misaligned vocational programs. Ghana and Nigeria focus on expanding basic, vocational, and adult education, though regional disparities, resource limitations, and literacy gaps prevail, with ongoing improvements bolstered by international support.

### Asia-Pacific
Taiwan, Hong Kong, Sri Lanka, and Indonesia feature compulsory education systems shaped by local history and religious influences, emphasizing exam-based advancement and robust higher education sectors. Taiwan boasts high university participation and research standing, with government and international support fostering innovation. Hong Kong integrates bilingual and experiential curricula, increasing patriotic and experiential elements, and leverages progressive methods and AI in schools. Sri Lanka provides competitive government schooling and limited access to higher education, undergoing reforms to enhance autonomy and eliminate controversial policies. Indonesia balances academic, vocational, and religious tracks, driving moderate values, improved teacher qualifications, and higher national education standards, though resource disparities persist.

### Armenia
Armenia offers free, compulsory K-12 education and achieves near-universal literacy, though dropout rates rise in older grades due to child labor and quality issues. The standardized curriculum is enriched by cultural emphasis, and higher education, regulated via admissions exams, aligns with international standards like Bologna, with both public and private options.
