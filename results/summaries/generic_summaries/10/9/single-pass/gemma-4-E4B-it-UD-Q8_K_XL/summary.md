# Educational Systems Overview

## Republic of Ireland
**History and Structure:** Free upper second-level education was introduced in 1967, and the Irish language requirement for a second-level certificate was removed in 1973. Education is compulsory from age six to sixteen or completion of three years of second-level education.
**Curriculum and Levels:** Instruction is primarily in English, though Gaelscoileanna use Irish as the primary medium. The framework maps to EQF/EHEA/NFQ levels, ranging from Level 1 to Level 10 (Doctorate). Primary school consists of eight years, while secondary education includes the three-year Junior Cycle and the two-year Senior Cycle, culminating in the Leaving Certificate.

## Israel
**Structure and Tracks:** The system has three tiers: primary (grades 1–6), middle school (grades 7–9), and high school (grades 10–12). Schools operate across four tracks: state-secular, state-religious, independent religious, and Arab.
**Societal Value and Higher Education:** Israeli society highly values education as a path to socioeconomic status. Tertiary education is subsidized, and students may defer military service to study. University admissions require the Bagrut matriculation exams and the Psychometric Entrance Test (PET).

## Nigeria
**Structure and Stages:** The system is divided into primary (6 years), Junior Secondary School (JSS, 3 years), and Senior Secondary School (SSS, 3 years), leading to the SSCE.
**Funding and Quality:** Federal-sponsored colleges are merit-based and considered elite, while state-sponsored schools face budget and staffing issues. Private schools are expensive but offer better resources. Vocational training is a major focus, although formal TVET faces challenges regarding qualification opacity.

## Sri Lanka
**Administration and Structure:** Education is overseen by multiple ministries, leading to administrative inefficiencies. The structure includes primary (5 years), junior secondary (4 years), senior secondary (2 years, leading to GCE O/Ls), and collegiate (2 years, leading to GCE A/Ls).
**School Types:** Schools include government National, Provincial, and Pirivenas (Buddhist monastic colleges), alongside private, international, and semi-government institutions.
**Tertiary Education:** State university education is free but highly competitive, with admission based on GCE A/Ls.

## Taiwan
**Compulsion and Levels:** Public education is compulsory for 12 years. The curriculum is in Mandarin, and discipline in public schools is very strict.
**Higher Education:** The system is similar to the US model. Tertiary education includes junior colleges (Associate degree) and universities (Bachelor's, Master's, Doctorate).
**Supplementary Education:** Private cram schools are very popular due to intense academic pressure.

## Egypt
**System and Challenges:** The system is highly centralized, with basic education (ages 4–14) being compulsory and free in government institutions. Secondary education has general and technical tracks.
**Challenges:** Despite expansion in access, quality is a major concern, evidenced by poor rankings in TIMSS. A significant reliance on private tutoring exists to compensate for public school deficiencies.
**Alternative Systems:** The Al-Azhar system operates concurrently, emphasizing religious studies.

## Iran
**Centralization and Values:** Education is highly centralized under the Ministry of Education. The post-1979 revolution brought significant Islamization of the system, including gender segregation.
**System Features:** The education progression includes pre-primary, compulsory elementary (6 years), lower-secondary (3 years), and upper-secondary (3 years). University attendance requires performance on the Konkur exam.
**Gender and Privatization:** Women's enrollment in higher education has risen significantly, and private schools are becoming increasingly influential, sometimes viewed as corporate businesses.

## Indonesia
**Colonial Legacy and Structure:** The Dutch colonial period introduced a tiered system based on social status. Post-independence, the system aims for secular wisdom, with compulsory education spanning twelve years.
**School Types:** There are Islamic schools (pesantren, madrasah, sekolah islam) alongside state-run public schools.
**Curriculum and Pedagogy:** Public schools emphasize rote learning and the principles of Pancasila, though this role has diminished post-1998.

## Turkey
**Modernization and Curriculum:** Post-Republican era reforms centralized education and aimed for secularism, abolishing the separate religious school channels of the past. Compulsory education was extended to 12 years.
**Curriculum and Bias:** While religion is now taught (with religious instruction integrated), state-approved textbooks are criticized for promoting Turkish nationalism and denying the Armenian genocide.
**Higher Education:** Public universities are heavily state-funded, while private options are expensive.

## Armenia
**Structure and Access:** Secondary education is compulsory and free. The system is structured into primary (4 years), middle school (5 years), and high school (3 years), culminating in national exams.
**School Types:** Public schools are dominant (97%) and follow a national curriculum, while private schools operate under licensing rules but rely on tuition fees.
**International Integration:** Armenia is actively involved in European educational cooperation, aligning with the Bologna Process.

## France
**Governance and Ideology:** The Ministry of National Education governs all programs, upholding *laïcité* (secularism). Mandatory schooling is enforced from age 6.
**Structure:** Education moves from preschool to *collège* (middle school, 4 years) and then *lycée* (high school, 3 years), preparing students for the *baccalauréat*.
**Higher Education:** The system features public universities and elite *grandes écoles*. State funding keeps tuition low for EU/EEA citizens, though private sector participation is increasing.

## Norway
**Mandate and Structure:** The system is highly standardized and free for all children aged 6–16. The structure includes elementary (6–12), lower secondary (13–16), and upper secondary (16–19).
**Pedagogy and Reform:** The 1994 reform merged branches, aiming for flexibility between general and vocational paths. While grades exist in lower secondary, official grading starts in the upper secondary level.
**Private Sector:** Private schools are heavily grant-aided but must adhere to specific pedagogical or religious alternatives.

## England
**Compulsion and Framework:** Education is compulsory from age 5 to 18. The curriculum is defined by the National Curriculum across Key Stages.
**School Types:** The system includes state-funded comprehensive, academy, and free schools, alongside fee-paying independent schools.
**Post-16:** Post-16 education offers pathways like Sixth Form colleges, A-levels, and apprenticeships, with a focus on vocational/technical options via UTCs.
**Standards:** Schools are regularly inspected by Ofsted, and performance data is tracked via PISA.

## Sweden
**Comprehensive System:** Education is based on a nine-year comprehensive school (*grundskola*) for ages 6–16, offering 16 compulsory subjects.
**Progression:** Upper secondary school (*gymnasieskola*) is elective and divided into preparatory or vocational paths.
**Funding and Autonomy:** Public schools are the norm, and independent schools (*friskolor*) receive public funding but are scrutinized for potentially lowering standards.
**International Standing:** Sweden's PISA scores have fluctuated, leading to political discussion about educational improvements.

## Ghana
**Structure and Access:** The system is divided into basic (12 years, ages 4–15), secondary (academic or vocational), and tertiary. Lessons are primarily in English.
**Access and Equity:** While enrollment is high, tertiary education access is uneven, favoring wealthy and male students.
**Governance:** The Ministry of Education oversees policy, supported by local offices and autonomous bodies.

## Hong Kong
**Bilingualism and Structure:** The system has shifted toward Chinese as the primary medium of instruction in secondary schools, though English Medium Instruction (EMI) schools remain prestigious.
**Structure:** Compulsory education runs from age 6 to 18, divided into primary, junior, and senior secondary years, culminating in the HKDSE.
**Curriculum and Internationalization:** The system has moved toward active/experiential learning pedagogy. International schools are numerous, offering various curricula like the IBDP.

## International Comparison
**PISA and Rankings:** Ireland ranks high in PISA reading. Israel and Turkey have high tertiary attainment rates, though Israeli scores are skewed by excluding certain groups. England ranks well in PISA for 15-year-olds.
**Key Trends:** Many countries show a trend towards increased privatization, vocational focus (e.g., Norway, HK), and challenges related to equity, teacher quality, and curriculum standardization (e.g., Egypt, Ghana).
