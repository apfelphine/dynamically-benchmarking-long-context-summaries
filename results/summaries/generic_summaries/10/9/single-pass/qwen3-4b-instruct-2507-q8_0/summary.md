**Education in the Republic of Ireland**  
- Free upper secondary education was introduced in 1967, a landmark reform.  
- The Irish language is a core subject in all public schools, with exemptions for students with valid reasons.  
- Education is compulsory from ages 6 to 16 or until completion of three years of second-level education.  
- The structure includes primary (ages 4–16), junior cycle (3 years, culminating in the Junior Certificate), and senior cycle (2 years, culminating in the Leaving Certificate).  
- The school day typically runs from 8:20 to 16:00 with 28 hours of tuition weekly.  
- A range of school types exist: national schools (multidenominational), gaelscoileanna (Irish-language schools), multidenominational schools, and preparatory schools.  
- Special needs education is supported by the National Council for Special Education and includes Special Needs Assistants (SNAs).  
- The government addresses educational disadvantage through initiatives like DEIS (Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools), which includes reduced class sizes and targeted support.  
- International students are a significant part of the education system, with many Irish universities educating global leaders.  
- The education system is recognized for strong performance in reading and mathematics in international assessments (PISA).  

**Education in Israel**  
- The system is divided into primary (1–6), middle (7–9), and high school (10–12) with a focus on academic excellence and societal mobility.  
- A key feature is the matriculation (Bagrut) exam, which determines access to higher education and employment.  
- The education system is highly competitive, with high university entrance rates and strong performance in science and mathematics.  
- The Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) community faces challenges due to limited mainstream education and low workforce participation.  
- The government has implemented affirmative action policies to improve access for Arab and Haredi students.  
- The system emphasizes higher education as a pathway to socioeconomic advancement.  
- Despite strong academic performance, disparities exist in funding and access, especially for Arab and Haredi students.  

**Education in Nigeria**  
- The system follows a 6-3-3-4 structure: 6 years of primary, 3 years of junior secondary, 3 years of senior secondary, and 4 years of tertiary education.  
- The national curriculum is standardized, with exams like the Senior Secondary Certificate Examination (SSCE) administered by WAEC.  
- Private schools are common and often more expensive, with rising fees.  
- Challenges include teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, and a growing reliance on private tutoring.  
- The government has introduced reforms to improve access and quality, including the Universal Basic Education (UBE) program.  
- Female education has improved, but gender-based barriers and teen pregnancy remain significant challenges.  
- Vocational education is promoted through TVET institutions, though implementation faces challenges.  

**Education in Sri Lanka**  
- The system is structured into primary (ages 4–14), junior secondary (ages 10–14), senior secondary (ages 14–17), and tertiary levels.  
- The national curriculum is taught in Sinhala, Tamil, or English, with religious instruction in many schools.  
- The education system is highly centralized, with the Ministry of Education overseeing policy and curriculum.  
- The government has implemented reforms to improve access and quality, including the expansion of private and international schools.  
- The system emphasizes national identity and religious values, with a strong presence of Buddhist and Muslim schools.  
- Challenges include teacher shortages, low teacher morale, and regional disparities in access.  
- The government has introduced initiatives like the DEIS program to address educational disadvantage.  

**Education in Taiwan**  
- The system spans 12 years of compulsory education (ages 5–18), with a focus on academic excellence and national identity.  
- A rigorous curriculum emphasizes science, mathematics, and language, with high performance in international assessments.  
- The education system is highly centralized, with the Ministry of Education overseeing curriculum and teacher training.  
- The government has introduced reforms to promote equity, including the integration of religious and secular values.  
- Private and international schools are common, with a growing emphasis on international curricula.  
- The system is known for high university entrance rates and strong performance in science and engineering.  
- Challenges include high student-to-teacher ratios, teacher shortages, and the growing cost of private education.
