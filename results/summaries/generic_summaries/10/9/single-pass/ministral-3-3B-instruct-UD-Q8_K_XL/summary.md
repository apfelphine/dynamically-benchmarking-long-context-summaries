### **Education in Ireland**

#### **History**
Ireland introduced free upper secondary education in **1967**, marking a historic milestone. The Irish language requirement for a second-level certificate was abolished in **1973**. The system emphasizes inclusivity, ensuring all students from ages **5 to 16** (or until completing three years of second-level education) receive education.

#### **Structure**
Education in Ireland is structured into three main phases: **primary, secondary, and tertiary**. Primary education spans **eight years** (Junior Infants to Sixth Class), while secondary education is divided into **Junior Cycle (three years)**, **Transition Year (optional)**, and **Senior Cycle (two years)**. The curriculum includes **English, Irish, mathematics, science, and social studies**, with optional subjects like art, music, and technology.

- **Primary Education (Ages 5–12):**
  - Compulsory for all children.
  - Curriculum includes core subjects like English, Irish, mathematics, and science.
  - Students start at age **5** (Junior Infants) and complete by age **12**.

- **Secondary Education (Ages 12–18):**
  - **Junior Cycle (Grades 1–3):** Focuses on foundational skills.
  - **Transition Year (Optional):** A one-year program for self-directed learning.
  - **Senior Cycle (Grades 4–6):** Prepares students for Leaving Certificate exams.
  - **Leaving Certificate:** The terminal exam for secondary education, taken after Grade 6.

- **Tertiary Education:**
  - Includes universities and colleges offering degrees up to doctoral level.
  - Admission is based on performance in Leaving Certificate exams.
  - **National Framework for Qualifications (NFQ):** Standardizes qualifications across levels.

#### **Special Features**
- **Irish Language:** Irish is a core subject taught in all public schools, with exemptions for students with significant periods abroad or learning difficulties.
- **International Baccalaureate (IB):** Some schools offer the IB program.
- **Vocational Education:** Opportunities for technical and vocational training.
- **Free Education:** Upper secondary education is free, though some schools charge small fees for trips or exams.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Disparities in Education:** Some schools face funding and resource shortages.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Efforts are made to improve teacher retention and qualifications.
- **Digital Education:** Increasing use of technology in classrooms.

### **Education in Israel**

#### **History**
Israel’s education system evolved from colonial-era institutions to a modern, secular framework. The **1948 establishment of the State of Israel** led to the creation of a unified education system, though religious schools remain prominent. The system is divided into **three tiers**: primary (grades 1–6), middle school (grades 7–9), and high school (grades 10–12).

#### **Structure**
- **Compulsory Education:** From kindergarten through Grade 12.
- **Curriculum:** Balances secular and religious studies, with a focus on critical thinking and national identity.
- **School Tracks:**
  - **State-Secular (Mamlachti):** General education.
  - **State-Religious (Mamlachti Dati):** Intensive Jewish studies.
  - **Independent Religious (Haredi):** Focuses on Torah study.
  - **Arab Schools:** Teach in Arabic, emphasizing Arab history and culture.

#### **Key Features**
- **Matriculation Exams (Bagrut):** Mandatory for high school graduation and university admission.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on technical and vocational education.
- **Higher Education:** Universities like **Hebrew University, Technion, and Tel Aviv University** are globally recognized.
- **International Students:** Popular destination for international students due to high-quality education and strong job prospects.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Religious Schools:** Haredi and religious schools face criticism for low participation in the workforce.
- **Equity Issues:** Disparities in funding and resources between Jewish and Arab sectors.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Efforts to improve teacher training and retention.

### **Education in Nigeria**

#### **History**
Nigeria’s education system has evolved from colonial-era institutions to a modern, decentralized framework. The **1976 Universal Primary Education (UPE)** program aimed to make primary education free and compulsory, though challenges persisted. The system is now structured around **9–3–4 (nine years of primary and junior secondary, followed by three years of senior secondary and four years of tertiary education)**.

#### **Structure**
- **Primary Education (Ages 5–12):** Six years, leading to the **First School Leaving Certificate**.
- **Junior Secondary School (JSS, Ages 12–15):** Three years, leading to the **Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE)**.
- **Senior Secondary School (SSS, Ages 15–18):** Three years, leading to the **Senior Secondary School Certificate Examination (SSSCE)**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, polytechnics, and colleges offering degrees up to doctoral level.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Universal Primary Education (UPE) and Universal Basic Education (UBE) programs.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on technical and vocational education.
- **International Schools:** Over **129 international schools** catering to expatriates and affluent locals.
- **A-Level Programs:** Interim Joint Matriculation Board (IJMB) and Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board (JUPEB) for direct entry into universities.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Funding Shortages:** Limited resources for schools and universities.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Many schools lack qualified teachers.
- **Out-of-School Children:** High rates of child labor and school dropout.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **History**
Sri Lanka’s education system traces back to **2300 years**, with early schools based on Buddhist temples. The **1938 Education Act** made education free and compulsory, and the system evolved to include **national, provincial, and religious schools**. The **1978 Universities Act** established separate universities, though governance remains centralized.

#### **Structure**
- **Primary Education (Ages 5–10):** Five years, leading to the **Scholarship Examination**.
- **Junior Secondary School (Ages 10–14):** Four years, leading to the **General Certificate of Education (GCE) Ordinary Level (O/L)**.
- **Senior Secondary School (Ages 14–18):** Two years, leading to the **GCE Advanced Level (A/L)**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities like **University of Colombo, University of Peradeniya, and University of Ruhuna**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Compulsory from age 5 to 14.
- **National Curriculum:** Standardized across schools.
- **Special Education:** Services for students with disabilities.
- **International Schools:** Offering **IGCSE, A-Levels, and IB programs**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Centralized Governance:** High inefficiency and mismanagement.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and poor working conditions.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **History**
Taiwan’s education system was influenced by **Dutch, Qing, and Japanese rule**, with modern institutions established after **1949**. The **12-year compulsory education** policy was introduced in **2014**, replacing the previous 9-year system. The system emphasizes **Mandarin as the primary language** and integrates **military education** in high schools.

#### **Structure**
- **Preschool (Ages 3–5):** Optional, regulated by the **Early Childhood Education and Care Act**.
- **Elementary School (Ages 6–11):** Six years, leading to the **National Senior High School Entrance Exam**.
- **Junior High School (Ages 12–14):** Three years, leading to the **National Vocational School Entrance Exam**.
- **Senior High School (Ages 15–17):** Three years, leading to the **National University Entrance Exam**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities like **National Taiwan University, National Chengchi University, and National Tsing Hua University**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** 12 years, starting at age 6.
- **Military Education:** Mandatory in high schools.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offering **associate degrees** alongside traditional universities.
- **International Programs:** Strong emphasis on **English and Mandarin**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Overemphasis on Exams:** High pressure on students.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and high workloads.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.

### **Education in France**

#### **History**
France’s education system was shaped by **Napoleon’s reforms** and **Jules Ferry’s 1881 laws**, which made education **free, secular, and compulsory** for children aged 6–12. The system emphasizes **literacy, critical thinking, and civic education**, with a focus on **French literature and history**.

#### **Structure**
- **Preschool (Ages 3–6):** Maternelle, introducing basic skills.
- **Primary School (Ages 6–11):** École élémentaire, leading to the **Baccalauréat**.
- **Middle School (Ages 11–15):** Collège, preparing for high school.
- **High School (Ages 15–18):** Lycée, leading to the **Baccalauréat**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, grandes écoles, and vocational schools.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Public schools are free, though some private schools charge fees.
- **Baccalauréat:** The terminal exam for high school graduation.
- **Grades:** A–E scale, with A being the highest.
- **International Schools:** Over **105 international schools** offering **IB, A-Levels, and other curricula**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and high workloads.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.
- **Equity Issues:** Disparities in funding and resources between public and private schools.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **History**
Norway’s education system dates back to **1842**, with the **folkeskole** (people’s school) introduced in **1889**. The system emphasizes **compulsory education** and **equality**, with a focus on **critical thinking and civic engagement**.

#### **Structure**
- **Primary School (Ages 6–12):** Grunnskole, leading to the **National Test**.
- **Lower Secondary School (Ages 13–16):** Ungdomsskole, leading to the **University Entrance Exam**.
- **Upper Secondary School (Ages 16–19):** Videregående skole, leading to the **Bachelor’s Degree**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities and university colleges offering degrees up to doctoral level.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** 12 years, starting at age 6.
- **Free Education:** Public schools are free, though some private schools charge fees.
- **Grading System:** A–F scale, with A being the highest.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offering **apprenticeships and technical training**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and high workloads.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.
- **Equity Issues:** Disparities in funding and resources between rural and urban schools.

### **Education in England**

#### **History**
England’s education system evolved from **charity schools** and **grammar schools** in the **19th century** to a **state-funded system** under **1944 Education Act**. The system emphasizes **compulsory education**, **free school meals**, and **equality of opportunity**.

#### **Structure**
- **Early Years (Ages 3–5):** Nursery and Reception classes.
- **Primary School (Ages 5–11):** Key Stages 1 and 2.
- **Secondary School (Ages 11–16):** Key Stages 3 and 4.
- **Sixth Form (Ages 16–18):** Leading to **A-Levels, BTECs, and IB Diplomas**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, colleges, and vocational training.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** From age 5 to 16.
- **National Curriculum:** Standardized across schools.
- **Grades:** A–G scale, with A being the highest.
- **International Schools:** Over **7% of students attend private schools**, with options like **A-Levels, IB, and GCSEs**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and high workloads.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.
- **Equity Issues:** Disparities in funding and resources between public and private schools.

- **Upper Secondary School (Ages 16–19):** Gymnasieskola, leading to the **Bachelor’s Degree**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities and university colleges offering degrees up to doctoral level.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** 9 years, starting at age 6.
- **Free Education:** Public schools are free, though some private schools charge fees.
- **Grading System:** A–E scale, with A being the highest.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offering **apprenticeships and technical training**.

### **Education in Ghana**

#### **History**
Ghana’s education system evolved from **colonial-era mission schools** to a **modern, decentralized framework**. The **1961 Education Act** introduced **free and compulsory primary education**, and the system has since expanded to include **universities, polytechnics, and vocational training**.

#### **Structure**
- **Kindergarten (Ages 4–6):** Two years, introducing basic skills.
- **Primary School (Ages 6–11):** Six years, leading to the **Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE)**.
- **Junior High School (Ages 12–15):** Three years, leading to the **WASSCE**.
- **Senior High School (Ages 15–18):** Three years, leading to the **WASSCE**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, polytechnics, and colleges offering degrees up to doctoral level.

#### **Key Features**
- **Free Education:** Primary and junior secondary education are free.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on **technical and vocational education**.
- **International Schools:** Over **100 international schools** catering to expatriates and affluent locals.
- **Adult Education:** Programs for **functional literacy and vocational training**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Funding Shortages:** Limited resources for schools and universities.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Many schools lack qualified teachers.
- **Out-of-School Children:** High rates of child labor and school dropout.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.

### **Education in Hong Kong**

#### **History**
Hong Kong’s education system was influenced by **British colonial rule**, with schools established to teach **English and Western studies**. The **1997 handover** led to reforms emphasizing **Chinese language and culture**, though international schools remain popular.

#### **Structure**
- **Kindergarten (Ages 3–6):** Three years, introducing basic skills.
- **Primary School (Ages 6–11):** Six years, leading to the **Primary 6 Examination**.
- **Junior Secondary School (Ages 12–14):** Three years, leading to the **Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE)**.
- **Senior Secondary School (Ages 14–18):** Three years, leading to the **HKDSE**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, colleges, and vocational training.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** From age 6 to 18.
- **Medium of Instruction:** English or Chinese (Cantonese).
- **International Schools:** Over **175 international schools** offering **IB, A-Levels, and other curricula**.
- **Vocational Training:** Strong emphasis on **technical and vocational education**.

#### **Challenges & Reforms**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low wages and high workloads.
- **Digital Education:** Growing reliance on technology, though infrastructure remains a challenge.
- **Equity Issues:** Disparities in funding and resources between public and private schools.
