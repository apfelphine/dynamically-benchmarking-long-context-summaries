**Education in Norway**  
Norway's education system is structured into elementary (barneskole, ages 6–12), lower secondary (ungdomsskole, ages 13–16), and upper secondary (videregående skole, ages 16–19) levels, all compulsory until age 16. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with special education supported by trained specialists and a focus on individual needs. Private schools are limited and must follow international or religious/pedagogical curricula, with government grants. Grading uses a 1–6 scale, with 2–6 being passing. Students transition schools frequently, and the "camp school" (leirskole) is authorized. Higher education is state-funded, with a strong emphasis on research and quality, and includes a national curriculum and reforms like *Kunnskapsløftet* to promote general studies and vocational pathways.  

**Education in Sweden**  
Sweden's system is based on a nine-year comprehensive school (grundskola), with compulsory education from ages 6–16. The curriculum includes core subjects like Swedish, math, and English, with elective language choices. Grading uses A–F, with a total score calculated for university applications. The system includes a strong focus on student choice and a national curriculum. Special education is integrated into mainstream schools. Higher education includes universities, university colleges, and private institutions, with a strong emphasis on research and international standards. The system has undergone reforms, including curriculum updates and a shift in grading.  

**Education in Armenia**  
Armenia's system spans 12 years of compulsory education, with a strong focus on national identity and language. Primary and secondary education is free and compulsory, with a high enrollment rate. Special education is provided in local schools, with a focus on individual needs. Higher education is expanding, with a growing number of private institutions and international programs. The government has invested in education, with a focus on improving literacy and access.  

**Education in Hong Kong**  
Hong Kong's system is structured into primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, with compulsory education from ages 6–18. The curriculum is bilingual (English and Chinese), with a strong emphasis on exam performance. The system includes a range of international schools and a focus on vocational education. Higher education is highly competitive, with a strong emphasis on university entrance exams. The system has faced criticism over the role of private schools and the quality of education.
