# Comprehensive Summary of Education Systems in Eighteen Countries including Taiwan

## Education in Norway  
Norway’s three-tier grunnskole system comprises elementary (ages 6–12), lower secondary (13–16), and upper secondary education (16–19), offering academic and vocational tracks. Public education dominates, promoting inclusivity and requiring teachers to have master’s degrees. Higher education requires upper secondary certification, distinguishes universities’ theoretical focus from colleges’ professional skills, and is regulated by NOKUT.

## Education in Sweden  
Sweden mandates nine years of schooling plus voluntary preschool under a national curriculum with standardized testing. Upper secondary education offers academic and vocational tracks. Independent schools use alternative pedagogies amid debates over privatization and grade inflation. Declining international assessments have prompted reforms.

## Education in Armenia  
Armenia provides compulsory 12-year general and specialized schooling, emphasizing Armenian alongside Russian, English, and Chinese languages. Public schools blend national culture and global trends, while private schooling risks inequity. European integration efforts coexist with attendance and quality challenges.

## Education in Hong Kong  
Hong Kong’s system merges Western and Confucian influences with voluntary kindergarten, compulsory primary and secondary education, and the HKDSE exam. Demographic changes prompted full-day classes. Diverse school types exist. Tertiary education includes public and self-financing institutions, with adult education supporting social integration.

## Education in Indonesia  
Indonesia requires 12 years of education with academic and vocational paths guided by the 2013 curriculum, integrating Islamic pesantren and religious education. Challenges include funding, quality gaps, rural access, and teacher training despite rising enrollment.

## Education in Ghana  
Ghana mandates 12 years of free basic education, with academic and vocational secondary tracks culminating in WASSCE exams. Vocational training advances through COTVET amid infrastructure and gender disparities, with private schools increasing. Decentralized governance aids literacy improvements.

## Education in Egypt  
Egypt offers compulsory basic and secondary education and broad higher education options, including Al-Azhar religious and international schools. Challenges include inequity, infrastructure deficits, rote learning, low teacher pay, graduate unemployment, and regional disparities. COVID-19 accelerated distance learning adoption.

## Education in England  
England’s compulsory education runs from ages 5 to 18, featuring diverse school types mostly state-funded; some private schools are exempt from the National Curriculum. The curriculum progresses through Early Years and Key Stages, overseen by Ofsted. Post-16 options include academic, vocational, apprenticeships, and higher education with fee caps and inclusive funding.

## Education in Sri Lanka  
Sri Lanka provides free, compulsory education to age 14 across five stages with key exams controlling university entry. Competitive university admissions and prevalent private tutoring coexist with vocational education aligned with NVQ levels, which faces criticism for rigidity.

## Education in France  
France mandates free, secular education from age 3 with a national curriculum covering preschool to secondary levels culminating in baccalauréat or vocational CAP exams. Higher education includes selective grandes écoles and open universities following European standards. Teachers undergo rigorous qualification. Laïcité governs schooling, banning conspicuous religious symbols.

## Education in Israel  
Israel’s compulsory education spans grades 1–12 with secular, religious, Haredi, Arab, and integrated schools. Haredi and Arab communities face workforce and educational integration challenges with disparities in resources and outcomes. Education stresses Jewish/Zionist values; military service often precedes university. Affirmative measures address gaps amid debates about inclusiveness and teacher pay.

## Education in Turkey  
Turkey enforces 12 years of education divided into primary, middle, and secondary stages, offering general, vocational, and Imam Hatip religious tracks. Higher education encompasses numerous public universities. Religious education reintroduced from grade 4 promotes nationalism aligned with Atatürk’s legacy and state-controlled historical narratives that deny the Armenian genocide. Increasing religious content and declining minority language education contrast with Treaty of Lausanne protections.

## Education in the Republic of Ireland  
Ireland mandates schooling from 6 to 16 or three years of secondary education, with free upper secondary education since 1967. The largely Roman Catholic-delivered primary system is overseen by the National Council for Curriculum and Assessment. Secondary education includes Junior Cycle, optional Transition Year, and Senior Cycle finishing with the Leaving Certificate. Ireland ranks highly internationally in literacy and numeracy, with strong supports for special needs and disadvantaged students.

## Education in Iran  
Iran’s education system blends ancient traditions and post-1979 Islamization, including optional pre-primary and compulsory elementary and lower secondary education. Upper secondary offers multiple tracks with higher education accessed via the Konkour exam, focusing on Islamic curricula. Despite high dropout rates and urban private school dominance causing inequality, Iran excels in STEM education with a female majority in universities. Recent reforms aim to redesign curricula, enhance vocational training, and promote ideological and global knowledge goals.

## Education in Nigeria  
Nigeria’s 6-3-3-4 system starts at age 5 with six years of primary education, plus junior and senior secondary, culminating in SSCE or NECO exams. The Universal Basic Education program offers nine years of free compulsory schooling. Secondary education covers core and vocational subjects. Schools range from federal to state and private/religious institutions.

### Tertiary Education  
Nigeria has 153 NUC-registered universities with federal, state, and private types. Entry requires SSCE/GCE passes and JAMB scores, with advanced entry via IJMB and JUPEB. Undergraduate programs last 4–6 years by field.

### Vocational and Informal Education  
Vocational education awards National Technical Certificates via NABTEB, promoting apprenticeships despite political instability and limited national commitment. Informal education supports literacy and employment but suffers underfunding; NGOs like CELLE advance programs.

### Gender and Regional Challenges  
Female education faces gender bias, poverty, teen pregnancy, religious customs, and militancy. UNICEF aids retention through cash transfers. Rural secondary education suffers poor infrastructure, insecurity, and staffing issues despite government plans.

### Literacy and Digital Literacy  
As of 2022, literacy stands at about 69%, lower in northern regions affected by poverty and conflict. The government targets 95% digital literacy by 2030 through youth programs and partnerships.

## Education in Taiwan  
Taiwan’s education, shaped by colonial and political influences, implemented 12 years compulsory education by 2014, including financial preschool support. It comprises regulated preschool (ages 3–6), compulsory elementary (grades 1–6) with core subjects (Mandarin, English), junior high (7–9) preparing for senior high entrance, and senior high (10–12) divided into academic and vocational tracks. Discipline is strict; corporal punishment is officially banned though reportedly persists.

### Senior High and Vocational Education  
Senior high offers regular academic tracks with university entrance via exams or recommendation, and vocational tracks across agriculture, industry, and business. Vocational education is promoted as a university alternative.

### Higher Education and Teacher Training  
Over 95% of high school graduates enter university, which follows a US-style model with public and private institutions. Public universities emphasize research and offer lower tuition, while private schools excel in languages, law, and business. Graduate programs last 2–7 years; Academia Sinica hosts an international English-language program. The Ministry of Education supports teacher professional development via a nationwide digital platform coordinated by National Kaohsiung Normal University.

### Junior Colleges and Institutes of Technology  
Five-year junior college programs start post-junior high, awarding associate degrees; two-year programs follow vocational or senior high pathways. Institutes of technology and universities of science and technology provide associate to doctoral degrees with flexible credit requirements.

### Private Education and Supplementary Services  
Cram schools supplement core learning, focusing on English, math, and physics. Private kindergartens often feature advanced subjects and English immersion, growing despite some controversy. The government aims to increase kindergarten enrollment. The 3+3 high school system allows bypassing junior high senior exams via combined junior-senior private schools.

### Additional Educational Services  
Special education supports students aged 2 to 22 with disabilities or giftedness. Nursing education offers multiple pathways to professional licensure. Taiwan is also a popular destination for international Chinese language learners; in 2015, about 40,000 mainland Chinese students studied there, mostly short-term.
