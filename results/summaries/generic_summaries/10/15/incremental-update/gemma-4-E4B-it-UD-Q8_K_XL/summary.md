## Global Education Systems Comparison: A Comprehensive Overview

### Diverse National Education Models
Global education systems exhibit vast diversity. Examples range from **France**'s centralized, secular focus to **Ireland**'s highly-ranked system offering free upper secondary education (ages 6-16). Other models include **Sri Lanka**'s rigid structure through Grade 9, **England**'s National Curriculum (ages 5-18), and **Ghana**'s mix of learning styles. Systems vary in centralization; **Egypt** and **Iran** are centralized, though Iran enforces Islamization and sex segregation. **Turkey** is also centralized and secular, expanding compulsory education to 12 years. **Israel** uses a tiered compulsory system evaluated by the *Bagrut* exam across religious and secular tracks, while **Indonesia** integrates Pancasila into its two-semester structure. Countries like **Sweden** and **Norway** align tiers with ECTS, and **Armenia** provides free K-12 education under Bologna Process standards.

### Focus on Taiwan's Education Framework
**Taiwan** mandates compulsory schooling through junior high, extending to twelve years in Mandarin, traditionally maintaining strict discipline. The progression involves Elementary (Grades 1-6), Junior High (Grades 7-9, culminating in national exams), and Senior High (Grades 10-12), which offers regular and skill-based tracks. Higher education is highly valued, with over 95% of graduates attending university since 2008. University entrance relies on recommendations or national exams.

**Taiwan's Higher Education and Private Sector:**
Taiwan's tertiary sector includes numerous public and private universities. Graduate schools are common, and the Taiwan International Graduate Program supports English-language science programs, with doctoral studies lasting four to seven years. Vocational education is offered through 87 institutions, providing associate to doctoral degrees. The private sector is characterized by prevalent **Cram Schools** that supplement academic pressure in subjects like math and English, alongside popular private preschools. A specific private policy, the 3+3 High School System, allows students to bypass the junior high CAP test by remaining in the same private school for senior high.

**Comparative Trends and Challenges:**
Globally, there is a trend toward system harmonization, often seen in Bologna Process adoption. Key international challenges include socioeconomic inequality, corruption, and low teacher morale. National hurdles vary, such as **Nigeria**'s multi-tiered system struggling with infrastructure deficits, staff shortages, and low adult literacy (62.02% in 2018). In **Taiwan**, special education is supported via centralized or decentralized programs, and the nation hosts many international students.
