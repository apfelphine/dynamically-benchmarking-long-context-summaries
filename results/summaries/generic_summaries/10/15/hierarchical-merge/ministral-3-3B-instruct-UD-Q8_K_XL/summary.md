### **Nordic Education Systems: Norway & Sweden**
The **Nordic education models** prioritize **universal access, vocational integration, and inclusive principles**, with Norway’s **non-graded primary phase** (6–12 years) and Sweden’s **structured grade-based system** (1–9 years) both emphasizing **literacy, numeracy, and English**. Both countries integrate **vocational pathways**—Norway’s **2-year apprenticeships** and Sweden’s **3-year upper secondary programs**—aligning education with labor demands. Higher education follows the **Bologna Process**, with Norway’s **3-year bachelor’s degrees** and Sweden’s **180-credit programs** supporting professional vocations. Despite strengths in equity, **teacher shortages** and **digital literacy gaps** persist, particularly in vocational training.

### **Hong Kong’s Education System: Challenges and Reforms**
Hong Kong’s **two-phase junior/senior secondary system** (free primary via PEVS vouchers, 3-year senior secondary with HKDSE assessments) blends **local and international standards**, though **political instability** and **funding constraints** limit reforms. Recent shifts include **patriotism education** (e.g., subsidized trips to China) and **project-based learning**, but **elite school enrollment declines** due to tensions. **Vocational pathways remain underdeveloped** (~1.6% completion rate), and **private sector dominance** raises equity concerns. Challenges include **teacher shortages, rote-learning, and digital literacy gaps**, with reforms focusing on **vocational-alignment and group projects**—though **political stability** remains a hurdle.

### **Turkey’s Education System**
Turkey’s **higher education system** (76 state + 1,000+ private universities) offers **2–6-year programs**, with **tuition ranging from $1,000 to $30,000** and **YÖS exams or foreign credentials** for admission. **Research-focused**, it attracts **~800,000 foreign students**, reflecting **secularism and nationalism**, though **mandatory Islamic instruction** and **denial of the Armenian genocide** in textbooks persist. The **K-12 system** (6–16 years) integrates **Islamic education, foreign languages, and minority languages**, with **private textbook adoption reforms** to reduce state bias. **Vocational education** is underdeveloped, and **teacher training gaps** remain critical.

### **Ireland’s Education System**
Ireland’s **compulsory 6–16-year system** includes **English-medium primary/secondary** and **Irish-medium immersion schools**, with **DEIS funding** improving disadvantaged schools. Secondary education spans **Junior Cycle (12–15)**, **Transition Year (15–16)**, and **Senior Cycle (16–19)**, culminating in the **Leaving Certificate**. Higher education is **globally ranked top 20**, with **inclusive systems** supporting **special needs education (NCSE/SNAs)** and high **PISA scores (2nd in reading, 11th in maths)**. **Catholic influence persists** in voluntary schools, though **secular reforms** expand access.

### **Iran’s Education System**
Iran’s **free, compulsory 6–16-year system** emphasizes **research-driven, inquiry-based learning**, integrating **global knowledge and Islamic values**. Recent reforms prioritize **STEM education**, with women dominating **STEM disciplines (70% of engineering graduates)**. Challenges include **high dropout rates (~4M unenrolled children)**, **teacher shortages (~60,000 gaps)**, and **post-pandemic digital learning via LMS/Shad software**. **Foreign language programs** (e.g., French since 2022) expand, though **insecurity and poverty** hinder access. **Vocational training** is limited, and **protest restrictions** at universities persist.

### **Indonesia’s Education System**
Indonesia’s **12-year compulsory system** (primary, junior high, senior high/vocational) faces **teacher shortages, uneven quality, and vocational underdevelopment**. The system includes **universities, institutes, and polytechnics**, with **vocational programs** (~1.6% completion rate) dominated by **Islamic schools (pesantren/madrasah)** and polytechnics. **Decentralization post-1998 weakened *Pancasila*’s role**, and **COVID-19 disruptions** exacerbated **digital literacy gaps**. Reforms focus on **labor-market alignment** (e.g., **two-semester academic calendar**) and **vocational expansion**, though **funding and teacher training** remain bottlenecks.

### **Ghana’s Education System**
Ghana’s **12-year compulsory free education** framework (introduced 1987) aims for **equity but faces private sector dominance and vocational gaps**. The system includes **BECE and WASSCE assessments**, with **6 public universities, 49 private universities, and 10 polytechnics**. **Vocational programs** (~1.6% completion rate) are underdeveloped, and **digital divides** persist. **COVID-19 disrupted learning**, with **informal TVET training** (90% of the sector) unregulated. Reforms target **vocational expansion** (e.g., **COTVET National Apprenticeship Programme**) and **digital literacy programs**, though **teacher training gaps** and **gender literacy disparities** (males: 83.5%, females: 74.5%) remain.

### **Taiwan’s Education System**
Taiwan’s **12-year compulsory system** (since 2014) blends **colonial and ROC influences**, with **strict discipline** (uniforms, cleanup, supplementary classes). Secondary education includes **three tracks** (liber arts, sciences, engineering), accessed via **national exam recommendations or unified tests**. Higher education comprises **universities, graduate schools, and vocational training**, with **military education mandatory for high school students**. **Private education dominates** (cram schools, private kindergartens), and **special education** supports ages 2–22 with disabilities/giftedness. **International programs** attract **~40,000 mainland Chinese students**.

### **Comparative Insights: Vocational Integration and Equity**
Across regions, **vocational education** and **equity** remain focal points, with **Nordic models** excelling in **integration and funding**, while **Hong Kong, Indonesia, and Ghana** struggle with **underfunded pathways**. **Turkey and Iran** prioritize **secular/Islamic values** but face **vocational gaps and political restrictions**. **Ireland** leads in **inclusive higher education**, while **Taiwan** balances **discipline and internationalization**. **Key trends**:
- **Nordic/European models**: Strong vocational-alignment, universal access.
- **Middle East/Africa**: Vocational underdevelopment, funding disparities.
- **Future priorities**: **Digital education, teacher retention, and labor-market alignment**.
