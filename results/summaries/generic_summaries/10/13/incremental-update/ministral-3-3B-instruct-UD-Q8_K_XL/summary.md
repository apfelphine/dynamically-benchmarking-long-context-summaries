### **Swedish, Israeli, Hong Kong, Irish, Ghanaian, Nigerian, English, Taiwanese, French, International, Iranian, Turkish, and Pre-Primary Education Systems: Comparative Overview**

#### **Structured Compulsory Education Systems**
Sweden’s **12-year upper secondary system** splits into **preparatory (67%) and vocational (33%) tracks**, while Ireland’s **5–16-year curriculum** culminates in the **Leaving Certificate** (87.7% university progression). Taiwan’s **12-year compulsory education** integrates **military training and vocational pathways** since 2016, and Turkey’s **4+4+4 system** (2012) mandates **4 years each of primary and secondary**, with **vocational tracks** (general or religious). Hong Kong’s **3-year kindergarten, 6 primary, and 3 junior secondary phases** lead to *HKDSE*, emphasizing **patriotism and vocational ties to China’s GBA**. Ghana’s **12-year compulsory basic education** transitions to **letter-based grading (WASSCE)**, while Nigeria’s **9-3-4 system** relies on **SSCE/GCE exams** for tertiary entry.

#### **Vocational & Technical Education (TVET) & Apprenticeships**
Taiwan’s **vocational high schools** (since 2016) align with **industry demands**, reducing reliance on university-only pathways. Ghana/Nigeria’s **TVET programs** remain underutilized, with Nigeria’s **UBE-funded adult literacy programs** and **technical colleges** offering limited apprenticeships. Turkey’s **vocational high schools** integrate **2–4-year apprenticeships**, though **recent reforms** (e.g., exclusion of evolution) spark debates. Iran’s **vocational tracks** provide **associate degrees**, balancing **religious and technical education**.

#### **Curriculum & Nationalistic Indoctrination**
Taiwan’s curriculum emphasizes **Mandarin, STEM, national identity, and military training**, with **strict discipline**. France’s system follows a **standardized curriculum** with **specialized tracks**, while Turkey’s curriculum includes **core subjects (Turkish, Math, Science, Foreign Languages)** + electives, with **mandatory Atatürk-centric indoctrination** (e.g., genocide denial framing) and **Arabic introduced as an elective (2016–17)**. Iran’s curriculum aligns with **OERP-affiliated training centers**, ensuring **gender-segregated and religiously aligned** instruction.

#### **Higher Education & Post-Secondary Pathways**
Taiwan’s **95%+ university enrollment** includes **prestigious public universities**, while France’s **state-run universities** and **grandes écoles** follow standardized curricula. Turkey’s **state-funded universities** (167+ public institutions) offer **low fees**, with **private universities charging ~$30K/year**; **YÖS exams** dominate admissions. Iran’s **free higher education** contrasts with **mandatory religious studies**, and **Türkiye Scholarships** attract **165K+ applicants annually**.

#### **Teacher Development & Performance**
Taiwan’s **Nationwide Teacher In-Service Education Web** supports lifelong learning, while France’s **Écoles normales (ÉNS)** and **INSPE** regulate training. Turkey’s system is **centralized under the Ministry of National Education**, with **standardized curricula** and **controversial reforms** (e.g., textbook quality, Atatürk-focused education). Iran’s **OERP-affiliated training centers** ensure **gender-segregated instruction**, embedding **religious ethics** in teacher preparation.

#### **COVID-19 & Digital Disparities**
The pandemic exposed **digital gaps**, particularly in **Ghana/Nigeria/Iran/Turkey/France**. Turkey’s **mobile courses (1938–1974)** adapted to **digital training**, while **Iran’s Payame Noor University** expanded **distance learning**. Taiwan’s **strict closures (2020–23)** led to **teacher burnout**, and **Hong Kong/France** struggled with **limited digital tools**.

#### **Emerging Trends & Policy Shifts**
- **Turkey’s 12-year compulsory education** expands access, but **recent reforms** (e.g., curriculum changes, textbook controversies) spark debates.
- **Iran’s mandatory university service** and **vocational reforms** balance **religious and technical education**.
- **Taiwan’s vocational expansion** and **France’s *baccalauréat* reforms** modernize pathways.
- **Nigeria’s JAMB standardization** improves tertiary admissions, while **digital literacy programs** aim to bridge gaps.
- **Private education growth** in **Taiwan (cram schools)** and **France (contract vs. non-contract schools)** reflects shifting demand.

#### **Key Comparative Trends**
- **Cost & Access**:
  - **Sweden/Ireland/England/Taiwan/France/Iran/Turkey**: **Tuition-free state-funded education** ensures **universal access**, with **Taiwan’s vocational tracks** and **France/Iran/Turkey’s mandatory service** diversifying pathways.
  - **Hong Kong**: **UGC-funded free universities** but **expensive private schools**; **patriotism-driven curriculum** limits critical thinking.
  - **Turkey’s optional pre-primary and vocational tracks** highlight **flexibility**, while **Iran’s free higher education** contrasts with **mandatory religious studies**.
  - **Ghana/Nigeria**: **UBE’s nominal costs** deter poorer students; **JAMB’s standardization** improves tertiary admissions but **digital divides** persist.

- **Curriculum Rigor vs. Equity**:
  - **Taiwan/France/Iran/Turkey**: **High-stakes exams** and **standardized curricula** prioritize **performance**, with **Turkey’s nationalist indoctrination** and **Iran’s religious observance** creating distinct frameworks.
  - **Ireland/England**: **Balanced curricula** with **vocational pathways** (e.g., **T-levels, Leaving Certificate mocks**).
  - **Ghana/Nigeria**: **Core subject focus** (Math, English, Civic) with **limited vocational integration**, reflecting **resource constraints**.

#### **Private & Supplementary Education**
- **Taiwan**: **Cram schools** dominate, with debates over **language development**.
- **France**: **Private schools** follow the state curriculum (under contract) or offer independent programs, including **105 international schools** for English-medium education.
- **Turkey**: **Private schools** (e.g., **Mektep**) cater to **secular and religious families**, with **Dershane** (traditional religious tutoring centers) now academic high schools since 2015.
- **Turkey’s Minority Schools**: Established by **Jewish/Greek/Armenian communities** under the **Lausanne Treaty**, offering **Turkish-language instruction** alongside minority languages.

#### **Religious, Nationalistic, and Language Integration**
- **Turkey**: **Religious studies** (since 1980) are **constitutionally mandated**, with **Atatürk’s secular values** emphasized alongside **Arabic as an elective (2016–17)**. **State textbooks deny Armenian genocide**, framing events as "imperialist manipulation."
- **Iran**: **Religious ethics** are **mandatory** in teacher training and **curricula**, with **gender-segregated instruction**.
- **France**: **Secularism** is core, but **105 international schools** offer **English-medium education** with flexible curricula.
- **Historical Context**: Turkey’s **Lausanne Treaty (1923)** guaranteed minority rights, but **Jewish schools shifted to Turkish-medium instruction** due to policy shifts, while **Kurdish schools** emerged under **Turkification policies**.

#### **Textbook & Media Criticisms**
- **Turkey**: State-approved textbooks are **criticized for low quality**, leading to **high demand for private textbooks** despite official bans. **Genocide denial** is embedded in history lessons.
- **France**: **Standardized textbooks** align with **national curriculum**, though **international schools** offer varied programs.
