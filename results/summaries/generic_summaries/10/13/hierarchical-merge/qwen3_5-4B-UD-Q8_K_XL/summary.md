# Global Comparative Analysis of Education Systems

Regional analyses highlight a spectrum of challenges, from centralized funding deficits to structural fragmentation. In Asia, the Middle East, and West Africa, nations like Indonesia, Egypt, Nigeria, and Ghana struggle with low quality, teacher shortages, wealth gaps, and reliance on private tutoring or institutions. Specific hurdles include fragmented administration in Sri Lanka, political instability and inequality in Israel, and federal stratification in West Africa. Globally, critical systemic deficits persist, particularly regarding inadequate ICT integration and gaps in Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), underscoring a tension between expanding access and maintaining quality.

Europe demonstrates divergent models of equity and performance, ranging from Norway's unified rigor to Sweden's inclusive approach facing academic integrity debates. England operates a unified state-funded model with strong equity monitoring, though it excludes Elective Home Education. While England has significantly elevated standards since 2010—driven by the academy model and a national funding formula that supports inclusivity for Special Educational Needs—other regions face distinct issues. For instance, France maintains a mandatory, secular system divided into *collège* and *lycée*, while Turkey's secular-nationalist model has recently faced criticism over curriculum adjustments, including the removal of evolution theory and the denial of the Armenian genocide.
