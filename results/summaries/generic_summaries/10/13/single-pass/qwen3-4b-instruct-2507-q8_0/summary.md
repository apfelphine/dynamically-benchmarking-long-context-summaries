### **Education in Indonesia**

**History:**  
- **Islamic Kingdoms Period:** Islamic boarding schools (Pesantren) emerged, blending Islamic and Hindu-Buddhist traditions.  
- **Dutch Colonial Period:** Introduced formal education, initially segregated by social status. Dutch schools (e.g., HIS, HCS) were for natives and Chinese, while Europeans had separate institutions. By 1930, 3,108 public schools for natives existed.  
- **Japanese Occupation (WWII):** Education was restructured to promote Japanese nationalism, with indoctrination and military drills; enrollment dropped significantly.  
- **Post-Independence (1945):** A new system was created to be non-discriminatory and nationalist. Religious schools (Pesantren, Madrasah) gained support. By 1961, 46.7% of the population was literate.  

**Structure:**  
- **Early Education:** PAUD (preschool) covers Taman Bermain (playgroup) and Taman Kanak-Kanak (kindergarten).  
- **Primary & Secondary:** Compulsory for 12 years. Students attend state-run or religious schools. The curriculum emphasizes Pancasila (nationalism, unity, democracy, social justice). Rote learning and teacher authority dominate.  
- **Islamic Schools:** Three types—Pesantren (traditional), Madrasah (integrated secular/religious), and Sekolah Islam (state curriculum with Islamic content).  
- **Higher Education:** Offers academic, vocational, and professional programs. Governed by the Indonesian National Qualification Framework (KKNI).  

**Challenges:**  
- Teacher shortages, especially in remote areas.  
- Inequities in access and quality.  
- High student-to-teacher ratios (17:1 primary, 15.2:1 secondary).  
- Funding remains a persistent issue.

### **Education in Armenia**

**Literacy & History:**  
- Literacy reached 100% by 1960. Soviet-era education emphasized state control, with curricula and politics tightly linked. Post-independence reforms focused on Armenian history, language, and culture.  

**K–12 System:**  
- Compulsory, free, and spans 12 years. Public schools dominate (97%), with private schools offering specialized programs.  
- Preschool to secondary education includes structured subjects and assessments.  

**School Types:**  
- **Public Schools:** Government-run, with national curriculum.  
- **Private Schools:** Offer specialized curricula and access to resources.  
- **Religious Schools (e.g., Madrasahs):** Provide religious instruction.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Strong presence of state universities (e.g., Yerevan State University).  
- Armenia joined the Bologna Process and is a member of the European Higher Education Area.  
- Challenges include student absenteeism and inequality in access.  

**Challenges:**  
- Student absenteeism has grown, especially in rural areas.  
- Gender disparities in education access.  
- Need for curriculum reform and teacher development.

### **Education in Egypt**

**History:**  
- Ancient Egypt had schools like "Bar Ankh" and institutions like al-Azhar and Dar al-Ilm.  
- Muhammad Ali introduced a dual system: traditional Islamic schools (mansouri) and modern secular institutions (madrasas).  
- British occupation led to neglect of education, especially for Egyptians.  
- Post-independence, free public education was introduced.  

**Structure:**  
- Compulsory for 12 years.  
- Basic education (ages 4–14) followed by secondary (ages 15–17).  
- Matriculation exams (Bagrut) determine university admission.  

**Challenges:**  
- High student-to-teacher ratios and reliance on private tutoring.  
- Inequities in access, especially for rural and marginalized groups.  
- Low quality of teaching and curriculum.  
- Gender disparities in dropout rates.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Extensive system with public and private institutions.  
- Strong performance in science and engineering.  
- Challenges in access and quality, especially for non-elite students.

### **Education in Norway**

**History & System:**  
- Education began in the 19th century with a focus on universal access.  
- The 9-year comprehensive school (grunnskole) system replaced earlier structures.  

**Structure:**  
- **Primary (6–16):** Compulsory, with 16 subjects. Grading system uses A–E.  
- **Secondary (16–19):** Gymnasium (preparatory) or vocational tracks.  
- **Higher Education:** State-funded, with a focus on quality and equity.  

**Key Features:**  
- Emphasis on student choice and individual development.  
- Strong focus on active learning and student-centered pedagogy.  
- Comprehensive school system with equal access to education.  

**Challenges:**  
- Inequities in access and quality, especially in rural areas.  
- Teacher shortages and low teacher salaries.  
- Criticism of the grading system and curriculum.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

**Structure:**  
- Compulsory from age 4 to 14.  
- Three tiers: primary (ages 4–14), secondary (ages 14–18), and tertiary.  

**School Types:**  
- **Government Schools:** 9,782 schools, with a mix of national, provincial, and pirivena (Buddhist religious schools).  
- **Private Schools:** Offer English-medium education and are growing.  
- **Arab and Muslim Schools:** Offer Arabic and Islamic education.  

**Higher Education:**  
- 15 state universities; growing private institutions.  
- High competition for university admission.  
- Limited access for students from marginalized communities.  

**Challenges:**  
- Inequities in access and quality.  
- Low teacher quality and teacher shortages.  
- High dropout rates in rural areas.  
- Need for reform in curriculum and teacher training.

### **Education in Israel**

**Structure:**  
- Three tiers: primary (ages 6–12), middle (ages 12–15), and high school (ages 15–18).  
- Four tracks: state-secular, state-religious, independent religious (Haredi), and Arab.  

**Key Features:**  
- High emphasis on academic achievement and national identity.  
- Haredi schools offer limited secular education.  
- Matriculation exams (Bagrut) determine university admission.  

**Challenges:**  
- Inequities in access and quality, especially for Haredi and Arab students.  
- Teacher shortages and low teacher salaries.  
- Need for reform in curriculum and teacher training.

### **Education in Hong Kong**

**History & System:**  
- British colonial period introduced bilingual education.  
- Post-1997, education shifted toward Chinese-medium instruction.  

**Structure:**  
- Compulsory from age 6 to 18.  
- Public schools dominate, with EMI (English-medium) schools being elite.  
- National exams (HKDSE) determine university admission.  

**Key Features:**  
- High academic performance in international assessments.  
- Growing emphasis on vocational and technical education.  

**Challenges:**  
- Inequities in access and quality, especially for non-English-speaking students.  
- Teacher shortages and low teacher salaries.  
- Need for reform in curriculum and teacher training.
