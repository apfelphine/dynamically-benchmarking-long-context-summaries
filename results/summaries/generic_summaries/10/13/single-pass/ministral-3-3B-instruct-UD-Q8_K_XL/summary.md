### **Education in Indonesia**

#### **Historical Overview**
Indonesian education evolved through distinct periods, shaped by colonial and indigenous influences. During the **Islamic kingdoms period**, Islamic boarding schools (*Pondok Pesantren*) emerged, emphasizing religious and cultural education. The **Dutch colonial period** introduced formal education, stratified by social status, with schools like *Sekolah Rakjat* (folk schools) catering to native Indonesians. The **Japanese occupation** consolidated education under Japanese curricula, promoting indoctrination of Japanese culture.

Post-independence, Indonesia established a secular, anti-discriminatory education system, emphasizing nationalism and Islamic values. The **1945 Constitution** guaranteed universal education, and the Ministry of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology (*Kemendikbudristek*) oversaw its implementation.

---

#### **Early Education**
- **Pre-school (PAUD)**: Includes *Taman Bermain* (playgroup) and *Taman Kanak-Kanak* (kindergarten). Mandatory for ages 2–4, with 99.35% of kindergartens privately operated.
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory for 12 years, starting at age 6. Students choose between state-run schools (supervised by *Kemendikbudristek*) or private/religious schools.

---

#### **Public and Secondary Education**
- **Structure**: Primary (6 years), Junior High (3 years), Senior High (3 years), and tertiary education.
- **Curriculum**: Emphasizes Pancasila (national principles) and Islamic values. Grading system uses A–F, with rote learning as a dominant pedagogy.
- **Challenges**: High student-to-teacher ratios, funding disparities, and quality issues persist. The **2013 curriculum** introduced specialized groups (e.g., natural sciences, social sciences) with structured hours per subject.

- **Islamic Schools**:
  - **Pesantren**: Traditional Islamic boarding schools with religious focus.
  - **Madrasah**: Integrate religious and secular education.
  - **Sekolah Islam**: Follow secular curriculum with Islamic curricula.

---

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: Public and private institutions, with public universities receiving state funding. Admission based on national exams.
- **Vocational Education**: Offered through polytechnics and technical colleges.
- **International Recognition**: Top universities rank globally, though challenges include funding and teacher quality.

### **Education in Armenia**

#### **Literacy and Education System**
- **Literacy**: Achieved 100% by 1960, with low numeracy in the 18th–19th centuries.
- **Education**: Free primary and secondary education, with Russian as a secondary language. English is rapidly gaining popularity.
- **Structure**:
  - **Preschool**: Ages 2–6, with 808 community, 10 public, and 50 private institutions.
  - **Secondary Education**: Compulsory for 12 years, structured into primary, middle, and high schools. High enrollment rates (91.3% primary, 90.1% secondary).

---

#### **Higher Education and Exams**
- **Universities**: 26 state and 33 private institutions. Top institutions include Yerevan State University and Yerevan State Medical University.
- **Exams**: Standardized tests determine university admissions. Armenia is a member of the Bologna Process and Erasmus+ programs.
- **Challenges**: High student absenteeism and dropout rates, particularly among immigrant students.

### **Education in Sri Lanka**

#### **Historical and Administrative Context**
- **Colonial Influence**: British introduced formal education, with missionary schools dominating until independence.
- **Post-Independence**: Free and compulsory education introduced, with a focus on national languages (Sinhala/Tamil) and religious instruction.
- **Structure**: Primary (5 years), Junior Secondary (4 years), Senior Secondary (2 years), and tertiary education. Compulsory until grade 9.

---

#### **School Types and Tertiary Education**
- **Government Schools**: 10,155 schools (373 national, 9,782 provincial), with 4.2 million students and 235,924 teachers.
- **Private Schools**: 66 private schools, including international schools offering IB, GCSE, and A-levels.
- **Madrasas**: 205 registered madrasas teaching Islamic education.
- **Tertiary Education**: 15 state universities, with competitive admissions based on GCE Advanced Level exams. Open University of Sri Lanka offers distance learning.

---

#### **Vocational Education and Challenges**
- **Vocational Training**: Managed by the Tertiary and Vocational Education Commission. Challenges include low completion rates and outdated curricula.
- **Criticisms**: Rigid and competitive education system, lack of private university establishment, and gender disparities.

### **Education in Norway**

#### **Structure and Compulsory Education**
- **Compulsory Education**: Ages 6–16, divided into *grunnskole* (primary, 7 years) and *ungdomsskole* (lower secondary, 3 years).
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on Swedish, English, mathematics, and core subjects. Grading system uses A–F, with standardized national tests in grades 3, 6, and 9.
- **Upper Secondary Education**: 3-year program (*videregående skole*), with vocational and preparatory tracks. Mandatory attendance until age 19.

---

#### **Higher Education and Special Education**
- **Higher Education**: Free for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens. Universities and university colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Admission based on grades and standardized tests.
- **Special Education**: Inclusive schools for students with disabilities, supported by the Educational/Psychological Service.
- **Challenges**: High student absenteeism, particularly among immigrant students.

### **Education in Hong Kong**

#### **History and School Structure**
- **Colonial Influence**: British missionaries established early schools, with English as the primary language.
- **Post-1997**: Target Oriented Curriculum (TOC) introduced, emphasizing project-based learning. Recent reforms include a focus on patriotism and national security.
- **Structure**:
  - **Primary/Secondary**: Compulsory for 6 years (ages 6–12), followed by 3 years of secondary education. Students take the Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE).
  - **International Schools**: 175 schools offering curricula from the UK, US, and IB.

---

#### **Higher Education and Challenges**
- **Universities**: 8 UGC-funded universities (e.g., HKU, CUHK, HKUST), with tuition fees capped at HK$20,000 per year.
- **Challenges**: High student dropout rates, particularly among international students. Recent controversies include restrictions on foreign teachers and curriculum changes.

### **Education in England**

#### **Compulsory Education and School Types**
- **Compulsory Education**: Ages 5–18, divided into Foundation Stage (ages 3–5), Key Stages 1–4 (ages 5–16), and post-16 education.
- **School Types**:
  - **State-funded**: Grammar, comprehensive, and academies.
  - **Private**: Fee-paying schools, including public schools for 13–18-year-olds.
- **Curriculum**: National Curriculum covers core subjects (English, Maths, Science) and electives (arts, languages, etc.).

---

#### **Higher Education and Funding**
- **Higher Education**: Free for EU/EEA students; tuition fees up to £9,535 per year for domestic students. Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees.
- **Challenges**: High student debt, funding disparities, and competition for university places.

### **Education in Taiwan**

#### **Structure and Compulsory Education**
- **Compulsory Education**: 12 years (ages 6–18), starting with elementary school (6 years) and junior high school (3 years).
- **Curriculum**: Mandarin as the primary language, with foreign languages (English, French, German) and science/math electives.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and junior colleges offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Popular fields include engineering and business.

---

#### **Special Education and Challenges**
- **Special Education**: Services for students with disabilities, including centralized classes and resource rooms.
- **Challenges**: High pressure on students, reliance on cram schools (*buxiban*), and limited resources in rural areas.

---

### **Education in France**

#### **History and Governance**
- **History**: Modern education system established by Jules Ferry in 1881, emphasizing free, secular, and compulsory education.
- **Governance**: Ministry of National Education oversees primary, secondary, and higher education. Private schools are regulated but not controlled by the state.

---

#### **School Structure and Higher Education**
- **Primary/Secondary**: Compulsory for 12 years, with standardized curriculum. Private schools follow the state curriculum or offer specialized programs.
- **Higher Education**: Licence (bachelor’s), Master’s, and Doctorat degrees. Grandes écoles are elite institutions for engineering, business, and law.
- **Challenges**: High student debt, competition for university places, and debates over secularism and religious instruction.

---

### **Education in Iran**

#### **Structure and Compulsory Education**
- **Compulsory Education**: 9 years (ages 6–15), starting with elementary school (*Dabestan*) and followed by lower-secondary (*Motovasseteh avval*) and upper-secondary (*Motovasseteh dovom*) education.
- **Curriculum**: Emphasizes Islamic values, with mandatory prayer time and gender segregation. Foreign languages (English, Arabic) are taught.

---

#### **Higher Education and Challenges**
- **Higher Education**: Universities and technical institutes offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Admission based on national exams (*Konkour*).
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, privatization of education, and restrictions on women’s education. Recent reforms aim to improve quality and global integration.

### **Education in Turkey**

#### **History and Compulsory Education**
- **History**: Founded by Atatürk, the Turkish Republic established a secular education system. The 1924 Law of Integration of Education combined separate educational channels.
- **Compulsory Education**: 12 years (ages 6–18), starting with primary school (8 years) and followed by secondary education.

---

#### **Secondary and Higher Education**
- **Secondary Education**: General, vocational, and technical tracks. Students take the University Entrance Exam (*YÖS*) for university admission.
- **Higher Education**: 167 universities, with public universities offering low fees and private universities charging high tuition. Research coordinated by TÜBİTAK.
- **Challenges**: Curriculum controversies, teacher shortages, and debates over secularism and religious instruction.

---
### **Key Themes Across Countries**
- **Compulsory Education**: Universal access, though disparities exist in quality and funding.
- **Higher Education**: Increasing enrollment, with challenges in funding, teacher quality, and student outcomes.
- **Special Education**: Inclusive policies, though implementation varies.
- **Challenges**: Funding disparities, teacher shortages, and societal pressures on students.
