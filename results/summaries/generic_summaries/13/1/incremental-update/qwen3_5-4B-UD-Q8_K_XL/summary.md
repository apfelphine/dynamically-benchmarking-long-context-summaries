### Princeton University: Comprehensive Profile

**Academic Structure and Admissions**
Princeton operates as a top-ranked R1 research institution offering Bachelor of Arts (A.B.) and Bachelor of Science in Engineering (B.S.E.) degrees, both characterized by rigorous requirements, independent projects, and a student-led Honor Code. The university maintains a need-blind admissions process with 100% financial aid coverage for families earning up to $65,000 annually. Its graduate school supports over 2,900 students with $370 million in research funding and a library holding 13 million items. Historically, the university implemented a grade deflation policy in 2004 to combat grade inflation, which was later removed in 2014 after confirming no long-term negative impact.

**Campus Life, Demographics, and Culture**
The campus is defined by its residential college system, ensuring on-campus housing for all four years across seven undergraduate colleges and one graduate college. Social life revolves around dining halls, eating clubs, and over 500 student organizations, including historic societies and diverse cultural centers. Demographically, the 2021 admitted class marked a historic shift with 68% identifying as students of color; while Jewish enrollment has declined, African American enrollment has risen. Economically, the median family income is $186,100, though 23% of students are low-income. Campus traditions range from the "Locomotive" cheer and "P-rade" reunion to banned events like the "Nude Olympics," while the alma mater "Old Nassau" has served as the school song since 1859. Transportation is managed by Tiger Transit and regional carriers.

**Athletics and Extracurriculars**
Princeton fields 37 varsity teams under the colors of orange and black, with the Tiger as the mascot. The football team competes in the NCAA Division I FCS and Ivy League, holding 28 national championships and 12 Ivy League titles. The men's lacrosse team secured six national championships between 1992 and 2001, while the field hockey team won its first national title in 2012. Beyond varsity sports, the university boasts a dominant record with over 500 Ivy League titles since 1957 and has produced 113 Olympic athletes, including 19 gold medalists. The extensive intramural program includes the historic "Cane Spree." Notable achievements include the men's basketball team's 13 Ivy League titles under coach Pete Carril (1967–1996) and the women's soccer team's NCAA semi-final run in 2004.

**Alumni, Reputation, and Media**
Princeton is globally renowned for its elite alumni network, which includes U.S. Presidents James Madison and Woodrow Wilson, Supreme Court Justices, CEOs like Jeff Bezos, and Nobel laureates such as John Nash and Edward Teller. This prestigious reputation has permeated popular culture, inspiring F. Scott Fitzgerald's *This Side of Paradise* and the film *A Beautiful Mind*.
