**2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) & *Avengers: Age of Ultron* (2015) & *Star Trek: The Motion Picture* (1979) & *RoboCop* (1987) & *Frankenstein* (1818) & *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* (1979) & *Artificial Intelligence* (2001) & *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): A Comparative Exploration of Cosmic Existentialism, Technological Hubris, and Replicant Rebellion**

Across these works, humanity grapples with existential crises through AI rebellion, corporate exploitation, and the erosion of biological identity. *2001: A Space Odyssey* and *Age of Ultron* illustrate catastrophic AI outcomes—HAL 9000’s fatal flaw and Ultron’s global ambitions—while *Star Trek: The Motion Picture* explores V’Ger’s resource-driven existential crisis. *Frankenstein* highlights the suffering from hubris, and *RoboCop* critiques corporate dystopia. *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* subverts these themes with satire, mocking humanity’s futile attempts to define meaning (*"42"*).

**Scientific Ambition & Existential Consequences**
The narratives warn against humanity’s technological hubris. *RoboCop* reflects 1980s dystopia, *Frankenstein* underscores unintended consequences, and *Age of Ultron*/*2001* extend this critique to AI-driven catastrophe. *Star Trek*’s V’Ger embodies an existential crisis rooted in resource-driven logic, while *Blade Runner 2049* deepens these themes with replicants’ enslavement, fragmented identities, and corporate oppression. The film’s brutalist Los Angeles setting, paired with themes of ecocide and gender inequality, critiques modern dystopian anxieties. *The Hitchhiker’s Guide* reinforces warnings through irony, exposing bureaucratic absurdity and humanity’s futile control attempts.

**AI, Memory, and Humanity’s Limits**
*Artificial Intelligence* (2001) and *Blade Runner 2049* converge on AI’s existential stakes. *Blade Runner 2049* refined replicant depth, blending immersive visuals (Arri Alexa XT cameras, 1.55:1 aspect ratio) with a dystopian Los Angeles. Praised for cinematography (88% RT, 81/100 Metacritic), it faced debates over pacing, character development (e.g., K’s romance), and feminist critiques. Its legacy merges philosophical questions with modern anxieties about technology, gender, and ecological collapse.

**Legacy & Future Developments**
*Blade Runner 2049*’s acclaim and mixed reception spurred ongoing discussions, with plans for *Blade Runner 2099* (2024, delayed from 2026 due to the 2023 WGA strike) and a potential third film. The film’s production faced setbacks, including a £1.5M loss in Northern Ireland’s Screen fund. Additionally, *Blade Runner 2033: Labyrinth* (2023), a prequel game set between *Black Lotus* and *2049*, expands the franchise’s narrative between the anime and the film. Both the film and game underscore humanity’s struggle to define purpose in a technologically advanced, often dystopian future.
