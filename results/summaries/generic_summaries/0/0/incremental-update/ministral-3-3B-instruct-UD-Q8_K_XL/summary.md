**Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979) & *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Existential Sci-Fi & Visual Revolution**
The 23rd-century *Star Trek: The Motion Picture* introduced **V’Ger**, an alien energy entity driven by humanity’s destruction, while blending existential themes with Douglas Trumbull’s pioneering visual effects. Kubrick’s *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968) redefined sci-fi with minimalist storytelling and the enigmatic **monolith**, probing humanity’s purpose and evolution. Both films set the stage for later works exploring AI ethics (*Age of Ultron*), dystopian futures (*Blade Runner*), and corporate dystopias (*RoboCop*).

**Blade Runner & Dystopian Depth**
Ridley Scott’s *Blade Runner* (1982) and Denis Villeneuve’s *Blade Runner 2049* (2017) deepened existential questions about **memory, identity, and systemic oppression**, using IMAX cinematography and a sprawling runtime. While Villeneuve’s film faced critiques over pacing and gender representation, its themes of **climate collapse** and **AI-driven exploitation** cemented its place as a philosophical sci-fi masterpiece.

**The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy: Absurd Cosmic Comedy & Enduring Legacy**
Douglas Adams’ *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* (1979) launched a universe of absurdity, following Arthur Dent’s journey with the **humorous Hitchhiker’s Guide** (voiced by Fry, Jones, and Moore). The series’ **"Ultimate Question"** (42) and iconic characters—**Ford Prefect, Zaphod Beeblebrox, Marvin the Paranoid Android, and Trillian**—became pop-culture touchstones. Its **radio adaptations** (1985–2005) expanded its reach, including anniversary editions and original scripts, while **text-based games** (1982–84) and a 2004 online revival preserved its legacy.

**Cultural Impact & Merchandise**
The franchise’s influence extended to **Towel Day (May 25)**, merchandise (towels, T-shirts, action figures), and spin-offs like short stories (*"Young Zaphod Plays It Safe"*) and novels (*Starship Titanic*). The number **42** and references to *Dent*, *Zaphod*, and *Marvin* permeated sci-fi, video games, and pop culture. Adams’ prescient predictions (e.g., Babel Fish, voice assistants) aligned with modern tech, while adaptations like the 2014 Radio 4 revival honored his original vision.
