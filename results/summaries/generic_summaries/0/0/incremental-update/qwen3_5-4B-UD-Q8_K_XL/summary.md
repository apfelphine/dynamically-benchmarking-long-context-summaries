### Media Adaptations and Legacy

*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* franchise spans a diverse array of media, including the 1981 BBC television series, a delayed Hulu adaptation, and a 2005 film directed by Deep Sehgal which diverged from the source material. The story has also flourished in stage productions, audio expansions, and various digital formats such as text adventures, mobile games, and comics. Key narrative threads remain consistent across these formats, centering on Arthur Dent's journey, the search for the "Ultimate Question," and the revelation that "6 x 9" is the answer to life, the universe, and everything.

### Expanded Context and Cultural Impact

With over 14 million copies sold, the novels—often described as a "trilogy in five parts"—have secured an enduring cultural footprint. The franchise's influence extends beyond text and screen to include extensive merchandising ("Hitch-Hikeriana") like towels, T-shirts, and action figures, as well as annual fan events such as "Towel Day." The cultural imprint is further solidified by the ubiquity of the number 42 in pop culture and science fiction, as well as references in works like *Doctor Who*, *Star Trek*, and Radiohead's *OK Computer*.

### Literary Expansions

The legacy of the work continues through additional literature, including related stories by Douglas Adams and Terry Jones, and published collections of radio and television scripts that preserve the original creative voices and production notes.
