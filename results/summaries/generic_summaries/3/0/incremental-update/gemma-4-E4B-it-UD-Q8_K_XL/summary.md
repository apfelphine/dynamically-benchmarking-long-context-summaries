**Transhumanism, AI Futures, and the Singularity:** Transhumanism envisions a "posthuman" state via biotech and AI, anticipating a technological singularity around 2045. This transition carries risks, primarily concerning **AI alignment** and hostile outcomes, while aiming for goals like immortality and mind uploading.

**Accelerationism: Ideology, Variants, and Critiques:** Accelerationism generally advocates for intensified systemic change driven by technology, rejecting fixed notions of humanity. It encompasses variants such as **Prometheanism** (overcoming capital constraints). Ideologically, it manifests in several ways:
*   **Right-wing accelerationism** (e.g., Nick Land) supports unregulated capitalism toward a "technocapital singularity," sometimes aligning with **The Dark Enlightenment**.
*   **Left-wing accelerationism** seeks to democratize technology to transcend capitalism, often embracing global forces and relating to **Xenofeminism**.
*   **Effective accelerationism (e/acc)** prioritizes unrestricted technological progress to solve global problems.

This concept is complex; critiques have noted capital's resilience (Franco Berardi), warned of colonial risks in global application (Yuk Hui), and seen racialized interpretations like "Blacceleration" (Aria Dean). It has also been invoked by political theorists (Slavoj Žižek) and dissidents, but most alarmingly, it is used by far-right extremists advocating for violent racial conflict to establish white ethnostates.

**Broader Context and Risks:** The singularity presents severe existential threats if superintelligence is misaligned. Accelerationism is linked to **hyperstition** (future beliefs shaping the present), and critics contest its determinism.

**Data Privacy and Regulation:** Regarding governance, global data protection is structured by regulations like the EU's GDPR (2018), which mandates strict rules for the Digital Single Market, emphasizing **data subject rights** (access, erasure) and **Data Protection by Design and Default**, with the UK maintaining its "UK GDPR."
