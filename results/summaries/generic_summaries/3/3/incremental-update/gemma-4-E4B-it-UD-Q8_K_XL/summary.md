**AI Futures, Technology, and Ethics**
Accelerationism and Transhumanism push to transcend human limitations via technology, raising concerns about dehumanization. This intersects with the **Technological Singularity** (AI surpassing human intellect), predicted to arrive closer to 2100, involving risks like AI misalignment. Philosophical work, such as John L. Pollock's research on **defeasible reasoning** and the **OSCAR** prototype, informs AI development.

**AI Governance and Risk Management**
Managing risks, from autonomous weapons to superintelligence, requires AI ethics focused on fairness and transparency to counteract **algorithmic biases** and the **"Black-Box Problem."** Governance necessitates both **Technical Measures** (like XAI) and **Regulatory Frameworks**. Globally, responses include evolving **legislation** (e.g., EU AI Act 2024) and the involvement of bodies like **DARPA**.

**Regulation in the US**
US regulation is evolving through federal, state, and academic actions. Key milestones include the 2022 AI Bill of Rights and President Biden's 2023 Executive Order. Current efforts involve congressional legislation targeting AI deepfakes and state laws (California, Tennessee) addressing bias and liability, while agencies like NIST and FTC provide guidance amid public concern.

**Information Integrity and Societal Impact**
**Deepfakes**, created using methods like GANs, present an "epistemic threat" by undermining trust in **Art, Entertainment, and Politics**, facilitating **Fraud, Scams, Blackmail**, and disinformation. Countermeasures involve algorithmic detection and **blockchain verification**.

**Industry Influence and Political Dynamics**
In the current political landscape, industry influence is growing, as evidenced by Silicon Valley companies and investors donating \$200 million in August 2025 to pro-AI super PACs, criticizing politicians who they deem insufficiently supportive of artificial intelligence ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.
