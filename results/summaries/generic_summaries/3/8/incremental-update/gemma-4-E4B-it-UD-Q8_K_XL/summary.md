AI regulation is a global focus, exemplified by the EU AI Act and data privacy regulations like GDPR. Concurrently, Transhumanism—the push for radical human enhancement toward a "posthuman" state using science and technology (such as NBIC)—is a subject of intense debate.

The movement encompasses concepts like Immortalism and mind uploading, advocating for cognitive and morphological freedom. However, it faces significant scrutiny across ethical, societal, and practical fronts:

**Ethical and Philosophical Concerns:**
Critics question the morality of radical alteration, fearing it diminishes life's meaning (as suggested by Bill McKibben), blurs human identity, or replaces intrinsic human values. Philosophers also worry that modifying human nature undermines egalitarian ideals.

**Societal and Economic Impacts:**
Debate centers on the potential for exacerbating social inequality through access to enhancements, creating a "genetic divide." While some proponents suggest genetic engineering could democratize privileges, concerns remain about cultural uniformity and societal control. Furthermore, the movement navigates tensions between antinatalism (discouraging reproduction) and pronatalism (addressing demographic decline).

**Risk and Advancement:**
Discussions surrounding existential risks are varied; while some advocate for cautious, precautionary progress, transhumanists favor rapid technological advancement. Critics argue this technological focus overlooks deeper philosophical issues. Moreover, the movement is increasingly being co-opted by anti-democratic groups as a unifying stereotype against "liberal-globalist" values. Solutions proposed to mitigate risks and inequality range from outright bans to implementing public policies.
