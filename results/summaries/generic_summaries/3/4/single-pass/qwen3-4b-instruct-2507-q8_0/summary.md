The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** is a comprehensive EU law establishing strict standards for the collection, processing, and protection of personal data. It applies to any organization, whether in the EU or not, that processes personal data of individuals located in the EU, especially when offering goods or services or monitoring behavior within the EU. Key principles include lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, and integrity. Data subjects have extensive rights, including access, rectification, erasure, data portability, and objection to automated decisions. Controllers and processors must implement technical and organizational measures, including data protection by design and by default, and maintain records of processing activities. A Data Protection Officer (DPO) is required for certain high-risk processing, and organizations must appoint a representative in the EU if they operate outside the EU.

The GDPR introduces a robust framework for data security, mandating breach notifications within 72 hours and imposing significant penalties—up to €20 million or 4% of global turnover—for violations. It includes specific provisions for international data transfers, requiring appropriate safeguards such as standard contractual clauses or adequacy decisions. The regulation also establishes a harmonized enforcement structure through independent supervisory authorities (SAs) across member states, coordinated by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), which ensures consistency and cooperation.

Despite its broad scope, the GDPR includes notable exemptions, such as personal or household activities, law enforcement, and national security. Misconceptions persist, including the idea that consent is required for all data processing or that individuals have an absolute right to erasure. The regulation has had significant global influence, prompting the adoption of similar privacy laws in jurisdictions like California (CCPA) and China (PIPL), and has been cited as a "Brussels effect" in shaping international privacy standards.

The GDPR has also been a catalyst for increased compliance costs, with businesses investing heavily in data governance, consent management, and security. While it has improved data transparency and user awareness, concerns remain about administrative burdens, unclear implementation, and the potential for misuse in blockchain or AI systems. Enforcement has been inconsistent, with some member states facing backlogs and challenges in applying the law uniformly. The regulation's impact on digital markets and consumer trust has been positive, with studies showing increased public awareness of data rights and a shift toward privacy-conscious business practices.
