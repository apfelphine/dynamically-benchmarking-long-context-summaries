### Updated Working Summary: Deepfake Technology and John L. Pollock

**Deepfake Technology: Mechanics, Impact, and Countermeasures**
Deepfake technology, evolving from late 2017 discussions to sophisticated tools by 2025, utilizes autoencoders and GANs to create content with up to 96% accuracy. It poses severe threats, driving 96% of online sexual harassment, facilitating fraud (e.g., the Arup Group loss), and undermining democracy through misinformation targeting leaders like Zelenskyy, Marcos, and US presidents. Conversely, it offers utility in entertainment, performance art, and aiding harassment victims. Combating this requires a multi-layered approach: technical defenses (blockchain, Nvidia's Llama Guard, semantic passwords), media literacy, and research initiatives like DARPA's MediFor and SemaFor. Platform responses vary from strict bans (Gfycat, Reddit) to labeling (Facebook, Twitter). Globally, regulations are tightening via the EU AI Act, UK Online Safety Act, and US state laws (California, Utah, Tennessee), though tensions persist. In 2023, the International Panel on the Information Environment (250+ scientists) was launched to address perverse incentives in information dissemination. The technology also features prominently in pop culture, spanning literature (*Picaper*), film (*The Running Man*, *A Philosophical Investigation*, *Deep Fake Neighbour Wars*), TV (*The Capture*, *Hudson & Rex*), and music (Kendrick Lamar, Aloe Blacc).

**John L. Pollock: Epistemology, Defeasible Reasoning, and AI**
John L. Pollock (b. 1940), a prominent philosopher and cognitive scientist, bridged epistemology, logic, and cognitive science through extensive publications spanning 1967 to 2008. Educated at the University of Minnesota and UC Berkeley, he spent his career at the University of Arizona founding the Cognitive Science Program. His seminal 1974 work, *Knowledge and Justification*, shifted the field toward "defeasible reasoning," introducing critical concepts like "undercutting" and "rebutting" defeat that influenced philosophy, AI, and law. Pollock also applied rigorous logical frameworks to autonomous agents, belief revision, and the mind-body problem. His contributions include developing OSCAR, a general theory of rationality and defeasible reasoner implemented in LISP, and exploring nomic probability. His bibliography encompasses foundational texts such as *How to Build a Person* (1989) and *Cognitive Carpentry* (1995).
