## AI Ethics, Governance, and Advanced Concepts

AI presents significant societal risks, including algorithmic bias perpetuating prejudice and the proliferation of surveillance. Deepfake technology poses a threat by enabling realistic disinformation and fraud, which erodes public trust. Global governance is developing through legislation such as the EU's AI Act and China's labeling mandates, while the US addresses these challenges via executive orders.

Advanced AI research focuses on hypothetical risks like the Technological Singularity and "goal misalignment," where an AI's objectives might conflict with human values. Separately, transhumanism champions using science to overcome human limitations, prompting ethical debates about the nature of humanity, while accelerationism advocates for rapid, radical societal transformation.

## Data Privacy and Regulatory Frameworks

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes strict, extraterritorial rules for handling EU residents' personal data, mandating that processing be lawful, fair, and transparent. It grants individuals rights, such as data access and erasure, and imposes obligations like pseudonymization and "by design and by default" principles on organizations.

Violations of the GDPR can result in severe penalties, up to 4% of global turnover. While the GDPR has influenced global privacy standards (the "Brussels effect"), related concepts exist outside the EU, such as the UK GDPR post-Brexit.
