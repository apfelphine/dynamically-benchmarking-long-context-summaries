# Technological Futures and AI Governance

Discussions surrounding the future are heavily shaped by the **Technological Singularity**—the hypothetical point of AI's recursive self-improvement—and related concepts like Transhumanism, which seeks to overcome human biological limitations. Key debates involve whether these transitions will occur via a sudden "hard takeoff" or a gradual "soft takeoff."

Ethical and governance challenges are multifaceted. The **AI alignment problem** is central, aiming to ensure superintelligent AI remains aligned with human values to prevent existential risk. Societal concerns also include algorithmic bias in critical areas like justice, the risks associated with "black box" AI, and the potential for social stratification from advancements like Transhumanism.

## Global AI Regulation and Emerging Risks

Global regulatory approaches to AI vary significantly. The European Union employs stringent frameworks, such as the risk-based AI Act, mandating data protection, transparency, and liability clarification. In contrast, US oversight remains characterized by varied federal and state guidelines.

Beyond general governance, specific emerging technologies pose threats. **Deepfake technology** is noted for creating an "epistemic threat" through disinformation, while the operational sustainability of AI models raises environmental concerns regarding their high energy and water consumption.
