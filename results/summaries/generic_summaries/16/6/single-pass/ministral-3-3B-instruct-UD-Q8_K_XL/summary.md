### **Lund University**

#### **History**
Lund University traces its origins to 1085, when the Katedralskolan (cathedral school) was established, making it Scandinavia’s oldest institution. Founded in 1425 as a Franciscan studium generale, it became the first university in Scandinavia in 1438. After Sweden’s acquisition of Scania in 1658, the university was founded in 1666, initially named Academia Carolina. By the 19th century, it expanded with four faculties: Law, Theology, Medicine, and Philosophy. The university faced challenges during the Scanian War (1676) but later grew under reforms, including the establishment of the Order of the Cross and Crown in 1939. Notable figures include Samuel Pufendorf, a juridical historian, and Esaias Tegnér, a prominent poet.

#### **Campus and Facilities**
Lund University’s main campus is in Lund, Scania, with over 100 buildings spread across 1 km². Key facilities include the main library (Helgonabacken), the Biomedical Centre, and the Clinical Research Centre in Malmö. The university also operates satellite campuses in Helsingborg and Ljungbyhed. Notable museums include the Biological Museum, which houses over 10 million specimens, and the Artlab Gallery, showcasing contemporary art.

#### **Academics and Research**
Lund University is renowned for its interdisciplinary research, particularly in neurology, nanotechnology, climate change, and stem cell biology. It ranks among the top universities in Europe and is known for its groundbreaking innovations, such as diagnostic ultrasound and the artificial kidney. The university offers around 275 educational programs and 1,400 courses, with several programs taught in English. Notable alumni include Nobel Prize winners and Fields Medalists.

#### **Student Life and Governance**
Student life is governed by the student nations, the Academic Society (AF), and student unions. The university is known for its vibrant student culture, including the Lund Carnival and the Academic Society’s theater productions. The university is divided into nine faculties, including the Faculty of Humanities and Theology, the Faculty of Engineering, and the School of Aviation. The university’s governance includes a University Board, Vice-Chancellor, and a diverse faculty and student representation.

### **University of Western Ontario**

#### **History**
Founded in 1878 as The Western University of London, Ontario, the university initially focused on Arts, Divinity, Law, and Medicine. It became non-denominational in 1908 and expanded significantly after World War II. In 2012, the university rebranded itself as "Western University" to emphasize its international focus. Notable figures include Sir Frederick Banting, who received the Nobel Prize for discovering insulin, and Alice Munro, the Nobel Prize winner in Literature.

#### **Campus and Facilities**
The university’s main campus is in London, Ontario, with a total area of approximately 170.8 hectares. Key facilities include the University College, the Physics and Astronomy Building, and the Western Interdisciplinary Research Building (WIRB). The university also operates research parks, including Discovery Park, Advanced Manufacturing Park, and the Sarnia-Lambton Research Park. Notable museums include the Museum of Ontario Archaeology and the Artlab Gallery.

#### **Academics and Research**
Western University is a publicly funded research university with a strong focus on life sciences, humanities, and social sciences. It is ranked among the top universities in Canada and globally, with a research income of over $249 million in 2017. The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, including the Ivey School of Business and the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry. Notable alumni include John Robarts, the 17th Premier of Ontario, and Margaret Chan, the 7th Director-General of the World Health Organization.

#### **Student Life and Governance**
Student life is managed by the University Students’ Council and the Society of Graduate Students. The university is known for its party culture and sports teams, including the Western Mustangs. The university’s governance includes the Board of Governors, the Senate, and the President and Vice-Chancellor. Notable traditions include the Western Mustangs Fight Song and the annual Western Mustangs Band performances.

### **University of Auckland**

#### **History**
The University of Auckland began as Auckland University College in 1883, becoming the first university in New Zealand. It expanded significantly in the mid-20th century and was granted its royal charter in 1961. The university has been a leader in research and innovation, particularly in the fields of medicine, engineering, and the arts. Notable alumni include Helen Clark, the former Prime Minister of New Zealand, and Sir Peter Blake, a renowned sailor.

#### **Campus and Facilities**
The university’s main campus is in Auckland, New Zealand, with additional campuses in Whangārei and Newmarket. Key facilities include the City Campus, Grafton Campus, and Newmarket Campus. The university also operates the Leigh Marine Laboratory, the South Auckland Campus, and the Goldie Estate Wine Science Centre. Notable museums include the Art Collection, which features works by significant artists such as Colin McCahon and Frances Hodgkins.

#### **Academics and Research**
The University of Auckland is renowned for its research and academic programs, particularly in the fields of medicine, science, and business. It is ranked among the top universities in New Zealand and globally, with a strong focus on interdisciplinary research. The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, including the Auckland Law School and the Auckland Business School. Notable alumni include Sir Peter Blake, the first New Zealander to win the America’s Cup, and Helen Clark, the former Prime Minister of New Zealand.

#### **Student Life and Governance**
Student life is managed by the Auckland University Students’ Association (AUSA). The university is known for its vibrant student culture, including the annual Welcome Week and the University Challenge quiz program. The university’s governance includes the Chancellor, Vice-Chancellor, and a diverse faculty and student representation. Notable traditions include the University of Auckland’s fight song and the annual Welcome Week celebrations.

It was established to provide Colombia with professionals who could contribute to the country's development and become leaders in their fields.
