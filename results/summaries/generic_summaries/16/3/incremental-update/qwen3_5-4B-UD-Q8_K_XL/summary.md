### Peking University (PKU)

Established in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, PKU has evolved into a premier institution with campuses in Beijing, Shenzhen, and Hanzhong, featuring an 8-million-volume library and extensive research facilities. Under the leadership of Cai Yuanpei (1917–1927), the university championed academic freedom, survived political shifts, played a pivotal role in the 1919 May Fourth Movement, and relocated to Kunming during WWII. Academically, PKU holds top-tier global rankings in Mathematics (Global 28), Physics (Global 25), and Nanoscience (Global 4), alongside strengths in engineering, life sciences, and social sciences including Economics and Biomedical Engineering. It achieved global prominence through Nobel laureate Tu Youyou's 2015 discovery of artemisinin and, as of 2025, ranks 14th globally in QS rankings and 13th in Times Higher Education, holding the top position in Asia for employability and research performance.

The university operates 55 schools and 137 undergraduate majors, including a 2021 opening of the Changping campus and the School of Computer Science, with a 2025 faculty split into Informatics and Engineering. Its main campus, situated on former Qing imperial gardens, blends traditional Chinese landscaping with American-style buildings and houses museums containing ancient artifacts, such as Warring States period funerary objects, and is home to the 120th-anniversary library and the National School of Development—a top-five influential think tank in China. The Shenzhen Graduate School, a satellite campus founded in 2001, is expanding with new undergraduate programs and a dedicated campus.

PKU fosters international engagement through its "Global Excellence Strategy," unveiled for the 121st anniversary, which aims to leverage the "Fourth Industrial Revolution" by gathering global resources. The university serves approximately 7,000 international students and offers dual-degree programs with Waseda University, as well as joint programs with Yale, Cornell, LSE, and Stanford. Its history is marked by influential leaders like Mao Zedong, who worked there, alongside Cai Yuanpei, its second president who championed academic freedom and the modern university concept. The university is distinguished by an impressive roster of alumni and staff, including Nobel laureates Tu Youyou, Qian Sanqiang, and Deng Jiaxian; prominent figures in literature and philosophy such as Lu Xun and Hu Shih; and business leaders like Baidu's Robin Li. Recent notable figures include economist Michael Pettis and chess champions Hou Yifan and Ding Liren. Notable alumni also include prominent figures in Chinese politics and governance, such as Zhao Leji (Chairman of the NPC Standing Committee), Li Keqiang (former Premier), and Bo Xilai (former Minister of Commerce).
