### **University of Manchester**
- **History**: Traces roots to the Mechanics' Institution (1824) and Owens College (1851). Merged with UMIST in 2004, forming the current University of Manchester. Historically strong in science, humanities, and engineering; notable figures include Ernest Rutherford, Alan Turing, and Michael Polanyi.
- **Campus**: Central location in Manchester; two main parts: North (Sackville Street) and South (Oxford Road). Houses major facilities and is integrated into the city's fabric.
- **Academics**: Divided into three faculties (Biology, Medicine, Health; Science and Engineering; Humanities). Strong research profile, ranked 5th in UK for research power (2021 REF). Home to the National Graphene Institute and a leading research center in materials science.
- **Student Life**: Active student union, sports clubs, and cultural activities. Notable for student protests over rent and living conditions, leading to rent reductions.
- **Notable People**: Includes Nobel laureates, scientists (e.g., John Dalton, James Chadwick), and prominent figures in arts and politics (e.g., Anthony Burgess, Norman Foster).

### **State University of Campinas (Unicamp)**
- **History**: Founded in 1962; expanded rapidly through the 1970s–1990s. Grew to 24 institutes and schools. Faced internal and government tensions, leading to a 1983 restructuring of governance.
- **Campus**: Main campus in Campinas, designed with a central circular plaza and radial academic buildings. Includes the School of Medical Sciences' teaching hospital (Hospital das Clínicas).
- **Academics**: Offers 70 undergraduate programs and 153 postgraduate programs. Strong in engineering, technology, and health sciences. Active in research and community outreach.
- **Student Life**: Offers student housing, student-run clubs, and cultural activities. Students engage in community outreach and extension programs.
- **Notable People**: Includes Nobel laureates (e.g., Sir John Sulston), and notable figures in science and public service.

### **University of Sydney**
- **History**: Established in 1850 as the University of Sydney. One of Australia's first public, non-denominational universities. Expanded through the 20th century with the addition of new faculties and research centers.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Sydney; satellite campuses in Camden, CBD, and other locations. Offers teaching and research facilities.

- **History**: Founded in 1636 as Harvard College. Evolved into a world-renowned research university with a strong emphasis on liberal arts and research.
