### **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**

#### **History**
RMIT traces its origins to the **Working Men's College of Melbourne**, founded in the 1880s by Francis Ormond. Initially a night school for working-class students in art, science, and technology, it expanded in the early 20th century, contributing to wartime efforts through military training. In 1934, it became the **Melbourne Technical College**, and in 1960, it was renamed **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**. The university gained royal patronage in 1954 and became a public university in 1992, merging with the Phillip Institute of Technology to form **RMIT University**.

#### **Campuses**
- **Melbourne City Campus**: The largest and central campus, featuring historic and contemporary architecture, including the **Storey Hall**, **Swanston Academic Building**, and **Design Hub**. It houses the university’s administrative headquarters, faculties of Arts, Science, Education, Pharmacy, Veterinary Science, Economics, Business, Architecture, and Engineering.
- **Bundoora Campus**: Located 18 km from the City campus, specializing in aerospace engineering, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, medical sciences, and social sciences.
- **Brunswick Campus**: Focused on vocational design programs, including product design, fashion design, graphic design, printing, publishing, and textiles.
- **Flight Training Programs**: Conducted at **RAAF Williams**, a historic air force base.
- **International Campuses**:
  - **Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam**: Established in 2001, with a focus on academic and residential facilities.
  - **Hanoi, Vietnam**: Opened in 2004.
  - **Barcelona, Spain**: A coordinating center for programs with European partners.

#### **Organization and Governance**
RMIT University is governed by a **Council** comprising 21 members, including the Chancellor, Vice-Chancellor, and elected staff and student representatives. The university operates under two divisions: **Higher Education Division** and **Vocational Education and Training (VET) Division**. It comprises **17 academic schools** grouped into three colleges: **College of Business and Law**, **College of Design and Social Context**, and **STEM College (incorporating sciences, engineering, computing, and health sciences)**.

#### **Academics**
RMIT is ranked **#199 globally** (14th nationally) in the 2024 Aggregate Ranking of Top Universities. It emphasizes **applied research** and **outcome-related research**, with a focus on design, technology, health, globalization, and sustainability. Notable research centers include the **RMIT Research Repository** and **RMIT Training**, which operates **Informit**, a leading online database of research from Australia and the Asia-Pacific.

#### **Student Life**
- **RMIT Link**: The university’s campus union, offering social, cultural, educational, sporting, and recreational programs.
- **RUSU (University Student Union)**: Represents students, advocating for their rights and interests.
- **Accommodation**: Offers several student accommodation facilities, including **RMIT Village**, **Cambridge Court**, and **College Square** on the City campus, and **Walert House** on the Bundoora campus.
- **Student Demographics**: Approximately **54% male and 46% female**, with a significant international student population.

#### **Notable People**
- **Graduates**: Include Australian skier Lydia Lassila, Irish Australian footballer Jim Stynes, film director James Wan, and Vietnamese actress Mai Phương Thúy.
- **Alumni**: Over **280,000 graduates** in 130 countries, with notable figures in politics, science, arts, and business.

### **University of the Andes (Uniandes)**

#### **History**
Founded in **1949** in Bogotá, Colombia, as the first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university in the country. The university was established to provide skilled professionals for Colombia’s industrialization and to foster critical and ethical education. It initially modeled itself after American universities and offered interdisciplinary programs. Uniandes has expanded its academic offerings and international partnerships over the years.

#### **Campus**
Located in Bogotá’s historical center, the campus includes renovated industrial and religious buildings, with a total area of **180,551 sq m**. Key facilities include the **Central cafeteria**, **gym and sports facilities**, and **health services**. The university also operates an **Internships Center** for the Medicine Faculty at Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá.

#### **Organization and Governance**
Uniandes operates under a **Board of Trustees**, **Board of Directors**, and a **Rector**. The university is divided into **14 academic units**, including **10 faculties**, the **CIDER (Centro Interdisciplinario de Estudios sobre Desarrollo)**, the **Alberto Lleras Camargo School of Government**, **Conecta-te (Centro de Innovación en Tecnología y Educación)**, and the **Dean of Students**. The university is governed by a **statute** that defines its structure and policies.

#### **Academics**
Uniandes offers **interdisciplinary programs**, allowing students to design their own curricula. The university is known for its **critical and ethical formation**, emphasizing social and civic responsibility. It has partnerships with over **119 universities** in **34 countries** worldwide.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Demographics**: Approximately **55% male and 45% female**, with **30% of students coming from outside Bogotá**.
- **Student Union**: The **University of the Andes Students' Representative Council (SRC)** and **University of the Andes Union (USU)** represent students, advocating for their interests.
- **Student Activities**: Includes clubs and societies, student media, and various cultural and recreational programs.

#### **Notable People**
- **Notable Alumni**: César Gaviria (former President of Colombia), María Ángela Holguín (Ministry of Foreign Affairs), and Gabriela Tafur (Miss Colombia 2018).
- **Mascot**: Seneca, a goat adopted by students in the late 1940s.

### **University of Manchester**

#### **History**
The University of Manchester traces its origins to the **Mechanics' Institution** (1824) and the **Owens College** (1851), founded by John Owens. The university was granted a royal charter in **2004** after merging with the **Victoria University of Manchester** and the **University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology (UMIST)**. It is known for its contributions to science, engineering, and the humanities.

#### **Campuses**
- **South Kensington Campus**: The main campus, featuring historic buildings like the **John Owens Building**, **Royal School of Mines**, and **Royal College of Science**. It is home to the **Imperial Institute** and the **Science Museum**.
- **White City Campus**: A new campus for innovation and entrepreneurship, including research facilities and postgraduate accommodation.
- **Silwood Park**: A postgraduate campus focused on ecology, evolution, and conservation.
- **Hospitals**: Affiliated teaching hospitals, including **Charing Cross Hospital**, **Hammersmith Hospital**, and **St Mary’s Hospital**.

#### **Organization and Governance**
The university is divided into **three faculties**: **Biology, Medicine and Health**, **Science and Engineering**, and **Humanities**. It operates under a **Board of Governors** and a **Senate**, with the **President and Vice-Chancellor** as the chief executive officer. The university is a member of the **Russell Group** and the **European University Association**.

#### **Academics**
Manchester is ranked **#35 globally** in the **QS World University Rankings (2025-26)** and **#53 in the Times Higher Education World University Rankings (2025-26)**. It is renowned for its **research in science, engineering, and medicine**, with contributions from **25 Nobel laureates** among its faculty and students. The university is also known for its **entrepreneurial culture** and **innovative research**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: Approximately **50% international students**, with a diverse demographic.
- **Student Union**: The **University of Manchester Students' Union** represents students and offers various clubs, societies, and facilities.
- **Sports**: Over **80 sports clubs**, including rugby, cricket, and water polo. The university competes in the **BUCS (British University & College Sports)** competitions.
- **Student Media**: Includes the **University Challenge** quiz program and student newspapers.

#### **Notable People**
- **Nobel Laureates**: Includes **Ernest Rutherford**, **James Chadwick**, and **Sir John Sulston**.
- **Notable Alumni**: Anthony Burgess, Roger Allam, Benedict Cumberbatch, and Sir Norman Foster.

### **Lund University**

#### **History**
Founded in **1666** in Lund, Sweden, as a means of making Scania Swedish. The university was initially named **Academia Carolina** after Charles X Gustav of Sweden. It is one of Scandinavia’s oldest and largest research universities, known for its contributions to science, medicine, and humanities.

#### **Campuses**
- **Lund Campus**: The main campus, located in the city of Lund, featuring historic buildings like the **Main Building** and the **Academic Society (AF)**. It includes the **Biological Museum**, **Library**, and **Hospital**.
- **Malmö Campus**: Home to the **Faculty of Fine & Performing Arts**, including the **Malmö Art Academy**, **Malmö Academy of Music**, and **Malmö Theatre Academy**.
- **Helsingborg Campus**: A satellite campus in Helsingborg, focusing on **Service Management and Communication**.
- **Teaching and Training at the School of Aviation (LUSA)**: Located in Ljungbyhed, approximately 40 km from Lund.

#### **Organization and Governance**
Lund University is divided into **nine faculties** and **over 20 research centers**. The university is governed by a **University Board** and a **Vice-Chancellor**, with the **Academic Society (AF)** playing a central role in student life.

#### **Academics**
Lund University is ranked **#75 globally** in the **QS World University Rankings (2025)** and is known for its **research in neurology, nanotechnology, climate change, and stem cell biology**. It is also recognized for innovations such as **diagnostic ultrasound, the artificial kidney, and Bluetooth technology**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Nations**: Traditional residential colleges for students, offering housing and social activities.
- **Academic Society (AF)**: A central organization for student life, including theater companies, radio, and the **Lundakarnevalen** (Lund Carnival).
- **Student Unions**: Nine student unions, each representing students from different faculties.
- **Student Demographics**: Approximately **24% Asian, 29% European, 8% Pasifika, and 7% Māori**.

#### **Notable People**
- **Nobel Laureates**: Includes **Carl Linnaeus**, **Sune Bergström**, and **Arvid Carlsson**.
- **Notable Alumni**: Samuel Pufendorf, Knut Wicksell, and Hans Alfredsson.

### **State University of Campinas (Unicamp)**

#### **History**
Founded in **1962** in Campinas, Brazil, as a response to the need for a research center to promote development and industrialization in the region. Initially, only the **School of Medicine** functioned, but the university expanded rapidly in the following decades. It faced a crisis in the 1980s due to conflicts between the academic community and the government, but later stabilized and became a leading research and education center in Latin America.

#### **Campuses**
- **Main Campus**: Located in Barão Geraldo, Campinas, covering **350 hectares**. It includes the **School of Medical Sciences**, **Hospital das Clínicas**, and the **National Synchrotron Light Laboratory (LNLS)**.
- **Limeira Campus**: Focused on **technology programs**, including **Sanitary Technology** and **Construction Technology**.
- **Piracicaba Campus**: Home to the **School of Odontology**.
- **Paulínia Campus**: A multidisciplinary research center focused on **biosciences**.
- **COTUCA and COTIL**: Technical high schools offering specialized education.

#### **Organization and Governance**
Unicamp is structured into **24 units**, including **10 institutes** and **14 schools**. The university operates under a **University Council (CONSU)**, a **Rector**, and a **rectorship** responsible for administration and academic policies.

#### **Academics**
Unicamp offers **70 undergraduate programs** and **153 postgraduate programs**, covering a wide range of fields. It is known for its **strong focus on research and innovation**, particularly in **biological sciences, engineering, and medicine**. The university is also recognized for its **extension programs** and **community outreach activities**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Housing**: Limited on-campus housing, with most students living in **repúblicas** (private fraternity-style housing) or studio apartments.
- **University Restaurants**: Known as **bandejões**, offering affordable meals to students.
- **Athletics**: No university-sponsored athletic programs, but students organize internal competitions and external events.

#### **Notable People**
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes **Eldorado Santos**, a Brazilian aviator, and **Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva**, former President of Brazil.

### **Imperial College London**

#### **History**
Founded in **1845** as the **Royal College of Chemistry**, Imperial College London merged with the **Royal School of Mines** and the **Royal College of Science** in **1907** to form the **Imperial College of Science and Technology**. It gained degree-awarding powers in **2003** and became fully independent of the University of London in **2007**. Imperial is known for its contributions to science, medicine, and engineering.

#### **Campuses**
- **South Kensington Campus**: The main campus, featuring historic buildings like the **Royal School of Mines**, **Royal College of Science**, and the **Imperial Institute**. It includes the **Natural History Museum**, **Science Museum**, and **Victoria and Albert Museum**.
- **White City Campus**: A new campus for innovation and entrepreneurship, including research facilities and postgraduate accommodation.
- **Silwood Park**: A postgraduate campus focused on **ecology, evolution, and conservation**.
- **Hospitals**: Affiliated teaching hospitals, including **Charing Cross Hospital**, **Hammersmith Hospital**, and **St Mary’s Hospital**.

#### **Organization and Governance**
Imperial College is divided into **four faculties**: **Engineering**, **Medicine**, **Natural Sciences**, and **Imperial Business School**. It operates under a **Council**, with the **President and Provost** as the chief executive officer. The university is a member of the **Russell Group** and the **European University Association**.

#### **Academics**
Imperial College London is ranked **#2 globally** in the **QS World University Rankings (2025-26)** and is known for its **research in science, engineering, and medicine**. It is renowned for its **entrepreneurial culture** and **innovative research**, including contributions to **medical technology, AI, and renewable energy**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: Approximately **50% international students**, with a diverse demographic.
- **Student Union**: The **Imperial College Union** represents students and offers various clubs, societies, and facilities.
- **Sports**: Over **350 sports clubs**, including rugby, cricket, and water polo. The university competes in the **BUCS (British University & College Sports)** competitions.
- **Student Media**: Includes the **Felix** newspaper and **Imperial College Radio**.

#### **Notable People**
- **Nobel Laureates**: Includes **Sir Alexander Fleming**, **Abdus Salam**, and **Sir John Sulston**.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes **Rajiv Gandhi**, **Teo Chee Hean**, and **Sir Brian May**.

### **University of Western Ontario**

#### **History**
Founded in **1878** as **The Western University of London, Ontario**, by Bishop Isaac Hellmuth. It initially offered programs in **Arts, Divinity, Law, and Medicine**. The university became non-denominational in **1908** and expanded significantly after World War II. It rebranded as **Western University** in **2012** to emphasize its international focus.

#### **Campuses**
- **Central Campus**: Located in London, Ontario, covering approximately **170.8 hectares**. It includes **University College**, the **Physics and Astronomy Building**, and various student residences.
- **Discovery Park**: A research park adjacent to the central campus.
- **Advanced Manufacturing Park**: A research park focused on **wind engineering and clean technology**.
- **Sarnia-Lambton Research Park**: A research park in Sarnia, focused on **clean technology and biotechnology**.

#### **Organization and Governance**
Western University is governed by a **Board of Governors** and a **Senate**. The university is divided into **11 faculties and schools**, including the **Don Wright Faculty of Music**, **Faculty of Arts & Humanities**, **Faculty of Education**, **Faculty of Engineering**, **Faculty of Health Sciences**, and the **Ivey School of Business**.

#### **Academics**
Western University is ranked **#114 globally** in the **QS World University Rankings (2024)** and is known for its **strong research in life sciences, social sciences, and engineering**. It is recognized for its **interdisciplinary research** and **career outcomes**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Demographics**: Approximately **43.9% male and 56.1% female**, with **90.1% Canadian students**.
- **Student Union**: The **University Students' Council (USC)** and the **Society of Graduate Students** represent students.
- **Athletics**: The **Western Mustangs** compete in the **Ontario University Athletics Conference**. The university offers **intramural sports** and **varsity teams**.
- **Media**: Includes the **Gazette**, a student newspaper, and **RadioWestern**, a campus radio station.

#### **Notable People**
- **Nobel Laureates**: Includes **Sir Frederick Banting**, who discovered insulin.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes **John Robarts**, **Margaret Chan**, and **Alice Munro**.
