### **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**
- **History**: Founded in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne, evolving through key phases: expansion into technical education, royal patronage in 1954, renaming to Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology in 1960, and becoming a public university in 1992 via merger with the Phillip Institute of Technology. Notable for its role in training military personnel and contributing to Australia’s war efforts.
- **Campuses**: 
  - **Melbourne City**: Historic, central campus with Victorian-era and modern buildings; includes the Old Melbourne Gaol site with preserved heritage structures.
  - **Bundoora**: Northern campus with parkland setting; offers engineering, medical sciences, and social sciences.
  - **Brunswick**: Dedicated vocational design campus for fashion, product, and media design.
  - **Point Cook**: Site for flight training at RAAF Williams base.
  - **Hamilton**: Regional research site in rural Victoria.
- **Organisation**: Divided into Higher Education and Vocational Education and Training (VET) Divisions. Governed by the RMIT Council, with the Vice-Chancellor as chief executive. Offers 17 academic schools across three colleges.
- **Academics**: Strong focus on applied research in design, technology, health, and sustainability. Ranked globally in QS, THE, and ARWU rankings. Graduates show high employer satisfaction (84.7%) and strong employment outcomes (71.8% full-time employment for undergraduates).
- **Collections**: 
  - **Libraries**: Central network with Swanston Library (largest), and specialized collections at each campus.
  - **Galleries**: RMIT Gallery features contemporary art and design; student-run Artland program showcases emerging artists.
- **Student Life**: 
  - **Link (campus union)**: Offers social, cultural, and recreational programs.
  - **RUSU (student union)**: Advocates for student rights and runs clubs, media, and events.
  - **Accommodation**: Self-catered apartments and hostels; some affiliated with traditional colleges.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Lydia Lassila (Olympic skier), Jim Stynes (footballer), James Wan (director), and Mai Phương Thúy (Vietnamese actress).

### **São Paulo State University (Unesp)**
- **History**: Founded in 1933 as USP’s counterpart; expanded through regional campuses in the 1950s–1970s. Modeled after the University of California system; established as a multi-campus public university in 1976.
- **Facilities**: 1,900 labs, 30 libraries, 62.8 million m² of land; includes hospitals (Botucatu and Bauru) and research farms.
- **Campuses**: 23 campuses across São Paulo state, including major ones in Araçatuba, Araraquara, Botucatu, and Bauru.
- **Administration**: Governed by a rector appointed by the state governor; includes 10 student representatives and academic directors.
- **Academics**: Offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 11,000 postgraduate students. Strong research focus in bioenergy, climate change, and nanotechnology.
- **International Programs**: 140 exchange agreements with institutions in 52 countries; launched "International Courses" in 2013.
- **Rankings**: Ranked 2nd in Brazil and 11th in emerging countries by THE; recognized in global university rankings.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Néle Azevedo (sculptor), Ana Maria Braga (journalist), and Rafael Coutinho (comics artist).

### **Peking University**
- **History**: Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking; evolved through the early 20th century with key figures like Cai Yuanpei. Played a central role in China’s intellectual and political movements.
- **Campus**: Located on former imperial gardens; features traditional Chinese-style landscaping and historical buildings like the Weiming Lake and Boya Pagoda.
- **Academics**: 55 schools and departments; 137 majors and 59 PhD programs. Strong in science, medicine, and engineering.
- **Research**: World-leading in natural sciences and health; home to the Liggins Institute and has a strong research culture.
- **Rankings**: Ranked #1 in Asia by QS (2025), #13 globally by THE, and #2 in Asia-Pacific by THE. Notable for its research output and academic excellence.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Tu Youyou (Nobel laureate), John F. Kennedy (U.S. president), and Nobel laureate John Harsanyi.
- **International Presence**: Partnerships with Harvard, MIT, and Waseda University; has a global outreach strategy.

### **University of the Andes (Colombia)**
- **History**: Founded in 1949 as a non-sectarian, non-political institution to train professionals for national development. Expanded through the 1950s and 1960s with international programs.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Bogotá; additional campuses in Newton, Brighton, and other cities.
- **Administration**: Governed by a Board of Trustees and a rector; emphasizes pluralism, diversity, and academic freedom.
- **Academics**: Offers 14 academic units; strong in business, law, and engineering.
- **Student Life**: 
  - **AHANA**: Student-led initiative promoting cultural diversity and inclusion.
  - **Mascot**: Seneca, a goat symbol of the university.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes César Gaviria (President of Colombia), María Ángela Holguín (Minister of Foreign Affairs), and Fernando Botero (Minister of National Defense).

### **Harvard University**
- **History**: Founded in 1636 as a Puritan institution; evolved through the 19th and 20th centuries with key figures like Charles Eliot and James B. Conant. Gained global prominence through expansion and research.
- **Campuses**: 
  - **Cambridge**: Main campus with Harvard Yard; includes key buildings like Memorial Church and the Harvard Medical School.
  - **Allston**: Home to Harvard Business School and innovation labs.
  - **Longwood**: Hosts medical and public health schools.
- **Academics**: 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees. Strong in engineering, business, and social sciences.
- **Research**: World-leading in biomedical research; has over 11,000 faculty and 1,600 graduate students in medicine.
- **Rankings**: Ranked #1 globally in THE, QS, and ARWU rankings. Consistently top-ranked in research and academic performance.
- **Controversies**: Faced criticism over antisemitism and government funding issues in 2025; defended its autonomy in legal actions.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes John F. Kennedy, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and 162 Nobel laureates.

### **University of Sydney**
- **History**: Founded in 1850 as Australia’s first secular university; expanded through the 20th century with women’s admission in 1881 and the addition of new faculties.
- **Campuses**: 
  - **Main Campus**: Central hub with historic architecture and modern facilities.
  - **Camden, CBD, and other campuses**: Offer specialized programs in health, business, and the arts.
- **Academics**: 8 faculties; strong in medicine, business, and engineering.
- **Rankings**: Ranked #47 globally (QS), #8 nationally (AFR), and #2 in Australia by THE.
- **Student Life**: 
  - **Student Union (USU)**: Runs clubs, catering, and events.
  - **Charles Perkins Oration**: Honors Indigenous contributions to education.
- **Controversies**: Sexual harassment, staff underpayment, and environmental issues have been reported.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes 8 prime ministers, 24 Rhodes Scholars, and Nobel laureates in chemistry and economics.

### **Boston College**
- **History**: Founded in 1849 as a Jesuit institution; moved to Chestnut Hill in 1907. Expanded through the 20th century with the addition of new schools and programs.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Chestnut Hill; additional campuses in Newton, Brighton, and Brookline.
- **Academics**: 9 schools, including the Carroll School of Management and the Lynch School of Education.

- **Student Life**: 
  - **AHANA**: Promotes diversity and inclusion.
  - **Athletics**: Men’s and women’s teams compete in the ACC.
- **Controversies**: Student protests over racism and LGBTQ+ inclusion; issues related to staff pay and student housing.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes John F. Kerry, Paul Cellucci, and Leonard Nimoy.

### **University of Manchester**
- **History**: Traces roots to the Mechanics' Institution (1824); merged in 2004 to form the current University of Manchester.
- **Campuses**: 
  - **Main Campus (Oxford Road and Sackville Street)**: Central hub with historic and modern buildings.
  - **White City**: New campus focused on innovation and entrepreneurship.
- **Academics**: Divided into three faculties: Biology, Medicine, and Health; Science and Engineering; and Humanities.
- **Research**: World-leading in physics, chemistry, and engineering; home to the National Graphene Institute.
- **Rankings**: Ranked 35th globally (QS), 53rd globally (THE), and 28th nationally (Complete University Guide).
- **Student Life**: 
  - **Student Union**: Largest in the UK; runs clubs and events.
  - **Athletics**: Strong in water polo and rugby.
- **Controversies**: Student rent strikes and protests over living conditions and housing.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Ernest Rutherford, Alan Turing, and Michael Polanyi.

- **Academics**: 11 faculties including Arts, Science, Engineering, and Medicine.
- **Research**: Strong in cognitive neuroscience, environmental science, and health.

  - **USC and Society of Graduate Students**: Advocate for student rights.
  - **Athletics**: Western Mustangs compete in U Sports.

- **Notable Alumni**: Includes John Robarts (Premier of Ontario), Margaret Chan (WHO Director-General), and Galen Weston (business leader).
