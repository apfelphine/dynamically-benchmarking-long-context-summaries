## Summary of Educational Institutions

### Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)
RMIT originated from the Working Men's College of Melbourne, founded by Hon. Francis Ormond in the 1880s. It evolved through several stages: as the Working Men's College (1887–1960), then as the Royal Melbourne Technical College, and finally as the Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (1960–1992). In 1992, it merged with the Phillip Institute of Technology to become RMIT University. The institution has expanded internationally, opening branches in Ho Chi Minh City (2001) and Hanoi (2004), and establishing a center in Barcelona (2013). RMIT operates across various campuses in Australia, including City, Bundoora, and Brunswick, and maintains international partnerships.

### São Paulo State University (Unesp)
Founded in 1933, Unesp was created from coordinating several state colleges in São Paulo. Inspired by the University of California's multi-campus system, Unesp developed numerous campuses across the state. It is one of six public higher education institutions in São Paulo. Unesp possesses extensive facilities, including 1,900 laboratories and 30 libraries, and manages hospitals. Academically, it offers 70 undergraduate programs and 153 postgraduate programs. Admissions are restricted by a state-wide general admissions exam called *vestibular*.

### Peking University
Established in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, the university underwent several name changes, becoming the National University of Peking in 1919. Under the leadership of Cai Yuanpei, it embraced academic freedom during the early Republican period. The university has achieved global recognition, highlighted by alumnus Tu Youyou winning the 2015 Nobel Prize. It operates a main campus featuring traditional Chinese-style landscaping, alongside satellite campuses like the Shenzhen Graduate School. The university is distinguished by its high international cooperation, including dual-degree programs with Waseda University.

### University of the Andes (Colombia)
Founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, Uniandes was established as Colombia's first nonsectarian and non-politically affiliated university. It aimed to provide technical and liberal education to foster national development, modeling itself on American universities. Uniandes maintains political neutrality despite periods of national violence. The university is governed by a Board of Trustees, and its mission emphasizes fostering pluralism and civic responsibility among its students.

### Harvard University
Founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony, Harvard initially served a Puritan religious mission. Over centuries, it has evolved, notably through the secularization efforts led by Charles William Eliot. It is renowned globally for its academic preeminence, holding the world's largest university endowment ($\$50.7$ billion as of 2023). It comprises various schools, including Medicine, Law, and Business, and features major campuses in Cambridge and Allston. The university has recently navigated significant political controversies regarding federal funding.

### University of Sydney
Established in 1850, the University of Sydney was Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university. It has a rich history of admitting women equally from 1881. The main campus is located in Camperdown and Darlington and is architecturally significant. The university is highly ranked internationally and noted for its strong graduate outcomes. It has faced various controversies concerning sexual harassment and staff underpayment.

### Boston College
Founded in 1849 by the first Jesuit community, Boston College was established to serve the Irish Catholic immigrant population. It relocated its main campus to Chestnut Hill in 1907. The university expanded its offerings through various schools and colleges. Financial stability was achieved through aggressive capital campaigns, growing its endowment significantly. BC maintains a strong religious and Jesuit identity while actively engaging in social issues and student advocacy.

### University of Auckland
Originating as a college of the University of New Zealand in 1883, the University of Auckland was empowered by its own Act in 1961. The university has focused on growth and consolidation across multiple campuses, including Grafton and Newmarket. It is known for its commitment to innovation and entrepreneurship, notably through the Unleash Space. The university has navigated controversies regarding campus restructuring and fossil fuel divestment.

### University of Jordan
Founded in 1962 by a royal decree, the University of Jordan was established to address the national aspiration for higher education in Jordan. It features main campuses in Amman and Aqaba. The university's structure includes scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties. It emphasizes international orientation, evidenced by its numerous bilateral agreements. The university has a significant output of research and has produced numerous alumni in high political and scientific roles.

### University of Manchester
Its roots trace back to the Mechanics' Institution in 1824, linked to Manchester’s industrial heritage. The modern University of Manchester was formed in 2003 through the merger of the Victoria University of Manchester and the University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology. It is a major research hub, historically linked to numerous Nobel laureates, and maintains strong ties to industry. It has recently faced student protests regarding rent increases.

### Lund University
Lund University has medieval origins, with its studium generale founded in 1425. The modern university was established in 1666 after the Treaty of Roskilde. It is a leading Scandinavian research university, noted for its historical contributions to science and technology (e.g., Bluetooth, artificial kidney). Student life is structured around student nations and the Academic Society (AF). It ranks highly in global sustainability rankings.

### State University of Campinas (Unicamp)
Founded in 1962, Unicamp was established to promote development in the interior of São Paulo state. Its main campus in Barão Geraldo features a distinct radial design. The university has experienced periods of rapid growth and political conflict between the academic community and the state government. It is recognized for its strong research focus and its extensive community outreach programs.

### Imperial College London
Imperial College was established in 1907, unifying several institutions including the Royal College of Science and the Royal School of Mines. It is a globally elite, research-intensive institution, known for blending science, technology, and medicine. It hosts major research centers like the Francis Crick Institute. Imperial has a highly international student body and is noted for its entrepreneurial culture.

### University of Western Ontario (Western)
Founded in 1878, Western University is a publicly funded research university in London, Ontario, Canada. It has developed a campus centered around Collegiate Gothic architecture. Western emphasizes research across four fields, with a strong focus on the brain and human environment. It actively supports student life through its student unions and sports programs, while navigating issues of sexual violence and student accommodation.
