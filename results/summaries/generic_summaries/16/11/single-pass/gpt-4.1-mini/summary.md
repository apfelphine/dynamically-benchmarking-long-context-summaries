# Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)

Founded in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne through the philanthropy of Francis Ormond, RMIT has evolved from a night school for working men into a major public university. It received royal patronage in 1954, becoming the only Australian university permitted to use "Royal" in its title. After merging with various institutions and expanding internationally, RMIT became a public university in 1992 and now operates multiple campuses across Australia (Melbourne City, Bundoora, Brunswick, and others) and abroad in Vietnam and Spain. It offers a broad range of vocational and higher education programs organized into colleges covering business, design, STEM, and vocational education.

RMIT emphasizes applied research in design, technology, and sustainability and is complemented by extensive library networks and galleries, including the RMIT Gallery and specialized collections. Its student life features active unions, a wide array of cultural and sporting activities, diverse accommodation options, and a multi-faith Spiritual Centre. Graduates are noted for strong employability, with the university ranked within the top 200 globally across several major rankings.

# São Paulo State University (UNESP)

Formed in 1976 through the federation of isolated colleges established by the São Paulo state government, UNESP was inspired by the University of California system. It operates 23 campuses and 33 institutes across the state of São Paulo, covering a diverse array of faculties including sciences, arts, engineering, and health. UNESP manages large facilities including laboratories, libraries, museums, agricultural farms, and hospitals.

Governed by a rector appointed by the state governor following internal academic elections, UNESP provides 171 undergraduate degrees and 118 postgraduate programs. Admissions rely on a state-wide exam system. The university has an extensive number of international exchange agreements and is ranked among the top emerging universities globally. Notable alumni have excelled in arts, academia, politics, and science.

# Peking University

Established in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, Peking University (PKU) has played a key role in China’s modern educational and political history, including its association with the May Fourth Movement. The university holds a main campus in former Qing dynasty imperial gardens, with notable architecture and cultural institutions. It has branch campuses in Shenzhen and satellite facilities in other regions.

PKU comprises 55 schools and departments offering undergraduate, master's, and doctoral degrees, with over 400 research institutions including state key labs. It ranks among the top universities globally and in Asia, especially known for its research output and comprehensive subject strength across sciences, humanities, medicine, and engineering. PKU maintains extensive international partnerships and dual degree programs.

# University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in 1948 during Colombia's Liberal Republic era to address the nation’s need for technical professionals, Universidad de los Andes was modeled after American universities emphasizing liberal education and technical excellence. The university began with 80 students and expanded under a politically neutral stance amid regional unrest. It pioneered American-style curricula and later developed international exchange programs.

Governed by a Board of Trustees and an administration led by a rector, the university comprises 14 academic units including faculties and interdisciplinary centers. It emphasizes interdisciplinary education with flexible curriculum design. The main campus is a mix of historic and modern buildings in Bogotá’s central area, with extensive student facilities and a symbolic goat mascot named Seneca. The university is renowned for high academic standards and producing notable alumni in politics, arts, and sciences.

# Harvard University

Founded in 1636 in Massachusetts, Harvard is the oldest U.S. institution of higher learning. Originally aligned with Puritanism, it evolved through the centuries into a secular, research-intensive university. Harvard has multiple campuses including the historic Cambridge Yard, Allston athletics and business campus, and the Longwood medical campus. It boasts a global reputation, extensive libraries, museums, and prominent alumni across politics, sciences, arts, and business.

Governed by the Harvard Corporation and Board of Overseers, Harvard has the world’s largest university endowment (~$50.7 billion). It offers comprehensive undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs through multiple faculties and schools. Harvard consistently ranks among the top universities worldwide, excelling in research, employability, and academic reputation. It maintains a vibrant student life with numerous media outlets, extensive athletics, and longstanding traditions.

# University of Sydney

Established via the University of Sydney Act 1850, it is Australia’s first university and one of the earliest secular universities in the British Empire. Renowned for its Victorian-era and neo-Gothic architecture, the main campus lies in inner-city Camperdown/Darlington with several satellite campuses. It was among the first universities to admit women equally and has a rich history of social activism.

The university is organized into eight faculties offering a broad range of disciplines, supported by extensive libraries, museums (including the Chau Chak Wing Museum), and research centers. It is highly ranked nationally and internationally for academic and graduate outcomes. Student life features active unions, cultural events like the Charles Perkins Oration, numerous residential colleges, and challenges such as efforts to improve sexual harassment reporting and satisfaction ratings.

# Boston College

Founded in 1863 primarily to serve Boston’s Irish Catholic immigrant community, Boston College (BC) is a Jesuit, Catholic research university. It relocated in 1909 from the South End to the spacious Chestnut Hill campus, featuring Gothic architecture. BC expanded to include multiple graduate and professional schools and embraced coeducation by 1970.

With a strong Jesuit identity, BC operates several campuses and has an endowment supporting a $3 billion fundraising campaign. It is broadly organized into nine schools including Arts & Sciences, Management, Law, and Nursing. Boston College is ranked among top national universities with a distinctive liberal arts tradition. Student life includes the AHANA diversity group, multiple student media outlets, and a comprehensive athletics program competing in the NCAA Division I Atlantic Coast Conference. The institution has a history of protests around tuition, race relations, and LGBTQ+ rights.

# University of Auckland

Originating in 1883 as Auckland University College under the University of New Zealand, the University of Auckland became autonomous in 1961 and adopted its Māori name Waipapa Taumata Rau in 2021. It has developed a strong research culture since the 1930s and currently operates multiple campuses including the central City campus, Grafton (medical), and Newmarket campuses.

The university is governed by a chancellor and vice-chancellor, organized into faculties spanning arts, business, engineering, law, medicine, and science. It is highly ranked nationally and internationally, lauded for its innovation and entrepreneurship initiatives. Its student body is diverse, and student life features active associations, multiple libraries, extensive accommodation, and a new recreation center named Hiwa.

# University of Jordan

Founded in 1962 as Jordan’s first and leading university, the University of Jordan’s main campus is situated in Amman, with a branch campus in Aqaba established in 2009. The university expanded steadily through the late 20th century despite regional political turmoil, adding faculties in sciences, humanities, medicine, engineering, and more.

It offers a broad spectrum of programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties with internationally recognized accreditations and research output. The university has strong library resources, numerous research centers, and a teaching hospital. Student activism and academic autonomy have increased since the 1990s. The university maintains extensive regional and international partnerships and has produced many notable alumni in government, science, arts, and media.

# University of Manchester

Formally established in 2004 by merging Victoria University of Manchester and UMIST, its origins date back to 1824 and Owens College (1851). The university has a strong Germanic heritage and a long tradition in science and engineering, including Nobel laureates and landmark discoveries such as the nuclear atom and electronic computer.

Its campus is urban and integrated into Manchester city, consisting mainly of the North (Sackville Street) and South (Oxford Road) campuses, with extensive historic and modern buildings and cultural institutions like the John Rylands Library and Manchester Museum. It is divided into faculties in biology, medicine and health; science and engineering; and humanities, offering rich research output and attracting international students worldwide. The university boasts active student unions, extensive athletics, and a strong higher education reputation.

# Lund University

Dating back to a medieval studium generale (1425), Lund University was formally founded in 1666 in southern Sweden. Starting with four faculties, it developed through centuries into Sweden’s top research university, known for its breadth of disciplines, research excellence, and rich student life traditions including nation-based societies.

Its campuses are mainly in Lund and Malmö, covering a large urban area with historic buildings as well as modern facilities. Lund is a research leader in EU science funding and has notable innovations such as diagnostic ultrasound and Bluetooth. The university houses multiple museums, extensive libraries, and notable alumni and faculty spanning sciences, humanities, politics, and business.

# State University of Campinas (Unicamp)

Founded in the 1960s by the São Paulo state government to promote regional development, Unicamp has rapidly grown to become a leading research university in Latin America. Its flagship campus in Barão Geraldo near Campinas is uniquely designed with radial academic blocks, large hospital facilities, and multidisciplinary research centers.

Unicamp comprises 24 schools and institutes offering 70 undergraduate and numerous graduate programs, with competitive national entrance exams supplemented by affirmative action. It emphasizes extension and outreach programs and employs a highly qualified faculty. Funding is mainly governmental but faces economic challenges. Student life includes limited on-campus housing and vibrant off-campus fraternity-style living. The university is known culturally for its association with area 51 folklore and maintains renowned libraries and research facilities.

# Imperial College London

Established in 1907 through the amalgamation of several Royal Colleges of Science and Mines, Imperial College London is a science-focused, research-intensive university in central London with campuses in South Kensington, White City, and Silwood Park. Initially part of the University of London, it gained independence in 2007.

Organized into faculties of Engineering, Medicine, Natural Sciences, and Business, Imperial is globally ranked among the top universities, noted for entrepreneurship, research output, and innovation. Its facilities include affiliated teaching hospitals and cutting-edge laboratories. Governance is through a council and executive leadership. Student life is vibrant with numerous clubs, unions, competitive sports, and unique traditions including rivalry mascots and drinking societies. Alumni include Nobel laureates and leading figures in science, politics, and business.

# University of Western Ontario (Western University)

Founded in 1878 in London, Ontario, initially affiliated with the Church of England, Western University is a comprehensive public research university that has evolved into one of Canada’s top universities. Its main campus features Collegiate Gothic and modernist architecture, extensive libraries, research parks, and residential facilities.

The university is organized into 11 faculties including Arts, Engineering, Health Sciences, Business, and Medicine, with large undergraduate and graduate populations. It is governed by a Board of Governors and Senate, with a substantial endowment and research funding. Western emphasizes research in life sciences, social sciences, and cognitive neuroscience. Student life includes active unions, media, clubs, and varsity sports notably the Western Mustangs. The university has faced issues such as sexual assault controversies but remains prominent nationally and internationally, producing numerous notable alumni in politics, science, and the arts.
