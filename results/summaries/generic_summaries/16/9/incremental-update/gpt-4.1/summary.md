### Summary

**Harvard University: History, Academics, and Impact**  
Founded in 1636, Harvard evolved from Puritan origins into a leading global university, encompassing 12 schools, an extensive library system, groundbreaking research, vibrant student organizations, and alumni including world leaders and Nobel laureates.

---

**Peking University: History, Academics, and Global Influence**  
Established in 1898, Peking University has shaped Chinese intellectual and political life, boasts Asia’s largest university library, excels in research and international collaboration, and has influential alumni.

---

**Boston College: Jesuit Identity, Academics, and Student Life**  
Founded in 1863 by Jesuits, Boston College expanded via campus growth and mergers, offers nine distinguished schools, renowned libraries and museums, dynamic student engagement, and accomplished alumni.

---

**Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT): Structure, Student Life, and Global Impact**  
Founded in 1887, RMIT operates internationally via four colleges and a commercial division, ranking highly in employability and research in design, technology, and health, with an active student and alumni community.

---

**University of Jordan: Growth, Academic Excellence, and Influence**  
Jordan’s first university, UJ (established 1962), offers over 200 programs with strengths in medicine, engineering, and humanities, and fosters international cooperation.

---

**São Paulo State University (UNESP): Structure, Research, and Influence**  
Founded in 1976, UNESP is a prominent Brazilian research institution with 23 campuses, competitive admissions, and expertise spanning agriculture, energy, dentistry, and humanities.

---

**State University of Campinas (Unicamp): Structure, Academics, Admissions, Student Life, and Research**  
Unicamp is a top Latin American research university offering diverse tuition-free graduate programs, embracing affirmative action and a strong focus on medicine and engineering, autonomous governance, and vibrant campus life.

---

**Lund University: Innovation, Excellence, and Global Partnerships**  
Founded in 1666, Lund excels in medical and technological innovation, sustainability, and boasts a vibrant student community and Nobel laureate alumni.

---

**University of the Andes (Colombia): Interdisciplinary Academics and Social Impact**  
Founded in 1948, Uniandes pioneered interdisciplinary academics, supports a dynamic student body, and has produced leaders in politics and science.

---

**Imperial College London: Structure, Reputation, Student Life, Research, and Alumni**  
Founded in 1907, Imperial is a top UK research university with rigorous governance, four faculties, global reputation, diverse student body, and Nobel laureate alumni.

---

**University of Sydney: History, Structure, Student Life, Reputation, and Alumni**  
Founded in 1850, Sydney champions merit-based access and global partnerships, dynamic student life, and a strong alumni body, while addressing controversies and environmental challenges.

---

**University of Western Ontario (Western University): History, Structure, Student Life, Research, Reputation, Alumni, and Issues**  
Founded in 1878, Western excels in medical and scientific research, arts, athletics, and advocacy, with notable alumni and initiatives to address social challenges.

---

**University of Auckland: Development, Academics, Student Life, Innovation, Alumni, and Controversies**  
Founded in 1883, Auckland leads New Zealand in academics and research, fosters entrepreneurship, maintains specialized archives, a diverse student body, and has faced staffing and curricular controversies.

---

**University of Manchester: Organisation, Excellence, Student Life, Research, Collections, Reputation, and Notable Affiliations**  
Established in its current form in 2004, Manchester comprises three faculties, robust research centers, historic campuses, and significant collections, including the UK’s largest non-legal deposit library, Manchester Museum, and Whitworth Art Gallery. The university is among the UK’s largest and most research-intensive (2023/24 income: £1.365 billion), ranked 35th globally (QS), and renowned for achievements in science and the arts, with 26 Nobel laureates and associations with luminaries like John Dalton, Ernest Rutherford, Alan Turing, and Brian Cox. Manchester attracts high application numbers (92,500 in 2024), a diverse student body (40% international), and has strong widening participation efforts. It features the UK’s largest students’ union, extensive extracurricular offerings, residential campuses, and competitive performances in University Challenge and sports. The university is affiliated with world leaders—including presidents and prime ministers across multiple countries—key UK government officials, and influential figures such as Chaim Weizmann, Israel’s first president, and Irene Khan of Amnesty International. Its arts alumni include acclaimed authors, Oscar-winning writers, actors like Benedict Cumberbatch, and pioneers of alternative comedy. Manchester’s employer reputation, industry partnerships, and social initiatives reinforce its global prominence.
