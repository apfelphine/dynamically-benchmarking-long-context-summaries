### **Harvard University**
- **History**: Founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony, Harvard began as a Puritan institution focused on training ministers. Key milestones include the acquisition of its first printing press (1638), the naming after John Harvard (1639), and the adoption of the "In Christi Gloriam" seal. The 19th century saw a shift toward Enlightenment ideals and secularism under leaders like Charles Eliot. The 20th century witnessed growing diversity and the integration of Radcliffe College (1971), culminating in its full merger in 1999.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Cambridge, MA, centered on Harvard Yard, with expansions in Allston (engineering, athletics) and Longwood (medical schools). Other sites include Dumbarton Oaks, Harvard Forest, and international centers.
- **Organization & Administration**: Governed by the Board of Overseers and Harvard Corporation. The largest university endowment in the world (~$50.7B), it funds operations through investment income (~$2B annually). Harvard has a unique lack of public access to governance documents and an academic senate.
- **Academics**: Offers 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees. Known for research excellence, especially in medicine and engineering. Libraries (Harvard Library, 20.4M items) and museums (Harvard Art Museums) are world-renowned.
- **Reputation & Rankings**: Consistently ranked #1 globally in world university rankings (QS, THE, ARWU). Recognized for producing influential alumni (e.g., 162 Nobel laureates, 188 living billionaires).
- **Student Life**: Undergraduate Council replaced by the Undergraduate Association. Harvard Crimson is the primary student newspaper. The university faces ongoing controversies, including student protests and government investigations into antisemitism and academic freedom in 2025.

### **Peking University**
- **History**: Established in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking. Under Cai Yuanpei (1917), it became a center for progressive thought and student activism. Key developments include the 1919 May Fourth Movement, the establishment of the National Southwestern Associated University during WWII, and the 21st-century expansion into international programs.
- **Campus**: Located on a former imperial garden in Beijing, with traditional Chinese-style landscaping. Additional campuses in Shenzhen and Aqaba.
- **Academics**: Offers 137 majors and 143 postgraduate programs across 55 schools. Known for research in natural sciences, engineering, and social sciences. The library (over 8M items) and research centers are significant.
- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked #1 in Asia (QS, 2025) and #14 globally in the 2025 QS World University Rankings. Recognized for high employability and research output.
- **Student Life**: Offers student media, clubs, and a strong international student presence. Student activism has historically been a key feature of campus life.

### **Boston College**
- **History**: Founded in 1849 by Jesuits to serve Irish Catholic immigrants. Expanded through the 20th century, with coeducation introduced in 1926. Key developments include the 1970 protests and the 1990s student activism.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Chestnut Hill, MA, with additional sites in Newton and Brighton. Features include the Gasson Hall and the Boston College Link campus union.
- **Organization & Administration**: Governed by a rector and a university council. The student body is 52% male and 48% female, with a strong Catholic and Jesuit identity.
- **Academics**: Offers 9 schools, including the Carroll School of Management and the Lynch School of Education. Known for business and education programs.
- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked 39th in the U.S. by Forbes and 59th by U.S. News. Known for producing influential alumni in politics, business, and the arts.
- **Student Life**: Features student media (The Heights, The Gavel), student organizations, and traditions like the "Red Bandanas" honoring 9/11 hero Welles Crowther.

### **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**
- **History**: Founded in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne. Evolved into a public university in 1992 through the merger with the Phillip Institute of Technology. Known for vocational education and applied research.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Melbourne, with additional sites in Bundoora, Brunswick, and international branches in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi.
- **Organization & Administration**: Governed by a council with 21 members, including student and staff representatives. Divided into higher education and vocational education divisions.
- **Academics**: Offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 10,000 postgraduate students. Known for applied research in design, technology, and health.
- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked 199th globally in the 2024 Aggregate Ranking of Top Universities. Recognized for strong graduate outcomes and high employability.
- **Student Life**: Features student media (The Swanston Gazette, Catalyst), student unions, and a vibrant arts and culture scene.

### **São Paulo State University (Unesp)**
- **History**: Founded in 1976 as a multi-campus public university inspired by the University of California model. Expanded through the 1980s and 1990s with new campuses and programs.
- **Campuses**: 23 campuses across São Paulo state, including Araçatuba, Araraquara, and Bauru. Offers over 45,000 students annually.
- **Organization & Administration**: Governed by a rector and a university council. Admissions are through a state-wide exam (vestibular).
- **Academics**: Offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 10,000 postgraduate students. Known for strong research in bioenergy, biotechnology, and climate change.
- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked 2nd among public universities in Australia and 151–200 in the Young University Rankings (2018).
- **Student Life**: Features student exchange programs and a strong emphasis on research and innovation.

- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked 75th globally in 2025 QS rankings. Known for strong research output and innovation.
- **Student Life**: Features student nations, the Academic Society (AF), and student unions. Known for a vibrant cultural and social life.

- **Reputation & Rankings**: Ranked 35th globally in 2025 QS rankings. Known for strong research output and innovation.
