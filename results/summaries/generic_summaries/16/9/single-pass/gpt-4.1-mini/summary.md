# Harvard University
Founded in 1636 in Massachusetts Bay Colony, Harvard began as a Puritan training college with a classical curriculum. It gradually secularized over the 19th century, expanding its academic offerings and admitting women to graduate programs. Today, it has a large endowment ($50.7 billion in 2023), diverse campuses in Cambridge, Allston, and Longwood, and is governed by the Harvard Corporation and Board of Overseers. Harvard offers extensive undergraduate and graduate programs across 12 schools, with strengths in research, especially biomedical sciences. Its library system is the largest academic library globally. Harvard maintains a strong athletic tradition, particularly in Ivy League sports, and has produced numerous notable alumni, including U.S. presidents and Nobel laureates. Recent controversies include clashes with the U.S. government over alleged antisemitism and funding freezes.

# Peking University
Established in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, PKU evolved into China’s leading progressive university. It fostered freedoms in thought and played a central role in the May Fourth Movement. Today, PKU operates a main historic campus in Beijing with traditional Chinese architecture and multiple research facilities. It comprises 55 schools and departments offering a broad array of programs with significant research output, particularly in science and technology. It ranks among the top universities in Asia and globally, with strong international collaborations. PKU has nurtured numerous influential alumni in politics, science, and the arts, including Nobel laureate Tu Youyou.

# Boston College
Founded by Jesuits in 1863 in Boston, Boston College began as an institution for Irish Catholic immigrants and expanded to become a major research university with multiple schools, becoming fully coeducational by 1970. Its main Gothic-style campus sits on Chestnut Hill, with additional Newton, Brighton, and Brookline campuses. Boston College is organized into nine schools including Arts & Sciences and Law, with an annual operating budget over $1 billion and a significant fundraising campaign underway. The university fields Division I varsity teams known as the Eagles and has a robust student media scene. It has a diverse student body and maintains active programs addressing social issues such as racism and LGBTQ inclusion.

# Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)
Founded in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne, RMIT developed into a dual-sector public university with strong vocational and higher education offerings. Campuses are primarily in Melbourne (City, Bundoora, Brunswick) and international sites in Vietnam and Spain. It is governed by a council and led by a vice-chancellor. Academically, RMIT focuses on applied research in design, technology, health, and sustainability, with a strong reputation regionally and an emphasis on graduate employability. The university supports vibrant student life through its union, multiple media outlets, and accommodation facilities, and boasts notable alumni in arts, sports, and business.

# University of Jordan
Founded in 1962 as Jordan’s first national university, its main campus is in Amman with a branch in Aqaba established in 2009. The university expanded its faculties rapidly from humanities to medicine, engineering, and business, achieving academic autonomy and significant research output. It hosts over 78 undergraduate and more than 140 postgraduate programs. The Jordan University Hospital serves as a major teaching and research hospital. It publishes multiple scholarly journals and holds national prominence in higher education. Notable alumni include Jordanian politicians, academics, scientists, and regional leaders.

# São Paulo State University (Unesp)
Established in 1976 by consolidating numerous independent colleges across São Paulo state, Unesp is a large public university with 23 campuses and multiple institutes spanning sciences, arts, and health. Its main campus is in a park-like setting, and it manages hospitals and clinical centers. Governed by a rector chosen by the state governor from elected university candidates, it offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 100 postgraduate degrees. Admissions are highly competitive via a state-wide exam. Unesp is recognized for applied research and has a growing international profile, with notable alumni in arts, sciences, and humanities.

# Lund University
Founded in 1666 in Sweden’s Scania region, Lund University has medieval origins as a center for religious education. It grew through the 18th and 19th centuries and expanded greatly in the 20th century, becoming a major comprehensive university with facilities across Lund, Malmö, and Helsingborg. It houses nine faculties covering humanities, sciences, law, medicine, engineering, and social sciences. Lund is internationally renowned for research excellence and innovation, including numerous Nobel laureates among alumni and faculty. Student life is vibrant, centered on historic student nations and societies.

# University of the Andes (Colombia)
Founded in 1948 in Bogotá amid political and social upheaval, Universidad de los Andes was conceived as a nonsectarian, nonpolitical institution focused on technical and liberal education for Colombia’s development. Modeled on the American university system, it started with 80 students and expanded into multiple faculties and research centers. Governance includes a Board of Trustees and Academic Council. It offers 14 academic units, including faculties and interdisciplinary centers, and ranks among Colombia’s top universities. Alumni include national political leaders, scientists, and business figures.

# Imperial College London
Established by royal charter in 1907 from earlier scientific and technical colleges, Imperial College London is a world-leading public research university specializing in science, engineering, medicine, and business. The main campus is in South Kensington with satellite campuses, including a biomedical site at White City and a rural ecology site at Silwood Park. Imperial is governed by a council and led by a president and vice-chancellor. It hosts extensive interdisciplinary research centers and has global collaborations, notably with MIT. Imperial ranks among the top universities worldwide and is known for entrepreneurial culture and STEM excellence.

# University of Sydney
Founded in 1850 as Australia’s first university and one of the first secular universities in the British Empire, it is renowned for early coeducation and extensive academic offerings across eight faculties and professional schools. The main campus features historically significant neo-Gothic architecture and hosts numerous museums and libraries, including the major Chau Chak Wing Museum. The university ranks among top Australian institutions with strong research output. It supports active student organizations and has produced multiple prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and leading figures across disciplines. Challenges include sexual harassment concerns and staff underpayment issues.

# University of Western Ontario (Western University)
Founded in 1878 in London, Ontario, Western University is a large Canadian public research institution with a main campus featuring Collegiate Gothic architecture and modern facilities. It has extensive library and museum collections, multiple student residences, and research parks. Academically divided into 11 faculties, it offers wide undergraduate and graduate programs. Western is known for strong research in brain science and health, and has a vibrant student life with numerous clubs and varsity teams. It has produced Nobel laureates and notable alumni across politics, science, and arts.

# University of Auckland
Established in 1883 as Auckland University College, it became an independent university in 1961 and is New Zealand’s largest university. Its main campuses are in Auckland’s city center, Grafton, and Newmarket, with additional regional and overseas facilities. The university offers diverse programs across multiple faculties and invests strongly in research, innovation, and entrepreneurship with dedicated centres and funds. It maintains rich libraries and student housing. High numbers of international and Maori students are enrolled. Recent controversies include restructuring, fossil fuel divestment activism, and governance issues.

# State University of Campinas (Unicamp)
Founded in 1966 in São Paulo state to foster regional development, Unicamp rapidly grew to become one of Latin America’s leading research universities. Its primary campus is in Barão Geraldo near Campinas, with additional campuses in Limeira, Piracicaba, and Paulínia. Unicamp administers two technical high schools and operates a major hospital. The university is governed by an elected rector and consists of 24 schools and institutes across sciences, engineering, health, and humanities. Admission is highly competitive via its own national exam. Unicamp emphasizes research, extension programs, and has a vibrant student culture centered on off-campus housing.

# University of Manchester
Tracing its origins to Owens College founded in 1851, and merging with UMIST in 2004, the University of Manchester is a large UK public research university located in the city of Manchester. It is organized into faculties of biology/medicine/health, science/engineering, and humanities. The campus is integrated within the city, with historic Gothic buildings and modern research facilities. The university is renowned for numerous Nobel laureates, research innovations, and its extensive library and museum collections. It hosts a large, diverse student body with a rich student life, including the UK's largest student union and notable sports programs.
