## São Paulo State University (UNESP)

Founded in 1976, UNESP is a leading Brazilian public university with 23 campuses, recognized for its excellence in bioenergy, biotechnology, climate change, nanotechnology, and marine sciences. The university features competitive admissions, elected governance, international partnerships, distinguished faculty and alumni, and vibrant student life.

## State University of Campinas (Unicamp)

Unicamp, established in 1962 across multiple cities, is known for Brazil’s largest teaching hospital, top technical schools, rigorous inclusive admissions, tuition-free education, outreach programs, digital resources, diverse housing, subsidized dining, strong student culture, and historical links to the Varginha Incident.

## University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in Bogotá in 1948, Uniandes is acclaimed for interdisciplinary education, global collaborations, distinguished faculty and alumni, and rich campus traditions.

## Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)

With roots in 1887, RMIT expands over Melbourne, Vietnam, and Spain, emphasizing applied STEM research, encompassing four colleges, 17 schools, and extensive global partnerships.

## University of Sydney

Australia’s oldest university (1850) offers inclusive admissions, diverse support services, renowned architecture, the nation's largest academic library, a global reputation, prominent alumni, and a legacy of equity activism.

## Peking University

Founded in 1898, this prestigious Chinese institution includes 55 schools and Asia’s largest university library, with notable Nobel laureates and leadership in research.

## University of Auckland

Established in 1883, the University of Auckland (Waipapa Taumata Rau) serves over 47,000 students, excelling in arts, engineering, medicine, law, campus development, entrepreneurship, and is known for influential alumni.

## Harvard University

Founded in 1636, Harvard shapes Boston’s academic environment, hosts the world’s largest academic library, holds a vast endowment, features renowned museums, leads in biomedical innovation, fosters dynamic campus life, and exemplifies global leadership.

## University of Manchester

Since 1851, Manchester has led science and humanities across three faculties, includes iconic sites like Jodrell Bank Observatory, counts numerous Nobel laureates, drives city regeneration initiatives, and cultivates diverse student experiences.

## Boston College

A leading Jesuit institution since 1863, Boston College comprises nine schools, maintains selective admissions, offers expansive libraries, acclaimed media, robust arts and athletics, strong traditions, and notable alumni.

## University of Jordan

Established in 1965, Jordan’s largest university offers comprehensive programs, leading research, international engagement, and alumni prominent in leadership and diplomacy.

## University of Western Ontario

Founded in 1878 (now Western University), it serves 35,000+ students, known for affiliated colleges, distinctive architecture, premier facilities, research parks, rigorous admissions, financial aid, major libraries, sustainability, diversity, reforms in gender-based violence, and dynamic student governance. Distinguished alumni include Nobel and Pulitzer laureates, Rhodes Scholars, and leaders.

## Lund University

Founded in 1666, Lund is a leading Scandinavian research university offering broad academic programs, major campuses, strengths across humanities, sciences, business, law, environmental research, vibrant student life, global partnerships, and Nobel laureate alumni.

## Imperial College London

Imperial College London ranks among the world’s top research universities, with a £235.2 million endowment and substantial annual income primarily from tuition and research grants. Imperial excels in science, engineering, medicine, and business, and holds the #2 global spot (QS 2026) and #1 UK graduate employability ranking, with a highly competitive, internationally diverse student body. Its MBA program leads in entrepreneurship, underpinned by links with MIT, the Enterprise Lab, and vibrant student life with 350+ clubs supported by the Imperial College Union. Traditions include the Great Exhibition Road Festival, historic graduation ceremonies at Royal Albert Hall, the Bottle Match, mascot parades, and student expeditions. The medical faculty operates major teaching hospitals, and its alumni and academics include numerous Nobel and Fields laureates (Alexander Fleming, Abdus Salam, Simon Donaldson, Martin Hairer), 90+ Royal Society Fellows, public figures (Brian May, Dame Sally Davies), and notable contributors across fields—such as astronaut Andreas Mogensen, flight data recorder inventor David Warren, NASA’s Nicola Fox, car designer Nikolas Tombazis, former Indian PM Rajiv Gandhi, Singapore’s Teo Chee Hean, CEOs (Ralph Robins, Ian Read, Andrew Rickman), author H.G. Wells, BBC's Pallab Ghosh, and Olympic rowing medalists.
