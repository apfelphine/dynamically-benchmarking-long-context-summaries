# Summary of Universities

## São Paulo State University (UNESP)
UNESP, founded in 1976, is one of São Paulo's six public higher education institutions. It originated from the coordination of 14 isolated institutes established by the state government in the 1950s and 1960s, inspired by the University of California's multi-campus system.

The university boasts extensive facilities, including 1,900 laboratories, 30 libraries with approximately 900,000 books, and 62.8 million m² of land, alongside clinical and veterinary hospitals. UNESP operates 23 campuses across the state, offering 171 undergraduate courses and supporting over 10,000 postgraduate students.

## University of the Andes (Colombia)
Founded in November 1948 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, Uniandes was established as Colombia's first nonsectarian and nonpolitically affiliated university. Its initial goal was to provide professionals capable of advancing the nation's development, modeled after the American university system.

Uniandes maintains a policy of political neutrality and has developed strong international connections, including a three/two program with the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign. The university is governed by a Board of Trustees, and its mission emphasizes pluralism, academic excellence, and critical ethical formation for solving Colombian problems.

## Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT University)
RMIT evolved from the Working Men's College of Melbourne, opening in 1887 as a night school for workers. It gained royal patronage and was renamed the Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology in 1960, eventually becoming a public university in 1992 after merging with the Phillip Institute of Technology.

RMIT is structured into two divisions: Higher Education and Vocational Education and Training (VET). It has a significant global presence, with campuses in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi (Vietnam), and a center in Barcelona, Spain. The university focuses heavily on applied research in areas like design, technology, health, and sustainability.

## University of Sydney
Established by the University of Sydney Act 1850 (NSW), it is Australia's first public, non-denominational, and secular university. The institution has a rich history, notably admitting women on an equal basis in 1881, and holds a royal charter.

Sydney is known for its strong academic reputation, ranking highly in global surveys. Its main campus in Camperdown/Darlington houses the administrative headquarters, while it maintains numerous satellite campuses. The university boasts significant collections, including the Chau Chak Wing Museum and the largest academic library in the Southern Hemisphere.

## Peking University
Established as the Imperial University of Peking in 1898, the university transitioned to the National University of Peking after the Xinhai Revolution. It gained prominence during the New Culture Movement, fostering intellectual freedom.

In the 21st century, the university has achieved global acclaim, highlighted by Nobel laureate Tu Youyou's recognition. Peking University focuses on international cooperation, evidenced by dual degree programs with Waseda University, and has expanded its presence with campuses in Shenzhen and Changping.

## University of Auckland
The University of Auckland began as a constituent college of the University of New Zealand in 1883. It transitioned to being empowered by its own Act in 1961 and has been recognized for its research culture since the 1930s.

The university is known for its commitment to innovation and entrepreneurship, running programs like the Unleash Space. It operates multiple campuses in Auckland, Grafton, and Newmarket, and maintains international outreach through its University of Auckland Innovation Institute in China.

## Harvard University
Founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony, Harvard began with a strong Puritan mission, symbolized by its early seal. Over time, it evolved to embrace liberal and secular ideas, notably through the work of Charles William Eliot.

Harvard is recognized globally as a preeminent research institution, maintaining the world's largest academic library with 20.4 million holdings. It is noted for its dominance among the world's elite, producing numerous U.S. presidents and Nobel laureates, despite facing recent political controversies regarding autonomy.

## University of Manchester
Tracing its roots to the Mechanics' Institution in 1824, the university developed a strong heritage in science and engineering, contributing to discoveries like the nuclear nature of the atom. It formally became the University of Manchester in 2004 after merging with UMIST.

Manchester is a major research centre and a member of the Russell Group, known for its strong performance across sciences, humanities, and social sciences. Its campus is integrated into the city, and it maintains a significant presence in cultural institutions like the Manchester Museum and the John Rylands Research Institute.

## State University of Campinas (Unicamp)
Unicamp was established by the São Paulo State Government in 1962 to promote interior development. Its main campus in Barão Geraldo is designed radially, allowing for strong collaboration between different sciences.

The university is a major center in Latin America, offering 70 undergraduate programs and 153 postgraduate programs. Despite rapid expansion, it faces financial challenges due to high payroll costs relative to its government funding, yet it maintains strong academic autonomy.

## University of Jordan
The University of Jordan was founded in 1962, following national aspirations for higher education. It operates main campuses in Amman and Aqaba, reflecting a national strategy for educational expansion.

The university is structured into Scientific, Humanitarian, and Medical faculties. It emphasizes international orientation, expanding graduate offerings and engaging in research development. The University Jordan Library serves as a major repository of historical and regional documents.

## University of Western Ontario (Western University)
Founded in 1878, Western University evolved to embrace a less regional identity, rebranding as "Western University" in 2012. It is a publicly funded research university with strong ties to its community.

Its central campus is known for its Collegiate Gothic architecture, while the university also operates multiple research parks. Western emphasizes student life through its student unions and maintains a focus on sustainability across its operations.

## Lund University
Lund University, established in 1666, is one of Scandinavia's oldest and largest research universities. Its history is marked by the influence of the Swedish Crown and its strong tradition in education.

Lund is highly regarded internationally, particularly for its research output and sustainability efforts (ranking #1 globally in the 2026 QS World University Sustainability Ranking). It has a strong student culture rooted in "student nations" and the Academic Society (AF).

## Imperial College London
Imperial College was formed in 1907 through the unification of several London institutions, establishing itself as a science and technology leader. It has since gained full degree-awarding powers and is recognized globally for its entrepreneurial culture.

The college is highly international, with over half of its students from outside the UK or EU. It is globally ranked as one of the elite universities, noted for strong collaborations with institutions like MIT and its focus on applied research and commercialization.

## Boston College
Founded in 1849 by Jesuit priests to serve the Irish Catholic immigrant population, Boston College moved its main campus to Chestnut Hill in 1907. It developed into a major institution with diverse schools, including Law and Management.

BC continues to grow its infrastructure, exemplified by its $1.6 billion master plan. The university emphasizes its Catholic and Jesuit tradition, while also actively addressing modern issues such as racism and student diversity through initiatives like AHANA.
