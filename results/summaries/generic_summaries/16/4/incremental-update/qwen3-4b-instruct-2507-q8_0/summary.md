## Boston College

Founded in 1857 by Jesuit priest John McElroy in Boston’s South End, Boston College began as a Catholic educational institution for Irish immigrant youth, grounded in the Jesuit *Ratio Studiorum*. It relocated to Chestnut Hill in 1907 under President Thomas I. Gasson, marking a major expansion of its academic and campus infrastructure. By the 1920s, the university established graduate schools and became coeducational in 1926, with full coeducation achieved in 1970. Financial stability was achieved under President J. Donald Monan, who oversaw a significant endowment growth to over $590 million by 1996. Under President William P. Leahy, the university initiated church reform and launched a $1.6 billion campus revitalization plan in 2007, transforming its physical and academic landscape. The main Chestnut Hill campus, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, features the iconic Gasson Hall and expanded facilities, serving as a center of academic and cultural life.

Boston College operates across three primary campuses: Chestnut Hill (main campus), Newton (housing and law school), Brighton (theology and athletics), and Brookline (Messina College for first-generation students). The university maintains an annual budget of $1.02 billion and launched the "Soaring Higher" campaign in 2023 with a goal of raising $3 billion, prioritizing financial aid, student life, and academic enhancement. It emphasizes inclusive access through targeted support programs, including Messina College, which serves first-generation and underrepresented students. The institution is known for its strong academic programs, particularly in business, theology, and the sciences, and has a long-standing tradition of leadership in Catholic education and service.

Boston College is deeply rooted in its Jesuit mission, promoting intellectual rigor, social justice, and service to others. It maintains a diverse and vibrant student life, supported by a wide range of student organizations, clubs, and interfaith initiatives. The university has a strong alumni network, with notable graduates in business, politics, science, and public service, including U.S. senators, judges, and prominent nonprofit leaders. Its commitment to academic excellence, financial accessibility, and community engagement continues to shape its identity as a leading private research university in the United States.

**Student Diversity and Inclusivity:** Boston College serves a diverse student body, with 58% White, 11% Hispanic, 10% Asian, 8% foreign, and 4% Black, and a significant 13% from low-income backgrounds. In 1979, the university introduced the "AHANA" initiative (African-American, Hispanic, Asian, Native American) to promote cultural inclusivity, a framework now widely adopted across campus life. This commitment to diversity is reinforced through inclusive programming, student organizations, and interfaith initiatives.

**Student Life and Media:** A rich array of student publications—including *The Heights*, *The Gavel*, *The Torch*, and *Sub Turri*—alongside radio (WZBC) and television (BCTV), provide platforms for student expression. The university also supports academic discourse through numerous scholarly journals.

**Athletics:** Boston College’s Eagles compete in NCAA Division I, primarily in the ACC, with strong programs in football, hockey, and basketball. The team’s mascot, Baldwin the Eagle, honors the bald eagle and the spirit of victory. The fight song "For Boston," composed in 1885, is one of America’s oldest. The team wears red bandanas in tribute to Welles Crowther, a 9/11 hero. The men’s hockey team has won three NCAA titles (2008, 2010, 2012), and the women’s team has reached the Frozen Four seven times. The football program boasts 690+ wins, produced a Heisman Trophy winner (Doug Flutie), and has generated 13 consensus All-Americans and over 200 NFL players. Alumni Stadium (44,500 seats) hosts football, while Conte Forum and Kelley Rink serve as home venues for basketball and ice hockey.

**Women’s Lacrosse:** The Eagles women's lacrosse team competes in NCAA Division I as part of the Atlantic Coast Conference, playing at Newton Soccer Complex and Alumni Stadium.

**Student Activism and Inclusivity:** Boston College has a history of student activism, including protests in 1970 over tuition and lack of transparency that led to the end of ROTC ties. In 2017 and 2018, student-led protests against racism followed incidents of defacement and vandalism. In 2003–2005, students successfully expanded the university’s non-discrimination policy to include LGBTQ+ protections. The university recognizes alumni through terms such as "Eagles," "Golden Eagles" (for 50-year alumni), and "Double" and "Triple Eagles" (for alumni with multiple degrees or ties to BC High). Notable alumni include John Kerry, Paul Cellucci, and Leonard Nimoy.

## Harvard University

Founded in 1636, rooted in Puritanism with a mission to advance learning; evolved through Enlightenment and secular influences, expanding access and diversity in the 19th–20th centuries; merged Radcliffe in 1999; experienced leadership changes in the 21st century, including a brief presidency by Claudine Gay (2024), who resigned amid antisemitism and plagiarism allegations. In 2025, it was named for investigation into antisemitism under the Trump administration. Harvard is located in Cambridge, Massachusetts, with additional campuses in Allston, Longwood, and globally, and holds the largest university endowment in the world ($50.7 billion as of 2023). The university maintains a large residential undergraduate program, diverse graduate and professional schools (including Law, Medicine, Business, and Engineering), and a decentralized governance structure with limited public access to its statutes. It has a long history of student-led divestment campaigns against controversial investments and continues to expand in Allston and invest in innovation and research across its campuses. These actions reflect its ongoing role as a global leader in higher education, research, and institutional resilience.

**Notable Faculty:** Harvard is home to prominent scholars and public figures, including Stephen Breyer, Elena Kagan, Janet Yellen, Elizabeth Warren, Amartya Sen, Robert Reich, B.F. Skinner, and Henry Louis Gates Jr., whose research and public service have significantly influenced policy and academia.

**Cultural Significance:** Harvard is frequently depicted in literature and film as a symbol of tradition, elitism, and intellectual rigor. It appears in works by Faulkner, Thomas Wolfe, and John P. Marquand (e.g., *The Late George Apley*, *The Second Happiest Day*), and in films such as *Love Story*, *The Paper Chase*, *Prozac Nation*, *Legally Blonde*, *Homeless to Harvard*, and *The Social Network*, reinforcing its iconic status in American cultural memory.

## University of the Andes (Colombia)

Founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón as Colombia’s first nonsectarian, non-political university, the University of the Andes was established to train professionals for national development and foster post-war leadership. Modeled after American universities, it offers liberal and technical education and includes early programs such as a three-year "three/two" exchange with U.S. institutions (e.g., MIT, Vanderbilt). Supported by Colombian industries, philanthropies, and government via ICETEX, the university has developed key initiatives including academic conferences with global scholars, the establishment of the Female Section (1953), and strategic faculty recruitment. Governed by a Board of Trustees (30–45 members, including 2 professors and 2 students) and a Board of Directors overseeing the 5-year PDI development plan. The Rector manages operations, budgets, and appointments, while Deans and the Academic Council handle academic and disciplinary matters. The university has 14 academic units and 20 administrative units, employing 745 and 853 non-faculty staff respectively. As of 2021, it had 95% of undergraduates from top 1% high school graduates, with 55% male and 45% female students. Students follow an interdisciplinary curriculum with four cycles (CBP, CSH, CP, CPC). It hosts international students via exchange programs (119+ universities in 34 countries) and operates in Bogotá’s historic center, featuring renovated industrial and religious buildings, 180,551 sq m infrastructure, and 445,000 books. The campus includes La Caneca (sports center), health services, and student facilities. The mascot, Seneca the goat, is a cultural symbol. Notable alumni include Colombian presidents, ministers, artists, and scientists.

## Peking University (China)

Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking during the Hundred Days' Reform, it was established by Emperor Guangxu and led by Liang Qichao. Initially a leading educational institution, it was restructured after the 1911 Xinhai Revolution and renamed the National University of Peking in 1919. It remains one of China’s most prestigious universities. During the early Republic of China period, it became a center of intellectual and progressive thought, playing a key role in the 1919 May 4th Movement. In World War II, it joined the National Southwestern Associated University in Kunming, preserving academic continuity during the war. In modern times, it has expanded academically and geographically, establishing new schools, research centers, and international partnerships. It hosts a vast academic ecosystem with 55 schools, over 130 majors, and extensive research facilities. Peking University consistently ranks among the world’s top universities, particularly in Asia, with top positions in global rankings such as QS, Times Higher Education, and U.S. News & World Report. Its campus, located on a former imperial garden, features traditional Chinese architecture and renowned museums.

**Global Academic Excellence:** Peking University is globally recognized for its academic excellence, ranking 11th in the Times Higher Education Global Employability University Ranking 2025 and 12th in the world for most Highly Cited Researchers (Clarivate, 2024). It ranks 13th globally in the CWTS Leiden Ranking (2024) and is among the top institutions in research output, particularly in natural sciences, health sciences, and computer science. The university excels in numerous subject areas, with strong national and global rankings in fields such as mathematics, engineering, life sciences, medicine, and social sciences. It consistently leads in Asia and is a top performer in the BRICS & Emerging Economies rankings.

**International Engagement and Programs:** Peking University features one of Asia’s largest libraries and hosts top-tier academic programs, including the National School of Development (ranked among China’s top think tanks) and the BiMBA program (ranked among Asia’s best MBA programs). Its Shenzhen Graduate School, established in 2001, offers joint programs and a new campus under a strategic partnership with Shenzhen. The university has extensive international collaborations, including dual-degree programs with Waseda University (Japan), Cornell, Yale, London School of Economics, and others, and operates joint research centers with Stanford, Freie Universität Berlin, and Washington University in St. Louis. It launched the "Global Excellence Strategy" based on the "CLOUDS" framework to strengthen global engagement. The university also participates in art research initiatives with the University of Chicago and UNESCO. Notable alumni include Nobel laureate Tu Youyou, former Chinese Premier Li Keqiang, Robin Li (co-founder of Baidu), and Hou Yifan (chess champion). Influential faculty include Cai Yuanpei and Michael Pettis.

## São Paulo State University (USP)

Founded in 1926 in Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo State University (USP) is Brazil’s first state university and a cornerstone in the development of public higher education in the country. Established during a period of national expansion and institutional modernization in the early 20th century, USP emerged as a model for public university systems in Latin America. It began with the founding of medical faculties in the 19th century and evolved into a comprehensive public university system, with USP playing a pivotal role in shaping Brazil’s academic and research infrastructure. As a leading institution in science, engineering, and social sciences, USP is known for its strong research output, extensive graduate programs, and commitment to public service. ## São Paulo State University (Unesp)

Founded in 1976, São Paulo State University (Unesp) is a public university system inspired by the University of California's multi-campus model, established to expand access to higher education beyond São Paulo’s capital. It began with 14 campuses and now includes 23 campuses and 33 institutes across the state. Named "Universidade Estadual Paulista" by its first rector, Luiz Ferreira Martins, with a tribute to journalist Júlio de Mesquita Filho, Unesp is one of six major public higher education institutions in São Paulo, alongside USP, UNICAMP, UNIFESP, UFABC, UFSCar, and FATEC.

Unesp offers 171 undergraduate programs in 62 professions, serving over 45,000 students annually, with about 5,600 new graduates each year. It provides 118 postgraduate programs, including 117 master’s and 93 doctoral degrees. The university has 1,900 laboratories, 30 libraries (holding ~900,000 books), museums, gardens, five experimental farms, and medical facilities including the Hospital de Clínicas de Botucatu and the State Hospital of Bauru. Admissions are managed through the state-wide vestibular exam (organized by VUNESP), with additional exams required for architecture, arts, and design. Unesp operates under a rector appointed by the state governor, presiding over a council of institute directors and academic representatives. The university has seen a steady rise in research publications since 2012 and maintains a decentralized administrative structure with regional autonomy.

**Admissions and International Engagement:** Unesp employs a two-phase admissions process with general and specific subject exams; students apply to only one course. It has 140 international exchange agreements with institutions in 52 countries, focusing on areas like Agricultural Sciences, Alternative Energy, Dentistry, and Literature. Recognized globally, Unesp ranks among Brazil’s top universities, notably in research (second in Brazil for international scientific articles in 2010), and is ranked in the top 300 globally by ARWU (2017) and Webometrics (2017). Notable alumni and faculty include prominent Brazilian artists, scientists, and intellectuals.

## Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)

Founded in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne, RMIT evolved through expansion and reorganization, becoming the Royal Melbourne Technical College in 1954 and later RMIT University in 1992 after merging with the Phillip Institute of Technology. It pioneered vocational and dual-sector education, expanded its campuses, and engaged with Southeast Asia via the Colombo Plan. RMIT is Australia’s only university with royal patronage and operates three main campuses in Melbourne—**City**, **Bundoora**, and **Brunswick**—with additional sites in **Ho Chi Minh City (Vietnam)**, **Hanoi (Vietnam)**, and a coordinating centre in **Barcelona (Spain)**. The university is governed by the **RMIT Council**, led by the Chancellor and Vice-Chancellor, and includes elected staff and student representatives. It is divided into two divisions—**Higher Education** and **Vocational Education and Training (VET)**—organised into academic colleges. RMIT offers undergraduate, postgraduate, and vocational programs, with a strong emphasis on design, engineering, and regional research. Its campuses feature historic buildings and modern facilities, blending urban integration with heritage preservation. RMIT expanded internationally by becoming Vietnam’s first foreign-owned university, with academic partnerships in China, Singapore, and Malaysia.

**Academic Structure and Programs:** RMIT is organized into four academic colleges: Business and Law, Design and Social Context, STEM, and Vocational Education. It offers a wide range of schools and programs, emphasizing applied research, design, technology, health, and sustainability. RMIT ranks highly globally and nationally in university rankings, with strong graduate outcomes—84.7% employer satisfaction, 71.8% full-time employment for undergraduates, and 98% for postgraduates. The university has a strong research portfolio, over 50 research centers, and a network of specialized libraries, including Swanston and Beanland Libraries. RMIT Gallery is a prominent public art venue with a significant collection of Australian art. Student life is supported by RMIT Link (campus union) and RUSU (student union), which promote cultural, social, and recreational activities.

**Student Life and Engagement:** RMIT offers a vibrant student life through RUSU, which oversees student representation in areas such as education, environment, international students, and LGBTQ+ and women's rights, along with student media including *The Swanston Gazette*, *Catalyst*, *RMITV*, *SYN*, and *3RRR*. Student accommodation includes self-catered apartments and affiliated residential colleges like International House and Queen’s College. Demographics show a slight male majority, with a significant gender imbalance in STEM fields. The Spiritual Centre, located in the historic Old Melbourne Gaol chapel, provides multi-faith worship and chaplaincy services. RMIT graduates are highly employable, ranked 51st globally in 2011, with a global alumni network of 280,000. Notable alumni include James Wan, Rove McManus, Lydia Lassila, and Jim Stynes. A key tradition is the graduation parade, which culminates at Federation Square with a military escort and a mayor’s "writ of passage" to the ceremony at Docklands Stadium.
