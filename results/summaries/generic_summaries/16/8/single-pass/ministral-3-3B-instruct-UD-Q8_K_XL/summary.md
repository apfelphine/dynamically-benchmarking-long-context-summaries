## **Boston College**

### **History**
Boston College was founded in 1857 by Jesuit priests, initially as a preparatory institution for Irish Catholic immigrants. It received a university charter in 1863, launching its collegiate instruction in 1864. Early on, it emphasized Latin, Greek, philosophy, and theology, with a strong Jesuit Ratio Studiorum influence. The university expanded significantly in the 20th century, moving from its South End campus to Chestnut Hill in 1907, and later adopting co-education in 1970. Notable expansions included the establishment of graduate schools, law school, and the Carroll School of Management. Under President J. Donald Monan, the university faced financial challenges but achieved substantial growth, including a major capital campaign and increased endowment.

### **Campuses**
Boston College operates multiple campuses:
- **Chestnut Hill Campus**: The main 175-acre campus, home to over 120 buildings, including Gasson Hall and the McMullen Museum of Art.
- **Connors Family Retreat**: A retreat and conference center in Dover, Massachusetts.
- **Weston Observatory**: A research facility in Weston, Massachusetts.
- **Newton Campus**: A residential area for freshman and the Boston College Law School.
- **Brighton Campus**: Houses the School of Theology and Ministry and athletic facilities.
- **Pine Manor College**: A financially struggling liberal arts institution acquired in 2020.

### **Organization and Administration**
Boston College is governed by a Board of Trustees, with a focus on Jesuit values. The university operates eight research libraries, including the McMullen Museum of Art, and maintains close ties with the Church of Saint Ignatius of Loyola. It is need-blind for domestic applicants and offers a range of academic programs across nine constituent colleges and schools.

### **Academics**
Boston College offers a diverse range of undergraduate and graduate programs, including the prestigious Order of the Cross and Crown honor society. The university is ranked highly in national and global rankings, with notable achievements in research publications and interdisciplinary studies. Notable programs include the Carroll School of Management, which ranks highly in business education, and the Boston College Law Review, a prominent legal journal.

### **Student Life**
Boston College has a student body with a diverse demographic, including 58% White, 11% Hispanic, and 8% Asian students. Student life is enriched by various clubs, societies, and media outlets, such as *The Heights*, a student newspaper. The university emphasizes student engagement through initiatives like the AHANA program, which supports African-American, Hispanic, Asian, and Native American students. The university also hosts notable traditions, including the annual graduation parade and the celebration of the Alma Mater.

### **Notable People**
Boston College has produced notable alumni, including former U.S. Secretary of State John F. Kerry, Nobel laureate Tu Youyou, and prominent figures in academia, business, and sports. The university is also home to the Order of the Cross and Crown, a prestigious honor society for outstanding students.

## **University of Western Ontario**

### **History**
Founded in 1878 as The Western University of London, Ontario, the university initially focused on arts, divinity, law, and medicine. It became non-denominational in 1908 and expanded significantly after World War II. The university merged with Brescia University College in 2024, integrating its operations into the main campus. Notable figures include Sir Frederick Banting, who received the Nobel Prize for discovering insulin, and Alice Munro, the Nobel laureate in literature.

### **Campuses**
The main campus is located in London, Ontario, and includes several residential halls and academic buildings. Notable structures include University College and the Physics and Astronomy Building. The university also operates affiliated colleges, such as Huron University College and King’s University College.

### **Organization and Administration**
The university is governed by a Board of Governors and the Senate, with the president serving as the chief executive officer. It comprises 11 faculties and schools, including the Don Wright Faculty of Music and the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry. The university is known for its strong research output and is a member of the Association of Universities and Colleges of Canada.

### **Academics**
Western University offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs, with a focus on research and innovation. The university is ranked highly in national and global rankings, particularly in the fields of medicine, engineering, and law. Notable programs include the Ivey School of Business and the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry.

### **Student Life**

Student life is supported by various clubs, societies, and media outlets, such as the *Gazette*, a student newspaper. The university emphasizes leadership education and student exchange programs, fostering a vibrant campus community.

### **Notable People**
