# Boston College
Founded in 1863 by Jesuit community leader John McElroy to educate Irish Catholic immigrants, Boston College began with a strong preparatory focus and Jesuit Ratio Studiorum curriculum. Initially located in Boston’s South End, it moved to Chestnut Hill in 1909 under President Thomas Gasson, who envisioned a grand campus symbolizing Jesuit scholarship. The institution expanded academically through the 20th century, introducing graduate schools, professional programs, and becoming fully coeducational in 1970. Presidents Monan and Leahy oversaw significant financial stabilization, capital campaigns, and campus revitalization.

Boston College’s main Chestnut Hill campus features Gothic architecture and is connected by public transit. It operates additional campuses in Newton (including its Law School), Brighton, and Brookline (home to Messina College for first-generation and underprivileged students). The university comprises nine colleges and schools, maintains a strong Jesuit Catholic tradition with multiple chapels and mass services, and has partnerships with local parishes. Its student body is ethnically diverse (58% White; 11% Hispanic; 10% Asian). BC competes athletically in the ACC with notable programs in football, ice hockey, and women’s lacrosse. Student media, arts ensembles, and honor societies foster a rich campus life. The university has faced student protests on tuition, racial climate, and LGBT inclusion.

# Harvard University
Founded in 1636 as a Puritan seminary, Harvard evolved from a clergy-training school to a premier global research university. Its early curriculum mirrored English university models, but the 19th and 20th centuries saw gradual secularization and diversification of its student body. Harvard became formally coeducational after merging with Radcliffe College in 1999 and appointed its first female president in 2007. Recent decades involved administrative modernization and controversies, including conflicts with federal authorities over policies related to antisemitism and free speech.

Harvard’s main campus is in Cambridge, Massachusetts, centered on Harvard Yard, with additional campuses in Allston (including Business and Engineering) and Longwood (Medicine). It hosts numerous libraries (largest academic library system globally), museums (notably the McMullen Museum of Art), and research centers. The university consists of 12 professional schools covering disciplines from Medicine to Business. It enjoys top global rankings, with strong research output and a diverse student population (33% White, 22% Asian, 14% International). Harvard fields extensive NCAA Division I athletics, with historic rivalries and multiple national championships. Alumni and faculty include multiple Nobel laureates, U.S. presidents, and other global leaders. Harvard maintains a prominent cultural presence in literature and film.

# University of Jordan
Established in 1962 as Jordan’s first national university, the University of Jordan began with the Faculty of Arts and expanded rapidly despite regional political and economic challenges. It operates a main campus in Jubaiha, Amman, and a branch in Aqaba (opened 2009), with modern facilities including academic buildings, student housing, and research centers.

Academically, the university has 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical faculties, including engineering, arts, business, law, medicine, and nursing. The School of Engineering is its largest faculty, all departments accredited by ABET. The university maintains a large library with over one million volumes, historic archives, and specialized centers for Arabic language and Islamic studies. Jordan University Hospital serves as a major teaching and research hospital with international accreditations. The university is ranked as Jordan’s top institution and holds numerous international exchange agreements. Notable alumni span politics, diplomacy, science, and the arts.

# Peking University
Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, Peking University is China’s premier institution known for academic freedom, democratic governance, and leadership in historical movements such as the May Fourth Movement. It grew into a comprehensive research university with 55 schools and departments offering extensive undergraduate and graduate programs.

The campus blends traditional Chinese architecture with modern facilities, including research institutions and museums. Beyond its main campus, PKU has satellite campuses in Shenzhen and Changping, and international partnerships with universities worldwide. It consistently ranks among Asia’s top universities and holds high global research standings. PKU is notable for Nobel laureate Tu Youyou and numerous leading academics in sciences, humanities, and business. Its research centers cover a broad spectrum, including economic development, biomedical engineering, and mathematics.

# Lund University
Founded in 1666 on medieval educational roots dating to 1085, Lund University is Scandinavia’s oldest university. Initially established to integrate the Scania region into Sweden, it evolved through centuries marked by growth in student numbers and academic reputation.

The university comprises nine faculties, including humanities, engineering, medicine, and economics, spread across campuses in Lund, Malmö, and Helsingborg. It features historic Gothic and modern architecture, several specialized museums, and one of Sweden’s largest libraries. Lund is internationally recognized for research excellence in neurology, nanotechnology, and sustainability, contributing innovations like diagnostic ultrasound and Bluetooth technology. Student life is vibrant, centered around historic student nations, the Academic Society, and active student unions.

# Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)
Established in 1887 as the Working Men’s College of Melbourne, RMIT evolved into a leading public university renowned for vocational and higher education. It pioneered technical and design education, expanded through mergers, and established international campuses in Vietnam and Europe.

RMIT operates multiple campuses in Melbourne’s city center, outer suburbs (Bundoora, Brunswick), and international sites (Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Barcelona). It offers diverse academic programs via four colleges spanning business, design, STEM, and vocational education. Governed by a council with broad representation, RMIT emphasizes applied research and industry partnerships. Student life includes a campus union (Link), an independent student union (RUSU), extensive clubs and media, and multi-faith spiritual services. Its facilities include major libraries and art galleries, and it is noted for graduate employability and research in design, health, and sustainability.

# Imperial College London
Originating in 1907 from the merger of several scientific and technical schools in London, Imperial College is a leading UK university focusing on science, engineering, medicine, and business. It gained independence from the University of London in 2007 and has since expanded with a new campus at White City for innovation and entrepreneurship.

Imperial’s campuses include the historical South Kensington site, the White City innovation hub, and Silwood Park for ecology research. The college has four faculties covering engineering, medicine, natural sciences, and business. It hosts interdisciplinary centers addressing global challenges and partners with leading research institutions worldwide. Imperial is globally ranked among the top universities, noted for cutting-edge research, entrepreneurial culture, and competitive admissions. Student life is active with over 350 clubs, unique historical mascotry traditions, and a strong emphasis on interdisciplinary student engagement.

# São Paulo State University (UNESP)
Founded in 1976 as a multi-campus public university inspired by the University of California system, UNESP is one of Brazil’s leading institutions with 23 campuses and 33 institutes across São Paulo state. It evolved from a federation of isolated institutes created in the mid-20th century.

UNESP offers 171 undergraduate programs and 118 postgraduate ones, employing about 3,500 faculty. It has extensive facilities, including teaching hospitals, research farms, museums, and libraries with ~900,000 volumes. Admissions are through a competitive vestibular exam with affirmative action policies. The university actively engages in international exchanges, ranks among top Latin American universities, and focuses research on bioenergy, nanotechnology, pharmaceutical development, and climate change. Its administration balances academic autonomy with state funding challenges.

# University of Western Ontario (Western University)
Founded in 1878, Western University is a major Canadian research university located in London, Ontario. Initially affiliated with the Anglican Church, it became non-denominational in 1908 and expanded steadily, adding multiple faculties including Medicine, Business, Engineering, Law, and Music.

The campus is a blend of Collegiate Gothic and modern architecture, featuring mature green spaces and several research parks. Western operates six libraries, art galleries, student housing, and extensive athletic facilities. Enrolment includes over 30,000 undergraduates and 5,000 graduates, with significant domestic and international students. It offers diverse academic programs, strong research funding, and student life supported by an active students' council and numerous clubs. Western is recognized for achievements in various fields and has notable alumni in politics, science, business, and arts.

# University of the Andes (Colombia)
Founded in 1948 amid Colombia’s political and social transformation, the Universidad de los Andes was established as Colombia’s first nonsectarian, nonpolitical university to provide high-quality, internationally modeled education. Mario Laserna Pinzón led its foundation, emphasizing liberal education alongside technical proficiency.

Starting with 80 students, the university expanded early faculties and incorporated exchange programs with U.S. institutions. It maintains a diverse academic structure of ten faculties and multiple centers, governed by a board of trustees and academic councils. The main campus in Bogotá blends historical and modern buildings, with robust libraries and sport facilities. Its mission focuses on pluralism, independence, and social responsibility. Alumni include leaders in politics, academia, art, and science.

# State University of Campinas (Unicamp)
Established in 1966 in the district of Barão Geraldo, Campinas, Brazil, Unicamp grew rapidly from its origins as a medical school. It expanded to multiple schools and institutes, becoming one of Latin America’s foremost research universities. The campus was designed radially around a central plaza, promoting interdisciplinary collaboration.

Unicamp comprises 24 units and two technical high schools, with approximately 70 undergraduate programs and over 150 postgraduate courses. It operates the Hospital das Clínicas and maintains extensive research centers. Admissions are highly competitive, supported by affirmative action for public-school students. Student housing is limited, with off-campus living common. Unicamp is known for scientific and technological innovations, robust academic governance, and a strong commitment to community outreach.

# University of Sydney
Australia’s first university, founded in 1850, Sydney University pioneered secular, public education admitting women as early as 1881. Its campus spans inner-city suburbs with heritage sandstone Gothic revival architecture.

The university encompasses eight faculties including Arts, Medicine, Science, Law, and Business. It operates extensive libraries, museums (including the Chau Chak Wing Museum), and research facilities. Sydney hosts vibrant student life with unions representing undergraduate and postgraduate students. It boasts notable alumni including prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and cultural figures. Recent decades have seen administrative controversies, student activism, and campus modernization projects. It ranks among Australia’s leading universities academically.

# University of Manchester
Formed in 2004 by the merger of Victoria University of Manchester and UMIST, the University of Manchester has roots dating to 1824 and Owens College of 1851. It played a crucial role in industrial research and scientific advancements such as the discovery of the atomic nucleus and early computers.

Located in central Manchester, it comprises faculties of Biology, Medicine and Health; Science and Engineering; and Humanities. The university runs cultural institutions including John Rylands Library and Manchester Museum. It is part of the UK’s research elite with a Nobel laureate heritage. Student life includes a large union, extensive sports clubs, and traditions such as University Challenge victories. Accommodation spans three campuses with a mixture of historic and modern facilities.

# University of Auckland
Founded in 1883 as Auckland University College, the University of Auckland is New Zealand’s largest institution by enrollment, with campuses in the city center, Grafton (health sciences), and Newmarket (engineering and science). It emphasizes research, innovation, and entrepreneurship through dedicated centers and incubators.

The university offers diverse academic programs across faculties such as Arts, Business, Law, Engineering, Medicine, and Science. Its academic library is the largest in the Southern Hemisphere. Student life is supported by the Auckland University Students’ Association and includes extensive accommodation options. The university has faced recent controversies over restructuring and fossil fuel divestment. It maintains multiple international partnerships and ranks as New Zealand’s top university.
