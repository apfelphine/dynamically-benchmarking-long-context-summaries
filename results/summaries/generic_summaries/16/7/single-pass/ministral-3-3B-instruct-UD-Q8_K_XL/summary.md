### **University of Jordan**

#### **Campuses**
- **Amman Campus**: Located in Jubaiha, near the capital, with 1,200 dunums of land. Features 12 gates, student parking, and a central location near significant landmarks. Includes twelve faculties, including the School of Engineering (ABET-accredited) and the King Abdullah II School of Information Technology.
- **Aqaba Campus**: Established in 2009, focused on regional development and academic growth. Features modern facilities, including a women’s dormitory under construction.

#### **History**
- Founded in 1962 as a response to British aid negotiations for higher education. King Hussein bin Talal’s royal decree established it as the first national university in Jordan.
- Early years saw rapid expansion despite political instability, with new faculties and research centers added. The university became autonomous in 1998.
- Notable milestones include the Aqaba branch (2009), the introduction of blended learning, and the establishment of the Jordan University Hospital (1973).

#### **Academics**
- Offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across three streams: Scientific, Humanitarian, and Medical faculties.
- **Scientific Faculties**: Includes Schools of Science, Agriculture, Engineering, and Information Technology.
- **Humanitarian Faculties**: Includes Schools of Arts, Sharia, Business, Education, Law, and Physical Education.
- **Medical Faculties**: Includes Schools of Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Dentistry, and Rehabilitation Sciences.
- **Library**: One of the largest in Jordan, with over one million items and extensive digital resources.

#### **Research**
- Recognized for its high research output, particularly in health sciences and engineering.
- Notable contributions include the discovery of the Arambourgiania fossil bones and advancements in medical research.
- Rankings: Consistently ranked among the top universities in Jordan and the Arab world.

#### **International Affiliations**
- Hundreds of student exchange programs, including agreements with U.S. universities like Vanderbilt and Murray State.
- Member of the International Association of Universities, Federation of the Universities of the Islamic World, and the Mediterranean Universities Union.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Union**: Governs student affairs, including clubs, societies, and advocacy.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes former Prime Ministers, scientists, and public figures, such as Rami Hamdallah and Khaled al-Karaki.

### **Boston College**

#### **History**
- Founded in 1857 by Jesuit priests, initially as a preparatory institution for Irish Catholic immigrants.
- Granted a university charter in 1863, becoming a leading Jesuit institution in the U.S.
- Known for its academic rigor, Jesuit values, and expansion into graduate and professional schools.

#### **Campuses**
- **Chestnut Hill Campus**: The main campus, spanning 175 acres, with over 120 buildings. Features Gothic and modernist architecture.
- **Newton Campus**: Formerly the Newton College of the Sacred Heart, now housing the Law School and athletic facilities.
- **Brighton Campus**: Acquired in 2004, includes the School of Theology and a men’s baseball facility.
- **Other Properties**: Includes the Connors Family Retreat, Weston Observatory, and additional properties.

#### **Academics**
- **Schools and Colleges**: Nine constituent colleges and schools, including the Carroll School of Management, Lynch School of Education, and Boston College Law School.
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the top national universities, with strong rankings in business, law, and education.
- **Research**: Publishes journals such as the Boston College Law Review and C21 Resources.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Media**: The Heights (student newspaper), The Gavel (independent magazine), and The Torch (Catholic student newspaper).
- **Athletics**: Competitive Division I teams in the NCAA, including the Boston College Eagles football team.
- **Notable Traditions**: Fight song "For Boston," the Order of the Cross and Crown (honor society), and the annual Winter Ball.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Includes former U.S. Presidents John F. Kerry and John McCain, Nobel laureates, and prominent figures in academia, business, and entertainment.

### **Lund University**

#### **History**
- Founded in 1425 as a Franciscan studium generale, making it the oldest university in Scandinavia.
- Renamed Lund University in 1666, following Sweden’s acquisition of Scania.
- Known for its strong research output and contributions to fields like medicine, physics, and engineering.

#### **Campuses**
- **Main Campus**: Located in Lund, with over 100 buildings and a large student population.
- **Satellite Campuses**: Includes campuses in Malmö, Helsingborg, and an aviation school in Ljungbyhed.
- **Research Facilities**: Home to the Biological Museum, Library, and Hospital, with extensive research centers.

#### **Academics**
- **Faculties**: Nine faculties, including the Faculty of Humanities, Engineering, Fine & Performing Arts, Law, Medicine, Science, Social Sciences, and School of Aviation.
- **Research**: Known for innovations like diagnostic ultrasound, artificial kidney, and Bluetooth technology.
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the top universities in Scandinavia and globally, with strong performance in research and sustainability.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Nations**: Traditional residential colleges for students, offering housing and social activities.
- **Academic Society (AF)**: Central organization for student life, including theater, radio, and cultural events.
- **Student Unions**: Nine student unions, representing students in various faculties.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni and Faculty**: Includes Nobel laureates, Fields Medalists, and influential figures in science, literature, and business.

### **Harvard University**

#### **History**
- Founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony, the first university in the U.S.
- Named after John Harvard, a Puritan clergyman who donated his library and funds.
- Known for its prestigious reputation, research output, and historic traditions.

#### **Campuses**
- **Cambridge Campus**: The main campus, centered on Harvard Yard, with historic buildings like Memorial Church and Massachusetts Hall.
- **Allston Campus**: Home to Harvard Business School, Innovation Labs, and athletics facilities.
- **Longwood Campus**: Located in Boston, housing the Schools of Medicine, Dental Medicine, and Public Health.
- **Other Sites**: Includes Dumbarton Oaks, Harvard Forest, and the Arnold Arboretum.

#### **Academics**
- **Schools**: Twelve schools, including the Harvard Medical School, Harvard Law School, and Harvard Business School.
- **Undergraduate and Graduate Programs**: Offers 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees.
- **Research**: One of the largest endowments in the world, valued at over $50 billion.
- **Libraries**: The largest academic library in the world, with over 20 million holdings.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Government**: Undergraduate Council and Graduate Council.
- **Student Media**: The Harvard Crimson, the university’s primary student newspaper.
- **Athletics**: Competitive Division I teams in the Ivy League, including the Harvard Crimson football team.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Includes 26 U.S. presidents, Nobel laureates, and prominent figures in science, business, and politics.
