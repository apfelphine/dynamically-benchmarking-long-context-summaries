# Peking University
Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking amid calls for educational reform, Peking University evolved through political upheavals, notably playing a central role in the 1919 May Fourth Movement advocating national sovereignty and academic freedom. The university survived World War II by relocating as part of the National Southwestern Associated University. In the 21st century, it has expanded with new campuses, research centers, and international collaborations, including notable laureates like Nobel Prize winner Tu Youyou. The main campus integrates traditional Chinese landscaping with modern facilities, and the university offers extensive academic programs across 55 schools, maintaining a strong research output and ranking among Asia's top universities. Its alumni include influential political leaders, scientists, writers, and entrepreneurs.

# Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)
Established in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne, RMIT evolved into a comprehensive public university by merging with institutes and expanding campuses across Melbourne and internationally, including Vietnam and Spain. The main City campus blends Victorian and modern architecture within Melbourne's urban fabric, supported by satellite campuses at Bundoora and Brunswick. The university operates through Higher Education and Vocational Education divisions, offering diverse programs across business, design, STEM, and vocational schools. RMIT emphasizes applied research and innovation, operates an extensive library system, and fosters a vibrant student life including the student union (RUSU), arts and sports collectives, and multiple media outlets. Its graduates enjoy strong employability and notable alumni span arts, sports, and public service.

# Harvard University
Founded in 1636 in Massachusetts, Harvard is the oldest U.S. university with deep colonial and religious roots evolving into a secular, prestigious research institution. Its main campus is in Cambridge with professional schools spread across Allston and Longwood. Governance is by the Harvard Corporation and Board of Overseers. Harvard holds the largest endowment globally (~$50.7 billion), enabling extensive research and financial aid. The university offers numerous undergraduate and graduate programs and is renowned for its faculty and alumni, including U.S. presidents and Nobel laureates. Harvard is highly ranked worldwide for research and global reputation. Recent events include leadership changes and contentious federal government investigations impacting funding and policies. Harvard maintains a broad legacy in academics, athletics, and cultural prominence.

# São Paulo State University (Unesp)
Founded in 1976 as a multi-campus system inspired by the University of California, Unesp coordinates 23 campuses across São Paulo state with faculties ranging from sciences and arts to engineering and health. It operates 1,900 laboratories and 30 libraries, including hospitals and clinics. Governance includes a rector chosen by the state governor from elected nominees and a University Council. Unesp offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 100 postgraduate courses with competitive admissions based on a rigorous exam (vestibular). It maintains numerous international partnerships and ranks highly among Brazilian and Latin American universities. Research focuses on areas like bioenergy and biotechnology, supported by 3,500 faculty and over 45,000 students.

# Boston College
Founded in 1863 by Jesuits to serve Irish Catholic immigrants in Boston, Boston College relocated to Chestnut Hill in the early 20th century. It expanded academically through multiple graduate schools and became fully coeducational by 1970. The university encompasses several campuses including Chestnut Hill, Newton, Brighton, and Brookline. Governed as a Jesuit Catholic institution, Boston College comprises nine schools including arts, management, nursing, and theater. It is nationally ranked among U.S. universities and has a rich tradition of student activism and cultural engagement. Its athletics teams, known as the Eagles, compete mainly in the ACC with notable sports programs and traditions. Boston College actively promotes diversity (e.g., AHANA program) and supports a variety of student media, clubs, and arts initiatives.

# University of the Andes (Colombia)
Founded in 1948 during Colombia's Liberal Republic period by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the Universidad de los Andes was established as a nonsectarian institution modeled after U.S. universities with a mission to develop skilled technical professionals and leaders for Colombia. It began with 80 students and quickly expanded despite Colombia’s political unrest. The university maintains political neutrality and has a governance structure including a Board of Trustees and Academic Council overseeing 14 academic units. The campus combines renovated historical and modern buildings in Bogotá. It offers interdisciplinary curricula and international exchange programs with over 119 partner universities. Alumni include prominent political figures, diplomats, scientists, and cultural personalities.

# University of Jordan
Established in 1962 in Amman to meet national educational demands, the University of Jordan (UJ) has expanded to include faculties of science, humanities, medicine, and law, with autonomous governance through a Board of Trustees and academic councils. Its main campus in Jubaiha and a branch campus in Aqaba offer extensive academic programs and research centers, including Jordan University Hospital, a major teaching hospital. UJ hosts over one million library items and numerous scientific journals. The university maintains numerous international affiliations, student unions, and notable alumni spanning government, academia, arts, and diplomacy.

# University of Sydney
Australia's first university, established in 1850, the University of Sydney is renowned for its historical Neo-Gothic architecture and expansive urban campus in Camperdown/Darlington. It has eight faculties encompassing arts, business, engineering, medicine, science, architecture, music, and law. The university is highly ranked globally and nationally, with a robust research profile and extensive library and museum collections. Its student body is represented by both an SRC and the University of Sydney Union (USU), providing wide-ranging social and cultural activities. The university has faced challenges including staff disputes, student protests, and concerns about sexual harassment, but remains a leading institution with significant alumni contributions.

# Lund University
Founded in 1666 in Sweden to integrate the Scania region into Sweden, Lund University is one of Scandinavia's oldest and largest universities. It operates campuses in Lund, Malmö, and Helsingborg and offers around 275 programs, many in English. The university is known for historic buildings and a vibrant student life centered on student nations and the Academic Society. Lund is a research leader in various fields, with notable contributions such as ultrasound and Bluetooth technology. It ranks in the top 100 globally and maintains strong international partnerships. Its alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates, renowned scientists, artists, and politicians.

# University of Auckland
Beginning as Auckland University College in 1883, the University of Auckland became independent in 1961 and is New Zealand's largest university. It comprises multiple campuses in Auckland plus one in Whangārei, offering diverse faculties including arts, business, engineering, law, medical sciences, and education. It has a strong research and innovation focus, including entrepreneurship centers and global partnerships. The university has a large and diverse student population, supported by the longstanding Auckland University Students' Association. Recent years have seen closures of satellite campuses, student protests, and legal challenges, but it remains the top-ranked university in New Zealand.

# Imperial College London
Formed in 1907 by royal charter, Imperial College London originated from major scientific and technical institutions in South Kensington, including the Royal College of Science and the Royal School of Mines. It is a world-leading research university specializing in science, engineering, medicine, and business, with campuses in South Kensington, White City, and Silwood Park. Imperial is famed for its cutting-edge research, large international student body, and entrepreneurial spirit. Governed by a council with a president and provost, it has multiple faculties and interdisciplinary research centers. Imperial is ranked among the world's best, with numerous Nobel laureates and prominent alumni, and offers vibrant student life through its union and numerous societies.

# University of Manchester
Tracing roots to 1824 with Owens College, the University of Manchester was formed in 2004 by merging Victoria University of Manchester and UMIST. It is a large, research-intensive university located in a central city campus fragmented across two main areas. It houses notable facilities like the John Rylands Library and Jodrell Bank Observatory. Manchester offers diverse faculties across science, engineering, medicine, humanities, and social sciences, with strong research missions and numerous Nobel laureates. The student body is diverse and engaged in active student unions and competitive sports. The university ranks in the top 50 globally and is known for significant contributions to science and humanities.

# University of Western Ontario (Western University)
Founded in 1878 in London, Ontario, Western University is a major Canadian research institution with historic roots in Anglican education. Its expansive campus includes Collegiate Gothic architecture and modern facilities, supporting over 30,000 undergraduates and 5,000 graduate students. Western operates multiple libraries, art galleries, and research parks, including a pioneering wind tunnel facility. It has faculties across arts, business, engineering, health sciences, law, and music. Governed by a Board of Governors and Senate, its student life includes active unions, media outlets, and athletic teams known as the Western Mustangs. Western ranks among Canada's top universities, with notable alumni in politics, science, arts, and business.

# State University of Campinas (Unicamp)
Founded in 1966 in São Paulo state, Brazil, Unicamp is a leading public research university with a large suburban campus designed to promote interdisciplinary collaboration. Initially centered on medical sciences, it rapidly expanded into diverse fields and includes satellite campuses in Limeira, Piracicaba, and Paulínia. The university offers 70 undergraduate and 153 graduate programs with admission through a highly competitive exam. Unicamp is governed autonomously with elected leadership and hosts extensive research, extension, and community outreach programs. Student life is vibrant though on-campus housing is limited, with many students living privately. Unicamp plays a key role in Brazil’s academic and scientific development and has a reputation for social inclusion initiatives.
