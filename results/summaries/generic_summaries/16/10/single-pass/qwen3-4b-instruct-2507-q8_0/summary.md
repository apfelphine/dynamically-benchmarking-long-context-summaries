### **Peking University**
- **History**: Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, it evolved through the Republican era, the May Fourth Movement, and WWII, eventually becoming the National University of Peking. Key figures like Cai Yuanpei transformed it into a modern institution. In the 21st century, it gained global recognition, notably with Professor Tu Youyou winning the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2015.
- **Campus & Facilities**: Located on former imperial gardens, the campus features traditional Chinese-style landscaping, including pagodas, gardens, and historical buildings. It hosts museums like the Arthur M. Sackler Museum and the Museum of University History.
- **Academics**: Offers 55 schools, 137 majors, and extensive research infrastructure, including 420 research institutions. It is ranked among the top universities in Asia, with QS and THE rankings placing it first in Asia.
- **International Engagement**: Maintains partnerships with Waseda University, the Open University, and various global institutions. It operates a global excellence strategy focused on innovation and diversity.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureate Tu Youyou, political leaders like Zhao Leji, and prominent scientists such as Qian Sanqiang, Deng Jiaxian, and Lin Yutang.

### **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**
- **History**: Originated in 1887 as the Working Men's College of Melbourne, it became the Royal Melbourne Technical College in 1954 and evolved into RMIT University in 1992 through a merger with the Phillip Institute of Technology.
- **Campuses**: Operates in Melbourne (City, Bundoora, Brunswick) and has international campuses in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi. Offers dual degree programs with Waseda University and partnerships with institutions in Europe and Asia.
- **Academics**: Offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 10,000 postgraduate programs. Known for applied research in design, technology, and health. Ranked in the top 200 globally by THE and QS.
- **Student Life**: Features a vibrant student union (RMIT Link), student media, and diverse cultural activities. Known for high graduate employability, with 84.7% employer satisfaction.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Australian skier Lydia Lassila, film director James Wan, and entrepreneur Jim Stynes.

### **Harvard University**
- **History**: Founded in 1636, it began as a Puritan institution and evolved into a global leader in higher education. Key developments include the expansion of women’s education, the adoption of secular ideals under Charles Eliot, and a shift toward research excellence.
- **Campuses**: The main campus in Cambridge, Massachusetts, includes Harvard Yard, the Memorial Church, and the John A. Paulson School of Engineering. Other campuses include Allston and Longwood.
- **Academics**: Offers 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees. Renowned for research in medicine, engineering, and social sciences. Ranked first in the world by THE and QS.
- **Reputation**: Holds the highest global university rankings, with a strong emphasis on research and academic freedom. Known for its liberal arts curriculum and influence in global education.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes U.S. Presidents John F. Kennedy and Franklin D. Roosevelt, Nobel laureates, and influential figures in science, politics, and business.

### **São Paulo State University (Unesp)**
- **History**: Founded in 1949, it emerged from a movement to modernize higher education in Brazil. The university expanded through the 1950s and 1960s, with campuses established in major cities.
- **Campuses**: Operates 23 campuses across São Paulo state, including Araçatuba, Araraquara, and Bauru. Features over 1,900 laboratories and 30 libraries.
- **Academics**: Offers 171 undergraduate programs and over 10,000 postgraduate programs. Known for research in bioenergy, nanotechnology, and climate change.
- **International Engagement**: Has 140 exchange programs with institutions in 52 countries. Ranked second among public universities in Brazil in 2012.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Brazilian politicians, scientists, and cultural figures such as César Gaviria and María Ángela Holguín.

### **Boston College**
- **History**: Founded in 1849 as a Jesuit institution, it moved to Chestnut Hill in 1907. Expanded through the 20th century with the addition of new schools and programs.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts, with additional campuses in Newton and Brighton. Features a strong religious and academic tradition.
- **Academics**: Offers nine schools, including the School of Business and the School of Law. Ranked 39th nationally in U.S. News & World Report.
- **Student Life**: Features a student union, a strong arts and cultural program, and a rich tradition of student activism. Known for its high graduate employability and student satisfaction.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes U.S. Senator John Kerry, U.S. Secretary of Energy Ernest Moniz, and actor Leonard Nimoy.

### **University of the Andes (Colombia)**
- **History**: Founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón to serve Colombia’s need for technically trained professionals. Developed a strong international reputation through its academic programs and research.
- **Campus**: Located in Bogotá, the campus features a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings and new facilities. Includes a central library, gym, and student housing.
- **Academics**: Offers 14 academic units and over 100 programs. Known for research in environmental science, engineering, and social sciences.
- **International Engagement**: Has academic exchange programs with over 119 universities in 34 countries. Ranked in the top 100 globally by THE.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Colombian politicians César Gaviria and María Ángela Holguín, as well as scientists and public figures.

### **University of Jordan**

### **University of Sydney**

### **Lund University**
- **History**: Founded in 1666 in Lund, Sweden, it evolved into one of Scandinavia’s leading research universities. Expanded significantly in the 20th century with the addition of new faculties and research centers.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Lund, with satellite campuses in Malmö and Helsingborg. Features a rich architectural heritage and a strong research culture.
- **Academics**: Offers over 275 programs and is known for research in neurology, nanotechnology, and climate change. Ranked 75th globally in the 2025 QS rankings.
- **Innovation**: Home to groundbreaking innovations such as medical ultrasound and the artificial kidney. Known for its strong ties to industry and public health.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates in physics and chemistry, and influential figures in science, politics, and business.

### **Imperial College London**
- **History**: Founded in 1907 as the Royal College of Science and Technology, it merged with medical schools in the 20th century and became fully independent in 2007.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in South Kensington, with a new White City campus focused on innovation and entrepreneurship. Features world-class research facilities.
- **Academics**: Offers 4 faculties and over 19,000 students. Known for research in engineering, medicine, and business. Ranked 2nd globally in the 2026 QS rankings.
- **Research & Innovation**: Home to the Francis Crick Institute and a strong focus on commercialization. Known for groundbreaking work in medicine and technology.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates in physics and chemistry, and influential figures in science and technology such as Sir Michael Uren and Sir Brian May.

### **University of Manchester**
- **History**: Traces its roots to the Mechanics' Institution (1824) and Owens College (1851). Merged with UMIST in 2004 to form the University of Manchester.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in Manchester, with satellite campuses in Fallowfield and the City. Features a strong focus on research and innovation.
- **Academics**: Offers 3 faculties and over 40,000 students. Known for research in engineering, medicine, and the social sciences.
- **Research & Innovation**: Home to the National Graphene Institute and a strong focus on industry partnerships. Ranked 3rd largest university in the UK.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureates Ernest Rutherford and Alan Turing, and influential figures in science and business such as Sir John B. Goodenough and Sir Richard Feynman.

### **University of Western Ontario**
- **History**: Founded in 1878 as the Western University of London, Ontario, it evolved into a major research university with affiliated colleges and a strong focus on science and medicine.
- **Campuses**: Main campus in London, Ontario, with satellite campuses in Huron and King's. Features a strong focus on research and community outreach.
- **Academics**: Offers 11 faculties and over 30,000 students. Known for research in life sciences, social sciences, and the humanities.
- **Student Life**: Features a large student union, over 200 student clubs, and a vibrant cultural scene. Known for high graduate employability and strong student engagement.
- **Notable Alumni**: Includes Nobel laureate Sir Frederick Banting, former UN Director-General Margaret Chan, and Canadian politicians such as John Robarts and David Peterson.
