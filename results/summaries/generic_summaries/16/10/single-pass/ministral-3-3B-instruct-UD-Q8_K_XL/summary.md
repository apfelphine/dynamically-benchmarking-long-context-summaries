### **Peking University**

#### **History**
Peking University (PKU) was founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking under the Hundred Days' Reform, inspired by intellectuals like Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao. Initially, it served as both an educational and administrative hub but faced closure after the conservative Empress Dowager Cixi’s coup. Renamed in 1912, it expanded under Cai Yuanpei, who introduced academic freedom and diverse faculty, including Hu Shih and Liang Shuming. PKU became a focal point for the 1919 May Fourth Movement, protesting the Treaty of Versailles, and later played a role in the establishment of the National Southwestern Associated University during WWII. In the 21st century, PKU gained global recognition, including the Nobel Prize for Tu Youyou in 2015 for her discovery of artemisinin.

#### **Campus**
PKU’s campus is situated in the former Qing dynasty imperial gardens, blending traditional Chinese architecture with modern facilities. Key features include Weiming Lake, the West Gate with its painted murals, and the university’s museums, such as the Museum of University History and the Arthur M. Sackler Museum of Art and Archaeology. The campus also houses the Peking University Health Science Center (PKUHSC) and has branches in Hanzhong, Shaanxi, and Shenzhen.

#### **Academics**
PKU comprises 55 schools and departments, offering 137 undergraduate majors, 59 master’s programs, and 59 doctoral programs. It boasts a vast library with over 8 million holdings and is a leader in research, with 420 research institutions, including 7 State Key Laboratories. The university is ranked among the top universities in Asia and globally, with consistent top-tier rankings in subjects like engineering, medicine, and social sciences.

#### **Notable People**
PKU has produced numerous influential figures, including Nobel laureate Tu Youyou, former Chinese Premier Li Keqiang, and renowned scholars like Hu Shih and Liang Shuming. The university also hosts the National School of Development, a top-tier think tank, and has strong ties with international institutions.

### **Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT)**

#### **History**
RMIT traces its origins to the Working Men's College of Melbourne, founded in 1887, which aimed to provide education to working-class individuals. It expanded into a technical college and later became the Royal Melbourne Technical College in 1954. RMIT University was established in 1992 after merging with the Phillip Institute of Technology. The university has since expanded globally, establishing campuses in Vietnam, Spain, and other regions.

#### **Campus**
RMIT’s main campus in Melbourne is a historic site with a mix of Victorian and interwar architecture. It includes the iconic Storey Hall, the Design Hub, and the Swanston Academic Building. RMIT also operates campuses in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, and Barcelona, Spain, and has partnerships with institutions in Hong Kong, Shanghai, Singapore, and Malaysia.

#### **Academics**
RMIT is known for its applied research and vocational education, with a focus on design, technology, health, globalization, and sustainability. It offers a wide range of programs, including undergraduate and postgraduate degrees, as well as vocational certificates and diplomas. RMIT is ranked among the top universities in Australia and globally, with a strong reputation in engineering, business, and design.

#### **Notable People**
RMIT has produced notable alumni in various fields, including Australia’s first female skier, Lydia Lassila, and renowned figures like James Wan, the director of films such as *Conan the Barbarian* and *The Conjuring*. RMIT also has a strong alumni community in Australia and internationally.

### **Harvard University**

#### **History**
Harvard University was founded in 1636 by the Massachusetts Bay Colony and named after John Harvard, a Puritan clergyman. It initially focused on training ministers and offered a classical curriculum. In the 19th century, Harvard expanded its academic freedom and diversified its faculty. The university became a leader in higher education, with notable figures like John F. Kennedy, Barack Obama, and Nobel laureates. Harvard’s reputation grew significantly in the 20th century, with its endowment becoming the largest in the world.

#### **Campus**
Harvard’s main campus in Cambridge, Massachusetts, is centered around Harvard Yard, featuring historic buildings like Massachusetts Hall and Memorial Church. The campus also includes the Harvard Business School, Kennedy School of Government, and the School of Engineering and Applied Sciences. Harvard has additional campuses in Allston, Longwood, and Dumbarton Oaks, Washington D.C.

#### **Academics**
Harvard is a large, highly residential research university offering 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees. It is renowned for its interdisciplinary approach, with a strong emphasis on research and innovation. Harvard’s libraries, including Widener Library, are among the largest in the world, and its museums, such as the Harvard Art Museums, are significant cultural institutions.

#### **Notable People**
Harvard has produced numerous influential alumni and faculty, including eight U.S. presidents, 162 Nobel laureates, and 24 Rhodes Scholars. Notable figures include John F. Kennedy, Barack Obama, and Stephen Hawking. The university is also known for its contributions to literature, science, and politics.

### **University of the Andes (Colombia)**

#### **History**
The University of the Andes was founded in 1949 as a non-sectarian and politically neutral institution. Its establishment was driven by the need for skilled professionals to support Colombia’s modernization and industrialization efforts. The university initially offered limited programs but expanded rapidly, becoming a key institution in Colombia’s higher education landscape. It has since gained international recognition for its academic excellence and contributions to research and innovation.

#### **Campus**
The University of the Andes campus is located in Bogotá, Colombia, within the city’s historical center. The campus includes a mix of renovated industrial and religious buildings, offering facilities such as classrooms, laboratories, and a central library. The university also operates an internship center for the Medicine Faculty in Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá.

#### **Academics**
The University of the Andes offers a wide range of undergraduate and postgraduate programs across various disciplines. It is known for its interdisciplinary approach, allowing students to design their own curricula. The university has strong international exchange programs and is recognized for its research contributions in fields like biology, medicine, and social sciences.

#### **Notable People**

The University of Sydney was founded in 1850 as the first public, non-denominational university in Australia. It was established to provide education to all classes, regardless of social status. The university has a rich history, with notable contributions from figures like John Dalton and John Anderson. The University of Sydney’s main campus is located in Camperdown and Darlington, within the inner-city suburbs of Sydney. It features beautiful architecture and a mix of historic and modern buildings. The University of Sydney offers a wide range of undergraduate and postgraduate programs across various faculties, including Arts, Engineering, Medicine, Science, and Law. ### **University of Manchester**

#### **History**
The University of Manchester traces its roots to the Mechanics’ Institution, founded in 1824. It was later renamed Owens College and became a constituent college of the Victoria University of Manchester in 1880. The university merged with the University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology (UMIST) in 2004, forming the current University of Manchester. It is known for its contributions to science, engineering, and the humanities.

#### **Campus**
The University of Manchester’s main campus is located in Manchester, UK, and includes historic buildings like the Old Quadrangle and the John Owens Building. The campus features the Manchester Museum, the Whitworth Art Gallery, and the Jodrell Bank Observatory.

#### **Academics**
The University of Manchester offers a wide range of undergraduate and postgraduate programs across various faculties, including Biology, Engineering, Medicine, and Humanities. It is known for its strong research output and international reputation. The university has a focus on innovation and has been a leader in research-intensive universities in the UK.

#### **Notable People**
The University of Manchester has produced numerous influential alumni and faculty, including Nobel laureates like Ernest Rutherford and Frederick Banting. Notable figures include former British Prime Minister, Tony Blair, and renowned scientist, Brian Cox.
