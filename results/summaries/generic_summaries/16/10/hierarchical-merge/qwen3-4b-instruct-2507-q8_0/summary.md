# **Merged Summary of Selected Universities**

## **Peking University (PKU)**  
Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking, Peking University is one of the world’s leading higher education institutions. Under President Cai Yuanpei, it championed academic freedom and intellectual diversity, playing a pivotal role in shaping modern Chinese thought and the 1919 May Fourth Movement. During WWII, it contributed significantly through the Southwestern Associated University. Today, PKU maintains its historic campus with traditional Chinese landscaping while expanding globally via initiatives such as the Oxford Campus, the Sino-Russian Mathematics Center, and new academic schools. It hosts world-class institutions like the National School of Development and the Shenzhen Graduate School, and its library is among the largest in Asia.  

Ranked 13th globally by Times Higher Education and 14th by QS World University Rankings (2025), PKU excels in teaching, research, and employability—particularly in Life Sciences & Medicine, Engineering, and Humanities. It leads in key subject areas, produces numerous Highly Cited Researchers, and has a high research output (notably in the Nature Index and CWTS Leiden Ranking). With 55 schools, 137 majors, and extensive research infrastructure, PKU remains a global hub for academic excellence and innovation. It fosters international collaboration through dual-degree programs with institutions like Waseda University, Cornell, and Yale, emphasizing creativity, leadership, openness, diversity, and global impact. Notable alumni include Nobel laureate Tu Youyou, politicians Li Keqiang and Zhao Leji, scientists Deng Jiaxian and Yu Min, business leader Robin Li, and influential figures such as Cai Yuanpei and Mao Zedong.

## **RMIT University**  
Established in 1992 through the merger of the Royal Melbourne Technical College and the Phillip Institute of Technology, RMIT is a public university in Victoria, Australia, governed by the RMIT Council and led by the Vice-Chancellor. It operates across two divisions—Higher Education and Vocational Education and Training (VET)—through four academic colleges: Business and Law, Design and Social Context, STEM, and Vocational Education. RMIT has international campuses in **Hanoi, Vietnam (since 2004)** and **Barcelona, Spain (2013)**, and maintains strategic partnerships in China, Singapore, and Malaysia. Renowned for applied research in design, technology, health, sustainability, and globalisation, RMIT hosts over 50 research centres and provides access to more than 19,000 peer-reviewed research records via the **RMIT Research Repository**, indexed by Google Scholar, the National Library of Australia, and WorldCat. Its **Informit database** is the largest in Australia and the Asia-Pacific region.  

RMIT ranks between **#125 and #301 globally (2024–2026)** and holds a national position of **10th to 20th in Australia**. Graduates demonstrate exceptional employability: **71.8% of undergraduates** and **98% of postgraduates** are employed, with average starting salaries of **A$66,800 and A$98,000**, respectively, and **84.7% employer satisfaction**. The university’s library network spans major campuses and includes specialized collections, with additional libraries in Vietnam. RMIT operates commercial subsidiaries such as **Informit, RMIT Publishing, and RMIT English Worldwide**, and has international entities in Vietnam and Spain. The **RMIT Gallery** in Storey Hall showcases contemporary art, design, and visual culture, managing key collections like those of Linsday Edwards and W. E. Macmillan. **RMIT Link** enhances campus life through arts, culture, and sports—including the Redbacks teams and free cinema—while **RUSU** represents students through departments in education, environment, international students, and LGBTQ+ rights, operating media outlets such as *The Swanston Gazette*, *Catalyst*, RMITV, and SYN. Student accommodation includes self-catered complexes and affiliated residential colleges. A vibrant graduation ceremony concludes with a city-wide procession at Federation Square, featuring a military escort. Notable alumni are active in film, sports, art, and business.

## **Harvard University**  
Founded in 1636, Harvard is a globally prestigious research university located in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Originally established to train ministers and advance Puritan learning, it evolved through 19th-century liberal reforms and the secularization championed by Charles W. Eliot into a modern institution emphasizing academic freedom and research. In 1999, Radcliffe College merged with Harvard, expanding its graduate and professional offerings. Today, Harvard offers 50 undergraduate majors, 134 graduate degrees, and 32 professional degrees, with small class sizes (median of 12 students) and a strong focus on residential learning and cutting-edge research in medicine and engineering. Governed by the Board of Overseers and the Harvard Corporation, it lacks publicly available statutes or an academic senate. With a $50.7 billion endowment as of 2023, Harvard maintains substantial investments in research and infrastructure.  

Harvard is consistently ranked at the top in international university rankings, renowned for its exceptional academic resources—including major libraries such as Houghton and Harvard-Yenching, and world-class museums like the Harvard Art Museums and the Museum of Comparative Zoology. It is a leader in research, innovation, and alumni achievement, with a student body that is diverse and affluent, supported by strong student governance and a prominent media presence. As a member of the Ivy League, Harvard is celebrated for its rich athletic history and notable rivalries with Yale, Cornell, and McGill. The university has produced an extraordinary number of influential alumni, including U.S. presidents, Nobel laureates, and global leaders, and is frequently depicted in popular culture as a symbol of elite education. It appears in significant literary and cinematic works such as those by Faulkner, Wolfe, and Marquand, and in films like *Love Story*, *The Paper Chase*, and *The Social Network*, often portraying academic pressure, student life, or social dynamics.  

Harvard faces ongoing political and legal challenges, including a 2023 union organizing effort, the resignation of President Claudine Gay over antisemitism and plagiarism, and a 2025 federal government crackdown under President Trump that led to a legal ruling declaring a freeze of $2 billion in research funding as illegal. Harvard continues to defend its academic autonomy amid rising political pressure, while student-led divestment campaigns have historically influenced its investment policies.

## **São Paulo State University (Unesp)**  
Founded in 1976, São Paulo State University (Unesp) is a leading public university in Brazil, modeled after the University of California system. With 23 campuses and 33 institutes across São Paulo, Unesp offers 171 undergraduate programs (covering 62 professions) and 118 postgraduate programs (117 master’s degrees, 6 professional master’s, and 93 doctorates), serving over 45,000 students annually. Admissions are conducted via the state-wide *vestibular* exam administered by VUNESP, with specialized exams for architecture, arts, and design; students apply to only one course. The university is governed by a rector elected every four years by academics and appointed by the state governor, with the University Council including institute directors and 10 student representatives. Unesp maintains strong international ties through 140 exchange agreements across 52 countries, including Argentina, Canada, China, France, Germany, and the United States, notably via its "International Courses" program (launched in 2013).  

Globally recognized, Unesp ranks 2nd among Brazilian public universities (2012), 11th best in emerging countries (THE, 2013), 3rd best in Brazil (ARWU 2017), and 8th in Latin America (Webometrics 2017). It is ranked 2nd in Brazil for international scientific publications (UNESCO, 2010). Research focuses on bioenergy, biotechnology, climate change, nanotechnology, pharmaceuticals, public policy, sea sciences, and stem cells. Notable alumni and faculty include Néle Azevedo, Ana Maria Braga, Mauro Galetti, and Antonio Candido.

## **Boston College**  
Founded in 1849 by the Jesuits in Boston, Massachusetts, Boston College began as a mission to serve Irish Catholic immigrants and received its university charter in 1863. It relocated to Chestnut Hill in 1907 under President Thomas I. Gasson, who envisioned it as a center of Jesuit scholarship. The university expanded academically in the 1920s, becoming coeducational in 1926 (full coeducation in 1970). Under President J. Donald Monan, it achieved financial stability and significantly grew its endowment through major capital campaigns. Under William P. Leahy, it addressed Catholic Church reforms and launched a $1.6 billion campus revitalization plan in 2009. The main campus in Chestnut Hill spans 175 acres and is accessible via the MBTA Green Line, with additional campuses in Newton, Brighton, and Brookline. Boston College comprises nine schools and colleges, emphasizing Catholic and Jesuit values and maintaining strong community ties.  

Ranked 37th in *U.S. News & World Report* and 88th in *Forbes*, with the Carroll School of Management ranked 3rd by *Bloomberg*, Boston College is accredited by the New England Commission of Higher Education. It supports extensive research through eight libraries housing over 12 million items. With a competitive 15.9% admit rate for the Class of 2028 and need-blind admissions for domestic applicants, the university fosters inclusivity via the AHANA initiative, which celebrates cultural diversity and has influenced other institutions. The campus is enriched by student publications such as *The Heights*, *The Gavel*, *The Torch*, and *The Stylus*, as well as WZBC radio and the yearbook *Sub Turri*. Boston College competes at the NCAA Division I level in the ACC, with notable achievements including five NCAA hockey titles and a Heisman Trophy winner, Doug Flutie. Traditions such as the fight song *"For Boston"*, the red bandana honoring Welles Crowther, and the Eagle mascot Baldwin are central to campus identity. The university has a legacy of activism, including protests over tuition in 1970, campaigns against racism in 2017–2018, and early advocacy for LGBT rights, leading to a revised non-discrimination policy in 2005. It uses gender-neutral terms such as "Heightswoman" and "Heightsonian" in place of the former "Heightsmen," with "Eagles" as the common nickname. "Golden Eagles" refer to 50-year alumni, while "Double Eagles" are those who earned both undergraduate and graduate degrees, and "Triple Eagles" attended Boston College High School. The university has over 179,000 alumni in 120+ countries, many of whom have received prestigious awards like Rhodes, Fulbright, and Truman Fellowships. Notable alumni include John Kerry, Ernest Moniz, Dannel Malloy, Margaret Heckler, Marty Walsh, Leonard Nimoy, Amy Poehler, and Chris O'Donnell.

## **University of the Andes (Colombia)**  
Founded in 1949 by Mario Laserna Pinzón, the University of the Andes (Colombia) is Colombia’s first nonsectarian, non-political university, modeled after American institutions to produce skilled professionals through liberal and technical education. Starting with 80 students, it expanded through the "three/two" program (3 years in Colombia, 2 in the U.S.), supported by Colombian industries, philanthropies, and ICETEX. The university operates under a governance structure including a Board of Trustees, Board of Directors, Rector, Academic Council, and Deans. Legally established in 1948 and granted university status in 1964, its statutes were last amended in 2009. As of 2021, it employs 745 non-faculty and 853 administrative staff, with assets valued at $426.1 million (COL$1.74 trillion). The student body is 55% male and 45% female, with 95% of undergraduates being top 1% high school graduates.  

The campus, located in Bogotá’s historic center, includes renovated religious and industrial buildings, 116 classrooms, 219 labs, a central library, and 26 computer rooms. It offers an interdisciplinary curriculum across four academic cycles and operates 14 academic units, including 10 faculties and interdisciplinary centers. The university maintains exchange programs with over 119 institutions worldwide and celebrates its mascot, Seneca the goat, annually. Notable alumni include former Colombian presidents and prominent professionals.

## **University of Jordan**  
Established on 2 September 1962 and inaugurated on 17 April 1965, the **University of Jordan** is the first and foremost university in Jordan, playing a central role in the nation’s educational and cultural development. Beginning with 167 students, eight faculty members, and a single Faculty of Arts, it expanded to include faculties in science, economics, medicine, engineering, law, and more. It pioneered the credit hour system (the first in the Arab world), hosted the first Jordanian theater production, and achieved academic autonomy. From 1970 to 1990, it grew significantly with the addition of ten faculties and research centers, supported by increased government funding. It regained full academic autonomy in 1998 and now offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical fields.  

Notable institutions include the ABET-accredited School of Engineering, the AACSB-accredited School of Sharia, Business, and Law, and five medical faculties—Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Dentistry, and Rehabilitation Sciences—established between 1971 and 1999. Jordan University Hospital (founded 1973) is a leading teaching and research hospital with JCI and HACCP accreditations. The University of Jordan Library (founded 1962) holds over a million items, including rare manuscripts and international documents, and serves 11,000 users daily. It hosts research centers such as the Institute of Archaeology, the Jordan Academy of Arabic, and the Center for Strategic Studies Jordan. The university publishes peer-reviewed journals through Jordan University Press and ranks among the top institutions in Jordan and the Arab world in global and regional rankings (QS, THE, ARWU). It maintains over 100 student exchange agreements, primarily with U.S. institutions, and has produced over 159,000 graduates, including Nobel-nominated mathematician Shaher Momani, scholars Mohammad Shaheen and Rula Quawas, political leaders Bisher Khasawneh and Rami Hamdallah, UN Women Executive Director Sima Sami Bahous, U.S. ambassador John Abizaid, cancer researcher Lubna Tahtamouni, poet Ayman Otoom, and Olympic taekwondo champion Ahmad Abughaush. Key campus landmarks include the Clock Tower, Green Lawn, the Archaeological Museum, Foreign Languages Garden, University Stadium, and a Hejaz Railway coach. The university celebrated its Golden Jubilee in 2012 and has made significant contributions in arts, literature, STEM, and public service across the Arab world and globally.  

A new women’s dormitory is under construction in 2024, and the university operates an Aqaba campus, established in 2009 to support regional development.
