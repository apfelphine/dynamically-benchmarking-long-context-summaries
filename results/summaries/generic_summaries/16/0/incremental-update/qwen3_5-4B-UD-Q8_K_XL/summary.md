### University of Jordan: History, Expansion, and Academic Scope

Founded in 1962 as Jordan's second public library, the University of Jordan (UJ) is a leading academic institution in the Arab world, consistently ranking among the top universities in the region. Established as a national university by King Hussein bin Talal, it began teaching in December 1965 with a single Faculty of Arts to provide domestic higher education and reduce reliance on studying abroad.

**Expansion and Infrastructure**
Driven by the goal of fostering national development, UJ has undergone significant expansion. By 1990, it had added ten new faculties—including Medicine, Engineering, and Law—and established the Jordan University Hospital (JUH) in 1975, the country's first university teaching hospital with international accreditations. Today, the university operates across two main campuses: the Jubaiha Campus in Amman (covering 1,200 dunums) and the Aqaba Campus, which opened in 2009 to spur regional development and features modern facilities, including ongoing construction of women's dormitories. Key landmarks include the Clock Tower and the specialized schools that define the campus environment.

**Academic Scope and Research**
UJ boasts extensive facilities, including the University of Jordan Library (UJL), which houses over one million items and serves 11,000 users daily. The institution is a hub for scientific research, holding unique collections such as the world's only discovered fossil bones of *Arambourgiania* and publishing peer-reviewed journals in medical, social, and agricultural sciences. In 2024, UJ ranked 498th globally in the QS World University Rankings and 801–900th in the Shanghai Academic Ranking. It currently offers 78 undergraduate and 143 postgraduate programs across scientific, humanitarian, and medical streams.

**International Engagement and Impact**
Committed to international collaboration, UJ is a member of global networks like the International Association of Universities and the Association of Arab Universities. It actively engages with international bodies to publish high-impact journals. The university has graduated over 159,000 students and is renowned for producing notable alumni in politics, science, academia, arts, and journalism. Distinctive figures include former Prime Ministers Bisher Khasawneh, Marouf al-Bakhit, and Fayez al-Tarawneh; diplomats John Abizaid and Hoshyar Zebari; international leaders such as Turkey's former PM Ahmet Davutoğlu and UN Women's Executive Director Sima Sami Bahous; and celebrated scholars, artists, and journalists like Wadah Khanfar. Jordan, where UJ is headquartered, maintains the highest number of researchers per million people in the Arab world, reflecting the institution's profound impact on the region.
