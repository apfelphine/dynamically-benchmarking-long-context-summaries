The transhumanist movement, rooted in Enlightenment ideals, explores speculative advancements like body augmentation and cognitive enhancements while addressing **procreative liberty** and the risks of a **"two-species divide."** Institutional efforts such as **Humanity+ (2006)** and the **Longevity Party (2012)** reflect its institutional growth, though democratic participation and inequality remain unresolved challenges.

**AI ethics** has evolved from foundational debates (e.g., Asimov’s *Three Laws*, Mary Shelley’s *Frankenstein*) to critical concerns over **accountability, Friendly AI alignment, and moral agency**, with frameworks like the **EU’s AI Act** and **Hague Summit 2023** attempting to address fairness issues. Persistent challenges include **human bias**, while advancements in **embodied cognition** (4E cognition)—integrating brain-body-environment dynamics—expand applications in **education, robotics, and clinical settings** (e.g., *Atlas* robots, autism therapies).

The **technological singularity**, first theorized in the 1950s by Turing and von Neumann, gained traction in the 1980s through works like **Vinge’s (1983–1988) "intellectual transition"** and **Solomonoff’s (1985) "infinity point"**—where self-improving AI could exponentially accelerate. By the 1990s, thinkers like **Minsky and Tipler** discussed superintelligent machines, while **Kurzweil’s 2005 book** (*The Singularity Is Near*) and **Bill Joy’s warnings** (2000s) highlighted risks of AI-driven job displacement. Institutional responses emerged by the 2000s, including **Singularity University (2009)** and **Obama’s 2016 acknowledgment** of AI’s economic impact. Definitions vary: **Vinge** focuses on rapid AI intelligence explosion, while **Kurzweil** emphasizes gradual exponential growth.

**Immortality and aging** remain central to transhumanist and singularity debates, spanning **nanotech cell repair**, **mind uploading**, and **digital ascension**. Historical roots trace back to **Condorcet (1780s)**, Campbell’s sci-fi (1932), and foundational works like **Moravec’s "intelligence explosion"** (1977/1988) and **Good’s (1965) predictions of human obsolescence**, now intertwined with **AI risks, societal evolution, and speculative immortality**. Governance frameworks are critical to navigating these emerging challenges.
