# Technology Ethics, Cognition, and Advanced AI

### AI Ethics and Societal Risk
AI systems present significant ethical challenges, primarily algorithmic bias, where flaws in training data perpetuate societal inequities in areas such as hiring and criminal justice. Addressing this necessitates defining and measuring fairness and deploying mitigation strategies across the AI development pipeline. Further governance issues include ensuring accountability for autonomous systems and increasing transparency via Explainable AI (XAI) to counter the "black box" problem.

The concept of the technological singularity describes a hypothetical, irreversible "intelligence explosion" where AI achieves superintelligence capable of recursive self-improvement beyond human control. The major concern associated with this is the existential risk stemming from misalignment—ensuring such superintelligence adheres to human values.

### Intelligence and Cognition
Philosophical inquiry into true machine intelligence questions whether symbol manipulation equates to genuine understanding. This debate is informed by the concept of embodied cognition, which argues that mental processes are shaped by the dynamic interaction between the brain, body, and environment, suggesting that physical experience grounds mental states.

Separately, Transhumanism promotes the radical enhancement of human capabilities through technology, including AI and biotechnology, though this raises ethical debates regarding social stratification and human identity. Modern evaluations of intelligence are moving beyond simple conversational tests to assess novel concept generation.
