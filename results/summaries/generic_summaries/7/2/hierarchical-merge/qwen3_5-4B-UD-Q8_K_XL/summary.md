### Comparative Overview of Global Education Systems

Germany operates under a decentralized federal model combining tracked secondary education with a robust dual vocational system and accessible public universities. While this structure drives high performance and adult re-entry, it faces challenges including limited English proficiency, regional disparities, and systemic barriers like strict entry limits that exclude socioeconomic groups. Ideological deadlock has prevented formal affirmative action, leaving issues regarding parental influence and early performance unresolved.

China has shifted from a post-conflict literacy drive to a modernization strategy characterized by near-universal elementary enrollment but significant urban-rural divides. Despite reforms such as the "Double Reduction" policy, the system remains dominated by the high-stakes *Gaokao* exam and political alignment, resulting in faculty shortages, a memorization-focused curriculum, and a mismatch between domestic degrees and job markets. India confronts a dual crisis of expanding access and declining quality within a framework where states retain significant autonomy, struggling with low secondary retention, teacher absenteeism, and severe infrastructure deficits.

### The Greek Education System

Greece maintains a highly structured curriculum unified under the Hellenic Qualifications Framework (HQF), an eight-level system aligned with European standards. The secondary phase consists of free, compulsory *Gymnasium* (ages 12–15) followed by the non-compulsory *Lyceum* (ages 15–18), where students specialize in Humanities, Science, or Economic/Computer Studies. A distinctive feature is the early emphasis on foreign languages starting in kindergarten and a unique assessment approach that delays formal grading until the third year of primary school.

Higher education admission is primarily determined by the Pan-Hellenic Examinations, which weigh exam scores against high school GPA, though specialized athletes and vocational graduates may bypass these requirements. Significant restructuring occurred since the 1930s, notably the integration of former Technological Educational Institutes (TEIs) into the current tertiary structure between 2013 and 2019. While diverse pathways exist, including public universities, military academies, and vocational schools, the system faces severe criticism regarding corruption, safety, and legal compliance, with investigations revealing embezzlement, educator misconduct, and constitutional violations.
