## Global Education Systems Overview

### Comparison of National Systems
Educational structures vary significantly across nations. Greece has a nine-year compulsory education starting with preschool, requiring Modern Greek and English, and features specialized tracks like vocational high schools, though it faces issues with corruption. China's system is centrally governed by state policy, mandating nine years of compulsory education and striving for modernization to integrate vocational and lifelong learning, yet it is characterized by intense, high-stakes examinations and strict CCP censorship.

India presents a diverse system, reformed under the 2020 NEP to adopt a 5+3+3+4 structure, with vocational education being integrated. Despite mandates for free education up to age 14, India struggles with infrastructural deficits, rote learning, high dropout rates, and rural/gender disparities. Germany operates a decentralized model where compulsory schooling extends to age 16, channeling students into academic or vocational tracks (*Duale Ausbildung*).

### Common Challenges in Education
Many global systems grapple with significant inequalities. China suffers from disparities in quality between urban and rural areas, while India contends with infrastructural and quality deficits across its varied landscape. Furthermore, issues like rote memorization, gender inequality, and the challenges faced by vulnerable populations (such as migrant children in China) persist across different educational contexts.
