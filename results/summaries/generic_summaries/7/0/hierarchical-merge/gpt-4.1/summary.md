## Greece

Greece enforces nine years of compulsory schooling, emphasizing foreign language acquisition and evolving assessment methods to align with the Hellenic Qualification Framework and European standards. Upper secondary education offers general and vocational tracks with apprenticeships leading to recognized qualifications. Public higher education is largely tuition-free, though regulatory, disciplinary, and corruption issues persist. Ongoing reforms seek to modernize curricula, enhance practical skills, diversify pathways, and foster civic engagement.

## India

India’s education system is overseen by both central and state authorities, with free public schooling available until age 14. The 2020 National Education Policy introduced a flexible structure and modernized curriculum, but quality, equity, infrastructure, and teacher absenteeism—especially in rural and marginalized areas—remain challenges. Programs like Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan and the Midday Meal Scheme have improved access, yet persistent gender and socioeconomic gaps endure. The country’s higher education includes globally renowned institutions, but faces issues in accreditation, politicization, and labor market relevance, prompting continued reforms for inclusiveness and international engagement.

## Germany

Germany’s state-managed education system features varied school structures and curricula, with compulsory education lasting from ages 6 to 16. Secondary tracks lead to academic or vocational specialization, highlighted by a robust dual vocational training model. Public universities are tuition-free, and literacy levels are high; however, university access and achievement remain tied to socioeconomic status. Reforms focus on closing achievement gaps and addressing persistent inequalities.

## China

China has built an expansive education system since the 1980s, with nine years of compulsory, tuition-free schooling and high government investment. Standardized curricula emphasize Chinese, mathematics, English, sciences, and political-moral education, with efforts to promote creativity and integrate ICT and modern infrastructure. State control over curricula and restricted academic freedom persist. Vocational, technical, private, and international schools add structural diversity, but rural and minority regions face ongoing disparities. Higher education has scaled rapidly, but faces funding, quality, and equity issues, addressed through reforms in teacher training and targeted investments. Despite overall progress and outstanding international performance by urban students, China continues to struggle with regional gaps, barriers for disadvantaged groups, mental health challenges, and discrimination.

## Comparative Insights

Across Greece, India, Germany, and China, education reform targets modernization, equity, and workforce relevance. Germany excels in literacy and vocational integration, while India implements broad policy reforms to tackle deep-seated issues. Greece and China focus on expanding access and modernizing systems. Nevertheless, all four nations face disparities, infrastructure challenges, and the need to adapt to changing educational and labor market demands.
