# Global Education System Overviews

## Country-Specific Systems
**Germany** operates a decentralized system managed by 16 states, offering varied pathways from preschool onward. Secondary education involves early tracking into specialized schools or the *Duale Ausbildung* vocational training. While public university tuition is typically free, academic outcomes are influenced by socioeconomic status.

**Greece** maintains a comprehensive structure aligned with the EQF, where secondary education progresses through *Gymnasium* and *Lyceum*. University admission relies on standardized "Pan-Hellenic Examinations." However, the Greek system struggles with issues including corruption, student bullying, and tuition fees.

**China** utilizes a centrally controlled system that emphasizes science and technology to support economic goals. Its structure is defined by the intensely competitive *Gaokao* for university entry, though it faces challenges concerning regional disparities and moving past rote learning.

**India**'s education structure is governed by both Central and State governments, guided by the NEP 2020, which advocates for a "5+3+3+4" framework and instruction in the mother tongue early on. Systemic hurdles include resource scarcity, digital inequities, and corruption.

## Systemic Challenges in Higher Education
Across various nations, higher education access is often mediated by high-stakes testing, leading to societal pressures. In India, this pressure around fields like medicine and engineering is amplified by expensive private coaching, creating significant financial and mental health burdens.

General challenges observed include achievement gaps correlated with socioeconomic status (Germany), skills gaps between graduate competency and industry needs (India), and the need to balance academic rigor with vocational relevance globally.
