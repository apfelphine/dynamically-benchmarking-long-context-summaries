## Comparative Overview of Education Systems

**Global Literacy Benchmarks:**
A comparative dataset is available detailing literacy rates for various countries and global categories, including data for the 15+ and 15-24 age groups. For example, India's 15+ literacy rate is cited at 73.4%, benchmarked against World averages and statistics for developing/Sub-Saharan Africa.

**National Education System Profiles:**

**Indian Education:**
India is reforming under the National Education Policy 2020 (NEP 2020), shifting its structure from "10+2" to "5+3+3+4" and prioritizing mother tongue instruction until at least Class 8. NEP 2020 also modernizes assessment by introducing scheduled exams (Classes 2, 5, 8) and having PARAKH handle Class 10/12 board exams. Higher education features flexible, multi-disciplinary degrees with multiple exit options. Legally, free and compulsory education (ages 6-14) is guaranteed under Article 21A and 46. Despite reforms, challenges persist, including regional/gender literacy/STEM disparities, poor infrastructure, high student-teacher ratios, and the dominance of rote learning. Funding issues and private expenditure gaps in rural areas exacerbate access problems. Systemic issues include corruption, high-stakes exam coaching, and limited scope for "inclusive education" beyond children with disabilities. Oversight is managed by bodies such as NCERT, CBSE, CISCE, and UGC.

**Chinese Education:**
China's CCP-managed system is reforming under "Double First-Class Construction" to address regional inequality, teacher shortages, and academic pressure, while managing the pressures of the *gaokao* culture, ideological constraints, and the *Hukou* system.

**German Education:**
Germany operates a decentralized, free system until age 18, heavily stressing vocational training (*Duale Ausbildung*). University entry is secured via the *Abitur* or *NC* at *Universitäten* and *Fachhochschulen*, blending Humboldt and Bologna models, with financial aid available through *BAföG*. Key difficulties include socioeconomic stratification and variations in *Gymnasium* streaming.

**Greek Education:**
The Greek tiered system moves from primary (*Dimotiko*) through compulsory lower secondary (*Gymnasium*, ages 12-15) to non-compulsory upper secondary (*Lyceum*, ages 15-18), incorporating early English. Secondary tracks include General ($\Gamma\text{E}.\Lambda.$), Vocational (EPAL), and EPAS Apprenticeship, all aligning with the HQF. Tertiary access requires the Lyceum Certificate and performance in national "Pan-Hellenic Examinations," amid ongoing corruption concerns.
