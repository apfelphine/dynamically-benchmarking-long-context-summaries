## **University of Chicago**

### **History**
The University of Chicago traces its origins to the **Old University of Chicago**, founded in 1857 by Baptist educators and Senator Stephen A. Douglas. After financial struggles and the Great Chicago Fire, it closed in 1886. In 1890, a new institution was established, initially coeducational and funded by John D. Rockefeller and Marshall Field. William Rainey Harper became its first president, shaping its academic rigor and research focus. The university adopted the Latin motto *Crescat scientia; vita excolatur* ("Let knowledge grow from more and more; and so be human life enriched") in 1911.

During World War I, faculty like Albion Small and Paul Shorey supported the war, while student participation was mixed. Post-war, the university expanded its research, including the founding of the **Oriental Institute** and the **Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures**. The **Chicago School of Thought** emerged in the social sciences, with the **Social Science Research Council** established in 1923.

In the 1920s, Robert Maynard Hutchins became president, implementing a new core curriculum and reorganizing graduate education. The university faced financial challenges during the Great Depression but later thrived under Hutchins’ leadership. Post-WWII, it became a hub for scientific innovation, including the **Metallurgical Laboratory’s** role in the Manhattan Project, where Enrico Fermi engineered the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction.

The university’s reputation grew through academic reforms, including the **New Plan** (1931) and the establishment of the **Enrico Fermi Institute** and **James Franck Institute**. In the 1960s, student activism led to the **Kalven Report**, emphasizing academic freedom and non-partisanship. The university’s **Quadrangles** were recognized as one of the most beautiful college campuses in the U.S. by *Travel+Leisure*.

In the 21st century, the university continued to innovate, with expansions like the **Mansueto Library** (2011) and the **Keller Center** (2019). It also faced financial strain, leading to budget cuts and debates over student-faculty ratios.

---

### **Campus**
The main campus spans **217 acres** in Hyde Park and Woodlawn, Chicago, divided by the Midway Plaisance. The **Main Quadrangles**, designed by Henry Ives Cobb, feature Gothic Revival architecture. Notable buildings include **Mitchell Tower** (inspired by Oxford’s Magdalen Tower) and **Rockefeller Chapel** (neo-Gothic style). The campus includes the **Chicago Pile-1** site, a National Historic Landmark, and **Robie House**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Transportation is provided by the **CTA Red Line, Green Line, and Metra**, along with campus shuttles. Safety measures include increased patrols and security cameras. The university also operates **satellite campuses** in Hong Kong, London, Paris, and New Delhi, as well as research facilities like **Argonne National Laboratory** and **Fermilab**.

---

### **Academics**
The University of Chicago is a **research-intensive institution** with a **quarter system** (four terms: Summer, Autumn, Winter, Spring). It offers **51 undergraduate majors** and **33 minors**, divided into four divisions: Biological Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences, and Humanities. The **core curriculum** includes 15 courses across seven subjects and a foreign language proficiency requirement.

Graduate programs span **10,546 students** across 4 divisions (biological sciences, humanities, physical sciences, social sciences) and 7 professional schools. The university is classified as an **"R1: Doctoral University – Very High Research Activity"** by the Carnegie Classification. Key research areas include **physics, chemistry, sociology, and economics**, with notable contributions to the **Chicago School of Economics** and **sociology**.

The university is home to **140+ research centers**, including the **Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures** and the **Becker Friedman Institute**. It is also a founding member of the **Association of American Universities** and collaborates with institutions like **Argonne National Laboratory** and **Fermilab**.

### **Administration and Finance**
The university is governed by a **Board of Trustees**, with the president overseeing academic and financial affairs. The **endowment** was valued at **$10.1 billion** in 2024, the 21st-largest among U.S. educational institutions. The university employs **3,418 academic staff** and **23,217 administrative staff**, with a combined annual budget of **$5.2 billion**.

Recent financial challenges led to budget cuts and debates over debt accumulation. The university has faced criticism for increasing student-faculty ratios and reducing research faculty involvement.

### **Student Body and Admissions**
The university enrolled **7,569 undergraduates** and **10,968 graduate students** in fall 2024. The **Class of 2025** is **53% male and 47% female**, with **27% Asian, 19% Hispanic, and 10% Black**. The university is **need-blind** for domestic applicants, with an acceptance rate of **4.7%** (2023). The **middle 50% SAT scores** for undergraduates are **1510–1570**, and the **average MCAT score** for medical students is **519**.

The university has a **highly residential** culture, with **58% of undergraduates living on campus**. Student life includes **400+ clubs**, a **student government**, and **Greek organizations**. The **University of Chicago Scavenger Hunt** and **Kuviasungnerk/Kangeiko** (winter festival) are notable traditions.

## **King’s College London**

### **History**
King’s College London was founded in **1829** as a rival to **University College London (UCL)** in response to religious and social tensions. It was named after **King George IV** and initially focused on **Anglican education**, though it allowed nonconformists to enroll. The college opened in **1831** with **William Otter** as its first principal.

In **1835**, King’s became affiliated with the **University of London**, which granted degrees. The college expanded its medical and military departments, and in **1845**, it established a **Military Department** to train officers. In **1846**, a **Theological Department** was created to train Anglican priests.

The **King’s College London Act of 1882** transformed the college into a **non-profit institution** and extended its educational opportunities for women. In **1903**, religious tests were abolished for staff, except in the Theological Department. During **World War I**, King’s opened its medical school to women, and in **1916**, Linetta de Castelvecchio became the first woman to head the **Department of Italian**.

In **1918**, King’s merged with **UCL**, but regained legal independence in **1980**. The college underwent several mergers, including with **Queen Elizabeth College (1985)** and **Chelsea College (1985)**, and with the **Institute of Psychiatry (1997)**. In **2006**, King’s was granted independent degree-awarding powers.

Today, King’s is a **global leader in research and education**, with a reputation for excellence in **law, medicine, and social sciences**.

- **Denmark Hill Campus**: Houses the **Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology & Neuroscience (IoPPN)**.

King’s also operates **halls of residence**, **sports grounds**, and a **Defence Academy of the United Kingdom** in **Shrivenham**. The university is a member of the **Russell Group** and the **Institutional Network of the Universities from the Capitals of Europe (UNICA)**.

## **Zhejiang University**

### **History**
Zhejiang University was founded in **1897** as **Qiushi Academy** in Hangzhou, China, by Mayor Lin Qi. It was renamed **Chekiang University** in 1902 and later **National Chekiang University (NCKU)** in 1928. During the **Second Sino-Japanese War**, the university relocated to **Meitan, Guizhou**, where it played a key role in preserving Chinese cultural artifacts.

After the **Chinese Civil War (1949)**, the university was renamed **Zhejiang University** and became a **Soviet-style polytechnic institution**, focusing on engineering and natural sciences. During the **Cultural Revolution (1966–1976)**, the university was shut down, and research and education were severely disrupted.

In the **1980s**, Zhejiang University began to reform, adopting a **multidisciplinary approach** and expanding its research capabilities. In **1995**, it was selected into **Project 211**, a national program to develop world-class universities. In **1998**, it merged with **four other universities** to form the **new Zhejiang University**, which became one of China’s most prestigious institutions.

Today, Zhejiang University is a **global leader in research and education**, with a reputation for excellence in **engineering, medicine, and social sciences**.

## **Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)**

### **History**
MIT was founded in **1861** as a **land-grant institution** to promote **industrial science and technology**. The first classes were held in **1865** in the **Mercantile Building** in Boston. In **1912**, MIT acquired its current campus in **Cambridge**, designed by **William W. Bosworth**.

During **World War II**, MIT played a crucial role in **military research**, including the **Radiation Laboratory** and the **Manhattan Project**. The university also contributed to **post-war technological advancements**, such as the **development of the first digital computer (Whirlwind)** and the **World Wide Web**.

In the **1960s and 1970s**, MIT faced protests over its involvement in **military research**, including **helicopters, smart bombs, and nuclear missiles**. Despite these challenges, the university continued to grow, with **OpenCourseWare (OCW)** launched in **2002** to make course materials freely available online.

Today, MIT is a **global leader in research and innovation**, with a reputation for excellence in **engineering, science, and technology**.
