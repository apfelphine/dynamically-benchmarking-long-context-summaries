### **Zhejiang University**

#### **History**
Zhejiang University traces its origins to **Qiushi Academy (1897)**, established by Hangzhou’s mayor Lin Qi. After reforms in 1902, it became **Chekiang University (1902)** and **Chekiang Higher Institute (1903)**. The university faced closure due to political turmoil in 1914. In 1928, it was revived as **National Chekiang University (NCKU)** under the Kuomintang (KMT) and later became **Zhejiang University (ZJU)** after 1947. During the **Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945)**, ZJU relocated to Guizhou, China. Under Mao Zedong’s rule (1949–1976), ZJU underwent significant restructuring, shifting focus to engineering and Soviet-style education. The **Cultural Revolution (1966–1976)** disrupted operations, but the university resumed activities post-1978.

#### **Campuses**
ZJU operates seven main campuses:
- **Zijingang Campus** (Hangzhou): Core academic and residential hub.
- **Yuquan Campus** (Hangzhou): Historical and administrative center.
- **Xixi Campus** (Hangzhou): Focuses on arts and sciences.
- **Huajiachi Campus** (Hangzhou): Agriculture and engineering.
- **Zhijiang Campus** (Hangzhou): Law and humanities.
- **Zhoushan Campus** (Zhoushan Island): Ocean College.
- **Haining International Campus** (Haining): International collaborations.
- **Ningbo Campus** (Ningbo): Newly established.

#### **Academics**
ZJU is a **comprehensive research university** with **11 branches of learning**: philosophy, literature, history, education, science, economics, law, management, engineering, agriculture, and medicine. It offers **bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees** across 20 schools and 17 administrative departments. The university is ranked among the **top 10 globally** in several fields, including engineering, computer science, and medicine.

#### **Admissions and Rankings**
- **Admissions**: Highly competitive, with a rigorous selection process.
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the **top 20 globally** (e.g., **QS World University Rankings 2025**).
- **Research**: Strong focus on **engineering, medicine, and computer science**, with over **4,000 faculty members** and **11 State Key Laboratories**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: 65,821 full-time students (2023), with a diverse demographic.
- **Traditions**: Notable traditions include the **"Qiushi College"** (liberal arts education) and **"Chu Kochen Honors College"** (elite undergraduate program).
- **Organizations**: Over **600 student organizations**, including academic, cultural, and athletic groups.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Includes **Chen Duxiu** (co-founder of the Chinese Communist Party), **Li Qiang** (current Premier of China), and **Huang Fu** (former President of the Republic of China).
- **Faculty**: Notable figures include **Shing-Tung Yau** (Fields Medalist) and **Su Buqing** (mathematician).

### **University of Chicago**

#### **History**
Founded in **1890** by the American Baptist Education Society, the University of Chicago began as a coeducational institution. It was initially funded by **John D. Rockefeller** and later became a center for **social sciences and liberal arts education**. Key figures include **William Rainey Harper** (first president) and **Robert Maynard Hutchins** (famous for his educational reforms). The university played a pivotal role in **World War II** through its research and development, including contributions to the **Manhattan Project**. In the 1960s, it faced protests over its involvement in the Vietnam War. The university has since expanded its global presence with campuses in **Hong Kong, London, and Paris**.

#### **Campuses**
- **Main Campus**: Located in Hyde Park, Chicago, featuring iconic buildings like **Rockefeller Chapel** and **Mitchell Tower**.
- **Satellite Campuses**: Includes **Booth School of Business** (Hong Kong, London, Chicago) and **Center in Paris**.

#### **Academics**
- **Structure**: Organized into the **College**, **four graduate divisions**, **seven professional schools**, and the **Graham School of Continuing Liberal and Professional Studies**.
- **Admissions**: Highly selective, with an acceptance rate of **4.7%** (2023).
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the **top 10 globally** (e.g., **QS World University Rankings 2025**).
- **Research**: Strong focus on **social sciences, economics, and natural sciences**, with over **$423 million spent on research in 2018**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: 7,569 undergraduates, 10,968 graduates, and 750 non-degree students (2024).
- **Traditions**: Includes **"Hail, Chicago, Hail!"** (campus anthem), **"Big Game"** (football rivalry with Northwestern), and **"Latke-Hamantash Debate"** (annual debate on Jewish traditions).
- **Organizations**: Over **400 student organizations**, including academic, cultural, and athletic groups.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Includes **Nobel laureates** like **Robert Millikan** (Physics) and **John D. Rockefeller** (business).
- **Faculty**: Notable figures include **Robert Maynard Hutchins** (education reformer) and **Vannevar Bush** (scientist and WWII leader).

### **King’s College London**

#### **History**
Founded in **1829** as **King’s College London**, it was established in response to the **Free Church Movement** and the need for a secular university. The college was named after **King George IV** and initially focused on **medicine, law, and theology**. It played a significant role in the **discovery of DNA** and has been a leader in **medical education and research**. King’s College London has undergone several mergers, including with **Queen Elizabeth College** and **Chelsea College**, and gained **independent degree-awarding powers** in 2006.

#### **Campuses**
- **Strand Campus**: Central London, featuring **King’s College London Chapel** and **Maughan Library**.
- **Guy’s Campus**: Near London Bridge, home to **medical and dental schools**.
- **Waterloo Campus**: Lambeth, featuring **Florence Nightingale Faculty of Nursing**.
- **St Thomas’ Campus**: Lambeth, home to **medical and dental schools**.
- **Denmark Hill Campus**: Southwark, home to **psychiatry and neuroscience**.

#### **Academics**
- **Structure**: Organized into **nine faculties**, including **arts and humanities, law, medicine, and science**.
- **Admissions**: Highly competitive, with an acceptance rate of **18%** (2023).
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the **top 30 globally** (e.g., **Times Higher Education 2025**).
- **Research**: Strong focus on **medicine, law, and social sciences**, with over **£256 million spent on research in 2023**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: 22,000 students (2023), with a diverse demographic.
- **Traditions**: Includes **"Duel Day"** (annual duel reenactment) and **"Macadam Cup"** (rugby rivalry with UCL).
- **Organizations**: Over **300 student organizations**, including academic, cultural, and athletic groups.

#### **Notable People**

### **Stanford University**

#### **History**
Founded in **1885** by **Leland and Jane Stanford** in memory of their son, Leland Stanford Jr., Stanford University was initially modeled after **Cornell University**. It quickly became a leader in **engineering and technology education**. The university played a key role in the **development of Silicon Valley** through its research and partnerships with industry. Stanford has faced controversies, including **discrimination against Jewish students** and **military research during the Vietnam War**. Despite these challenges, it remains one of the **most prestigious universities in the world**.

#### **Campuses**
- **Central Campus**: Located in **Palo Alto, California**, featuring **Memorial Church** and **Stanford Mausoleum**.
- **Off-Campus**: Includes **Hopkins Marine Station** (Pacific Grove), **Redwood City Campus**, and **China Campus**.

#### **Academics**
- **Structure**: Organized into **seven schools**, including **Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, and Business**.
- **Admissions**: Extremely competitive, with an acceptance rate of **4%** (2023).
- **Rankings**: Consistently ranked among the **top 5 globally** (e.g., **QS World University Rankings 2025**).
- **Research**: Strong focus on **engineering, computer science, and business**, with over **$1.8 billion spent on research in 2021**.

#### **Student Life**
- **Student Body**: 6,996 undergraduates, 10,253 graduates (2019–2020).
- **Traditions**: Includes **"Hail, Stanford, Hail!"** (campus anthem), **"Big Game"** (football rivalry with UC Berkeley), and **"Fountain Hopping"** (annual tradition of visiting campus fountains).
- **Organizations**: Over **600 student organizations**, including academic, cultural, and athletic groups.

#### **Notable People**
- **Alumni**: Includes **Nobel laureates** like **Herbert Hoover** (Economics) and **Larry Page** (Google co-founder).
- **Faculty**: Notable figures include **Frederick Terman** (pioneer of Silicon Valley) and **Philip Zimbardo** (psychologist).
