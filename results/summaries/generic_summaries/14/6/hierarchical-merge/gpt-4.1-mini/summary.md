# Zhejiang University (ZJU) and Tsinghua University

Zhejiang University, established in 1897 as Qiushi Academy, is a leading comprehensive research university in China with over 65,000 students across seven campuses. It excels in interdisciplinary programs including agriculture, engineering, IT, and medicine, with 11 State Key Laboratories and seven affiliated hospitals supporting its research. Recognized globally (ARWU #24, QS #44), it is part of major national initiatives such as Double First-Class and Project 985, and governed under the Chinese Communist Party, continuing to expand its international collaborations despite past controversies.

Tsinghua University, founded in 1911 with Boxer Indemnity funds, has evolved from a Soviet-modeled engineering school into China’s premier multidisciplinary research institution. With 20 schools, it excels in engineering, computer science, business, and sciences, holding top global rankings (ARWU #18, U.S. News #11). Located in Beijing, Tsinghua integrates traditional and Western architecture and hosts significant research centers, including classified military projects. It maintains close CCP governance and selective admissions, spearheading international partnerships like dual MIT degrees and the Schwarzman Scholars program.

# King’s College London (KCL)

Founded in 1829 by King George IV as an Anglican counterpart within the University of London, King’s College London is a multidisciplinary institution renowned for its medical research, particularly psychiatry, psychology, and neuroscience at the Denmark Hill campus. It offers diverse programs across arts, sciences, health, and social sciences, supported by collaborations like King’s Health Partners and the Francis Crick Institute. Internationally ranked (QS #31, THE #36), KCL had income exceeding £1.27 billion in 2023/24 and hosts a vibrant student community with 300+ societies and 14 Nobel laureates among its alumni. Its history includes pioneering women’s education and key scientific discoveries, notably contributions to the DNA double helix.

# Stanford University

Established in 1885 in Palo Alto, Stanford University is globally recognized for fostering innovation and entrepreneurship. With seven academic schools and approximately 17,000 students, it offers a residential experience alongside premier research institutions such as SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and Hoover Institution. Backed by a $36.5 billion endowment, Stanford has pioneered breakthroughs including DNA synthesis, MRI, and ARPANET, and its alumni have founded major tech companies like Google and PayPal. The university supports a rich cultural life with over 600 student organizations and 36 varsity sports teams, emphasizing inclusivity and free expression.

# Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)

MIT, founded in 1861 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, is a world leader in science, technology, and interdisciplinary education. Hosting roughly 4,600 undergraduates and nearly 7,000 graduate students, MIT maintains a highly selective 4.1% acceptance rate and a diverse student body with strong female STEM representation. Its faculty includes over 1,000 members and 37 Nobel laureates. MIT’s innovation culture has produced foundational technologies like the RSA cryptosystem, Lisp and Julia languages, and significant battery advancements. Traditions such as the Brass Rat ring and Independent Activities Period enrich student life, which includes 31 varsity sports. The university’s alumni have founded influential companies including Intel and Dropbox, collectively generating an economic impact comparable to the world’s 11th-largest economy.

# University of California, Berkeley

Established in 1868 as California’s first land-grant university, UC Berkeley is a top public research institution known for its historic political activism and commitment to diversity. It enrolls over 45,000 students with an 11.4% acceptance rate and offers 107 undergraduate degrees across 15 colleges. The campus blends historic Beaux-Arts architecture with natural features and fosters a dynamic student life rich in political organizations, arts, media, and athletics. Berkeley alumni and faculty have contributed to major scientific advances such as atomic bomb development, CRISPR, and PCR, and founded industry giants like Apple, Tesla, and OpenAI, consistently ranking as a "Public Ivy."

# California Institute of Technology (Caltech)

Caltech, originating from Throop College in the early 20th century, is a premier private research university specializing in science and engineering. With a highly selective admission rate near 3.14%, it offers 28 STEM majors and operates significant facilities including NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the Keck Observatory. Its small, diverse student body benefits from a strong residential culture featuring traditions like the Millikan pumpkin drop and “Ditch Day.” Caltech’s faculty includes 48 Nobel laureates and numerous national academy members. Alumni have driven transformative innovations such as the transistor and pH meter and founded companies like Intel and Quora. The institution is notable for its role in landmark discoveries like gravitational waves and extensive commercialization of research in multiple fields.
