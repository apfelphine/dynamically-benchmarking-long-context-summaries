King's College London is a leading research university in the UK, part of the Russell Group and the "golden triangle" of elite universities. It joined the University of London in 1998 and is a founding member of the Russell Group, UNICA, EUA, ACU, and FutureLearn. It is a key member of King’s Health Partners and the Francis Crick Institute, and collaborates with Imperial College London and UCL in nanotechnology, geometry, and number theory. The university has strong international partnerships, including the PLuS Alliance.  

Academically, King’s offers diverse programs with a focus on medicine, research, and interdisciplinary studies. Its 2022 average entry qualification was 168 UCAS points, with a 39.3% offer rate. Graduation ceremonies are held in January and summer, with unique venues reflecting its medical heritage.  

King’s has a significant research profile, with £256.9 million in research income in 2023/24 and ranked 9th in UK research quality (REF 2021). It is a major center for healthcare education and biomedical research, home to the MRC Centre for Neurodevelopmental Disorders and two NIHR Biomedical Research Centres.  

The university’s library system includes the historic Maughan Library, Foyle Special Collections, and specialized libraries across campuses, housing rare books, historical archives, and subject-specific resources. King’s also has a strong commitment to diversity, with 64% female students and a broad international student body.
