# Education Systems Overview

## Romania, Australia, Malaysia, Portugal, Philippines, and South Africa

Romania requires 14–15 years of education culminating in a national Baccalaureate, aligned with the Bologna Process for higher education. Challenges include underfunding, urban-rural disparities, minority inclusion issues, and brain drain. Early English language instruction is common. Australia’s system mandates compulsory schooling varying by state, supported by a national curriculum yielding strong, if uneven, international results. Key issues include Indigenous education gaps, school violence, funding equity, and religious education policies. Malaysia offers a multicultural system featuring national and vernacular schools, with the Dual Language Program boosting English in STEM at some cost to Malay proficiency. Affirmative action for Bumiputera students fuels equity debates, amid ongoing efforts to reduce exam pressure and address workforce alignment.

Portugal’s reformed education comprises nine years of basic education, three years secondary general or vocational tracks, and higher education split between universities and polytechnics under European standards. Free public education is supported by social programs, though dropout rates and rural illiteracy remain concerns. The Philippines implemented K–12 by 2017, emphasizing Filipino nationalism and mother tongue instruction, but faces acute classroom shortages, significant learning poverty, and teacher qualification challenges exacerbated by COVID-19 disruptions. South Africa’s system spans early childhood (Grade R) through Grade 12, with education access and quality hampered by socioeconomic disparities, uneven funding, and resource gaps. Post-apartheid reforms aim for inclusivity, though issues like high dropout rates, school violence, and infrastructure deficits persist.

## South Africa

South Africa’s education evolved from apartheid-era segregation toward inclusivity following reforms such as the South African Schools Act (1996) and recognition of multiple official languages. The sector includes public schools with variable fee structures and private schools, and a consolidated university system. Despite progress, challenges remain in academic performance, dropout rates, infrastructure, and rural access. Efforts to integrate educational technology started in the late 1990s but are limited by low internet penetration and teacher training gaps; government programs aim to expand connectivity by 2030. The COVID-19 pandemic magnified inequities and accelerated digital learning adoption, prompting targeted interventions to improve equity and outcomes.

## United States

The U.S. education system includes widespread pre-K participation and predominantly public K–12 schooling funded largely through local property taxes, causing significant funding disparities. Despite high per-student spending, academic performance has stagnated, with further COVID-19-related enrollment declines and learning setbacks. Higher education comprises thousands of institutions, with growing concerns over rising tuition, student debt, and adjunct faculty reliance. Persistent systemic inequities stem from funding models, racial and socioeconomic disparities, and political controversies over curriculum content, including debates over affirmative action, LGBTQ topics, and sex education. Federal support programs exist but gaps in equitable access and outcomes remain.

## Finland

Finland is globally recognized for its equitable, tuition-free education system emphasizing student well-being, creativity, and inclusivity. Compulsory education runs from ages 7 to 18, featuring nine years of comprehensive basic education followed by flexible academic or vocational upper secondary tracks. The system values small class sizes, teacher autonomy, minimal homework, and a broad curriculum including multilingual education. Teacher education is rigorous, requiring master’s degrees and high professionalism. Finland promotes phenomenon-based learning and 21st-century skills, while higher education offers tuition-free merit-based access. Facing demographic challenges, the system adapts through university mergers and enhanced vocational pathways. National initiatives support digital literacy and AI education, and Finland exports educational expertise globally.
