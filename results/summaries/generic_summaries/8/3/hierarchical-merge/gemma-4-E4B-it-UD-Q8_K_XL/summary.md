## Global Education System Comparisons and Challenges

**National System Models and Governance**
Global education systems display diverse structures, ranging from highly centralized models to decentralized, state-led approaches. For instance, Australia utilizes a federal system with a national curriculum (ACARA), while Romania relies on centralized exams ($\text{Bacalaureat}$) and faces underfunding issues. Malaysia balances public, religious, and international schooling under a National Education Blueprint, while the Philippines mandates a 13-year K–12 system despite resource shortages.

**Systemic Challenges and Contrasting Philosophies**
Many nations struggle with equity and performance. The US system suffers from funding disparities tied to local property taxes, creating achievement gaps rooted in socioeconomic and racial inequality. Similarly, South Africa contends with severe resource scarcity and historical disparities, while Romania faces regional inequality. In contrast, Finland prioritizes equity and holistic development through high-quality, largely free education with minimal high-stakes testing, serving as a global model for uniform quality.

**European and Developing Trends**
Other national models present unique features. Portugal’s education structure incorporates a Bologna Process alignment in higher education but has battled high dropout rates linked to budget cuts. Across various systems, governance approaches differ significantly, with some nations focusing on comprehensive blueprints (Malaysia) or standardized mandates (Philippines), while others like the US grapple with systemic strain and rising higher education costs.
