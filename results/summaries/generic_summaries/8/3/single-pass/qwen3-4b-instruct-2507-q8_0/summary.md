**Education in Romania**  
Romania’s education system is compulsory from age 6 to 18, spanning 15 years (kindergarten to 12th grade). It is largely state-run, with minimal private participation. The system evolved from 19th-century reforms under Alexandru Ioan Cuza, though mass illiteracy persisted until the 1940s. The communist era introduced political propaganda and compulsory physical labor. Since 2020, kindergarten (including a mandatory "middle form" and preparatory year) and the last two years of high school have been made compulsory, extending education to 14 years.  

Kindergarten is structured into three groups (small, middle, big), with the middle group becoming compulsory in 2023. The "preparatory school year" (clasa pregătitoare) is mandatory and part of primary education. Kindergartens vary in quality and services, with private institutions being more expensive and accessible to upper-middle-class families.  

Primary and secondary education follow a structured curriculum with a focus on core subjects. In primary school, students learn math, language, science, music, and physical education. In secondary school, students take national exams (Evaluarea Națională) to determine high school placement. The Baccalaureate exam is required for university entry, assessing students in Romanian, foreign languages, and core academic subjects.  

The system faces challenges in rural areas, where access to schools, transportation, and qualified teachers is limited, leading to high dropout rates after age 14. Literacy is nearly universal (0.83% illiterate), but disparities exist among ethnic minorities. The system is underfunded, with parents contributing to class funds.  

**Education in Australia**  
Australia’s education system is decentralized, with states managing schools and curriculum. Compulsory education runs from age 5 to 17, with government schools accounting for 64% of students. A national curriculum (Australian Curriculum) is implemented since 2010.  

Schools are categorized into government, Catholic, and private institutions. Government schools are free, while private schools charge tuition. The curriculum emphasizes core subjects, with a focus on student performance and equity.  

Preschool is optional, with government-supported early childhood education. Primary and secondary education follow a structured progression, with students progressing through grades. The system includes a national assessment program (NAPLAN) and emphasizes student well-being and extracurricular activities.  

Tertiary education is heavily subsidized, with public universities offering degrees. International students are a significant part of the education market. The system faces challenges in Indigenous education and rural access, with ongoing efforts to improve equity and outcomes.  

**Education in Malaysia**  
Malaysia’s education system is structured into pre-primary, primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. Compulsory education spans from age 6 to 17. Primary education includes a 6-year cycle with a focus on core subjects and mother tongue instruction.  

Secondary education is divided into general, technical, and artistic streams. Students choose a track based on aptitude and interest. The national curriculum includes a strong emphasis on language, with English and Malay as core subjects.  

The system has faced criticism over language policies, with debates around the use of Chinese and Tamil as mediums of instruction. Private schools, especially Chinese independent schools, offer alternative curricula.  

The government has implemented reforms to improve equity and access, including the Dual Language Program (DLP) to promote bilingualism. Challenges include regional disparities, racial quotas in universities, and teacher shortages.  

**Education in Portugal**  
Portugal’s education system is compulsory from age 6 to 18, with a 12-year basic education cycle (K–12). The system emphasizes a balanced curriculum with a focus on core subjects.  

Pre-primary education is optional, with kindergarten being a key stage. Primary education includes a structured curriculum with a focus on literacy and numeracy. Secondary education is divided into junior and senior high school, with students choosing from academic, technical, or artistic tracks.  

The system has faced challenges related to teacher quality, school funding, and curriculum implementation. The government has introduced reforms to improve outcomes, including the Matatag curriculum (launched in 2023) to streamline subject areas.  

**Education in the Philippines**  
The Philippines has a 12-year K–12 system, compulsory from age 5 to 18. The system emphasizes a strong foundation in core subjects, with a focus on mother tongue-based instruction.  

Pre-primary education is optional, with kindergarten being a key stage. Primary education includes a structured curriculum with a focus on literacy and numeracy. Secondary education is divided into junior and senior high school, with students choosing from academic, technical, or arts tracks.  

The system faces challenges related to teacher quality, school funding, and rural access. The government has implemented reforms to improve equity and outcomes, including the Matatag curriculum (launched in 2023) to streamline subject areas.  

**Education in Finland**  
Finland’s education system is renowned for its equity, student well-being, and teacher autonomy. Compulsory education spans from age 6 to 18, with a 9-year comprehensive school (peruskoulu) followed by upper secondary education.  

Early childhood education is universal and emphasizes play-based learning and social development. Comprehensive schools have small classes, minimal homework, and a focus on student well-being.  

Upper secondary education offers academic or vocational tracks, with a national matriculation exam (Ylioppilastutkinto) for university admission. Tertiary education is accessible and free for domestic students, with a strong emphasis on research and practical skills.  

The system is highly decentralized, with municipalities managing schools and teachers having significant autonomy. Finland is a global model for educational excellence, with ongoing reforms to integrate phenomenon-based learning.  

**Education in the United States**  
The U.S. education system is decentralized, with state and local governments managing schools. Compulsory education runs from age 5 to 18, with a 12-year K–12 cycle.  

Public education is free, with a focus on standardized testing and college preparation. The system emphasizes core subjects, with a strong focus on academic achievement.  

Challenges include teacher shortages, socioeconomic disparities, and the school-to-prison pipeline. The system has faced criticism over standardized testing, racial achievement gaps, and unequal access to resources.  

**Key Takeaways**  
- Romania: A state-led system with strong rural disparities and ongoing reforms.  
- Australia: A decentralized system with strong national curriculum and equity efforts.  
- Malaysia: A multilingual system with significant regional and racial disparities.  
- Portugal: A balanced system with ongoing reforms to improve equity and outcomes.  
- Philippines: A K–12 system with challenges in teacher quality and rural access.  
- Finland: A globally recognized model of equity, well-being, and teacher autonomy.  
- United States: A decentralized system with significant challenges in equity and teacher shortages.
