## Education in Romania

Romania’s education system spans 15 years and culminates in the National Baccalaureate. Ongoing reforms seek closer alignment with European standards amid persistent underfunding, rural and minority disparities, and substantial brain drain.

## Education in Australia

Australian education comprises state-managed public, Catholic, and independent schools, funded federally. The system boasts high Year 12 completion rates and diverse pathways, but faces challenges including Indigenous support gaps, declining test scores, university funding issues, and changing international enrollment trends.

## Education in Malaysia

Malaysia’s exam-driven, multi-stream system is influenced by Islamic and colonial legacies and administered by several ministries. Recent reforms address equity issues in language, gender, and admissions, yet systemic disparities persist.

## Education in Portugal

Portugal’s secondary education offers general, professional, and artistic tracks, with higher education access based on exams and quotas. Recurrent issues include dropouts, illiteracy, teacher unrest, and debates over merit, compounded by ongoing EU reform pressures.

## Education in the Philippines

The Philippines’ K–12 structure is overseen by CHED, TESDA, and DepEd, with specialized tracks and reforms aimed at equity. The system faces affordability concerns, teacher shortages, poor infrastructure, learning poverty, low outcomes, and ongoing COVID-19 impacts.

## Education in South Africa

South Africa grapples with entrenched racial and geographic disparities, underqualified teachers, overcrowded schools, and high dropout rates. Policy initiatives like No Fee schools and technology integration have had limited impact amid systemic inequities and graduate unemployment.

## Education in the United States

The decentralized U.S. system spans early childhood to compulsory K–12, alongside competitive, expensive higher education systems favoring affluent students and driving credential inflation. Reliance on local property taxes results in funding disparities, particularly for poor and rural communities. Federal support is limited and prioritizes special needs.

Governance is state-based, with national focus on math and reading, sometimes neglecting other subjects. Racial and income-based achievement gaps persist due to institutional racism, standardized testing, and disciplinary practices like the "school-to-prison pipeline." Teacher shortages, burnout, underfunded pensions, rising college costs, student debt, gender gaps, pandemic impacts, and ongoing safety debates challenge quality and equity. Teacher retention is bolstered by mentoring and emotional support, while low parental involvement hinders early literacy. Curriculum diversity, sex education debates, technology, and textbook policy remain prominent.

## Education in Finland

Finland’s education system is lauded internationally for its equitable, tuition-free, and non-selective approach, with compulsory schooling from ages 7–18. Teachers are highly qualified, school governance is municipal, and the curriculum is holistic and supportive. Students freely choose academic or vocational upper secondary tracks; both are tuition-free, with academic pathways culminating in a national matriculation exam for higher education entrance. Tertiary education encompasses research universities and applied science institutions, both employing transparent, merit-based admissions. Since 2017, non-EU/EEA students pay tuition while EEA students remain exempt.

Key recent reforms include the 2016 National Curriculum Framework introducing mandatory phenomenon-based learning for ages 7–16, emphasizing 21st-century skills. The Bologna Process is fostering closer integration between vocational and academic qualifications, new postgraduate opportunities, and inter-institutional cooperation. Higher education is restructuring via institutional mergers and increased collaboration to address globalization and demographic demands. Academic unemployment has risen following expanded student intake in the 1990s, leading to admission and funding cuts.

Finland supports extensive adult education, open university, and “Free Education” institutions, with early childhood learning emphasizing play and family support. The Ministry of Education upholds universal access to public domain works as a right. Finnish educational influence extends abroad through Finnish curriculum schools for expatriates and “Finnish-inspired” institutions, supported by the Education Finland export program. Finland invests in media and technology initiatives, aiming for global leadership in artificial intelligence education. All students study at least two foreign languages, with mandatory Swedish instruction remaining controversial for its perceived limited utility.
