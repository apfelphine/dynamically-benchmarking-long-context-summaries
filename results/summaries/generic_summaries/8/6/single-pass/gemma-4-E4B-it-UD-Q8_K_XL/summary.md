# Summary of Global Education Systems

## South Africa
**Structure and Governance:** Education is managed by the Department of Basic Education and the Department of Higher Education and Training, both funded by central government taxes. Key legislation includes the South African Schools Act of 1996 and the National Education Policy Act of 1996. School Governing Bodies (SGBs) handle fundraising, with primary school SGBs comprising parents, teachers, and support staff.

**Basic Education:** Primary and secondary schooling spans Grade 1 to Grade 12. Education is divided into General Education and Training (GET) and Further Education and Training (FET) bands. Funding depends on the neighbourhood's poverty level (quintile), with grants varying significantly. School fees are subject to legal exemptions for very poor families, though this mostly benefits the extremely poor.

**Higher Education and Technology:** The tertiary sector is vibrant, with autonomous universities administered by the South African Qualifications Authority (SAQA). ICT adoption has been slow due to barriers like lack of infrastructure and resources, although national initiatives like South Africa Connect aim to improve broadband access.

## Finland
**System and Structure:** Finland features a compulsory nine-year comprehensive school system (ages 7-18), which integrates academic and vocational tracks after age 15. Education is highly decentralized, with municipalities funding and administering most schools, and private schools being strictly regulated against tuition fees and selective admission.

**Pedagogy and Support:** Early childhood education is vital, emphasizing play and holistic development before formal learning begins at age seven. The system promotes equality, featuring small class sizes and a lack of high-stakes testing. Teachers must hold a master's degree, and students benefit from free lunches and school healthcare.

## Portugal
**System and History:** Portugal's educational history shows a significant increase in literacy, driven by state-led efforts in the mid-20th century. The system is structured around a three-cycle basic education (1-9 years), followed by a three-year secondary cycle (10-12 years).

**Higher Education and Access:** Higher education is split between theoretical universities and practical polytechnics. The Bologna Process has reformed the system. Access to higher education is merit-based, though financial strain has led some institutions to increase tuition fees.

## United States
**Structure and Funding:** The U.S. system is decentralized, with states holding the most control over public education. Funding relies on local, state, and federal sources, but reliance on local property taxes leads to funding inequities between rich and poor districts.

**Performance and Equity:** Despite high rates of post-secondary attainment, educational gaps persist across race and income levels. Standardized testing is a major focus, though critics argue it reflects systemic inequities rather than pure academic ability. Student loan debt is a significant national issue.

## Australia
**Operation and Funding:** Education is primarily regulated by states and territories. Government schools educate about two-thirds of students and are free, though parents often pay voluntary fees. The federal government supports non-government schools.

**Curriculum and Equity:** A national Australian Curriculum is progressively implemented across all levels. Efforts are focused on "closing the gap" for Indigenous Australians, though significant achievement disparities remain across Indigenous and non-Indigenous students.

## Malaysia
**Language and Policy:** The system is characterized by linguistic diversity, with primary education offered in Malay, Chinese, and Tamil mediums. Policy debates center on language maintenance versus national unity.

**Structure and Education:** Secondary education lasts five years. The Dual Language Program (DLP) attempts to boost English proficiency in STEM subjects. The system faces challenges regarding the implementation of curriculum goals and the role of vernacular schools.
