### **Education in South Africa**

#### **Structure and Policies**
South Africa’s education system is governed by two main departments: the **Department of Basic Education** and the **Department of Higher Education and Training**. The Basic Education Act (1996) and National Education Policy Act (1996) establish frameworks for access, quality, and governance. Funding comes from central government taxes, with government schools supplementing salaries through grants, while independent schools rely on private funding.

The **School Governing Body (SGB)** manages fundraising, with parents, teachers, and support staff involved in primary schools, and expanded to include learners in high schools. The system is divided into **General Education and Training (GET)** and **Further Education and Training (FET)**, with **Foundation Phase (Grades R–3)**, **Intermediate Phase (Grades 4–6)**, and **Senior Phase (Grades 7–9)** under GET, and **FET (Grades 10–12)** for vocational training.

#### **Basic Education System**
Education spans **Grades 1 (ages 6–7) to 12 (ages 18–21)**, culminating in a **matriculation certificate** for university admission. The system includes **Kindergarten (Grade R)**, **Elementary (Grades 1–6)**, **Junior High (Grades 7–9)**, and **Senior High (Grades 10–12)**. Learner ratios average **30 learners per teacher** and **480 learners per school**, with disparities across provinces.

School income comes from government grants, fundraising, and fees, though higher fees exclude poorer students. **No-Fee Schools** receive increased grants for low-income areas. Private schools operate independently, often with state subsidies.

#### **Higher Education and Training**
University entrance requires a **Matriculation Endorsement**, though some universities set additional requirements. Over **1 million students** enroll in universities, colleges, and universities of technology. The **National Qualifications Framework (NQF)** oversees higher education. Graduate unemployment persists due to overemphasis on humanities and underemphasis on STEM fields.

The **Extension of Universities Act (1959)** initially segregated universities by race, but post-apartheid reforms merged institutions into **22 new universities** by 2005.

#### **History**
Education in South Africa traces back to Dutch and British colonial influences, with early schools focusing on biblical instruction. The **Bantu Education Act (1953)** under apartheid segregated education, promoting racial inferiority. The **Afrikaans Medium Decree (1974)** sparked protests, leading to the **Soweto Uprising (1976)**. Post-apartheid reforms introduced **Curriculum 2005**, later abandoned due to inefficacy.

#### **Educational Technology Use**
ICT adoption in education has been slow due to infrastructure gaps, limited resources, and digital divide issues. The **South Africa Connect** initiative aims to provide broadband access to schools by 2030. Despite challenges, ICT integration is expanding, particularly post-COVID-19, though disparities persist.

#### **Performance and Challenges**
Poor academic performance, high dropout rates, and school violence are critical issues. **70% of Limpopo schools** had a **0% pass rate** in 2020. The system struggles with weak institutional functionality, undereducated teachers, and insufficient learning time.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **Structure and Policies**
Finland’s education system is **universal, free, and compulsory** from **Grade 1 to 9**, with **upper secondary education (10–12)** becoming compulsory in 2021. The system emphasizes **child-centered learning**, small class sizes, and a focus on **21st-century skills** like collaboration and critical thinking.

Finland’s **National Curriculum Framework (2016)** mandates **phenomenon-based learning**, integrating traditional subject-based instruction. The **Finnish Qualifications Authority** oversees education standards.

#### **Early Childhood Education**
High-quality **daycare and preschool** (ages 1–7) are critical for social and cognitive development. Finland’s **free universal daycare** (since 1990) and **preschool** (since 1996) are widely attended. Early childhood education emphasizes **play-based learning** and **multilingualism**.

#### **Basic Comprehensive Education**
The system includes **6-year primary school (Grades 1–6)**, followed by **3-year middle school (Grades 7–9)**, and **upper secondary education (Grades 10–12)**. Students choose between **academic (lukio)** and **vocational (ammattikoulu)** tracks. **Grades 1–9** are compulsory, with **upper secondary education** becoming compulsory in 2021.

#### **Upper Secondary Education**
Upper secondary education lasts **3–4 years**, with students choosing between **academic (lukio)** and **vocational (ammattikoulu)** paths. **Admissions** are merit-based, with no legacy preferences. **Universities of applied sciences** focus on practical skills, while traditional universities emphasize research.

#### **Higher Education**
Finland’s higher education system includes **universities (yliopisto)** and **universities of applied sciences (ammattikorkeakoulu)**. **Tuition fees** apply only to non-EEA students (€1,500–€18,000/year). **Bachelor’s degrees** take **3–4 years**, with **master’s and doctoral programs** available.

#### **Teachers and Quality**
Teachers must hold a **master’s degree** and are highly respected. **Admission to university programs** is competitive. Finland’s **teacher training** is rigorous, with a focus on **emotional intelligence and pedagogical skills**.

#### **Future Prospects**
Finland’s education system is recognized globally for its **high standards and outcomes**. The **Bologna Process** integrates vocational and academic qualifications, and Finland aims to **educate 60–70% of its population to a higher degree**. Recent reforms focus on **digital transformation** and **21st-century skills**.

### **Education in Portugal**

#### **Structure and Policies**
Portugal’s education system is **free for basic and secondary education**, with **higher education** being more expensive. The **Bologna Process** has reformed higher education, aligning with European standards. **Compulsory education** spans **9 years (Grades 1–9)**, followed by **3 years of secondary education (Grades 10–12)**.

#### **Educational Stages**
- **Pre-primary Education**: Nursery (Creche) and Kindergarten (Jardim de Infância) for ages 3–5.
- **Basic Education**: **9 years (Grades 1–9)**, divided into **1st Cycle (Grades 1–4)**, **2nd Cycle (Grades 5–6)**, and **3rd Cycle (Grades 7–9)**.
- **Secondary Education**: **3 years (Grades 10–12)**, with options for **general, professional, or artistic programs**.

#### **Higher Education**
Portugal’s higher education system includes **universities and polytechnics**. **Degrees** range from **Bachelor’s (licenciatura)** to **Master’s (mestrado)** and **Doctorate (doutoramento)**. **Admission** is based on **secondary school credentials and ENES exams**. **Private universities** operate independently but face scrutiny over quality.

#### **Issues and Criticisms**
Portugal faces challenges like **high dropout rates**, **poor PISA scores**, and **inadequate teacher training**. The **Bologna Process** has led to **curriculum changes**, but **implementation issues** persist. **Affordability** remains a concern, particularly for higher education.

### **Education in the United States**

#### **Structure and Policies**
The U.S. education system is **decentralized**, with **federal, state, and local governance**. **Compulsory education** is from **Kindergarten (ages 5–6) to 12th Grade (ages 17–18)**. **Public schools** are funded by local taxes, while **private schools** operate independently.

#### **Educational Stages**
- **Preschool**: Non-compulsory early childhood education.
- **K–12 Education**: **12 grades (Kindergarten–12th)**, with **middle school (Grades 5–8)** and **high school (Grades 9–12)**.
- **Higher Education**: **4-year colleges/universities** and **vocational/junior colleges**. **Tuition fees** vary widely, with **average costs** around **$111,868** for public universities and **$161,904** for private universities.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Standardized Testing**: Controversial due to **inequities in access** and **cultural bias**.
- **Teacher Shortage**: Low salaries and **high turnover** contribute to a **critical shortage**.
- **Affirmative Action**: The **2023 Supreme Court ruling** (Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard) overturned race-based admissions.
- **School Violence**: **167,000 students** were paddled in 2011–2012, and **school violence** remains a concern.
- **Educational Inequality**: **Racial achievement gaps**, **socioeconomic disparities**, and **access to resources** persist.

### **Education in Australia**

#### **Structure and Policies**
Australia’s education system is **decentralized**, with **state and territory governments** responsible for regulation. **Compulsory education** spans **6–15 years**, with **free public schools** and **subsidized private schools**. **Tertiary education** is subsidized through the **Higher Education Contribution Scheme (HECS)**.

#### **Educational Stages**
- **Preschool**: **Kindergarten (ages 4–6)**, with **15 hours/week funding** for government preschools.
- **Primary and Secondary Education**: **7 years (Grades 1–6)** and **5 years (Grades 7–12)**, with **optional combined schools (K–12)**.
- **Higher Education**: **Universities and TAFE colleges**, with **public universities** being the majority.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **School Funding**: The **Gonski Report (2010)** recommended **fairer funding**, but **implementation has been slow**.
- **Indigenous Education**: **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students** face **significant disparities** in attendance and academic performance.
- **Bilingual Education**: **Bilingual programs** for Indigenous languages are underfunded and face **policy changes**.
- **School Violence**: **48% of school principals** reported **physical violence** in 2024.

### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **Structure and Policies**
The Philippines’ education system is **compulsory from Kindergarten to Grade 12 (K–12)**, with **free public education** for pre-elementary, elementary, and high school. **Tertiary education** is governed by the **Commission on Higher Education (CHED)**.

#### **Educational Stages**
- **K–12 System**: **1 year of Kindergarten + 12 years of primary and secondary education**.
- **Primary Education**: **6 years (Grades 1–6)**, with **Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE)**.
- **Secondary Education**: **4 years (Grades 7–10)** and **2 years (Grades 11–12)**, with **four tracks (Academic, TVL, Sports, Arts)**.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Learning Outcomes**: **Only 10% of Grade 5 students** achieve **minimum reading and math standards**.
- **Dropout Rates**: **12% of senior high school students** leave before completion.
- **Affordability**: **High costs** for private schools and **informal fees** discourage attendance.
- **COVID-19 Impact**: **61% of expected schooling years** were lost by age 18.

### **Education in Romania**

#### **Structure and Policies**
Romania’s education system is **compulsory for 14 years**, with **free public schools** and **limited private options**. **Kindergarten (ages 3–6)** and **preparatory school (Grade 0)** are optional. **Secondary education** spans **Grades 5–12**, with **four types of high schools** (National College, Theoretical High School, Military College, Economic/Technical College).

#### **Educational Stages**
- **Kindergarten**: **3–6 years**, with **preparatory school (Grade 0)** becoming compulsory in 2023.
- **Primary School**: **5 years (Grades 1–5)**, followed by **middle school (Grades 6–9)** and **high school (Grades 10–12)**.
- **Higher Education**: **Universities and institutes**, with **Bachelor’s (3 years)**, **Master’s (2 years)**, and **Doctorate (3 years)** degrees.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Rural Education**: **16% of children aged 7–10** and **25% aged 11–14** do not attend school.
- **Language Policy**: **Controversies over religious education** and **mother tongue instruction**.
- **Funding**: **Secondary education is underfunded**, with **parents contributing** to class funds.

### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **Structure and Policies**
Malaysia’s education system is **bilingual**, with **Malay as the national language** and **English as the medium of instruction**. **Compulsory education** spans **6 years (Grades 1–6)**, followed by **5 years of secondary education (Grades 7–12)**. **Tertiary education** is heavily subsidized.

#### **Educational Stages**
- **Preschool**: **Non-compulsory**, with **playgroup (ages 3–4)** and **kindergarten (ages 4–6)**.
- **Primary Education**: **6 years (Grades 1–6)**, with **Dual Language Program (DLP)** for **Science and Mathematics in English**.
- **Secondary Education**: **5 years (Grades 7–12)**, with **Form 1–5** and **SPM (Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia)** exams.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Language Policy**: **Controversies over Malay vs. English instruction** in **Science and Mathematics**.
- **Racial Quotas**: **Affirmative action policies** remain in place for **public universities**.
- **School Uniforms**: **Mandatory uniforms** are being phased out in favor of **flexible dress codes**.
