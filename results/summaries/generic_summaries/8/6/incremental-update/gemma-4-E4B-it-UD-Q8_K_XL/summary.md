**Comparative Education Systems Summary**

**Malaysia Education:**
Malaysia's education system, managed by the Ministry of Education, operates on a two-semester structure, reflecting indigenous and colonial influences. The system includes various school types: National Schools (SK, Malay-medium), National-type Schools (SJK, non-Malay-medium), Islamic Religious Schools, Chinese Independent High Schools (using Mandarin), and International Schools. The curriculum evolves through blueprints, notably the 2006–2010 and 2013–2025 Blueprints, which aim for universal access, top international performance, and reduced socioeconomic gaps, increasing compulsory schooling to 11 years and introducing KSSM/KSSR. Language policy remains politically sensitive; while the 1967 National Language Act prompted a shift to Malay-medium, recent debates seek to enhance enrollment via elective Chinese and Tamil courses. Admissions aim for meritocracy, allowing industry professionals to teach without postgraduate degrees. Post-SPM options include Form 6 (STPM) or matriculation. Regarding quotas, the government maintains a 90% quota for Bumiputeras in public universities, which was expanded in 2019 to prioritize the B40 income group. In 2020, the 10% non-Bumiputera matriculation vacancies were specifically allocated: 5.43% to Chinese, 3.72% to Indian, and 0.85% to others.

**Other National Education Systems:**
*   **Romania:** Operates a 14-year compulsory, free, public system influenced by French and Soviet models, leading to the National Baccalaureate. Higher education involves 56 public and 41 private institutions under the Bologna scheme, despite challenges like low enrollment and rural dropout rates.
*   **Philippines:** Utilizes a mandated 13-year K–12 structure with free basic education, but faces significant hurdles including insufficient funding, space constraints, low foundational skills (70.8% functional literacy), poor PISA scores, and "brain drain" due to skill mismatches.
*   **Australia:** Requires secular schooling from age five to seventeen with state-specific qualifications. Despite efforts for Aboriginal and CALD groups, core proficiency lags below 60%, and school violence is a disciplinary issue. Tertiary education is managed under the Gonski Reviews across 42 universities.
*   **United States:** Education funding is heavily state/locally dependent (86%). Key problems include vast socioeconomic and racial achievement gaps, student debt surpassing \$1.5 trillion, academic dishonesty, teacher shortages, and limitations on race-conscious admissions.
*   **Portugal:** Provides a free tripartite basic education structure (4/2/3 cycles) culminating in 9th-grade specialization. While tertiary education aligns with the Bologna Process, the country contends with high adult functional illiteracy (48%) and rising tuition fees.
*   **South Africa:** System stability is hindered by historical segregation, leading to violence and weak STEM performance. Infrastructure deficits affect technological progress, although primary education permits 12 home languages.
*   **Finland:** Prioritizes equality and holistic development through universal, free early childhood education based on play. Compulsory schooling (grades 1-9) defers specialization, supported by autonomous, municipality-funded schools that promote bilingualism and phenomenon-based learning.
