# Global Educational System Comparisons

## System Structures and Key Features
Global education systems exhibit wide structural diversity. Finland is noted for its emphasis on equality, holistic, play-based compulsory education (ages 7-18), and requiring Master's degrees for all teachers, while Malaysia employs a multi-track system featuring national, Islamic, and Chinese schools. In contrast, Romania enforces 15 years of compulsory education, requiring a national Baccalaureate Exam for high school progression, and the Philippines utilizes a centralized K-12 structure with specialized Senior High School tracks.

## Challenges and Disparities
Many nations face significant hurdles related to funding, equity, and outcomes. The US system suffers from socioeconomic disparities driven by local property tax funding, alongside issues like high student debt and the school-to-prison pipeline. South Africa struggles with graduate unemployment and STEM/healthcare skill mismatches. Meanwhile, the Philippines and Romania both grapple with insufficient funding, leading to low PISA scores and dropout rates in rural areas.

## Varied National Pressures
Other countries present unique challenges: Portugal battles high adult functional illiteracy and dropout rates in its tiered system, while Australia navigates recent declines in PISA scores despite its high global standing, focusing on Indigenous equity. The US teaching profession additionally contends with low salaries and high burnout rates, contrasting with systems like Finland's focus on high professional standards.
