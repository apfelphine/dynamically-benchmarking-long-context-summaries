## Education System Overviews

### Portugal
Portugal mandates free education for ages 6–18, covering early childhood, basic, upper-secondary, and vocational tracks. Persistent challenges include dropout rates, literacy, diploma recognition, and safety concerns.

### South Africa
South Africa’s Grade R–12 system is state-funded with a quintile framework supporting “No Fee” schools for disadvantaged communities. Key challenges involve financial constraints, infrastructure, teacher shortages, overcrowding, rural connectivity gaps, and pronounced inequality.

### Australia
Australia requires schooling up to age 17 across public, private, and Catholic schools, regulated by state and national authorities. The system emphasizes inclusivity and international pathways. Ongoing concerns include achievement gaps, Indigenous disparities, violence, and regulatory complexity.

### Finland
Finland provides free, equitable education emphasizing teacher autonomy, well-being, and broad academic/vocational choices. Higher education is tuition-free for EU/EEA students, with reforms focusing on lifelong learning and technology.

### Romania
Romania enforces 15 years of compulsory education, starting with a preparatory year and diverse secondary paths. Major challenges are urban-rural divides, funding, informal fees, minority inclusion, early language policies, and alignment with European Bologna standards.

### United States
The U.S. system includes optional preschool, compulsory K–12, and varied postsecondary options, with diverse institutions and local funding leading to disparities. Issues include completion times, cost and debt, adjunct reliance, standardized testing, curriculum debates, teacher shortages, immigrant integration, and higher education access.

### Malaysia
Malaysia’s education system includes Malay, Chinese, Tamil, and English-medium schools, shaped by language and inclusion reforms. Primary education is compulsory; secondary and higher education options vary, with university admissions favoring bumiputeras. Challenges center on racial polarization, gender and geographic disparities, infrastructure, and support for special needs students.

### Philippines
The Philippines offers public, private, international, science, Chinese, and Islamic schools, with the academic year running June–March and uniforms optional since 2008. The reformed K–12 system features kindergarten, elementary, junior high, and senior high, with compulsory curricula and specialized tracks. Technical-vocational education provides certifications via TESDA, and alternative learning options like the Alternative Learning System serve out-of-school youth. Curriculum reforms use 19 mother tongues, Filipino, and English, alongside initiatives such as Matatag (2023).

Higher education is regulated by CHED, encompassing public and private institutions, with admissions via university entrance exams. Policy aims include access, Filipino identity development, and Indigenous cultural relevance.

Despite rising enrollment, the Philippines grapples with classroom shortages, limited funding, large class sizes, and modest teacher pay, resulting in poor learning outcomes and underperformance in international assessments. The COVID-19 pandemic worsened instructional losses. There are mismatches between education and job market needs, contributing to brain drain, and persistent calls for increased investment and systemic reform to address inequalities and improve teacher quality.

## Common Challenges Across Systems

- Resource limitations (funding, infrastructure, teacher supply)
- Disparities based on geography, socioeconomic status, minority group, or language
- Curriculum alignment and relevance for workforce needs
- Impact of recent crises, such as the COVID-19 pandemic
- Ongoing reforms emphasizing equity, inclusivity, and adaptability to global standards
