## Comparison of Education Systems: Global Overview

This summary compares various global education systems, focusing on structures, pedagogies, and systemic challenges, with detailed examinations of the Malaysian and Philippine educational landscapes.

**Global System Profiles:**
*   **United States:** A decentralized, locally-funded system struggling with socioeconomic disparities, high student debt, mental health issues, and racial gaps.
*   **Finland:** Operates a high-quality, non-selective, tuition-free model emphasizing holistic, phenomenon-based learning and minimizing high-stakes testing.
*   **Portugal:** Requires nine years of Basic and three of Secondary Education but contends with high dropout rates and functional illiteracy, partially due to *numerus clausus*.
*   **South Africa:** Provides Basic (Grades 1-12) and Higher Education across 12 languages but faces challenges related to inequality, school violence, and weak higher education infrastructure.
*   **Australia:** Features shared federal and state authority, focusing on funding equity (Gonski Reports) and Indigenous outcomes within a strong international higher education sector.
*   **Romania:** Mandates 15 years of schooling; while adopting the Bologna scheme, it struggles with secondary underfunding, rural dropouts, and centralized university entry exams.

**Malaysian Education System:**
Influenced by Islamic and British systems (per the **Razak Report (1956)**), Malaysia covers preschool through secondary education with specialized tracks. The **National Education Blueprint 2013–2025** aims for universal access and improved rankings by enforcing 11 years of compulsory schooling and standardized curricula. Tertiary education is subsidized, maintaining a 90% quota at public universities.

**Philippine Education Evolution and Challenges:**
Philippine education transitioned from the **American Period (1901–1945)** to the current **K–12 System** (implemented in 2012) to meet international standards, extending basic education from Kindergarten through Senior High School (Grades 1–12). Primary education is mandatory, and early learning often integrates mother tongues. The K–12 framework allows for specialization in tracks like Academic (ABM, STEM, HUMSS), Technical-Vocational-Livelihood (TVL), Sports, or Arts and Design, with vocational training leading to official Certificates of Competency (COC) or National Certificates (NC I/II).

Despite having over 27.2 million enrolled students in 2021, the Philippine system faces severe hurdles:
*   **Resource and Funding Issues:** Challenges include a shortage of classrooms, inadequate funding, and high cost recovery rates in higher education, even where primary/secondary education is free.
*   **Learning Outcomes and Workforce:** Low scores in international assessments, coupled with poor foundational skill attainment, contribute to an education-employment mismatch and skilled labor emigration.
*   **Teacher Quality:** Concerns exist over teacher salary issues and quality control, evidenced by low passing rates in the Licensure Examination for Teachers and ineffective practices.
*   **Systemic Strain:** The COVID-19 pandemic worsened these issues, causing learning loss due to insufficient remote learning resources. The system also serves diverse populations, including those supported by the Alternative Learning System (ALS) and indigenous learning methods.
