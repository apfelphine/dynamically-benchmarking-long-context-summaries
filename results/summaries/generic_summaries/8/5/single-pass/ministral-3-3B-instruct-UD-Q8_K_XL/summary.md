### **Education in Portugal**

#### **Historical Overview**
Portugal’s education system traces back to the Christian clergy’s early role in education, with universities established as early as **1290**. During the Portuguese Empire, institutions like the **Real Academia de Artilharia, Fortificação e Desenho** (1792) and **Goa Medical College** (1842) were founded. However, by the **late 19th century**, illiteracy rates remained high (over **80%**), and higher education was restricted to the elite.

The **First Republic (1890–1926)** and the **Estado Novo regime (1932–1974)** under Salazar saw significant improvements in literacy rates, dropping to **40.4%** by 1950. The **1952 Plan for Popular Education** aimed to eradicate illiteracy, achieving near-universal literacy among school-age children by the late 1950s. Despite progress, Portugal lagged behind Western European standards in higher education.

#### **Education Structure**
- **Compulsory Education**: Nine years of basic education (1st–9th grades), divided into three cycles (1st–4th, 5th–6th, 7th–9th).
- **Secondary Education**: Three-year compulsory secondary education (10th–12th grades), with options for general, professional, or artistic tracks.
- **Higher Education**: Divided into **universities** (research-focused) and **polytechnics** (practical, vocational). The **Bologna Process** (2006) introduced a three-cycle system (licenciatura, mestrado, doutoramento).
- **Pre-primary Education**: Kindergarten (3–5 years) is optional but widely attended. Nursery care (0–3 years) is primarily private.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: Emphasizes literacy, numeracy, and general education, with specialized tracks in STEM, humanities, and arts.
- **Grading**: Uses a 10-point scale (1–10), with continuous assessment.
- **International Rankings**: Struggles with **PISA scores**, though improvements have been noted since 2010.
- **Challenges**: High dropout rates, particularly in secondary education; disparities in funding between public and private schools; and criticism of the Bologna Process implementation.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **K–12 Transition**: Aligned with international standards, though implementation faced resistance.
- **Digital Education**: Increased use of technology, though infrastructure gaps persist.
- **Teacher Training**: Mandatory master’s degrees for teachers, with reforms to improve quality.

### **Education in South Africa**

#### **Historical Context**
South Africa’s education system evolved under colonial and apartheid regimes. The **Bantu Education Act (1953)** segregated education, favoring white students, while **Christian National Education** promoted racial segregation. Post-apartheid reforms (1994) introduced a unified system, but disparities persisted.

#### **Education Structure**
- **Basic Education**: Grades 1–12, divided into **General Education and Training (GET)** (Grades R–9) and **Further Education and Training (FET)** (Grades 10–12).
- **Compulsory**: Grades 1–9 (ages 5–14), with free school meals and uniforms.
- **Secondary Education**: Grades 10–12, leading to the **Matriculation Certificate**, enabling tertiary education.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and technikons, with **Matriculation Endorsement** required for admission. **Brain drain** remains a concern.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on **outcomes-based education (OBE)**, though reforms (e.g., **Curriculum 2005**) were abandoned due to inefficiencies.
- **Funding**: Highly variable, with **quintile-based grants** (e.g., $807 per child in poorest quintile vs. $134 in richest).
- **Challenges**: High dropout rates (especially in rural areas), poor infrastructure, and **school violence** (e.g., gang conflicts, sexual assaults).
- **International Comparisons**: Struggles with **PISA scores**, particularly in math and science.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **National Curriculum Framework (2016)**: Shifted to **phenomenon-based learning** alongside traditional subjects.
- **Digital Education**: Initiatives like **South Africa Connect** (broadband for schools) to improve access.
- **Teacher Training**: Focus on **digital skills** and **critical educational factors** to address poor performance.

### **Education in Australia**

#### **Historical Overview**
Australia’s compulsory education began in the **1870s**, with each state adopting its own system. The **Australian Qualifications Framework (1995)** unified higher education, vocational education, and school-based education. The **Gonski Report (2010)** proposed funding reforms, though implementation was delayed.

#### **Education Structure**
- **Primary and Secondary Education**: Grades K–12, with **Australian Curriculum** (2010–present).
- **Compulsory**: Grades 1–10 (ages 5–15), though some states allow earlier entry.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and TAFE (Technical and Further Education), with **Higher Education Contribution Scheme (HECS)** subsidizing tuition.
- **International Schools**: Follow curricula like **IB, Cambridge, or state standards**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA)** sets standards for pre-Year 1 to Year 9, with **senior secondary** (Years 10–12) tailored to individual needs.
- **Funding**: **Gonski 2.0 (2018)** aimed to address disparities, though progress has been slow.
- **Challenges**: **PISA rankings** (16th in reading, 29th in math), teacher shortages, and **school funding inequities** (e.g., rural vs. urban).
- **Innovations**: **School of the Air** (remote learning), **digital transformation** post-COVID.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **Bologna Process Adoption**: Aligned with European higher education standards.
- **Teacher Pay**: **Gonski 2.0** proposed funding increases, though implementation lags.
- **Indigenous Education**: Focus on **closing the gap** in literacy, attendance, and graduation rates.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **Historical Context**
Finland’s education system is rooted in **Lutheranism**, with literacy rates rising from **50%** in the late 18th century to **97.6%** by 1880. The **19th-century system** included **pedagogio** (basic reading/writing), **trivial school** (grammar), and **gymnasium** (pre-university). Post-WWII, Finland adopted a **comprehensive school** model (1972–present).

#### **Education Structure**
- **Basic Education**: Nine years (1st–9th grades), with **peruskoulu** (basic school).
- **Upper Secondary Education**: Three to four years (15–18 years old), with options for **vocational** or **academic** tracks.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **universities of applied sciences (AMK/YH)**, with **tuition-free** for EU/EEA students (non-EU: ~€1,500/year).
- **Early Childhood Education**: Universal daycare (8 months–5 years), with **play-based learning** emphasized.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **National Curriculum Framework (2016)** integrates **phenomenon-based learning** with traditional subjects.
- **Grading**: **4–10 scale**, with **no high-stakes testing** (except matriculation exams).
- **Teacher Training**: Mandatory **master’s degrees**, with high demand for qualified educators.
- **International Rankings**: Consistently top-tier in **PISA** (e.g., 1st in reading in 2015).

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **Digital Education**: **AI and coding** initiatives (e.g., University of Helsinki’s AI course).
- **Teacher Shortages**: **10% cut in new student places** in universities of applied sciences (2007–2008).
- **Bilingualism**: Mandatory **Finnish/Swedish** and **English** (with Swedish opt-out).

### **Education in Romania**

#### **Historical Overview**
Romania’s education system was **compulsory in 1864** (4 years) but remained underfunded. The **communist era (1947–1989)** introduced **political indoctrination** and **Bantu-style education** (segregated by ethnicity). Post-communism saw reforms, but **brain drain** and **funding gaps** persisted.

#### **Education Structure**
- **Compulsory**: 14 years (kindergarten to 12th grade), with **preparatory school year** (clasa pregătitoare) added in 2020.
- **Primary Education**: Grades 1–4 (ages 6–10), followed by **gymnasium** (grades 5–8).
- **Secondary Education**: Grades 9–12, with options for **theoretical**, **technical**, or **vocational** tracks.
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **institutes**, with **Bologna Process** adoption (2006).

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **15 compulsory subjects** (e.g., Romanian, math, history), with **optional subjects** (e.g., foreign languages, arts).
- **Grading**: **1–10 scale**, with **continuous assessment** and **final exams** (Bacalaureat).
- **Challenges**: **Rural dropout rates** (25% after 8th grade), **funding disparities**, and **language debates** (e.g., vernacular schools).
- **International Comparisons**: **Low PISA scores**, though literacy rates are near-universal.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **K–12 Transition**: **14-year compulsory education** (2020), with **Kindergarten Education Act** (2012).
- **Digital Education**: **eLearning initiatives**, though infrastructure remains weak.
- **Teacher Training**: **Mandatory master’s degrees** for educators.

### **Education in the United States**

#### **Historical Overview**
The U.S. education system began with **New England’s free public schools** (18th century) and expanded through **Horace Mann’s reforms** (19th century). The **Civil War** led to **land-grant colleges**, while **Brown v. Board of Education (1954)** mandated desegregation. Post-WWII saw **massive growth in higher education**, though **No Child Left Behind (2001)** and **COVID-19** disrupted progress.

#### **Education Structure**
- **K–12 Education**: Grades K–12, with **compulsory** until age 16 (18 in some states).
- **Elementary School**: Grades K–5 (ages 5–11).
- **Secondary School**: Grades 6–12 (ages 11–18), with **junior high** (6–8) and **senior high** (9–12).
- **Higher Education**: Universities, colleges, and **community/junior colleges**, with **tuition costs** rising sharply.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **Common Core State Standards** (2010–present), though **state variations** persist.
- **Funding**: **Local property taxes** dominate, with **federal aid** (e.g., Title I) for low-income schools.
- **Challenges**: **Standardized testing**, **teacher shortages**, **achievement gaps** (racial, socioeconomic), and **affordability** (student debt crisis).
- **International Rankings**: **Below average in PISA** (e.g., 29th in math), though **college attainment** is high.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **Biden’s Education Plan**: **Student debt relief**, **teacher pay increases**, and **digital equity**.
- **Teacher Pay**: **$60,000 minimum salary** (proposed in 2022).
- **Affirmative Action**: **Supreme Court ruling (2023)** overturned race-based admissions.

### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **Historical Context**
Malaysia’s education system evolved from **Sekolah Pondok** (Islamic schools) to **British colonial schools** (e.g., Penang Free School). The **Razak Report (1956)** unified the system, with **Malay-medium national schools** and **vernacular schools** (Chinese/Tamil). Post-independence, **Dual Language Program (DLP)** and **K–12 reforms** (2012) were introduced.

#### **Education Structure**
- **Primary Education**: Grades 1–6 (ages 7–12), with **Dual Language Program (DLP)** for STEM subjects.
- **Secondary Education**: Grades 7–12 (Form 1–5), with **Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia (SPM)** exams.
- **Higher Education**: Universities, **polytechnics**, and **matriculation programs** (10% non-Bumiputera quota).
- **School Types**: **National schools** (Malay-medium), **vernacular schools** (Chinese/Tamil), and **international schools**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **Malay as primary language**, with **English for STEM** (DLP).
- **Grading**: **1–20 scale**, with **SPM exams** determining university admission.
- **Challenges**: **Language debates** (e.g., DLP controversies), **racial quotas**, and **funding disparities**.
- **International Rankings**: **Low PISA scores**, though **private universities** are globally recognized.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **K–12 Implementation**: **12-year compulsory education** (2012–present).
- **Dual Language Program (DLP)**: **2,420 schools** (2023), with **criticisms over implementation**.
- **Teacher Training**: **Raised entry bar** (top 30% graduates).

### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **Historical Overview**
The Philippines’ education system began with **Spanish colonial schools** (e.g., **Colegio de San Juan de Letran**). Post-independence, the **K–10 system** (1945–2012) was replaced by **K–12** (2012–present). The **COVID-19 pandemic** disrupted learning, with **61% of students losing expected years of schooling**.

#### **Education Structure**
- **K–12 Education**: Kindergarten to 12th grade, with **compulsory** from Kindergarten to 10th grade.
- **Primary Education**: Grades 1–6 (ages 6–12), with **Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE)**.
- **Secondary Education**: Junior high (7–10) and senior high (11–12), with **specialized tracks** (STEM, arts, sports).
- **Higher Education**: Universities and **technical-vocational schools**, with **CHED** overseeing programs.

#### **Key Features**
- **Curriculum**: **K–12 reforms** (2012–present), with **Mother Tongue** and **English** as primary languages.
- **Grading**: **A–F scale**, with **final exams** (e.g., **SPM**).
- **Challenges**: **High dropout rates** (especially in rural areas), **funding gaps**, and **education-employment mismatch**.
- **International Rankings**: **Low PISA scores**, though **alternative learning systems (ALS)** cater to out-of-school youth.

#### **Recent Reforms**
- **K–12 Implementation**: **9-year process** (2008–2017), with **Kindergarten Education Act (2012)**.
- **Digital Education**: **ALS and eLearning** post-COVID, though **infrastructure remains weak**.
- **Teacher Training**: **Scholarship program** (2022) to improve quality.
