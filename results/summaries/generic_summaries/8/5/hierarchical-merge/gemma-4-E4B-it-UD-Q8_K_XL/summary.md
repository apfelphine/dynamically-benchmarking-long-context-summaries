# Global Educational Systems: A Comparative Overview

## System Structures and Models
Global education systems exhibit significant structural variation. Finland utilizes a decentralized municipal model emphasizing holistic learning and minimal homework. In contrast, the US employs a decentralized, state-managed K-12 system marked by socioeconomic and racial achievement gaps tied to local funding. Other notable structures include Portugal's system integrating research-focused Universities and practical Polytechnics under the Bologna Process, and Australia's state-regulated system standardized by the Australian Qualifications Framework.

## Challenges and Disparities
Many nations grapple with issues of equity and quality. South Africa's sector suffers from historical racial segregation and weak institutional function, while Portugal deals with adult functional illiteracy. Resource disparities are widespread; Romania faces underfunding in secondary and rural schools, leading to dropout risks. The US struggles with inequities in testing and high teacher turnover due to low wages.

## Regional Comparisons
Specific comparisons highlight diverse challenges. Malaysia offers diverse educational pathways, including the Dual Language Program, while setting quotas for university access, prioritizing the B40 income group. The Philippines utilizes a K–12 structure with Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE) in early grades but contends with low foundational skills and high learning poverty.
