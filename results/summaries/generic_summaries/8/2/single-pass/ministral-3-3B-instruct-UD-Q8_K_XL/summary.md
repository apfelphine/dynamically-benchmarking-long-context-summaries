### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **History**
Malaysia’s education system traces back to Islamic schools (*sekolah pondok*), madrasah, and early colonial-era English-language schools established by the British in Penang, Malacca, and Singapore. The British sought to improve Malay education, though initially, Malay students were limited to primary-level instruction. The **Razak Report (1956)** unified the education system, introducing a national curriculum with Malay-medium schools (*sekolah kebangsaan*) and vernacular schools (*sekolah jenis kebangsaan*) for Chinese and Tamil students. Post-independence, the system expanded, though debates persisted over language policies, particularly the shift from Malay-medium to English-medium instruction in science and mathematics.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
Education in Malaysia is divided into **preschool, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**. The **National Curriculum** governs public schools, with Malay as the primary medium of instruction. **Primary education** spans **Years 1–6**, followed by **secondary education** (Form 1–5). **Kindergarten** is optional, but compulsory for **Year 1** in public schools. **Tertiary education** includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational training, with **universities** offering degrees up to doctoral levels.

**Key Features:**
- **Dual Language Program (DLP):** Introduced in 2016, offering English-medium instruction in STEM subjects.
- **Vernacular Schools:** Chinese (*SJK(C)*) and Tamil (*SJK(T)*) schools retain their mother tongues as mediums of instruction.
- **International Schools:** Offer curricula from the UK, US, and other countries.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Language Policy:** Criticisms over the shift from Malay-medium to English-medium instruction, particularly in science and mathematics.
- **Funding & Infrastructure:** Many schools lack adequate resources, leading to overcrowding and underfunded facilities.
- **Teacher Quality & Retention:** Low salaries and workloads contribute to high teacher turnover.
- **Equity & Access:** Disparities in educational opportunities between urban and rural areas, and between different ethnic groups.

### **Education in Romania**

#### **History**
Romania’s education system evolved under **communist rule**, influenced by Soviet models, and later adapted to European standards. **Compulsory education** was introduced in 1864, but illiteracy persisted until the 1950s. Post-communism, Romania adopted the **Bologna Process**, aligning higher education with European standards. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **early childhood education** and **vocational training**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Preschool (Jardim de Infância):** Ages 3–6, optional but encouraged.
- **Primary School (1st–6th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 6–12.
- **Secondary School (7th–12th Grade):** Compulsory from 2020, ages 12–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities and polytechnics, with **Bachelor’s, Master’s, and Doctoral degrees**.

**Key Features:**
- **Bilingual Education:** English and French are widely taught, with Latin introduced in secondary school.
- **Standardized Testing:** National exams (*Examenul Național de Bacalaureat*) determine university admission.
- **Vocational Training:** Integrated into secondary education for career-focused students.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Shortfalls:** Underinvestment in schools and teacher training.
- **Rural Education Gaps:** Limited access to quality education in rural areas.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low salaries and lack of professional development.
- **Language Barriers:** Low proficiency in English and French among some students.

### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **History**
The Philippines’ education system was shaped by **Spanish colonial rule**, which introduced Catholic schools and early universities. Post-independence, the system expanded under American influence, leading to the **K–12 curriculum** (2012). The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **vocational training** and **alternative learning systems (ALS)** for out-of-school youth.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Kindergarten:** Optional, ages 5–6.
- **Primary School (Grades 1–6):** Compulsory, ages 6–12.
- **Secondary School (Grades 7–12):** Compulsory, ages 12–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, colleges, and vocational schools.

**Key Features:**
- **K–12 Curriculum:** Expanded from 10 to 12 years, including **mother-tongue-based education** and **STEM-focused senior high school**.
- **Alternative Learning System (ALS):** Provides education for out-of-school youth.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offer technical and trade certifications.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Disparities:** Unequal distribution of resources between urban and rural areas.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low salaries and lack of professional development.
- **Dropout Rates:** High among students from low-income backgrounds.
- **Language Policy:** Debates over the use of **Tagalog, English, and mother tongues** in instruction.

### **Education in Australia**

#### **History**
Australia’s education system was **colonially influenced**, with **free public schooling** introduced in the 19th century. The **Australian Curriculum** (2010) standardized learning outcomes, while **universities** and **vocational education** (TAFE) expanded post-WWII. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **international accreditation**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Preschool (Kindergarten):** Optional, ages 3–6.
- **Primary School (Years 1–6):** Ages 6–12.
- **Secondary School (Years 7–12):** Ages 12–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, TAFE, and vocational colleges.

**Key Features:**
- **Australian Curriculum:** Standardized learning outcomes across states.
- **International Schools:** Offer curricula from the UK, US, and other countries.
- **Vocational Education:** Focus on practical, career-oriented training.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Disparities:** Unequal distribution of resources between states.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low salaries and high workloads.
- **Rural Education Gaps:** Limited access to quality education in remote areas.
- **Language Policy:** Bilingual education for Indigenous and CALD communities.

### **Education in the United States**

#### **History**
The U.S. education system was **state-driven**, with **compulsory education** laws enacted in the late 19th century. The **No Child Left Behind Act (2001)** and **Common Core Standards** (2010) standardized testing and curriculum. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **college readiness** and **vocational training**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Preschool:** Optional, ages 3–5.
- **Primary School (Kindergarten–5th Grade):** Ages 5–11.
- **Secondary School (6th–12th Grade):** Ages 11–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, colleges, and vocational schools.

**Key Features:**
- **Standardized Testing:** National assessments (*NAEP, PISA*) measure student performance.
- **College Admissions:** SAT/ACT exams and high school transcripts determine university entry.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offer technical and trade certifications.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Disparities:** Unequal distribution of resources between states.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low salaries and high workloads.
- **Achievement Gaps:** Disparities in performance between racial, socioeconomic, and gender groups.
- **Language Policy:** Debates over **English-only instruction** and **bilingual education**.

### **Education in Portugal**

#### **History**
Portugal’s education system was **church-driven** in the medieval period, later influenced by **Salazar’s authoritarian regime**. Post-revolution, the system expanded under the **Bologna Process**, aligning higher education with European standards. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **vocational training** and **adult education**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Preschool (Jardim de Infância):** Optional, ages 3–6.
- **Primary School (1st–9th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 6–15.
- **Secondary School (10th–12th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 15–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools.

**Key Features:**
- **Bologna Process:** Adopted in 2006, introducing **Bachelor’s, Master’s, and Doctoral degrees**.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offer technical and trade certifications.
- **Adult Education:** Programs for non-traditional learners.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Shortfalls:** Underinvestment in schools and teacher training.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low salaries and lack of professional development.
- **Language Barriers:** Low proficiency in English among some students.
- **Rural Education Gaps:** Limited access to quality education in rural areas.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **History**
Finland’s education system was **Lutheran-influenced**, with **compulsory education** introduced in the 19th century. Post-WWII, the system expanded under **social democracy**, emphasizing **equality and student-centered learning**. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **vocational training** and **internationalization**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Preschool (Kindergarten):** Optional, ages 3–6.
- **Primary School (1st–6th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 6–12.
- **Secondary School (7th–9th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 12–15.
- **Upper Secondary School (10th–12th Grade):** Compulsory, ages 15–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities and universities of applied sciences.

**Key Features:**
- **Student-Centered Learning:** Emphasis on **critical thinking, creativity, and collaboration**.
- **Free Education:** No tuition fees for domestic students.
- **Vocational Schools:** Offer practical, career-oriented training.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low salaries and high demand.
- **Funding Disparities:** Unequal distribution of resources between regions.
- **Language Policy:** Bilingual education for Finnish and Swedish speakers.
- **Internationalization:** Growing demand for English-language education.

### **Education in South Africa**
#### **History**
South Africa’s education system was **apartheid-driven**, with **racial segregation** in schools. Post-apartheid, the system was **decentralized**, with **national and provincial education departments** managing schools. The system includes **kindergarten, primary, secondary, and tertiary education**, with a focus on **equity and inclusion**.

#### **School Grades & Structure**
- **Foundation Phase (Grades R–3):** Ages 4–8.
- **Intermediate Phase (Grades 4–6):** Ages 8–11.
- **Senior Phase (Grades 7–9):** Ages 11–14.
- **Further Education and Training (Grades 10–12):** Ages 14–18.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, colleges, and vocational schools.

**Key Features:**
- **Bilingual Education:** Official languages include **Zulu, Xhosa, Afrikaans, and English**.
- **No Fee Schools:** Subsidized for low-income families.
- **Vocational Training:** Integrated into secondary education.

#### **Issues & Challenges**
- **Funding Shortfalls:** Underinvestment in schools and teacher training.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low salaries and lack of professional development.
- **Racial Disparities:** Unequal access to quality education.
- **Language Policy:** Debates over **mother-tongue instruction** and **English-only instruction**.
