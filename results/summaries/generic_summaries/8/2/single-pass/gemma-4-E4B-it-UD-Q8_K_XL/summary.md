# Summary of Global Education Systems

## Education in Malaysia
**History and Structure:** Early education in Malaysia involved Islamic schools like *Sekolah pondok*. The British colonial period saw the establishment of prestigious English-language schools, alongside policies limiting Malay education to primary levels. Post-decolonization, the Razak Report (1956) established a national system featuring Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil-medium schools at primary levels.
**Curriculum and Policy:** The system has undergone various reforms, including the 1996 Education Act. Language remains a key issue, addressed partially by the Dual Language Program (DLP), which allows English instruction in STEM subjects. Public schools are categorized as Malay-medium National Schools (SK) or non-Malay-medium National-type Schools (SJK).
**Other Sectors:** Private missionary schools, initially English-medium, gradually transitioned to Malay-medium. Chinese Independent High Schools operate with their own examination system (UEC), though their qualifications are not recognized by public universities.

## Education in Romania
**Structure and Compulsion:** Education is compulsory for 15 years (including preparatory kindergarten). The system resembles the French model, with a strong historical influence from the Soviet system during the communist era.
**Curriculum and Grading:** The system features a clear progression through primary, middle, and high school. Grading differs significantly: the first four years use qualitative descriptors (e.g., Excellent, Satisfactory), while grades 5 through 12 use a 1-to-10 numerical system.
**Higher Education:** Tertiary education is organized into university and polytechnic subsystems. The Bologna Process has guided reforms, allowing for mobility between these systems, though private institutions face scrutiny regarding quality.

## Education in the Philippines
**Historical Evolution:** Pre-colonial education relied heavily on oral tradition. The Spanish period introduced formal education via religious orders, alongside the 1863 Educational Decree, which established free public education. The American period centralized the system, leading to the eventual adoption of the K–12 curriculum.
**Current System:** The current K–12 system (13 years total, compulsory) was phased in starting in 2012. Education is free in public schools for basic education. The curriculum emphasizes Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE) in the early years.
**Challenges:** The system faces issues related to policy implementation, classroom shortages, and poor learning outcomes, with significant disparities between urban and rural areas.

## Education in Australia
**Regulation and Structure:** Education regulation is managed by state and territory governments. The system is underpinned by the Australian Qualifications Framework, unifying qualifications across schooling, VET, and tertiary levels.
**Schools and Funding:** Approximately two-thirds of students attend government schools, which are free from compulsory tuition. Non-government schools include Catholic and private institutions, which typically charge fees.
**Assessment and Equity:** Standardized assessments like NAPLAN measure literacy and numeracy. Significant disparities exist between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians, addressed via "closing the gap" targets.

## Education in the United States
**Structure and Policy:** Education is decentralized, with states exercising most control, though federal initiatives like No Child Left Behind and Every Student Succeeds Act have attempted to assert central control through standardized testing.
**Challenges:** Major issues include significant socioeconomic and racial achievement gaps, the high cost of higher education leading to massive student loan debt, and teacher shortages exacerbated by low pay and burnout.
**Curriculum and Assessment:** Curricula vary widely by district. Standardized testing is controversial, as critics argue it reflects systemic inequities rather than pure academic ability.

## Education in Portugal
**System and Progression:** The system is structured around Basic Education (9 years), which branches into three types of secondary programs (general, technical, artistic). Tertiary education is divided into university (theory-focused) and polytechnic (practice-oriented) subsystems.
**Access and Quality:** Access to higher education is governed by national exams. While public education is largely free, the private sector is prominent. Concerns exist regarding the quality and rigor of some private institutions and the ambiguity between professional and academic qualifications.

## Education in Finland
**Pedagogy and Structure:** Finnish education is characterized by its equity and focus on holistic development, emphasizing that "you are not taught, you learn" through play in early years. The system mandates a nine-year comprehensive school, followed by compulsory upper secondary education (academic or vocational).
**Quality and Teachers:** Teachers are highly autonomous and well-respected, requiring a Master's degree. There are no high-stakes standardized tests for basic education.
**Global Focus:** The country is actively exporting its educational model, promoting Finnish-style learning abroad.

## Education in South Africa
**Structure and History:** The system operates from Grade R to Grade 12, divided into General Education and Training (GET) and Further Education and Training (FET). Historically, education was segregated by race, a legacy of the Apartheid era.
**Funding and Equity:** While government grants are linked to neighborhood poverty quintiles, school fees and reliance on external funding create socioeconomic barriers.
**Technology and Violence:** The adoption of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has been slow due to infrastructure gaps. School violence remains a significant social issue, prompting intervention programs.

## International Education Systems
**Asia (China/Philippines):** Chinese independent high schools operate parallel to state systems, while the Philippines has recently adopted the K–12 model to align with global standards.
**Western Models (UK/Canada/Germany):** These systems are noted for varied curriculum structures, the prominence of international curriculum adoption, and a focus on specialized tracks.
**Key Themes Across Systems:**
*   **Language Policy:** Language is a persistent point of contention across Malaysia, the Philippines, and the US.
*   **Funding Models:** Systems range from heavily subsidized public models (Finland, Australia) to mixed public/private models (US, Portugal).
*   **Equity Issues:** Disparities based on socioeconomic status, race, and geography are prevalent in almost all surveyed nations.
*   **Global Trends:** There is a widespread movement towards modernizing curricula (e.g., K–12, phenomenon-based learning) and adapting to global standards (e.g., Bologna Process).
