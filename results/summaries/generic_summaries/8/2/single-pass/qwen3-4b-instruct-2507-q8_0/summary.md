**Education in Malaysia** is a structured system with a long history rooted in traditional Islamic and early colonial schools. The modern system evolved through key phases: British colonial influence introduced English-medium schools, leading to criticism over language policies that marginalized Malay and non-Malay communities. The 1950s saw the adoption of the Razak Report, establishing a national school system with Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil-medium schools, aiming for national unity. Over time, the system expanded with the introduction of the Dual Language Program (DLP) to enhance English proficiency in Science and Mathematics, though it faced criticism for undermining Malay language mastery and creating rigid implementation criteria.

Public education is divided into primary (Grades 1–6), secondary (Grades 1–5), and post-secondary levels, with a mandatory 10-year compulsory education (from age 6 to 16) and a plan to extend it to 14 years by 2025. The system includes a range of school types—national schools (Malay-medium), vernacular schools (Chinese and Tamil-medium), and private missionary schools—many of which have transitioned to Malay-medium instruction. Chinese Independent High Schools offer a distinct curriculum with a focus on Chinese language and culture, and their Unified Examination Certificate (UEC) is recognized internationally.

Key challenges include racial and linguistic polarization, especially in primary and secondary education, and persistent disparities in academic outcomes. The government has introduced reforms such as the National Education Blueprint (2006–2025), which aims to improve equity, access, and student outcomes through curriculum modernization, teacher development, and infrastructure improvements. However, issues like teacher shortages, inadequate funding, and underperformance in standardized tests remain. The system continues to balance national integration with cultural preservation, particularly through the efforts of organizations like Dong Jiao Zong, which advocate for multilingual education to protect ethnic identities.
