# Education Systems Across Selected Countries

## Malaysia, Romania, and the Philippines

Malaysia’s diverse education system reflects its multicultural society through multilingual instruction in Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil. It offers preschool through secondary education, with secondary schooling becoming mandatory by 2024. The system includes various types of schools (national, vernacular, religious, technical) and post-secondary options such as Form 6, quota-restricted matriculation favoring Bumiputeras, and university programs emphasizing research and vocational training. Challenges include rural resource gaps, ethnic equity concerns, and criticism of affirmative action policies.

Romania mandates 14–15 years of education starting with expanded kindergarten, followed by elementary (grades 1–8) and secondary education (four years), compulsory since 2020. Secondary school selection relies on national exams, and graduates must pass a digital National Baccalaureate. Public elementary education is free but often conducted in shifts. Higher education aligns with the Bologna Process, featuring national quality assurance and exchange programs. Despite high literacy rates, rural and marginalized students face access and funding disparities.

The Philippines adopted a K–12 system in 2017, covering Kindergarten through senior high school with academic and technical-vocational tracks. Public education is free and compulsory but struggles with overcrowding, underfunding, teacher quality, and regional inequalities, exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Completion rates fall at higher levels, especially among disadvantaged groups. Recent reforms include free tertiary education, expanded alternative learning, and a curriculum emphasizing Filipino identity. Higher education is largely private and regulated by the Commission on Higher Education, with entrance exams replacing previous standardized tests.

## Australia and the United States

Australia’s federally coordinated system covers preschool to tertiary education, with compulsory schooling from ages 5–6 to 15–17. Most students attend government schools, with significant Catholic and private school enrollments. Senior secondary education provides academic and vocational pathways leading to certifications aligned with the Australian Qualifications Framework. The system prioritizes inclusivity, especially Indigenous education through initiatives like "Closing the Gap" and bilingual Aboriginal programs. Australia ranks highly in international assessments and hosts numerous globally ranked universities, attracting many international students. Challenges include funding pressures, school violence, and regional disparities in religious education.

The United States has a vast and decentralized education system spanning early childhood through postsecondary levels, with compulsory schooling typically from ages 5–6 to 16–18. Approximately 85% attend public schools funded primarily by local taxes, but academic performance remains stagnant with ongoing racial and socioeconomic inequities. Curriculum quality varies widely amid debates over standardized testing, religious education funding, and content controversies, including sex education and history narratives. Teacher shortages, low pay, and burnout are widespread, contributing to high turnover. Disciplinary policies have marginalized vulnerable youth, reinforcing the "school-to-prison pipeline," while immigrant students face placement and language barriers. Higher education faces soaring tuition, growing debt exceeding $1.5 trillion, uneven graduation rates, reliance on adjunct faculty, and legal scrutiny over affirmative action. The COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated funding and equity challenges.

## Portugal, Finland, and South Africa

Portugal’s education features nursery, kindergarten, Basic Education (6–14 years), and Secondary Education (15–17 years), with a curriculum emphasizing Portuguese, math, environment, and foreign languages. Public schooling is free with some private nursery care. Secondary education is divided into academic, vocational, and artistic tracks aligned with EU frameworks. Admission to higher education relies on diplomas and competitive exams, with quotas for former colony students sparking fairness debates. Teacher education requires advanced degrees, and safety initiatives like Escola Segura address school wellbeing. Persistent challenges include policy instability, dropout rates, and regional disparities.

Finland emphasizes equal access and quality, providing free universal daycare focused on social skills and independence. Compulsory education spans ages 7 to 18 with inclusive, small classes lacking streaming, supported by free meals, healthcare, and transportation. Upper secondary education is mandatory and offers academic and vocational tracks with free tuition. Higher education distinguishes theory-oriented universities and applied sciences institutions, with prestigious teacher education requiring a master's degree. A 2016 curriculum fosters phenomenon-based learning, critical thinking, and multilingualism. Finnish institutions collaborate internationally and charge tuition only to non-EEA students. Vocational and academic paths are integrated under the Bologna Process, supporting labor market needs despite concerns about graduate unemployment. Lifelong learning is supported by adult education and digital initiatives.

South Africa’s education system is shaped by its apartheid legacy and ongoing reforms. Basic education comprises General Education and Training (Grades R–9) and Further Education and Training (Grades 10–12), with Grade R compulsory. Schools operate under funding from government grants and supplemented by fees, which can restrict access despite no-fee policies. Higher education includes 22 universities with challenges in graduate employability and low STEM participation. Efforts to integrate ICT by 2030 face infrastructural and teacher skill deficits, worsened by the COVID-19 pandemic. Institutional weakness, social issues like teen pregnancy and crime, and significant school violence—including gang-related incidents—impede outcomes. Programs such as Hlayiseka aim to reduce violence and improve learner safety.

---

Overall, these countries demonstrate diverse education systems shaped by cultural, economic, and policy factors. While Malaysia, Romania, and the Philippines focus on expanding access and multilingual education amid resource challenges, Australia and Finland exemplify inclusive, high-quality systems aligned with international standards. The United States grapples with equity and funding disparities despite substantial investment, whereas Portugal and South Africa face persistent regional, social, and infrastructural challenges impacting education quality and access.
