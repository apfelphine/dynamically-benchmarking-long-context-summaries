### **U.S. and Philippine Education Systems: Comparative Historical and Modern Contexts**

#### **19th Century Foundations**
The U.S. established **free, tax-funded elementary schools** under Horace Mann (1837), though Southern resistance persisted alongside **parochial schools**. The **Morrill Land-Grant Acts (1862, 1890)** and the **Committee of Ten (1892)** formalized a **12-year curriculum**. Under **American colonial rule (1899–1946)**, the Philippines adopted a **centralized 6-year elementary + 4-year secondary system**, training **Thomasite teachers** and founding institutions like the **Philippine Normal School (PNU)**. High schools emerged in 1910, emphasizing practical skills, with **free, material-based education** and no religious influence.

---

#### **20th Century Reforms and Colonial Legacy**
By **1940**, the U.S. achieved near-universal school attendance, with reforms like:
- **1925:** *Pierce v. Society of Sisters* upheld private schools.
- **1946:** *National School Lunch Act* provided subsidized meals.
- **1954:** *Brown v. Board* advanced desegregation, though systemic barriers persisted.

The Philippines faced **Japanese occupation (1942–1945)**, which suppressed American education values, promoting **Tagalog-centric curricula**. Post-war, **Executive Order No. 94 (1947)** established **DepEd**, and the **1987 Constitution** made **elementary education compulsory**, later expanded to **free secondary schooling via RA 6655 (1988)**. Malaysia’s system reflects **colonial-era reforms**, transitioning from **vernacular schools** to **Malay-medium national schools** post-independence. **Racial quotas in Malaysian public universities** remain at **90% for bumiputera**, with **B40-income group prioritization**, while non-bumiputera students receive **5.43% (Chinese), 3.72% (Indian), and 0.85% (others)** seats.

**South Africa’s System:**
Rooted in **colonial segregation**, South Africa transitioned from **mission schools** to state schools post-1994. The **Basic Education Act (1996)** and **National Education Policy Act (1996)** dismantled apartheid-era divisions, mandating **compulsory education from Grade R**, though **no-fee schools** remain targeted at the poorest 40% of schools. **English became mandatory from Grade 1 (2009)**, with **Afrikaans schools exempt**. **Educational Technology adoption** faced barriers like **low internet access (~22% in 2017)**.

---

#### **21st-Century Systems: Australia’s Education Structure**
Australia’s **21st-century education system** is structured as **compulsory from age 5–6**, with **Pre-Year 1 (ages 4.5–5.5)** as the entry point. It integrates **alternative pathways**, such as the **International Baccalaureate (IB)**, alongside government-endorsed certifications like **Senior Secondary Certificates** (e.g., NSW HSC, VIC VCE, WA WACE). Key assessments include **NAPLAN** (standardized tests in reading, writing, numeracy) and **international comparisons** via **PISA, TIMSS, and PIRLS**.

**Bilingual Education:**
Australia’s Indigenous bilingual education began in the **1950s**, with early success in remote areas like **Yirrkala School (1980)**. Modern programs, such as **"both-ways" education**, focus on **language maintenance/revival** (e.g., **Yuendumu, Yirrkala**), though **policy reversals** (e.g., 2008–2012 mandates) have disrupted progress. Current efforts integrate funding into general school budgets.

**Religious Education:**
Australia’s system is constitutionally secular, but **special religious education (SRE)** varies by state:
- **NSW/QLD:** Offered by approved organizations.
- **VIC:** Non-compulsory (≤30 mins/week).
- **WA:** Includes both **non-curriculum SRE** and **curriculum-based religious education**.

**School Violence:**
Persistent issues persist; **48% of school principals** reported physical violence in 2024, with historical data showing high suspensions and injuries.

**Tertiary Education:**
Australia’s system includes **37 public and 3 private universities**, with **1.18M students enrolled in 2021**. **International students (812K in 2019)** drive growth, projected to rise to **940K by 2025**, particularly from **China, India, and Vietnam**.

**Education Rankings:**
Australia ranks **2nd in the Education Index (2018)** but performs **16th in PISA reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science (2018)**. University rankings highlight institutions like the **University of Melbourne (35th globally, 2020)**.

#### **Comparative Insights and Key Takeaways**
Australia’s system emphasizes **specialized and vocational pathways**, with **NAPLAN-driven assessments** and **PISA comparisons**. While **PISA rankings reveal proficiency gaps**, reforms like **Gonski funding** aim to address **equity and regional disparities**. **Bilingual education** remains a contentious but evolving policy, particularly for **Indigenous communities**. **Religious education** operates under a **state-specific framework**, balancing secularism with cultural diversity. **School violence** and **tertiary education growth** (especially international) are critical areas of focus.

**Key Challenges Across Systems:**
- **Teacher shortages, low salaries, and funding gaps** (Romania, Philippines).
- **Vocational-market misalignment and diploma mills** (Portugal, Philippines).
- **Rural-urban disparities and illiteracy** (Romania, Portugal, South Africa).
- **Historical inequities and racial quotas** (Malaysia, South Africa).
- **Digital integration barriers** (Romania, South Africa, Portugal).
- **Safety and violence concerns** (Portugal’s *Escola Segura*).
- **Australia’s PISA performance gaps** and **hidden costs in government schools**.
