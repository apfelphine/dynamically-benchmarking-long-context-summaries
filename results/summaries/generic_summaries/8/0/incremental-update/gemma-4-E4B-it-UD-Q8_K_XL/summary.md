## Global Education Systems: Comparative Overview

### System Highlights & Regional Comparisons
This overview contrasts national education systems based on structure, philosophy, and challenges.
*   **Finland:** Provides nine years of free, equitable basic education, focusing on social skills and multilingualism through phenomenon-based learning, supported by autonomous teachers.
*   **U.S.:** Faces significant issues, including \$1.5 trillion student debt, socioeconomic and racial disparities, low international rankings, the school-to-prison pipeline, and teacher shortages due to low compensation, despite federal mandates (ESSA, IDEA).
*   **Philippines:** Offers free K–12 education using Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE). While achieving a 93.1% literacy rate, it struggles with infrastructure deficits, low teacher salaries, and inadequate early childhood support.
*   **Malaysia:** Its curriculum integrates Islamic and British traditions, taught in various languages, while managing challenges such as racial quotas (e.g., 90% public university quota in 2019).

### South African Education Transformation and Challenges
South Africa's Grades R–12 system, managed by the DBE and DHET, finances education via a "quintile" system and supports 12 official languages. Key hurdles include union influence, weak institutional performance, outdated curricula, school violence, and a digital divide.

### International Models: Romania, Portugal, and Australia
*   **Romania:** Requires 14 years of compulsory education, culminating in a national placement test and the Bacalaureat. Tertiary education aligns with the Bologna scheme, but the system struggles with high dropout rates after eighth grade, urban-rural gaps, youth emigration, and underfunded secondary education.
*   **Portugal:** Achieved universal literacy by the late 1950s, with nine years of Basic Education and three years of Secondary Education. Despite high university enrollment, the sector suffers from poor-quality private institutions, ambiguous Polytechnics, functional illiteracy (48% of adults), and high dropouts.
*   **Australia:** National policy is governed by the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF). Compulsory schooling starts around age six and is split between government and non-government sectors. Government schools educate about two-thirds of students for free, while non-government options include large Catholic providers and various independent philosophies. Quality is assessed via NAPLAN and international measures (PISA, TIMSS). Tertiary education involves universities (including the research-intensive Group of Eight) and TAFE providers, supported by a loan scheme (HECS), and hosts a large international student body.

### Key Cross-Cutting & Specific Educational Issues
Across systems, common challenges include teacher shortages, low compensation, educational inequality tied to socioeconomic status, violence, and managing diverse curricula. In Australia, while the system addresses funding disparities (Gonski Report) and promotes bilingual education for Indigenous and CALD groups, school violence remains a concern. Furthermore, Australia's constitution is secular, though religious instruction varies by state. Internationally, Australia holds a high ranking (0.929 in 2018 Education Index) and a significant presence in global education as an international student destination.
