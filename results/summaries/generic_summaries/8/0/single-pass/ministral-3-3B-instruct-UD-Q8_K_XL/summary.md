### **Education in the United States**

#### **History**
The U.S. education system evolved significantly from the 19th century onward, with key milestones including:
- **19th Century:** Massachusetts led early public education reforms, emphasizing literacy and basic skills. After the Civil War, high schools emerged, though segregated schools in the South lagged behind. The Office of Education (1867) aimed to standardize education but faced limited enforcement. Parochial schools grew due to Catholic immigration, and compulsory education laws were introduced. The **Committee of Ten (1892)** recommended a 12-year curriculum, including grammar schools and high schools.
- **Early 20th Century:** The **Brown v. Board of Education (1954)** mandated desegregation, though resistance persisted. The **Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965)** provided federal funding for disadvantaged students, and the **No Child Left Behind Act (2001)** emphasized standardized testing.
- **20th Century:** High school enrollment surged post-WWII, and higher education expanded, though disparities persisted. The **Great Recession (2007–2009)** led to budget cuts, and the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted in-person learning, highlighting inequities in access to technology and resources.

#### **Education Stages**
- **K–12 Education:** Compulsory from ages 5–18, divided into elementary (K–6), middle/junior high (7–8), and high school (9–12). Students may repeat grades if failing.
- **Higher Education:** Includes colleges and universities, with 4-year degrees common. **Community colleges** offer associate degrees and vocational training. **Tuition costs** vary widely, with public universities generally cheaper than private ones.
- **Vocational Education:** Provided through community colleges and specialized programs, focusing on technical skills.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Standardized Testing:** Mandated by **No Child Left Behind** and later **Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA)**, though criticized for overemphasizing test scores and creating inequities.
- **Teacher Shortages:** Low pay, burnout, and lack of resources contribute to high turnover rates. **Adjunctification** (hiring non-tenured instructors) is common, raising concerns about teaching quality.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist based on race, socioeconomic status, and geography. **COVID-19** exacerbated gaps due to unequal access to technology and resources.
- **Affirmative Action:** The **2023 Supreme Court ruling** (Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard) banned race-conscious admissions, sparking debates about diversity in higher education.

#### **Statistics**
- **Literacy and Employment:** 89% of adults have a high school diploma, and 34% hold a bachelor’s degree. College graduates earn significantly more than high school graduates.
- **Achievement Gaps:** Black and Hispanic students often underperform compared to white and Asian students in standardized tests, with systemic factors (poverty, segregation) contributing to these disparities.
- **Education Costs:** Average tuition for public universities is ~$10,000/year, while private institutions can cost up to $50,000/year. Student loan debt has risen sharply, affecting economic mobility.

### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **History**
- **Pre-Colonial to Spanish Period:** Oral traditions and early schools like **Sekolah Pondok** and **madrasah** were the primary forms of education. Spanish missionaries introduced formal education, establishing schools and universities (e.g., **Universidad de San Ignacio**).
- **American Period:** The **Philippine Commission (1901)** introduced a centralized public school system, with the **Thomasites** (American teachers) training Filipino educators. The **Morrill Land-Grant Acts** funded agricultural and engineering schools.
- **Post-Independence:** The **K–12 curriculum** (2012) expanded basic education to 12 years, aligning with global standards. The **K–4th Year system** (1945–2017) was phased out, with Kindergarten becoming compulsory in 2011.
- **Contemporary Issues:** The **COVID-19 pandemic** disrupted schooling, leading to learning loss and mental health challenges among students.

#### **Education Stages**
- **K–12 System:** Compulsory from ages 5–18, divided into:
  - **Pre-elementary (Kindergarten):** Ages 5–6.
  - **Elementary (Grades 1–6):** Ages 6–12.
  - **Junior High (Grades 7–10):** Ages 12–16.
  - **Senior High (Grades 11–12):** Ages 16–18.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools. **Public universities** (e.g., University of the Philippines) are state-funded, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Alternative Learning System (ALS):** Non-formal education for out-of-school youth, offering modular courses to earn certificates.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Funding and Infrastructure:** Schools lack adequate classrooms, textbooks, and internet access, particularly in rural areas. **COVID-19** exacerbated these challenges.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with lower attendance rates in conflict-affected areas.
- **Vocational Training:** Limited access to quality vocational programs, though the **Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)** aims to address this.
- **Language Policy:** The **Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE)** system promotes local languages, but implementation varies.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~32 million students aged 5–24 in 2020, with **K–12 enrollment rising from 24 million (2013–2014) to 28 million (2022–2023)**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is 95.9%, but secondary completion rates are lower (72.6%).
- **Learning Outcomes:** Only **10% of primary students** meet minimum reading standards, and **17%** achieve minimum math standards by the end of primary education.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **History**
- **19th Century:** Literacy campaigns and the **Royal Academy of Turku (1640)** laid the foundation for education. The **Finnish language** replaced Swedish as the primary medium of instruction.
- **20th Century:** The **comprehensive school system** (1972) replaced earlier models, emphasizing equality and student-centered learning. The **Bologna Process** (2006) aligned Finnish education with European standards.
- **21st Century:** Focus on **phenomenon-based learning** (2016) and **21st-century skills** (collaboration, critical thinking). Finland’s education system is globally recognized for its high standards.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Basic Education:** Compulsory for ages 7–16, divided into:
  - **Comprehensive School (Grades 1–9):** Ages 7–15.
  - **Upper Secondary Education (Grades 10–12):** Ages 15–18.
- **Higher Education:** Two sectors—**universities** (research-focused) and **universities of applied sciences** (practical, work-oriented). **Tuition fees** apply only to non-EU/EEA students.
- **Early Childhood Education:** Free universal daycare and preschool (ages 8 months–6 years), emphasizing play-based learning.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Teacher Quality:** Highly trained and respected, but **low pay** and **high workloads** contribute to teacher shortages.
- **Internationalization:** Finland exports its education model globally, with programs like **Education Finland** promoting Finnish pedagogies.
- **Digital Transformation:** Schools use technology to enhance learning, though **equity in access** remains a challenge.

#### **Statistics**
- **Literacy:** Nearly universal, with **93.1% of Finns aged 10–64** having basic literacy.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **24th in math, 19th in science, and 12th in reading** (2003), though recent improvements have been noted.
- **Higher Education:** **60–70% of each cohort** enters higher education, with **universities of applied sciences** focusing on vocational training.

### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **History**
- **Colonial Period:** British schools (e.g., **Penang Free School**) and **Malay College Kuala Kangsar** (1905) were established. The **Razak Report (1956)** introduced a national school system with Malay and English as primary languages.
- **Post-Independence:** The **Education Act (1996)** standardized the curriculum, and **Dual Language Program (DLP)** was introduced to teach Science and Mathematics in English.
- **Contemporary Issues:** **COVID-19** disrupted schooling, and **language policy debates** continue over the use of Malay, Chinese, and Tamil in schools.

#### **Education Stages**
- **K–12 System:** Compulsory from ages 5–18, divided into:
  - **Primary (Year 1–6):** Ages 6–12.
  - **Secondary (Form 1–5):** Ages 13–17.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools. **Public universities** (e.g., University Malaya) are highly subsidized, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Special Schools:** Include **Chinese Independent High Schools** (e.g., **UEC**) and **International Schools** (e.g., **IB Program**).

#### **Key Issues**
- **Language Policy:** Debates over **Dual Language Program (DLP)** and the use of Malay, Chinese, and Tamil in schools.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with **lower enrollment rates in rural areas**.
- **Vocational Training:** Limited access to quality vocational programs, though **polytechnics** aim to address this.
- **Standardized Testing:** The **Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia (SPM)** exam is highly competitive, with **high dropout rates** due to pressure.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~32 million students aged 5–24, with **K–12 enrollment rising from 24 million (2013–2014) to 28 million (2022–2023)**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is high, but secondary completion rates vary by region.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **below average** in math and science, with **learning poverty** affecting many students.

### **Education in South Africa**

#### **History**
- **Colonial Period:** Mission schools and **Bantu education** (1953) were introduced to educate Black Africans separately. The **Apartheid system** segregated schools by race.
- **Post-Apartheid:** The **South African Schools Act (1996)** and **National Education Policy Act (1996)** aimed to integrate schools and provide equal access. **Curriculum 2005** was later abandoned due to inefficiencies.
- **Contemporary Issues:** **COVID-19** disrupted schooling, and **school violence** remains a significant challenge.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Basic Education:** Compulsory from ages 7–16, divided into:
  - **Foundation Phase (Grades R–3):** Ages 5–8.
  - **Intermediate Phase (Grades 4–6):** Ages 8–11.
  - **Senior Phase (Grades 7–9):** Ages 11–14.
  - **Further Education and Training (Grades 10–12):** Ages 14–18.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, technikons, and vocational schools. **Public universities** are highly competitive, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Alternative Education:** Includes **home schooling** and **School of the Air** for remote students.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Funding and Infrastructure:** Schools lack adequate resources, particularly in rural areas. **COVID-19** exacerbated these challenges.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with **lower enrollment rates in rural areas**.
- **School Violence:** Gang violence and bullying are prevalent, with **40% of children** reporting being victims of crime at school.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low pay and **high workloads** contribute to teacher shortages.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~4 million students in primary and secondary schools, with **64% attending government schools**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is high, but secondary completion rates vary by region.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **below average** in math and science, with **high dropout rates** due to systemic issues.

### **Education in Romania**

#### **History**
- **19th Century:** Education became compulsory in 1864, with **Alexandru Ioan Cuza** introducing free primary education. The **communist era** influenced education, emphasizing political propaganda.
- **Post-Communism:** The **Bologna Process** (2006) aligned Romanian education with European standards. **COVID-19** disrupted schooling, leading to learning loss.
- **Contemporary Issues:** **Teacher shortages** and **funding issues** remain challenges.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Compulsory Education:** 14 years (from age 2 to 16), divided into:
  - **Kindergarten (Grades R–3):** Ages 3–6.
  - **Primary School (Grades 1–4):** Ages 6–9.
  - **Gymnasium (Grades 5–8):** Ages 9–13.
  - **High School (Grades 9–12):** Ages 13–18.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools. **Public universities** are highly competitive, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Alternative Education:** Includes **distance learning** and **vocational training**.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Funding and Infrastructure:** Schools lack adequate resources, particularly in rural areas. **COVID-19** exacerbated these challenges.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low pay and **high workloads** contribute to teacher shortages.
- **Language Policy:** Debates over the use of Romanian and other languages in schools.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with **lower enrollment rates in rural areas**.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~3.2 million students in 2013, with **compulsory education expanding to 14 years in 2020**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is high, but secondary completion rates vary by region.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **below average** in math and science, with **high dropout rates** due to systemic issues.

### **Education in Portugal**

#### **History**
- **19th Century:** Education became compulsory in 1864, with **Alexandru Ioan Cuza** introducing free primary education. The **Estado Novo** (1933–1974) expanded education but faced criticism for low quality.
- **Post-Revolution:** The **Carnation Revolution (1974)** led to the founding of new universities and the expansion of higher education. **COVID-19** disrupted schooling, leading to learning loss.
- **Contemporary Issues:** **Funding issues** and **teacher shortages** remain challenges.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Basic Education:** Compulsory from ages 6–18, divided into:
  - **1st Cycle (Grades 1–4):** Ages 6–9.
  - **2nd Cycle (Grades 5–6):** Ages 9–11.
  - **3rd Cycle (Grades 7–9):** Ages 11–14.
  - **Secondary Education (Grades 10–12):** Ages 14–18.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, polytechnics, and vocational schools. **Public universities** are highly competitive, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Alternative Education:** Includes **vocational training** and **distance learning**.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Funding and Infrastructure:** Schools lack adequate resources, particularly in rural areas. **COVID-19** exacerbated these challenges.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low pay and **high workloads** contribute to teacher shortages.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with **lower enrollment rates in rural areas**.
- **Language Policy:** Debates over the use of Portuguese and other languages in schools.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~4 million students in primary and secondary schools, with **higher education enrollment rising from 7% (2003) to 11% (2007)**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is high, but secondary completion rates vary by region.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **below average** in math and science, with **improvements since 2010**.

### **Education in Australia**

#### **History**
- **Colonial Period:** Mission schools and **Sekolah Pondok** were established. The **Gonski Report (2010)** recommended funding reforms to address educational inequities.
- **Post-Colonial:** The **Australian Curriculum** (2010) was introduced to standardize education. **COVID-19** disrupted schooling, leading to learning loss.
- **Contemporary Issues:** **Funding disparities** between states and **teacher shortages** remain challenges.

#### **Education Stages**
- **K–12 Education:** Compulsory from ages 5–18, divided into:
  - **Primary School (Grades 1–6):** Ages 5–11.
  - **Secondary School (Grades 7–12):** Ages 11–18.
- **Higher Education:** Includes universities, TAFE colleges, and vocational schools. **Public universities** are highly subsidized, while private institutions offer diverse programs.
- **Alternative Education:** Includes **home schooling** and **School of the Air** for remote students.

#### **Key Issues**
- **Funding and Infrastructure:** Schools lack adequate resources, particularly in rural areas. **COVID-19** exacerbated these challenges.
- **Educational Inequality:** Disparities exist between urban and rural regions, with **lower enrollment rates in rural areas**.
- **Teacher Quality:** Low pay and **high workloads** contribute to teacher shortages.
- **Standardized Testing:** The **NAPLAN** exam assesses basic skills, though **PISA scores** show below-average performance in math and science.

#### **Statistics**
- **Enrollment:** ~4 million students in primary and secondary schools, with **64% attending government schools**.
- **Completion Rates:** Primary education completion rate is high, but secondary completion rates vary by region.
- **PISA Scores:** Ranked **16th in reading, 29th in math, and 17th in science** (2018). **Learning poverty** affects many students.
