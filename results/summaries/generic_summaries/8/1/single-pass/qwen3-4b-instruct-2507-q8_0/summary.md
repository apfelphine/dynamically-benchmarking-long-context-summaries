### **Education in the Philippines**

**History:**
- **Pre-colonial:** Oral traditions and community-based learning; use of the Baybayin script; values like reverence, obedience, and truth.
- **Spanish Period:** Religious orders established schools; taught Christianity, Spanish, and culture; introduced printing in local scripts. Spanish education was initially religious, with schools for boys and girls, and later expanded to include vocational training.
- **First Public Schools (1863):** Free public education introduced; required for all Filipinos regardless of race or class; led to growth in public schools and a rise in Filipino intellectuals.
- **First Republic (1898):** Schools reopened after Spanish defeat; new institutions like the Instituto Burgos and Liceo de Manila established.
- **American Period:** Centralized system introduced; Thomasites (American teachers) trained Filipino teachers; high schools created to meet industrial needs; curriculum focused on practical skills.
- **Japanese Period (1942–1945):** Education was used for ideological indoctrination; schools reopened; vocational training emphasized.
- **Post-WWII to Present:** Education system evolved; the 1987 Constitution made elementary education compulsory; the K–12 system introduced in 2008, replacing the 10-year system with a 12-year cycle including kindergarten.

**Structure & Stages:**
- **K–12 System:** Includes kindergarten through Grade 12; divided into pre-elementary, elementary, junior high, and senior high.
- **Pre-elementary (Kindergarten):** Ages 5–6; taught in mother tongue, with emphasis on reading, writing, and numeracy.
- **Elementary (Grades 1–6):** Taught in mother tongue (early grades), with gradual transition to Filipino and English. Subjects include Math, Filipino, Science, and Social Studies.
- **Secondary (Grades 7–12):** Divided into junior high (Grades 7–10) and senior high (Grades 11–12). Senior high offers specialized tracks: Academic, Technical-Vocational-Livelihood (TVL), Sports, and Arts and Design.
- **Higher Education:** Tertiary institutions (public and private) offer degrees; the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) oversees quality and standards.

**Statistics:**
- 28 million students enrolled in K–12 (2022–2023); 640,000 out-of-school learners in Alternative Learning System (ALS).
- Completion rates: 95.9% primary, 81.7% lower secondary, 72.6% upper secondary.
- Learning outcomes: 93.1% basic literacy, 70.8% functional literacy; however, only 10% of primary students meet minimum reading standards.
- Disparities: Urban poor and conflict-affected areas have lower socio-emotional skills; regional literacy rates vary (e.g., Central Luzon: 92.8%, Bangsamoro: 81%).

**Issues:**
- **Affordability:** School supplies, transportation, and informal fees create financial barriers.
- **Education-employment mismatch:** Graduates often lack skills aligned with labor market needs; brain drain in healthcare.
- **Impact of Pandemic:** Learning loss due to school closures; mental health issues increased.
- **Inadequate Funding:** Underinvestment in classrooms, teacher salaries, and teaching materials.
- **Quality of Teachers:** Low passing rates in the Licensure Examination for Teachers (LET); teacher turnover and low pay are major concerns.
- **Lack of Classrooms:** Shortage of 148,000 classrooms; red tape and corruption cited as causes.

**Alternative Learning System (ALS):**
- Non-formal education for dropouts; includes diagnostic tests, remedial programs, and final assessments.
- Graduates receive certificates allowing access to formal education and employment.

**Indigenous Schools & Special Needs:**
- Indigenous learning systems recognized in the 1987 Constitution.
- Schools for the handicapped cater to children with intellectual, visual, hearing, and orthopedic impairments.

### **Education in Malaysia**

**History:**
- **Origins:** Early Islamic schools (Sekolah Pondok) and madrasahs existed before modern secular education.
- **British Colonial Period:** English-language schools established; Malay-medium schools introduced to serve the Malay elite; English-medium schools were phased out in favor of Malay-medium schools.
- **Decolonization & Independence (1957):** The Razak Report established a national school system with Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil-medium schools; bilingual education introduced.
- **Post-Independence:** Government promoted national language policies; English was made compulsory; the Dual Language Program (DLP) introduced to improve English proficiency in science and math.
- **Modern Era:** DLP expanded to include more subjects; debates over language policy continue.

**Structure & Stages:**
- **Public Schools:** Follow a national curriculum with Malay as the main medium; Chinese and Tamil schools are government-aided.
- **Private Schools:** Include Chinese Independent High Schools (SJK(C)) and other vernacular schools; offer Chinese or Tamil as medium of instruction.
- **Chinese Independent High Schools:** Offer a 6-year program with three junior and three senior levels; students take the Unified Examination Certificate (UEC).
- **International Schools:** Follow international curricula (e.g., IB, Cambridge); offer global education.
- **Dual Language Program (DLP):** Offers English instruction in key STEM subjects; aims to improve English proficiency and global competitiveness.

**Statistics:**
- 3.2 million students enrolled in 2013; 3.2 million students in 2023.
- 10% of students in Chinese Independent High Schools take the UEC; UEC is not recognized for entry into public universities.
- Language policy: English is mandatory in all schools; Chinese and Tamil are taught as elective subjects.

**Issues:**
- **Language Policy:** Controversy over Malay replacing Chinese/Tamil as medium of instruction; Chinese and Indian communities oppose this.
- **Racial Quotas:** 90% of university places reserved for Bumiputera (Malay) students; criticized as discriminatory.
- **Gender Gap:** 2:1 ratio of boys to girls in polytechnics and public higher education institutions.
- **Affordability:** High cost of private schools and international education; financial burden on students and families.
- **Quality of Education:** Concerns about standardized testing and curriculum rigidity; overemphasis on English and STEM.

**Schools & Curriculum:**
- **Public Schools:** Offer national curriculum; students take national exams (UPSR, SPM).
- **Private Schools:** Offer Chinese or Tamil-medium education; some offer international curricula.
- **Vernacular Schools:** Chinese and Tamil schools remain a significant part of the education system; government has not fully integrated them into the national system.

### **Education in the United States**

**History:**
- **19th Century:** New England led in public education reform; Horace Mann promoted teacher training and the "three Rs."
- **Post-Civil War:** High schools began to emerge; southern states lagged behind.
- **20th Century:** Compulsory education laws passed across states; the 1965 Elementary and Secondary Education Act provided federal funding; the 1975 Education for All Handicapped Children Act established special education.
- **21st Century:** No Child Left Behind Act (2001) emphasized standardized testing; Common Core State Standards introduced; pandemic disrupted learning.

**Structure & Stages:**
- **K–12 System:** Divided into elementary (K–5), middle/junior high (6–8), and high school (9–12).
- **Elementary School:** Focus on foundational skills in reading, writing, and math.
- **Middle School:** Transition to more specialized subjects; students often take standardized tests.
- **High School:** Offers academic, vocational, and technical tracks; students may take college preparatory courses.
- **Higher Education:** Over 4,000 institutions; public and private universities; degrees include bachelor's, master's, and doctoral.

**Statistics:**
- 89% of adults completed high school; 34% have a bachelor’s degree or higher.
- Average salary for college graduates exceeds $51,000.
- Reading literacy rate: 99%; science and math performance below average compared to other developed nations.

**Issues:**
- **Standardized Testing:** High-stakes testing has led to teaching to the test; criticism over equity and bias.
- **Teacher Shortage:** High turnover rates and low salaries; many teachers moonlight to supplement income.
- **Affordability:** College costs are rising; student loan debt exceeds $1.5 trillion.
- **Racial Achievement Gap:** Black and Hispanic students perform worse than white and Asian students in academic achievement.
- **School-to-Prison Pipeline:** Disproportionate incarceration of minority youth due to harsh disciplinary policies.
- **Inequality:** Disparities in access and outcomes based on race, income, and geography.

**Curriculum & Policies:**
- **Curriculum:** Varies widely by district; some schools offer religious instruction.
- **Standardized Testing:** Required in many states; used to assess school performance and teacher effectiveness.
- **Teacher Pay:** Average salary is lower than in other professions; teacher shortages persist due to low pay and job stress.

### **Education in Romania**

**Overview:**
- **Compulsory Education:** 14 years (from kindergarten to Grade 12); expanded in 2020 to include kindergarten and two years of high school.
- **Pre-primary Education:** Kindergarten for children aged 3–6; includes play-based learning and early socialization.
- **Primary and Secondary Education:** Divided into 5 years of primary (Grades 1–5), 4 years of middle school (Grades 6–9), and 2 years of high school (Grades 10–12).
- **Higher Education:** 56 public and 41 private institutions; follows the Bologna process with three cycles (bachelor’s, master’s, doctoral).

**Structure:**
- **Kindergarten:** Three groups (small, middle, big) based on age; includes foreign language, computer, and arts classes.
- **Primary School:** 5 years; includes math, language, science, and arts.
- **Middle School:** 4 years; includes more advanced subjects and specialized tracks.
- **High School:** Two tracks—academic (lukio) and vocational (ammattioppilaitos); students take national exams (Bacalaureat).

**Statistics:**
- Literacy rate: 97.6% (2021); higher among Roma and Turkish minorities.
- 3.2 million students enrolled in 2013; 3.2 million in 2023.
- 10% of students in rural areas do not attend school after Grade 8.

**Issues:**
- **Rural Education:** Limited access to schools; children often commute long distances.
- **Religious Education:** Controversial; parents can opt out; debates over whether religion should be taught in a confessional or historical way.
- **Funding:** Underfunded; parents contribute "class funds" (illegal in some cases).
- **School Violence:** Prevalent in some areas; physical and verbal aggression reported.

### **Education in Finland**

**Overview:**
- **Compulsory Education:** 14 years (from age 7 to 18); includes kindergarten, primary, and secondary education.
- **Early Childhood Education:** Highly valued; free universal daycare for children aged 8 months to 5 years.
- **Basic Education:** 9-year comprehensive school (Grades 1–9); small class sizes (up to 20 students); emphasis on play-based learning.
- **Upper Secondary Education:** 4 years (Grades 10–12); students choose between academic (lukio) or vocational (ammattioppilaitos) tracks.

**Structure:**
- **Kindergarten:** Focus on social and emotional development; children learn through play.
- **Comprehensive School:** Small classes; no high-stakes testing; students learn through inquiry and exploration.
- **Upper Secondary:** Academic programs prepare students for university; vocational programs prepare students for employment.

**Statistics:**
- Literacy rate: nearly universal (0.83% illiterate in 2021).
- 90% of brain growth occurs before age 5.
- 83% of upper academic school students complete the matriculation exam (Bacalaureat).

**Issues:**
- **Funding:** Public schools are well-funded; private schools receive limited support.
- **Teacher Training:** Teachers are highly trained and unionized; have significant autonomy.
- **Equity:** Strong focus on equity; all students receive the same educational opportunities regardless of background.

### **Education in Australia**

**Structure & Stages:**
- **Compulsory Education:** From age 5 to 17 (varies by state).
- **Primary and Secondary Education:** Divided into pre-primary (Kindergarten), primary (Grades 1–6), and secondary (Grades 7–12).
- **School Types:** Government, Catholic, and private schools; most students attend government schools.
- **Tertiary Education:** 1,185,450 students enrolled in universities and TAFE colleges.

**Statistics:**
- 4,086,998 students enrolled in 2023.
- 64% in government schools; 19.7% in Catholic schools; 16.3% in private schools.
- PISA rankings: 16th in reading, 29th in math, 17th in science (2018).

**Issues:**
- **Indigenous Education:** Significant disparities; only 73.8% of Indigenous students complete Year 12.
- **School Funding:** Inequities between urban and rural schools; underfunded in remote areas.
- **Bilingual Education:** Supported in Indigenous communities; bilingual programs in the Northern Territory have shown success.
- **Religious Education:** Controversial; some schools offer religious instruction; others do not.

### **Education in South Africa**

**Structure & Policies:**
- **Basic Education:** From Grade R (age 5–6) to Grade 12 (age 15–21); matriculation certificate required for higher education.
- **Higher Education:** 1 million students enrolled; universities and technikons offer degrees.
- **Historical Context:** Apartheid-era policies led to racial segregation in education; Bantu Education Act (1953) created inferior schools for Black students.

**Issues:**
- **Poverty and School Fees:** High fees prevent poor children from attending affluent schools.
- **Violence:** 40% of children report being victims of crime in schools; gang fighting and sexual assault are concerns.
- **Performance:** Poor academic performance due to weak institutional functionality, uneducated teachers, and lack of learning time.
- **ICT Use:** Limited access to technology in rural areas; internet and infrastructure remain barriers.

### **Education in Portugal**

**History:**
- **Pre-19th Century:** Christian clergy led education; illiteracy rates high.
- **19th–20th Century:** Literacy improved under the Estado Novo; illiteracy dropped from 80% to 97% by 1960.
- **Post-1974:** Education democratized; new universities founded; Bologna process adopted in 2006.

**Structure:**
- **Pre-primary (Creche/Jardim de Infância):** Optional; free in state-run schools.
- **Basic Education (Grades 1–9):** Divided into three cycles (1st–4th, 5th–6th, 7th–9th).
- **Secondary Education (Grades 10–12):** General, professional, or artistic tracks.
- **Higher Education:** University and polytechnic systems; degrees include licenciatura (Bachelor), mestrado (Master), and doutoramento (PhD).

**Statistics:**
- Literacy rate: 97% (1960); high in urban areas.
- 35% of university-age citizens attend higher education (2020).

**Issues:**
- **Private vs. Public Schools:** Private institutions are elite; access based on ability to pay.
- **Teacher Education:** Teachers must hold a Licenciado degree; some institutions lack rigorous standards.
- **School Violence:** Increasing in some areas; private schools have lower levels of violence.
- **Quality of Education:** Concerns about low standards in private institutions; some seen as diploma mills.

### **Common Themes Across Systems:**
- **Compulsory Education:** All systems have compulsory education, though duration and structure vary.
- **Language Policy:** Language of instruction and multilingual education are central to national identity and equity.
- **Teacher Quality:** A recurring issue across systems; teacher shortages, low pay, and lack of training affect outcomes.
- **Equity and Access:** Disparities in access based on race, income, geography, and socio-economic status.
- **Technology and Innovation:** Increasing use of technology in classrooms, especially during the pandemic.
- **Standardized Testing:** Used in many countries to assess performance, but criticized for promoting test-based learning and inequity.
- **Higher Education:** A major determinant of social mobility; access and affordability remain challenges.
