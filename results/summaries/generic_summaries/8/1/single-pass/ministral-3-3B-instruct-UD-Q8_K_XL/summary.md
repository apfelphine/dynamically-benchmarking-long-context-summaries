### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **History**
The education system in the Philippines evolved through colonial and post-colonial periods, shaped by Spanish, American, and later Japanese influences.

- **Pre-colonial Period**: Oral traditions, storytelling, and basic skills were taught in communities, with reverence for Bathala, obedience to authority, and respect for truth and righteousness as core values.
- **Spanish Period (1565–1898)**: Formal education was introduced by religious orders, establishing schools and universities. Spanish was taught alongside Christianity, and the first printing press was established in 1590. The Spanish Educational Decree of 1863 introduced free public education, though religious schools were common.
- **First Republic (1898–1902)**: The short-lived Philippine Independence movement established schools like the Instituto Burgos and the Academia Militar. The Malolos Constitution mandated free and compulsory education.
- **American Period (1898–1946)**: Public education expanded, with the establishment of the Philippine Normal School and the University of the Philippines. The K–12 curriculum was introduced in 2008, aligning with global standards.
- **Japanese Period (1942–1945)**: Education was indoctrinated with Japanese ideologies, with restrictions on English and promotion of vocational training.
- **Post-War Era**: The Philippines transitioned to a more centralized educational system, with the establishment of the Department of Education (DepEd) and the K–12 curriculum’s full implementation in 2012.

#### **Educational Stages**
The education system in the Philippines is structured into several stages:

- **Kindergarten**: Preparatory for primary education, focusing on foundational skills in mother tongue.
- **Primary Education (K–6)**: Compulsory, covering basic literacy, numeracy, and social studies.
- **Junior High School (Grades 7–10)**: Introduces specialized subjects and prepares students for senior high school.
- **Senior High School (Grades 11–12)**: Offers four tracks (Academic, Technical-Vocational-Livelihood, Sports, Arts and Design) with specialized curricula.
- **Higher Education**: Includes universities and colleges, with over 1,975 institutions offering diverse programs.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to Grade 12.
- **Curriculum**: The K–12 system emphasizes foundational skills and prepares students for tertiary education, employment, and entrepreneurship.
- **Disparities**: Significant educational disparities exist across regions and socioeconomic groups, affecting learning outcomes and access.
- **Funding and Infrastructure**: Schools face shortages of classrooms, textbooks, and learning equipment, impacting educational quality.
- **Teacher Quality**: Low teacher retention and inadequate teacher training contribute to educational challenges.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 32 million students aged 5 to 24 enrolled in schools in 2020.
- **Completion Rates**: Primary education completion rate is 95.9%, with lower rates for lower and upper secondary education.
- **Learning Outcomes**: Functional literacy rates are high, but foundational skills attainment remains low, with 10% of Grade 5 students achieving minimum reading standards and 17% achieving minimum mathematical standards.
- **International Comparisons**: Filipino students perform below average in mathematics, science, and reading compared to other developed countries.

### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **History**
Malaysia’s education system has evolved through British colonial rule, decolonization, and independence, influenced by policies aimed at national unity and cultural preservation.

- **Origins**: Early schools included Sekolah Pondok, madrasah, and Islamic schools, with early Malay literature indicating their existence before the colonial era.
- **British Colonial Period (1816–1957)**: Schools were established in Penang, Malacca, and Singapore, with early English-language schools like the Penang Free School. The British promoted Malay-language education to maintain control, though initially limiting secondary education to Malay students.
- **Decolonization and Independence (1957–1996)**: The Razak Report (1956) established a national school system with Malay-medium schools and English-medium schools. The Education Act of 1996 reformed the system, integrating vernacular schools into the national curriculum.
- **Post-Independence**: The system expanded to include Islamic schools, Chinese independent high schools, and international schools. The Dual Language Program (DLP) was introduced to enhance English proficiency in key subjects.

#### **Educational Stages**
The Malaysian education system is structured into several stages:

- **Preschool**: Optional, focusing on early childhood development.
- **Primary Education (Years 1–6)**: Compulsory, taught in Malay, English, Chinese, or Tamil mediums.
- **Secondary Education (Forms 1–5)**: Malay-medium schools use Malay as the main language, with English as a compulsory subject. The Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia (SPM) exam is taken at the end of Form 5.
- **Post-Secondary Education**: Includes matriculation (pre-university) and tertiary education, with options for universities, polytechnics, and vocational training.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **National Language Policy**: Malay is the national language, with English as a compulsory subject. The Dual Language Program (DLP) aims to enhance English proficiency in STEM subjects.
- **Vernacular Schools**: Chinese and Tamil vernacular schools are retained, though their status is debated due to concerns over cultural identity and national unity.
- **Compulsory Education**: Secondary education is not yet compulsory, though plans are in place to make it so by 2024.
- **Funding and Infrastructure**: Schools face challenges in maintaining adequate facilities and resources, particularly in rural areas.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 2.4 million students enrolled in primary and secondary education in 2023.
- **Completion Rates**: Secondary education completion rates are not yet fully established due to non-compulsory status.
- **Learning Outcomes**: Students perform well in international assessments like PISA, with strong results in science and mathematics.
- **International Comparisons**: Malaysia is recognized for its high-quality education system, particularly in STEM fields.

### **Education in the United States**

#### **History**
The U.S. education system has evolved through colonial times, the Civil War, and the 20th century, with significant influences from social movements and technological advancements.

- **19th Century**: Public education was encouraged by states like Massachusetts, with Horace Mann leading reforms. The Civil War led to the establishment of land-grant colleges for agriculture and engineering.
- **Early 20th Century**: Compulsory education laws were passed, and the Committee of Ten recommended a 12-year curriculum. The Tuskegee Normal and Industrial Institute was founded to train African-American teachers.
- **Mid-20th Century**: The Civil Rights Movement led to desegregation of schools, and the No Child Left Behind Act (2001) emphasized standardized testing.
- **21st Century**: The Great Recession led to budget cuts, and the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted education, leading to increased reliance on technology.

#### **Educational Stages**
The U.S. education system is divided into several stages:

- **Early Childhood Education**: Preschool and kindergarten, focusing on foundational skills.
- **K–12 Education**: Compulsory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with a focus on core subjects and standardized testing.
- **Higher Education**: Includes colleges and universities, with over 7,000 institutions offering diverse programs.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with varying ages depending on the state.
- **Standardized Testing**: The No Child Left Behind Act and subsequent reforms emphasize standardized testing to assess student performance.
- **Affordability**: High costs of higher education and disparities in access based on socioeconomic status.
- **Teacher Shortages**: Low teacher retention and inadequate salaries contribute to a shortage of qualified educators.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 76 million students enrolled in schools from kindergarten to graduate school in 2000.
- **Completion Rates**: 89% of adults have completed high school, with 34% holding a bachelor’s degree or higher.
- **Learning Outcomes**: U.S. students perform below average in science and mathematics compared to other developed countries, though reading literacy rates are high.
- **International Comparisons**: The U.S. ranks 10th among industrial countries for percentage of adults with college degrees.

### **Education in Romania**

#### **History**
Romania’s education system has been influenced by French and Soviet models, with significant changes post-communism.

- **19th Century**: Education became compulsory in 1864, with four years of primary school. Literacy campaigns eradicated illiteracy by the 1950s.
- **Communist Era**: The system was influenced by the Soviet model, with political propaganda and compulsory physical labor for students.
- **Post-Communism**: The system was reformed to align with European standards, with the introduction of the K–12 curriculum and Bologna Process reforms.

#### **Educational Stages**
The Romanian education system is structured into several stages:

- **Kindergarten**: Preschool education for children aged 3–6, with a preparatory school year (clasa pregătitoare) for ages 6–7.
- **Primary Education (Grades 1–9)**: Compulsory, covering general education and introducing professional subjects in the 9th grade.
- **Secondary Education (Grades 10–12)**: Offers academic and vocational tracks, with the Baccalaureate exam for university admission.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with recent expansions to include the preparatory school year and middle form.
- **Curriculum**: The system emphasizes continuous assessment and focuses on foundational skills and cultural values.
- **Rural Education**: Rural schools face challenges in providing adequate resources and infrastructure.
- **Teacher Quality**: Low teacher retention and inadequate training contribute to educational challenges.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 3.2 million students enrolled in the educational system in 2013.
- **Completion Rates**: High completion rates for primary education, with challenges in secondary education.
- **Learning Outcomes**: Functional literacy rates are high, with only 0.83% of the population being illiterate.
- **International Comparisons**: Romania ranks highly in international assessments, with four universities in the Top 700 globally.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **History**
Finland’s education system is renowned for its high quality and emphasis on student-centered learning.

- **19th Century**: Literacy campaigns and the introduction of the kansakoulu ("people's school") system.
- **20th Century**: The system expanded to include comprehensive schools and vocational education, with a focus on student-centered learning.
- **Post-Soviet Era**: The system continued to emphasize play-based learning, early childhood education, and holistic development.

#### **Educational Stages**
The Finnish education system is structured into several stages:

- **Early Childhood Education**: High-quality daycare and kindergarten for children aged 3–6, focusing on play-based learning.
- **Comprehensive School (Grades 1–9)**: Compulsory, covering general education and extracurricular activities.
- **Upper Secondary Education (Grades 10–12)**: Offers academic and vocational tracks, with the matriculation examination for university admission.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with recent expansions to include upper secondary education.
- **Curriculum**: Emphasizes play-based learning, student-centered approaches, and extracurricular activities.
- **Teacher Quality**: Highly trained and respected, with competitive admission to teaching programs.
- **International Comparisons**: Finland ranks highly in international assessments, with students performing well in science and mathematics.

### **Education in Australia**

#### **History**
Australia’s education system has evolved through colonial times, federation, and modern reforms.

- **Colonial Era**: Early schools were established by missionaries and settlers, with compulsory education introduced in the late 19th century.
- **Federation**: The Australian Qualifications Framework was introduced in 1995 to standardize qualifications across states and territories.
- **Modern Era**: The K–12 curriculum was introduced in 2010, with ongoing reforms to improve educational outcomes.

#### **Educational Stages**
The Australian education system is structured into several stages:

- **Preschool**: Optional, focusing on early childhood development.
- **Primary and Secondary Education (Years K–12)**: Compulsory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with a focus on core subjects and standardized testing.
- **Higher Education**: Includes universities and vocational education, with over 42 universities offering diverse programs.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with varying ages depending on the state.
- **Curriculum**: The Australian Curriculum sets expectations for what all young Australians should be taught.
- **Funding**: Schools face challenges in maintaining adequate facilities and resources, particularly in rural areas.
- **Teacher Quality**: Low teacher retention and inadequate salaries contribute to a shortage of qualified educators.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 4 million students enrolled in primary and secondary schools in 2023.
- **Completion Rates**: High completion rates for primary education, with challenges in secondary education.
- **Learning Outcomes**: Australian students perform well in international assessments, with high literacy and numeracy rates.
- **International Comparisons**: Australia ranks highly in educational outcomes, with students performing well in science and mathematics.

### **Education in South Africa**

#### **History**
South Africa’s education system has been shaped by colonialism, apartheid, and post-apartheid reforms.

- **Colonial Era**: Early schools were established by missionaries and settlers, with compulsory education introduced in the late 19th century.
- **Apartheid Era**: The system was segregated, with separate education for different racial groups. The Bantu Education Act of 1953 introduced a separate education system for black students.
- **Post-Apartheid**: The system was reformed to eliminate racial segregation and promote equality, with the introduction of the Curriculum 2005 and the National Education Policy Act.

#### **Educational Stages**
The South African education system is structured into several stages:

- **Foundation Phase (Grades R–3)**: Compulsory, covering basic literacy and numeracy.
- **Intermediate Phase (Grades 4–6)**: Introduces more advanced subjects.
- **Senior Phase (Grades 7–9)**: Prepares students for further education.
- **Further Education and Training (Grades 10–12)**: Offers academic and vocational tracks, with the matriculation certificate for university admission.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from Grade R to 12th grade, with recent expansions to include recurrent education programs.
- **Curriculum**: The system emphasizes continuous assessment and focuses on foundational skills and cultural values.
- **Indigenous Education**: Challenges in providing adequate resources and infrastructure in rural areas.
- **Teacher Quality**: Low teacher retention and inadequate training contribute to educational challenges.

#### **Statistics and Performance**
- **Enrollment**: Over 14 million students enrolled in primary and secondary schools in 2023.
- **Completion Rates**: High completion rates for primary education, with challenges in secondary education.
- **Learning Outcomes**: Significant disparities exist between Indigenous and non-Indigenous students, with low performance in international assessments.
- **International Comparisons**: South Africa faces significant challenges in educational outcomes, with low literacy and numeracy rates.

### **Education in Portugal**

#### **History**
Portugal’s education system has evolved through colonial times, the Carnation Revolution, and modern reforms.

- **Colonial Era**: Early schools were established by the Catholic Church, with compulsory education introduced in the 19th century.
- **Carnation Revolution**: The system was reformed to eliminate colonial influences and promote equality, with the introduction of the Plan for Popular Education.
- **Modern Era**: The Bologna Process was adopted in 2006 to align with European standards, with ongoing reforms to improve educational outcomes.

#### **Educational Stages**
The Portuguese education system is structured into several stages:

- **Pre-primary Education**: Optional, focusing on early childhood development.
- **Basic Education (Grades 1–9)**: Compulsory, covering general education and introducing professional subjects in the 9th grade.
- **Secondary Education (Grades 10–12)**: Offers academic and vocational tracks, with the Certificado de Habilitações do Ensino Secundário for university admission.

#### **Key Features and Challenges**
- **Compulsory Education**: Mandatory from kindergarten to 12th grade, with recent expansions to include recurrent education programs.
- **Curriculum**: The system emphasizes continuous assessment and focuses on foundational skills and cultural values.
- **Higher Education**: The Bologna Process has reformed higher education to align with European standards, with ongoing challenges in funding and quality.
- **International Comparisons**: Portugal faces challenges in educational outcomes, with low literacy and numeracy rates compared to other European countries.
