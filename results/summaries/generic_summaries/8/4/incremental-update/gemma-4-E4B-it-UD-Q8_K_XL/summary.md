Global education systems exhibit structural diversity (e.g., Australia's decentralized vs. Portugal's integrated model) and face varied challenges, such as infrastructure gaps in South Africa or systemic issues in Romania.

### National System Overviews
*   **Philippines:** Operates a K–12 framework with free secondary education and ALS support, but struggles with low primary reading standards, a large out-of-school population (1.6 million in 2023), funding gaps, corruption, and teacher quality issues.
*   **Malaysia:** Mandates primary education and is expanding to secondary by 2024, while managing linguistic diversity (Malay, Chinese, Tamil), gender gaps in higher education, and affirmative action for Bumiputera students.
*   **Finland:** Features a fully state-funded, compulsory system (ages 7-18) that emphasizes phenomenon-based learning and AI integration, requiring K-12 educators to hold a selective master's degree.
*   **United States:** Education has evolved via landmark legislation (e.g., *Brown v. Board*), with compulsory schooling typically until ages 16 or 18. System performance is closely tied to socioeconomic status, and OECD rankings are low.

### Deep Dive into U.S. Education
**Governance, Funding, and Higher Ed:**
K-12 power is decentralized, with states primarily funding public education, while federal oversight exists. Post-secondary education is marked by high costs and substantial student loan debt (\$1.5 trillion by 2018), along with "credential inflation." Furthermore, the Supreme Court banned race-based admissions in 2023.

**K-12 Challenges, Equity, and Outcomes:**
Curricula vary despite standardized testing mandates (NAEP). Persistent issues include academic dishonesty, debates over literacy instruction validity, and privacy concerns. Achievement gaps persist across ethnicity, with Black and Hispanic students often scoring lower and having higher dropout rates than Asian and Caucasian students, a disparity rooted in income inequality and historical discrimination. Immigrant students' grade placement often relies on birthdates rather than demonstrated knowledge, and weak English-language (EL) support can lead to long-term Limited English Proficiency (LEP) status. Systemic inequality is severe, with gaps creating costs equivalent to a permanent recession, and the school-to-prison pipeline disproportionately affects disadvantaged youth.

**Teaching and Parental Involvement:**
Teacher shortages are exacerbated by low pay, burnout, and job dissatisfaction, requiring proposed solutions like better mentorship and support systems. Despite Americans borrowing and buying more books than many European peers, teachers cite low parental involvement in early childhood education, which is linked to serious outcomes; notably, a 2003 survey indicated that 70% of incarcerated individuals in the US could not read above a fourth-grade level, illustrating the connection between reading failure and criminal activity.
