### **Australia, Portugal, Romania, South Africa, Malaysia, Finland, Philippines, United States: Comparative Education Systems**

Australia’s education system, with **9,477 schools** (64% government, 36% non-government), faces disparities in **maths performance (51% gaps)**, **school violence (48% incidents)**, and **Indigenous attainment gaps (51% Year 12 completion in remote areas)**. Despite **NAPLAN/PISA strengths in reading/science (9th globally in 2022)**, reforms under **$20B+ BFSA/Gonski 2.0 funding** aim to address systemic inequities, including **teacher shortages and funding gaps**. Higher education includes **37 public universities (26.7% international students)** and **132+ TAFE/VET providers**, generating **$94B/year**, with primary teachers requiring a **4-year Licenciado degree** and secondary teachers needing a **Licenciado in Education or related field**.

Malaysia’s system integrates **Islamic Religious Schools (SRA/SMKA/SAR)**, **vernacular schools**, and **public/private options**, with **UCSCAM opposing a unified Malay-medium system**. Reforms include **reduced school uniforms, compulsory English by 2016, and expanded B40 access**, while **non-bumiputera university seats are capped at 10%**. Colonial policies preserved ethnic identities, but **multilingual education remains contentious**.

Finland’s **9-year compulsory school (ages 7–18)** emphasizes **small classes (≤20 students), minimal grading, and universal free education**, with **two languages (Finnish/Swedish) + arts/outdoor activities**. Upper secondary (15–18) is now compulsory, offering **vocational or academic paths**, and **merit-based tertiary access** ensures equity. The Philippines’ **K–12 system (13 years)** expanded but struggles with **low preschool attendance (30.5%)**, **6% out-of-school students**, and **regional disparities (e.g., Bangsamoro at 81% literacy)**. **Islamic schools** follow the **SMC curriculum**, while **specialized high-school tracks (STEM, Arts, Vocational)** cater to elite students, though **teacher shortages and budget cuts** strain quality.

South Africa’s system grapples with **funding disparities, historical segregation, and infrastructure gaps (22% internet access)**, while **Portugal faces functional illiteracy (48%)** and **teacher qualifications requiring a Mestre (since 2007)**. **Romania’s rural absenteeism (25%)** and **limited kindergarten access** highlight equity challenges, contrasting with **Finland’s universal free education model**.

### **United States: Education System Challenges & Inequities**
The U.S. system, shaped by **19th-century reforms**, persists with **21st-century inequities**, including:
- **Funding disparities**: Despite **$11,000/student spending**, the U.S. ranks **37th in GDP education investment**, with **local/state taxes (86%)** exacerbating gaps. **Judicial reforms** (*McCleary v. Washington*, *Abbott v. Burke*) push for equity.
- **Literacy & Crime Links**: **70% of U.S. prison inmates** struggle with **basic reading (below 4th grade)**, tied to **academic failure and crime**, despite **higher book borrowing and purchases** than many European peers.
- **Systemic Barriers**: **Teacher shortages (14–16% turnover)**, **underfunded schools**, and **bias in standardized testing** (e.g., **college-readiness gaps by race/socioeconomic status**) persist. **Credential inflation risks** and **affirmative action bans** (*SFFA v. Harvard*) further limit access.
- **Parental Engagement Gaps**: **Insufficient early education supervision** hampers literacy development, while **birthday-based grading** misaligns with immigrant students’ readiness.
- **Curriculum & Language**: **English-only policies** dominate, with **limited EL interventions** worsening **LEP status**. **Foundational knowledge debates** (e.g., math rigor) and **restrictive policies** (e.g., Florida’s *Parental Rights in Education Act*) limit equity.
