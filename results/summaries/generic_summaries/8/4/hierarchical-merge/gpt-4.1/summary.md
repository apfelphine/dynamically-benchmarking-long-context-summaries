## Comparative Overview of Global Education Systems

Education systems across Australia, Portugal, Romania, South Africa, Malaysia, Finland, the Philippines, and the United States reveal both common challenges and distinct features shaped by history, policy, and socioeconomic contexts. Most countries mandate free and compulsory education, with structures ranging from centralized oversight (Portugal, Finland, Malaysia) to federal-state coordination (Australia, U.S.). Persistent challenges include regional disparities, funding gaps, under-resourced rural or minority communities, achievement and attainment gaps, and ongoing reforms targeting inclusion, equity, and modernization. Vocational and academic tracks are prevalent, with international models like the Bologna Process influencing tertiary education in Europe. Digital literacy, teacher training, and infrastructure remain focal points for improvement, especially following the COVID-19 pandemic.

### Country Highlights

- **Australia** balances national standards and local administration, but faces funding inequities for Indigenous and remote students.
- **Portugal** has improved access since the 1970s, but still contends with lower attainment rates and adult illiteracy.
- **Romania** struggles with rural and minority disparities, quality and funding gaps, and the impact of student migration.
- **South Africa** deals with legacy inequalities, infrastructure shortages, and high dropout rates, further strained by the pandemic.
- **Malaysia** reflects colonial and racial influences in its diverse school types and policies, with ongoing debates around inclusivity and language instruction.
- **Finland** leads in quality and innovation, emphasizing teacher training, minimal homework, and export of its educational model, though some reforms face internal debate.
- **Philippines** has expanded access via a comprehensive K–12 system, but faces significant challenges including regional inequities, low teacher salaries, and brain drain.
- **United States** features decentralized governance and world-renowned universities but suffers from significant resource inequalities, achievement gaps, high student debt, and literacy concerns tied to broader social outcomes.

Across these systems, reforms aim to address professional qualifications, inclusivity, and modern learning demands, while striving to overcome the entrenched issues of resource disparity, access, and equity in educational outcomes.
