# Education Systems Across Selected Countries

### Australia, Portugal, and Romania
Australia’s education system operates largely at the state level with nationally unified curricula and qualifications, offering compulsory schooling through mid-teens and addressing challenges like Indigenous education disparities and school violence. It is noted for strong international rankings and a sizable international student population. Similarly, Portugal provides general, artistic, and vocational secondary tracks regulated by national exams and centrally coordinated admissions, yet contends with high dropout rates and resource inefficiencies. Romania offers compulsory elementary education, with vocational and military secondary options, concluding with a national Baccalaureate exam; it faces urban-rural disparities in funding and access. All three systems emphasize structured pathways and European standards alignment, with Australia distinct in Indigenous issues focus and both Portugal and Romania working toward modernization and equity improvements.

### Romania and South Africa
Romania’s higher education comprises mainly public universities, harmonized with Western curricula and active in European mobility, though rural underfunding and dropout rates present access challenges and contribute to brain drain. South Africa’s education spans Basic and Higher levels managed by distinct agencies. Post-apartheid reforms have expanded access, but persistent rural-urban and quality disparities remain. Over a million higher education students confront graduate unemployment tied to skills mismatches. The government is advancing digital infrastructure and teacher training, accelerated by COVID-19 revelations of educational inequities.

### Malaysia
Malaysia’s multicultural education system includes Malay, Chinese, Tamil vernacular, Islamic religious, Chinese independent, and international schools, with compulsory primary education offered in multiple languages. Secondary education offers diverse national and international academic tracks. Language policy seeks balance among Malay, English, and mother tongues, though ethnic disparities and affirmative action favoring Bumiputeras persist. Its higher education includes public research universities, private institutions, and foreign branches, guided by National Education Blueprints focused on quality and equity.

### Finland and the Philippines: Contrasting Models
Finland exemplifies a highly equitable system offering free, inclusive education from early childhood to university, with merit-based admissions, holistic social support, innovative curricula including phenomenon-based learning and AI literacy, plus multilingualism. The Philippines, with a history shaped by Spanish and American colonialism, provides free public primary education using mother tongue instruction early on before transitioning to Filipino and English. The 2017 K–12 extension and 2023 “Matatag curriculum” emphasize Filipino identity, governance decentralization, and vocational-academic pathways. Despite a 93.1% basic literacy rate, challenges include regional disparities, poor senior high retention, underfunding, and learning losses exacerbated by COVID-19. Alternative learning programs aid out-of-school youth, but brain drain and employment mismatches remain significant issues.

# Education in the United States
The U.S. education system, primarily state and locally governed, features compulsory K–12 education and a large, diverse higher education sector of over 7,000 institutions. Public schools educate 85% of K–12 students with about a 90% graduation rate; roughly 35% complete bachelor’s degrees. Funded mainly through property taxes, it exhibits significant equity gaps favoring wealthier districts. Despite high per-student spending, U.S. educational outcomes are average internationally with persistent racial and socioeconomic achievement disparities. Challenges include low parental literacy engagement, politicized curricula, equity in standardized testing, teacher shortages, and safety concerns. Libraries play a key role in literacy promotion, and ongoing efforts aim to improve equitable funding, teacher support, and systemic equity to elevate educational quality and access nationwide.
