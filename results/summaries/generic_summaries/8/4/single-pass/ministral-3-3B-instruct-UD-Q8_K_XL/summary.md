### **Education in Australia**

#### **Timeline of Compulsory Education**
Australia introduced compulsory education gradually from 1871, with Western Australia being the first state to mandate it. By 1900, all states had implemented compulsory education for primary and secondary levels.

#### **Education System Structure**
- **Primary and Secondary Education**: Government schools educate ~64% of students, while non-government schools (Catholic and independent) educate ~36%. Tertiary education is heavily subsidized through student loan programs (HECS).
- **National Framework**: The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) unifies qualifications across higher education, vocational education (VET), and school-based education. The national curriculum is progressively implemented since 2010.
- **International Education**: Australia is the third-largest provider of international education globally, with strong growth in student numbers.

#### **Regulation, Funding, and Funding**
- **Regulation**: Education is managed by states and territories, with federal government funding for non-government schools, public universities, and tertiary education subsidies.
- **Funding**: Federal and state governments fund education, with federal contributions for non-government schools and public universities. The Gonski Report (2010) proposed a national funding agreement, though implementation was delayed.
- **School Year**: The academic year runs from late January/early February to early/mid-December for primary and secondary schools, and late February to mid-November for universities.

#### **Preschool Education**
- **Historical Context**: Preschool education is relatively recent, with preschools historically informal and unregulated.
- **Current Status**: Federally encouraged since 2009, preschools offer quality early childhood education and care. Government-funded preschools provide 15 hours per week for children aged 4–5.
- **Types of Preschools**: Mostly state-run, though some are run by local councils, community groups, or private organizations.

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **Enrollment and Staffing**: In 2023, 4,086,998 students were enrolled in 9,629 primary, secondary, and special schools. Government schools educate 64% of students, while Catholic (19.7%) and independent (16.3%) schools educate the rest.
- **Compulsory Attendance**: Compulsory attendance is mandated from age 6 to 17, depending on the state or territory.
- **Curriculum**: The Australian Curriculum, Assessment, and Reporting Authority (ACARA) administers the national curriculum, covering subjects like English, math, science, and social studies. Specialized schools include selective, special, and specialist schools.
- **School Uniforms**: Most schools require students to wear prescribed uniforms.

#### **Qualifications and Assessment**
- **National Qualifications**: The Senior Secondary Certificate of Education is issued by each state/territory. Vocational education and training (VET) qualifications are recognized through the AQF.
- **NAPLAN**: The National Assessment Program – Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN) is a standardized test assessing reading, writing, language, and numeracy skills. It is administered annually to students in Years 3, 5, 7, and 9.

#### **School Violence and Religious Education**
- **School Violence**: School violence remains a concern, with incidents of physical and sexual assaults reported. Programs like Hlayiseka aim to curb violence.
- **Religious Education**: Australia is a secular country, but religious instruction varies by state. Special Religious Education classes are available in some government schools.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Higher Education**: Tertiary education includes universities and vocational education (TAFE). Public universities are heavily subsidized, with student loans (HECS) for those who do not qualify for grants.
- **International Students**: Australia has a high ratio of international students, with strong growth in tertiary education sectors. The government invests significantly in international education.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **School Funding**: The Gonski Report (2010) proposed a national funding agreement, but implementation was delayed. Recent funding agreements aim to increase federal support for public schools.
- **Indigenous Education**: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children face significant educational disadvantages, including lower literacy and numeracy levels, school attendance, and Year 12 attainment rates.
- **Bilingual Education**: Bilingual education programs exist for Indigenous languages and immigrant communities, though funding and implementation vary.
- **School Violence**: Persistent issues with school violence, including bullying and physical assaults, are addressed through programs and policies.

### **Education in Portugal**

#### **Historical Context**
- **Early Education**: Portuguese universities date back to 1290, with early education influenced by the Christian clergy. Literacy rates were low until the 20th century.
- **20th Century**: Illiteracy rates were high under authoritarian regimes, but literacy campaigns in the 1950s significantly reduced illiteracy. Post-revolution reforms expanded education access.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Preschool**: Optional from ages 3–5, with free state-run kindergartens (Jardins de Infância). Private preschools are common.
- **Basic Education**: Nine years of schooling divided into three cycles (1st–4th, 5th–6th, 7th–9th). Compulsory from age 6.
- **Secondary Education**: Compulsory since 2012, consisting of three-year cycles after basic education. Students can choose between general, professional, or artistic programs.
- **Higher Education**: Divided into university and polytechnic systems. The Bologna Process has reformed higher education, introducing licenciatura (bachelor’s), mestrado (master’s), and doutoramento (doctorate) degrees.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Basic Education**: Covers subjects like Portuguese, math, science, history, and foreign languages. Exams are taken at the end of each cycle.
- **Secondary Education**: Students take national exams at the end of 11th and 12th grades. Admission to higher education is based on exam scores and grades.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is competitive, with exams and grades determining entry. Universities and polytechnics offer research-oriented and practical programs, respectively.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Education Quality**: Concerns over low higher education participation rates and quality. The Bologna Process has been contested by students and educators.
- **Teacher Training**: Teachers must have a master’s degree, but there are concerns about teacher quality and retention.
- **School Violence**: School violence has increased, though private schools generally report lower levels of violence.
- **International Schools**: Private international schools cater to expatriate families, offering curricula from various countries.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Policy Instability**: Education policies have been erratic, leading to concerns about long-term planning and effectiveness.
- **Functional Illiteracy**: High rates of functional illiteracy among adults, particularly in lower-income groups.
- **Private Universities**: Criticisms of private universities, including concerns about academic integrity and quality.

### **Education in Romania**

#### **Overview**
- **Compulsory Education**: Compulsory for 15 years, from kindergarten to Grade 12. Historically influenced by French and Soviet models.
- **School Structure**: Divided into kindergarten, primary, secondary, and vocational schools. Kindergarten is optional but widely attended.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Kindergarten**: Offers preschool education for children aged 3–6. Compulsory in 2020 for children aged 5–6.
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6, with a preparatory class (clasa pregătitoare) for children aged 6–7. Compulsory from age 6.
- **Secondary School**: Grades 7–12, with a transition to vocational education after Grade 12. Compulsory from age 14.
- **Vocational Education**: Offers specialized training in technical, agricultural, and service fields.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–3 use a qualitative grading system (FB, B, S, N/I). Grades 4–12 use a 1–10 grading system.
- **Secondary School**: Students take national exams (Evaluarea Națională) at the end of Grade 8. Graduation requires passing the Baccalaureate exam (Examenul Național de Bacalaureat).
- **Foreign Languages**: English is the most widely taught foreign language, followed by French and German.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Rural Education**: Significant disparities in educational opportunities between rural and urban areas. Many rural schools lack resources and infrastructure.
- **Teacher Quality**: Concerns over teacher quality, including low salaries and inadequate training.
- **School Violence**: School violence is a growing issue, with incidents of bullying and physical aggression.
- **Indigenous Education**: Limited support for indigenous languages and cultures in schools.

#### **International Comparisons**
- **Literacy**: Almost universal literacy, with low illiteracy rates among the general population but higher rates among Roma and Turkish minorities.
- **Performance**: Romania ranks highly in international assessments but faces challenges in improving educational outcomes.

### **Education in South Africa**

#### **Structure and Policies**
- **Basic Education**: Free and compulsory from Grade 1 to 12. Divided into General Education and Training (GET) and Further Education and Training (FET).
- **Higher Education**: Free public education, with the National Qualifications Framework (NQF) regulating tertiary education. Universities and technikons are autonomous institutions.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Kindergarten**: Grade R, which is compulsory but not all primary schools offer it.
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6, with a focus on foundational skills in reading, writing, and numeracy.
- **Secondary School**: Grades 7–12, with a transition to FET after Grade 10. Compulsory from age 7.
- **Vocational Education**: Offers specialized training in technical and service fields.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Primary School**: Students take the Primary School Achievement Test (UPSR) at the end of Grade 6.
- **Secondary School**: Students take the Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia (SPM) at the end of Grade 12. Specialized schools offer unique curricula in science, arts, and sports.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is based on matriculation grades and entrance exams.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Funding**: Underfunded schools, particularly in rural and low-income areas. High dropout rates and poor learning outcomes.
- **Language**: Language policies have led to debates over the use of indigenous languages in schools.
- **Racial Quotas**: Historical racial quotas in higher education have been abolished, but concerns remain about equity.
- **School Violence**: High levels of school violence, including gang-related incidents and bullying.

### **Education in Malaysia**

#### **History**
- **Early Education**: Madrasah and Sekolah Pondok were the earliest forms of schooling. British colonial policies influenced education, leading to the establishment of English-medium schools.
- **Post-Independence**: The Razak Report (1956) established a national education system with Malay and English as mediums of instruction. The Dual Language Program (DLP) was introduced to address language issues.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Preschool**: Optional, with most children starting at age 4. Free government preschools are available.
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6, with a focus on foundational skills. Compulsory from age 6.
- **Secondary School**: Grades 7–12, with a transition to pre-university education after Grade 12. Compulsory from age 12.
- **Vocational Education**: Offers specialized training in technical and service fields.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Primary School**: Students take the Primary School Achievement Test (UPSR) at the end of Grade 6.
- **Secondary School**: Students take the Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia (SPM) at the end of Grade 12. Specialized schools offer unique curricula in science, arts, and sports.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is based on matriculation grades and entrance exams. The Dual Language Program (DLP) offers English instruction in key subjects.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Language**: Debates over the use of Malay and English in schools. The Dual Language Program (DLP) aims to address language issues.
- **Racial Quotas**: Historical racial quotas in higher education have been abolished, but concerns remain about equity.
- **School Violence**: School violence is a growing issue, with incidents of bullying and physical aggression.
- **International Schools**: Private international schools cater to expatriate families, offering curricula from various countries.

### **Education in Finland**

#### **Overview**
- **Compulsory Education**: Nine years of comprehensive school from Grade 1 to 9. Upper secondary education (lukio) is compulsory from age 15.
- **Early Childhood Education**: High-quality daycare and preschool education are widely available and free. Focuses on play-based learning and holistic development.
- **Higher Education**: Divided into universities and universities of applied sciences. Admission is based on high school grades and entrance exams.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Comprehensive School**: Grades 1–9, with a focus on foundational skills in reading, writing, and numeracy.
- **Upper Secondary Education**: Grades 10–12, with a focus on academic and vocational training. Compulsory from age 15.
- **Vocational Education**: Offers specialized training in technical and service fields.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Comprehensive School**: Students receive grades from 4 to 10. No high-stakes tests.
- **Upper Secondary Education**: Students take the matriculation examination (Ylioppilastutkinto) at the end of Grade 12.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is based on high school grades and entrance exams. Universities focus on research, while universities of applied sciences focus on practical training.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Teacher Quality**: High standards for teachers, with a focus on professional development and continuous learning.
- **International Comparisons**: Finland ranks highly in international assessments but faces challenges in improving educational outcomes.
- **School Violence**: Low levels of school violence compared to other countries.

### **Education in the Philippines**

#### **History**
- **Pre-Colonial**: Oral traditions and baybayin script were used for education. Education was primarily family-based.
- **Spanish Period**: Formal education was introduced by religious orders. The Spanish Educational Decree of 1863 established free public education.
- **American Period**: The K–12 curriculum was introduced in 2008, replacing the K–10 system. The K–12 system includes Kindergarten and 12 years of primary and secondary education.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Kindergarten**: Compulsory from 2011. Focuses on foundational skills in reading, writing, and numeracy.
- **Primary School**: Grades 1–6, with a focus on foundational skills. Compulsory from age 6.
- **Secondary School**: Grades 7–12, with a transition to pre-university education after Grade 12. Compulsory from age 12.
- **Vocational Education**: Offers specialized training in technical and service fields.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Primary School**: Students take the National Elementary Achievement Test (NEAT) at the end of Grade 6.
- **Secondary School**: Students take the National Achievement Test (NAT) at the end of Grades 3, 6, 10, and 12. Specialized schools offer unique curricula in science, arts, and sports.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is based on high school grades and entrance exams. The K–12 system includes a new curriculum for all schools nationwide.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Funding**: Underfunded schools, particularly in rural and low-income areas. High dropout rates and poor learning outcomes.
- **Language**: Mother tongue-based multilingual education (MTB-MLE) is used in primary schools. English is the primary language of instruction in secondary schools.
- **School Violence**: School violence is a growing issue, with incidents of bullying and physical aggression.
- **Teacher Quality**: Concerns over teacher quality, including low salaries and inadequate training.

### **Education in the United States**

#### **Overview**
- **Compulsory Education**: Compulsory from age 6 to 16 (18 in some states). Free public education from Kindergarten to 12th Grade.
- **Higher Education**: Over 4,495 colleges and universities, with a focus on research and practical training. Admission is based on high school grades, entrance exams, and extracurricular activities.

#### **Education Stages**
- **Preschool**: Optional, with many community-based programs and private preschools.
- **Elementary School**: Grades K–5, with a focus on foundational skills in reading, writing, and numeracy.
- **Middle School**: Grades 6–8, with a focus on academic and vocational training.
- **High School**: Grades 9–12, with a focus on academic and vocational training. Compulsory from age 15.

#### **Curriculum and Assessment**
- **Elementary and Secondary School**: Students take standardized tests to assess academic performance. High school graduation requires passing state-mandated exams.
- **Higher Education**: Admission is based on high school grades, entrance exams (SAT/ACT), and extracurricular activities. Universities focus on research, while community colleges offer practical training.

#### **Issues and Challenges**
- **Funding**: Underfunded schools, particularly in rural and low-income areas. High dropout rates and poor learning outcomes.
- **Standardized Testing**: Controversial due to concerns over equity and fairness. High-stakes testing can lead to grade inflation and cheating.
- **Teacher Quality**: Concerns over teacher quality, including low salaries and inadequate training.
- **Affirmative Action**: Controversial policies regarding race-based admissions in higher education.
- **School Violence**: School violence is a growing issue, with incidents of bullying and physical aggression.
