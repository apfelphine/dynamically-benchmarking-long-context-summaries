### **Education in Australia**

Australia has a national education system underpinned by the **Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF)**, covering school-based, vocational, and tertiary education. Compulsory education began in the 1870s, with full implementation by 1916. Government schools educate ~64% of students, while non-government schools (mainly Catholic and private) account for the rest. The **Australian Curriculum** (introduced in 2010) provides a national framework for primary and secondary education, with a focus on equity and quality.

**Preschool** is not compulsory but encouraged by federal policy, with programs required to follow the **Early Years Learning Framework**. Attendance is high (85.7% in the year before formal schooling), with government-funded programs available.

**Primary and secondary education** spans from pre-Year 1 to Year 12, with most students attending government schools. The academic year runs from late January to mid-December for primary/secondary and from late February to mid-November for tertiary institutions. The **national year 10 to 12 retention rate** (ARR) is 79.9%, indicating student engagement.

**Tertiary education** is primarily university-based, with public universities subsidized via a **HECS loan system**. Australia ranks 3rd globally in international education. In the **2018 PISA**, Australian students ranked 16th in reading, 29th in maths, and 17th in science, a decline in performance. However, the **Education Index (2018)** ranked Australia 0.929—second-highest in the world—highlighting strong human development outcomes.

**Funding** has been a major issue, with the **Gonski Reports (2010 and 2018)** calling for equitable funding, leading to a **Better and Fairer Schools Agreement (BFSA)** in 2025, raising public school funding to 25% of total costs.

**Indigenous education** faces significant disparities, with lower attainment in literacy, numeracy, and Year 12 completion. The government has set targets to close the gap, including improving early childhood education and school attendance.

**Bilingual education** is supported in the Northern Territory, with programs like "both-ways" teaching in Yirrkala and Areyonga schools, though policies have fluctuated.

**Religious education** in government schools is limited, with special classes allowed under state legislation. School violence remains a concern, with 48% of principals reporting physical incidents in 2024.

### **Education in Portugal**

Portuguese education is compulsory from age 6 to 16, with a structure of **General Education (Grades R–9)** and **Secondary Education (Grades 10–12)**. The **National Education Policy Act (1996)** and **Schools Act (1996)** provide legal frameworks for access, equity, and governance.

**Kindergarten (Grade R)** is mandatory and free, with a focus on play-based learning. The **Foundation Phase** (Grades R–3) is followed by the **Intermediate Phase** (Grades 4–6) and **Senior Phase** (Grades 7–9). Students must pass national exams (e.g., **Exame Nacional**) to enter secondary education.

**Secondary education** is divided into **General, Technological, and Artistic** tracks. Students take national exams in **Portuguese, Mathematics, and Science**. The **Baccalaureate (Bac)** is required for university admission.

**Private and religious schools** are common, with a mix of public and private institutions. The **National Curriculum** emphasizes language, history, and cultural studies. The **Dual Language Program (DLP)** is being introduced to improve English proficiency.

**Rural education** remains a challenge, with many children in remote areas facing transportation and school access issues. The **Constitutional Court (2014)** ruled that parents must opt-in for religious instruction.

**School violence** is a persistent issue, with reports of bullying and physical aggression. The **Hlayiseka program** aims to reduce violence.

**Higher education** is structured under the **Bologna Process**, with three cycles (Bachelor, Master, PhD). The **National Qualifications Framework** ensures national consistency. The **University of Lisbon, University of Coimbra, and University of Porto** are among the most prestigious.

**Language policy** has evolved, with English becoming a key subject. However, the **Bilingual Program** remains limited, and debates continue over language equity.

### **Education in Romania**

Romania’s education system is **compulsory from age 6 to 18**, with a structure of **General Education (Grades 1–9)** and **Further Education and Training (Grades 10–12)**. The **National Education Policy Act (1996)** and **Schools Act (1996)** are key legal frameworks.

**Preschool (Grade R)** is optional, with private and public options. The **Foundation Phase** (Grades R–3) is followed by the **Intermediate Phase** (Grades 4–6) and **Senior Phase** (Grades 7–9).

**Primary education** is mandatory and free, with a focus on foundational skills. The **Primary School Achievement Test (UPSR)** was replaced by the **UASA (Ujian Akhir Sesi Akademik)** in 2022.

**Secondary education** includes **General, Technological, and Artistic** tracks. Students take national exams in **Mathematics, Science, and Languages**. The **Bacalaureate (Bac)** is required for university admission.

**Private schools** are common, with a mix of public and private institutions. The **Dual Language Program (DLP)** aims to improve English proficiency.

**Rural education** faces significant challenges, with many children in remote areas unable to attend school. The **School Violence** issue remains a concern, with reports of bullying and physical aggression.

**Higher education** is structured under the **Bologna Process**, with three cycles (Bachelor, Master, PhD). The **National Qualifications Framework** ensures national consistency.

**Language policy** has evolved, with English becoming a key subject. The **Bilingual Program** remains limited, and debates continue over language equity.
