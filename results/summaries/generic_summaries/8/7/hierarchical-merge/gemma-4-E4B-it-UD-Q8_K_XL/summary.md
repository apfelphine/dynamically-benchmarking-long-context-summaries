# Global Education Systems: A Comparative Overview

## System Models and Governance
Global education models exhibit wide diversity, ranging from highly centralized to decentralized structures. Finland exemplifies a modern, equitable model featuring universal subsidized early education and a focus on small classes. Conversely, the US system is decentralized at the K-12 level, while Malaysia operates a unified national system alongside various specialized schools (Islamic, Chinese). Other examples include Australia's decentralized K-12 system, Romania's 15-year compulsory education structure, and Portugal's free basic/secondary education offering specialized pathways.

## National Challenges and Developmental Focus
Many nations confront persistent challenges related to equity, skills gaps, and resource distribution. The US battles socioeconomic disparities and low STEM performance, while the Philippines manages regional inequalities and aims to improve industry alignment through initiatives like MTB-MLE. Systemic concerns across the globe include graduate unemployment (e.g., South Africa), resource inequality between urban and rural areas (e.g., Romania), and issues of rote learning versus meritocracy (e.g., Malaysia).

## Curriculum and Higher Education
Educational pathways vary significantly, incorporating specific national policies. Malaysia navigates complex language policies balancing Malay centralization with multilingual support, while higher education access is influenced by government quotas. Australia utilizes NAPLAN and PISA for assessment within its diverse pathways, and Romania aligns with the Bologna scheme. Countries like Portugal and the Philippines are actively addressing higher education attainment and skill mismatches, respectively.
