# Gottlob Frege
Gottlob Frege (1848–1925) was a German logician and philosopher foundational to analytic philosophy and modern logic. Educated in mathematics and physics, he invented axiomatic predicate logic and developed a formal system (Begriffsschrift) demonstrating that mathematics derives from logic—a view called logicism. Frege introduced vital distinctions in philosophy of language, notably between sense and reference, influencing figures like Russell and Wittgenstein. His major works include *Begriffsschrift* (1879), *The Foundations of Arithmetic* (1884), and *Basic Laws of Arithmetic* (1893–1903). Despite pioneering work, his system faced consistency challenges (Russell’s paradox). Later revealed diary entries showed Frege harbored extreme right-wing and antisemitic views late in life. He was known as introverted but occasionally witty.

# Grace Hopper
Grace Hopper (1906–1992) was a pioneering American computer scientist and Navy rear admiral who developed early programming languages and compilers, notably influencing COBOL's creation. Educated in mathematics and physics (Vassar, Yale), she joined the Navy WAVES during WWII and worked on the Harvard Mark I computer. She championed programming languages that use English-like syntax, enhancing accessibility programming. Hopper led efforts in programming standards for FORTRAN and COBOL, directed Navy programming language groups, and after retirement worked as a consultant for Digital Equipment Corporation. She popularized the term "debugging" and was known for educational demonstrations using “nanoseconds.” Her legacy includes numerous honors, a Navy destroyer named after her, and the annual Grace Hopper Celebration of Women in Computing.

# Al-Khwarizmi
Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (c. 780–850) was a Persian mathematician, astronomer, and geographer whose works laid the foundation for algebra and popularized the Hindu-Arabic numeral system. His seminal book *Al-Jabr* introduced systematic solutions to linear and quadratic equations, coining the term algebra. He produced astronomical tables (*Zīj as-Sindhind*), trigonometric tables, and revised Ptolemy’s *Geography* with improved coordinates. Al-Khwārizmī also developed early mechanical devices and a treatise on the Jewish calendar. His works spread to Europe via Latin translations, profoundly influencing mathematics and science.

# Federico Faggin
Federico Faggin (b. 1941) is an Italian-American physicist and engineer known for pioneering silicon-gate MOS technology and designing the world's first commercial microprocessor, Intel 4004. After early work with Olivetti and SGS-Fairchild, he developed silicon-gate technology at Fairchild Semiconductor. At Intel, he led the microprocessor project and created the design methodology foundational for microprocessors and memories. He subsequently co-founded Zilog, designing the Z80 microprocessor family. Later, Faggin founded Synaptics, innovating in neural networks and touchpads, and Foveon, enhancing image sensors. He also proposes a quantum theory of consciousness in recent works.

# Per Brinch Hansen
Per Brinch Hansen (1938–2007) was a Danish-American computer scientist instrumental in operating systems, concurrent and distributed computing. He developed the RC 4000 multiprogramming system, introducing the operating system kernel concept and separation of mechanism and policy. He formalized the notion of monitors for concurrency, created Concurrent Pascal—the first concurrent programming language—and proposed remote procedure calls. Hansen authored foundational textbooks and influenced systems design, focusing on clarity and practicality. His legacy encompasses seminal contributions shaping modern OS and concurrency paradigms.

# Jacek Karpiński
Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish pioneer in computer engineering, notable for building one of the world’s first transistor-based computers: AAH (1957) and the AKAT-1 differential equation analyzer (1959). After his UNESCO scholarship in the US, he developed Poland’s first perceptron and designed the KAR-65 and K-202 minicomputers, employing advanced features like asynchronous operation and memory segmentation. Despite technical success, political and bureaucratic challenges limited his projects’ impact. Karpiński also engaged in resistance activities during WWII and is remembered for his innovations amid complex political contexts.

# Herbert A. Simon
Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001) was a polymath and pioneering cognitive scientist and economist, awarded the Nobel Prize for work on decision-making in organizations. Educated at University of Chicago, he advanced bounded rationality theory, rejecting perfect rationality by emphasizing satisficing in decision processes. Simon co-developed early AI programs with Newell, such as Logic Theory Machine and General Problem Solver. His work spanned psychology, economics, organizational theory, and artificial intelligence. Simon’s research shaped behavioral economics and cognitive psychology, focusing on human problem-solving and administrative behavior.

# Kenneth E. Iverson
Kenneth E. Iverson (1920–2004) was a Canadian mathematician and computer scientist best known for developing APL, a programming language notable for its concise notation based on arrays and matrix operations. Educated at Queen’s University and Harvard, he worked at Harvard and IBM, where he formalized the IBM System/360 architecture. Iverson’s notation evolved into APL and later languages, influencing functional programming and array processing. He received the Turing Award in 1979 and authored foundational texts on APL. Iverson later worked with I. P. Sharp Associates and Jsoftware, continuing language development including the J language.

# Edsger W. Dijkstra
Edsger W. Dijkstra (1930–2002) was a Dutch computer scientist renowned for his foundational contributions to algorithm theory, programming methodology, and operating systems. He pioneered structured programming, famously critiquing the GOTO statement, and invented Dijkstra’s algorithm for shortest paths. Dijkstra developed the THE multiprogramming system, introduced semaphores and monitors for process synchronization, and advanced programming as a rigorous discipline. Known for his clarity, rigour, and iconoclastic personality, he authored over 1,300 papers (EWDs). He worked at the Mathematisch Centrum, Eindhoven University, Burroughs, and the University of Texas at Austin.

# George Boole
George Boole (1815–1864) was an English mathematician and logician whose work established Boolean algebra, foundational for digital logic and computer science. Largely self-taught, Boole developed an algebraic approach to logic that extended Aristotelian logic, formalizing logical operations such as AND, OR, and NOT. His works influenced later logicians and underpinned Claude Shannon’s digital circuit design theory. Boole was a professor at Queen’s College Cork and authored seminal texts including *The Mathematical Analysis of Logic* and *Laws of Thought*. His legacy permeates computer science, mathematics, and engineering.

# Claude Shannon
Claude Shannon (1916–2001) was an American mathematician and electrical engineer, regarded as the father of information theory. His 1937 master’s thesis demonstrated how Boolean algebra could optimize relay and switching circuits, foundational to digital circuits. In 1948, he published *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*, inventing information entropy as a measure of information content and laying the foundation for digital communication and data compression. Shannon also made early contributions to cryptography and artificial intelligence, inventing mechanical mouse Theseus, and worked on early AI concepts. His work is central to the Digital Age.

# Ramon Llull
Ramon Llull (1232/33–1315/16) was a Majorcan philosopher, logician, and missionary known for his *Art*, a combinatorial system of universal logic using letters and diagrams aimed at proving theological truths and converting non-Christians. Llull’s *Art* evolved through quaternary and ternary phases and introduced concepts like the threefold correlatives (agent, patient, act). He advocated linguistic education for missionary work and traveled extensively for religious debates. Though controversial in his time, his combinatorial methods are now viewed as precursors to computation and logic. He was beatified in 1847.

# Donald Knuth
Donald Knuth (b. 1938) is an eminent American computer scientist, mathematician, and author, known for *The Art of Computer Programming*, a comprehensive multi-volume work on algorithms and programming theory. He pioneered analysis of algorithms and created TeX and Metafont systems for digital typesetting. Knuth introduced literate programming, emphasizing programs as readable literature. Educated at Case Western and Caltech, he developed early compilers and contributed extensively to programming languages and mathematics. He has received numerous awards, including the Turing Award and National Medal of Science, and is known for his lifelong dedication to documenting computing foundations.

# George Antheil
George Antheil (1900–1959) was an American avant-garde composer known for his pioneering mechanical and percussive music, notably *Ballet Mécanique*. Raised in New Jersey among German immigrants, he studied piano and modernist music influences. His radical works involved player pianos, airplane propellers, and mechanized sounds, sometimes provoking riots. He collaborated with Hedy Lamarr to invent frequency-hopping spread-spectrum technology for torpedo guidance—an early basis for secure wireless communication. Antheil later composed Hollywood film scores and wrote on diverse topics, including relationship advice and endocrinology.

# Lynn Conway
Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was an American computer scientist, electrical engineer, and transgender activist. She contributed pioneering work in computer architecture and was central to the development of VLSI design methodology alongside Carver Mead, revolutionizing microchip design and fabrication (Mead–Conway revolution). Conway invented multiprocessor scheduling and founded MOSIS for rapid chip prototyping. After transitioning in 1968, she restarted her career stealthily, overcame workplace discrimination, and later held influential academic and DARPA positions. Conway has advocated for transgender rights and diversity in technology.

# Geoffrey Hinton
Geoffrey Hinton (b. 1947) is a British-Canadian cognitive psychologist and computer scientist, a leading figure in artificial neural networks and deep learning. Educated at Cambridge and Edinburgh, he co-invented backpropagation for training multilayer networks and Boltzmann machines. As a researcher at University of Toronto, Google, and CIFAR, Hinton pioneered work enabling modern AI breakthroughs. He has developed capsule networks, t-SNE visualization, and proposed recent learning algorithms. Awarded the Turing Award (2018) and Nobel Prize in Physics (2024), Hinton also voices concerns on AI risks, believing AGI could emerge soon with profound societal impacts.

# Kurt Gödel
Kurt Gödel (1906–1978) was an Austrian-American logician and mathematician renowned for his incompleteness theorems, which proved fundamental limits to formal mathematical systems. Educated in Vienna, he contributed the completeness theorem for first-order logic and later showed that any sufficiently powerful axiomatic system cannot be both complete and consistent. Gödel also worked on set theory, relativity, and formulated an ontological proof of God’s existence. He was a close friend of Einstein at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. Gödel’s work revolutionized logic and mathematics, profoundly influencing computer science, philosophy, and epistemology.

# Ismail al-Jazari
Ismail al-Jazari (1136–c.1206) was a Muslim polymath, engineer, and inventor famed for his *Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices*. Serving as chief engineer at the Artuklu Palace, he devised numerous mechanical innovations including early crankshaft and camshaft mechanisms, water-raising machines (chain pumps, suction pumps), and sophisticated water clocks. He created humanoid automata with practical and entertainment purposes, such as the hand-washing automaton and musical robot bands. Al-Jazari’s work combined artistry and engineering, profoundly influencing mechanical engineering and automata, and is considered an important precursor to robotics and control systems.
