## Ramon Llull

Ramon Llull (1232–1316) was a Catalan philosopher, logician, and missionary from Majorca who is renowned for his development of "Llull's Art," a system of universal logic aimed at proving Christian theological tenets and facilitating interfaith dialogue. Initially leading a worldly life, Llull experienced a series of religious visions that led him to devote himself to spiritual study and missionary work, including efforts to convert Muslims and Jews. He advocated the study of Arabic and other languages and established educational institutions, contributing significantly to the cross-cultural intellectual exchange of his era. Llull wrote extensively: his corpus includes treatises on logic, dialogues, encyclopedic tree diagrams, proverbs, and novels.

Llull's Art was groundbreaking for its employment of combinatorial, algorithmic-like methods and symbolic diagrams. Despite initial academic resistance and later condemnation, his work influenced late medieval and Renaissance thinkers and contributed to the development of logic and computation theory. Llull is venerated as a blessed figure in Catholicism.

## George Antheil

George Antheil (1900–1959) was an American composer, pianist, and inventor celebrated for avant-garde music, notably the *Ballet Mécanique*, which featured mechanical instruments and experimental sounds. Antheil's European years brought him into Paris's modernist circles, collaborating with artists including Pound and Cocteau. His works often provoked riots for their radical style.

After returning to America, Antheil became a prolific film composer in Hollywood and shifted toward more tonal, accessible music. He pursued scientific and literary interests, including writing on endocrinology. Together with actress Hedy Lamarr, Antheil co-invented a pioneering frequency-hopping system during World War II to secure radio-controlled torpedoes, foundational to modern wireless communications. He is remembered for both his artistic and technological contributions.

## George Boole

George Boole (1815–1864) was an English mathematician and logician who became the foundational figure of Boolean algebra, instrumental for later digital computation. Self-taught in mathematics and languages, Boole pioneered symbolic logic and the principle of binary variables. His works established the algebraic basis for logic, set theory, and probability theory, influencing the fields of mathematics, engineering, and computer science.

Boole’s binary logic system, detailed in *The Mathematical Analysis of Logic* and *An Investigation of the Laws of Thought*, transformed understanding of mathematical and logical systems. His legacy endures in computer science, particularly as a basis for digital circuit design and information theory, following key advances by Claude Shannon. In 2015, his bicentenary was celebrated worldwide.

## Geoffrey Hinton

Geoffrey Hinton (b. 1947) is a British-Canadian computer scientist and a leading figure in artificial intelligence, especially deep learning and neural networks. Educated in psychology and AI, he developed impactful algorithms including backpropagation and Boltzmann machines, co-founded DNNresearch (acquired by Google), and led the influential CIFAR program with collaborators Yoshua Bengio and Yann LeCun.

Hinton’s recent work addresses hierarchical vision models (GLOM), learning algorithms (Forward-Forward), and the philosophical limitations and risks of AI. He is widely recognized for his mentorship and scientific output, receiving numerous accolades such as the Turing Award and the Nobel Prize in Physics (2024). Hinton has publicly warned of existential risks from AI and advocates for governance and universal basic income in anticipation of inevitable economic disruption.

## Kenneth E. Iverson

Ken Iverson (1920–2004) was a Canadian computer scientist responsible for inventing APL (A Programming Language) and later J, languages noted for concise mathematical array expressions, influential in technical computing. Iverson's tenure at Harvard and IBM fostered novel notational systems and the implementation of interactive APL environments. Later, with I. P. Sharp Associates and Jsoftware, he advanced language theory and tools for mathematical and educational contexts.

Iverson emphasized notation as both a practical and pedagogical tool, receiving the Turing Award in 1979 for his impactful work in programming language design and mathematical thinking.

## Ismail al-Jazari

Ismail al-Jazari (1136–1206) was a prolific medieval engineer in Upper Mesopotamia who merged craft and invention, producing influential mechanical devices including advanced water clocks, automata, water-raising machines, and tools with foundational mechanisms such as the crankshaft and camshaft. His *Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices* described numerous designs and innovative methods, laying groundwork for later engineering, robotics, and automata.

Al-Jazari's inventions displayed practical application and creative artistry, with philosophical and technical significance for hydraulic engineering, timekeeping, and robotics.

## Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010) was a Polish computer engineer known for several groundbreaking computers, notably the transistor-based AKAT-1 and the K-202 minicomputer, which outperformed Western contemporaries but suffered limited production due to political obstacles in communist Poland. He contributed to early machine learning by building one of the first perceptrons in the Eastern bloc. Despite controversial links to state intelligence, Karpiński is remembered as a visionary whose innovations faced administrative and political challenges.

## Kurt Gödel

Kurt Gödel (1906–1978) was an Austrian–American logician and mathematician, famous for his incompleteness theorems, which demonstrated inherent limitations in formal mathematical systems. Gödel’s work revolutionized logic and influenced philosophy, mathematics, and computer science. After fleeing Nazi Europe, he became a colleague and close friend of Einstein at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study. Gödel's philosophical and metaphysical interests included proofs of God’s existence and time travel solutions in general relativity.

His theories fundamentally reshaped the understanding of mathematical truths, formal systems, and the nature of computation.

## Federico Faggin

Federico Faggin (b. 1941) is an Italian–American physicist, engineer, and entrepreneur, known for inventing the silicon-gate technology (SGT) and leading the development of the Intel 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor. His career spans pivotal roles at Olivetti, Fairchild, Intel, and Zilog, with seminal contributions to microprocessor architecture and personal computing.

Later, Faggin founded companies like Synaptics—originating touchpad technology—and Foveon, and established a foundation to study consciousness, proposing quantum-theoretical views on its nature. He has received major honors, such as the National Medal of Technology and the Kyoto Prize.

## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was an American computer scientist and electrical engineer, celebrated for her work on computer architecture and innovation in VLSI (very-large-scale integration) chip design, including co-authoring the influential Mead–Conway text and founding the MOSIS prototyping system. After being dismissed from IBM for pioneering gender transition, Conway continued her career at Xerox PARC and later at the University of Michigan.

Her technical achievements revolutionized chip design, contributed to the foundry model and electronic design automation, and empowered numerous startups and academic programs. Conway was also a prominent transgender rights activist and educator, recognized with honorary degrees and numerous awards, including IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal.

## Grace Hopper

Grace Hopper (1906–1992) was an American computer scientist, naval officer, and pioneer in programming languages. She led the development of the first compiler (A-0) and significantly influenced the creation of COBOL, advocating for programming in human-readable English. Hopper's work at Harvard and UNIVAC advanced automatic programming and contributed to standardization of computer languages.

She served in the US Navy, retiring as Rear Admiral, and continued her influence as a lecturer and consultant. Hopper popularized the term "debugging" and was known for making computing accessible. Her legacy is honored by many awards, doctoral degrees, institutions, and the Grace Hopper Celebration of Women in Computing.

## Donald Knuth

Donald Knuth (b. 1938) is an eminent American computer scientist and mathematician, best known for authoring *The Art of Computer Programming*, a definitive multi-volume treatise on algorithms and programming. He pioneered digital typesetting with TeX and Metafont, inspired the literate programming paradigm, and contributed to algorithmic analysis and mathematical writing.

Knuth has received the ACM Turing Award, National Medal of Science, and many other honors. His educational efforts and humor have influenced computer science culture and pedagogy. Knuth is an articulate advocate for rigor, elegance, and historical appreciation in programming and mathematics.

## Gottlob Frege

Gottlob Frege (1848–1925) was a German mathematician, logician, and philosopher widely regarded as a founder of analytic philosophy and modern logic. Frege created the formal systems underlying predicate logic, introduced the sense and reference distinction in semantics, and sought to derive arithmetic from logical axioms (logicism). His work laid crucial foundations for set theory, mathematical logic, and philosophy of language.

While his theories revolutionized logic, his later years were marked by personal isolation and controversy due to antisemitic views. Frege’s intellectual legacy profoundly shaped twentieth-century analytic philosophy and mathematical logic.

## Claude Shannon

Claude Shannon (1916–2001) was an American mathematician, engineer, and the "father of information theory." His 1948 paper introduced the concept of information entropy, establishing the mathematical framework for digital communication, data compression, and error correction. Shannon's master's thesis connected Boolean algebra to digital circuit design, providing the basis for modern computers.

Shannon contributed to wartime cryptography, artificial intelligence (notably the "Theseus" learning machine), and popularized key advances in communications. He was renowned for his inventive spirit, scientific breadth, and foundational influence on the Information Age. Shannon received the National Medal of Science and many other awards; his centenary in 2016 was marked by international celebration.

## Al-Khwarizmi

Al-Khwarizmi (c. 780–850) was a Persian polymath whose works on mathematics, astronomy, and geography had lasting impact. His treatise *Al-Kitab al-Mukhtasar fi Hisab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala* established algebra as a separate discipline, and his algorithmic approaches to arithmetic introduced the term "algorithm" to the Western world. He systematized foundational concepts of algebra, arithmetic with Hindu numerals, trigonometric tables, and mapped the known world.

Al-Khwarizmi's writings were instrumental in transmitting mathematical knowledge from the Islamic world to medieval Europe, earning him recognition as the "father of algebra."

## Edsger W. Dijkstra

Edsger W. Dijkstra (1930–2002) was a Dutch computer scientist and programmer, renowned for his pioneering work in algorithms, programming languages, and software engineering. He formulated Dijkstra’s algorithm (for shortest paths), introduced semaphores for process synchronization, and advocated structured programming via his famous critique "Go To statement considered harmful."

Dijkstra’s writings, particularly his EWD manuscript series, emphasized simplicity and mathematical rigor in programming. He played a vital role in formalizing operating systems, contributed to programming language theory, and was recognized with the ACM Turing Award and other honors.

## Per Brinch Hansen

Per Brinch Hansen (1938–2007) was a Danish-American computer scientist instrumental in the development of operating systems, concurrent programming, and distributed computing. He introduced the operating system kernel concept, designed the RC 4000 multiprogramming system, and with C.A.R. Hoare, developed the monitor construct, foundational for synchronization in concurrent programming.

Brinch Hansen’s work created early concurrent languages (Concurrent Pascal, Edison, Joyce), advanced paradigms for parallel and distributed programming, and contributed comprehensive textbooks. He received the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award and was esteemed for his clear, practical approach to programming systems.

## Herbert A. Simon

Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001) was an American polymath, Nobel laureate economist, cognitive psychologist, and pioneer in artificial intelligence. Simon’s theory of "bounded rationality" revolutionized economic models of decision-making, emphasizing cognitive limitations and satisficing. His research contributed to administrative theory, organization science, and the simulation of human thought in AI, notably co-developing the Logic Theory Machine and General Problem Solver.

Simon’s interdisciplinary work spanned psychology, economics, and computer science, resulting in comprehensive theories of learning, expertise, problem-solving, and organizational behavior. He was honored with the Turing Award, Nobel Prize, National Medal of Science, and numerous academic distinctions.
