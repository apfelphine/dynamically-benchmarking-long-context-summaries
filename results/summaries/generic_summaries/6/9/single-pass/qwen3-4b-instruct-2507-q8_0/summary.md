**Concise Summary:**

- **Jacek Karpiński**: Polish engineer and computer scientist; survived WWII, contributed to early computing (AAH, AKAT-1, KAR-65, K-202); faced controversy for intelligence work; awarded multiple honors including Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta.
- **Kenneth Iverson**: Developed APL programming language; created APL and its formal description; influenced computing education and language design; awarded Turing Award.
- **Federico Faggin**: Pioneered silicon-gate technology; designed first microprocessor (Intel 4004); co-inventor of the 4004; founded Zilog and Synaptics; advocated for consciousness theory.
- **George Boole**: Founded Boolean algebra; developed symbolic logic; influenced digital circuit design; foundational to modern computing and AI.
- **Grace Hopper**: Pioneered COBOL; promoted programming in English; advocated for software standards; received numerous awards including Presidential Medal of Freedom.
- **Ramon Llull**: Created combinatorial logic system; influenced early computing and AI; developed dialogues and tree-based reasoning models.
- **Gottlob Frege**: Founded modern logic; developed formal logic and predicate calculus; introduced sense/reference distinction; influenced analytic philosophy.
- **George Antheil**: Composed "Ballet Mécanique"; invented frequency-hopping radio; contributed to early electronic music and communication technology.
- **Al-Khwarizmi**: Introduced Hindu-Arabic numerals; developed algebra and trigonometry; influenced modern mathematics and computing.
- **Geoffrey Hinton**: Key figure in neural networks; developed backpropagation and deep learning; co-winner of Turing Award; expressed concerns about AI risks.
- **Per Brinch Hansen**: Developed monitors and concurrent programming; authored influential books on operating systems and concurrency.
- **Ismail al-Jazari**: Invented mechanical automata and water-raising devices; pioneered early robotics and fluid mechanics.
- **Herbert A. Simon**: Pioneered decision theory and AI; developed bounded rationality and the "satisficing" model; won Nobel Prize in Economics.
- **Kurt Gödel**: Proved incompleteness theorems; developed ontological proof of God; contributed to logic, mathematics, and philosophy.
- **Edsger W. Dijkstra**: Advocated structured programming; invented Dijkstra’s algorithm and semaphores; known for elegant, concise writing and teaching.
- **Lynn Conway**: Co-invented VLSI design and MOSIS; revolutionized chip design; championed transgender rights and inclusion in STEM.

Each individual made foundational contributions to computing, mathematics, or engineering, with lasting impacts on modern technology and scientific thought.
