## Kurt Gödel

Kurt Gödel (1906–1978), Austrian logician and mathematician, revolutionized logic and philosophy through his completeness and incompleteness theorems, with deep influence on set theory, physics, and theology. Despite facing persecution and mental health challenges, Gödel became a leading figure at Princeton’s Institute for Advanced Study, befriended Einstein, and was widely honored.

## Lynn Conway

Lynn Conway (1938–2024) transformed VLSI chip design and superscalar architecture, overcoming career setbacks linked to her gender transition. Her innovations at Xerox PARC and work on patents and documentation standards shaped engineering and computer science. Conway actively promoted STEM diversity and transgender rights.

## Ismail al-Jazari

Ismail al-Jazari (12th–13th century), chief engineer at Artuklu Palace, authored the "Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices" and created automata, water clocks, camshafts, crankshafts, escapements, valves, segmental gears, and humanoid robots—profoundly influencing robotics and engineering in both Islamic and European traditions.

## Ramon Llull

Ramon Llull (1232/3–1315/6), Majorcan philosopher, developed a universal logical system blending combinatorial, algebraic, and metaphysical principles. He advocated interfaith dialogue, advanced scientific visualization, contributed to theology and literature, and anticipated computer science and voting theory.

## Donald Knuth

Donald Knuth authored *The Art of Computer Programming* and *Concrete Mathematics*, invented TeX and METAFONT, and pioneered literate programming, deeply shaping algorithms, digital typography, and programming education. He earned the Turing Award.

## Geoffrey Hinton

Geoffrey Hinton, British-Canadian psychologist and computer scientist, pioneered neural network techniques including backpropagation, Boltzmann machines, capsule networks, and t-SNE—driving AI advancement. He co-founded DNNresearch (acquired by Google), advocates AI regulation, and won major honors including the Turing Award.

## Claude Shannon

Claude Shannon (1916–2001), father of information theory, transformed computing through innovations in Boolean logic, communication, cryptography, coding theory, and signal processing. His work at Bell Labs included wearable computers and chess automata, laying the foundations for modern technology.

## George Antheil

George Antheil, composer and author, fused mechanical, romantic, and jazz styles. Co-inventor of frequency-hopping radio with Hedy Lamarr, he influenced wireless technology and both music and engineering.

## Edsger W. Dijkstra

Edsger W. Dijkstra (1930–2002), influential computer scientist, produced "EWD" manuscripts and advocated structured programming. He developed foundational algorithms and synchronization methods (semaphores), advanced parsing and self-stabilization, and elevated programming’s scientific rigor.

## Herbert A. Simon

Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001) originated "bounded rationality" and "satisficing," reshaping cognitive psychology, AI, organizational theory, and economics. His work was recognized with the Nobel Prize, Turing Award, and National Medal of Science.

## George Boole

George Boole (1815–1864), self-taught mathematician and logician, founded symbolic logic and Boolean algebra—key to computer science and information theory. His *Laws of Thought* enriched reasoning, probability, and technological progress.

## Per Brinch Hansen

Per Brinch Hansen (1937–2007), Danish computer scientist, furthered concurrent, distributed, and parallel programming, inventing Concurrent Pascal, SuperPascal, and essential operating system concepts like monitors and remote procedure calls.

## Kenneth E. Iverson

Kenneth E. Iverson (1920–2004) revolutionized programming languages with APL and J, authored major texts, and inspired recognition including species nomenclature.

## Al-Khwarizmi

Al-Khwarizmi (c. 820 CE), Persian mathematician at Baghdad’s House of Wisdom, systematized algebra, advanced numerals, created astronomical tables, refined geography, and developed calendars and measuring methods. The term "algorithm" derives from his name, honored in lunar and asteroid nomenclature.

## Jacek Karpiński

Jacek Karpiński (1927–2010), Polish engineer and WWII survivor, developed landmark computers (KAR-65, K-202), harmonic and equation analyzers, and an early perceptron amid political adversity. His inventions are preserved in Warsaw, with controversy over secret informant activity.

## Gottlob Frege

Gottlob Frege (1848–1925) transformed logic and analytic philosophy with foundational texts and predicate logic, establishing logicism and the sense/reference distinction, though his legacy was later marred by Russell's paradox and antisemitic views.

## Federico Faggin

Federico Faggin, Italian physicist, pioneered silicon gate technology (SGT), enabling key advances in DRAM, non-volatile memory, CCD sensors, and microprocessors. He led Intel’s microprocessor development, co-founded Zilog, and innovated at Synaptics and Foveon. He later researched consciousness, proposing a quantum theory in 2024, and received numerous awards.

## Grace Hopper

Grace Hopper (1906–1992), pioneering computer scientist and Navy rear admiral, contributed to the Harvard Mark I, created early compilers, and influenced COBOL and programming standards. Hopper advocated for networked and distributed computing, continued consulting after her Navy career, and inspired generations as a goodwill ambassador with energetic lectures. She popularized "debugging," received the Presidential Medal of Freedom, National Medal of Technology, over 40 honorary degrees, and numerous other honors. Her legacy endures in many institutions and awards named in her honor, including buildings, scholarships at Microsoft, a competition field in the FIRST Robotics Competition, a professorship at Yale, and Google’s Grace Hopper undersea network cable. The annual Grace Hopper Celebration of Women in Computing conference and notable appearances in media and software also commemorate her impact. She was recognized in major computing obituaries in 1992 and is celebrated as a symbol for computer science and women in STEM. Hopper died in 1992 and was buried with full military honors, with a family heritage of Scottish and Dutch roots.
