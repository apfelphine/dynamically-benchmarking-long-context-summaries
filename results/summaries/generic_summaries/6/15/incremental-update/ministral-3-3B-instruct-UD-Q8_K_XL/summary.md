### **Grace Hopper: Computer Science Pioneer & Naval Innovator**
Grace Hopper (1906–1992) was a trailblazing mathematician and computer scientist who reshaped **programming, naval computing, and industry standards**. After earning her **Ph.D. in mathematics from Yale (1934)**, she defied exclusionary norms by serving in the **U.S. Navy Reserve (1943–1949)**, programming the **Mark I computer**—a historic achievement despite the **38-year age limit**. Her groundbreaking contributions included co-developing **COBOL (1959)**, the cornerstone of **business computing**, and leading the **CODASYL committee**, which introduced **English-based programming**, a radical departure from machine code.

Hopper’s military and academic career spanned decades. As the Navy’s first **director of automatic programming (1954)**, she later advised **Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC)** and mentored engineers. Retired as a **commander (1966)**, she returned to active duty (1967–1986), earning the **Defense Distinguished Service Medal (1986)** and becoming the oldest active-duty officer, rising to **rear admiral (1985)**. Her innovations extended to **distributed computing** and **industry standards**, earning her **40+ honorary degrees**, including the **National Medal of Technology** and **Presidential Medal of Freedom (2016)**. Institutions like **Yale’s Grace Hopper College**, **NIST**, and **USS Hopper (DDG-70)** honor her legacy, while her influence persists in **Nvidia’s 2024 CPU/GPU generations**, **Google’s undersea cables (2021)**, and **Microsoft’s scholarships**. Beyond technology, Hopper broke barriers in **military and academia**, earning the nickname *"Grandma COBOL"* for her wit. Her **debugging term** (from the 1947 "moth incident") became industry standard, and the **Grace Hopper Celebration** (founded 2003) continues to empower women in computing.

### **Gottlob Frege: Logical Foundations & Philosophy of Language**
While Grace Hopper revolutionized computing, **Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)** redefined **logical formalism and philosophy of language**. Born in **Wismar, Germany**, Frege’s early work was shaped by his father’s **linguistic textbook**, fostering an interest in **formal systems**. After studying at **Jena and Göttingen**, he earned his **1873 Ph.D.** under **Ernst Schering**, advancing in philosophy and logic.

Frege’s foundational contributions emerged in *Begriffsschrift* (1879), where he introduced **quantified variables and axiomatic predicate logic**, dismantling intuition-based axioms. His **logicism** theory posited that **arithmetic could be derived from logic alone**, directly influencing **Russell’s *Principia Mathematica*** and **Gödel’s incompleteness theorems**. His **Basic Law V** and later refinements preserved consistency in formal systems. Beyond mathematics, his **sense/reference distinction** (*1892*) and **compositionality theory** reshaped philosophy of language, critiquing psychologistic interpretations and influencing **analytic philosophy**, including **Carnap, Russell, and Wittgenstein**.

Frege’s legacy spans **logic, mathematics, and philosophy**, with works like *Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik* (1884), *Grundgesetze der Arithmetik* (1893–1903), and *On Sense and Reference* (1892) laying groundwork for **second-order logic** and modern formal systems. Though his **antisemitic and right-wing diaries** (revealed posthumously) cast a shadow on his earlier image, his **intellectual rigor** and **sarcastic wit** ensured his enduring impact on **mathematical logic and analytic philosophy**.
