### Jacek Karpiński: Pioneer in Early Computing and Controversial Legacy

**Jacek Karpiński** (1927–2010) was a Polish engineer and mathematician who, despite severe disabilities including blindness and paralysis from war injuries, became a pioneering figure in early computing and artificial intelligence within the communist bloc.

*   **Hardware Innovations:** His technical achievements included the **AAH** (1957) vacuum-tube computer and the **AKAT-1** (1959), the world's first transistor-based differential equations analyzer. Later, he designed the **KAR-65** (1968), a high-speed modular computer built with limited resources that remained operational for two decades.
*   **The K-202 Project:** In 1970, Karpiński established the **K-202** project to create an affordable minicomputer using advanced microchips. While 30 prototypes were produced that outperformed Western competitors, the project failed to achieve mass production due to political interference, censorship, and lack of industrial support. Critics argue the project was deliberately sabotaged and underfunded, though only a few units survive today, including one in the Warsaw Museum of Technology.
*   **Later Life and Controversies:** After returning to Poland in the 1990s, he advised key political figures but faced numerous business failures, including a stint in Switzerland working on Nagra tape recorders. His legacy is complicated by revelations that he served as a secret **Stasi informant** from 1961 to the 1970s, collecting extensive technological intelligence.
*   **Recognition and Legacy:** Despite his controversial past, Karpiński was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2010. His life and achievements remain subjects of historical debate and cultural portrayal, notably in Roman Bratny's novel *Lot ku ziemi*.
