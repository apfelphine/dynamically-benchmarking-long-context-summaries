### **Donald Knuth**
Donald Knuth, born in 1938, is a seminal computer scientist known for founding algorithm analysis and authoring *The Art of Computer Programming*. Educated at Case Institute of Technology and Caltech, he began his career at Burroughs Corporation, influencing the ALGOL language, before joining Stanford University in 1969. There, he developed the **TeX** typesetting system, **Metafont** tools, and introduced **literate programming**. Beyond his technical innovations, Knuth is a prolific writer on recreational mathematics and CS history, a staunch opponent of software patents, and a recipient of the Turing Award (1974). He is recognized for his humor, his adoption of a Chinese name, and his teaching of *Concrete Mathematics*.

### **Lynn Conway**
Lynn Conway (1938–2024) was a pioneering engineer whose work in Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) revolutionized the industry. A Fellow of the IEEE and member of the National Academy of Engineering, she co-founded MOSIS at Xerox PARC in the 1970s, democratizing chip fabrication and establishing the modern foundry model. Her seminal work, co-authored with Carver Mead, transformed digital design methodologies, and she held multiple honorary doctorates. Conway is equally celebrated for her activism; after facing professional discrimination and being denied access to her children upon her 1968 transition, she became a vocal advocate for trans and LGBTQ+ rights, helping shape the IEEE Code of Ethics and coining the "Conway effect." Recognized as one of the "Stonewall 40" trans heroes and named one of Time's 21 Transgender People Who Influenced American Culture, she passed away in June 2024 at age 86 after living in rural Michigan with her husband, Charles Rogers.
