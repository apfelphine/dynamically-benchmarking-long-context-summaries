**Final Summary**

**Per Brinch Hansen** was a Danish computer scientist whose pioneering work advanced operating systems, concurrent programming, and distributed computing. He co-developed the **monitor concept** and introduced the **operating system kernel** and **mechanism/policy separation**, becoming a foundational figure in modern OS design. Author of *Operating System Principles* (1973) and *The Architecture of Concurrent Programs*, he developed **Concurrent Pascal**, **Edison**, and **Joyce**, and proposed **remote procedure calls (RPC)** and **distributed processes**. Recognized with the **IEEE Computer Pioneer Award (2002)**, he reflected on computing history and authored a memoir, passing away in 2007.

**George Antheil** was an avant-garde American composer known for *Ballet Mécanique* and other modernist works, which challenged traditional music with industrial themes. He composed over 30 film scores, advocated for modern music, and criticized Hollywood’s formulaic soundtracks. Co-inventing **frequency-hopping radio** with Hedy Lamarr during WWII, he also wrote the opera *Transatlantic*, moved to Germany, and returned to the U.S. in 1933 amid Nazi persecution. He authored a memoir and contributed to music journalism, and later explored controversial interests in female endocrinology.

**Herbert A. Simon** was a Nobel laureate in Economics (1978) and a foundational figure in behavioral economics, AI, and cognitive psychology. His seminal work *Administrative Behavior* introduced **bounded rationality** and **satisficing**, challenging traditional economic models. He co-developed the **Logic Theory Machine** and **General Problem Solver**, and proposed **production rules** and models like **EPAM/CHREST** for learning and problem-solving. A prolific author with over 1,000 papers, he received the **ACM Turing Award (1975)** and reshaped multiple disciplines through computer-based modeling of human cognition.

**Geoffrey Hinton** was a leading AI researcher born in 1947, known for co-inventing **backpropagation** and **Boltzmann machines**. He mentored Yoshua Bengio and Yann LeCun, winning the **2018 ACM A.M. Turing Award**. A strong advocate for AI safety, he warns of existential risks from AGI, job displacement, and catastrophic misuse, urging government regulation. A socialist, he moved to Canada to oppose U.S. military AI funding and co-sponsored AI safety legislation. He has received the **Nobel Prize in Physics (2024)**, FRS, FRSC, and numerous honors. He has faced personal loss from the deaths of both his first and second wives due to cancer, and has lived with depression since a back injury at 19. He is the great-great-grandson of George Boole and the great-great-grandfather of Charles Howard Hinton, and his middle name, George, honors George Everest.

**Edsger W. Dijkstra** was a Dutch computer scientist who pioneered structured programming, algorithms (e.g., **Dijkstra’s algorithm**), and distributed computing (e.g., **self-stabilization**). He criticized unstructured programming and the teaching of BASIC, advocating for clarity and mathematical rigor. His influential works include *A Discipline of Programming* and *Structured Programming*, and his 1968 essay "Go To Statement Considered Harmful" became a landmark in software engineering. He authored over 500 technical reports in the **EWD series**, including philosophical parables like "Mathematics, Inc." He received the **Turing Award (1972)** and is honored through the Edsger W. Dijkstra Memorial Lecture at the University of Texas at Austin.

**Ismail al-Jazari** (c. 1136–1206) was a pioneering 12th–13th century Islamic engineer and inventor from Upper Mesopotamia. His *Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices* details groundbreaking inventions such as the **crankshaft and crank-slider system**, **double-acting reciprocating pump**, **conical valve**, and **segmental gear**. He designed automated automata—including hydropowered peacocks, automatic doors, a drink-serving waitress, and a water-clock of drummers—demonstrating programmable, timed mechanical functions. His **castle clock** displayed celestial movements and featured automated musicians. Al-Jazari combined engineering with artistic illustration, reflecting a deep integration of art and technology in medieval Islamic innovation.

**Claude Shannon** was a foundational figure in information theory and digital computing. His 1937 master’s thesis on switching circuits using Boolean algebra revolutionized digital design. In 1948, he published *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*, introducing **information entropy** and establishing information theory. His work on cryptography, including the **one-time pad** and **signal-flow graphs**, and contributions to AI (e.g., *Theseus*, a maze-learning machine), co-organized the 1956 Dartmouth AI workshop, and developed pulse-code modulation. He formulated **Shannon’s maxim** (*The enemy knows the system*) and contributed to detection theory and combinatorics. A successful investor, he outperformed 1,025 mutual funds. Honored globally, he received the **National Medal of Science (1966)**, **Kyoto Prize (1985)**, and the **Claude E. Shannon Award (1973)**.

**Donald Knuth** is renowned for developing **TeX** and **METAFONT**, systems for high-quality typesetting. His influential works include *The TeXbook*, *The METAFONTbook*, and *Concrete Mathematics*. A passionate composer, he created *Fantasia Apocalyptica*, a musical translation of the Book of Revelation. He adopted the Chinese name **Gao Dena** in 1977 to connect with Chinese programmers and received the **Turing Award**, **National Medal of Science**, and **Kyoto Prize**. Known for his humor and satirical works (e.g., "iTeX"), he is a Fellow of many scientific societies.

**Lynn Conway** was a pioneering computer architect and VLSI innovator who co-developed the first superscalar processor architecture with IBM in the 1960s. She co-authored *Introduction to VLSI Systems* and published influential papers on VLSI design. She held multiple patents in remote control and teleinteraction systems and was married to **Charles "Charlie" Rogers** in 2002, living in **Jackson, Michigan**. She received numerous awards, including **IEEE Fellow**, the **IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal**, and induction into the **National Inventors Hall of Fame**. Despite facing discrimination—including being fired from IBM in 1968 after disclosing her gender transition—she rebuilt her career at Xerox PARC, co-developing the **Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology** and founding **MOSIS**, a transformative platform enabling university and small-company access to chip design. A key advocate for transgender rights, she coined the **"Conway effect"** to describe societal neglect of marginalized innovators. She passed away on **June 9, 2024**, at age 86, and was posthumously honored with the **National LGBTQ Wall of Honor in 2025**.
