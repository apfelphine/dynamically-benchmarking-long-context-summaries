**Federico Faggin** – Pioneered silicon-gate technology (SGT), enabling modern microelectronics. Key contributions include the Fairchild 3708 (first commercial SGT IC), the Intel 4004 (world’s first single-chip microprocessor), and the Intel 8008 and 8080. Later founded Zilog, creating the Z80 microprocessor. Advocated for quantum-based consciousness theory, proposing that consciousness is a unique quantum phenomenon. Received numerous awards, including the National Medal of Technology and Innovation and the Kyoto Prize.

**Jacek Karpiński** – A Polish engineer and computer scientist who developed the KAR-65 computer and the K-202 minicomputer. His work was innovative but faced political and administrative challenges in Poland. He also worked on the perceptron and contributed to early AI. Controversially, he was a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa (Polish intelligence). Known for his technical achievements and later life in Switzerland.

**Grace Hopper** – A U.S. Navy rear admiral and computer scientist who developed the first compiler and the programming language COBOL. She was instrumental in making programming accessible to non-specialists. Promoted the idea of using English-like statements in programming. Received the National Medal of Technology and the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Known for popularizing the term "debugging."

**Kenneth E. Iverson** – Developed the APL programming language, introducing a unique notation for mathematical and logical operations. His work laid the foundation for modern programming languages. Key contributions include the invention of the "fork" operator and the development of the J programming language. Awarded the Turing Award and the Harry H. Goode Memorial Award.

**Gottlob Frege** – A German logician who founded modern logic and formal semantics. His work on predicate logic and the distinction between sense and reference laid the groundwork for analytic philosophy. His work influenced Russell, Wittgenstein, and modern logic. Criticized for his antisemitism in later life.

**George Boole** – A British mathematician who developed Boolean algebra, foundational to digital electronics. His work on logic and probability influenced the development of modern computing. His legacy is evident in digital circuits and computer science.

**Al-Khwarizmi** – A Persian mathematician who developed algebra and introduced the Hindu-Arabic numeral system. His work laid the foundation for modern algebra and algorithms. The term "algorithm" derives from his name.

**Ramon Llull** – A Spanish philosopher and logician who developed a combinatorial system known as "Llull's Art." It was used to prove theological and philosophical claims through structured reasoning. His work influenced later developments in logic and AI.

**Per Brinch Hansen** – A computer scientist who developed the concept of monitors and the Concurrent Pascal programming language. He introduced key ideas in operating systems and concurrent programming. Authored influential textbooks on operating systems and concurrent programming.

**George Antheil** – A composer and inventor known for "Ballet Mécanique," a work that used mechanical elements to represent industrial rhythms. He also co-invented frequency-hopping radio technology with Hedy Lamarr. Known for his avant-garde music and contributions to early communication technology.

**Herbert A. Simon** – A cognitive scientist who developed the theory of bounded rationality and contributed to the field of artificial intelligence. He created the concept of "satisficing" and developed models of human decision-making. Awarded the Nobel Prize in Economics and the Turing Award.

**Geoffrey Hinton** – A Canadian AI pioneer who developed neural networks and deep learning. He co-founded DNNresearch and worked on breakthroughs in machine learning. Known for his work on deep neural networks and his recent concerns about AI risks. A recipient of the Turing Award and the Nobel Prize in Physics (2024).

**Edsger W. Dijkstra** – A Dutch computer scientist who developed key concepts in programming and algorithms, including Dijkstra’s algorithm, the shortest path problem, and the concept of monitors. He advocated for structured programming and is known for his influential writings and critiques of software engineering.

**Ismail al-Jazari** – A 12th-century engineer who designed sophisticated mechanical devices, including water-raising machines, automata, and clocks. His work influenced later developments in robotics and mechanical engineering.

**Claude Shannon** – A U.S. mathematician and information theorist who laid the foundation for information theory and digital circuit design. His work on Boolean algebra and switching circuits enabled the development of modern digital computers. He is credited with inventing the concept of the "bit" and the field of information theory.

**Kurt Gödel** – A logician who proved the incompleteness theorems, demonstrating that any formal system powerful enough to describe arithmetic contains true statements that cannot be proven within the system. His work profoundly influenced mathematics, logic, and computer science.

**Donald Knuth** – A computer scientist and author known for *The Art of Computer Programming*, a monumental series on algorithms and programming. He developed TeX and Metafont for digital typesetting and introduced literate programming. A recipient of the Turing Award and numerous other honors.

**Lynn Conway** – A computer scientist and transgender rights activist who co-developed the VLSI design methodology and the MOSIS foundry system. She was a pioneer in semiconductor design and advocated for transgender rights, coining the "Conway effect" to describe the marginalization of women and minorities in tech history. Inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
