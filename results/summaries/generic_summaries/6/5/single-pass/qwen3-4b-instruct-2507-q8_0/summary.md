**Al-Khwarizmi** – A Persian mathematician, astronomer, and geographer from Khwarazm (modern-day Uzbekistan/Turkmenistan). He laid the foundations of algebra, introducing the term "algebra" from his book *Al-Jabr*, and developed systematic methods for solving linear and quadratic equations. He promoted the use of Hindu-Arabic numerals, which spread to Europe via translations, giving rise to the term "algorithm." His works in astronomy, geography, and arithmetic were influential, including *Zīj as-Sindhind* and *Kitāb surat al-ard* (Geography). He also wrote on the Hebrew calendar and other subjects.

**Gottlob Frege** – A German logician and philosopher who founded modern logic. His work, particularly *Begriffsschrift*, introduced a formal system for logic and laid the groundwork for predicate logic. He argued that arithmetic is a branch of logic, a view known as "logicism." His distinction between sense and reference profoundly influenced analytic philosophy. He faced controversy over antisemitic views in his later years, though he was a rationalist and defender of scientific inquiry.

**Per Brinch Hansen** – A Danish computer scientist known for pioneering work in operating systems and concurrent programming. He developed the RC 4000 multiprogramming system, introduced the concept of monitors, and created the Concurrent Pascal programming language. His textbook *Operating System Principles* became a foundational text in the field. He contributed significantly to the development of concurrent and parallel computing.

**Grace Hopper** – An American computer scientist and naval officer who advocated for high-level programming languages. She developed the first compiler for a programming language (A compiler) and was instrumental in creating COBOL, a business-oriented language. She promoted the idea of programming in natural language and is credited with popularizing the term "debugging." She served in the U.S. Navy and later became a key figure in the computer industry.

**Herbert A. Simon** – A cognitive scientist and economist known for his work in decision-making, artificial intelligence, and organizational behavior. His book *Administrative Behavior* introduced the concept of "bounded rationality," where humans make decisions based on limited information. He co-developed the Logic Theory Machine and the General Problem Solver, and made foundational contributions to AI and cognitive psychology. He won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics in 1978.

**Federico Faggin** – An Italian engineer and computer pioneer who developed the silicon-gate technology (SGT), enabling the creation of modern integrated circuits. He designed the first microprocessor, the Intel 4004, and the Z80 microprocessor. He also contributed to the development of the first microcontroller, the Z8, and worked on touchpad and optical character recognition technologies. He proposed a theory of consciousness as a quantum phenomenon.

**Edsger W. Dijkstra** – A Dutch computer scientist known for his work in algorithms and programming methodology. He introduced the "Go To statement considered harmful" and championed structured programming. He developed Dijkstra's algorithm for shortest path problems and the semaphore concept for process synchronization. He authored *A Discipline of Programming* and is considered a foundational figure in computer science.

**Jacek Karpiński** – A Polish engineer and computer pioneer who designed the AKAT-1 differential equations analyzer and the K-202 minicomputer. He worked on early computers in Poland and was a key figure in the development of computer science in communist Eastern Europe. His work on the K-202 was notable for its technical innovation, though it was hindered by political and industrial resistance.

**Claude Shannon** – An American mathematician and information theorist who is considered the father of information theory. His work on switching circuits and Boolean algebra laid the foundation for digital computing. He invented the concept of information entropy and developed the mathematical theory of communication. He also contributed to early work in artificial intelligence, including the design of a chess-playing machine and the concept of the "Borda count" in voting theory.

**Kenneth E. Iverson** – A Canadian computer scientist known for creating the APL programming language, which introduced a powerful, concise notation for mathematical operations. He developed the language to improve programming efficiency and clarity. He also created TeX and Metafont for typesetting, and promoted literate programming. His work has had a lasting influence on programming languages and computer science education.

**Donald Knuth** – An American computer scientist and author best known for *The Art of Computer Programming*, a multi-volume work that remains a cornerstone of computer science. He developed TeX and Metafont for typesetting and pioneered the concept of literate programming. He also contributed to the development of algorithms, computational complexity, and the theory of computation. He won the Turing Award and is known for his rigorous and elegant writing style.

**George Boole** – An English mathematician and logician who founded Boolean algebra, a system of logic used in digital circuits. His work on symbolic logic and the principles of logic laid the foundation for modern computer science. He introduced the concept of "elective symbols" and the algebra of logic, which influenced the development of digital electronics and computer science.

**Lynn Conway** – An American computer scientist and transgender rights activist who co-developed the Mead–Conway VLSI design methodology and the MOSIS foundry system. She was a pioneer in integrated circuit design and helped establish the foundry model for chip manufacturing. She also advocated for transgender rights and was a vocal critic of the pathologization of gender identity.

**Ramon Llull** – A Catalan philosopher and inventor from the 13th century who developed a combinatorial system of logic known as "Llull's Art." It used a system of principles and diagrams to generate logical arguments and was used to convert non-Christians to Christianity. His work influenced later developments in logic, alchemy, and cognitive science.

**Kurt Gödel** – An Austrian logician known for his incompleteness theorems, which showed that in any formal system powerful enough to describe arithmetic, there are true statements that cannot be proven within the system. His work profoundly influenced mathematics, logic, and philosophy. He also contributed to the foundations of set theory and made contributions to the philosophy of mind and religion.

**George Antheil** – An American composer and inventor known for his experimental music and mechanical devices. He composed *Ballet Mécanique*, a work that combined music with mechanical elements. He also co-invented frequency-hopping radio technology with Hedy Lamarr, which is a foundational concept in modern wireless communication.

**Ismail al-Jazari** – A 12th-century engineer from Mesopotamia who designed and described numerous mechanical devices, including water-raising machines, automata, and water clocks. His work in *The Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices* includes early examples of crankshafts, valves, and gear systems, and influenced later developments in engineering and robotics.

**Geoffrey Hinton** – A Canadian artificial intelligence researcher and co-inventor of deep learning. He developed the backpropagation algorithm and Boltzmann machines, and co-authored foundational papers in neural networks. He won the 2018 Turing Award and has been a leading voice in AI research. In recent years, he has expressed concerns about the risks of artificial general intelligence (AGI) and the need for ethical and safe development.
