### **Grace Hopper**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Grace Hopper was born in New York City in 1895 to Scottish and Dutch parents. She was a curious child, dismantling alarm clocks as a young girl, and later embraced a backward-running clock as a symbol of her resistance to tradition. She attended Vassar College, where she earned a bachelor’s degree in mathematics and physics in 1928, and later earned a master’s degree from Yale University in 1930. She married Vincent Foster Hopper in 1930, but their marriage ended in divorce in 1945.

#### **World War II and Early Career**
During World War II, Hopper faced rejection from the Navy due to her age and weight, despite her qualifications as a mathematician. She joined the WAVES (Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service) and served in the Navy Reserve, contributing to the Mark I computer at Harvard University. Her work on the Mark I led to her recognition for pioneering contributions to early computing.

#### **UNIVAC and COBOL**
Hopper became an employee of Eckert–Mauchly Computer Corporation in 1949, where she worked on the development of the UNIVAC I, the first large-scale electronic computer. She advocated for the creation of a new programming language that would use English words, leading to the development of COBOL (Common Business-Oriented Language). Her work on compilers and automatic programming was foundational to modern data processing languages.

#### **COBOL and Standards**
Hopper played a key role in the creation of COBOL, which became the most widely used business language. She also contributed to the standardization of programming languages, advocating for the Defense Department to adopt distributed systems and developing validation software for COBOL. Her efforts in standardization significantly influenced the development of programming language dialects among major computer vendors.

#### **Retirement and Legacy**
Hopper retired from the Navy in 1966 and later worked as a senior consultant at Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC). She was awarded the Defense Distinguished Service Medal at her retirement ceremony in 1986. Throughout her career, Hopper was recognized for her groundbreaking work in computing, including the invention of the term "debugging" after discovering a moth in a computer. She received numerous awards, including the Presidential Medal of Freedom posthumously in 2016.

### **Federico Faggin**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Federico Faggin was born in Vicenza, Italy, in 1940. He studied physics at the University of Padua, earning a laurea degree summa cum laude. His early career included work at Olivetti, where he co-designed and led the implementation of a digital transistor computer and the first programmable electronic calculator.

#### **Silicon Valley Career**
Faggin joined SGS-Fairchild in 1967, where he pioneered the MOS metal-gate process technology and designed the first commercial MOS integrated circuits. He moved to Fairchild Semiconductor in 1968, where he led the development of silicon-gate technology (SGT), which revolutionized microelectronics. His contributions included the design of the Fairchild 3708, the first commercial integrated circuit using silicon-gate technology.

#### **Intel and Beyond**
Faggin joined Intel in 1970, where he designed the Intel 4004, the world’s first single-chip microprocessor. His work on the MCS-4 family of microprocessors, including the 4004, 4001, 4002, and 4003, was pivotal. He also contributed to the development of the Z80 microprocessor at Zilog, which became a cornerstone of early personal computers.

#### **Later Innovations**
Faggin co-founded Synaptics in 1986, where he introduced the touchpad and optical character recognizer. He later founded Foveon, where he revitalized the company with innovative image sensors. Additionally, he founded the Federico and Elvia Faggin Foundation, focusing on the scientific study of consciousness.

#### **Theory of Consciousness**
Faggin proposed a theory of consciousness based on quantum physics, suggesting that consciousness is a purely quantum phenomenon unique to each individual. His work on artificial neural networks and consciousness studies has influenced modern discussions on the nature of intelligence and consciousness.

### **Gottlob Frege**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Frege was born in 1848 in Wismar, Germany. His father was a teacher, and his mother was a descendant of Philipp Melanchthon. Frege studied mathematics and philosophy at the University of Jena and later at Göttingen, where he was influenced by Ernst Karl Abbe and other prominent mathematicians and philosophers.

#### **Work as a Logician**
Frege’s *Begriffsschrift* (1879) revolutionized logic by introducing quantified variables and axiomatic predicate logic, laying the foundation for modern mathematical logic. His work in logic and the foundations of arithmetic, including *Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik* (1884) and *Grundgesetze der Arithmetik* (1893), was foundational for Bertrand Russell’s *Principia Mathematica* and other foundational works in mathematics.

#### **Philosophical Contributions**
Frege introduced the distinction between sense (*Sinn*) and reference (*Bedeutung*), a key concept in the philosophy of language. His contributions to the philosophy of mathematics and logic were instrumental in shaping analytic philosophy and the linguistic turn in philosophy.

#### **Political Views and Legacy**
Frege’s later life was marked by antisemitism, as revealed in his diary entries from 1924. Despite this, his work remains influential in logic, philosophy, and computer science. Frege is remembered for his rigorous approach to logic and his foundational contributions to the field of mathematics.

### **Jacek Karpiński**

#### **Family and Early Life**
Jacek Karpiński was born in 1927 in Turin, Italy, into a family of Polish intellectuals and alpinists. His father, Adam Karpiński, was an aeronautic engineer and inventor, and his mother, Wanda Czarnocka-Karpińska, was a physician and dean of the University of Physical Education in Warsaw.

#### **Wartime and Post-War Career**
Karpiński’s life was profoundly impacted by World War II, including the death of his father during an expedition in the Himalayas. He joined the Home Army and participated in numerous operations, including the Warsaw Uprising. After the war, he attended university in Radomsko and later in Łódź, where he graduated in 1951.

#### **Technological Innovations**
Karpiński’s early work included the development of the AAH (Analytical Analyzer of Harmonics), a computer based on vacuum tubes, which improved weather forecasting precision. His most significant achievement was the AKAT-1, the world’s first differential equations analyzer based on transistors, which was recognized for its innovative design and aesthetic appeal.

#### **UNESCO Scholarship and Later Work**
Karpiński was awarded a UNESCO scholarship in 1960, allowing him to study in the United States. He later developed the perceptron, the first known artificial neural network in the Communist Bloc. His work on the KAR-65 computer, a mathematical machine for data analysis, was another major contribution, though its development was hindered by censorship and political pressures.

#### **Controversies and Legacy**
Karpiński worked as a secret informant for the Służba Bezpieczeństwa (Polish secret police) from 1961, providing technological data to the intelligence services. Despite controversies surrounding his cooperation, he was recognized for his pioneering work in computer science and engineering. He was posthumously awarded the Officer’s Cross of Polonia Restituta in 2009.

### **Al-Khwarizmi**

#### **Life and Contributions**
Al-Khwarizmi (c. 780–c. 850) was a Persian mathematician and astronomer who lived during the Islamic Golden Age. He is best known for his work in algebra and arithmetic, which introduced the Hindu–Arabic numeral system to the Western world. His book *Al-Jabr wa'l-Muqabala* (The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing) laid the foundation for modern algebra.

#### **Mathematical and Astronomical Work**
Al-Khwarizmi systematized the solution of linear and quadratic equations, introducing the term "algebra" from his book. His work on arithmetic, including the *Kitab al-Hisab al-Hindī*, introduced the decimal numeral system to Europe. He also contributed significantly to astronomy, geography, and cartography, including the *Zīj as-Sindhind*, which provided astronomical tables and improved geographical data.

#### **Legacy**
Al-Khwarizmi’s work had a profound impact on the development of mathematics and science in the Islamic world and Europe. His contributions to algebra and arithmetic are foundational to modern mathematical practices. He is also honored with a lunar crater and an asteroid named after him.

### **Kenneth E. Iverson**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Kenneth Iverson was born in 1920 in Camrose, Alberta, Canada. He served in the Canadian Army and Royal Canadian Air Force during World War II and later earned a B.A. from Queen’s University and M.Sc. and Ph.D. degrees from Harvard University.

#### **Career and Contributions**
Iverson’s work focused on developing a notation for describing and analyzing data processing, leading to the creation of APL (A Programming Language). His contributions included the development of the IBM System/360 and the implementation of APL, which became a standard language for data processing. Iverson also made significant contributions to education, using APL to teach programming and mathematical concepts.

#### **Later Work and Legacy**
After leaving IBM, Iverson worked at I.P. Sharp Associates and later founded Jsoftware, where he developed the J language. Iverson was awarded the Turing Award in 1979 for his pioneering work in programming languages and their applications. His work laid the foundation for modern data processing and programming languages.

### **Per Brinch Hansen**

#### **Early Life and Education**

Brinch Hansen’s work in operating systems and concurrent programming was groundbreaking. He introduced the concept of a microkernel and developed Concurrent Pascal, a language for concurrent programming. #### **Distributed Computing and Legacy**
Brinch Hansen also contributed to the development of distributed computing and remote procedure calls. He was awarded the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award in 2002 for his pioneering work in operating systems and concurrent programming. His contributions have significantly influenced the development of modern operating systems and concurrent programming languages.

### **George Boole**

#### **Early Life and Education**
George Boole was born in 1815 in Lincoln, England. He was self-taught in mathematics and logic, influenced by his father, a shoemaker, and his interest in the German language. Boole taught mathematics and later became a professor of mathematics at Queen’s College, Cork.

#### **Mathematical and Logical Contributions**
Boole’s work on symbolic logic, particularly his *Mathematical Analysis of Logic* (1847), introduced the concept of Boolean algebra, which forms the basis for modern digital computing. His contributions to logic and probability theory were foundational for the development of computer science and artificial intelligence.

#### **Legacy**
Boole’s work on Boolean algebra has had a profound impact on the field of computer science. He is remembered for his pioneering contributions to logic and mathematics, and his influence extends to modern digital circuits and computing systems. His name is associated with the Boolean data type in many programming languages.

### **Herbert A. Simon**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Herbert Simon was born in 1916 in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. His parents were Jewish immigrants from Germany, and he attended Milwaukee Public Schools. Simon studied political science and economics at the University of Chicago, where he earned his B.A. and Ph.D.

#### **Career and Contributions**
Simon’s work in organizational decision-making and behavioral economics was groundbreaking. His book *Administrative Behavior* (1947) introduced the concept of bounded rationality, which has significantly influenced the fields of economics and management. Simon was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics in 1978 for his pioneering research in decision-making processes within economic organizations.

#### **Artificial Intelligence and Legacy**
Simon was also a pioneer in artificial intelligence, collaborating with Allen Newell to develop the Logic Theory Machine and the General Problem Solver. His work on problem-solving and cognitive psychology has had a lasting impact on the field of artificial intelligence. Simon’s contributions to economics, psychology, and artificial intelligence have made him one of the most influential social scientists of the 20th century.

### **Ramon Llull**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Ramon Llull was born in 1232 or 1233 in Palma, Majorca. He was the son of a wealthy family of Barcelona patricians and was educated in the Latin tradition. Llull’s early life was marked by a religious calling, influenced by visions and a desire to convert Muslims and heretical Christians.

#### **Art and Missionary Work**
Llull’s *Ars* (Art) was a system of universal logic based on a set of general principles activated in a combinatorial process. His work on the *Ars inventiva veritatis* and *Ars amativa* introduced a new approach to logic and knowledge, which he applied to convert Muslims and promote theological humanism. Llull’s writings on the *Ars* and his dialogues, such as *The Book of the Gentile and the Three Wise Men*, were influential in medieval thought.

#### **Legacy**
Llull’s contributions to logic and theology have had a lasting impact on intellectual history. His work on the *Ars* and his missionary efforts laid the foundation for modern combinatorial logic and the study of knowledge. Llull was beatified in 1847 by Pope Pius IX.

#### **Legacy**
Shannon’s work on information theory and Boolean algebra has had a lasting impact on the development of modern computing and communication systems. ### **Kurt Gödel**

#### **Early Life and Education**
Kurt Gödel was born in 1906 in Brünn, Austria-Hungary. He studied mathematics and philosophy at the University of Vienna and later earned his Ph.D. under Hans Hahn’s supervision.

#### **Career and Contributions**
Gödel’s most significant contributions include the incompleteness theorems, which proved that any consistent formal system capable of describing the natural numbers must contain undecidable propositions. His work on computability and recursive functions laid the foundation for modern computer science and logic.

#### **Legacy**
Gödel’s incompleteness theorems have had a profound impact on the fields of mathematics, logic, and computer science. His contributions to the development of modern logic and computability theory have made him one of the most influential mathematicians of the 20th century. His work on the ontological argument for God’s existence has also been influential in philosophy.
