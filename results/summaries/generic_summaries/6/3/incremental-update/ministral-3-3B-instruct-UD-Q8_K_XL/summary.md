**Foundational Theorists and Pioneers in Computing & AI**

The evolution of computing and AI was profoundly shaped by visionaries like **Herbert A. Simon, Per Brinch Hansen, and Edsger W. Dijkstra**, whose work in decision-making, structured programming, and distributed systems laid essential frameworks. Simon’s *bounded rationality* and *satisficing* theories, Hansen’s **Concurrent Pascal**, and Dijkstra’s structured algorithms, alongside **Claude Shannon’s** 1948 information theory, provided the mathematical foundation. **Grace Hopper’s** advancements in **COBOL and FORTRAN** directly influenced modern systems, while **Lynn Conway** and **Federico Faggin** advanced VLSI and semiconductor innovation, including the **Intel 4004 (1971)**. Theoretical contributions from **Kurt Gödel**’s incompleteness theorems and **Jacek Karpiński**’s resilience frameworks further enriched the field.

**George Boole’s** 1849 *Boolean algebra* revolutionized digital logic, enabling **Claude Shannon’s** 1937 application to electromechanical relays—a principle still central to **Boolean data types** and computing. Shannon’s daughter, **Geoffrey Hinton**, became a Turing Award-winning AI researcher, advancing neural networks with **backpropagation** and advocating for ethical AI frameworks like **Islands-of-agreement**, while warning of **AGI risks** and prompting global regulatory discussions.

Beyond computing, **Ramon Llull’s** (1232–1315) work in **combinatorial logic** (*L’Art*) and **Quaternary/Ternary Phases** introduced structured theological and missionary methods, including **tree-based knowledge systems** and **dialogical conversion techniques**. His **Tree of Science** and **proverbial logic** (e.g., *Blanquerna*) made his ideas accessible through novels and diagrams, despite initial academic rejection. Llull’s influence extended to mystics like **Nicholas of Cusa** and logicians such as **Leibniz**, later reviving under **Cisneros** and achieving **beatification (1847)**.

In the **20th–21st centuries**, Llull’s ideas resonated in **surrealism, philosophical fantasy, and metafiction**, appearing in works like **Salvador Dalí’s *Alchimie des Philosophes*** and **Jorge Luis Borges’ *"Ramon Llull’s Thinking Machine"*. His **Ars Magna** was recognized as a precursor to computer science, while his lost manuscripts introduced early **electoral methods** (e.g., **Borda count**, **Condorcet criterion**). Key translations, such as *Ramon Llull’s New Rhetoric* (1994) and *Doctor Illuminatus: A Ramon Llull Reader* (1994), preserved his works, including *The Book of the Lover and the Beloved* and *Ars Brevis*.

From **Boole’s logical algebra** to **Llull’s combinatorial logic**, these pioneers demonstrated how **mathematical rigor, interdisciplinary creativity, and ethical foresight** shaped computing’s evolution—bridging theory, innovation, and enduring cultural impact.
