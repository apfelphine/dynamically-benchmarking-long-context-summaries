### **Education in Pakistan**

#### **Stages of Formal Education**
**Primary Education**
- Only **67.5%** of Pakistani children complete primary school. The curriculum follows the English model, with subjects including Urdu, English, mathematics, science, social studies, and religious education (Islamic Studies).
- Pre-school includes Play Group, Nursery, and Kindergarten (KG). Middle school (years 6–8) often follows single-sex education, though co-education exists in cities.
- **2009 net primary school attendance rate** was **66%** (below the global average of 90%).

**Secondary Education**
- Begins at **year 9** and lasts four years. Students take standardized tests (SSC-I and SSC-II) after years 9 and 10, respectively, to earn a **Secondary School Certificate (SSC)**.
- After SSC, students enter intermediate colleges (years 11–12) and take exams (HSSC-I and HSSC-II) to earn a **Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC)**.
- Alternative qualifications include **General Certificate of Education (GCE)** and **International General Certificate of Secondary Education (IGCSE)**.
- **Technical Education** offers vocational training, including **Technical School Certificate (TSC)** and **Diploma of Associate Engineering (DAE)**.

**Tertiary Education**
- **6%** of Pakistanis (9% of men, 3.5% of women) were university graduates in 2007. The government aims to increase this to **10% by 2015** and **15% by 2020**.
- Professional degrees include engineering, medicine, law, and nursing, typically requiring **4–5 years**. Teacher education programs vary, with some requiring **12 years of schooling** followed by additional degrees.
- **Master’s and Ph.D.** programs are available, though postgraduate education faces challenges, including lack of qualified professionals.

#### **Non-formal and Informal Education**
- Public vocational training centers and apprenticeship systems cater to low-skilled individuals.
- **Madrassas** (Islamic seminaries) teach religious subjects alongside some secular subjects, often attracting the poor due to free food and shelter.
- **Informal education** is provided by NGOs, corporates, and community-based initiatives, including **MagnifiScience Centre** in Karachi.

#### **Gender Disparity**
- Pakistan ranks **second-worst** in gender inequality globally. **18%** of Pakistani women have completed **10+ years of schooling**.
- **Progressive improvements** include:
  - **Female-to-male enrollment ratios** improved from **0.47 (1990–91)** to **0.74 (1999–2000)** in primary education.
  - **Primary education** became a legal right for children aged **5–16** in 2010.
- **Challenges** include:
  - **Low female enrollment** in rural areas due to cultural barriers, safety concerns, and high dropout rates.
  - **Gender segregation** in schools, though some research shows no segregation in visited schools.
  - **STEMM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Mathematics, Medicine) representation** remains low, with **80% of medical students being women** but few practicing in rural areas.

#### **Qualitative Dimension**
- **Declining education quality** due to:
  - **Shortage of teachers** and outdated curriculum.
  - **Lack of professional and communicative skills** among graduates.
  - **Universities’ high costs** deter many students.
- **Reforms** include:
  - **Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989)** and **Millennium Development Goals (UN, 1990)** to improve education.
  - **Free and compulsory education** for all children.

#### **Teacher Education**
- **Teacher training programs** lack qualified professionals, with **one-third of universities in Balochistan** lacking professors in education.
- **Funding and consistency** are critical for improving teacher quality.
- **Challenges** include:
  - **Outdated curriculum** and lack of human rights training.
  - **Short-term training programs** that focus on memorization rather than application.

#### **Technical and Vocational Education**
- **Technical and Vocational Education Training (TVET)** aims to prepare students for employment, offering courses in engineering, agriculture, and healthcare.
- **Government initiatives** include:
  - **Investment in TVET sites** and teacher salaries.
  - **Equity in programs** and modernized curricula.

#### **Distance Learning**
- **Teleschool** (educational TV channel) provides lessons for kindergarten through high school.
- **Allama Iqbal Open University (AIOU)** offers flexible learning through online and offline modes.
- **Digital education** is expanding, though rural students lack internet access.

#### **Achievements**
- **Notable alumni**:
  - **Abdus Salam** (Nobel Prize in Physics).
  - **Atta-ur-Rahman** (UNESCO Science Prize, natural product chemist).
  - **Arfa Abdul Karim Randhawa** (youngest Microsoft Certified Professional).
  - **Nergis Mavalvala** (MacArthur Fellow, gravitational wave researcher).

#### **Education Expenditure**
- **2% of GDP** spent on education (2009). The government aims to increase this to **7% by 2015**.
- **Criticism**: Historical spending trends show stagnation, with **public expenditure** rarely exceeding **2.8%** of GDP since 1972.

### **Education in Russia**

#### **Levels of Education**
- **General education** includes:
  - **Preschool (0 years)**.
  - **Primary (4 years)**.
  - **Basic (5 years)**.
  - **Secondary (2 years)**.
- **Professional education** includes:
  - **Training for professions** (short-term).
  - **Vocational education** (3–4 years).
  - **Higher education** (Bachelor’s, Specialist, Master’s, Ph.D.).

#### **Preschool and Primary School**
- **Kindergarten enrollment** increased from **67% (1999)** to **84% (2005)**.
- **Public kindergartens** are regulated by regional authorities, while the Ministry of Education oversees brief preschool preparation programs.
- **Compulsory education** now includes **9 years** (previously 10).

#### **Secondary School**
- **Nine-year secondary education** is compulsory since **2007**, with **double-shift schools** common in areas with insufficient capacity.
- **Unified State Examinations (USE)** replaced university entrance exams in **2007**, though resistance persists.

#### **Education for the Disabled**
- **Physical disabilities**: Specialized schools for the blind, deaf, and mobility disorders.
- **Mental disabilities**: Compensatory classes and specialized boarding schools for mild disabilities.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Over 50% of Russian adults** have attained tertiary education (higher than OECD average).
- **Public universities** include **8.1 million students** (2007–2008), with **6.2 million** in state-owned institutions.
- **Traditional model**: Single-stage education (5–6 years) leading to a specialist diploma.
- **Bologna Process transition**: Replaced traditional model with **bachelor’s (4 years) and master’s (2 years)** degrees.

#### **Science Outreach**
- **Federal Law (2021)** establishes "outreach activity" to promote knowledge dissemination, though critics argue it imposes prior censorship.

#### **Government Propaganda**
- **"Patriotic lessons"** introduced in schools to promote national identity and patriotism, following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.

#### **Education in Languages**
- **National language (Russian)** is mandatory, with **minority languages** taught in specialized schools.

### **Education in Poland**

#### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary school (szkoła podstawowa)**: 8 years (previously 6 years).
- **Junior high school (gimnazjum)**: 3 years (abolished in 2017).
- **Upper secondary education**: Prepares students for higher education or vocational training.

#### **Upper Secondary Education**
- **General education**: **Liceum** (4 years) leading to **Matura** (university entrance exam).
- **Vocational education**: **Technikum** (5 years) and **Zasadnicza Szkoła Zawodowa** (3 years).
- **Grades**: Numeric system (2–5), with **5.5/6.0** as "exceeds expectations."

#### **Foreign Languages**
- **English** is the most widely taught, followed by German, French, and Spanish.

#### **History**
- **Compulsory education** introduced in **1919** after WWI.
- **Reforms (1999)**: Shifted from **8+4 years** to **6+3+3 years**.
- **Reforms (2017)**: Extended primary school to **8 years** and merged junior high with upper secondary.

#### **Criticism**
- **School violence**: Zero-tolerance policies introduced in **2006** after a student suicide.
- **Declining international rankings**: Reading and science scores below average.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **History**
- **Pre-division period**: Confucian values shaped education, emphasizing meritocracy and bureaucratic exams.
- **Post-war years**: Decentralized education system introduced under U.S. military government.
- **1980s–1990s**: Rapid economic growth led to increased emphasis on **vocational education** and **competitive university admissions**.

#### **School Grades**
- **Elementary school (1–6)**: Ages 6–13.
- **Middle school (7–9)**: Ages 12–15.
- **High school (10–12)**: Ages 15–18.

#### **Primary Education**
- **Curriculum**: Focuses on reading, writing, mathematics, and social studies.
- **English education**: Intensive focus due to globalization, with many students studying abroad.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **High schools**: Specialized tracks (science, foreign language, art) or general state high schools.
- **Vocational education**: **Meister Schools** (German-style) for skilled trades, with **100% employment rates** after graduation.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: Most prestigious route, with **competitive admissions** based on **College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT)**.
- **Vocational education**: Alternative to universities, with **lower prestige** but practical skills.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Competitive education**: **Hagwons** (cram schools) dominate after-school study.
- **Social stigma**: Vocational careers are still stigmatized despite reforms.
- **Health concerns**: **Overwork and stress** among students, leading to high suicide rates.

### **Education in the Czech Republic**

#### **Elementary School**
- **First stage (1–5 years)**: Single teacher, subjects include Czech, mathematics, science, and foreign languages.
- **Second stage (6–9 years)**: More specialized subjects, including physics, chemistry, and biology.

#### **Secondary School**
- **Vocational schools**: 2–3 years, leading to a vocational certificate.
- **Grammar schools**: 4 years, prepare for university.
- **Maturita exam**: Universal leaving qualification for university admission.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: Public and private, offering **Bachelor’s, Master’s, and Ph.D.** degrees.
- **Higher professional schools**: Offer professional tertiary education.

#### **Calendar and Holidays**
- **School year**: September to June, divided into two semesters.
- **Holidays**: Autumn, Christmas, spring, and summer breaks.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary education (basisschool)**: 8 grades (1–8), with **Cito test** in grade 8 to determine secondary education path.
- **Secondary education**: **VMBO, HAVO, VWO** (preparatory middle-level applied education, higher general continued education, preparatory scientific education).

#### **Vocational Education and Higher Education**
- **MBO (middle-level applied education)**: Vocational training, 1–4 years.
- **HBO (higher professional education)**: Professional-oriented, 4 years.
- **WO (scientific education)**: Academic, 3 years for bachelor’s.

#### **History**
- **1800s**: Stratified system based on socioeconomic class.
- **1960s–1990s**: Reforms introduced **VMBO, HAVO, VWO** and standardized curriculum.

#### **Criticism**
- **Early separation into educational levels** may disadvantage gifted students.
- **Declining international rankings** in reading and science.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Timeline of Compulsory Education**
- **1871–1943**: Introduced in provinces like Ontario and Quebec.

#### **Gender and Language-Based Schools**
- **Section 23 of the Charter**: Ensures minority language education.
- **Separate schools**: Guaranteed for religious minorities (e.g., Catholic schools in Ontario).

#### **Education Levels**
- **Early childhood**: Kindergarten (age 5).
- **Primary and secondary**: Grades 1–12, with **Grade 12** as the final year.
- **Tertiary education**: Universities and vocational institutions.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Social promotion policies**: Lower academic standards.
- **Grade inflation and lack of corrective feedback**.

### **Education in Japan**

#### **History**
- **Pre-modern era**: Confucian and Buddhist education.
- **Meiji Restoration (1868)**: Adopted Western education models.
- **Post-WWII**: Decentralized education, nine-year compulsory schooling.

#### **School Levels**
- **Elementary (1–6)**: Ages 6–12.
- **Junior high (7–9)**: Ages 12–15.
- **Senior high (10–12)**: Ages 15–18.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **Lower secondary**: Focuses on core subjects.
- **Upper secondary**: Specialized tracks (academic or vocational).

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: Most prestigious, with **competitive admissions**.
- **Vocational education**: **Junior colleges** and **colleges of technology**.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Extreme academic pressure**: Leads to **bullying, suicide, and mental health issues**.
- **Lack of creativity**: Emphasis on rote learning over critical thinking.

### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Stages of Education**
- **Pre-primary (K–2)**: Ages 3–5.
- **Primary (3–8)**: Ages 6–12.
- **Secondary (9–12)**: Ages 13–16.
- **Tertiary**: Universities and vocational training.

#### **National Curriculum**
- **Mandatory subjects**: Bengali, English, mathematics, science, and social studies.
- **Stream options**: Arts, Commerce, Science.

#### **Gender Disparity**
- **Progressive improvements**: Gender parity in primary and secondary education.
- **Challenges**: Low female enrollment in rural areas, cultural barriers.

#### **Literacy Rate**
- **2022**: **74.66%** (up from **51.77% in 2011**).

### **Education in Mexico**

#### **Structure of Basic Education**
- **Primary (primaria)**: Grades 1–6.
- **Junior high (secundaria)**: Grades 7–9.
- **High school (preparatoria)**: Grades 10–12.

#### **Educational Integration**
- **Inclusive education**: Students with disabilities integrated into mainstream classrooms.

#### **Quality of Education**
- **Low completion rates**: Only **45%** finish secondary school.
- **Reforms**: Shifted control from teachers' union to government.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Licenciatura (4 years)**: Bachelor’s degree.
- **Maestría (2 years)**: Master’s degree.
- **Doctorado (3–4 years)**: Doctoral degree.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

#### **Background**
- **Islamic education**: Core focus on Quran, Islamic studies, and Arabic.
- **Modern reforms**: Emphasis on **STEM subjects** and **vocational training**.

#### **Education Management**
- **Free education**: All levels are free.
- **Ministry of Education**: Oversees public schools and universities.

#### **Girls' and Women's Education**
- **60% of university students**: Women.
- **First female lawyers**: Granted legal licenses in 2013.

#### **Gifted Education**
- **Special programs**: Enrichment and acceleration for gifted students.

#### **Special Education**
- **Students with disabilities**: Educated in special institutions or mainstream schools.

#### **Private Education**
- **Government support**: Free textbooks and financial aid.

#### **Literacy Rate**
- **2007**: **85% adult literacy**, **98% youth literacy**.

### **Education in New Zealand**

#### **History**
- **Māori education**: Traditional wānanga and modern kura kaupapa.
- **State education**: Free, compulsory, and secular since **1878**.

#### **Early Childhood Education**
- **Kindergarten (3–5)**: Teacher-led or parent-led services.

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
- **13-year levels**: Years 1–13.
- **State schools**: Free, secular, and government-funded.
- **State-integrated schools**: Retain special character (e.g., Catholic schools).

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Regulated by NZQF**: Includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges.

#### **Funding**
- **State schools**: Government funding + voluntary donations.
- **Private schools**: Tuition fees (NZ$20,000/year).

#### **Standards and Bullying**
- **Declining math scores**: Concerns over teaching methods.
- **Bullying**: Widespread issue, with **1 in 5 high school students** reporting cyber-bullying.
