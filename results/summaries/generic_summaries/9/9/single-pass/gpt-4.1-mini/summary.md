# Summary of Education Systems

---

## Canada
Education in Canada is mainly under provincial jurisdiction, with compulsory education introduced at varying times from 1871 to 1943 across provinces. The federal government's role is limited to military and Indigenous education. The country spends about 5.2% of GDP on education, with strong investment in tertiary education, boasting the highest global tertiary completion rate (51% in 2010). Most schools are co-educational, and bilingual education (English and French) is widely available, including second-language immersion programs.

Assessment agencies like the Canadian Education Statistics Council collaborate on pan-Canadian indicators, while international assessments such as PISA evaluate student performance. School boards increasingly collect race- and identity-based data to address disparities, leading to debates over equity and fairness. The education system includes public, private, and religious schools, with constitutional protections for certain religious and linguistic education systems. The length of schooling varies by province but typically spans Kindergarten (starting age 4 or 5) to Grade 12, with exceptions such as Quebec ending secondary education at Grade 11 followed by CEGEP.

Tertiary education is provided by universities and vocational colleges, offering degrees and diplomas. Universities require competitive admission based on academic performance and other achievements. Vocational education delivers practical training and certifications. Private universities exist but are less common and sometimes controversial. Indigenous education and programs for equity and inclusion are present but vary regionally.

## Czech Republic
Elementary education consists of nine grades, split into a five-year first stage and a four-year second stage, with a focus on Czech language, foreign languages, sciences, and arts. Secondary education includes vocational schools, professional schools, lyceums, and grammar schools; many culminate with the "maturita" exam required for university entry. Vocational and professional schools may offer dual qualifications.

Higher education comprises public and private universities and higher professional schools, with bachelor's, master's, doctoral degrees, and professional titles awarded. University education is free in Czech for first-time students in Czech, with paid options for international students or instruction in English. The academic calendar runs from September to June, with multiple holidays. Special and practical schools serve students with disabilities or learning challenges.

## Bangladesh
The education system is divided into pre-primary, primary (classes 3-8), secondary (9-12), and tertiary levels, with education offered in Bengali or English mediums. National curriculum textbooks are standardized, and standardized exams occur at multiple grade levels. Students choose among Arts, Commerce, or Science streams from class 9 onward.

Primary education is overseen by the Ministry of Primary and Mass Education, while secondary and tertiary levels fall under the Ministry of Education. There are numerous government and private schools, including cadet colleges and international schools. The Technical and Vocational Education system provides practical training from one month to four years, with diploma programs allowing progression to bachelor's degrees.

Alternative education includes international schools (following British or IB curricula), madrasah education (Qawmi and Alia systems), and refugee education for Rohingya children. Literacy has improved significantly, with government programs aiming to boost education but facing challenges such as poverty, gender disparities, and infrastructure deficits. Primary and secondary education completion rates vary, with ongoing efforts to address quality and access.

## Poland
Compulsory education starts typically at age six or seven with eight years of primary school, followed by upper secondary education in various formats including general secondary schools (liceum) and vocational schools (technikum and basic vocational schools). The education reform in 2017 replaced the three-year gimnazjum with an extended primary school.

Tertiary education follows the Bologna Process with three-year bachelor's and two-year master's degrees, while doctoral programs take about three years. University grading uses a 2.0 (fail) to 5.0 (excellent) scale. Foreign languages, mainly English and German, are widely taught, and the system reflects historical influences from the 12th century and reforms in 1999 and 2017. Violence policies promoting "zero tolerance" in schools were introduced following incidents in 2006.

## Saudi Arabia
Education, rooted in Islamic traditions, is free and managed by the Ministry of Education with pre-primary to tertiary levels. Kindergarten attendance is not compulsory, primary education lasts six years, followed by three years each of intermediate and secondary education. Secondary paths include general and technical streams with a strong religious curriculum that has been recently reformed for modernization.

Higher education consists of government universities, private colleges, and specialized institutions. Female education has advanced with women comprising a majority of university students, supported by large scholarship programs. Gifted education programs have expanded since 2002, and special education integrates disabled students with varied support.

Private schools are common, with government supervision and aid. International schools serve expatriate communities, including a network of Philippine schools. Literacy rates show gender disparity but have improved significantly in youth. Criticism exists over rote learning dominance and religious intolerance in curricula, with ongoing reforms targeting curriculum modernization and inclusivity.

## Pakistan
Pakistan’s formal education is structured in primary (grades 1-5), middle (6-8), secondary (9-10), and higher secondary (11-12), followed by tertiary education. The system is influenced by British models with Urdu and English mediums. Public expenditure on education is low (~2% GDP). Quality issues include outdated curricula, teacher shortages, gender disparities favoring males, and inadequate infrastructure.

Secondary education culminates in standardized exams (SSC and HSSC), with alternative qualifications like O/A levels offered by British boards. Tertiary education offers bachelor’s, master’s, MPhil, and PhD programs in various fields, alongside vocational and technical training.

Non-formal education, madrasahs, and international schools coexist. Literacy has improved but remains uneven across gender and regions. Gender disparity is significant, especially in rural areas, affecting enrollment and academic performance. Teacher education reform, technical/vocational training, and distance learning initiatives (especially during COVID-19) are active areas for development. The education system also faces challenges from socio-religious dynamics affecting science education and inclusivity.

## New Zealand
Education includes early childhood, primary (Years 1–8), intermediate (Years 7–8), and secondary (Years 9–13) schooling. Education is free and compulsory between ages 6 and 16, with widespread choices for homeschooling and Māori-medium education. The National Curriculum guides state schools; private and state-integrated schools exist with distinct funding models.

Tertiary education encompasses universities and vocational institutions within the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Funding depends on enrollment and school socio-economic decile, with student loans and allowances supporting higher education. Māori language revitalization is ongoing, with immersion schools and curricula.

Challenges include declining academic performance in numeracy and literacy, significant bullying incidence, and disparities in achievement among Māori and Pacific Island students. Extracurricular activities and shadow education are common, with continued debates over balancing academic rigor and wellbeing.

## Russia
Education comprises general education (preschool, primary, basic, secondary) and professional education (vocational and higher education). Compulsory general education covers nine years, with an optional two-year upper secondary stage. Vocational schools provide occupational training over three years.

Higher education includes bachelor's, specialist, master's, and postgraduate degrees (Candidate and Doctor of Sciences). The system is transitioning to Bologna standards but retains traditional structures. Unified State Examinations standardize university admissions.

Special education serves children with physical and mental disabilities in dedicated or integrated settings. Outreach activities for education outside formal programs are legislated. Recent state influences include patriotic education. Multiple ethnic languages are taught in respective schools.

## Mexico
Basic education spans primary (grades 1–6), junior high (7–9), and high school (10–12). Compulsory education covers primaria and secundaria; preparatoria is increasingly compulsory. Vocational tracks and distance learning exist alongside traditional academic paths.

Higher education mirrors the US/Bologna model with bachelor's (4 years), master's (2 years), and doctoral (3+ years) degrees. Intercultural universities address indigenous enrollment gaps.

The historical evolution includes colonial, post-independence, and modern reforms balancing secularism and church influence. Challenges include infrastructure deficits, quality concerns, and conflicts with teachers’ unions.

## South Korea
Education is deeply influenced by Confucianism, emphasizing meritocracy and rigorous academic competition. The system includes six years elementary, three years middle school (compulsory), and three years high school, which may be general, vocational, or specialized (science, arts, international).

University admission relies heavily on the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT). Vocational education has expanded via Meister Schools inspired by German models to address skilled trade shortages and counter stigma against non-university tracks.

Higher education is widespread, with numerous universities offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. English education is a major focus, despite historically low proficiency. Challenges include social stigma on vocational training, intense academic pressure leading to health concerns, and mismatch between jobs and majors.

## Japan
Education began in the 6th century, evolving through Confucian and Buddhist influences, Western-style reforms during the Meiji Restoration, and post-WWII American-modeled systems. Compulsory education lasts nine years (elementary and junior high). High school is not mandatory but nearly universally attended.

The school year runs April to March, with defined grade levels from elementary to tertiary. Secondary education prepares students for university entrance, often supplemented by private cram schools (juku). Higher education comprises universities, junior colleges and specialized colleges.

Current challenges include high academic pressure causing stress and health issues, criticism of rote learning, bullying, and insufficient support for special needs students. Inclusion and equal rights for disabled students have gained recent attention.

## Netherlands
Education is coordinated nationally, with compulsory schooling from age five to eighteen (part-time after sixteen). Elementary school lasts eight grades; secondary education streams students by ability and interest into VMBO (vocational), HAVO (general), or VWO (pre-university).

The system offers multiple pathways allowing movement between levels and emphasizes early tracking, which has faced criticism for misplacement and social inequality. Vocational education (MBO), higher professional (HBO), and academic university (WO) paths follow secondary completion. The Dutch system aligns with the Bologna process for tertiary education.

Religious particularism allows for several types of publicly funded schools with differing philosophies. The government oversees quality and funding, including accreditation processes. Educational performance has slightly declined in international rankings recently.
