Global education systems are highly varied, ranging from centralized (South Korea) to politically guided (Russia), and facing diverse challenges such as skills gaps (New Zealand) or literacy deficits (Pakistan). Japan emphasizes intense university preparation to drive economic growth, sometimes leading to mental health issues.

In contrast, the Netherlands features a nationally and locally structured system compulsory until age 18 or diploma, which has evolved significantly from its early class-based structure. Key milestones include compulsory education starting at age 6-12 (1901) and the 1963 *Mammoetwet*, which reorganized secondary education based on student capability. Current reforms aim for integration, such as standardizing early secondary schooling (*basisvorming*) and fusing lower education streams.

The educational pathway includes elementary and secondary education, with upper secondary specialization occurring via 'profiles' (e.g., Culture & Society, Economy & Society). While the system is nationally assured and adheres to the Bologna process (since 2002) using ECTS, it faces criticism regarding early separation based on IQ testing, as IQ can develop further with stimulation. Furthermore, inequalities exist due to differences in parental engagement and a correlation between lower parental income and advising less education for students. International assessments (PISA) also show a decline in the Netherlands' educational standing since 2006, with noted drops in primary reading, arithmetic, and science standards.

Vocational and higher education are segmented:
*   **Vocational Education (MBO):** Offers school-based or apprenticeship routes, leading to certification typically within four years for job entry or progression to HBO.
*   **Higher Education:** Comprises **Universities of Applied Sciences (HBO)**, focused on professional training (accepting MBO-4, HAVO, VWO graduates), and **Research Universities (WO)**, focused on scientific learning (typically accepting VWO and HBO graduates). Admission requirements are structured, with WO usually requiring VWO or an HBO propaedeuse.
