### **Education in the Czech Republic**

#### **Elementary School**
The Czech education system divides elementary school into two stages: the first five years (grades 1–5) and the next four years (grades 6–9), which are compulsory. Subjects include Czech language, mathematics, science, history, geography, foreign languages (usually English), art, music, physical education, and handcrafts. Students can continue at an elementary school, an eight-year academy (Osmileté gymnázium), or a six-year academy (Šestileté gymnázium). The latter two are rare; most students opt for elementary school continuation.

#### **Secondary School**
Secondary education in the Czech Republic requires entrance exams in mathematics, English, or Czech. Students can pursue vocational certificates (2–3 years) or academic qualifications (4 years) leading to university admission. Options include professional schools (lyceums), grammar schools, and vocational schools. The *maturita* exam, mandatory for university admission, includes Czech language, world literature, mathematics, and a foreign language.

#### **Tertiary Education**
The Czech Republic has a long history of higher education, with Charles University in Prague founded in 1348. Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Public universities are free for first-time students under 26, while private institutions charge tuition fees. The Bologna Process has influenced degree structures, though traditional five-year specialist programs persist. Higher professional schools (VOŠ) offer vocational degrees, and universities focus on research and academic excellence.

### **Education in Poland**

#### **Compulsory Education**
Poland’s education system is divided into primary (grades 1–8), junior high (gimnazjum, grades 9–11), and secondary education. Primary education is now 8 years, replacing the previous 6-year primary + 3-year junior high system. The *gimnazjum* (junior high) was abolished in 2017, with primary education extended to 8 years and secondary education extended by one year. Students must pass standardized exams at the end of primary and secondary school to earn certificates.

#### **Upper Secondary Education**
Upper secondary education prepares students for university or the workforce. Options include general secondary schools (liceum), vocational schools (technikum), and specialized lyceums. The *matura* exam, required for university admission, includes Czech, world literature, mathematics, and a foreign language. Vocational schools offer certificates in trades like accounting, mechanics, or IT.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Poland follows the Bologna Process, with tertiary education divided into bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and doctoral programs. Vocational education is handled by post-secondary schools (szkoła policealna), offering 2.5-year programs. The grading system ranges from 2 (fail) to 5 (excellent), with additional grades like 5.5 or 3-. Foreign language education is mandatory, with English being the most common. The education system has faced reforms, including the 2017 changes to primary and secondary education.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
Education in Canada is provincially managed, with K-12 (Kindergarten to Grade 12) being the most common structure. Compulsory attendance varies by province, ranging from ages 5 to 16. Schools are divided into public (English/French), private, and religiously affiliated. The curriculum includes language arts, mathematics, and science, with additional focus on social studies, arts, and physical education. Some provinces offer bilingual education (French/English immersion).

#### **Tertiary Education**
Post-secondary education includes universities (offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees) and vocational institutions (offering diplomas, certificates, and apprenticeships). Universities are highly competitive, with admission based on high school transcripts, standardized tests, and extracurricular activities. Vocational schools focus on technical and trade skills, with apprenticeships providing hands-on training. Canada invests heavily in tertiary education, with universities ranked globally.

#### **Special Features**
Canada’s education system is influenced by constitutional protections for religious and language-based schools. The *Charter of Rights and Freedoms* ensures bilingual education in most provinces. International schools cater to expatriate communities, while Indigenous education programs address cultural and historical needs. The system emphasizes inclusivity, with initiatives like anti-racism and Aboriginal studies.

### **Education in Pakistan**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
Pakistan’s education system is divided into primary (grades 1–5), junior school (grades 6–8), and secondary education (grades 9–12). Primary education is not compulsory, with only 67.5% of children completing it. Secondary education requires passing national exams (SSC and HSSC) to earn a matriculation or intermediate certificate. Vocational education is available through technical schools, offering diplomas in engineering, agriculture, and trades.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Higher education is divided into professional degrees (4–5 years) and general degrees (3 years). Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral programs, with public institutions being more affordable. The education system faces challenges, including low literacy rates (60.65% as of 2023), gender disparities, and poor infrastructure. Madrasas (Islamic schools) are also significant, with many offering free education in exchange for religious instruction.

#### **Gender Disparity and Challenges**
Pakistan’s education system has faced gender inequality, with only 18% of women completing 10+ years of education. Madrasas and cultural norms often limit girls’ access to education. Reforms aim to improve literacy, vocational training, and gender equality, but progress remains slow. The education system also struggles with outdated curricula, lack of qualified teachers, and inadequate infrastructure.

### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
Bangladesh’s education system is divided into pre-primary (K-2), primary (3–8), secondary (9–12), and tertiary levels. The national curriculum is taught in Bengali or English, with Bengali mandatory for all students. Primary education is free and compulsory, but secondary education is not. Madrasahs (Islamic schools) and international schools offer alternative curricula.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Tertiary education includes public, private, and international universities. Public universities are heavily subsidized, while private universities charge tuition fees. The education system has faced criticism for rote learning and lack of industry-oriented skills. Reforms aim to improve quality, including free textbooks for primary and secondary school students.

#### **Challenges and Reforms**
Bangladesh’s education system faces issues like low literacy rates (74.66% as of 2022), gender disparities, and poor infrastructure. The government has introduced reforms to make education more accessible and relevant, including free primary education and scholarships for girls. Madrasahs and international schools cater to diverse educational needs, with some offering English-medium education.

### **Education in Russia**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
Russia’s education system includes preschool (ages 2–6), primary (grades 1–4), basic general education (grades 5–9), and secondary education (grades 10–11). Compulsory education lasts 11 years, with students required to pass standardized exams. Vocational training is available through specialized schools, offering certificates and high school diplomas.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Higher education in Russia is divided into bachelor’s (4 years), master’s (2 years), and doctoral programs. The system has transitioned from a traditional five-year specialist model to the Bologna Process, with universities offering both academic and vocational degrees. Public universities are free, while private institutions charge tuition fees. The education system has faced criticism for overcrowded classrooms, outdated curricula, and lack of international recognition.

#### **Special Education and Reforms**
Russia has a strong focus on special education for students with disabilities, with specialized schools and inclusive programs. Reforms aim to improve quality and accessibility, including the introduction of the Unified State Examination (USE) for university admissions. The education system has also faced criticism for political influence, with debates over patriotic education and censorship.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
Saudi Arabia’s education system is free and compulsory for all students. Primary education lasts 6 years (grades 1–6), with English introduced from grade 1. Secondary education lasts 3 years, with students choosing between general and technical tracks. The curriculum emphasizes Islamic studies, mathematics, science, and social sciences.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Higher education in Saudi Arabia includes government universities (offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees) and private institutions. The system has faced criticism for rote learning and lack of innovation. Reforms aim to improve quality, including the introduction of English as a medium of instruction and vocational training. The government has also increased funding for higher education to meet labor market demands.

#### **Gender and Vocational Education**
Women make up 60% of university students, with Saudi Arabia offering scholarships for female students. Vocational education is expanding, with schools offering technical and trade skills. The education system has also faced criticism for promoting intolerance and extremism, with reforms aimed at modernizing the curriculum.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
South Korea’s education system is highly competitive, with primary education lasting 6 years (grades 1–6) and secondary education lasting 3 years (grades 7–9). Students must pass entrance exams to enter secondary school. The curriculum emphasizes academics, with intense focus on exams and extracurricular study (hagwons). Vocational education is also available, with Meister Schools offering technical training.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Higher education in South Korea includes universities (offering bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees) and vocational colleges. The system is highly competitive, with university admissions based on the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT). The education system has faced criticism for overemphasis on exams, lack of creativity, and poor student health. Reforms aim to reduce pressure and improve quality.

#### **International Education and Criticism**
South Korea has a strong international education sector, with many students studying abroad. The education system has also faced criticism for social stigma against vocational education and excessive focus on university admissions. Reforms aim to balance academic and vocational education, with a focus on critical thinking and creativity.

### **Education in New Zealand**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
New Zealand’s education system is free and compulsory for all students from age 5 to 16. Primary education lasts 8 years (grades 1–8), with secondary education lasting 5 years (grades 9–13). The curriculum includes English, mathematics, science, social studies, and technology. Māori language education is also available, with schools offering immersion programs.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Tertiary education in New Zealand includes universities, polytechnics, and colleges. The system is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework (NZQF). Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees, while polytechnics offer vocational and applied degrees. The education system has faced criticism for low academic standards and lack of innovation.

#### **Special Features**
New Zealand’s education system emphasizes inclusivity, with special schools for students with disabilities. The system also offers home schooling and international schools for expatriate communities. Reforms aim to improve quality and accessibility, including the introduction of the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) and Te Whāriki (Māori curriculum).

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Primary and Secondary Education**
The Netherlands’ education system is divided into primary (8 years, grades 1–8), secondary (4 years, grades 7–10), and vocational education. Primary education is free, with secondary education offering three tracks: VMBO (vocational), HAVO (academic), and VWO (university-preparatory). The curriculum emphasizes practical skills and theoretical knowledge.

#### **Tertiary Education**
Higher education in the Netherlands includes universities of applied sciences (HBO) and research universities (WO). The system follows the Bologna Process, with bachelor’s (4 years), master’s (2 years), and doctoral programs. Vocational education is also available, with schools offering certificates and diplomas. The education system has faced criticism for overemphasis on exams and lack of creativity.

#### **International Education and Criticism**
The Netherlands has a strong international education sector, with many students studying abroad. The education system has also faced criticism for social stratification and lack of innovation. Reforms aim to improve quality and accessibility, including the introduction of the New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) and Te Whāriki (Māori curriculum).
