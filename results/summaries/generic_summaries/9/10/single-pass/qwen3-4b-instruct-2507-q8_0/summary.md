### **Education in the Czech Republic**
- **Elementary School (základní škola):** Nine grades, split into two stages. First stage (grades 1–5) includes core subjects like Czech, math, science, and foreign languages; second stage (grades 6–9) adds more advanced subjects and is the last compulsory stage. Students may continue in elementary schools, attend eight- or six-year academies, or transition to grammar schools.
- **Secondary School:** Requires entrance tests in math, English, or Czech (administered by CERMAT). Offers vocational certificates (2–3 years) or academic *maturita* (four years). *Maturita* includes mandatory subjects like Czech, literature, math, and a foreign language, plus optional exams.
- **Special and Practical Schools:** Special schools for developmentally disabled students are being phased out; practical schools teach self-sufficiency. "Integrated education" for underperforming students is growing.
- **Tertiary Education:** Includes public universities (e.g., Charles University, Palacký University) and higher professional schools (VOŠ). Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees with titles like *Bc.*, *Mgr.*, *Ing.*, and *PhDr.*. Public universities are seen as higher quality than private ones.
- **Calendar:** School year runs from September to June, split into two semesters with breaks. Holidays include autumn, winter, spring, and summer breaks. Children's Day (1 June) is a cultural event, not a holiday.

### **Education in Poland**
- **Primary School (podstawówka):** 8 years (grades 1–8), previously 6 years with a middle school (gimnazjum). Reformed in 2017 to eliminate middle school, extending primary education to 8 years.
- **Secondary Education:** General education in *liceum* (3 years) leads to *matura*. Vocational training in *technikum* (5 years) or *zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa* (3 years) leads to a certificate or *matura*. *Liceum profilowane* offers vocational training in specific fields. Special schools prepare students for *matura*.
- **Tertiary Education:** Follows the Bologna model. Bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and PhD programs. Grading uses a 2–5 scale; a 5.0 is top. Foreign language exams are part of entrance requirements.
- **Foreign Languages:** Students learn two foreign languages (e.g., English, German). A 2017 reform requires students to study two languages, with one taught three times a week and the other twice.
- **History:** Education emphasized national identity and political history, with a shift toward a more Eurocentric and nationalist curriculum in recent reforms.
- **Violence:** A 2006 reform introduced a "zero tolerance" policy, giving teachers civil servant status and allowing them to report violent acts with legal consequences.

### **Education in Canada**
- **Timeline:** Compulsory education introduced in provinces starting in the 1870s. Education is provincial, with variations in length and structure.
- **Primary and Secondary:** Grades K–12; kindergarten (age 5) to Grade 12. Secondary education ends at Grade 12 (age 17). Some provinces (e.g., Quebec) end at Grade 11.
- **Tertiary Education:** Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Universities (research-focused) and colleges (vocational) provide degrees. Private institutions are more expensive and less prestigious than public ones.
- **Grading:** Uses a 2–5 scale; 3.0 is the lowest passing grade. Students may retake failed courses.
- **Language and Religion:** French immersion and English immersion are available. Religious schools (e.g., Catholic) are publicly funded in Ontario.
- **Divisions:** Schools are grouped by language (e.g., French, English) and religion. Length of study varies by province (e.g., Ontario allows a "victory lap" of a fifth year).
- **Schools:** Public, private, and religious schools exist. Private schools are common in Quebec.

### **Education in Pakistan**
- **Stages:** Pre-primary (3–5 years), primary (3–8), secondary (9–12), tertiary.
- **Curriculum:** Students study arts, computer science, science, mathematics, religion, and social studies. English and Urdu are core languages.
- **Examinations:** Standardized tests (SSC and HSSC) are required at the end of each level. Alternative qualifications (e.g., GCE, IGCSE) are available.
- **Tertiary Education:** Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Teacher education includes pre-service and postgraduate programs.
- **Gender Disparity:** Significant gender gap in education, especially in rural areas. Female enrollment and performance are improving.
- **Quality and Challenges:** Education is outdated, with poor infrastructure, teacher shortages, and lack of relevance to modern needs. Government spending on education is low (2.2% of GDP).
- **Madrassas:** Provide religious education; often teach rote memorization of Quran and Islamic texts. Criticized for promoting extremism.
- **Literacy:** Increased from 51.77% (2011) to 60.65% (2023). Rural areas still face significant challenges.

### **Education in Bangladesh**
- **Structure:** Pre-primary (class k-2), primary (class 3–8), secondary (class 9–12), tertiary.
- **Curriculum:** National curriculum in Bengali or English. Students choose between arts, commerce, or science streams at class 9–10.
- **Examinations:** Standardized tests (JSC, SSC, HSC) are mandatory. Students must pass to advance.
- **Schools:** Government, private, and madrasahs exist. Madrasahs are religious schools; Alia and Qawmi madrasahs are common.
- **Gender Disparity:** Gender parity achieved in primary and secondary education. Women are underrepresented in higher education.
- **Education Expenditure:** Public spending on education is around 2% of GDP.
- **Literacy:** Increased from 51.77% (2011) to 74.66% (2022). Rural areas still face challenges.
- **Vocational Education:** Technical and vocational training is available through government and private institutions.

### **Education in Russia**
- **Levels:** General and professional education. General education includes preschool, primary (4 years), basic (5 years), and secondary (2 years). Professional education includes training, vocational education (3–4 years), and higher education (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).
- **Preschool and Primary:** Kindergartens are available from age 2; enrollment is not mandatory. Teachers are mostly women.
- **Secondary Education:** Nine-year compulsory education (grades 1–9). Students may choose vocational training or continue to secondary school. The Unified State Examination (USE) is required for university admission.
- **Higher Education:** Traditional model includes a five-year specialist degree. Transition to the Bologna model (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD) began in 2007.
- **Vocational and Special Education:** Vocational schools offer training in technical and practical fields. Special education for disabled students is provided in special schools or inclusive classrooms.
- **Religious Education:** Islamic studies are mandatory in primary and secondary schools. Religious content is prominent, especially in early education.
- **Education Expenditure:** Public spending on education is around 2% of GDP.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**
- **Background:** Education rooted in Islamic values. Schools are primarily Islamic, with a strong focus on religious studies.
- **Structure:** Pre-primary (3–5 years), primary (6 years), intermediate (3 years), secondary (3 years). Students can attend technical or vocational schools.
- **Higher Education:** Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. King Saud University is the first university.
- **Girls’ Education:** Women constitute 60% of university students. Government provides scholarships and supports female education.
- **Gifted Education:** Programs for gifted students include enrichment activities and summer programs.
- **Special Education:** Students with disabilities are supported in regular schools with special education teachers and resources.
- **Private Education:** Private schools are common, especially for English-language education. The government regulates private institutions.
- **Criticism:** Education system criticized for being overly religious and rote-based, with limited focus on science and critical thinking.

### **Education in South Korea**
- **History:** Influenced by Confucian values. Modern education system began in the 19th century, with reforms during the Japanese occupation and post-liberation.
- **Structure:** Primary (grades 1–6), middle (7–9), high school (10–12), tertiary (college/university).
- **Primary and Secondary:** Students learn core subjects (Korean, math, science, social studies). English is taught from grade 1.
- **Vocational Education:** Vocational schools (Meister Schools) train students in skilled trades. These schools are gaining popularity.
- **Higher Education:** Bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. University entrance is highly competitive, with the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) being key.
- **Criticism:** High academic pressure, excessive use of cram schools (hagwons), and student health issues. Students often study late and have limited free time.
- **English Education:** English is emphasized due to globalization. Parents invest heavily in English language training.

### **Education in New Zealand**
- **Structure:** Compulsory education from age 5 to 16. Schools include state, state-integrated, and private. Students follow the National Curriculum (NZC) or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools.
- **Schools:** Primary (Years 1–8), secondary (Years 9–13). Students can transition between levels based on performance.
- **Māori Language:** Māori language education is growing, with immersion schools (kōhanga reo, kura kaupapa) offering full immersion in Māori.
- **Tertiary Education:** Regulated under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework. Students receive government funding or loans.
- **Funding:** State schools receive per-student funding; private schools rely on tuition. Special needs students receive ORS funding.
- **Criticism:** Education standards have declined in international rankings (TIMSS). Bullying and student well-being are concerns.

### **Education in the Netherlands**
- **Structure:** Compulsory education from age 5 to 18. Includes primary (basisschool), secondary (VMBO, HAVO, VWO), and higher education (MBO, HBO, WO).
- **Secondary Education:** VMBO (vocational), HAVO (general), and VWO (academic). Students are assessed annually; retaking years is common.
- **Higher Education:** MBO (vocational), HBO (professional), and WO (academic). Follows the Bologna model with bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees.
- **International Schools:** 152 international schools offer curricula in English or other languages.
- **History:** Education system reformed in the 1960s to create a more structured and equitable system.
- **Criticism:** Early level placement decisions (based on IQ tests) may misclassify students. Performance in international rankings has declined.

### **Education in Mexico**
- **Structure:** Primary (1–6), junior high (7–9), high school (10–12). Secondary education is not compulsory.
- **Special Education:** Students with disabilities are integrated into regular classrooms with support from USAER professionals.
- **Quality of Education:** Only 45% of students complete secondary school. Rural schools face infrastructure issues.
- **Higher Education:** Bachelor’s (Licenciatura), master’s (Maestría), and doctoral (Doctorado) degrees. Postgraduate programs are available.
- **Intercultural Universities:** Established to improve indigenous enrollment in higher education.
- **International Schools:** 151 international schools offer curricula in English.
- **History:** Education was historically under the Catholic Church; post-independence reforms led to secularization and state control.

### **Education in Japan**
- **Structure:** Compulsory education from age 6 to 15. Primary (1–6), junior high (7–9), senior high (10–12). Higher education includes universities, junior colleges, and vocational schools.
- **Grading:** Uses a 5-point scale (shū to fuka), with 5 being excellent. Academic performance is heavily emphasized.
- **Extracurricular Activities:** Shadow education (e.g., juku, mogi shiken) is widespread to prepare for university entrance exams.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (e.g., University of Tokyo, Kyoto University) are prestigious and selective. Bachelor’s degrees take 4 years.
- **Criticism:** High academic pressure, school violence, and student mental health issues. Students have limited free time and face bullying.
- **Inclusive Education:** Government reforms in the 1990s promote integration of students with disabilities into mainstream classrooms.
