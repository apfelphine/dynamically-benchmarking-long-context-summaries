### Education Systems in Poland and the Czech Republic

**Poland**
Poland's education system, rooted in the 12th century with the 1364 Jagiellonian University and compulsory education established in 1919, has undergone significant structural evolution. It shifted from an 8+4/5 structure under the 2017 PiS government (which prioritized a Polish-centric curriculum) back to a 6-year primary, 3-year junior high, and 3-year senior high model that boosted OECD rankings. Key features include mandates for two foreign languages in lower secondary schools, university admissions based on exams, and a 2006 "zero tolerance" policy granting teachers civil servant status with severe penalties for violence. Illiteracy was reduced to 1.2% by 1978, and education remains mandatory until age 18.

**Czech Republic**
The Czech Republic utilizes a tiered elementary system (5 years with single teachers followed by 4 years introducing more subjects and languages) leading to either 6-year or 8-year academies. Secondary education, reformed in 2016/2017, requires entrance tests in mathematics, English, or Czech for schools offering the *maturita* degree, which serves as the universal leaving qualification. This degree mandates Czech language/literature plus mathematics or a foreign language (up to B1). The system diversifies into vocational schools (2–3 years), professional schools/lyceums (4 years), and grammar schools. Historically, special schools for developmentally disabled students have been replaced by practical schools (1–2 years for self-sufficiency) and integrated models, though challenges regarding the over-representation of Romani pupils persist.

**Tertiary Education and Administration**
Czech higher education is anchored by the Charles University (founded 1348) and Palacký University (1576), with Masaryk University as the second largest. Institutions include public universities (free for first-time attendees under 26, perceived as higher quality than private ones), private universities (tuition €2,000–€10,000, sometimes criticized for scandals), and Higher Professional Schools (VOŠ) offering professional training culminating in a "DiS" title. Programs last 2–6 years, offering degrees from Bachelor's (Bc.) to Doctorate (PhDr., JUDr., etc.). International students can study for free in Czech or pay for English instruction. The academic calendar runs from September to June, divided into two semesters with breaks including Christmas (9–12 days), summer (62+ days), Easter, and mid-term, while Children's Day (June 1) is celebrated with trips rather than as a public holiday.
