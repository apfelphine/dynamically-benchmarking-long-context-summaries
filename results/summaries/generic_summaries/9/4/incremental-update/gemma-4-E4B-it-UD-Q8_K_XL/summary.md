**Global Comparative Education Systems Overview**

**System Characteristics (General):**
Education quality globally varies between centrally controlled systems (e.g., Russia, Bangladesh) and those based on national frameworks (e.g., NZ, Saudi Arabia). Common international challenges include improving academic outcomes, reducing dropouts, ensuring equity, supporting student welfare, balancing modernization with cultural heritage, and addressing insufficient public investment and weak infrastructure (e.g., Pakistan). While some nations utilize data for identifying shortcomings, transparency in reporting remains a worldwide issue.

**Regional System Highlights & Structure:**
*   **Canada:** Operates a decentralized K-12 system managed at the provincial/territorial level, which includes federal funding for Indigenous education and provisions for minority languages, alongside a tertiary sector encompassing universities, colleges, and trades.
*   **Poland:** Features an 8-year primary stage, followed by upper secondary tracks—general studies (*liceum*) leading to the *Matura*, or vocational training (*technikum*). Its tertiary education follows the Bologna scheme, utilizing a numerical grading system (2.0 to 5.0). Recent reforms mandate two foreign languages in lower secondary education, and compulsory schooling now extends to age 18.
*   **Czech Republic:** Employs a two-stage elementary school, where the final four years are compulsory and require two foreign languages. Secondary education includes vocational and professional tracks alongside *lyceums*. The universal entry qualification for higher education is the standardized *Maturita* exam, requiring Czech proficiency and either math or a foreign language. Furthermore, special schools for disabled children are increasingly being replaced by elementary and practical schools, with integration of under-performing students becoming more common.
    *   **Tertiary Education:** The system includes public and private universities and specialized academies. Public universities offer free Czech-language study for first-time attendees (state funding ceases after age 26); international students can study free in Czech or pay in English. Private tuition ranges from €2,000–€3,000 annually, though public institutions are generally considered higher quality. Degree structures involve a 3-year Bachelor's (*Bc./BcA*, requiring *Maturita*), Master's, and Ph.D. (requiring a Master's). Professional schools (*VOŠ*) grant the honorific "DiS." The academic year spans September to June in two semesters.
*   **Pakistan:** Faces severe educational deficits, highlighted by a 60.65% literacy rate in 2023 and low primary completion, worsened by minimal public spending ($\sim2\%$ of GDP) and inadequate infrastructure.
*   **Bangladesh:** While literacy is improving (74.66% in 2022) and gender parity exists at lower levels, the system struggles with low public investment ($\sim2\%$ of GDP) and high teacher absenteeism (15.5%), alongside the presence of subsidized and private madrasas.
*   **Russia:** Implements a centralized curriculum and standard USE testing, increasingly promoting national identity through mandatory "patriotic lessons" and limiting access for non-Russian speakers.
*   **Saudi Arabia:** Provides free, compulsory primary education that merges Islamic tradition with contemporary standards to support Vision 2030, though rote learning persists.
*   **South Korea:** Is marked by significant academic pressure fueled by *hagwons* and Confucian values, leading to welfare concerns.
*   **New Zealand:** Maintains a free, compulsory, secular 13-year national curriculum, though disparities in equity between Pākehā and Māori/Pacific Island students remain an issue.
*   **Netherlands:** Utilizes a stratified secondary structure (VMBO, HAVO, VWO) and a three-cycle higher education framework that reflects socioeconomic differentiation.
*   **Japan & Mexico:** Both mandate basic education; Japan features specialized advanced academic tracks, while Mexico's SEP strives for national unity despite poor performance and high dropout rates.
