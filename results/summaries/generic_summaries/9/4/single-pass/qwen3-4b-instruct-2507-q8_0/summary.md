### **Education in Japan**

**History:**  
Formal education began in the 6th century with Chinese cultural influences, emphasizing Confucianism, Buddhist teachings, and literature. The Kamakura period saw the rise of the samurai class, reducing court influence. During the Edo period, the Yushima Seidō became the central educational institution. The Meiji Restoration (1868) introduced Western-style education, establishing the University of Tokyo and promoting merit-based selection. Post-WWII reforms under the Allied occupation emphasized decentralization, pacifism, and coeducation, leading to a nine-year compulsory education system. The "Yutori education" policy (2000s) aimed to reduce academic pressure but was later abolished due to declining academic performance, with improvements noted in TIMSS scores.

**School Levels:**  
The academic year begins in April, with two to three terms and a six-week summer break. The structure includes:  
- **Primary (1–6):** Compulsory education, covering core subjects.  
- **Junior High (7–9):** Lower secondary education.  
- **Senior High (10–12):** Upper secondary education, leading to university admission.  
- **University:** Four-year bachelor’s degrees, with additional master’s and doctoral programs.  

**Subjects:**  
Core subjects include Japanese, mathematics, science, foreign languages (English, Korean, etc.), social studies, physical education, and moral education. Textbooks are certified by the Ministry of Education (MEXT).

**Academic Grading:**  
Grading uses a 5-point scale (Shū to Fuka), with Shū (excellent) and Yū (very good) being the highest. A numerical system (5 to 1) is used in secondary schools.  

**Extracurricular Activities (Shadow Education):**  
Highly emphasized due to exam pressure. Includes *juku* (private cram schools), *mogi shiken* (practice exams), and *ronin* (students preparing for university exams). Over 86% of students with college plans participate.  

**Government Intervention & Reforms:**  
The Basic Act on Education (2007) and the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) promote equal access for students with disabilities. The "Resource Room System" and "inclusive education" policies aim to integrate students with special needs into mainstream classrooms.  

**Criticisms:**  
Intense academic pressure leads to stress, school violence, and suicide. Students face significant psychological harm. The system is criticized for fostering rote memorization and lacking cultural and artistic sensibility.  

**International Education:**  
International schools (classified as *kakushu gakko*) offer English-medium curricula and are not considered part of compulsory education.

### **Education in Mexico**

**History:**  
Pre-Columbian education was centered on Mesoamerican societies with schools (*calmecac* and *cuicacalli*) for warriors and priests. Spanish colonization introduced Catholic missionary schools. Post-independence, liberal reforms led to secular public education. The 1920s saw a push for modern education, and the 1960s–1970s brought expansion due to economic growth.  

**Basic Education:**  
Divided into primary (primaria, grades 1–6), junior high (secundaria, grades 7–9), and high school (preparatoria, grades 10–12). Compulsory until age 16.  

**Curriculum & Qualifications:**  
The National Curriculum (NZC) and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) cover eight learning areas. Students work toward the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA), with Cambridge or IB as alternatives.  

**School Types:**  
- **State Schools:** Government-funded, free, secular.  
- **State-Integrated Schools:** Government-funded but with religious or cultural specializations (e.g., Catholic). Charge attendance dues.  
- **Private Schools:** Tuition-based, often offering international curricula.  

**Special Education:**  
Students with disabilities are supported through inclusive education, with special needs programs and support services.  

**Higher Education:**  
Follows the Bologna Process. Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. The National Autonomous University of Mexico ranks among the top in the country.  

**Funding & Challenges:**  
State schools receive per-student funding, with additional support for socio-economic needs. Private schools rely on tuition. Challenges include rural infrastructure gaps and low secondary school completion rates (45%).  

**Criticisms:**  
Low secondary school completion (45%), poor infrastructure in rural areas, and a lack of international competitiveness in education.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

**General Overview:**  
Compulsory education begins at age 5 and ends at 18. The system is decentralized, with public, special (religious), and general-special (neutral) schools receiving equal government funding.  

**Elementary Education (Basisschool):**  
Grades 1–8. Students take the Cito test at age 12 to determine secondary school placement.  

**Secondary Education:**  
- **VMBO (Vocational):** 4 years, combining vocational and theoretical training.  
- **HAVO (General):** 5 years, preparing for HBO (university of applied sciences).  
- **VWO (Pre-university):** 6 years, preparing for WO (research universities).  

**Curriculum & Profiles:**  
Students choose from four academic profiles (C&M, E&M, N&G, N&T) in upper secondary.  

**Higher Education:**  
- **HBO (University of Applied Sciences):** 4-year degrees, focused on professional training.  
- **WO (Research Universities):** 3-year bachelor’s degrees, leading to master’s and PhD.  

**Vocational Education:**  
MBO (middle-level applied education) offers 1–4 year programs in technical and vocational fields.  

**International Education:**  
International schools (classified as *kakushu gakko*) offer English-medium curricula and are not part of compulsory education.  

**Criticisms:**  
The Cito test may misplace students. Early separation into levels leads to academic disadvantages. The system is criticized for promoting rote learning and limited student autonomy.

### **Education in New Zealand**

**History:**  
Pre-European, Māori schools (*wānanga*) taught traditional knowledge. Missionary schools introduced Christianity and literacy. The 1878 national system was secular and free, modeled on the Canterbury system.  

**Early Childhood Education:**  
Includes kindergartens, *kohanga reo*, and *playcentre*. All services must follow the *Te Whāriki* curriculum.  

**Primary & Secondary Education:**  
- **Primary (Years 1–8):** 8 years, with Years 7–8 known as intermediate.  
- **Secondary (Years 9–13):** 5 years, with students choosing between academic or vocational paths.  

**Curriculum & Qualifications:**  
The National Curriculum (NZC) and *Te Marautanga o Aotearoa* (TMoA) are followed. Students work toward the NCEA, with Cambridge or IB as alternatives.  

**School Types:**  
- **State Schools:** Government-funded, free.  
- **State-Integrated Schools:** Government-funded with religious or cultural specializations.  
- **Private Schools:** Tuition-based.  

**Māori Language Education:**  
Māori language is taught in immersion schools (*kura kaupapa*) and as a subject in English-medium schools.  

**Higher Education:**  
Follows the Bologna Process. Offers bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.  

**Criticisms:**  
Low performance in international assessments (TIMSS). Bullying is widespread. The system is criticized for excessive focus on exams and lack of student creativity.

### **Education in South Korea**

**History:**  
Confucian values shaped early education. The Joseon dynasty emphasized literacy and exams. During Japanese occupation (1910–1945), education was used for assimilation. Post-liberation, the U.S. influenced a democratic, merit-based system.  

**School Grades:**  
- **Primary (1–6):** Ages 6–12.  
- **Middle (7–9):** Ages 12–15.  
- **High School (10–12):** Ages 15–18.  

**Primary Education:**  
Subjects include Korean, mathematics, science, social studies, and physical education.  

**Secondary Education:**  
- **Special High Schools:** For gifted students (e.g., science, foreign language).  
- **Vocational Schools:** Offer technical training.  

**Higher Education:**  
- **University:** Four-year bachelor’s degrees.  
- **Master’s & PhD:** Two- to four-year programs.  

**English Education:**  
English is taught from Grade 1. Parents invest heavily in private English schools.  

**Criticisms:**  
- **Academic Pressure:** Excessive competition, long study hours, and high suicide rates.  
- **Hagwons:** Private cram schools are widespread and expensive.  
- **Mismatch:** Only 36.8% of graduates have jobs matching their majors.  
- **Health Issues:** Students sleep less than the OECD average.  

**Reforms:**  
The government is shifting toward competency-based education and expanding vocational training through *Meister Schools*.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

**Background:**  
Education is rooted in Islamic values. The first Department of Education was established in 1926. The system emphasizes religious studies, with 9 periods per week at the primary level.  

**Pre-primary & Primary:**  
- Pre-primary (ages 3–5).  
- Primary (6–12 years), with English introduced from Grade 1.  

**Secondary Education:**  
- 3 years, with options for general or technical training.  
- Religious studies are mandatory, with 5 subjects (Tawhid, Fiqh, Tafsir, Hadith, Quran).  

**Tertiary Education:**  
- 4 years (humanities/social sciences), 5–6 years (medicine, engineering).  
- 24 government universities and 101 private institutions.  

**Girls’ Education:**  
Women now constitute 60% of university students. The first female university graduates received legal licenses in 2013.  

**Criticisms:**  
- Overemphasis on rote learning and religious instruction.  
- Lack of technical and entrepreneurial skills.  
- Anti-Semitic and anti-Christian content in textbooks.  
- Government has introduced reforms to remove intolerant language.  

**International Education:**  
International schools (classified as *kakushu gakko*) offer English-medium curricula.

### **Education in Russia**

**Levels of Education:**  
- **General:** Preschool, primary (4 years), basic (5 years), secondary (2 years).  
- **Professional:** Vocational, bachelor’s, master’s, and postgraduate degrees.  

**Primary & Secondary:**  
- 11-year compulsory education (grades 1–11).  
- Unified State Exams (USE) introduced in 2003 to standardize university admissions.  

**Higher Education:**  
- Traditionally a five-year specialist program, now transitioning to a Bologna-style bachelor’s (4 years) and master’s (2 years).  

**Special Education:**  
- For physical and mental disabilities, with specialized institutions and inclusive classrooms.  

**Criticisms:**  
- Outdated curriculum and poor teacher training.  
- Excessive focus on exams and rote learning.  
- Government propaganda promoting "patriotic education" in schools.  

**Religious Education:**  
- Islamic studies are mandatory, with content promoting religious orthodoxy.

### **Education in Bangladesh**

**Structure:**  
Divided into pre-primary, primary (3–8), secondary (9–12), and tertiary levels.  

**National Curriculum:**  
- Students choose between Arts, Commerce, or Science streams in secondary school.  
- Standardized exams (JSC, SSC, HSC) are based on multiple-choice and practical components.  

**Schools:**  
- Government, private, international, and madrasas (Islamic schools).  
- Madrasas account for 19% of secondary enrollment.  

**Gender Disparity:**  
- Women face significant barriers in education, with lower literacy rates (42% vs. 67% for men).  

**Criticisms:**  
- Poor infrastructure, teacher absenteeism, and outdated curriculum.  
- Low enrollment in higher education.  

**Literacy Rate:**  
- 74.66% (2022), up from 51.77% (2011).

### **Education in Pakistan**

**Structure:**  
- Primary (1–5), middle (6–8), secondary (9–12), and tertiary levels.  

**Secondary Education:**  
- Students take national exams (SSC and HSSC) to gain entry into colleges.  
- Offers streams in pre-medical, pre-engineering, commerce, and humanities.  

**Tertiary Education:**  
- Bachelor’s degrees (4–5 years), with pass or honors options.  
- Master’s and PhD programs in various fields.  

**Gender Disparity:**  
- Women face significant barriers in education and employment.  
- Only 18% of women have 10+ years of schooling.  

**Criticisms:**  
- Poor quality of education, lack of teacher training, and outdated curriculum.  
- Religious content in textbooks promotes intolerance.  

**Literacy Rate:**  
- 60.65% (2023), with regional disparities.

### **Education in Canada**

**Compulsory Education:**  
- Begins at age 5–7, ending at age 18.  
- Varies by province (e.g., Quebec ends at Grade 11).  

**School Levels:**  
- K–12 (Kindergarten to Grade 12).  
- Secondary education ends at Grade 12 in most provinces.  

**Post-Secondary:**  
- Universities (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD) and vocational institutions (diplomas, certificates).  

**Religious & Language-Based Schools:**  
- Separate schools for Catholic, Islamic, and other religious groups.  
- French immersion and English immersion programs.  

**Criticisms:**  
- Academic standards are criticized for being too rigid.  
- Some provinces have high grade inflation and lack of corrective feedback.

### **Education in Poland**

**Compulsory Education:**  
- Primary school (grades 1–8) replaced the previous 6-year primary + 3-year gimnazjum system in 2017.  
- Upper secondary education leads to the *matura* (university entrance exam).  

**Secondary Education:**  
- General (liceum), vocational (technikum), and specialized schools.  

**Tertiary Education:**  
- Follows the Bologna system (3-year bachelor’s, 2-year master’s).  
- Grading uses a 2–5 scale, with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade.  

**Criticisms:**  
- Curriculum focuses on nationalism and elite leaders, neglecting social classes.  
- School violence and lack of student autonomy are concerns.

### **Education in the Czech Republic**

**Elementary School:**  
- 9 grades, divided into two stages.  
- Subjects include Czech, foreign languages, math, science, and arts.  

**Secondary School:**  
- Students must pass the *maturita* exam for university admission.  
- Vocational, professional, and grammar schools offer different pathways.  

**Tertiary Education:**  
- Charles University (founded 1348) and Palacký University are among the oldest.  
- Bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and PhD programs.  

**Grading System:**  
- 2.0 to 5.0 scale, with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade.  

**Calendar & Holidays:**  
- School year starts in September, ends in June.  
- Holidays include winter, summer, and spring breaks.

**Summary:**  
Each country has a unique educational system shaped by historical, cultural, and political factors. While many emphasize academic excellence, there is growing recognition of the need for inclusive, student-centered, and holistic education that balances academic achievement with well-being, equity, and global competitiveness.
