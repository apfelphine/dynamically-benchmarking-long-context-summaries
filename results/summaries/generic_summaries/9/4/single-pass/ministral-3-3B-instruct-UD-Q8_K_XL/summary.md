### **Education in Japan**

#### **Historical Overview**
Japan’s formal education began in the 6th century AD, influenced by Chinese Confucian and Buddhist teachings. Early education focused on courtly culture, but samurai and merchant classes also developed their own educational systems. The Edo period saw the rise of samurai schools (*hankō*) and terakoya, which taught basic literacy and arithmetic. Western learning was introduced during the Meiji Restoration (1868), leading to compulsory education modeled after Prussia. The University of Tokyo was established in 1877, and higher education expanded with reforms emphasizing Western-style learning.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary Education (6–12 years):** Compulsory, divided into elementary (grades 1–6) and junior high (grades 7–9). English became compulsory in elementary schools in 2011.
- **Secondary Education (12–18 years):** Includes senior high (grades 10–12) and vocational training. Students often attend *juku* (cram schools) to prepare for university entrance exams.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., University of Tokyo, Kyoto University) and vocational schools. The system is highly competitive, with selective admissions and intense academic pressure.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Nine years (elementary + junior high).
- **Exams:** Rigorous entrance exams for university and vocational schools.
- **Extracurricular Activities:** Heavy emphasis on *juku* and shadow education for exam preparation.
- **Criticisms:** High stress levels, bullying, and concerns over rote learning and lack of creativity.

### **Education in Mexico**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in Mexico traces back to pre-Hispanic times, with schools like *calmecac* (warrior training) and *cuicacalli* (priest training). Spanish colonial rule introduced Catholic schools, but secular education became a priority after independence. The 19th century saw reforms under Juárez, establishing public, secular education. The 20th century saw expansion, including compulsory primary education and the establishment of universities like the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM).

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (6–12 years):** Grades 1–6 (compulsory), followed by junior high (grades 7–9) and high school (grades 10–12).
- **Secondary Education:** Includes *preparatoria* (grades 10–12), vocational training (*técnica*), and distance learning (*telesecundaria*).
- **Tertiary Education:** Public and private universities (e.g., UNAM, ITESM). The system follows the Bologna Process, with bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary and preparatoria (grades 1–12).
- **Vocational Paths:** *Técnica* and *telesecundaria* offer practical training.
- **Challenges:** Low completion rates in secondary education, rural infrastructure gaps, and gender disparities.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Historical Overview**
The Netherlands introduced compulsory education in 1800, with reforms under the Batavian Republic. The 19th century saw the establishment of primary schools, gymnasia (Latin schools), and universities. The 20th century modernized the system, introducing vocational education and standardized testing (e.g., *Cito* exams). The Netherlands adopted the Bologna Process in 2002, aligning tertiary education with European standards.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (4–12 years):** Grades 1–8 (eight-year primary school). English is introduced in grades 7–8.
- **Secondary Education:** Divided into vocational (VMBO), academic (HAVO), and scientific (VWO) tracks. Students take the *Cito* exam in grade 8 to determine their track.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities of applied sciences (HBO) and research universities (WO). The system uses a three-cycle model (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (8 years) and secondary (up to 18 years).
- **Vocational Focus:** Strong emphasis on practical skills (e.g., *VMBO*).
- **Criticisms:** High stress from early academic tracking and declining international rankings.

### **Education in New Zealand**

#### **Historical Overview**
Māori schools (*wānanga*) taught traditional knowledge before European colonization. Formal education was introduced by missionaries in the 19th century. The 20th century saw the establishment of a free, secular national education system (1878), modeled after the Canterbury system. The 21st century has emphasized Māori language education (*kura kaupapa*) and inclusive practices.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (5–13 years):** Grades 1–8 (elementary) and intermediate (grades 7–8).
- **Secondary (13–18 years):** Grades 9–13 (high school). Students can choose between Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or the National Certificate of Educational Achievement (NCEA).
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities, polytechnics, and correspondence schools. The system is regulated by the New Zealand Qualifications Framework.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (8 years) and secondary (up to 16 years).
- **Māori Language:** Strong emphasis on bilingual education (*kura kaupapa*).
- **Criticisms:** Bullying, low academic standards in some areas, and disparities in rural education.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in South Korea has been shaped by Confucian values, with schools like *seodang* (village schools) and *seowon* (private academies) teaching literacy and governance. The Meiji Restoration introduced Western-style education, and the 20th century saw rapid modernization, including compulsory primary education and the establishment of universities. The 1980s–90s saw reforms to increase higher education opportunities, but the system remains highly competitive.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (6–13 years):** Grades 1–6 (elementary) and middle school (grades 7–9).
- **Secondary Education:** High school (grades 10–12) with tracks for academic and vocational training. Students take the *College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT)* for university admission.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., Seoul National University, Yonsei University) and vocational schools. The system emphasizes meritocracy and high academic standards.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (6 years) and middle school (3 years).
- **Extensive Private Tutoring:** *Hagwons* (cram schools) are common, leading to high stress levels.
- **Criticisms:** Overemphasis on exams, gender disparities, and mental health issues among students.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in Saudi Arabia traces back to Islamic schools (*kuttabs*) and mosques. The 20th century saw the establishment of public schools and universities (e.g., King Saud University, 1957). The system emphasizes Islamic studies and religious content, with reforms in the 21st century introducing secular and vocational education.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Pre-Primary (3–5 years):** Kindergarten.
- **Primary (6–12 years):** Grades 1–6. English is introduced in grade 1.
- **Intermediate (12–15 years):** Grades 7–9.
- **Secondary (15–18 years):** Grades 10–12. Students can choose between general and technical education.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., King Saud University) and vocational training. The system follows Islamic values and modern curriculum reforms.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (6 years) and intermediate (3 years).
- **Religious Emphasis:** Islamic studies are central to the curriculum.
- **Criticisms:** Limited access to higher education, gender disparities, and criticism of extremist content in textbooks.

### **Education in Russia**

#### **Historical Overview**
The Soviet Union established a universal education system in the early 20th century, with free, compulsory primary and secondary education. The 1990s saw privatization and reforms, leading to a decline in public schools. The 21st century has focused on modernizing the system, including the adoption of the Bologna Process for tertiary education.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (6–11 years):** Grades 1–4 (elementary), 5–9 (middle), and 10–11 (senior).
- **Secondary Education:** Compulsory for 9 years (grades 1–9). Vocational training options available.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities and colleges. The system follows the Bologna Process (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** 9 years (grades 1–9).
- **Vocational Paths:** Technical schools and colleges offer practical training.
- **Criticisms:** Declining quality, overcrowded schools, and outdated curriculum.

### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in Bangladesh has evolved from British colonial schools to a national curriculum system. The 20th century saw the establishment of universities (e.g., Dhaka University, 1921) and reforms to improve access and quality. The 21st century has emphasized free primary education and gender parity.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (6–12 years):** Grades 1–8 (primary) and 9–12 (secondary).
- **Secondary Education:** Grades 9–12. Students can choose between arts, commerce, and science streams.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., University of Dhaka, Jahangirnagar University) and vocational schools. The system follows the National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB).

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (8 years).
- **Vocational Paths:** Technical and vocational education boards offer practical training.
- **Criticisms:** Low literacy rates, gender disparities, and rural education gaps.

### **Education in Pakistan**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in Pakistan has roots in British colonial schools and Islamic seminaries (*madrassas*). The 20th century saw the establishment of universities (e.g., University of Punjab, 1947) and reforms to improve access. The system remains highly influenced by religious and cultural values.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (6–12 years):** Grades 1–5 (junior school), 6–8 (middle school), and 9–12 (secondary).
- **Secondary Education:** Grades 9–12. Students take the SSC and HSSC exams for admission to higher education.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., University of Karachi, Peshawar University) and vocational schools. The system follows the National Education Policy.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (8 years).
- **Religious Influence:** Madrasas and Islamic studies are central to the curriculum.
- **Criticisms:** Low quality, gender disparities, and lack of vocational training.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Historical Overview**
Education in Canada was influenced by British colonial schools and French Catholic schools. The 20th century saw the establishment of provincial education systems and reforms to improve access. The 21st century has emphasized bilingual education and inclusive practices.

#### **Structure of Education**
- **Primary (5–13 years):** Kindergarten (K) to Grade 12.
- **Secondary Education:** Grades 9–12. Students can choose between academic and vocational tracks.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (e.g., University of Toronto, McGill University) and vocational colleges. The system follows the Bologna Process.

#### **Key Features**
- **Compulsory Education:** Primary (8 years) and secondary (up to 18 years).
- **Bilingual Education:** English and French are official languages.
- **Criticisms:** High costs, declining standards in some areas, and rural education gaps.
