### **Education Systems: Global Overview (Final)**

**North America & Latin America**
*   **Canada:** Operates under provincial jurisdiction with a K–12 model (mandatory ages 5–16/18). Governance involves local boards and independent charter schools, particularly in Alberta. Higher education relies on GPA and extracurriculars without a national accreditation body. Challenges include fee hikes, grade inflation, and ongoing efforts to support Indigenous studies and minority language rights.
*   **Mexico:**
    *   **Historical Evolution:** The system evolved from Pre-Hispanic elite schools to a Colonial model dominated by Catholic friars, which excluded women and mixed-race children. Under President Benito Juárez, education became free, secular, and compulsory. The Porfiriato modernized the workforce, while the Mexican Revolution centralized control under the SEP to promote nationalism. Post-1940s growth raised standards, though early illiteracy rates remained critical.
    *   **Reforms & Structure:** A pivotal 2013 reform shifted control from the Teachers Union (SNTE) to central and state governments. The system spans nursery, primary, secondary, and *preparatoria*. Higher education follows the US and Bologna models (4-year bachelor's, 2-year Master's, 3-year Doctorate).
    *   **Current Status & Achievements:** School attendance rose from 3 million (1950) to over 32 million (2011), with youth literacy reaching 97% by 2004. Intercultural Universities and international schools address indigenous representation.
    *   **Recent Developments:** In February 2019, the SEP launched a free textbook program featuring Mexican art. Academic rankings highlight the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) at 103rd globally, while the private Monterrey Institute of Technology (ITESM) leads in private rankings. Private business schools IPADE and EGADE also secured top-10 status in international recruiter surveys.
    *   **Outcomes & Challenges:** Despite improvements, secondary completion (45%) and university attendance (25%) remain low due to infrastructure deficits, rural underfunding, and systemic special education issues.

**Asia**
*   **South Korea:** Rooted in Confucian meritocracy, the system is characterized by intense parental investment, excessive study hours, and a disconnect between university majors and job markets. A stigma surrounds vocational schools compared to prestigious "SKY" universities.
*   **Japan:** Evolved from Buddhist influences to a modernized Prussian model, followed by post-WWII democratic reforms and "Yutori" stress-reduction policies. Compulsory education covers elementary and junior high with negligible dropout rates. The system relies heavily on "shadow education" (*juku*), which leads to psychological harm and safety concerns, and is criticized for stifling independent thinking.
*   **Bangladesh:** Administered through a multi-ministry framework, the system features a 5.0 GPA and active gender equality promotion. While literacy has risen significantly (from 51.77% to 74.66%), challenges persist due to low public spending (~2% of GDP), teacher absenteeism, overcrowding, and a rote-learning curriculum.
*   **Saudi Arabia:** Established in 1926, the system integrates Islamic values with modern science and is free. Adult literacy reached 85%. Reforms aim to reduce rote memorization, introduce vocational training, and use English as a lingua franca.
*   **New Zealand:** A secular state system with compulsory education for ages 5–19. Significant achievement gaps exist between Pākehā, Māori, and Pacific Island students, though Māori-medium immersion schools show revitalization and higher completion rates. Challenges include declining math standards and bullying.

**Europe & Middle East**
*   **Netherlands:** Features early tracking at age 8 via the *Cito* test into vocational (VMBO) or academic tracks (HAVO/VWO). Higher education is split into scientific (WO) and professional (HBO) cycles under the Bologna process. Challenges include early tracking misclassifications and socioeconomic disparities.

**Global Trends**
Consistent challenges across diverse systems include resource allocation, teacher training, curriculum relevance (shifting from rote to critical thinking), and the pressures of standardized testing or shadow education.
