# Global Education Systems: Key Features and Challenges

## South Korea, Japan, The Netherlands, Russia, Poland, Mexico, Pakistan
Countries like South Korea and Japan exemplify competitive, exam-driven education frameworks rooted in traditional values, often leading to student stress and social issues. Both nations have implemented reforms emphasizing inclusive, competency-based learning while battling intense private tutoring cultures. The Netherlands and Poland offer flexible, tracked systems that support academic and vocational options, although performance disparities and recent declines exist. Russia largely maintains state-controlled, integrity-challenged education, bolstering patriotic curricula since recent conflicts. Mexico and Pakistan face chronic underfunding, access problems, and persistent inequalities, though targeted reforms and open/university programs are improving reach. Despite distinct approaches, these nations all confront challenges in equity, quality, and adaptation to changing societal needs.

## New Zealand
New Zealand’s education builds on inclusive, government-funded models with strong Māori traditions. Achievement gaps for Māori and Pacific Island students persist, alongside declining student wellbeing noted in recent years. National attention increased following high-profile bullying cases, prompting the Positive Behaviour for Learning initiative and wider calls for anti-bullying policy reform. While progress is ongoing, effective implementation of welfare measures and consistent guidance from authorities remain priorities.

## Bangladesh, Czech Republic, Saudi Arabia, Canada
Bangladesh has improved literacy and gender parity through free primary education and ongoing support for girls, but struggles with inadequate resources and urban-rural disparities impede broader progress. The Czech Republic upholds universal nine-year elementary schooling with rigorous paths into academic and vocational secondary programs, and free public higher education for newcomers. Saudi Arabia’s system has modernized rapidly—advancing women’s university participation and special needs programs—yet still faces inclusion and teacher training challenges. Canada operates provincially diverse, bilingual education with robust protections for minority and religious groups, high rates of tertiary attainment, and ongoing initiatives promoting equity and smooth transitions to advanced learning.
