**International Education System Comparisons**

Global education systems show diverse structures and face varied challenges, such as cultural biases (Poland), intense competitive stress (Japan), structural inequities (Netherlands), data/identity issues (Canada), and resource/HR deficits (Pakistan, Bangladesh).

**National System Profiles:**

*   **Bangladesh:** Operates a comprehensive system from Pre-Primary to Tertiary, with distinct general and religious tracks. The NCTB manages the curriculum, with MOPME/DPE overseeing primary and nine BISEs managing secondary. Tertiary education is regulated by UGC, featuring both public and private institutions, while DTE handles Technical/Vocational training. The system includes subsidized Madrasah and NGO non-formal education, with refugee education supported by international aid. Although the national literacy rate has risen significantly (from 51.77% in 2011 to 74.66% in 2022), it struggles with low public expenditure (around 2% of GDP), poor Human Resource Development, low teacher morale, enrollment constraints due to limited seats and rising costs, and high dropout/repetition rates in rural areas. The curriculum integrates religious education, though specialized teaching can be lacking. Goals aim for free education, but the system relies heavily on rote learning and lacks outcome-based university education.
*   **Canada:** Has a provincially managed structure spanning Early Childhood to Secondary, with publicly funded tertiary options including research Universities and Colleges/Vocational Institutions.
*   **Saudi Arabia:** Is an Islamic-rooted system with 24 government universities that prioritize critical thinking and English while maintaining religious influence and rote learning; female participation in higher education is 60%.
*   **Czech Republic:** Uses a 9-grade elementary level leading to diverse secondary paths culminating in the *maturita*, and public university initial instruction is free.
*   **New Zealand:** Maintains a free, compulsory, secular 13-year national system governed by the NZ Qualifications Framework, emphasizing equity for Māori and Pacific Island students.
*   **Mexico:** Features a structure rooted in indigenous culture, maintaining high literacy across its Primary (1-6) and High School (10-12) levels.
*   **Poland:** Reformed post-1999 to meet Bologna requirements (8-year primary, 4/5-year high school), but it faces criticism regarding Eurocentric bias.
*   **Other Noteworthy Systems:**
    *   **Korean Education:** Defined by a centralized, Confucian-influenced meritocracy.
    *   **Dutch Education:** Uses tiered secondary tracks (VMBO, HAVO, VWO) until age 18, leading to discussions about early tracking disparities.
    *   **Russian Education:** Standardizes higher education via the USE since 2009, including fee-charging Master's programs with patriotic themes.
    *   **Japanese Education:** Characterized by standardization after grade nine by MEXT, leading to high student stress driven by *juku* culture.
    *   **Pakistani Education:** Despite having strong universities, it is hampered by severe underfunding (less than 2% of the budget), poverty, and gender disparities.
