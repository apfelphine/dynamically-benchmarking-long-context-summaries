## Global Education Systems: Comparative Overview

Educational reform and access expansion are prevailing trends across diverse countries. Nations like Saudi Arabia and Bangladesh are shifting from traditional, religious-based systems to state-regulated models, prioritizing curriculum modernization, technology integration, and gender equity, despite enduring resource gaps and cultural barriers. Pakistan also faces challenges, including persistent gender and social disparities, low completion rates, teacher absenteeism, and biases in curricula—especially against minorities and women—though NGOs play a significant role in informal education and ongoing literacy improvements. Canada’s provincially controlled system provides high attainment and diverse offerings but wrestles with achievement gaps, while New Zealand and the Netherlands emphasize inclusivity and flexible learning but contend with inequities and declining outcomes.

In Mexico, reforms have steered the system away from religious and centralized control, improving standards but struggling with dropout rates and rural disparities. The Czech Republic and Poland maintain public-supported foundational and vocational education, focused on inclusivity and curriculum evolution. Japan balances high achievement and well-being with competitive sorting mechanisms and ongoing mental health challenges, underscoring broader concerns about regional equity and access.

## Regional Specificities and Reforms

South Korea’s system is marked by intense competition and Confucian values, relying heavily on high-stakes exams and private tutoring (hagwons), but faces issues such as skill mismatches, student stress, and underappreciation of educators. Russian education follows a comprehensive track system with standardized university entry and a strong tradition of tertiary attainment, further bolstered by reforms aligning with global standards and efforts toward inclusion of disabled students and ethnic minorities. Although “patriotic education” and recent legislative changes—such as mandatory Russian language exams for foreign students—aim to promote cohesion, concerns about ideological control and limited institutional support for smaller minority languages remain.

Across all countries, curriculum reform, teacher training modernization, and structural inclusivity remain central policy goals. However, most systems continue to grapple with entrenched disparities related to ethnicity, gender, region, and institutional quality, impeding efforts to achieve universal education and equitable outcomes.
