### **Education Systems Across Selected Countries (Updated)**

#### **Saudi Arabia**
Saudi Arabia’s education system integrates **Islamic *Kuttabs*** with modern reforms, supported by **27.6% of government spending in 2004** and the **2020–2030 Vision Plan**, prioritizing **pre-primary education, STEM, and bilingual instruction**. Public universities dominate, though **70% of graduates focus on humanities**, with women (60% of enrollments) facing barriers in technical fields. Illiteracy stands at **13.7%**, and women’s labor force participation is low (**14.4%**), often limited to education/public health roles. Special education and gifted programs struggle with **resource shortages and negative school culture**, while private/international schools thrive under strict regulations. **Indigenous language instruction** remains minimal, with no dedicated programs for languages like Dolgan, Itelmen, or Eskimo.

#### **Bangladesh**
Bangladesh’s **three-tiered system** (Pre-primary to Tertiary) mandates **12 years of compulsory education**, with **Bengali/English** as primary languages. Gender equity is prioritized (60% of primary teaching roles for women), but **standardized exams (JSC/SSC/HSC)** rely on **MCQs**, limiting depth. Specialized boards (Qawmi, Alia) and international schools coexist with **Madrasahs**, dominating Islamic education. **Rohingya refugee education** is limited (~33% enrollment in 2020), supported by **UNICEF’s 1,600 learning centers** for **145,000 students**, while NGOs like **BRAC** address dropouts via non-formal education. Despite rising literacy (**74.66% in 2022**), **religious education mandates** and **high drop-out/repetition rates** persist. Indigenous languages like **Yukaghir** have no formal school instruction.

#### **Japan**
Japan’s system emphasizes **inclusion since 2006**, with **tuition-free public schools** (since 2010) and **vocational training for disabled students**. Admissions depend on **rigorous exams**, and **Juku (cram schools)** are critical for university prep (**86% of students** use them). **English education** is compulsory since 2011, though **6th-place ranking in Asia (2020)** reflects heavy reliance on private tutoring. Vocational education remains stigmatized, with **overemphasis on university admissions** driving extreme competition. Indigenous language instruction is **absent except for limited regional teaching** (e.g., Ainu languages in Hokkaido).

---

#### **Pakistan**
Pakistan’s system remains **highly unequal**, with **60.65% literacy (2023)**, the second-lowest in South Asia. **High-performing districts** exceed **80% literacy**, while **Balochistan and FATA** lag. **Religious curricula** often promote extremist narratives, and **minority literacy rates** (e.g., **Christians: 34%**) are critically low. **COVID-19 disrupted access**, with **teleschool programs** reaching K-12 students but leaving many behind. **Indigenous languages** like **Balochi** have minimal formal instruction, with no dedicated schools for languages such as **Balti or Shina**.

---

#### **South Korea**
South Korea’s system, rooted in **Confucian meritocracy**, prioritizes **literacy growth (93% by the late 1980s)** and economic success through **rigid curricula and standardized testing**. **English education** emphasizes **oral proficiency**, though **6th-place ranking in Asia (2020)** reflects heavy investment in private tutoring. Vocational education remains stigmatized, with **overemphasis on university admissions** driving extreme competition. **Indigenous language instruction** is **sparse**, with **Tatar, Bashkir, and Mari** having the most schools (~30–1,000+), while languages like **Yukaghir** have **no formal instruction**.

---

#### **Russia**
Russia’s education system spans **ISCED levels**, with **9 years of compulsory education** (extended to 11 in some regions). **Preschool enrollment dropped to 7.44M (2020)**, though mandatory for protected groups. **Secondary education** offers vocational training, with **3-year programs** or specialized high schools. **Unified State Examinations (USE)** standardize admissions since 2003, though transparency is criticized. **Tertiary education** consolidates universities, adopting a **4-year bachelor’s → 2-year master’s** structure under the **Bologna Process reforms**. **Patriotic education** since 2020 includes mandatory lessons on **nationalism and history**, sparking backlash. **Indigenous language education** is extensive for **Tatar, Bashkir, and Chuvash** (1,000–2,000+ schools), but **Dolgan, Itelmen, Nganasan, Eskimo, and Yukaghir** have **no formal instruction**, with minimal coverage for **Vepsian**. **Disability education** remains segregated, with specialized training for physical disabilities and exclusion for mental disabilities.

#### **New Policy in Russia**
In **December 2024**, Russia’s **State Duma approved a law restricting foreign students** from enrolling in schools unless they pass a **Russian language proficiency exam**, further tightening access to higher education.
