# Summary of Global Education Systems

## New Zealand Education
New Zealand's educational history began with traditional Māori knowledge transfer via *wānanga*. Formal European schooling started in 1815, evolving to a free, compulsory, and secular national system in 1878. Early childhood education is offered through centre-based (like Kindergarten and Licensed Centres) and family-led services (like Kohanga Reo). Primary and secondary schooling covers 13 year levels (Years 1–13), with primary lasting eight years (Years 1–8). Curricula are either the national New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) for English-medium schools or Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (TMoA) for Māori-medium schools. The system features three school types: state schools, state integrated schools, and private schools, with funding based on student numbers and socio-economic decile. Māori language education has seen a revival since the 1980s, resulting in Kura Kaupapa Māori.

## Saudi Arabia Education
Education in Saudi Arabia, rooted in Islam, is free at all levels and primarily overseen by the Ministry of Education. Pre-primary education for 3–5 year olds is not mandatory for primary enrollment. Primary education lasts six years (age 6–12) and includes Islamic studies; English was mandated from the first grade in 2021. Intermediate and secondary education each last three years, with specialized paths available. Higher education includes 24 government universities, and a major government scholarship program sends nationals abroad. While religious content is central, recent reforms aim to improve instruction in sciences and arts, though the system faces criticism regarding rote learning and intolerance.

## Mexico Education
Basic education is structured into three levels: primary school (*primaria*, grades 1–6), junior high school (*secundaria*, grades 7–9), and high school (*preparatoria*, grades 10–12). Educational integration mandates the inclusion of students with special needs in regular classrooms, supported by specialized units (USAER), though challenges include staff training and accessibility. Quality is a concern, with low progression rates from secondary to higher education. Historically, education has seen conflicts between the Catholic Church and liberal calls for secular state control.

## Bangladesh Education
The education system spans Pre-primary (Class K–2), Primary (Class 3–8), Secondary (Class 9–12), and Tertiary levels, with options for English or Bengali instruction at all levels. The National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB) manages the curriculum, and standardized examinations (JSC at Grade 8, SSC at Grade 10, HSC at Grade 12) determine progress. Students must complete 12 years of education. Education is delivered through government, private, and international schools, with Madrasah education playing a significant role, especially for poorer families. Current goals include ensuring compulsory primary education for all.

## Japan Education
Formal education began with Chinese cultural adoption in the 6th century, evolving through the Edo period and the Meiji Restoration, which adopted Western learning to modernize the nation. Post-WWII reforms modeled the system after the American style. The academic year starts in April and includes compulsory elementary (K–6) and junior high (7–9) education. Upper secondary is generally not compulsory but has high enrollment. Tertiary education includes universities and colleges of technology. The system is known for its intense competition, driven by entrance examinations, which necessitates extensive "shadow education" (*juku*).

## Canada Education
Education is primarily a provincial matter, leading to variation across provinces and territories. Kindergarten programs vary in length. Secondary school is generally Grade 10–12. Post-secondary education is offered through universities (research-focused) and vocational/community colleges (career-focused). The Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms protects minority language education. The system is characterized by strong provincial autonomy, while federal funding supports Indigenous education.

## The Netherlands Education
The Dutch system is coordinated by the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, with local government control. Compulsory education starts at age five, with partial compulsion continuing until 16. Education is stratified into three main routes after elementary school: VMBO (pre-vocational), HAVO (higher general education), and VWO (preparatory scientific education). These routes lead to tertiary education via MBO (applied education), HBO (applied sciences), or WO (research universities), following the Bologna process. Assessment relies on annual reviews and the non-mandatory Cito test to guide placement.

## Czech Republic Education
Elementary school consists of nine grades in two stages. Secondary education pathways include vocational, professional, and general/grammar schools, culminating in the *Maturita* exam, which is required for university entry. Tertiary education is provided by public and private universities, adhering to a Bologna scheme. The system is transitioning from older structures, with reformed curricula focusing on specific national and Eurocentric elements.

## South Korea Education
Korean education is heavily influenced by Confucian values, emphasizing meritocracy and competition. Primary school is compulsory (Grades 1–6). The system is characterized by an "education fever," driven by fierce competition for elite universities (SKY). Vocational education, while historically strong, suffers from social stigma, leading to the implementation of "Meister Schools" to boost skilled trades. Intense academic pressure leads to high rates of student stress and concerns over student health.

## Pakistan Education
Formal education covers pre-school through secondary school (Grades 1–12), with the curriculum inspired by the English system. Primary completion rates are low (around 67.5%). The system is heavily influenced by Islamic seminaries (*Madrassas*), which cater to poor families. Gender disparity is a major issue, with lower female enrollment and literacy rates, although some recent progress has been noted. Quality is challenged by underfunding, outdated curricula, and a reliance on rote learning.

## Poland Education
The Polish system underwent major reforms in 1999 and 2017. Compulsory education currently spans eight years of primary school. Upper secondary education offers general (*liceum*) or vocational (*technikum*). Tertiary education follows the Bologna scheme (3-year Bachelor's, 2-year Master's). The system has seen efforts to improve international rankings, while the 2017 reforms have been criticized for focusing heavily on Polish-centered nationalism.

## Russia Education
Russia has both general and professional education tracks. General education spans from preschool through secondary school. Tertiary education is evolving from a traditional five-year specialist model to the Bologna-aligned two-tier system (4-year Bachelor's, 2-year Master's). The system is undergoing centralization efforts to reduce the high number of institutions, and there is an increasing state push for "patriotic lessons." Outreach activities are also regulated.

## International Education Systems (Overview)
Across these diverse nations, international schooling is present in most countries, often catering to foreign workers. Countries like the US and UK curricula are adopted, and ISC tracks international schools. In many nations, the private sector plays a significant, though varied, role in providing educational options.
