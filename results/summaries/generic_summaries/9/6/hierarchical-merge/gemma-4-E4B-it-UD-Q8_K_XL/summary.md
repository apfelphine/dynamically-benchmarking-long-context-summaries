## Global Education System Comparisons

Global education systems exhibit vast diversity in structure, governance, and philosophy, ranging from centralized, compulsory models (like New Zealand's or South Korea's) to decentralized provincial controls (like Canada's). Approaches to funding and tracking vary significantly; for example, the Netherlands uses a tiered system aligned with the Bologna Process, while nations like Bangladesh struggle with low public spending and teacher absenteeism despite achieving basic literacy.

Countries are implementing tailored modernization strategies to address sector-specific issues. Saudi Arabia is promoting gifted creativity under its 2030 vision, while New Zealand contends with equity issues for Māori and Pacific Island students. Other systems incorporate specialized tracks, such as Mexico’s Intercultural Universities supporting indigenous enrollment, and Japan's efforts to manage academic pressure.

### Regional System Highlights

In Eastern Europe, Russia’s system maintains Soviet traditions while aligning toward the Bologna Process, promoting "patriotic education" across its large population. Poland’s structure, reformed since 2017, follows the Bologna scheme for tertiary education, though its curriculum has drawn criticism for nationalistic leanings.

Pakistan’s education sector faces major systemic challenges, including chronic underinvestment and patriarchal barriers, which result in low primary completion rates and gender disparity. Despite these hurdles, the nation reports a literacy rate of 60.65% for those aged 10 and over, while its formal, non-formal, and religious sectors continue to operate.
