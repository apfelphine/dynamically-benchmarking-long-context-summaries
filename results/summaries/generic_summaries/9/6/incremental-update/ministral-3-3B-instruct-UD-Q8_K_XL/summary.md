#### **Historical Context & Governance**
Japan’s system evolved from **Confucian foundations** into a **Prussian-inspired compulsory model**, establishing institutions like the **University of Tokyo (1877)**. Post-WWII, it adopted a **6-year elementary, 3-year secondary, 4-year university structure (1947)**, later introducing **Yutori education (1980s–2011)** to ease exam pressure. Governed by **MEXT**, it operates under **centralized prefectural oversight**.

Saudi Arabia’s system, governed by the **Ministry of Education** and the **Supreme Committee for Educational Policy**, emerged under **King Abdulaziz (1932)**, with **27.6% of government expenditure** allocated to education (2004–2006). Mexico’s system traces back to **colonial Franciscan schools** and **Juárez’s secular reforms (1867)**, becoming a **compulsory primary system** with Catholic influence persisting in universities. Bangladesh’s system developed a **12-year compulsory education system post-independence (1971)**, structured under the **Bangladesh Education Board (BEB)**, with **parallel Islamic Madrasah systems** offering **Arabic-centric curricula** up to **Kamil level**, followed by general education access.

Canada’s system operates under **provincial/territorial governance**, with **historical variations** (e.g., **Ontario’s Grade 13 until 2003**, **Newfoundland/Labrador’s Grade 11 until 1983**). Governed by **provincial boards**, Canada emphasizes **local governance** while ensuring **constitutional protections** for minority-language and religious schools. **Vocational pathways** now include **certificates, diplomas, associate degrees (2 years)**, and **4-year apprenticeships**, with **over 200 community colleges** serving rural/remote areas.

The **Czech Republic** follows a **centralized yet decentralized governance model**, with education managed by the **Ministry of Education, Youth, and Sports**, rooted in **Charles University (1348)** and **Palacký University (1576)**. **Poland** introduced an **8-year primary school (2017 reforms)**, replacing the previous 6-year structure, while **junior high schools were abolished**, shifting students into **general or vocational tracks**. Russia’s system, structured under the **Ministry of Education and Science**, follows a **nine-year compulsory education framework (since 2007)**, with **general schooling** divided into **elementary (1–4)**, **basic (5–9)**, and **secondary (10–11)** phases. **Vocational pathways** emerge after **9 years**, offering **2-year continuation** or **3-year vocational programs**, while **patriotic education initiatives** since **2022** embed **nationalist narratives** in schools.

#### **Pakistan’s Education System: Challenges & Global Context**
Pakistan’s education sector, despite **global contributions**, faces **funding constraints (~2% of GDP)** and **infrastructure crises**, particularly in **Punjab**, where **unsafe school buildings** delay repairs. **Madrasas** dominate rural areas due to **poor state support**, with **textbooks perpetuating stereotypes and extremist narratives**. **2023 literacy rates (60.65%)** rank it **second-lowest in South Asia after Afghanistan**, driven by **poverty, gender inequality, feudalism, and weak oversight**. **Teacher absenteeism, lack of awareness, and inadequate institutions** exacerbate systemic failures.

**Provincial disparities** are stark:
- **Punjab** leads with **66.25–77.30% literacy**, including **Islamabad (83.97%)** and **Lahore (79.62%)**.
- **Sindh** shows **moderate rates (38.14–72.26%)**, with **Karachi districts** excelling.
- **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** ranges from **37.15–65.55%**, with **Abbottabad (77.34%)** and **Upper Chitral (95% youth literacy)** as outliers.
- **Balochistan** has the lowest rates (**10.1–55.86%**), though **Karachi South (78.57%)** stands out.

**Youth literacy (15–24) surpassed 85%** in **13 districts**, including **Punjab’s Gujrat (90.90%)**, **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s Upper Chitral (95%)**, and **Islamabad (88.68%)**. **Mean years of schooling** improved from **2.28 (1990)** to **5.16 (2018)**, with **Islamabad (10.70 years)** and **Gilgit-Baltistan (5.17 years)** leading, while **Balochistan remained lowest (3.10 years)**.

**International comparison** underscores Pakistan’s lag: **Indonesia (90%)** and **Malaysia (89%)** outperform, while **439 international schools** (recognized by **PMDC**) cater to **English-medium education abroad**. **SICAS institutions** in China offer **WHO-approved programs**, reflecting **limited domestic capacity**.

**Pakistan’s Curriculum**:
- **Religious dominance**: **Madrasas** (12,000–40,000) offer **free boarding**, appealing to poorer families due to **religious and cost advantages**, but **formal integration** remains limited.
- **Gender disparities**: **18% of women** have **≥10 years of schooling**, with **42% female literacy** vs. **67% male**, and **high dropout rates** in **STEMM fields** (e.g., 80% female medical students drop out).
- **Vocational programs** (e.g., **DAE in engineering**) are **emerging but underfunded**, with **limited special education support** and **gender/equity gaps** in rural areas.
- **Teacher education**: Programs range from **12–16 years** (associate/bachelor’s) to **2–4-year MPhil/PhD programs**, criticized for being **exam-focused** and lacking **practical training**.
