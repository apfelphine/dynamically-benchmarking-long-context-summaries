### **Education in Russia**

#### **Levels of Education**
Russia’s education system is divided into **general** and **professional** education. **General education** includes:
- **Preschool (level 0)** – optional, regulated by regional/local authorities.
- **Primary general education (level 1)** – 4 years.
- **Basic general education (level 2)** – 5 years.
- **Secondary general education (level 3)** – 2 years.
- **Additional general education** – school-based clubs and groups.

**Professional education** includes:
- **Training for professions** – 1–several months, post-primary education.
- **Vocational education** – 3–4 years, post-basic education.
- **Higher education**:
  - **Bachelor’s degree** – 4 years, post-secondary.
  - **Specialist degree** – 5–6 years, post-secondary (medical/engineering specialties).
  - **Master’s degree** – 2 years, post-graduate.
  - **Postgraduate education** – 2–4 years (Candidates of Sciences, Doctors of Sciences).

#### **Preschool and Primary Education**
- **Preschool enrollment** increased from 67% (1999) to 84% (2005), with 68% of 5-year-olds enrolled in kindergartens (2002 census).
- **Kindergartens** are regulated by regional/local authorities, with limited fees (≤20% of costs).
- **Compulsory primary education** now spans 9 years (2007 reform), previously 10 years (10th grade optional).
- **School infrastructure** issues persist, especially in rural areas, with shortages of kindergarten slots in cities like Moscow.

#### **Secondary Education**
- **Compulsory education** now lasts 9 years (2007), previously 10 years.
- **School structure**:
  - **Full 9-year programme** (36,248 schools).
  - **Basic 9-year programme** (10,833 schools).
  - **Elementary-only schools** (10,198 schools).
- **Vocational training** post-9th grade, with options for specialized professional schools.
- **Standardized exams** (Unified State Examinations, USE) introduced in 2003 to replace university entrance tests, though resistance persists.

#### **Education for the Disabled**
- **Physical disabilities**: Specialized schools for blind/hearing-impaired children; vocational training for working-age disabled individuals.
- **Mental disabilities**: Compensatory classes in mainstream schools for delayed development; specialized boarding schools for persistent but mild disabilities.
- **Inclusive education**: Limited integration; some schools offer semi-segregated training.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Higher education** follows a **traditional Soviet model** (5–6 years for specialist degrees) and is transitioning to the **Bologna Process** (4-year bachelor’s + 2-year master’s).
- **Public vs. private**: 87.3% of institutions are private; state universities face consolidation efforts.
- **Postgraduate degrees**: Candidates of Sciences (2–4 years) and Doctors of Sciences (10 years), requiring original research.
- **Vocational schools**: Meister Schools (German-style) introduced to address vocational shortages.

#### **Science Outreach and Government Propaganda**
- **Outreach activities**: Mandated by law (2021) to promote knowledge dissemination, though criticized for prior censorship.
- **Patriotic education**: New "Important Conversations" lessons introduced post-Ukraine invasion, emphasizing national identity and traditional values.

#### **Education in Languages of Russia**
- **Multilingual education**: Schools teach regional languages (e.g., Tatar, Chechen) alongside Russian, with varying enrollment rates.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **Structure and History**
- **Pre-division period**: Confucian values shaped education, emphasizing meritocracy and civil examinations.
- **Post-war years**: Shifted to democratic education; standardized curriculum introduced in the 1950s.
- **Modern system**: 6-4-3-4 academic schedule (6 years primary, 4 middle, 3 vocational/high school, 4 college).

#### **School Levels**
- **Elementary school (1–6)**: Compulsory; curriculum includes Korean, math, social studies, and English.
- **Middle school (7–9)**: Compulsory; transition to high school begins at age 12.
- **High school (10–12)**: Non-compulsory; divided into **specialty tracks** (science, foreign languages, arts) and **normal state high schools**.
- **Vocational education**: Meister Schools (German-style) introduced to modernize vocational training; 20% of high school students attend vocational schools.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Traditional model**: 5–6-year specialist degrees; transitioning to **Bologna Process** (4-year bachelor’s + 2-year master’s).
- **University admissions**: Heavy reliance on **College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT)**; 90% of high school graduates apply to university.
- **Criticisms**: Overemphasis on exams, high dropout rates, and social stigma against vocational education.

#### **English Education**
- **Intensive focus**: Parents invest heavily in private English tutoring (hagwons) and overseas study.
- **Criticisms**: Overemphasis on exam preparation, leading to stress and mental health issues.

#### **Controversies**
- **Vocational education**: Stigma persists despite reforms.
- **Competitive education**: Excessive study hours, high suicide rates, and family financial burdens.
- **Social inequality**: Disparities in access to quality education, particularly in rural areas.

### **Education in Pakistan**

#### **Stages of Formal Education**
- **Primary education (3–8)**: 67.5% completion rate; curriculum includes arts, science, Urdu, English, and Pakistan Studies.
- **Secondary education (9–12)**:
  - **SSC (matriculation)**: 9th–10th grade; 8 subjects (e.g., math, English, Urdu, Islamic Studies).
  - **HSSC (intermediate)**: 11th–12th grade; 3 streams (pre-medical, pre-engineering, humanities).
- **Tertiary education**: 6% university graduates (2007); 4–5 years for professional degrees (e.g., medicine, engineering).

#### **Non-formal and Informal Education**
- **Madrassas**: Islamic seminaries; 12,000–40,000 schools; free food/shelter for students.
- **Vocational training**: Technical schools offer TSC (10th grade) and DAE (12th grade) diplomas.
- **NGOs**: Non-formal education for marginalized groups (e.g., BRAC).

#### **Gender Disparity**
- **Literacy rates**: 42% for women vs. 67% for men; 18% of women receive 10+ years of schooling.
- **Progress**: Gender parity in primary/secondary education; challenges remain in rural areas and higher education.
- **Transgender rights**: The **Pakistan Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act (2018)** recognizes transgender individuals and guarantees education, employment, and healthcare.

#### **Qualitative Dimension**
- **Shortages**: Lack of teachers, outdated curriculum, and poor infrastructure.
- **Reforms**: Free compulsory education, Millennium Development Goals (MDGs), and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

#### **Teacher Education**
- **Criticisms**: Short-term training, lack of human rights education, and outdated curriculum.
- **Challenges**: Teacher shortages, inadequate facilities, and lack of in-service training.

#### **Technical and Vocational Education**
- **TVET**: Focus on workforce development; challenges include inadequate funding and teacher training.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Structure and Compulsory Education**
- **Compulsory education**: Starts at age 5; ends at age 18 (11th grade).
- **School types**: Public, special (religious), and general-special schools; all regulated by the **Inspectie van het Onderwijs**.
- **Curriculum**: Standardized for primary and secondary education; differentiated by **VMBO, HAVO, VWO** (vocational, academic, and scientific tracks).

#### **Vocational Education and Higher Education**
- **Vocational education (MBO)**: 1–4 years; prepares for skilled trades or higher education.
- **Higher education (HBO/WO)**:
  - **HBO (universities of applied sciences)**: 4 years; professional focus.
  - **WO (research universities)**: 3–6 years; academic focus.
- **Bologna Process**: Adopted since 2002; 3-cycle system (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).

#### **Criticisms**
- **IQ testing**: Early separation into educational levels may disadvantage gifted students.
- **Declining rankings**: Lower than average in international comparisons (e.g., OECD).

### **Education in Poland**

#### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary school (szkoła podstawowa)**: 8 years (2017 reform); previously 6 years + 3-year gimnazjum.
- **Junior high school (gimnazjum)**: 3 years; subjects include Polish, history, foreign languages, and science.
- **Upper secondary education**: 4 years; options include **liceum** (general), **technikum** (vocational), and **zasadnicza szkoła zawodowa** (basic vocational).

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Bachelor’s (3 years)**: Follows Bologna Process; 2–6 years for professional degrees (e.g., medicine).
- **Grading system**: 2–5 scale; 5.5/6.0 for "exceeds expectations."
- **Vocational education**: 2.5-year **szkoła policealna**; teacher training programs.

#### **History and Reforms**
- **1999 reform**: Shifted from 8+4 to 6+3+3 years; improved rankings.
- **2017 reform**: Extended primary school to 8 years; criticized for nationalist curriculum.

### **Education in Japan**

#### **School Levels**
- **Primary school (1–6)**: Compulsory; 13-year levels (1–13).
- **Junior high school (7–9)**: Compulsory; 3 years.
- **Senior high school (10–12)**: Non-compulsory; 3 years; options include **college of technology** and **special training schools**.
- **Higher education**: Universities (4–6 years), junior colleges (2–3 years), vocational schools.

#### **Academic Grading**
- **Primary/secondary**: 5–1 scale; 5 = excellent, 1 = fail.
- **University**: 5.5 (exceeds expectations) to 0 (fail).

#### **Extracurricular Activities**
- **Shadow education**: Heavy reliance on **juku** (cram schools) for university prep; 86% of students participate.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Academic pressure**: High suicide rates, bullying, and mental health issues.
- **Lack of creativity**: Criticized for rote learning and lack of international perspectives.

### **Education in the Czech Republic**

#### **Elementary and Secondary Education**
- **Elementary school (základní škola)**: 9 years (2016 reform); previously 8 years.
- **Secondary education**:
  - **Vocational schools**: 2–3 years; certificates for trades.
  - **Grammar schools**: 4 years; prepare for university.
  - **Maturita exam**: Universal leaving qualification; includes Czech, English, and math.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: Public/private; 2–6 years (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).
- **Higher professional schools (VOŠ)**: 3–4 years; diplomas for vocational training.

#### **History**
- **1348**: Charles IV founded **Charles University** (Prague).
- **1999**: Bologna Process adopted; 3-cycle system (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD).

### **Education in Mexico**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (primaria)**: 1–6 years; bilingual education available.
- **Junior high (secundaria)**: 7–9 years; vocational training in **técnica**.
- **High school (preparatoria)**: 10–12 years; 3 streams (arts, commerce, science).

#### **Educational Integration**
- **Challenges**: Teacher training, accessibility, and curriculum adaptation for special needs students.
- **Progress**: 96.2% of 6–14-year-olds attend school (2015).

#### **Higher Education**
- **Bachelor’s (Licenciatura)**: 4 years; postgraduate (maestría, doctorado).
- **Intercultural Universities**: Established for indigenous populations.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Compulsory Education**
- **Age**: 5–16 (varies by province); 18 for diploma attainment.
- **School types**: Public (English/French), separate (religious), and private.
- **Grades**: K–12 (Kindergarten–Grade 12); Quebec has 11th grade (Secondary V).

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities**: Research-focused; 4-year bachelor’s, master’s, PhD.
- **Vocational**: Community colleges, technical schools; apprenticeships for skilled trades.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Inequality**: Disparities in funding, teacher training, and student achievement.
- **Bullying**: High rates of cyberbullying and physical violence.

### **Education in New Zealand**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (1–8)**: 8 years; intermediate (7–8).
- **Secondary (9–13)**: 5 years; NCEA (National Certificate of Educational Achievement).
- **School types**: State, state-integrated, private; Māori-medium schools.

#### **Curriculum**
- **National Curriculum**: NZC (English-medium), TMoA (Māori-medium).
- **Assessment**: NCEA for senior students; Cambridge International Examinations (CIE) or IBDP options.

#### **Funding**
- **State schools**: Government-funded; voluntary donations.
- **Private schools**: Tuition fees (~NZ$20,000/year).

### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Levels**
- **Pre-primary (K–2)**: 2 years.
- **Primary (3–8)**: 6 years; JSC exam (8th grade).
- **Secondary (9–12)**: 4 years; SSC (10th), HSC (12th).
- **Tertiary**: 44 government, 101 private, and 2 international universities.

#### **Grading System**
- **GPA**: 5.0 (A+) to 0.0 (F); 4th subject can boost GPA.

#### **Current Issues**
- **Poverty**: High dropout rates in rural areas; limited access to higher education.
- **Gender disparity**: Progress in primary/secondary education; challenges in higher education.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

#### **Structure**
- **Pre-primary (3–5)**: Kindergarten; not compulsory.
- **Primary (6–12)**: 6 years; English introduced in 2021.
- **Intermediate (13–15)**: 3 years; options for general/technical education.
- **Secondary (16–18)**: 3 years; technical institutes for vocational training.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities**: 24 government; 5–6 years for medicine/engineering.
- **Women’s education**: 60% of university students; Princess Nora bint Abdul Rahman University (PNU).

#### **Gifted Education**
- **Programs**: Enrichment classes, summer programs (SEP); 32% of gifted students enrolled (2019).

#### **Special Education**
- **Autism/mental disabilities**: Special institutions or mainstream schools with support.

#### **Criticisms**
- **Rote learning**: Criticized for lack of critical thinking and innovation.
- **Religious content**: Controversial for promoting extremism and intolerance.
