# Global Education System Comparisons

Various nations employ distinct educational models facing unique challenges. Mexico operates a secular system mandating high school completion while integrating indigenous populations, despite issues with secondary completion and special needs support. In contrast, New Zealand uses diverse primary/secondary options under the NZC, addressing achievement gaps among Māori and Pacific Island students via the ORS.

Other nations showcase different pressures: Japan is characterized by intense academic competition from entrance exams, while South Korea utilizes a highly competitive, meritocratic system fueled by private tutoring. The Netherlands structures education into tiered pathways (vocational, applied science, research) based on aptitude testing, which has drawn criticism regarding early tracking.

## Governance and National Approaches

Governance varies significantly; Canada manages K-12 education provincially, supporting diverse religious and language schools while federally focusing on groups like Indigenous students. Bangladesh covers Pre-primary through tertiary levels, specializing around Grade 11, yet struggles with rote learning and quality. Saudi Arabia is reforming its state-managed education under Vision 2030 to decrease reliance on rote learning.

Other systems present specific structures: Pakistan faces hurdles like low public spending (2% of GDP), a 60.65% literacy rate, and regional disparities, often necessitating reliance on Madrassas. The Czech Republic uses a nine-grade structure requiring the Maturita Exam for university entry, while Poland mandates eight years of primary education before students choose between general or vocational tracks, aligning with the Bologna scheme. Russia mandates nine years of K-12 education, uses the USE for university entry, and has recently emphasized "patriotic education."
