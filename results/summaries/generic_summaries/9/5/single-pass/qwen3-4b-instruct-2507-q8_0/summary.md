### **Education in Mexico**
- **Basic Education**: Divided into primary (Grades 1–6), junior high (Grades 7–9), and high school (Grades 10–12). Primary and junior high are compulsory; high school is now compulsory. Bilingual education is available from the start.
- **Educational Integration**: Mandated by law to include students with special needs in regular classrooms, supported by special education units (USAER). Challenges include teacher preparation, accessibility, and lack of collaboration.
- **Quality & Access**: Over 90% attend primary school, but only 62% attend secondary and 45% complete it. Rural schools face infrastructure issues. A 2013 reform shifted control from teachers' unions to government.
- **Higher Education**: Follows the Bologna Process with a 4-year bachelor’s, 2-year master’s, and 3-year doctorate. Degrees include Licenciatura, Ingeniero, and Arquitecto. Vocational programs (e.g., Conalep) offer technical training.
- **International Schools**: 151 schools, offering curricula in English outside English-speaking countries.

### **Education in New Zealand**
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 5 to 16. 13 year levels (Grades 1–13). Secondary education (Years 9–13) offers academic or vocational paths.
- **Curriculum**: National curriculum (NZC) or Māori-medium (TMoA) with eight learning areas. Students take NCEA or Cambridge/IB exams.
- **School Types**: State, state-integrated (religious), and private schools. State-integrated schools charge attendance dues.
- **Māori Language**: Offered in immersion schools (kura kaupapa) and as a subject in mainstream schools. Government policies promote its use.
- **Inclusive Education**: Special needs students are supported through resource rooms or full mainstreaming. Challenges include teacher training and school culture.
- **International Schools**: 152 schools, teaching in English or international curricula.

### **Education in Japan**
- **Structure**: 6 years of elementary (Grades 1–6), 3 years of junior high (Grades 7–9), 3 years of high school (Grades 10–12). Students may move between levels.
- **Curriculum**: Core subjects include Japanese, math, science, social studies, and foreign languages. Students take standardized tests (e.g., Cito).
- **Vocational Education**: VMBO offers practical training; HAVO/VWO provide academic paths. Students can move between levels based on performance.
- **Higher Education**: 4-year bachelor’s, 2-year master’s, and 3-year doctorate. Private institutions offer vocational training.
- **Extracurricular Activities**: High emphasis on "shadow education" (e.g., juku, practice exams). Over 86% of students participate.
- **Challenges**: High academic pressure, bullying, and over-reliance on entrance exams. Students face mental health issues.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**
- **Structure**: 6 years of primary (Grades 1–6), 3 years of intermediate (Grades 7–9), 3 years of secondary (Grades 10–12). Religious education is central.
- **Curriculum**: Religious studies dominate, with 9 periods weekly at primary level. Students take national exams (PSC, JSC, SSC, HSC).
- **Girls’ Education**: 60% of university students are female. Government provides scholarships and women's universities (e.g., PNU).
- **Gifted Education**: Programs (e.g., Mawhiba) offer enrichment and summer programs for gifted students.
- **Special Education**: Students with disabilities are mainstreamed or placed in special schools. Teachers are paid 30% more.
- **Private Education**: 48.9% of pre-primary and 8.2% of primary students attend private schools. Government funds infrastructure.
- **International Schools**: 203 schools, offering curricula in English or foreign languages.

### **Education in the Netherlands**
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 5 to 16. 8 grades in primary (Grades 1–8), followed by VMBO, HAVO, or VWO in secondary.
- **Curriculum**: VMBO (vocational), HAVO (general), and VWO (academic). Students take Cito test to determine path.
- **School Types**: Public, special (religious), and general-special (neutral) schools. All are government-financed.
- **Higher Education**: Bologna-aligned system with bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. HBO (universities of applied sciences) and WO (research universities).
- **International Schools**: 152 schools, offering international curricula.

### **Education in Bangladesh**
- **Structure**: 12 years of formal education (pre-primary to secondary). Students choose between arts, commerce, or science streams.
- **Exams**: JSC (Grade 8), SSC (Grade 10), HSC (Grade 12). All exams are standardized, multiple-choice, and practical.
- **Schools**: National curriculum in Bengali or English. Private schools offer international curricula (e.g., Cambridge, IB).
- **Madrasah Education**: Two types: Qawmi (private) and Alia (government-funded). Alia schools offer Islamic studies in Arabic and Bengali.
- **Vocational Education**: Technical and vocational training through institutions like polytechnics.
- **Challenges**: Gender disparity, poor infrastructure, and outdated curriculum. Literacy rate is 74.66% (2022).
- **International Schools**: 439 schools, offering curricula in English.

### **Education in South Korea**
- **Structure**: 6 years of elementary, 3 years of middle, 3 years of high school. High school offers academic or vocational tracks.
- **Curriculum**: Core subjects include Korean, math, science, and social studies. Students take the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) for university entry.
- **Higher Education**: 4-year bachelor’s, 2-year master’s, and 3-year doctorate. Private universities offer liberal arts and vocational programs.
- **Challenges**: High academic pressure, student suicides, and gender disparities. Vocational education is underfunded and stigmatized.
- **Meister Schools**: Vocational training programs based on German models to prepare students for skilled trades.
- **English Education**: Emphasis on oral proficiency; private English schools are common.

### **Education in Canada**
- **Structure**: Compulsory from age 5 to 18. 12 years of K–12 education. Secondary education ends at Grade 12 (Quebec ends at Grade 11).
- **School Types**: Public, private, and religious schools. French immersion and English immersion available.
- **Higher Education**: Bachelor’s, master’s, and PhD degrees. Vocational institutions offer diplomas and apprenticeships.
- **Tertiary Education**: Canada has the highest tertiary enrollment rate globally. Universities are prestigious and research-intensive.
- **International Schools**: 152 schools, offering curricula in English or international languages.

### **Education in Russia**
- **Structure**: 11 years of compulsory education (Grades 1–11). Includes preschool, primary, and secondary levels.
- **Higher Education**: Traditionally a five-year specialist degree; now transitioning to Bologna model (bachelor’s + master’s).
- **Vocational Education**: 3-year programs after secondary education. Students may pursue military or civilian careers.
- **Postgraduate Education**: Candidate of Sciences (PhD equivalent) and Doctor of Sciences (highest academic degree).
- **Challenges**: Overcrowded schools, poor infrastructure, and political influence. The government promotes "patriotic education" in schools.
- **Languages**: Over 100 regional languages taught, with strong emphasis on Russian.

### **Education in the Czech Republic**
- **Structure**: 9-year primary (Grades 1–9), followed by 4-year secondary (Grades 10–13). Students take the Maturita exam for university entry.
- **Secondary Types**: Vocational, professional, grammar, and lyceum schools. Grammar schools prepare students for university.
- **Tertiary Education**: Bachelor’s (3 years), master’s (2 years), and PhD programs. Public universities offer free instruction in Czech or English.
- **Calendar**: School year runs from September to June, divided into two semesters with breaks.
- **Education for the Disabled**: Special schools for physical and mental disabilities; integrated education is growing.

### **Education in Pakistan**
- **Structure**: Pre-school (3–5 years), primary (Grades 1–8), secondary (Grades 9–12). Students take national exams (SSC, HSSC).
- **Curriculum**: Subjects include arts, science, math, and religious education. Urdu and English are primary languages.
- **Gender Disparity**: Women face significant barriers in education and employment. Literacy rate is 60.65% (2023).
- **Tertiary Education**: Bachelor’s (3–5 years), master’s (2 years), and PhD programs. Professional degrees require accreditation.
- **Madrassas**: Teach Islamic studies and are common in rural areas. Often criticized for promoting extremist ideologies.
- **Challenges**: Poor infrastructure, teacher shortages, and gender-based discrimination. Literacy rate lags behind regional averages.

### **Education in Poland**
- **Structure**: 8 years of primary school (Grades 1–8), followed by 4–5 years of upper secondary (liceum or technikum).
- **Reforms**: 2017 reform extended primary school to 8 years, abolished gimnazjum, and increased vocational education.
- **Grading System**: 2–5 scale with 3.0 as the minimum passing grade. Failing grades are common and can be retaken.
- **Foreign Languages**: English is dominant; students learn two foreign languages (e.g., English and German).
- **History**: Poland was one of Europe’s most educated nations historically. The Jagiellonian University (founded 1364) is one of the oldest in Europe.
- **Violence**: "Zero tolerance" policy introduced in 2006 to combat school violence. Teachers have civil servant status.
