### **Education in Mexico**

#### **Structure of Basic Education**
Mexico’s basic education system comprises three stages:
- **Primary School (Primaria, Grades 1–6):** Ages 6–12, bilingual education possible.
- **Junior High School (Secundaria, Grades 7–9):** Ages 12–15, includes vocational training (técnica) and distance learning (telesecundaria).
- **High School (Preparatoria, Grades 10–12):** Ages 15–18, divided into SEP-incorporated and university-incorporated programs, with vocational options (tecnología, comercio).

#### **Educational Integration**
- **1993 Reform:** Mandated inclusion of students with disabilities into regular classrooms. Students with severe disabilities attend **Centros de Atención Múltiple (CAM)**, while others receive support via **Unidades de Servicio y Apoyo a la Educación Regular (USAER)**.
- **Challenges:** Lack of teacher training, accessibility issues, and insufficient resources hinder effective integration.

#### **Quality of Education**
- **Progressive Attendance:** Over 90% of children attend primary school, but only 62% reach secondary school, and 45% complete it. After secondary school, only 25% proceed to higher education.
- **OECD Criticism:** Mexico ranks below average in mathematics, science, and reading.
- **Reform Initiatives:** Shifted control from the **Sindicato Nacional de Trabajadores de la Educación (SNTE)** to central and state governments, leading to increased teacher autonomy and curriculum reforms.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Structure:** Follows the US model with a **Licenciatura** (bachelor’s degree), followed by a **Maestría** (master’s) and **Doctorado** (PhD), aligning with the **Bologna Process**.
- **Intercultural Universities:** Established in 2004 to improve enrollment of indigenous populations.
- **International Schools:** Over 150 international schools catering to expatriates and foreign students.

### **Education in New Zealand**

#### **Structure**
- **Compulsory Education:** Free and compulsory from age 5 to 16, with most students continuing until age 17.
- **School Levels:**
  - **Primary (Years 1–8):** Ages 5–13, includes intermediate (Years 7–8).
  - **Secondary (Years 9–13):** Ages 13–18, with options for **middle schools** (Years 7–10) and **senior schools** (Years 11–13).
- **Curriculum:** Follows the **New Zealand Curriculum (NZC)** or **Te Marautanga o Aotearoa** (Māori-medium). Core subjects include English, mathematics, science, and social studies.

#### **Special Education**
- **Inclusive Education:** Students with disabilities are integrated into mainstream classrooms with support from **USAER** or special schools.
- **Challenges:** Limited training for teachers, accessibility issues, and systemic barriers.

#### **International Education**
- **152 International Schools:** Offer curricula in English, including **Cambridge International Examinations (CIE)** and **International Baccalaureate (IB)**.

### **Education in Japan**

#### **School Levels**
- **Primary (Years 1–6):** Ages 6–11, compulsory.
- **Junior High (Years 7–9):** Ages 12–15, compulsory.
- **Senior High (Years 10–12):** Ages 15–18, non-compulsory but highly selective.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (4-year bachelor’s), junior colleges, colleges of technology, and vocational schools.

#### **Vocational Education**
- **Meister Schools:** Revamped vocational training to address labor shortages in skilled trades.
- **Criticism:** Heavy focus on exams, lack of creativity, and mental health issues among students.

#### **Extracurricular Activities**
- **Shadow Education:** Extensive after-school programs (juku) to prepare for university entrance exams.

### **Education in Saudi Arabia**

#### **Structure**
- **Pre-Primary (Kindergarten):** Ages 3–5, not compulsory.
- **Primary (Grades 1–6):** Ages 6–12, compulsory. English introduced from Grade 1.
- **Intermediate (Grades 7–9):** Ages 12–15.
- **Secondary (Grades 10–12):** Ages 15–18, with options for general and specialized education.

#### **Higher Education**
- **Universities:** 24 government universities, including **King Saud University** (founded 1957). Private universities and vocational training are also available.
- **Gender Disparity:** Women constitute 60% of university students, but face societal barriers.

#### **Religious Education**
- **Core Curriculum:** Heavy emphasis on Islamic studies, including Quran memorization and interpretation.
- **Criticism:** Criticized for promoting extremism, intolerance, and lack of critical thinking.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (Basisschool):** Ages 4–12, 8 grades.
- **Secondary (Voortgezet Onderwijs):** Ages 12–18, divided into:
  - **VMBO (Vocational):** 4 years, prepares for vocational training.
  - **HAVO (General):** 5 years, prepares for higher education.
  - **VWO (Academic):** 6 years, prepares for university.
- **Vocational Education:** **MBO** (1–4 years), **HBO** (4 years), and **WO** (3 years).

#### **Criticism**
- **Grade Separation:** Early division based on IQ tests can disadvantage students.
- **Declining Standards:** Lower international rankings in reading, math, and science.

### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Structure**
- **Pre-Primary (KG):** Ages 3–5.
- **Primary (Grades 3–8):** Ages 6–12, compulsory.
- **Secondary (Grades 9–12):** Ages 13–17, leading to **SSC (10th)** and **HSC (12th)** exams.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (public/private), vocational training, and **Technical and Vocational Education**.

#### **Challenges**
- **Gender Disparity:** Low female enrollment and literacy rates.
- **Quality Issues:** Poor infrastructure, outdated curriculum, and teacher shortages.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (Grades 1–6):** Ages 6–11.
- **Middle School (Grades 7–9):** Ages 12–15.
- **High School (Grades 10–12):** Ages 15–18, non-compulsory but highly competitive.
- **Vocational Education:** **Meister Schools** for skilled trades, with high employment rates.

#### **Criticism**
- **Academic Pressure:** Extensive use of **hagwons** (cram schools), leading to mental health issues.
- **Social Stigma:** Vocational education is often undervalued.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Structure**
- **Early Childhood:** Kindergarten (ages 4–6), optional.
- **Primary (Grades 1–6):** Ages 5–11.
- **Secondary (Grades 7–12):** Ages 11–17, leading to a **diploma**.
- **Tertiary Education:** Universities (research/undergraduate), colleges (vocational), and **private universities**.

#### **Key Features**
- **Multilingual Schools:** English and French education available.
- **Compulsory Attendance:** Varies by province (ages 5–16 or 18).

### **Education in Russia**

#### **Structure**
- **Preschool:** Ages 2–6.
- **Primary (Grades 1–4):** Ages 6–10.
- **Middle (Grades 5–9):** Ages 10–15.
- **Secondary (Grades 10–11):** Ages 15–18, leading to **maturity certificate**.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (bachelor’s/master’s/PhD), vocational schools.

#### **Challenges**
- **Vocational vs. Academic:** Strong emphasis on academic education over vocational training.
- **Teacher Shortages:** High absenteeism and outdated curriculum.

### **Education in the Czech Republic**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (Grades 1–9):** Ages 6–15, compulsory.
- **Secondary (Grades 10–13):** Ages 15–18, leading to **maturity certificate**.
- **Vocational Schools:** 2–4 years, preparing for trades or university.
- **Higher Education:** Universities (public/private), **VOŠ** (vocational schools).

#### **Key Features**
- **Maturita Exam:** Universal leaving qualification for university admission.

### **Education in Poland**

#### **Structure**
- **Primary (Grades 1–8):** Ages 6–14, compulsory.
- **Secondary (Grades 9–12):** Ages 14–18, leading to **Matura** exam.
- **Vocational Schools:** 3–5 years, preparing for trades or university.

#### **Reforms**
- **2017 Education Reform:** Extended primary school to 8 years, abolished **gimnazjum**.
- **Criticism:** Controversial changes to history curriculum and teacher autonomy.
