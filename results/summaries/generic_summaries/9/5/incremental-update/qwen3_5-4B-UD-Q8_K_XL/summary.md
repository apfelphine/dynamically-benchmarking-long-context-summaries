### Final Summary: Global Education Systems Overview

**South Korea** operates a high-pressure, meritocratic engine rooted in Confucian values, driving economic growth through a centralized 6-4-3-4 structure and intense focus on the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT). Despite revitalization efforts in vocational sectors like "Meister Schools," the system remains rigid, characterized by widespread private cram schools (*hagwons*), excessive study hours, and severe student stress, leading to the lowest happiness scores in the OECD. Recent reforms include banning late-night *hagwons* and restricting coffee in schools, though the near-universal pursuit of SKY university degrees and the stigma against vocational training persist.

**Canada** utilizes a decentralized framework where provincial governments dictate structures, while the federal level manages funding, military colleges, and Indigenous education. The system progresses through Early Childhood, Elementary, Intermediate, and Secondary stages, with variations such as Quebec's two-year CEGEP requirement. Language rights under the Charter ensure minority language education, and religious separate schools are protected in specific provinces. Tertiary education is globally renowned for research, divided between universities and vocational institutions, with admission reliant on GPAs and extracurriculars. Funding accounts for 5.2% of GDP, and private universities face ongoing debates regarding costs and human rights.

**Russia** features a standardized system divided into General, Professional, and Specialized tracks, with nine years of secondary education being compulsory since 2007. Instruction shifts from single teachers to specialists by year 5, utilizing a 5-point grading scale. The Unified State Examinations (USE) replaced local tests to reduce corruption, while the transition to the Bologna Process (Bachelor's and Master's) has sparked criticism regarding the loss of practical recognition and increased costs. Recent legislative changes mandate patriotic education following the invasion of Ukraine, regulate "science outreach," and require foreign citizens to pass a Russian language exam for school entry.

**The Czech Republic** maintains a nine-year elementary stage followed by secondary schools offering diverse paths, including vocational, professional, lyceum, and grammar schools. Entry to university requires the universal *maturita* exam, which includes mandatory Czech language/literature and a second subject. The system is anchored by historic universities like Charles University (founded 1348), offering free Czech-language study for domestic students under 26, alongside paid English options for international students. The academic calendar runs from September to June, featuring extended Christmas and summer vacations.

**Pakistan** faces a challenging education landscape marked by deep disparities, low completion rates (~67.5%), and profound gender inequality driven by patriarchal structures. While formal education covers preschool through Year 8, with tertiary options including "Pass" and "Honours" degrees, the system suffers from outdated curricula, a shortage of qualified teachers, and reliance on memorization rather than critical skills. Significant gender gaps persist, with male literacy at ~67% versus ~42% for females, exacerbated by safety concerns, high costs, and a teaching profession dominated by males. Regional data from the 2023 Census reveals stark contrasts, with Islamabad leading at 83.97% literacy compared to Balochistan and FATA, which struggle with poverty and institutional scarcity. Additionally, religious influences are strong; madrasas serve as a primary alternative for the poorest, and the national curriculum faces criticism for marginalizing minorities and reinforcing traditional gender roles.

**Poland** boasts a rich educational history, established by the 1364 Jagiellonian University and the 1773 state ministry of education. The current system, following a 2017 reform, extends primary schooling to eight years and restructures secondary education into general, technical, and vocational tracks. Tertiary education aligns with the Bologna Process (3-year Bachelor's, 2-year Master's). A 2006 "zero tolerance" policy grants teachers civil servant status to penalize violence against them, with consequences for parents. However, recent history curriculum changes have emphasized nationalism and political elites while removing references to the Non-Aligned Movement and "communism."
