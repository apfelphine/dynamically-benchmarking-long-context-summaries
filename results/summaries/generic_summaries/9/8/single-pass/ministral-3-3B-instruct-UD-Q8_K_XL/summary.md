### **Education in Bangladesh**

#### **Education System Overview**
Bangladesh’s education system is structured into three main levels: **pre-primary (K-2), primary (3–8), secondary (9–12), and tertiary education**. Students can study in either **English or Bengali**, with **60% of primary school positions reserved for women**. The **National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB)** develops textbooks and administers standardized exams in English or Bengali. Students must complete **12 years of education** (up to class 12), with no middle school system.

The curriculum includes **three streams** (Arts & Humanities, Commerce, Science) starting in class 9, with standardized exams at **PSC (5th grade), JSC (8th grade), SSC (10th grade), and HSC (12th grade)**. Exams are heavily weighted toward **written tests, MCQs, practicals, and viva-voice**, with no classroom performance considered.

#### **School Types**
Bangladesh features a diverse range of schools, including:
- **International schools** (English/French medium, e.g., Aga Khan Academy, French International School).
- **Religiously affiliated schools** (e.g., Madrasahs).
- **National curriculum schools** (Bengali/English medium).
- **Cadet colleges** (administered by the Bangladesh Military, with compulsory discipline).
- **Government plans** to build **1,000 primary schools in char areas** (flood-prone regions).

#### **Primary Education**
Managed by the **Ministry of Primary and Mass Education (MOPME)**, primary education is overseen by the **Directorate of Primary Education (DPE)**. Key responsibilities include teacher recruitment, in-service training, textbook distribution, and school infrastructure. The **National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB)** develops curricula, and **34.7 million textbooks** were distributed in 2022. The **JSC (8th-grade) and PEC (5th-grade) exams** were abolished in 2023.

#### **Secondary Education**
Controlled by **10 general education boards** (e.g., Barisal, Chittagong, Dhaka), secondary education includes **SSC (10th grade) and HSC (12th grade)** exams. Non-government schools have **School Management Committees (SMC)**, while government schools are managed by the **Directorate of Secondary and Higher Education (DSHE)**. Students can choose between **general education, vocational training, or specialized streams** (e.g., science, commerce, arts).

#### **Tertiary Education**
Regulated by the **University Grants Commission (UGC)**, tertiary education includes:
- **Public universities** (e.g., Dhaka University, University of Chittagong).
- **Private universities** (e.g., North South University, BRAC University).
- **International universities** (e.g., University of Dhaka, University of Asia Pacific).
- **Specialized colleges** (e.g., medical, engineering, agriculture).
- **Technical and vocational education** (e.g., polytechnics, vocational training centers).

#### **Alternative Education Systems**
- **International schools** follow **Cambridge, Pearson Edexcel, IB, or other international curricula**, preparing students for **O-Level, A-Level, or IBDP exams**.
- **Madrasah education** focuses on **Islamic studies** (Quran, Hadith, Fiqh) alongside general education. **Qawmi (private) and Alia (government-subsidized) madrasas** serve different demographics.
- **Refugee education**: UNICEF operates **1,600 learning centers** for Rohingya children, teaching Myanmar curriculum.
- **Non-formal education**: NGOs like **BRAC** provide education for dropouts, though many do not continue to secondary school.

#### **Grading System**
A **passing grade is 33%**, with a **GPA scale from 0 to 5.0**. The grading system includes:
- **Letter grades (A+, A, B, C, D, F)**.
- **Optional subjects** contribute to GPA calculation (e.g., GP in optional subject – 2).
- **Golden A+**: Achieving A+ in all subjects without exceeding 80% in each.

#### **Religious Education**
Religious studies are mandatory for **Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and Christianity**. Non-Muslim students study their respective religions, though some schools lack teachers for minority faiths. **Islamic history and heritage** are included in the national curriculum.

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Government initiatives**: Compulsory primary education, free education for girls up to class 10, stipends for female students, and a **food-for-education literacy movement**.
- **Challenges**: **Low primary school attendance in rural areas**, **teacher absenteeism (15.5%)**, **gender disparity**, and **high dropout rates**.
- **Literacy rate**: **74.66% (2022)**, up from **51.77% (2011)**.
- **Education expenditure**: **<2% of GDP**, with plans to increase to **7% by 2025**.

### **Education in Canada**

#### **Education System Overview**
Education in Canada is **provincially managed**, with **no federal control**. The system includes:
- **Early childhood education** (kindergarten, pre-primary).
- **Primary and secondary education** (K-12, with variations by province).
- **Tertiary education** (universities, colleges, vocational schools).

#### **Compulsory Education**
- **Primary and secondary education** is compulsory from **age 5 to 16** (varies by province).
- **Kindergarten** is optional but widely available (ages 4–6).
- **School attendance** is mandatory until **age 16 (except Manitoba, New Brunswick, Ontario)**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Primary and secondary education** spans **13 years** (Kindergarten to Grade 12).
- **Secondary schools** include **middle/high schools**, with variations in grading and curriculum.
- **Vocational schools** offer **certificates, diplomas, and associate degrees** in skilled trades.

#### **Tertiary Education**
- **Universities** (research and undergraduate) and **colleges** (vocational, community).
- **Public universities** (e.g., University of Toronto, McGill) are prestigious, while **private universities** (e.g., Quest University) are less common.
- **Admission criteria**: High school transcripts, standardized tests (e.g., SAT, ACT), and extracurricular activities.
- **Tuition fees**: Vary by province (e.g., **$10,000–$20,000/year** for international students).

#### **International Education**
- **International schools** follow **British, IB, or American curricula** (e.g., International School of Toronto).
- **Language-based schools**: **Section 23 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms** guarantees bilingual education (English/French).
- **Religious schools**: **Separate schools** (e.g., Catholic, Jewish) are funded by provincial governments.

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Education funding**: **~5.2% of GDP** (varies by province).
- **Quality concerns**: **Rote-learning, lack of critical thinking**, and **grade inflation**.
- **Bullying and school violence**: **High incidence in some provinces** (e.g., New Zealand ranks second in bullying).
- **Digital education**: **Increasing reliance on online learning**, especially post-COVID.

### **Education in Japan**

#### **Education System Overview**
Japan’s education system is **highly structured**, with **compulsory education from age 6 to 15**. The system includes:
- **Elementary school (shōgakkō, grades 1–6)**.
- **Junior high school (chūgakkō, grades 7–9)**.
- **Senior high school (kōtōgakkō, grades 10–12)**.
- **Tertiary education** (universities, junior colleges, vocational schools).

#### **School Structure**
- **Elementary school**: **4 years (ages 6–10)**, compulsory.
- **Junior high school**: **3 years (ages 12–15)**, compulsory.
- **Senior high school**: **3 years (ages 15–18)**, non-compulsory but highly competitive.
- **Universities**: **4–6 years**, with **bachelor’s (gakushi), master’s (shūshi), and PhD (hakushi)** degrees.

#### **Academic Grading**
- **Japanese grading system**: **1–5 scale** (1 = failing, 5 = excellent).
- **High school exams**: **Extremely competitive**, with **juku (cram schools)** aiding preparation.
- **Extracurricular activities**: **Heavy emphasis on shadow education** (e.g., juku, mogi shiken).

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Academic pressure**: **High suicide rates among students**, **school violence**, and **mental health crises**.
- **Bullying**: **Record 612,496 bullying cases in 2019**, with **10 students dying by suicide** due to bullying.
- **Vocational education**: **Limited focus on practical skills**, with **Meister Schools** (German-style) being introduced.
- **International education**: **30–40 international schools**, following **British, IB, or American curricula**.

### **Education in Russia**

#### **Education System Overview**
Russia’s education system is **divided into general and professional education**. Key levels include:
- **Preschool (0–6 years)**.
- **Primary general education (1–4 years)**.
- **Basic general education (5–9 years)**.
- **Secondary general education (10–11 years)**.
- **Tertiary education** (universities, vocational schools).

#### **School Structure**
- **Compulsory education**: **9 years (grades 1–9)**.
- **Vocational training**: **3–4 years**, leading to a **vocational certificate**.
- **Higher education**: **Bachelor’s (4 years), Master’s (2 years), PhD (3–4 years)**.
- **Special education**: **For disabled students**, with **resource rooms and specialized schools**.

#### **Grading System**
- **5-step scale (2–5)**, with **1 being a rare failing grade**.
- **Exams**: **Standardized tests (USE, Basic State Exam)**.

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Education funding**: **~2% of GDP**, with **consolidation of universities** (e.g., merging state universities).
- **Vocational education**: **Limited access to skilled trades**, with **Meister Schools** being introduced.
- **International education**: **439 international schools** (ISC definition).
- **Language policy**: **New law bans foreign students from attending Russian schools without a Russian language exam**.

### **Education in the Netherlands**

#### **Education System Overview**
The Dutch education system is **municipally managed**, with **compulsory education from age 5 to 18**. Key levels include:
- **Elementary school (basisschool, grades 1–8)**.
- **Secondary education (voortgezet onderwijs, grades 9–12)**.
- **Tertiary education (HBO, WO)**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Elementary school**: **8 years**, with **Cito test** in grade 8.
- **Secondary education**: **VMBO (vocational), HAVO (academic), VWO (scientific)**.
- **Vocational education (MBO)**: **1–4 years**, leading to **vocational diplomas**.
- **Higher education (HBO, WO)**: **4–6 years**, with **Bachelor’s, Master’s, and PhD** degrees.

#### **Grading System**
- **Letter grades (A–F)**, with **A being the highest**.
- **VMBO, HAVO, VWO**: **Differentiated curricula** based on **profiles (C&M, E&M, N&G, N&T)**.

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Education funding**: **~5.2% of GDP**, with **focus on digital education**.
- **International education**: **152 international schools** (ISC definition).
- **Vocational education**: **Strong MBO system**, with **HBO and WO for higher education**.

### **Education in South Korea**

#### **Education System Overview**
South Korea’s education system is **highly competitive**, with **universal primary and secondary education**. Key levels include:
- **Elementary school (chodeung hakgyo, grades 1–6)**.
- **Middle school (chugak, grades 7–9)**.
- **High school (changchuk, grades 10–12)**.
- **Tertiary education (colleges, universities)**.

#### **School Structure**
- **Elementary school**: **6 years (ages 7–12)**, with **mobile phone restrictions**.
- **Middle school**: **3 years (ages 12–15)**, compulsory.
- **High school**: **3 years (ages 15–18)**, non-compulsory but highly competitive.
- **Vocational education**: **Meister Schools** (German-style), with **apprenticeship-based training**.

#### **Grading System**
- **Letter grades (A–F)**, with **A being the highest**.
- **High school exams**: **Extremely competitive**, with **CSAT (College Scholastic Ability Test)**.

#### **Current Issues & Improvements**
- **Education funding**: **~4.5% of GDP**, with **focus on vocational education**.
- **Vocational education**: **Meister Schools** introduced to improve **skilled trades**.
- **International education**: **100,000+ Korean students in the U.S.**.
- **Social stigma**: **Negative perception of vocational education**, with **Meister Schools** being introduced to change this.
