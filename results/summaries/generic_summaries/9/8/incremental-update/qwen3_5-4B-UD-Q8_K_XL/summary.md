### South Korea: Higher Education and Admissions Landscape

South Korea's higher education system is defined by intense competition, high academic pressure, and a societal preference for university degrees, which carries significant stigma against vocational training despite its role in addressing the high mismatch rate between academic majors and actual jobs. Access is predominantly determined by the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) rather than GPA, within a stratified landscape dominated by private institutions. While English education has evolved from translation training to a proficiency-based focus driven by globalization, the system remains costly and relies heavily on private cram schools (*hagwons*). This entrance-exam-focused environment, characterized by excessive study hours, has led to severe concerns regarding student well-being, with South Korean students ranking among the lowest in OECD countries for life satisfaction. Consequently, the government is actively pursuing reforms to reduce the burden on students and improve vocational education.
