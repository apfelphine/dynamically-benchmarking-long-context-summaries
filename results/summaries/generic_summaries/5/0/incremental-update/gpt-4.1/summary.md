## Influential Thinkers: Whitehead, Torres Quevedo, Stallman, Turing, Pascal, Babbage, Lamarr, Bush, Lovelace, and Rubin

### Alfred North Whitehead: Life, Thought, and Legacy
Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was a mathematician and philosopher, co-author of *Principia Mathematica*. He shaped mathematics, logic, and process philosophy, influencing theology, ecology, education, and American pragmatism.

### Leonardo Torres Quevedo: Engineering, Automation, and Impact
Leonardo Torres Quevedo (1852–1936) advanced mechanical computation and automation, with key inventions—the Telekino and automatic calculators—alongside innovations in airship design and advocacy for Esperanto, leaving a lasting mark on engineering and early computing.

### Richard Stallman: Free Software Movement and Activism
Richard Stallman (b. 1953) founded the GNU Project and Free Software Foundation, developed Emacs and GCC, and initiated the GNU GPL license. He is a prominent activist for free software, digital rights, and against software patents and DRM.

### Alan Turing: Computability, AI, Biology, and Legacy
Alan Turing (1912–1954) pioneered computer science through the Turing machine and Universal Turing Machine, contributed to artificial intelligence, cryptanalysis, and developmental biology.

### Blaise Pascal: Mathematics, Physics, Philosophy, Religion, and Legacy
Blaise Pascal (1623–1662) made advances in probability, mechanics, hydraulics, and designed the Pascaline calculator. His philosophical and theological works, *Pensées* and *The Provincial Letters*, remain influential.

### Charles Babbage: Mechanical Computation, Engineering, Cryptography, and Multifaceted Influence
Charles Babbage (1791–1871) accelerated mechanical computation with the Difference and Analytical Engines and influenced cryptography, railway engineering, management, and science.

### Hedy Lamarr: Cinema, Wireless Innovation, and Cultural Recognition
Hedy Lamarr (1914–2000), famed actress, co-invented frequency-hopping spread spectrum technology, crucial for wireless communication—earning posthumous acclaim for her technical contributions.

### Vannevar Bush: Leadership in Science, Innovation, Policy, and Lasting Legacy
Vannevar Bush (1890–1974) shaped U.S. science policy, led WWII research, promoted international collaboration, and founded the NSF. His leadership and mentoring profoundly impacted research, innovation, and public policy.

### Ada Lovelace: Life, Work, Vision, and Cultural Impact
Ada Lovelace (1815–1852) collaborated with Charles Babbage on the Analytical Engine, pioneering concepts in computer science and early programming. Her legacy includes publications, memorials, the Ada language, and advocacy for women in STEM.

### Philip Rubin: Speech Science, Cognitive Research, Leadership, and Cultural Relevance
Philip Rubin is a psychologist, linguist, and musician, widely recognized for influential research in speech perception, production, articulatory synthesis, motor coordination, and perceptual organization. His publications from the 1970s through 2022 have shaped computational modeling of human speech and the ethical regulation of scientific research. At Haskins Laboratories and the NSF, he advanced research in audiovisual and multimodal speech, gestural biomechanics, embodied cognition, and led neuroscience and science policy committees. Rubin guided priorities for the White House OSTP, including the BRAIN Initiative, and promoted ethical approaches in emerging technologies. His leadership spans organizations such as FABBS, UConn, and key scientific commissions; he also participated in prominent STEM events. Rubin’s honors include Fellowships in leading scientific societies and research leadership awards. Depicted in a Drew Friedman 2022 illustration, his musical past (The Institution) complements his scientific career. He is married to Joette Katz, with two children.
