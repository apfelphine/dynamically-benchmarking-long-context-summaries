### Computing History and Innovation
Alan Turing revolutionized computing theory in 1936 with the Universal Machine, a concept foundational to modern AI and cryptography, while his wartime invention of the *bombe* significantly accelerated Allied efforts against Enigma ciphers. Charles Babbage served as a pioneer in British engineering, designing the Difference Engine and the Analytical Engine, which introduced punched-card programming and conditional branching. Ada Lovelace championed Babbage's vision by writing the first algorithm for the Analytical Engine in 1843, earning the title of the first computer programmer and foreseeing that machines could process symbols beyond mere calculation.

Hedy Lamarr co-invented frequency-hopping spread spectrum technology in the 1940s alongside George Antheil to guide torpedoes, laying the groundwork for modern secure wireless communications and GPS. Vannevar Bush, an inventor with degrees from Tufts, MIT, and Harvard, contributed to radio innovations and the development of the "profile tracer" and thermostatic switch. His career evolved into shaping U.S. scientific management; he established the Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) to direct wartime technologies and later championed the National Science Foundation to secure sustained government funding for basic research.

### Philip Rubin: Cognitive Science and Public Service
Philip Rubin is a distinguished cognitive scientist and linguist known for his pioneering work in speech perception, production, and articulatory synthesis. His research bridges theoretical models of human speech processing with practical applications, developing systems such as ASY, SWS, and HADES, as well as the SPIEL programming language. Theoretically, Rubin advances the understanding of human communication through a dynamical systems perspective, prioritizing spatiotemporal coordination and global spectral coherence over momentary signal features.

Beyond academia, Rubin has made notable cultural impacts through innovations in sine-wave speech synthesis that influenced media projects like *Star Wars* and a career in urban wall art photography. In the public sector, he served as Assistant Director for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences at the White House OSTP under President Obama and directed the NSF's Division of Behavioral and Cognitive Sciences, leading initiatives in cognitive neuroscience, open data, and mental health. His service also included advisory roles with NASA and the University of Connecticut, as well as leadership in the Federation of Associations in Behavioral and Brain Sciences and contributions to the ethical regulation of human subjects research.
