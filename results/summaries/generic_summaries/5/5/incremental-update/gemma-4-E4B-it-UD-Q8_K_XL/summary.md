**Pioneers of Computing and Scientific Vision:**
*   **Charles Babbage** designed mechanical computer precursors (Difference and Analytical Engines) and co-founded the Royal Astronomical Society (1820), collaborating with **Ada Lovelace**.
*   **Blaise Pascal** created the Pascaline calculator and made contributions to hydrostatics.
*   **Alan Turing**, a pivotal mathematician, developed the Turing machine (1936) and the Church–Turing thesis. His wartime contributions included developing the **Bombe** and inventing **Banburismus** for Enigma, alongside utilizing **Turingery** for Colossus. He proposed the stored-program computer design with the **ACE** (1946) and defined machine intelligence via the **Turing test**, facing career restrictions post-1952 until a 2013 pardon.
*   **Vannevar Bush** was instrumental in wartime science (Manhattan Project), envisioned the *memex* (influencing the WWW), and spurred basic science funding, leading to the 1950 establishment of the NSF.
*   **Philip Rubin** advises the White House OSTP on technology ethics for military research, focusing on embodied cognition and language biology.

**Key Innovators, Philosophers, and Free Software Advocacy:**
*   **Richard Stallman** spearheaded the free software movement, founding the Free Software Foundation and launching the GNU project in 1983 to create a free, Unix-compatible OS, advocating for copyleft (GPL) against software patents and DRM.
*   **Hedy Lamarr** co-invented a frequency-hopping guidance system with George Antheil in 1942, an early example of spread spectrum technology.
*   **Leonardo Torres Quevedo**, a Spanish engineer, advanced automation and early computing through "arithmophores," remote control (**Telekino**, 1904), and formal symbolic language for drawings (1907).
*   **Alfred North Whitehead**, a mathematician and philosopher, co-authored *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913). His philosophy centers on reality as dynamic "occasions of experience," rejecting materialism. He introduced **"prehension,"** championed a "transdisciplinary" education, and developed metaphysical concepts like **"panexperientialism."** His holistic approach spans mathematics, cosmology, and process metaphysics (*Process and Reality*), promoting interdependence across science and ecological ethics. His influence is seen in American progressive theology (e.g., Charles Hartshorne, John B. Cobb), is integrating with Taoism, Buddhism, and Confucianism in China to support "ecological civilization," and extends to business administration and organizational theory, where scholars like Mark Dibben apply his process metaphysics to management. Furthermore, Stout and Staton link his views to Mary Parker Follett's concepts of relational process, and his political philosophy favors persuasion over force.
