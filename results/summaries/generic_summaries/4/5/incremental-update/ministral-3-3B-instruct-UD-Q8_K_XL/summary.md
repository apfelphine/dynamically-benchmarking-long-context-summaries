**Noam Chomsky (1928–Present) & John von Neumann (1903–1957):**
Noam Chomsky’s transformational grammar (*Syntactic Structures*, 1957; *Aspects of Syntax*, 1965) revolutionized linguistics by introducing **universal grammar**, critiquing behaviorism through the **language acquisition device (LAD)** and bridging linguistics, cognitive science, and computing. His interdisciplinary work extended into **political activism**, challenging U.S. imperialism through critiques of conflicts like Vietnam and the Israeli-Palestinian struggle (*The Responsibility of Intellectuals*, 1967; *Manufacturing Consent*, 1988). John von Neumann’s contributions reshaped mathematics, physics, and computing via **ergodic theory, game theory, and von Neumann architecture**, though his anti-communist stance shaped military applications.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970):**
Russell’s work spanned **logical philosophy** (*The Principles of Mathematics*, 1903; *Principia Mathematica*, 1910–1913) and radical activism. His **Trinity College controversy** over pacifism during WWI led to dismissal and imprisonment, evolving into a nuanced pacifism. Post-war, he traveled to **Soviet Russia (1920)** and **China (1921–1922)**, co-founding the **Beacon Hill School (1927)**. Politically, Russell engaged with **Zionism, socialism, nuclear disarmament, arms embargoes**, and opposition to the **Vietnam War and Israel’s aggression**, advocating for **freedom of thought, human rights, LGBT rights**, and **universal basic income** through anarchist and socialist frameworks.

**Garry Kasparov (1963–Present):**
Born **Garik Kimovich Weinstein**, Kasparov adopted the surname **Kasparov** (1982) amid family displacement from the **1990s anti-Armenian pogroms** and his father’s leukemia. A chess legend, he trained under **Vladimir Makogonov** and **Alexander Shakarov**, dominating with an **attacking style**, winning **12 world titles (1985–2005)** and **8 Olympic golds**. His rivalry with computers peaked in the **1990s**, including defeats to **Deep Blue (1997)**. Beyond chess, he launched **education initiatives**, particularly in Africa, and co-founded opposition groups like **The Other Russia**, facing **imprisonment for resisting Putin’s regime**. Exiled from Russia (2013), Kasparov became a vocal critic of Putin’s **authoritarian rule**, condemning the **2022 Ukraine invasion** and advocating for **regime collapse, sanctions, and NATO support**. His critiques also address **AI, hyperautomation, UN ineffectiveness**, and historical/philosophical inquiries.

**Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914):**
Peirce’s philosophy integrated **mathematics, logic, and pragmatism**, pioneering **cardinal arithmetic, Boolean algebra, and axiomatic set theory**. His **three phenomenological categories**—**Firstness (qualities/abstract ideas)**, **Secondness (brute facts)**, and **Thirdness (representation/generality)**—structured his epistemology. Expanding Aristotle’s logic, Peirce developed **formal semiotic logic**, introducing a **triadic system (sign, object, interpretant)** to study thought processes. His **six-tiered hierarchy of sciences**—from **Science of Discovery** to **Practical Science**—classifies disciplines within *Science of Review*, blending theory with his lifelong polymathic work. His **triadic logic framework** and **pragmatic inquiry model** formalized relational logic, influencing **mathematics, computer science, and economics**. Peirce’s **fallibilism** and **pragmatic maxim** (*meaning arises from utility*) framed inquiry as a **dynamic, recursive process**, while his **metaphysics** included **modal realism, belief in God as an evolving hypothesis**, and **objective idealism ("matter as effete mind")**, balancing chance, necessity, and creative love.
