# Summary

## Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov, born in Baku in 1963, is a chess prodigy who rose rapidly through Soviet and international ranks to become the youngest-ever World Chess Champion in 1985 after a controversial match with Anatoly Karpov. He held the world title through several close matches until 2000 and was the highest-rated player for over two decades. Kasparov broke from FIDE in 1993 to form the Professional Chess Association, leading to a split world championship until reunification attempts faltered. After retiring from professional chess in 2005, he remained active in exhibitions, coaching notable players like Magnus Carlsen and Hikaru Nakamura, and created an online chess platform. His playing style was dynamic with deep strategic preparation; he contributed significantly to opening theory and was an early adopter of computer-assisted preparation. Kasparov also had a historic rivalry with Karpov, and was known for his physical fitness regimen uncommon among chess players.

Politically, Kasparov became a vigorous critic of Vladimir Putin’s administration after retiring, forming opposition groups in Russia and enduring harassment before emigrating in 2013. He has advocated for democracy, condemned Russian aggression including the annexation of Crimea and the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, and received Croatian citizenship. Kasparov authored numerous chess books and political writings, including a critical work titled *Winter Is Coming*, and briefly ran for FIDE presidency. He remains a prominent public intellectual and activist.

## Bertrand Russell

Bertrand Russell (1872–1970) was a British philosopher, logician, mathematician, and social critic, born into aristocracy. Orphaned young, he was educated at home and later Cambridge, excelling in mathematics and philosophy. A founder of analytic philosophy, he made seminal contributions to logic and the philosophy of mathematics, including co-authoring *Principia Mathematica* with Alfred North Whitehead.

Politically, Russell was an ardent pacifist during WWI, resulting in imprisonment and dismissal from Cambridge. He engaged deeply in social reform, education, nuclear disarmament, and anti-war activism throughout his life. Despite controversial early views on eugenics and a brief advocacy for a preemptive nuclear strike against the USSR, he later championed peace, socialism, and humanitarian ideals. He was a vocal critic of imperialism and Israeli policies while promoting freedom of expression and scientific skepticism.

Russell authored over 60 books and thousands of articles, influencing philosophy, mathematics, and social thought. He received numerous honors including the Nobel Prize in Literature (1950) and Order of Merit (1949). His legacy includes founding analytic philosophy, promoting intellectual freedom, and advancing global disarmament.

## Charles Sanders Peirce

Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914) was an American polymath and logician, considered the founder of pragmatism and a pioneer in semiotics, logic, mathematics, and philosophy. Educated at Harvard, he worked mainly for the U.S. Coast Survey while producing influential work in logic, scientific methodology, and philosophy.

Peirce developed the pragmatist maxim and a cycle of scientific inquiry involving abduction, deduction, and induction. He classified signs in triadic relations forming the basis of modern semiotics and pioneered relational logic and quantification. His metaphysics endorsed scholastic realism, objective idealism, fallibilism, and belief in absolute chance. Peirce's contributions to logic include the logic of relations, quantifiers, and diagrammatic reasoning.

Despite lacking stable academic positions and financial struggles, Peirce left foundational works in philosophy and science, influencing figures like William James and Bertrand Russell. His extensive unpublished manuscripts are still being studied. Peirce’s work has global influence across philosophy, logic, linguistics, and cognitive science.

## Noam Chomsky

Noam Chomsky (b. 1928) is an American linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, historian, and political activist. An influential figure in linguistics, he introduced transformational-generative grammar and posited that humans possess innate linguistic structures known as Universal Grammar, fueling debates on language acquisition and cognitive science.

Educated at the University of Pennsylvania and Harvard, Chomsky became a professor at MIT, revolutionizing linguistics and influencing philosophy and computer science. Politically, he is a prominent critic of U.S. foreign policy, capitalism, Israeli government actions, and media propaganda. He advocates anarcho-syndicalism and libertarian socialism and has been active in anti-war and social justice movements since the 1960s.

Chomsky’s extensive publications span linguistics, politics, and philosophy. Despite controversies over his political stances and defenses of free speech (notably the Faurisson affair), he remains a leading intellectual figure worldwide. He has received numerous awards, honorary degrees, and is regarded as a key figure of the cognitive revolution.

## Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) was a German polymath and philosopher who co-invented calculus and made profound contributions to metaphysics, logic, mathematics, law, and diplomacy. Educated at Leipzig and Altdorf, he served the House of Hanover in various advisory roles and promoted scientific societies.

Philosophically, Leibniz is known for his theory of monads—simple, indivisible substances constituting reality—and principles such as identity of indiscernibles, sufficient reason, and pre-established harmony. His *Théodicée* attempted to reconcile the existence of evil with a benevolent God, positing that this is the best of all possible worlds. He anticipated many modern ideas in topology, logic, and symbolic reasoning, seeking a universal characteristic language and calculus.

Leibniz’s work influenced later analytic philosophy, mathematics, and science, despite posthumous controversies such as the calculus priority dispute with Newton. He was a prolific correspondent on varied subjects, and his legacy includes foundational ideas in philosophy, mathematics, computing (binary system), and library science.

## John von Neumann

John von Neumann (1903–1957) was a Hungarian-American mathematician and polymath who contributed fundamentally to set theory, functional analysis, quantum mechanics, computer science, economics, and military technology. A child prodigy educated in Europe, he later worked at Princeton’s Institute for Advanced Study and had extensive involvement in wartime projects including the Manhattan Project.

Von Neumann developed the axiomatic foundations of quantum mechanics, operator algebras (von Neumann algebras), ergodic theory, and lattice theory including continuous geometry. He founded game theory with the minimax theorem and coauthored *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*. He pioneered computer architecture with the stored-program concept and influenced numerical analysis and meteorological computation.

His military work involved design of atomic and hydrogen bombs, particularly explosive lenses for the plutonium bomb, and development of ICBMs. Known for his rapid intellect, eidetic memory, and intuitive problem-solving, von Neumann left enduring legacies across mathematics, physics, economics, and computing, widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians and polymaths of the 20th century.
