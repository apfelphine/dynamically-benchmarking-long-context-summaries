### **Garry Kasparov**
- **Early Life & Chess Introduction**: Born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan, he adopted his mother’s surname Kasparov after her death. His father died of leukemia at age 7. He began studying chess at age 7, trained under Vladimir Makogonov, and won the Soviet Junior Championship twice by age 13.  
- **Rising Chess Career**: At 15, he qualified for the USSR Championship. By age 16, he won the Sokolsky Memorial, gaining recognition. He became a Grandmaster at 17 and rose rapidly through the world rankings, reaching No. 1 in 1984 at age 22, a record that lasted 12 years.  
- **World Championships**: Fought five matches against Anatoly Karpov, winning 21 games, losing 19, and drawing 104. His 1985 victory in Moscow is considered one of the greatest games in chess history.  
- **Break with FIDE**: After a dispute over the 1986 match, he formed the Grandmasters Association and refused to play under FIDE’s jurisdiction, leading to a split in the world championship. He won a 1993 match against Nigel Short under the PCA, creating two world champions.  
- **Post-Retirement**: Retired in 2005, citing lack of personal goals. Continued to play in rapid and blitz events, coached Magnus Carlsen, and supported chess in education.  
- **Legacy**: Known for his dynamic, aggressive playing style, extensive opening preparation, and contributions to chess theory (e.g., the Kasparov Gambit). A political activist, he criticized Putin’s regime and advocated for democracy in Russia.  

### **Bertrand Russell**
- **Early Life & Education**: Born in 1872 into a liberal aristocratic family, he lost his mother at age 11 and faced personal struggles. Educated at home and later at Cambridge, he became a prominent philosopher and logician.  
- **Philosophy & Science**: A founder of analytic philosophy, he championed logical rigor, scientific inquiry, and the rejection of dogma. Developed the "Will to Doubt" and argued that all beliefs are tentative.  
- **Political Activism**: Opposed World War I and was dismissed from Cambridge for pacifist views. Criticized U.S. involvement in Vietnam, supported the anti-nuclear movement, and advocated for disarmament.  
- **Views on Religion**: Declared himself an agnostic or atheist, arguing that religion fosters fear and dependency.  
- **Legacy**: Influential in philosophy, science, and social reform. His works include *The Problems of Philosophy*, *Human Knowledge*, and *The Conquest of Happiness*.  

### **Charles Sanders Peirce**
- **Early Life & Education**: Born in 1839 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, he studied at Harvard and developed a lifelong interest in logic and philosophy.  
- **Philosophy & Semiotics**: Developed the theory of signs, introducing the three categories (Firstness, Secondness, Thirdness), and the concept of pragmatism. His work laid the foundation for modern semiotics.  
- **Mathematics**: Made significant contributions to logic, probability, and the foundations of mathematics, including the development of Boolean algebra and the calculus of relations.  
- **Influence**: Considered one of the most original thinkers of the 19th century. His ideas influenced analytic philosophy, logic, and cognitive science.  

### **Noam Chomsky**
- **Early Life & Education**: Born in 1928 in Philadelphia, he was raised in a Jewish, leftist family. Studied at the University of Pennsylvania and earned a PhD in linguistics from Harvard.  
- **Linguistics**: Founded generative grammar, proposing that language is innate and biologically determined. His work revolutionized linguistics and cognitive science.  
- **Political Activism**: A leading critic of U.S. foreign policy, he argued that U.S. actions are driven by imperial interests. Authored *Manufacturing Consent*, analyzing media propaganda.  
- **Views on Society**: Advocated for democratic socialism, criticized capitalism, and promoted free speech and intellectual independence.  
- **Legacy**: Considered one of the most influential intellectuals of the 20th century. His work in linguistics and political theory remains foundational.  

### **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz**
- **Early Life & Education**: Born in 1646 in Leipzig, he studied at the University of Leipzig and later worked in Paris and Hanover.  
- **Mathematics & Calculus**: Co-invented calculus with Isaac Newton, introducing key notations (e.g., ∫ and d). His work on infinitesimals influenced later developments in non-standard analysis.  
- **Philosophy**: Developed the theory of monads and the principle of sufficient reason. Advocated for optimism and a rational, unified universe.  
- **Science & Technology**: Contributed to physics, topology, and computing. Designed early mechanical calculators and proposed binary arithmetic.  
- **Legacy**: Recognized as a polymath and one of the most important thinkers of the 17th century. His ideas on logic, set theory, and the nature of reality continue to influence modern science and philosophy.  

### **John von Neumann**
- **Early Life & Education**: Born in 1903 in Budapest, he showed prodigious mathematical talent. Earned degrees in chemistry and mathematics, and studied under David Hilbert at Göttingen.  
- **Mathematics & Physics**: Developed the foundations of quantum mechanics, introduced the von Neumann entropy, and created the von Neumann algebra.  
- **Computer Science**: Proposed the von Neumann architecture, which became the standard for modern computers. Pioneered algorithms like merge sort and the Monte Carlo method.  
- **Defense Work**: Played a key role in the Manhattan Project, designing the implosion method for the plutonium bomb. Advised on nuclear weapons and ICBMs.  
- **Legacy**: A polymath who made groundbreaking contributions across mathematics, physics, computing, and economics. Widely regarded as one of the greatest minds of the 20th century.

**Summary**: These figures—Kasparov, Russell, Peirce, Chomsky, Leibniz, and von Neumann—represent pivotal contributions across chess, philosophy, science, and technology. Each exemplifies intellectual depth, innovation, and a commitment to critical thinking, whether in the realm of logic, politics, or the foundations of modern science.
