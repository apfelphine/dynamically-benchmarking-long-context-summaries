# **Concise Summary**

## **Garry Kasparov**
Garry Kasparov, born Garik Weinstein in Baku, is a Russian former world chess champion and a prominent political dissident. A prodigy who began training at age seven, he rose through the ranks to become the youngest world No. 1 at 22 and the youngest world champion at 22, holding the top rating for over 20 years. His career was defined by a fierce rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, resulting in five world championship matches. A major controversy arose in 1993 when Kasparov and Nigel Short broke away from FIDE to form the Professional Chess Association (PCA), creating a split in the official world championship lineage until 2005.

Following his retirement in 2005 due to a lack of goals, Kasparov turned to politics, becoming a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin's administration. He organized opposition marches, ran unsuccessfully for the Russian presidency in 2008, and was arrested multiple times for his activism. He eventually fled Russia in 2013, later obtaining Croatian citizenship. His political views have ranged from support for democratic reforms to strong condemnation of Russian authoritarianism, leading to accusations of extremism and a designation as a "foreign agent" in 2020. Kasparov has also made significant contributions to chess theory, coaching stars like Magnus Carlsen, and established an online chess community.

## **Bertrand Russell**
Bertrand Russell (1872–1970) was a British philosopher, mathematician, and social activist who was a founder of analytic philosophy. Born into an aristocratic family, he was educated at home and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he excelled in mathematics and philosophy. His career was marked by a lifelong commitment to pacifism, free speech, and social justice. He faced dismissal from Cambridge in 1916 for refusing to pledge allegiance to the war effort and was imprisoned for lecturing against the U.S. entry into World War I.

Russell was a prominent advocate for nuclear disarmament, signing the Russell–Einstein Manifesto and campaigning against the Vietnam War. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1950. His personal life was complex, involving several marriages and children, and he later became a 3rd Earl Russell. He died in 1970 and is remembered as a defender of intellectual freedom and a critic of dogmatism, leaving behind a vast legacy in philosophy and social reform.

## **Charles Sanders Peirce**
Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914) was an American philosopher, scientist, and mathematician known as the founder of pragmatism and a pioneer in logic and semiotics. Born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, he suffered from a nervous condition that led to social isolation and financial hardship in his later years. He worked intermittently for the U.S. Coast Survey and held a non-tenured position at Johns Hopkins University, where he lectured briefly in logic.

Peirce's most enduring contributions include his theory of pragmatism, which defines the meaning of a concept by its practical consequences, and his development of semiotics (the theory of signs), classifying them into icons, indices, and symbols. He also made significant discoveries in logic, including the algebra of logic, and in mathematics, such as cardinal arithmetic for infinite numbers and the foundations of probability theory. Despite his profound influence on William James, John Dewey, and modern computer science, he remained largely unrecognized during his lifetime.

## **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz**
Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) was a German polymath and one of the most influential philosophers and scientists of the 17th century. Born in Leipzig, he was raised by his mother after his father's death and received a broad education through his father's extensive library. He studied law and philosophy in Leipzig, Altdorf, and Paris, and later served as a courtier and librarian for the House of Hanover.

Leibniz is credited with independently inventing calculus alongside Isaac Newton and for developing the binary number system, which anticipated modern computing. His philosophy, centered on monads and the principle of sufficient reason, sought to reconcile rationalism with Christian theology. He was a prolific diplomat and advocate for European unity and religious tolerance. Despite being lampooned by Voltaire, his reputation has recovered significantly in the 20th century, and he is celebrated for his contributions to logic, mathematics, and the philosophy of science.

## **John von Neumann**
John von Neumann (1903–1957) was a child prodigy and a Hungarian-American mathematician, physicist, and computer scientist who made fundamental contributions to multiple disciplines. Born into a wealthy Jewish family in Budapest, he mastered advanced mathematics as a teenager and earned degrees in both mathematics and chemical engineering. He became a prominent figure at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton and the University of Chicago.

Von Neumann founded the field of game theory and established the mathematical framework for quantum mechanics. He was a key architect of the modern computer, designing the stored-program architecture (von Neumann architecture) used in the ENIAC and IAS machines. His work on the Manhattan Project involved the design of the plutonium implosion lens for the atomic bomb. He also contributed to fluid dynamics, meteorology, and economics. Died young of cancer, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most versatile scientists of the 20th century.
