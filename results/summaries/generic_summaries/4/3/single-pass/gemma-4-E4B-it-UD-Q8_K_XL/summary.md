# Garry Kasparov
Kasparov was born Garik Kimovich Weinstein in Baku, Azerbaijan SSR, and adopted the surname Kasparov in 1990 after his father’s death and the family fled anti-Armenian pogroms. He began serious chess study at age seven, training at Mikhail Botvinnik's school, and quickly rose through the ranks, winning the World Junior Chess Championship in 1980.

In his career, Kasparov achieved the rank of world No. 1 player in January 1984, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1. He had a historic rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, winning the World Chess Championship in 1985. His relationship with FIDE strained, leading to his break and ejection from the organization in 1993 after his match against Nigel Short was played under the Professional Chess Association (PCA).

Kasparov lost the world title to Viswanathan Anand in 2000 against Vladimir Kramnik. He announced his retirement from regular competitive chess in March 2005, citing a lack of personal goals. Post-retirement, he has engaged in various chess activities and became a prominent political figure, vocally criticizing Putin's regime, and he holds a keen interest in education and has been involved in numerous international human rights efforts.

# Bertrand Russell
Bertrand Russell was a highly influential British philosopher, logician, and social critic. Born into an aristocratic family, his early life was marked by personal struggle and intellectual development, leading him to become an atheist and agnostic.

Academically, he was a founder of analytic philosophy, distinguished in mathematics, and published seminal works like *Principia Mathematica* with Alfred North Whitehead. His philosophical interests spanned metaphysics, logic, ethics, and epistemology.

Politically and socially, Russell was a fierce advocate for pacifism, nuclear disarmament, and free thought, famously protesting WWI and later campaigning against the Vietnam War. His views included support for a "scientific society" and a critique of dogma.

# Charles Sanders Peirce
Charles Sanders Peirce was an American philosopher and logician renowned for founding Pragmatism and developing a comprehensive theory of inquiry. His work is characterized by a pervasive three-category system: Firstness (quality of feeling), Secondness (reaction/actuality), and Thirdness (representation/mediation).

Peirce established his pragmatic maxim, which defines the meaning of a concept by its conceivable practical effects. He developed a three-phase model for scientific inquiry: abductive genesis, deductive application, and inductive testing.

In logic and semiotics, he pioneered the concept of the triadic sign relation (sign, object, interpretant) and created three key typologies of signs: Qualisign, sinsign, and legisign; Icon, index, and symbol; and Rheme, dicisign, and argument. He also made foundational contributions to mathematics, including work on infinite numbers and Boolean algebra.

# Noam Chomsky
Noam Chomsky is a linguist and political dissident whose work is recognized globally across academia and social movements. His linguistic theory, Generative Grammar, posits that language is based on innate, species-specific cognitive structures, a concept known as universal grammar.

His political activism is centered on critiques of U.S. foreign policy, American imperialism, and corporate media influence. Co-authoring *Manufacturing Consent*, he developed the propaganda model, arguing that mainstream media reinforces state ideologies through five filters.

Chomsky identifies as an anarcho-syndicalist or libertarian socialist, advocating for a system of direct worker control. He is a prominent figure in the anti-war movement, having consistently criticized U.S. involvement in conflicts such as Vietnam and the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

# Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
Gottfried Leibniz was a prolific polymath who made foundational contributions across philosophy, mathematics, and science. He sought to reconcile reason and faith, believing God created the "best of all possible worlds."

Philosophically, his most famous contribution is the theory of monads, which posits that the universe is composed of simple, unique, non-interacting substances, governed by the principle of pre-established harmony. His theological work, the *Théodicée*, attempts to justify God’s goodness despite worldly imperfection.

In mathematics, Leibniz is credited with co-inventing calculus, introducing modern notation ($\int$ and $d/dx$), and pioneering work in binary arithmetic and linear algebra, including the concept of the matrix. He is also considered a precursor to computer science due to his concept of *calculus ratiocinator* and *characteristica universalis*.

# John von Neumann
John von Neumann was an unparalleled genius in mathematics, physics, computer science, and economics, widely regarded as one of the 20th century's greatest intellectual figures.

In mathematics, he was instrumental in resolving foundational issues, notably providing the axioms for set theory to resolve Russell's paradox. He founded modern game theory, proving the minimax theorem, and made groundbreaking contributions to functional analysis, measure theory, and the theory of operator algebras.

In physics, he created the rigorous mathematical framework for quantum mechanics (the Dirac–von Neumann axioms), and his work on fluid dynamics, including blast waves, was critical to the Manhattan Project. He was also a pioneer in computing, developing the single-memory, stored-program architecture and the merge sort algorithm.
