### Charles Sanders Peirce
Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914) was a polymath who made significant contributions to logic, semiotics, and the philosophy of science, particularly through his development of **pragmatism**. Born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, Peirce faced personal challenges, including a nervous condition and social isolation, which hindered his academic career despite his intellectual brilliance. He worked intermittently for the U.S. Coast Survey, where his father's protection helped him, though he was dismissed in 1884 due to his relationship with Juliette Pourtalai. Peirce's philosophy, rooted in the **Metaphysical Club**, emphasized fallibilism and the scientific method, distinguishing abduction, deduction, and induction. Although contemporary recognition was slow, his work on existential graphs, the logic of relatives, and the theory of signs eventually gained acclaim, with major scholarly editions published posthumously.

### Garry Kasparov
Garry Kasparov (born 1963) is a legendary chess grandmaster widely regarded as the greatest player of all time. Born in Baku to a Jewish father and Armenian mother, he fled pogroms in 1990 and became the youngest World Chess Champion in 1985. His career was defined by intense rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, a series of world championship matches, and a dramatic break with FIDE in 1993, leading to a split in the world chess title. Post-retirement, Kasparov has been a vocal political dissident in Russia, opposing Vladimir Putin's administration, organizing protests, and advocating for democracy and human rights. He has also engaged in chess education, consulting for young players like Magnus Carlsen, and explored topics ranging from artificial intelligence to historical revisionism.

### Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) was a German polymath and one of the founding figures of modern philosophy and mathematics. Born in Leipzig, he excelled in law and philosophy before turning to mathematics, where he independently invented **calculus** (simultaneously with Isaac Newton) and developed the **binomial system**. His philosophical contributions include the theory of **monads**, the principle of **sufficient reason**, and the concept of pre-established harmony. Leibniz was also a prolific diplomat who advocated for a unified Europe and a universal language (*characteristica universalis*). Despite initial neglect and ridicule by Voltaire, his reputation recovered in the 19th century, and he is now celebrated as a precursor to computer science and modern logic.

### Bertrand Russell
Bertrand Russell (1872–1970) was a British philosopher, logician, and social activist who co-founded **analytic philosophy** with Alfred North Whitehead. Best known for his work *Principia Mathematica* and his identification of **Russell's paradox**, he was a prominent pacifist during World War I, leading to his dismissal from Trinity College, Cambridge. Russell's career spanned diverse interests, including mathematics, logic, ethics, and politics. He was a fierce critic of totalitarianism, opposing both Stalinist Russia and Nazi Germany, and later became a vocal advocate for nuclear disarmament and the Vietnam War. His life was marked by personal struggles with mental health and multiple marriages, culminating in his death in 1970.

### John von Neumann
John von Neumann (1903–1957) was a Hungarian-American mathematician and physicist often described as the most influential figure of the 20th century. A child prodigy, he made groundbreaking contributions to **set theory** (axiomatization), **quantum mechanics** (Dirac–von Neumann axioms), **game theory** (minimax theorem), and **computer science** (von Neumann architecture). His work on fluid dynamics during WWII led to the Manhattan Project, where he helped design the implosion mechanism for the atomic bomb. Beyond pure science, he founded the field of game theory, developed early concepts of artificial intelligence and cellular automata, and served as a high-ranking advisor to the U.S. government on nuclear strategy and ICBMs until his death from cancer at age 53.

### Noam Chomsky
Noam Chomsky (born 1928) is an American linguist, philosopher, and political activist who is widely credited with starting the **cognitive revolution** in linguistics. He developed the theory of **universal grammar** and **generative grammar**, arguing that humans possess an innate "language acquisition device." Beyond linguistics, Chomsky is a prominent critic of U.S. foreign policy, imperialism, and the media, famously articulating the "propaganda model" alongside Edward S. Herman. His political views, rooted in anarcho-syndicalism and libertarian socialism, have made him a target for criticism from conservative groups and the mainstream press, particularly regarding his stance on Israel and the Holocaust denial controversy. He has maintained a lifelong commitment to anti-war activism and social justice.
