**Pioneers of Science and Thought**
This group includes influential thinkers such as **Bertrand Russell** (British philosopher advocating pacifism and social justice), **Charles Sanders Peirce** (American pragmatist who formalized the scientific method using Abduction, Deduction, and Induction, and defined Information), **John von Neumann** (contributing to Set Theory, quantum mechanics, game theory, and stored-program architecture), **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz** (who explored the monad concept and developed a *calculus ratiocinator*), and **Noam Chomsky** (linguist known for universal grammar and critiques of U.S. imperialism).

**Garry Kasparov: Chess, Activism, and Intellectual Output**
Garry Kasparov, a former World No. 1 chess player (1984–2005, Elo 2851) who pioneered computer analysis—notably losing to IBM's Deep Blue in 1997 and Deep Junior in 2003—has become a major political activist. His criticism of the Russian regime intensified, leading to confrontations, arrest during Pussy Riot protests in 2012, and eventual exile in 2013. From exile, he has consistently condemned the Russian government over issues including anti-gay laws, the 2014 annexation of Crimea, the 2014 Sochi Olympics, and escalating after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, resulting in him being labeled a "foreign agent" and "terrorist" by Russia. Furthermore, he has voiced support for Artsakh's independence, welcomed the 2018 Velvet Revolution, and advocated for Bosnian people during the Yugoslav Wars, while also engaging in human rights advocacy as Chairman of the Human Rights Foundation and criticizing leaders like Trump and Musk. Kasparov's diverse output spans chess (authoring autobiographies and works like "His Revolution in the 70s"), politics (publishing "Winter Is Coming," advocating for a democratic alternative to the UN), and academia, where he has shown interest in historical revisionism. He is also involved in business and media, having consulted for projects like *The Queen's Gambit*, and resides in New York City.
